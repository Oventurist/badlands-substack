# Government-Sponsored Censorship

Government-sponsored censorship refers to actions taken by a government to suppress or restrict the dissemination of information, ideas, or opinions, including direct censorship through laws or regulations as well as indirect methods such as pressuring private entities to censor content.[1]

## The manufactured demand

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that the campus [[safe-spaces|safe space]] movement of 2016–2020 appeared to the casual observer to be an organic culture-war phenomenon, but was in fact accompanied by an agenda aimed at building public support for government censorship rather than protecting anyone from [[hate-speech|hate speech]].[1] Liberals who had historically found censorship repugnant were, after years of partisan strife, persuaded to demand government action against MAGA conservatives.[1]

## Party-agnostic mechanism

Central to the argument is that the [[deep-state|"deep state"]] — the unelected interagency blob — is not intrinsically pro-Democrat or liberal-sympathetic; it exists in both parties and is indifferent to which cause generates the public support it needs.[1] If censorship frameworks cannot be adopted under a Democrat-heavy federal government, the same frameworks will be attempted under a Republican-heavy one.[1] DeLarme presents the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]] as the Republican-coded instances of this pattern, and the EU's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] as its international counterpart.[1]

## Historical precedent

The article draws an explicit parallel to the post-9/11 period, when politicians persuaded Americans to relinquish longstanding rights in the name of safety, producing the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]] and mass warrantless surveillance by the intelligence community.[1] In the current cycle, "antisemitism" has replaced "terrorism" as the cudgel used to justify censorship.[1]
