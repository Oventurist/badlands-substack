# Government-Sponsored Censorship

Government-sponsored censorship refers to actions taken by a government to suppress or restrict the dissemination of information, ideas, or opinions. This includes direct censorship through laws or regulations as well as indirect methods such as pressuring private entities to censor content.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Argument from Badlands Media

In "A Glaring Double Standard," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that the campus [[safe-spaces|safe space]] movement of 2016–2020 was not merely an organic culture-war phenomenon but served an agenda aimed at building public support for government-sponsored censorship.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He contends that liberals, who once found censorship repugnant, were persuaded after years of partisan strife and psychological manipulation to demand government censorship of their perceived enemies, chiefly MAGA conservatives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme stresses that the [[deep-state|unelected interagency "blob"]] is not exclusively pro-Democrat or liberal-sympathetic; it operates within both parties and is indifferent to which cause generates sufficient public support for its aims.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) Where a censorship framework cannot be adopted under a Democrat-heavy federal government, the same framework will be attempted under a Republican-heavy one.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## The "hate speech" pretext

The essay identifies "[[hate-speech|protecting people from hate speech]]" as the foundational pretext of every censorship framework advanced by the interagency and international blob over the past decade, and the same rationale used to justify the European Union's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) Concrete American manifestations cited include the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]], both of which rely on the [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|IHRA definition of antisemitism]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) DeLarme compares the dynamic to the post-9/11 period, when politicians persuaded Americans to relinquish longstanding rights in the name of safety, producing the Patriot Act and mass warrantless surveillance by the intelligence community.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)
