# Nuclear Family

The **nuclear family** is described by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in "Parenting For Freedom" (May 12, 2023) as the field upon which the dramas and challenges of a child's life play out.[1] Humans receive the guidance, support, and encouragement they need from other people — father, mother, brothers, sisters, and extended family.[1]

## The developmental field

Within the family, the child navigates the progression from incapable infant to more capable toddler, to more capable child, to young adult.[1] At each stage the family, in concert with the outer culture of extended family, local community, and grand community, needs to encourage the child to take on more and more duties while learning to manage internal upheavals, gaps in wisdom, and social dynamics.[1]

## The chain of transmission

Deschamps describes three concentric layers: the nuclear family; the local community of people who interact with the child, acting as amplifiers of the values and ideals presented to them; and the greater community, expressed these days as mass media, where children explore and reinforce cultural conditions and influences.[1]

When there is an unbroken chain of beliefs and values running from parents to the greater culture, the child has an integrated web of support.[1] Deschamps contends that this no longer exists: instead the chain is disintegrated and broken, with parents saying and doing one thing, the community another, and mass media propagandizing further still.[1] The effect is deeply unsettling, producing confused and adrift children and adults who are less able to achieve success and are easily duped by cultural trends and propaganda.[1] He cites the transgender craze inflicting harm on young girls as an example of this broken chain, in which community and culture confuse instead of guide.[1]

The argument connects to the wider [[war-on-the-family|war on the family]] theme in Deschamps's work, and to the roles of the [[role-model|role model]] and the [[zone-of-proximal-development|zone of proximal development]].[1]
