# EU Russia Sanctions Resistance (2026)

In July 2026 the [[european-union|European Union]] encountered increasing internal resistance as it attempted to finalize its latest package of sanctions against [[russia|Russia]], with several member states pushing back over measures they said would disproportionately harm their own economies.[1] Because EU sanctions require unanimous approval, objections from even a handful of governments stalled negotiations and exposed growing divisions within the bloc over how far additional economic restrictions should go.[1]

## Objections by member state

Greece opposed restrictions affecting its shipping industry, while France and Italy objected to proposals involving travel restrictions on former Russian combatants.[1] Germany and Portugal resisted measures targeting imports of Russian fish over concerns for domestic industries.[1] The disagreements came despite the EU maintaining its broader sanctions regime against Russia, which had recently been extended through July 2027.[1]

EU officials continued to argue that sanctions remain an essential tool for pressuring Moscow over the war in Ukraine, but analysts said the negotiations highlighted the growing difficulty of maintaining consensus as national economic interests diverge.[1] Observers noted that while previous packages were approved after lengthy negotiations, the newest proposals met broader resistance from multiple member states rather than a single holdout.[1]

## Kash Patel Russia visit

Related reporting that FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] recently visited Russia drew attention after Russian media claimed he delivered a letter from President Trump to President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1] The [[fbi|FBI]] did not publicly confirm the reported visit or its purpose, and the White House did not release details regarding the reported communication.[1]
