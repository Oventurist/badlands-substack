# Ultimate Value

The "ultimate value," also called a value singularity, is the culminating concept of the February 24, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Steering Wheel of Civilization" by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]].[1] It is defined as a value with the power to incorporate all other good and true values into itself, and the essay identifies it as the quest to seek for, know, and strive to be like the Creator of all things.[1]

## Value mechanics

The essay builds toward the concept through what it calls value mechanics. Values are described as worthy things — health, friendship, a fulfilling life — and enjoyment of an activity is treated as evidence that one values it.[1] If a human being is like a car, values are the fuel in the tank: they motivate, inspire and push people forward.[1] The essay declares plainly that values are the steering wheel of civilization.[1]

A country is compared to a team, and a national goal to the shared focus that keeps a team on track; the illustration used is a team climbing Mount Everest, where each member invests unique specialties and passions in a collective effort that would be unattainable alone.[1] Each climber values the goal for personal reasons — the photographer for the images, the career hiker for entry into the elite club of Everest mountaineers — while values also overlap, as everyone at a dinner shares the value of eating food for individual reasons.[1]

## Conflicting value systems

Values should be woven together, harmonized and coordinated, since a single value pursued in isolation can be dangerous.[1] A criminal values whatever benefit a crime imparts while sacrificing the values of peace and respect for others' property, so that antisocial behavior can be understood as a disintegrated or conflicting value system.[1] For individuals, conflicting values cause wasted potential — an athlete with raw talent chasing girls instead of honing skills, a person eating food known to be toxic while aspiring to avoid disease, a husband coveting other women though he loves his wife.[1]

Conversely, a person whose values work together like a well-oiled machine can do almost superhuman things.[1] [[leonardo-da-vinci|Leonardo da Vinci]] is offered as a figure singular of mind and purpose who coordinated his ambitions so that they did not conflict, alongside [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as highly productive people who ensure their several ambitions move forward together.[1] The more coordinated a value system, the more productive the individuals and groups it motivates; modern civilization, for all its problems, is described as incredibly productive, with a dozen contractors now building a home in under a week where it once took a community months, and millions of homes heated without collecting a single piece of firewood.[1]

## The spiritual answer

Asking what the most sweeping common truth and value shared by all people might be, the essay declares that the answer is a spiritual one.[1] Some of the most successful civilizations in history are said to have had a shared spiritual identity — for the Jews the fatherhood of Yahweh, in Islam the prophet Mohamed — producing communities lasting over a thousand years, though both were exclusionary; Buddhism and Christianity are cited as offering universal spiritual identities open to all people regardless of status or nature.[1]

The [[founding-fathers|founding fathers of the United States]] are described as having gone "meta" with the concept by forming the country on a theistic belief system, an advance the essay calls powerfully effective in the technological race of human civilization.[1] Their argument was that all people could be united through a theistic worldview in which a Creator-God of all things is universally recognizable, so that a universal father of all things naturally and logically leads to a universal brotherhood and sisterhood of mankind.[1] This theistic center is said to have remained a unifying force on the world stage even after the British waged all-out cultural warfare following American independence, and the true American dream of a fair, just and free society united under a common belief is credited with reshaping the world.[1]
