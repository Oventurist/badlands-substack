# 2024 Toronto—St. Paul's By-Election

In June 2024 the governing Liberals suffered a surprise defeat in a special election for the Toronto—St. Paul's riding, an urban stronghold the party had held since 1993, losing the seat to the Conservatives.[1]

The staggering loss, arriving amid Canada's affordability crisis and a swelling public desire for political change, sharply increased pressure on Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] to resign for the good of his party.[1] Coverage placed Trudeau's difficulties in the company of a long list of sinking incumbent governments that emerged from the global pandemic with one foot in the political grave, from UK Conservative prime minister [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]], on track to be obliterated the following week, to France's [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] scrambling for survival and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in a dogfight against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Trudeau was being pressured to leave by the end of summer so his party would have time to select a new leader before the following year's election, and observed that the situation "sounds familiar" in light of parallel pressure on Biden.[1] She highlighted CBC host David Cochrane pressing Trudeau on whether he accepted that he was the main reason his party was struggling, and characterized Trudeau's reply — citing conversations with other leaders at the [[g7|G7]] and at the Ukraine peace conference in Switzerland, and noting global inflation, cost-of-living, interest rate, housing and childcare pressures — as detached from reality and as inadvertent confirmation of rising global populism.[1] In her framing, those conditions were the fault of the G7, Trudeau and their global handlers rather than inexplicable misfortune, and she closed by saying she doubted the regime could replace Biden while remaining curious whether it could replace Trudeau.[1]
