# Georgia Senate Bill 202

Georgia Senate Bill 202 is a Republican-backed Georgia election law enacted as part of a broader GOP effort to tighten voting rules nationwide following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2020 loss, which prompted widespread allegations of voter fraud among Republicans.[1]

## Justice Department litigation

A lawsuit filed under former President Joe Biden in June 2021 alleged that SB 202 would disproportionately hinder Black voters' access to the polls and that the law intentionally suppressed Black voters.[1] On March 31, 2025, Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] directed the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to dismiss the challenge, framing the case as politically motivated and arguing it misrepresented the law's effects.[1]

"Contrary to the Biden Administration's false claims of suppression, Black voter turnout actually increased under SB 202," Bondi said in the announcement, adding that "Georgians deserve secure elections, not fabricated claims of false voter suppression meant to divide us."[1]

## Related litigation

The dismissal came alongside other Georgia election-system rulings, including U.S. District Judge [[amy-totenberg|Amy Totenberg]]'s dismissal of [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]], the long-running challenge to the state's electronic voting machines.[1]
