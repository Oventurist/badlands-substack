# 2025 US Submarine Repositioning

The 2025 US submarine repositioning refers to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement in early August 2025 that he had ordered two U.S. nuclear-powered submarines to be moved to "appropriate regions," a step taken in response to inflammatory nuclear rhetoric from former Russian president [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]].[1]

## Background

Days before the announcement, Medvedev said that increased involvement from Washington in efforts to end Russia's war against Ukraine could bring the United States and Moscow closer to direct conflict.[1] Trump had previously announced an August 8 deadline for [[russia|Russia]] to reach a ceasefire with Ukraine, warning that failure to reach an agreement would trigger U.S. secondary sanctions — measures that could impose tariffs on Moscow's trading partners, including Brazil, [[china|China]], and India.[1] Trump slammed the former Russian leader for his remarks and said he had ordered the submarine repositioning in response.[1]

## Kremlin response

Kremlin spokesperson [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said on August 4, 2025, that Moscow urged caution in the use of "nuclear rhetoric."[1] "Russia is very attentive to the topic of nuclear non-proliferation. And we believe that everyone should be very, very cautious with nuclear rhetoric," he told Russian state media.[1]

## Commentary

In the Badlands News Brief, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the public dismantling of [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] does not merely exonerate Trump but begins to sow the seeds for a Russian redemption arc in the collective Western mind.[1] He wrote that Russia has for decades been painted as the ultimate boogeyman — "a salivating tiger ready to devour the West" — prodded relentlessly by the very hegemon claiming to defend against it.[1]

He catalogued the scars in the Russian historical experience: the Bolshevik Revolution and the period from 1922 to 1991 that stamped out the tsarist legacy and imprisoned and executed the last Romanovs, followed by the World Wars, in which Russia lost roughly 30 million people — 2% of its population in the first and 15% in the second — amid broken infrastructure and sustained martial and subversive attacks.[1] As detailed in his [[righteous-russia|Righteous Russia]] series, he characterized this not as random calamity but as a confluence of assaults that left Russia reeling, only to be recast in Western media as the "prime evil" — the threat in Hollywood films such as *Rambo* and *Rocky IV*, and in political narratives from Kennedy to [[barack-obama|Obama]] in which fear of the "gorging Soviet bear" justified endless militarism.[1] Russiagate's exposure, he argued, flips this framing so that the true villains are the globalists who poked the bear, and Trump's lengthy and productive phone call with [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] earlier in the year signals his awareness of this.[1]
