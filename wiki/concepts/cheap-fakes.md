# Cheap Fakes

"Cheap fakes" is a term that entered widespread American political usage in June 2024 to describe videos that are cropped, edited or selectively framed in a misleading way without the use of artificial intelligence.[1] The label was deployed aggressively by [[white-house|White House]] officials pushing back against a wave of viral clips that purportedly showed [[joe-biden|President Joe Biden]] appearing confused or disoriented, and which raised questions about his mental and physical fitness ahead of the 2024 election.[1] Reporting by [[the-hill|The Hill]] presented the phenomenon as the latest example of how technology might be used deceptively during the campaign, distinguishing it from AI-generated "deepfakes."[1]

## The triggering clips

Three clips in particular drove the term into the national spotlight.[1] One viral clip, first shared by a [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] account, depicted Biden apparently struggling to sit down at a D-Day ceremony in a chair critics claimed did not exist; the clip cut off before Biden actually took his seat.[1] Another came from Biden's trip to Italy for the Group of Seven meeting, where he and other world leaders watched a skydiving demonstration: a cropped version appeared to show Biden wandering off before the Italian prime minister nudged him back toward the group, while the fuller camera angle made clear he was gesturing toward a parachutist who had just landed.[1] The New York Post made the incident its front page the following day under the headline "Meander in Chief."[1]

## Badlands Media commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], characterised "cheap fakes" as a newly invented category of misinformation manufactured by panicked Democrats, noting that gotcha videos have been a staple of politics for as long as video has existed.[1] In her reading, the outrage amounted to an admission — "we know this bumbling old fool looks fragile and incompetent, but don't believe your eyes."[1] She drew a comparison with the editing of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Charlottesville remarks, which she argued produced the enduring "very fine people" claim that Biden had repeated as recently as the previous month.[1] She connected the episode to the wider collapse of mainstream newsroom credibility discussed in earlier Briefs, and argued that any effort to present Biden as a strong leader was itself "cheap and fake."[1]
