# Paperwall

Paperwall is the name given by [[citizen-lab|Citizen Lab]] researcher Alberto Fittarelli to a network of at least 123 Chinese-operated websites that posed as local news outlets in 30 countries in order to disseminate pro-Beijing disinformation.[1] The campaign was described in a Citizen Lab report published on February 7, 2024, and defined as "a large, and fast growing, network of anonymous websites posing as local news outlets."[1]

## Scope and methods

Fittarelli characterized Paperwall as "a sprawling influence operation serving both financial and political interests, and in alignment with Beijing's political agenda."[1] Countries reported as targets included the United States, South Korea, Japan, Russia, the United Kingdom, France, Brazil, Turkey and Italy.[1]

The sites adopted names invoking local geography or established mastheads to appear legitimate: Eiffel Post and Provence Daily in French; British FT for the United Kingdom; Sendai Shimbum and Fujiyama Times for Japan; Daegu Journal and Busan Online for South Korea; and Roma Journal and Napoli Money for Italy.[1] Only one site in the network targeted a US audience — UpdateNews.Info, whose domain was registered in July 2019, making it the first Paperwall website to be registered.[1]

## Assessment and commentary

The Epoch Times reported the findings, which were then discussed in the Badlands News Brief.[1] [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the operation's crudeness — recycling state media output and injecting pro-Beijing commentary — was inconsistent with the sophistication of Chinese thinking on [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] as expressed in [[unrestricted-warfare|Unrestricted Warfare]] since the 1990s.[1] He raised the possibility that repeatedly exposed low-grade campaigns serve secondary purposes, such as gathering intelligence on how such operations are detected, reinforcing the expectation that governments should watch for conventional-style influence work, or drawing attention away from covert multi-domain activity, concluding that "even when China gets caught, it is unwise to assume they are doing what they appear to be doing."[1]
