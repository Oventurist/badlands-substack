# IOC Female Category Policy

The IOC female category policy is a rule adopted by the [[international-olympic-committee|International Olympic Committee]] in March 2026 restricting eligibility for women's events at Olympic competitions to biological females, beginning with the 2028 Los Angeles Games.[1]

## Provisions

Eligibility is determined through a one-time screening for the SRY gene, with athletes testing negative qualifying for the female category.[1] The IOC stated the measure is intended to protect fairness, safety, and integrity in women's competition and that it will not be applied retroactively.[1]

Athletes who test positive for the SRY gene — including transgender competitors and certain intersex athletes — are ineligible for women's events but may compete in other categories, including male or open divisions.[1] The policy includes limited exceptions for specific medical conditions such as Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome.[1]

IOC President [[kirsty-coventry|Kirsty Coventry]] said the decision rested on scientific and medical guidance and cited competitive fairness and athlete safety.[1]

## Reception

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the policy as a win for women, noting that athletes with Y chromosomes are no longer permitted in Olympic women's sport, and argued that "women must not be gaslit into compromising their spaces."[1] She linked the reversal to a broader hoped-for retreat from what she characterized as a social contagion around pediatric gender medicine, while predicting the wider culture war would continue.[1]
