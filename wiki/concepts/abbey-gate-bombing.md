# Abbey Gate Bombing

The Abbey Gate bombing was a suicide attack carried out on August 26, 2021 at the Kabul airport during the American evacuation of Afghanistan. Thirteen United States service members were killed and many others were wounded, making it the deadliest single incident for U.S. forces during the withdrawal operation.[1]

## Missed opportunity to stop the bomber

At the first public hearing of the [[house-foreign-affairs-committee]] on the withdrawal, held in March 2023, Marine sergeant [[tyler-vargas-andrews]] testified that U.S. snipers stationed at the airport before the attack had an opportunity to kill the suspected bomber but were denied authority by military leadership.[1] His sniper team had been given detailed information about the suspect, including a physical description.[1]

When Vargas-Andrews asked intelligence personnel why the man had not been apprehended earlier, he was told that "the asset could not be compromised."[1] He was severely wounded in the bombing.[1]

## Related testimony and commentary

Vargas-Andrews additionally testified that the [[state-department]] turned Afghans away from the airport during the evacuation, which he described as condemning them to death at the hands of the [[taliban]].[1] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] framed the hearing as a further demonstration of the incompetence of the [[joe-biden]] administration and argued that such revelations continued to delegitimize it.[1]

## Capture of a mastermind announced (2025)

During his [[2025-trump-address-to-joint-session-of-congress|address to a joint session of Congress]] on March 4, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced the capture of one of the terrorists who masterminded the Abbey Gate bombing in Afghanistan, which killed 13 US servicemembers.[2] The surprise announcement was one of several made-for-TV moments in a speech otherwise dominated by tariffs and government downsizing.[2]

## Killing of the planner (2023)

In April 2023, senior U.S. administration officials said the [[isis-k|ISIS-K]] leader who planned the Abbey Gate attack had been killed by the [[taliban|Taliban]] in Afghanistan.[3] The [[white-house|White House]] declined to release his name but described him as the "mastermind" of the assault on one of the main entry points used by people trying to evacuate Afghanistan during the U.S. withdrawal.[3] Officials said they had learned of the death weeks earlier but needed time to confirm it, and that experts in the government held "high confidence" that the individual "was indeed the key individual responsible" for the August 26, 2021 bombing, which also killed more than 100 Afghan civilians.[3] The Taliban did not notify the United States; the determination was made from U.S. intelligence gathering and monitoring of threats and actors in the region.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the killing as fulfilment of the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] framework under which, in [[just-human|Just Human]]'s August 2021 analysis, the Taliban had been tasked by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with eliminating [[isis|ISIS]] in Afghanistan.[3]
