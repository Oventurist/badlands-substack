# Tyranny of Logic

The "tyranny of logic" is the second of three mechanisms described in "Hijacked Consciousness - The Spell," the July 29, 2025 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]].[1] The phrase denotes not the use of reason but its supremacy — "logic as a cage, as hierarchy, and as severance from meaning."[1]

## Argument

The essay holds that logic without soul becomes reduction, and that reduction becomes religion.[1] Students were told to think critically, the author writes, when what was meant was to "collapse wonder into method, rhythm into structure, and the heart into the brain and call it progress."[1] In ancient systems, by contrast, logic was "a tool - not a throne," one instrument within a whole cognition that also included story, symbol, sensation, rhythm, dream, intuition and myth.[1] The Greeks did not separate [[logos|logos]] from mythos; Egyptian scribes carried energy as well as records, and reason was placed in service to the divine rather than above it; across Indigenous traditions knowledge was transmitted through experience, story and ceremony, with logic bowing to rhythm, place and elders' wisdom.[1]

Modern education is said to have reversed this by crowning a single instrument of knowing.[1] The consequences listed include learning to dismiss what cannot be measured, to distrust what cannot be proven, to devalue what cannot be diagrammed, and above all to fear the irrational — understood not as the chaotic or violent but as the unquantifiable, poetic, mystical and deeply personal.[1] The dream was pathologized, myth became trivia, and the sacred became superstition; faith was placed in the visible and doubt in the invisible, and because the soul is not visible, "we learned to doubt the soul."[1]

The author presents this as deliberate: a systems-wide shift driven by those who understood that "to control the world, you must control the framework people use to perceive it," so that rather than burning the temples one need only train a generation to roll its eyes at the sacred.[1]

## Behaviorism

The essay attributes the practical machinery of the shift to the behaviorists, naming [[b-f-skinner|B. F. Skinner]] (operant conditioning, rewards or punishments), [[edward-thorndike|Edward Thorndike]] (the Law of Effect, "honey vs vinegar") and [[ivan-pavlov|Ivan Pavlov]] (classical conditioning), who are said to have seen humans "not as beings of light and story, but as reactionary meat waiting to be conditioned."[1] Their methods, the author writes, were weaponized: standardized testing replaced story, behavioral psychology edged out mythic development, and "Skinner's boxes" became classrooms.[1] The mystery of the cosmos was shrunk into stimulus and response.[1]

## Consequences and reversal

The spell is said to tighten further when logic meets machine: the child speaks in STEM acronyms, the sacred is reclassified as irrational bias, and "data literacy outranks embodied wisdom."[1] Children grow up fluent in formulas but starved of meaning, becoming adults who can file taxes and debate politics but cannot recall the last time they wept without shame — a condition the author calls "fragmentation dressed as expertise."[1]

The reversal proposed is not louder argument but "deeper remembering": truth arriving through resonance rather than analysis, beginning with a persistent dream, an unignorable gut feeling, or a silence that speaks louder than the facts.[1] In that restoration logic "remembers its place," serving rather than ruling and rejoining the song as "one quiet instrument tuned to meaning."[1]
