# 2025 Probationary Employee Firings Injunction

The 2025 probationary employee firings injunction refers to a series of federal court orders barring the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] from terminating thousands of probationary federal employees during its second-term workforce reductions.[1]

## The rulings

U.S. District Judge [[james-bredar|James Bredar]] initially ordered officials to temporarily reinstate probationary employees fired at 18 agencies, regardless of duty location.[1] On April 1, 2025, he replaced that order with a preliminary injunction that indefinitely barred the terminations but declined nationwide scope: it added two agencies, the [[department-of-defense|Defense Department]] and the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]], while restricting reinstatement to employees stationed in Washington, D.C., and the 19 plaintiff states.[1]

Bredar reasoned that only states had sued, and only to vindicate their own interests as states rather than as proxies for the workers, so that relief overriding the choices of non-party states would be inappropriate.[1]

## Reception

The case was cited in Badlands coverage as an example of the [[judicial-lawfare-against-the-trump-agenda|lawfare campaign against the Trump agenda]], one of 159 cases counted by the Just Security litigation tracker associated with [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]].[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the administration had anticipated such challenges and that appellate review would clarify contested legal authorities and set precedent.[1]
