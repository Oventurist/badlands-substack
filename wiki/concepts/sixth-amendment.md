# Sixth Amendment

The Sixth Amendment to the United States Constitution guarantees the rights of the accused in criminal prosecutions, including the right to a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury. Its jury guarantee is the subject of the constitutional dispute the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] agreed to hear in June 2026.

## The unresolved definition of "jury"

As reported in the Badlands Brief of June 16, 2026, the Court's 1970 decision in [[williams-v-florida|*Williams v. Florida*]] held that the Sixth Amendment does not require twelve jurors, permitting states to seat smaller panels in criminal cases. Six states, including Florida, still do so. The newly accepted case invites the Court to revisit that holding, with several justices — [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]] prominent among them — having signaled that the historical understanding of a jury at the time of the Founding may have required twelve members.

The originalist argument advanced in the litigation, as summarized in the Brief, is that because the Constitution invoked trial by jury without defining it, it thereby "declined to define it," so that the external standard of historical common-law practice supplies the content of the term. The petitioners are quoted through the observation "that it is not for the court to distinguish between the historic features of common law jury trials that (we think) serve important enough functions to migrate silently into the Sixth Amendment and those that don't."

## Significance in the Badlands corpus

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and commentator Jonathan Drake argue that the reasoning, if accepted, would reach well beyond the number of jurors to what Drake calls "any of their perversions of trial by jury" — that is, the full set of modern departures from the common-law jury. The case is presented as an opportunity for public education about constitutional juries and is linked in the same edition to the [[badlands-jury|GART Jury]] program run by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].
