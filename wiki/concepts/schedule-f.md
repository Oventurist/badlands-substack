# Schedule F

**Schedule F** is a category of federal government employment created by an executive order issued by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on October 21, 2020, in the final months of his first term in office.[1] Under the order, certain federal employees — by one estimate possibly a majority of them — who occupy positions capable of making or enforcing federal policy could be reclassified into the new Schedule F category, with the effect that they would no longer be entitled to traditional civil service protections and could be hired and fired far more easily by appointees of the duly elected president.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

In a March 6, 2023 opinion column for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], author [[j-b-white|J.B. White]] (writing as RattlerGator) argued that Schedule F should be understood not merely as a personnel-classification policy but as the "Tip of the Spear" of a broader organizing philosophy, and described it as a "nuclear weapon in the arsenal" of what he characterized as the true, clandestine [[operation-warp-speed|Operation Warp Speed]] — a top-secret battle plan aimed at what he called the criminal syndicate of criminal syndicates.[1] In his telling, this weapon was "locked and loaded" before Trump's transition "landing teams" entered the federal government as a "transitionary Special Operations force," a maneuver he compared to Army Rangers scaling the cliffs of Pointe du Hoc during the D-Day invasion.[1]

White framed Schedule F as the mechanism by which the [[donald-trump|Trump]] team could go on the offensive against entrenched bureaucratic actors rather than remaining in the defensive posture he attributed to traditional conservatism — defending "our traditional way of life… our constitutional republic… our embrace of freedom and commercial markets."[1] He linked the tool to the study of Soviet-era defector [[yuri-bezmenov|Yuri Bezmenov]], whose account of ideological subversion — demoralization, destabilization, and engineered chaos — White presented as the threat model that Schedule F was designed to counter, arguing that only by going on the offensive could the effort deliver rather than receive demoralization and destabilization.[1]

## The "imperative" framing

The column's title turns on the political meaning of the word "imperative": a situation or policy deemed critical, urgent, or necessary for the public good or national interest, and one carrying a sense of moral obligation and ethical responsibility.[1] White applied that framing both to Schedule F itself and to the base of the Republican Party's frustration with the refusal of what he termed the [[uniparty|UniParty]] to reduce federal spending, arguing that limiting federal spending is designed to reduce the power of "the leviathan" — centralized government's ability to bulldoze the rights of individual sovereign citizens.[1]

## Application to the 2023 Speaker fight

White contended that a "dramatic application" of Schedule F could be discerned in the January 2023 contest for [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election|Speaker of the House]], in which the House voted fifteen times before electing [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]].[1] In a tweet posted in the immediate aftermath, White wrote that Trump had won the speakership for McCarthy and in doing so had empowered the [[house-freedom-caucus|Freedom Caucus]].[1] He also stated that no lasting success in the wider operation was possible without the American people "recapturing our Congress and our Senate" from the lobbying efforts of what he called the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]], declaring that job number one was to recapture the Congress.[1]

## Origins and revocation

In the sequel column, published March 13, 2023, White summarized the policy's history: the Schedule F employment reform was developed primarily by the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] in 2020 and aimed to make it easier to fire or transfer certain federal employees deemed to be involved in policymaking.[2] It was widely criticized by government employee unions and other critics who argued that it would undermine the merit-based civil service system and politicize the federal workforce, and it drew opposition from both Democrats and Republicans.[2] In January 2021, [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] revoked the Schedule F executive order as one of his first acts in office, citing its impact on the non-partisan civil service.[2]

## Freedom Caucus advocacy

White reiterated that the [[house-freedom-caucus|Freedom Caucus]] supported Schedule F as a crucial reform of the civil service, seeing it as necessary if the federal government were ever to be responsive to "We The People."[2] Some members of the caucus wrote to President Trump in August 2020 calling on him to "drain the swamp" and implement the reform as part of reducing the size and influence of the federal bureaucracy, arguing it would remove [[deep-state|Deep State]] bureaucrats impeding the president's agenda — in White's phrasing, bureaucrats "leading an insurrection against the duly elected chief executive."[2] White noted he had been unable to locate a copy of the August 2020 letter but maintained the point stood.[2]

## Coverage in the federal trade press

White treated the volume of [[government-executive|Government Executive]] coverage of Schedule F between November 2020 and February 2023 as evidence that the policy is "a subject of existential interest" to senior federal officials, contrasting it with the absence of any results for the term on the libertarian magazine Reason's website.[2] The headlines he compiled tracked the [[office-of-management-and-budget|OMB]]'s reported designation of 88% of its employees for Schedule F, accelerating implementation by the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]], Biden's revocation, Trump's 2022 endorsement of a plan under which former staffers said 50,000 employees had been identified for removal, and successive Democratic legislative efforts — led in the Senate by [[tim-kaine|Tim Kaine]] — to block any future reclassification.[2]

## Schedule F as a litmus test

White argued that the development of Schedule F, the timing of its implementation at the end of Trump's first term, the alarm it caused among America's enemies, and its championing by the Freedom Caucus together enabled Team Trump to leverage "legitimate compromise" into a hidden victory in the [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election|contest for Speaker]] of the [[118th-congress|118th Congress]].[2] He predicted that across the 2024, 2026 and 2028 election cycles, Schedule F — if not passed into law — would serve as a durable litmus test for any Republican candidate, and, given near-universal support among Republicans, "one hellafied weapon" in successive political battles.[2]
