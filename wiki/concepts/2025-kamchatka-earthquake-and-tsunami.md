# 2025 Kamchatka Earthquake and Pacific Tsunami

On the morning of Wednesday, July 30, 2025, an 8.8-magnitude earthquake struck off Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula in the Far East, one of the strongest earthquakes ever recorded and the strongest in the region since 1952.[1] The temblor generated tsunami waves that reached Japan and Alaska and prompted tsunami warnings for Hawaii, North and Central America, and Pacific islands extending south toward New Zealand.[1]

## Immediate effects

Ports on the Kamchatka Peninsula near the epicenter flooded as residents fled inland.[1] In Honolulu, cars jammed streets and highways hours before waves were expected, with standstill traffic even in areas away from the shoreline.[1] Residents around much of the Pacific coast were advised to move to higher ground and warned that the tsunami danger could persist for more than a day; most locations where waves had already come ashore reported no significant damage in the initial hours.[1]

Reporting cited in the brief indicated that a tsunami struck coastal areas of Russia's Kuril Islands and Japan's northern island of Hokkaido shortly after the quake.[1] Aftershocks were reported to have set off tsunami activity across the northern Pacific, with warnings issued for Alaska, Hawaii, Chile, the Solomon Islands and areas south toward New Zealand, and waves of more than three meters considered possible along parts of the Ecuadorian coast.[1]

## Badlands coverage

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that as of late Tuesday night, Badlands contributors [[brad-zerbo|Brad Zerbo]] and Blue Eyes appeared to be in the clear in Alaska, while Hawaii and Japan remained under tsunami warnings.[1] She noted the news was chaotic and fast-moving and directed readers to [[badlands-daily|Badlands Daily]] for live updates.[1] The event was also discussed as one of several simultaneous major news stories — alongside the Manhattan shooting and the pending Durham annex release — that commentators suspected could serve as narrative distractions.[1]

## South American warnings

Tsunami warnings remained in place across parts of South America after the 8.8-magnitude earthquake struck off Russia's far eastern coast in the early hours.[2] Fears of a devastating tsunami in the United States and Japan faded over the course of Wednesday, July 30, 2025, after one of the strongest earthquakes ever recorded hit the sparsely populated Kamchatka peninsula.[2]

As authorities in Hawaii and parts of Japan, Russia and California downgraded their warnings, new evacuation warnings were issued along South America's Pacific coast.[2] Colombia, Chile and Ecuador's Galapagos Islands all saw restrictions and evacuations, including beach and school closures.[2] "Remember that the first wave is usually not the strongest," Chilean president Gabriel Boric warned on social media.[2] Several people were injured during the earthquake, but none seriously, and no major damage was reported.[2]
