# Flynn v. United States (malicious prosecution lawsuit)

The **Flynn malicious prosecution lawsuit** is a civil action filed on March 3, 2023 by retired Lt. Gen. [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]], former national security adviser to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], against the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the [[fbi|FBI]] and others, in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida.[1] Flynn demanded at least $50 million in compensation for what he alleged was a malicious investigation and prosecution.[1]

## Allegations

The complaint states that the defendant "maliciously investigated and prosecuted General Flynn by initiating and continuing a baseless counterintelligence investigation and by filing a criminal information lacking probable cause."[1] Flynn, previously head of the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] under the Obama administration, had been investigated by the FBI from August 2016 over supposed ties to Russia, and was charged in 2017 with lying to the FBI during an interview earlier that year.[1] The suit alleges that the FBI and, subsequently, prosecutors from the office of special counsel [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] pursued him for political reasons because they considered him a threat.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that with the truth about [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] exposed — that the use of FISA powers and official investigative bodies against Flynn had no basis in fact or process — damages could at last be pursued.[1] He assessed that $50 million would have little impact on the intelligence community Flynn had pledged to reform, but that a court ruling in Flynn's favour would effectively validate his claims against the intelligence apparatus and constitute a major victory in itself.[1]

Deschamps situated the case within the [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation war]] against the [[deep-state|Deep State]] and globalists, contending that a win would help convince the public of the depth of institutional corruption while not by itself defeating the Deep State, and that a loss would be another display of corruption fostering the disillusionment he considered necessary before large numbers of citizens would stop supporting and using the system.[1] He also expected that "the powers that be" had anticipated the case and were prepared to manage the resulting public relations.[1]
