# March 2024 $1.2 Trillion Funding Package

The March 2024 funding package was a $1.2 trillion, six-measure appropriations bundle unveiled by congressional leaders early on Thursday, March 21, 2024, intended to pass both chambers before a partial government shutdown that would begin just after midnight Saturday morning.[1]

## Contents

The package would boost military budgets through the end of September while holding funding for most non-defense agencies roughly even with existing levels.[1] Legislative text had been delayed by a fight over border-related funding.[1] Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] noted that the measure funded Border Control and national defense, blocked a cost-of-living increase for members of Congress, and provided money for federal education, health and labor programs as well as the IRS and the Departments of [[state-department|State]], Treasury and Homeland Security.[1]

## Politics

The bundle was the most substantial bipartisan legislation Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] had negotiated in his nearly five months leading the House, and was considerably more controversial than the package Congress cleared earlier that month.[1] Johnson called it a "serious commitment" to "strengthening our national defense" by moving the military toward its "core mission."[1] The bill would complete the funding work that vexed former Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] throughout his nine-month tenure.[1]

The compromise represented a conclusive defeat for House conservatives.[1] The Freedom Caucus had continuously pressured leadership to cut federal spending — even at the cost of a shutdown — and to pass the twelve regular appropriations bills individually.[1] After members of the right flank ousted McCarthy for defying those demands and averting a shutdown with Democratic help in September 2023, the group made similar demands of Johnson, who instead pursued bipartisan spending levels closely mirroring the deal McCarthy had reached with Biden the prior year.[1]

## Commentary

Fleuret described the outcome sardonically, writing that "as your borders remain open and the economy continues to careen off a cliff," the "clowns and cretins on the Hill" had assembled a $1.2 trillion package to keep the government running past midnight Saturday.[1]
