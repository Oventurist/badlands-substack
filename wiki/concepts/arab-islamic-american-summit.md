# Arab Islamic American Summit

The Arab Islamic American Summit was a gathering of fifty-five Muslim-majority countries hosted in [[riyadh|Riyadh]], Saudi Arabia, on May 21, 2017, and attended by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] on the first foreign trip of his presidency.[1] In Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s account, the summit was the opening move of a deliberate campaign to reorder the Middle East by first reordering [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[1]

## Origins

The summit concept is attributed to [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], whom Trump placed in charge of Middle East policy within days of the inauguration.[1] According to reporting in the [[new-yorker|New Yorker]] quoted at length in the article, Kushner and [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] framed the regional objective as annihilating the physical caliphate of [[isis|ISIS]] in Iraq and Syria and forcing the Gulf states to stop funding radical Islam.[1] A single gathering in Riyadh, Kushner argued, would let Trump introduce himself to the Muslim world, re-establish the American relationship with the Kingdom, put [[iran|Iran]] on notice, and signal Washington's backing for then-deputy crown prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1]

The proposal was reportedly opposed across the interagency — the [[state-department|State Department]], the Defense Department and the Treasury all objected, chiefly out of fear that endorsing M.B.S. would rupture the established American relationship with [[mohammed-bin-nayef|Mohammed bin Nayef]].[1] Kushner overrode the objections, telling a room of skeptical aides that the United States would not know whether the Saudis would deliver unless it tested them.[1]

## Proceedings

Trump's remarks, preserved in the White House archives, told the assembled leaders that "our friends will never question our support, and our enemies will never doubt our determination," and that partnerships would advance "security through stability, not through radical disruption."[1] He added that America was prepared to stand with the region "in pursuit of shared interests and common security," but that the nations of the Middle East could not wait for American power to crush terrorism for them.[1] Lange reads this as a line drawn in the sand: states were either against Islamic extremism or on the enemy's side, with no more playing both sides.[1]

The visit also featured the [[sword-dance|sword dance]] alongside [[salman-bin-abdulaziz|King Salman]] and the dedication of the [[global-center-for-combating-extremist-ideology|center to combat Islamic extremism]].[1]

## Aftermath

Lange, following the New Yorker, catalogues a rapid sequence of consequences: Trump's declaration that the U.S. embassy in Israel would move from Tel Aviv to Jerusalem; M.B.S. leapfrogging bin Nayef to become crown prince on June 21, 2017; and the Gulf monarchies' open confrontation with [[qatar|Qatar]], which began after remarks endorsing Iran and [[hamas|Hamas]] were attributed to Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad al-Thani]] two days after the summit.[1] On June 5, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain blockaded Qatar and broke off diplomatic relations — moves the New Yorker described as effectively acts of war — and Trump publicly tweeted his support, citing his recent trip.[1] The article treats the sequence as the Saudis passing Kushner's test, and as the prelude to the November 2017 [[saudi-purge|purge]] that removed [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1]
