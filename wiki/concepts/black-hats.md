# Black Hats

"Black Hats" is the counterpart term to [[white-hats|White Hats]] within the alternative-media and Q-adjacent commentary sphere, denoting the faction said to be working on behalf of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] against constitutional government in the United States.[1]

## Use in Badlands commentary

In a January 19, 2023 Badlands Media feature titled "How Do You Remove a POTUS?", contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used the Black Hat / White Hat vocabulary to map the factional disagreements inside what he called the "truther" community over events since 2021.[1] He described three broad camps. The first held that the White Hats were in complete control and that the expression "we are watching a movie" should be taken almost literally.[1] The second held that the Black Hats were in complete control and foresaw a bleak future, believing the White Hats had made a good attempt but were ultimately defeated in November 2020.[1] The third held an intermediate position: that the Black Hats were acting on their own accord while the White Hats used their evil against them.[1]

Carlson illustrated that intermediate position with a series of analogies — a judo master using an opponent's weight against him, a boxer setting up a knockout with soft left jabs, and a prosecutor maneuvering a defendant into self-incriminating statements.[1] He extended the boxing image to Muhammad Ali in his prime absorbing weak punches from an inferior, seemingly demented opponent, taking shots to the arms and occasionally the jaw for dramatic effect while wearing the challenger down and inducing him to drop his guard.[1]

## Related framing

The Black Hat concept in this usage is largely coextensive with the Deep State, the Democratic Party leadership and the [[rino|RINO]] wing of the [[republican-party|Republican Party]] as Badlands contributors describe them, rather than being a distinct organization.[1] Carlson's argument was that Black Hat actors initiating the removal of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] from office would, in the process, tether themselves to him — a Chinese finger trap in which the harder one pulls away, the tighter the trap becomes.[1]
