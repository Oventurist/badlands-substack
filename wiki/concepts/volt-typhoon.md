# Volt Typhoon

**Volt Typhoon** is the code name given to a China-backed hacking group that compromised hundreds of office and home-office routers in the United States in order to give the Chinese government access to data traversing them.[1] The operation was described by [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] as having been disrupted and shut down by the Bureau, an announcement he made on Wednesday, January 31, 2024, during testimony before the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party.[1]

## Targeting and methods

According to Wray's testimony, the compromised routers were outdated devices, a characteristic that made them "easy targets" for the intruders.[1] The group's operators used the router footholds to pursue access to critical American infrastructure sectors, including water treatment plants, the electrical power grid, oil and natural gas pipelines, and transportation systems.[1] Wray framed the campaign as part of a continuous effort, telling lawmakers that "today, and literally every day, they're actively attacking our economic security, engaging in wholesale theft of our innovation, and our personal and corporate data."[1]

The threat was first surfaced publicly in May 2023, when [[microsoft|Microsoft]] published research warning that the Volt Typhoon actors had been active since 2021 and were targeting United States cyber infrastructure using so-called living-off-the-land techniques.[1] Microsoft urged affected customers to change their security credentials.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the disclosure as an admission of extensive vulnerabilities in United States critical infrastructure, arguing that the digital revolution had itself increased exposure.[1] Citing the business insurance provider Embroker, she noted a reported 600 percent rise in cybercrime spanning theft, embezzlement, data hacking, and destruction.[1] Her central argument was that centralization breeds complexity, and that complexity had been sold to businesses as the cure for cybercrime: cloud providers such as Google, Amazon Web Services, and Microsoft marketed migration on the premise that they could afford the best available security talent.[1]

Ashe in America contended that the shared-responsibility model of modern cloud environments — in which the customer secures applications and data while the provider secures the platform — is misunderstood often enough to be a root cause or contributing factor in nearly every major reported breach, and that threats evolve faster than organizations can adapt.[1] She juxtaposed Wray's warnings against official assurances that cyberattacks cannot affect elections, noting sardonically that the same hardware and software components underpin election systems and that the guarantee traces to former [[cisa|CISA]] head [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]], alongside [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s promise to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that Beijing would not interfere in the 2024 election.[1] Her conclusion was a call to take Wray's threat warnings seriously and to decentralize, "especially elections."[1]
