# 2023 Tesla Autopilot Recall

The **2023 Tesla Autopilot recall** was a December 2023 regulatory action covering approximately two million [[tesla|Tesla]] vehicles in the United States, prompted by federal findings that the company's Autopilot driver-assistance system contained "insufficient" controls to ensure driver engagement.[1] The action was widely reported, including by the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] under the headline "Tesla recalls 2 million cars with 'insufficient' Autopilot safety controls."[1]

## Nature of the remedy

Despite the term "recall," the corrective action took the form of an over-the-air software update delivered to affected vehicles rather than a physical service visit. Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] compared the process to an overnight smartphone update and argued that the recall terminology created the false impression that two million owners would need to take time off work to bring vehicles to a dealership or repair shop.[1]

## The underlying safety dispute

Tesla's Autopilot already required drivers to keep their hands on the steering wheel and their eyes directed at the road, enforced through steering-wheel sensors and driver-facing cameras; when the system detected disengagement it would disable Autopilot and lock the feature out for a period.[1] Regulators concluded that drivers could circumvent these checks, and on that basis judged the system unsafe. Tesla disputed the finding on the merits but implemented an update making circumvention more difficult, a decision Carlson likened to settling out of court over an allegation one denies because litigating would cost more than complying.[1] He characterized the regulatory logic as blaming a weight-loss program for a participant who cheats on their diet.[1]

## Contested media narratives

Carlson argued the coverage was designed to depress Tesla's share price, deter prospective buyers, and damage [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] personally, contending that most readers absorb only headlines and that the residual negative impression persists even after the underlying facts are clarified.[1] He cited a viral video of a Tesla proceeding through a red light during a purported Autopilot demonstration, asserting that the drivers had switched Autopilot off shortly before reaching the intersection, and framed the clip as a deliberate attempt to malign Musk.[1]

## Wider context

The recall coverage appeared within days of the [[federal-communications-commission|FCC]]'s refusal to reinstate roughly $900 million in [[starlink]] rural broadband subsidies, and shortly after the release of an Obama-produced [[netflix]] film containing a prominent scene in which Tesla Autopilot systems malfunction and cause mass havoc.[1] Carlson argued that because films require long production lead times, the convergence indicated a campaign against Musk that had been in preparation for some time rather than a coincidence, and situated it within a broader [[deep-state|Deep State]] conflict also targeting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]
