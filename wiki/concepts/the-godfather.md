# The Godfather

*The Godfather* is the 1969 novel and 1972 film about an Italian-American crime family. In the Badlands Media essay [[a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths|A Journey of Ever-changing Truths]], it is analyzed not as entertainment but as an instrument of narrative management.

## The essay's account

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] states that he grew up believing the [[italian-mafia|Italian Mafia]] ran American organized crime because Hollywood taught him so, citing *The Godfather*, *The Godfather Part II*, *Goodfellas*, and *Casino*. On later examination he found that the novel "was published by a Jewish man," the film "was produced by a Jewish man," and "the movie's executive who signed off on the movie was also a Jewish man."

He further alleges that "Jewish gangster [[mickey-cohen|Mickey Cohen]]'s men even staged the shooting of the movie producer's car to make it seem that the Mafia didn't want the movie made," and argues the staging is exposed by its own outcome: had the Mafia truly objected, "the movie wouldn't have been made, or the movies that came later, and the movie's producer certainly wouldn't have survived the shotgun attack unscathed."

## Claimed function

The article's conclusion is that the film succeeded in its makers' actual goal: "it allowed the [[jewish-mafia|Jewish Mafia]] to disappear into the shadows," since afterwards "the Italian Mafia became synonymous with organized crime" and the Italian godfathers were taken to sit at the summit, rather than the Jewish Mafia above them or the [[cia|CIA]] above that.

Carlson emphasizes the timing as evidence of intent. The CIA, he writes, "couldn't allow the American public to know that their guy, [[meyer-lansky|Meyer Lansky]], was in charge of running their drugs out of the Golden Triangle during the Vietnam War," and the book appeared in 1969 with the film following in 1972, "in the heat of the Vietnam War." The essay asks pointedly whether this is "just a coincidence."
