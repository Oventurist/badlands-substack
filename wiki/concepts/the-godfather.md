# The Godfather

*The Godfather* — the 1969 novel and the 1972 film adapted from it — is treated by Erik Carlson as a deliberate narrative operation that fixed the [[italian-mafia|Italian Mafia]] in the public mind as the summit of American organized crime.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Claims made

Carlson states that the book was published by a Jewish man, the film produced by a Jewish man, and the studio executive who signed off on the film also a Jewish man.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) He adds that [[mickey-cohen|Mickey Cohen]]'s men staged the shooting of the producer's car to make it appear the Mafia opposed the project, and argues that genuine Mafia opposition would have stopped the film, its sequels, and the producer himself — who survived the shotgun attack unscathed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Claimed purpose

The film's success, in Carlson's reading, allowed the [[jewish-mafia|Jewish Mafia]] to disappear into the shadows: afterwards the Italian Mafia became synonymous with organized crime, obscuring both the Jewish Mafia above it and the [[cia|CIA]] above that.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) He emphasizes the timing — publication in 1969, release in 1972, in the heat of the Vietnam War, while [[meyer-lansky|Meyer Lansky]] allegedly ran CIA drugs out of the Golden Triangle — and asks whether this is "just a coincidence."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

Carlson also credits the broader film canon, including *Goodfellas* and *Casino*, with cementing his own youthful belief that the Italian Mafia ran organized crime in America.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)
