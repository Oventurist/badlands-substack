# The Godfather

The Godfather is the novel published in 1969 and the film released in 1972 that, in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s April 2025 essay, cemented the [[italian-mafia|Italian Mafia]] as the public face of American organized crime.[1]

## Alleged production background

Carlson writes that as he peeled back the layers he discovered the book was published by a Jewish man, the movie was produced by a Jewish man, and the studio executive who signed off on the movie was also a Jewish man.[1] He states that [[mickey-cohen|Mickey Cohen]]'s men staged the shooting of the producer's car to make it appear the Mafia did not want the film made, and argues that a genuinely opposed Mafia would have prevented the film and its sequels and that the producer would not have survived a shotgun attack unscathed.[1]

## Alleged purpose

Carlson's conclusion is that the film succeeded in its makers' goal: it allowed the [[jewish-mafia|Jewish Mafia]] to disappear into the shadows while Italian godfathers were installed in the public imagination as the summit of organized crime.[1] He emphasizes the timing — publication in 1969 and release in 1972, in the heat of the Vietnam War — as consistent with a need to shield [[meyer-lansky|Meyer Lansky]], whom he describes as running [[cia|CIA]] drugs out of the Golden Triangle, asking whether this was "just a coincidence."[1]
