# UN Declaration of Human Rights

The **UN Declaration of Human Rights**, dated 1947 in Badlands Media commentary, is cited by contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as one of the documents in which the universal freedoms of the post-war settlement were set down.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Content as characterised

Ehret identifies those freedoms as freedom from want, freedom from fear of war, freedom of speech and freedom of conscience — the [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]] articulated by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] in 1941 — and argues that they form the backbone of the spirit of the [[atlantic-charter|Atlantic Charter]], the [[united-nations|UN Charter]] and the original designs of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) He writes that these freedoms were outlined again in the Declaration, and once more in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence issued by the 1955 [[bandung-conference|Bandung Conference]] that set the tone for the [[non-aligned-movement|Non-Aligned Movement]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Relevance to the multipolar debate

Within the essay's argument, the Declaration belongs to a body of universally agreed principles that could serve as the basis for an emergency summit convened before nuclear war, in place of what Ehret calls an anti-humanist [[great-reset|Great Reset]] or transhumanist New World Order.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)
