# Progressive Jewish Thought and the New Anti-Semitism

*Progressive Jewish Thought and the New Anti-Semitism* is a paper published in early 2007 by the [[american-jewish-committee|American Jewish Committee]] and written by Indiana University English professor [[alvin-h-rosenfeld|Alvin H. Rosenfeld]].[1]

## Content and reception

The paper characterized a group of progressive American Jews who had voiced dissent toward Israel — among them historian Tony Judt, playwright Tony Kushner, and Washington Post columnist Richard Cohen — as standing "alongside" a new antisemitism that denies the legitimacy of Israel's existence.[1] David Harris, then executive director of the committee, wrote in the paper's introduction: "The most surprising—and distressing—feature of this new trend is the very public participation of some Jews in the verbal onslaught against Zionism and the Jewish state."[1]

Those named in the paper vehemently refuted its allegations, and Rabbi [[michael-lerner|Michael Lerner]] of Tikkun highlighted the consequences of levelling unfounded accusations, arguing that legislators privately fear being branded antisemitic if they criticize Israeli policy.[1]

## Significance in the Badlands account

The Badlands Media essay "Our 'Greatest Ally'" treats the paper as the high-water mark of a tendency to classify criticism of Israel — including criticism from Jews — as bigotry, part of a wider pattern the author says has drained the term [[antisemitism|anti-Semitism]] of meaning.[1] The essay places the paper alongside the treatment of [[norman-finkelstein|Norman Finkelstein]] as evidence of that pattern.[1]
