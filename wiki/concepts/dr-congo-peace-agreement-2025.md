# DR Congo Peace Agreement (2025)

The DR Congo peace agreement is a framework accord signed on November 15, 2025 in Doha between the government of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] and the [[congo-river-alliance|Congo River Alliance]] (AFC), a coalition of armed groups including the [[m23|M23]] rebel movement, aimed at ending a long-running armed conflict in the country's east that has killed thousands.[1] It followed months of mediation by [[qatar|Qatar]], the United States and the [[african-union|African Union]].[1]

## Terms

The document lays out eight protocols to be negotiated: an immediate cessation of hostilities, disengagement of forces, "unimpeded, safe, and sustained humanitarian access," and conditions for the return of displaced civilians, among others.[1] It also calls for the "full restoration of state authority over the entire national territory," including areas currently held by the rebels.[1]

## Russian endorsement

[[russia|Russia]] welcomed the agreement as another step toward "unblocking the conflict" and consistent with commitments previously made by the warring parties, in a statement by Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]].[1] Moscow said it remains "convinced that the only viable way to ensure security and lasting peace" in the eastern region is to "continue dialogue with the participation of international and regional mediators, with the prospect of achieving long-term national reconciliation."[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the accord as the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] trying to defuse an armed conflict over the eastern Congo's mineral belt, estimated at over $24 trillion in value — money that invites conflict whenever mineral rights change hands.[1] He traced the phased struggle to [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], which targeted [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]], and noted the eastern Congo holds the world's highest concentration of rare earth minerals, from which more than half the materials used in high-tech manufacturing are sourced.[1] He also flagged that a plane carrying a delegation from the Congolese ministry of mining exploded into flames on the runway that week while en route to investigate a mining bridge collapse that killed dozens; all members survived, but he found foul play hard to rule out.[1] Stabilizing Africa, he concluded, may prove even more difficult than stabilizing the Middle East.[1]
