# Convention of States

The Convention of States (COS) is a grassroots campaign to use Article V of the United States Constitution to convene a limited convention of the states, at which delegates would propose constitutional amendments aimed at federal fiscal restraint, reduced federal authority, and term limits for members of Congress and other federal officials.[1] The effort is driven by the organization Citizens for Self-Governance and operates politically through Convention of States Action.[1]

## Mechanics

Under the Article V pathway pursued by the movement, 34 states — two-thirds of the states — must pass matching resolutions before a convention can be called.[1] Any amendment proposed at such a convention would then require ratification by 38 states, three-fourths of the total, before becoming law.[1] Supporters treat the 38-state ratification threshold as the structural stop-gap against what critics warn could become a "runaway convention" that ranges beyond its stated subject matter.[1]

As of September 2025, nineteen states had passed the Convention of States Article V application in full, six more had passed it in one legislative chamber but not the other, and twenty-one additional states were actively considering the resolution.[1]

## 2025 national conference and Trump endorsement

Convention of States Action held its annual national conference in Indianapolis in September 2025, at a moment when calls to rein in federal spending, impose term limits, and curb the power of the federal government had not subsided despite President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s mandate to pursue those goals.[1] Trump shared a Just the News article about the conference on Truth Social on Monday, September 22, 2025, energizing the movement's advocates.[1]

In a responding statement, Convention of States Action said that "the fact that a sitting president is talking about Convention of States is a testament to the hard work and dedication of the millions of petition signers in every state who have backed this effort," and noted that administration figures including Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], Secretary of War [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], and Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] had expressed support in the past.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America, writing from Colorado, observed that her state has moved in the opposite direction: the only Convention of States–related measure to pass there in recent years was a 2021 measure rescinding all prior calls for a convention, and annual attempts by advocates are tabled and die.[1] She described herself as not entirely opposed to an Article V convention in principle, but raised the delegate-selection problem as decisive — who would be sent, whether delegates would be chosen by legislatures she regards as illegitimate or elected by the same compromised voting processes.[1] Her conclusion was that "Article V opens triple-locked doors for change" and that usurpers should never be allowed to open those doors.[1]
