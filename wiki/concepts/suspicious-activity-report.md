# Suspicious Activity Report

A suspicious activity report (SAR) is a filing generated by a United States financial institution and transmitted to the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] when a transaction is flagged for further review. SARs "often contain evidence of potential criminal activities, such as money laundering and fraud," according to a 2020 Senate report cited in coverage of the Biden family investigation.[1]

## Role in the Biden family investigation

In March 2023 the Treasury Department agreed to release SARs generated by the business transactions of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s family and their associates to the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]].[1] United States banks had flagged over 150 SARs involving [[hunter-biden|Hunter]] and [[james-biden|James Biden]], each covering "large" amounts of money tagged for further review.[1] The committee was pursuing nine alleged violations, including wire fraud and money laundering, and treated the SARs as a route to understanding how the family business operated and whether the president was compromised by foreign governments.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cautioned that documents provided by a Treasury Department under the Biden administration might be "missing, altered or incomplete," arguing that trust in federal agencies had eroded to the point where even complete disclosure would be unlikely to produce more than a token indictment from the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[1]
