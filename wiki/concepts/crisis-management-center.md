# Crisis Management Center

The Crisis Management Center was a $14 million technological outpost built in the Old Executive Office Building — the Second Empire-style building next to the White House where the [[national-security-council|NSC]] staff kept their offices — under the direction of [[john-poindexter|John Poindexter]] in the early 1980s.[1]

## Construction and capabilities

Since arriving at the White House in 1981 as a military assistant, Poindexter, an engineer by training, had been trying to fix the shambles that was the early-warning and crisis-management system of the national security apparatus, and was given the task of upgrading the Situation Room, which despite popular notions was a technological backwater lacking many basic necessities for keeping the president in touch with the world.[1] Officials saw threats across the horizon for which the president had little advance warning — from the Soviets to socialist forces in Latin America and, by 1983, suicidal terrorists.[1]

Poindexter made great strides quickly. The Situation Room was outfitted with modern communications equipment, and the new Crisis Management Center added video conferencing systems, large wall screens, and links to the systems carrying diplomatic, military and intelligence cable traffic. He even introduced the first early versions of "e-mail" to the White House. The combined nerve center of Situation Room and Crisis Management Center represented a generational leap for the White House.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that this new system, centered on the NSC where Poindexter and [[oliver-north|Oliver North]] played key roles, was designed to control the dissemination of information to the president on the Soviets, Latin American socialism and the new threat of terrorism — an extension of [[cia|CIA]] influence beyond the [[presidents-daily-brief|Presidential Daily Brief]] to the entire executive branch after the [[1983-beirut-barracks-bombings|Beirut barracks bombing]], and an early step toward [[total-information-awareness|Total Information Awareness]].[1]
