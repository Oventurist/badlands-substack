# Military-Industrial-Congressional Complex

The Military-Industrial-Congressional Complex (MICC) is the term Badlands Media uses for the interlocking network of defense contractors, military leadership and congressional patrons that it holds responsible for the direction of American foreign policy.[1] The formulation extends Dwight Eisenhower's better-known "military-industrial complex" by explicitly naming Congress as the third leg of the arrangement.[1]

In the "Deep State Spotlight" profile of [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]], contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that a career like hers — Ivy League education at [[yale-university|Yale]], affiliation with the [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]], and the post of U.S. permanent representative to [[nato|NATO]] — is not walked without being co-opted and groomed by the powers behind the MICC.[1] He identifies Vice President [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], for whom Nuland served as top foreign policy adviser, as another figure with intimately close ties to the complex.[1]

## NATO as the MICC's instrument

The profile treats the post-Cold War repurposing of NATO as the MICC's central project.[1] With the Soviet Union gone, European observers questioned whether the alliance retained any purpose; DeLarme cites a 2005 Radio Free Europe piece as a signal that NATO would instead become the complex's key to keeping the war machine running into the future, quoting Nuland's call for the alliance to be "retooled for the 21st century" and turned into the world's "multilateral security trainer of first resort."[1] Because [[united-nations|UN]] Security Council approval for the invasion of Iraq had been blocked — with China, Russia, France and Germany opposed — Nuland's declaration that NATO "must be the place where we talk about all the issues affecting our future" is read as a plan to bypass the multilateral checks that had constrained the [[us-security-state|U.S. security state]] for decades.[1] The same route was used in the Obama years to prosecute the NATO regime-change war against [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] in Libya, which DeLarme argues could never have won UN authorization.[1]

The article situates the MICC alongside the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]] and the wider [[neoconservatives|neoconservative]] movement as the machinery through which the [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]] and the subsequent [[nato|NATO]] expansion toward Russia were driven, and through which Washington's bipartisan consensus on the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] is sustained.[1]
