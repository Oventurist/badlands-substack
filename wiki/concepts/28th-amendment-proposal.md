# Proposed 28th Amendment on Gun Regulation

The proposed 28th Amendment is a constitutional amendment advanced in June 2023 by California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] that would impose stricter federal regulations on gun ownership while, in its sponsor's framing, leaving [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] rights intact.[1]

## Proposal

Newsom presented the amendment as aimed at curbing gun violence and protecting public safety, calling for "common sense constitutional protections and gun safety measures" that he said enjoyed broad support across party lines.[1] Second Amendment supporters immediately vowed to fight the effort.[1]

## Badlands commentary

In the Badlands News Brief, [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] argued that the proposal was a positioning move for a Newsom presidential run in 2028 and a precursor to what he called an executive-ordered gun confiscation of "epic and unconstitutional proportions."[1] He contended that an amendment written to suppress the Second Amendment went beyond contradiction and amounted to the elimination of guaranteed rights.[1]

Johnny Q noted that California is already among the most gun-restrictive states yet suffers persistent gun violence, attributing the bulk of it to armed robberies, gangs, and the illegal gun trade rather than licensed gun shops.[1] Where prohibited persons obtain firearms, he argued, the failures lie in background checks, federal ineptitude, and poor communication between state and federal systems — with mental health problems repeatedly going unnoticed at the point of purchase, and no accountability for state justice departments that keep inaccurate records while red-flagging law-abiding citizens.[1]

He predicted the amendment had no chance of passing and would be struck down in the courts, concluding that its real purpose was to pander to the far left and pacify moderate Democrats, many of whom own firearms and oppose further restrictions.[1] The commentary also disputed Newsom's claims by asserting that gun violence "infects nearly all blue cities."[1]
