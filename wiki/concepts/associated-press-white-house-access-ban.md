# Associated Press White House Access Ban

The Associated Press White House access ban was the February 2025 exclusion of [[associated-press|Associated Press]] reporters from White House events and from travel aboard Air Force One, imposed after the wire service declined to adopt the term "Gulf of America" in its style guide, and the litigation and press-pool restructuring that followed.[1]

## The ban and the AP lawsuit

The AP, a traditional fixture in the Oval Office and on Air Force One, sued White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] and other senior officials after its reporters were barred from those spaces over the outlet's decision to continue using "Gulf of Mexico" instead of "Gulf of America."[1] The [[white-house-correspondents-association|White House Correspondents' Association]] led a friend-of-the-court brief supporting the AP.[1]

## Judge McFadden's ruling

On Monday, February 24, 2025, U.S. District Judge [[trevor-mcfadden|Trevor McFadden]] in Washington denied the AP's request for immediate relief, allowing the ban to remain in place while the legal fight proceeds.[1] McFadden, a Trump nominee, said the AP's case differed from past disputes over broad White House access because it concerned specific areas rather than general admission.[1] He placed the litigation on an expedited track to consider the merits and said he would keep an "open mind," but concluded that at this early stage the outlet had not met its burden for emergency intervention.[1]

The [[white-house|White House]] responded that "asking the President of the United States questions in the Oval Office and aboard Air Force One is a privilege granted to journalists, not a legal right."[1]

## Takeover of the press pool

On Tuesday, February 25, 2025, Leavitt announced that for the first time in a century the White House — not the independent White House Correspondents' Association — would determine which news outlets form the press pool covering the president at space-limited meetings and events.[1] "Moving forward, the White House Press Pool will be determined by the White House Press Team," Leavitt said at a briefing.[1] Axios framed the move as an attempt to short-circuit the AP's lawsuit by altering decades-old protocols.[1] WHCA president and Politico reporter Eugene Daniels said in a statement that the change "suggests the government will choose the journalists who cover the president."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Ashe in America called the timing of McFadden's refusal "delightful" but disputed the Axios framing, arguing that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had long planned to dismantle what she called the gatekeeping of Washington media and had promised to do so.[1] She noted that the same figures now invoking press independence had never told the public that Biden was hand-selecting journalists and screening their questions, and characterized restricted-space coverage of the White House as a privilege that the press corps had taken for granted.[1] "The past five weeks were an audition… we're going in another direction," she wrote.[1]
