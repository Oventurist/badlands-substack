# SPLC Federal Fraud Prosecution

The SPLC federal fraud prosecution is a United States [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] criminal case against the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]] alleging that the nonprofit defrauded its donors in connection with a paid confidential informant program.[1]

## Charges and arraignment

The SPLC entered a not guilty plea in July 2026 during a remote arraignment in Montgomery, Alabama, following a superseding indictment that added detail but no new charges to the original case.[1] The Justice Department alleges the nonprofit misled donors by failing to disclose that millions of dollars in donations were used to pay confidential informants embedded in extremist organizations between 2014 and 2023, including reimbursements for activities tied to those groups.[1]

Federal prosecutors contend the conduct constituted wire fraud, false statements to federally insured banks, and conspiracy to commit money laundering.[1] The case is scheduled for trial in October.[1]

## Defense

The SPLC denies wrongdoing, arguing the paid informant program generated intelligence that was regularly shared with law enforcement and helped prevent violence, and has characterized the prosecution as politically motivated.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she hoped the case would go to trial so the extent of SPLC programs to "fabricate racism" by embedding activists inside extremist organizations could be examined.[1] She raised the question of whether the SPLC was functioning as an extrajudicial entrapment operation, drawing an analogy to the Whitmer kidnapping plot in which a majority of participants were federal assets, and speculated that many of the alleged klansmen involved may have been SPLC-linked and among the most vocal and action-oriented racists.[1] She noted that, unlike government entrappers, the SPLC operates with significantly less oversight, and that using NGOs creates plausible deniability for the government.[1] She said she and [[canncon|CannCon]] would stream the trial if it occurred, and expressed hope the organization would not accept a plea deal.[1]

## Announcement of the indictment (April 2026)

The Justice Department announced the 11-count federal indictment on April 21, 2026, accusing the Alabama-based nonprofit of wire fraud, bank fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering.[2] The case was announced by Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] alongside FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]].[2] Prosecutors allege the organization secretly routed more than $3 million between 2014 and 2023 to paid informants inside extremist groups including the Ku Klux Klan and neo-Nazi organizations while misleading donors about how contributions were used.[2] SPLC interim president and CEO [[bryan-fair|Bryan Fair]] denied wrongdoing, calling the allegations false and politically motivated, saying informants were used to gather intelligence on violent threats and to protect staff, and stating the practice had ended.[2] In an analysis published by Just Security, a former DOJ Fraud Section chief questioned whether the allegations satisfy customary federal fraud standards.[2]
