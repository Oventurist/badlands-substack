# 2024 Federal Death Row Commutations

The 2024 federal death row commutations were a mass clemency action taken by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on Monday, December 23, 2024, in which he commuted the sentences of 37 of the 40 people then held on federal death row, converting their punishments from execution to life imprisonment without the possibility of parole.[1] The action came weeks before President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was to take office on January 20, 2025, and left only three "hard cases" awaiting execution.[1]

## The announcement

In announcing the commutations, Biden said he was "more convinced than ever that we must stop the use of the death penalty at the federal level."[1] He framed the decision as consistent with the moratorium his administration had imposed on federal executions "in cases other than terrorism and hate-motivated mass murder."[1] Biden added: "Make no mistake: I condemn these murderers, grieve for the victims of their despicable acts, and ache for all the families who have suffered unimaginable and irreparable loss."[1]

Among those whose sentences were commuted were Billie Jerome Allen, sentenced to death in 1998; Carlos David Caro, on death row for more than 15 years; and Len Davis, on death row for more than 25 years.[1] The three inmates excluded from the action were two mass shooters and the perpetrator of the Boston Marathon bombing, according to reporting by The New York Times.[1]

## Cases spared

Coverage by [[breitbart|Breitbart]] emphasized the severity of the crimes committed by some of those spared.[1] Ricardo "Ricky" Sanchez Jr. and Daniel "Homer" Troya were convicted in 2009 of carjacking resulting in death and other felonies for the October 13, 2006 killings of an entire family — Jose "Lou" Escobedo, his wife Yessica Guerro Escobedo, and their sons aged 4 and 3 — whose bullet-riddled bodies were found beside Florida's Turnpike near Fort Pierce and Port St. Lucie.[1] Federal prosecutors said the pair's drug-trafficking boss, Danny Varela, ordered the killings to erase a large drug debt and to seize 15 kilograms of cocaine that Escobedo, the group's cocaine smuggler, was carrying; the killers drove off in both the family's Jeep Cherokee and their own Dodge Ram van.[1] Another commuted prisoner, Thomas Sanders, had been convicted in 2014 of kidnapping and murdering a 12-year-old girl.[1]

## Advocacy campaign

The commutations followed a weeks-long pressure campaign by [[amnesty-international|Amnesty International]] and other non-governmental organizations urging Biden to clear federal and military death rows.[1] Part of the advocacy argument was that the death penalty is applied in a racially discriminatory manner: of 460 federal defendants who faced capital charges, 119 were white and 341 came from minority groups, including 237 Black defendants.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands Media, Ashe in America characterized the move as consistent with what she described as the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]]'s pattern of "efforts to decriminalize crime," noting that Biden had taken execution off the table for nearly everyone on federal death row.[1] She stressed that the inmates were not being released, their sentences having been converted to life without parole.[1] She also voiced skepticism that "illegitimate presidents have pardon powers," suggesting that under a genuine truth-and-reconciliation process the only Biden clemency grants that would survive scrutiny would be his ceremonial Thanksgiving turkey pardons.[1]
