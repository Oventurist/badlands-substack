# MATURE Act

The Making Age-Verification Technology Uniform, Robust, and Effective (MATURE) Act is legislation introduced in February 2023 by Senator [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] that would bar children under the age of 16 from creating social media accounts.[1]

## Provisions

The bill would require platforms to verify that a user is over 16 before allowing account creation.[1] To satisfy that requirement, users would have to provide their full legal name, date of birth, and government-issued identification.[1]

## Rationale

In a statement accompanying the bill, Hawley argued that "children suffer every day from the effects of social media," that at best Big Tech companies are "neglecting our children's health and monetizing their personal information," and at worst "they are complicit in their exploitation and manipulation," concluding that it was "time to give parents the weapons they need to strike back."[1] Coverage of the introduction was carried by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] accepted that social media is "toxic" while noting that it retains genuine uses in the modern world.[1] He argued that the appeal of social media is a symptom of atomization: in the social conditions of the late 19th century, integrated local communities handled crime through community defense rather than police stations outside urban areas, and education through homeschooling and community-based instruction rather than large-scale public systems.[1] With neighbors now largely unknown to one another, the bulk of social interaction has migrated online, where quality of connection is lacking despite the ease of global contact.[1]

Deschamps added that for young people the toll of social media is all they know, that rising rates of mental illness among the young are partly attributable to the technology, and that an honest appraisal of its effects — when finally permitted — will likely be shocking.[1] The proposal's reliance on government-ID verification also sits within the broader debate over digital identity requirements that Badlands has covered.[1]
