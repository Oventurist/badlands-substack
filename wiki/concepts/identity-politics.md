# Identity Politics

Identity politics, in Badlands Media's usage, denotes a mode of political practice in which appeals to the interests and symbolic recognition of marginalized identity groups substitute for material governance, allowing officeholders to energize voters emotionally while leaving underlying corruption and policy failure untouched.[1]

## Newsom as exemplar

The December 2023 [[deep-state-spotlight|Deep State Spotlight]] profile of California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated him as the form's most accomplished practitioner among white male politicians.[1] Newsom's method, according to the piece, is to persistently raise existential concerns about basic liberties and rights — homosexual rights, abortion access, contraception, voting and civil rights — that are rarely genuinely at issue in contemporary California, persuading voters to look past corruption and failure.[1]

Newsom's national reputation was established in 2004 by his issuance of marriage licenses to same-sex couples at San Francisco City Hall, months after being elected mayor in part to address the city's homeless crisis; the licenses were overturned by local courts but won lasting adoration from social liberals, while street homelessness persisted without substantial resolution.[1]

## Hollow institutional gestures

DeLarme cited two later instances as characteristic. The reparations commission Newsom presided over carried an expansive and unprecedented mandate to examine the effects of slavery and racial discrimination on African American Californians, but lacked any authority to implement policy — rendering it, in DeLarme's assessment, hollow and ineffective while still furnishing a campaign credential.[1] Likewise, the appointment of [[laphonza-butler|Laphonza Butler]] to [[dianne-feinstein|Dianne Feinstein]]'s Senate seat was announced with emphasis on her being "the first openly Black lesbian to serve in Congress," omitting her prior role as a consultant to Newsom's operatives and lobbyist for Uber and AirBnB.[1]

The article situated identity politics alongside the [[culture-war|culture war]] as twin instruments by which politicians in both parties channel donor-class resources while inflaming voters with emotionally charged rhetoric.[1]
