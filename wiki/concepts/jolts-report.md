# JOLTS Report

The Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS) is a monthly labor-market data release produced by the [[bureau-of-labor-statistics|Bureau of Labor Statistics]] within the Department of Labor, tracking the number of open positions in the U.S. economy.[1]

## August 2023 collapse and revisions

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] had for months argued that the seasonally adjusted JOLTS figures were unreliable — "manipulated, seasonally-adjusted, and politically goalseeked" — publishing analyses including UBS research finding job openings far lower than the department reported, and noting that the Labor Department was estimating a large share of the number outright.[1]

In the July 2023 report released Aug. 29, against a consensus expecting a modest decline from 9.582 million to 9.5 million, the BLS reported just 8.827 million job openings — the first sub-9 million print since March 2021 and the third-largest miss on record.[1] The agency also cut the May figure from 9.582 million to 9.165 million; absent that downward revision the month-over-month drop would have approached 800,000 openings.[1] ZeroHedge characterized this as part of a pattern in which data points under the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration were revised sharply lower in subsequent months, describing it as a coordinated effort to make the economy look stronger and then quietly walk the numbers back.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that while the data was driving commodity and asset markets higher, the deeper story was that the administration was increasingly perceived as "a mass manipulator" of the narratives surrounding economic statistics — and that the manipulation itself "becoming THE story" was a serious problem for the regime.[1]
