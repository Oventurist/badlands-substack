# Congress of Vienna

The Congress of Vienna of 1815 was the conference that reorganised Europe after two decades of Napoleonic warfare. The Badlands Media article [[a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation|A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation]] describes it as "a weak attempt at another conference" following the [[peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]], arising only after the Napoleonic wars had "decimated Europe."

## Function in the article's argument

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] links the Congress to a companion essay on Henry Kissinger's admiration for the 1815 settlement, implying a critical view of the balance-of-power diplomacy it institutionalised. In the article's schema, Vienna belongs to the series of post-catastrophe settlements — Westphalia, Vienna, the [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]], the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods]] — that were negotiated only once war had become unsufferable. The author's point is that some of these conferences were shaped by honest agendas and some were not, but that all reflected an agreement that a new system was needed to avoid another plunge into war.
