# REAL ID

REAL ID is the federal identification standard for state-issued driver's licenses and identity cards, whose nationwide enforcement deadline took effect in 2025.[1]

## Relevance to the SAVE America Act debate

In March 2026 Badlands commentary, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] invoked REAL ID as a rebuttal to opposition arguments against the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]].[1] She observed that the arguments raised against requiring documentary proof of citizenship for voter registration — chiefly that millions of Americans lack ready access to birth certificates or passports — were the same arguments made about REAL ID the previous year, and asked why citizens who had already met the REAL ID deadline would face new barriers under the SAVE America Act.[1]

## Origins and 2025 enforcement deadline

The term REAL ID refers to the REAL ID Act of 2005, which established for a post-9/11 world more stringent requirements for driver's licenses and identification cards issued by U.S. states and territories.[2] The rationale was that states operated different security systems of varying efficacy, and the federal government sought standardization so it could be confident that bearers were who they claimed to be.[2] Implementation was much delayed by a range of factors, from states opposing the very concept, to pandemic-era delays, to ordinary governmental slowness.[2]

As the deadline for REAL ID enforcement at the nation's airports approached in March 2025, long lines formed outside Illinois Secretary of State facilities in Chicago's Loop.[2] Secretary of State [[alexi-giannoulias|Alexi Giannoulias]] opened a downtown REAL ID Supercenter at 191 N. Clark Street, requiring no appointment and no additional taxpayer funding, a step editorial commentary praised.[2] Supporters of the law argued that securing airports and other federal facilities requires more dependable identification given how easily driver's licenses can be faked.[2]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] asked whether President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] could abolish REAL ID by executive order, contrasting the flight requirement with the absence of an identification requirement in many American elections.[2]

## The May 7, 2025 deadline

Ahead of the original May 7, 2025 deadline, state and federal officials urged Americans to understand how REAL IDs differ from standard identification.[3] Under the law passed by [[congress|Congress]] in 2005, anyone travelling on domestic flights on or after that date would need a REAL ID to clear routine security screening and board an airline; the credential also governs access to some federal buildings and nuclear facilities.[3] Standard driver's licenses would no longer suffice for travelers aged 18 and older, though residents of Michigan, Minnesota, New York, Vermont and Washington holding Enhanced Driver's Licenses (EDLs) were exempt, EDLs being identifiable by an image of the U.S. flag and the word "Enhanced" at the top of the card.[3]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] mocked Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] — whom he called an "Instagram influencer" — for promoting the deadline while suggesting the credential would also fix American elections, responding that voter ID is "a panacea and a talking point that does not fix fake elections."[3]
