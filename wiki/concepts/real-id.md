# REAL ID

REAL ID refers to the federal identification standards established by the REAL ID Act of 2005, which set minimum security requirements for state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards and, after successive deadline extensions, took full effect for air travel and federal facility access under federal enforcement deadlines in 2025.

In the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]] debate of March 2026, REAL ID became a rhetorical hinge for Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]. Opponents of the bill, citing the [[brennan-center-for-justice|Brennan Center for Justice]], argued that more than 21 million Americans lack ready access to documents such as birth certificates and passports and would face significant hurdles complying with a documentary proof-of-citizenship requirement. Ashe in America responded: "Wait, don't all American citizens have REAL ID since that deadline last year? Why would they face new barriers with the SAVE America Act?" — arguing that the previous year's REAL ID enforcement deadline had already established a baseline of federally compliant identification for the adult population, and that opposition arguments about documentation barriers were recycled from the earlier REAL ID fight: "the arguments against the SAVE America Act are the same arguments that were forwarded about REAL ID last year."
