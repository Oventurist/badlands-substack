# Badlands Brief: Moves and Countermoves

"Moves and Countermoves" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily news-and-commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack. Dated March 16, 2026 and recorded with 153 likes, the issue pairs summarized wire reporting with signed commentary from the Badlands contributor collective — [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].

The edition covers four principal segments: the energy dimension of the war with [[iran|Iran]] and its effect on [[nato|NATO]]; an escalating fight over the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] involving President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s treason threats against the press and a reported criminal referral targeting [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]; the first federal conviction of an [[antifa|Antifa]] cell on terrorism-related charges in Texas; and Cuba's confirmation of negotiations with the Trump administration. A bonus item promotes [[badlands-blitz-episode-10|Badlands Blitz Episode 10]].

## Iran and the global energy war

The Brief's summary reports that the United States carried out strikes on Iranian military targets on [[kharg-island|Kharg Island]], the export hub handling roughly 90 percent of Iran's oil shipments. Trump said the attacks destroyed military facilities but avoided oil infrastructure, warning that broader strikes could follow if Iran interfered with shipping in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. Iran threatened retaliation against US-linked energy infrastructure in Gulf countries, and maritime traffic through the strait — a route carrying about one-fifth of world oil supply — was disrupted.

In an interview with the Financial Times, Trump warned that NATO faces a "very bad" future if allies do not help reopen the strait, calling on Gulf-oil-dependent states including [[china|China]], the United Kingdom, France, Japan and South Korea to contribute minesweepers and air defenses. [[national-economic-council|National Economic Council]] Director [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]] said the war had cost about $12 billion so far and that the administration had the funding it needed but might approach [[congress|Congress]] for more. Hassett argued that unlike in the 1970s the United States now produces abundant oil, that its trading partners are "more on the hook from imported oil," and that a government which "would murder 40,000 of their people just a few months ago" blackmailing states over transit is unacceptable: "we need to fix it and we are going to fix it fast."

Burning Bright reads the episode as "multipolar realignment and narrative disarmament operating in perfect harmony," arguing that a macro-narrative centered on energy is rising to subsume the war story emanating from Tehran. He contends that the weaponization of energy is real but selective in impact: Europe remains dangerously dependent on imported energy after pipeline disruptions and what he calls an ideological war against its own nuclear capacity, while the emerging [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar]] bloc adapts — [[russia|Russia]] directing oil to its largest customer through negotiated temporary arrangements, [[india|India]] responding to pressure with "characteristically sovereign bluntness," and Chinese commercial vessels moving through contested waters with minimal disruption. He attributes this to deliberate coordination with Washington's participation, noting that much of the [[truth-community|truth community]] misreads reports of Trump "making peace" as spontaneous rather than planned. He refers readers to his long-form essay "Trump's Test."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the same events as a narrative pincer against NATO. With the Iran war threatening maritime LNG shipments and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] suggesting Russia could stop supplying gas to European markets, the United States becomes Europe's only reliable supplier while consumer costs spike. Europe is thus forced to choose between sending ships to the Strait of Hormuz — risking entanglement with Iran and the loss of Persian Gulf LNG — or refusing Trump. He concludes that Europe's only remaining option is negotiation, with Putin, Trump or Iran, all three of whom would demand an end to support for Ukraine and a permanent peace with Russia, and that the three energy-independent powers are untouched: "That is what we call 'Checkmate.'"

## The war for the First Amendment

The Brief reports, citing [[rt|RT]] of March 16, 2026, that Trump threatened media organizations with treason charges over their coverage of the Iran war. In a [[truth-social|Truth Social]] post he accused "fake news" outlets of spreading false information fabricated by Tehran using artificial intelligence, alleging that Iran had circulated AI-generated images and footage of attacks on US military assets including refueling aircraft and the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln, and writing that outlets distributing "knowingly FAKE" information should face treason charges. RT dated the regional conflict to late February, when US and [[israel|Israeli]] forces launched coordinated strikes on Iran. Separately, former CIA officer [[john-kiriakou|John Kiriakou]] said on an RT program aired March 15 that he was "genuinely worried" about Carlson's safety following Carlson's claim of a possible [[cia|CIA]] criminal referral under the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]].

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry presents the treason headline as "only one stanza in an Epic Poem of a much larger war," describing three years of Badlands coverage of what he calls a Zionist effort to dismantle the First Amendment using "antisemitism" as a Trojan Horse. He traces it to legislation and executive orders in 38 states adopting the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]] definition of antisemitism, and to Florida's HB 269, sponsored by then-state senator [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]], which created offenses for harassment, threats or intimidation targeting religious or ethnic groups. Presented as a measure against graffiti and harassment, the bill in his account included a provision criminalizing flyers and pamphlets critical of Israel, since the IHRA definition equates anti-Zionism with antisemitism, and imposed harsh prison sentences. He notes that Fine and Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] made a "secret trip" to Jerusalem, where DeSantis signed the bill into law, and asks why a governor and state senator would travel to a foreign country to enact state legislation.

The account then moves to November 2025, when Fine called Carlson "the most dangerous antisemite in America" at the [[republican-jewish-coalition|Republican Jewish Coalition]] summit — singled out, the commentary argues, because of the size of his platform and its reach into middle America — and to March 5, 2026, when Fine called Carlson a "cancer" after a Carlson podcast on [[chabad-lubavitch|Chabad-Lubavitch]], recalling the discovery of "secret tunnels" beneath the group's Brooklyn headquarters two years earlier. Israeli influencers called for Carlson's imprisonment for inciting violence against Jews, blaming him for a school shooting in Michigan, while [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] amplified a Mediaite report of Republican Congressman [[max-miller|Max Miller]]'s call for Carlson to be investigated and jailed as an Iranian agent.

Bracketing the question of Carlson's sincerity, the commentary describes the pattern as a [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] to criminalize dissent against the Israeli government, and warns it will extend to criticism of the US government on incitement grounds. It argues Trump is subverting the effort by encouraging its advocates to "jump the shark" — noting his response to a question about a marine expeditionary force deployment ("You are a very obnoxious person") and his claim that the fake news uses AI to fabricate stories — thereby baiting Levin, [[laura-loomer|Loomer]] and their allies into demanding jail for hate speech while baiting the media into attacking him over press freedom. Two factions, the Zionists and the corporate media, are described as on a collision course over the First Amendment, ending with the single word: "Accelerate."

## Carlson and the FARA claim

A separate segment summarizes a [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] article of March 16, 2026 quoting Carlson as saying the supposed offense was "talking to people in Iran before the war" and that "They read my texts," with an X post attributed to him reading, "When you discover the CIA has been reading your texts in order to frame you for a crime." ZeroHedge later updated the article to report that "top admin officials" told [[axios|Axios]] reporter [[marc-caputo|Marc Caputo]] there was "no CIA investigation of Carlson," and that Loomer said she would take credit if Carlson were charged, having been "relentless" in contacting GOP representatives, law enforcement and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].

Ashe in America notes that Carlson is one of Levin's biggest public critics and recounts a conversation with a federal-agent friend in which she described what she calls "the final boss thing" — the idea that the public remains so dependent on outside authority that Trump himself must become the thing they fight before they stop waiting for a political savior. She says she hates the idea but that a Levin "love letter" with a [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] cameo brings it to mind. Referencing a discussion with Burning Bright on [[the-blitz|The Blitz]], she argues the truth community has an honesty problem and that many of its figures have become gatekeepers taking their cues from [[fox-news|Fox News]], summarized in the injunctions "Stop saying true but unfortunate things in an election year" and "while we're at war." Against this she invokes Senator Hiram Johnson's 1917 remark that "the first casualty when war comes, is truth," and Sun Tzu's "all warfare is based on deception."

She reports that calls to censor Badlands hosts amplified markedly that week, and credits [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] with resisting extraordinary pressure, quoting him: "There is no other hill that truly matters than the right of people to say what they believe." She closes with [[george-washington|George Washington]]'s 1783 address to his officers against a shadow effort to turn the army from the American cause, quoting his warning about "the secret Mover of this Scheme" who sought to exploit passions "without giving time for cool, deliberative thinking," and his conclusion that if men are precluded from offering their sentiments, "reason is of no use to us — the freedom of Speech may be taken away — and, dumb and silent we may be led, like sheep, to the Slaughter." Her federal-agent friend's reply — "people are emotional and may never reclaim their power" — she calls exactly what she would expect a Fed to say.

## Antifa convictions in Texas

Citing the Department of Justice, the Brief reports that a federal jury in Fort Worth convicted nine defendants for roles in a July 4, 2025 attack outside the [[prairieland-detention-center|Prairieland Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention center]] in Alvarado, Texas. Prosecutors described a "North Texas Antifa cell" involved in rioting, explosives offenses and material support to terrorists. The incident involved fireworks, vandalism and gunfire at responding officers; Alvarado Police Lieutenant Thomas Gross was shot and injured, and [[benjamin-song|Benjamin Song]] was convicted of attempted murder and firearms offenses. Eight defendants were convicted of rioting, material support, conspiracy to use explosives and using explosives, with fireworks cited as the devices. Daniel Sanchez-Estrada was convicted of corruptly concealing documents, and he and Maricela Rueda of conspiracy to conceal documents. Sentencing had not been announced.

Ashe in America, who says she usually regards "domestic terrorism" as an overused label, writes that attempting to bomb something for political purposes qualifies. She characterizes Antifa as "ostensibly an IC op that recruits vulnerable people, hires them to destabilize the nation, and pays them in meth and tacos," qualifying the claim with first-hand observation of Denver Antifa members. She quotes [[homeland-security-investigations|HSI]] Dallas Special Agent in Charge Travis Pickard, who called the case "a historic moment as it represents the nation's initial federal indictment targeting a coordinated group of Antifa cell members engaged in violent criminal activity." She nonetheless insists on due-process rights for the accused and asks whether an expanded domestic-terror label reaching speech, assembly and association without underlying criminal conduct would be acceptable simply because the defendants are Antifa, noting that it is not accepted when applied to [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]: "Principles are greater than politics. Equal justice under the law is nonnegotiable."

## Israel, Iran and Cuba

Under a heading announcing Cuba's confirmation of talks with the Trump administration, the Brief's summary block actually collects Israeli items. It reports that the [[israel|Israeli]] Justice Ministry unit reviewing clemency requests has not recommended a pardon for Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], who is standing trial on bribery, fraud and breach-of-trust charges he denies; Israeli law permits presidential pardons, though typically after conviction. Netanyahu said he does not know whether Iranians will oust their leadership and warned that Israel could target Iran's Supreme Leader if the conflict escalates further. Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Saar]] said only the Iranian people can bring down their government but that outside help may be necessary. The [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] said it struck dozens of targets in Tehran to "deepen the blow against the regime," and Iranian official [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] said the war will not end before Iran's enemies are made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry supplies the Cuban story: on a Thursday evening [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Diaz-Canel]] announced an emergency press conference, and the next morning appeared with [[raulito-castro|Raulito Castro]], grandson of [[raul-castro|Raul Castro]] and nicknamed "El Cangrejo," to say that [[cuba|Cuba]] was in open negotiations with the Trump administration, without further detail. He argues this all but confirms Caputo's Axios reporting that [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and Castro had been in secret talks about Castro taking over as leader, notes that Diaz-Canel is the first non-Castro to lead Cuba since [[fidel-castro|Fidel Castro]] in 1959, and observes that where neoconservatives had demanded the family's expulsion or prosecution for war crimes, Trump now appears set to install one of them as America's preferred Cuban leader — "following the template we have seen play out in other countries, such as [[venezuela|Venezuela]]."
