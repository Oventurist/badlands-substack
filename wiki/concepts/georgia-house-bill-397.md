# Georgia House Bill 397

Georgia House Bill 397 is election legislation considered by the Georgia Senate Ethics Committee in a late-night hearing in March 2025, attended by elections supervisors, poll workers and lawmakers.[1] Among its provisions is a requirement for an election night hand count.[1]

## Provisions

The bill would allow voter registration statuses to be challenged by the state election board in Atlanta, meaning that a voter anywhere in Georgia would have to travel to Atlanta to defend their registration status.[1] It would also require any person taking a voter registration application to ask whether the applicant is a U.S. citizen; if the applicant answers "no" or does not answer, the person must inform them that applying to vote as a non-citizen is a felony.[1] The bill would further end Georgia's membership in the [[electronic-registration-information-center|Electronic Registration Information Center]] (ERIC), which the secretary of state uses to identify voters who have moved out of state.[1]

## Debate

[[garland-favorito|Garland Favorito]], a lobbyist and supporter of the bill, framed the stakes broadly: "If the elections aren't right, what else do we have? We rely on the elections to elect the right people, to make the right decisions."[1] State Senator Jason Esteves objected that "if you are a qualified, registered voter, it should be easy. We shouldn't be making it hard to vote."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary argued that societies operate off consensus protocols, of which elections are one, and that "if you can't VERIFY the result, you can't accept the consensus."[1]
