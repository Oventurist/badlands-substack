# Trump v. United States

Trump v. United States is the 2024 Supreme Court decision on presidential immunity, which Badlands commentators treat as the controlling precedent governing whether former presidents can be compelled to answer for conduct outside their official duties.[1]

## Holding as invoked by Badlands

The nationally binding rule drawn from the case is stated plainly: there is no immunity for unofficial acts.[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America recalled the oral-argument exchange in which Justice Sonia Sotomayor pressed whether a president could order SEAL Team Six to assassinate a political opponent, and argued that the resulting limits on immunity are directly relevant to later accountability fights.[1]

## Oral argument (April 2024)

Ahead of the ruling, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] prepared to hear what Fox News described as possibly the most closely watched case of the term, involving the highest-profile appellant, after [[donald-trump]] offered a sweeping argument for why he should not face trial for alleged election interference.[2] The high court scheduled arguments for Thursday morning in a case that could determine the former president's personal and political future, with Trump — the presumptive GOP nominee — betting that his constitutional assertions would win a reprieve from the court's 6-3 conservative majority, three of whose members he had himself appointed.[2] The official question presented was whether, and if so to what extent, a former president enjoys presidential immunity from criminal prosecution for conduct alleged to involve official acts during his tenure in office.[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[ashe-in-america]] argued that historical precedent supports the immunity position only because no one had ever attempted to prosecute a president as she said Democrats were then doing.[2] She framed the outcome as a win-win: a ruling for Trump would neuter the witch hunts, while a ruling against him would place the alleged crimes of Obama, Biden, Bush and Clinton on the table for future legal remedy, since in her view Trump would beat the allegations on their merits regardless, leaving the matter before the high court one of clarity and precedent.[2]

## Application to the Clintons

The precedent was invoked in January 2026 after [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] declined subpoenas from the [[house-committee-on-oversight-and-accountability|House Oversight Committee]] in its [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] investigation.[1] Ashe in America conceded that Bill Clinton, as a former president, might claim a partial excuse, but argued that his protections end at official acts and that the Epstein-related conduct occurred long after he left office.[1] Hillary Clinton, never having held the presidency, was said to have no immunity claim at all despite her public service.[1]

The commentary paired Trump v. United States with the contempt prosecutions of Steve Bannon and Peter Navarro, who were imprisoned for defying congressional subpoenas, arguing that equal justice would require comparable consequences for the Clintons.[1]
