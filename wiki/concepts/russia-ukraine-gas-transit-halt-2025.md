# Russia–Ukraine Gas Transit Halt (2025)

On January 1, 2025, Russian gas stopped flowing to Europe via [[ukraine]], closing off a transit route that had operated for five decades.[1] Kyiv refused to allow any transit that would fund Moscow's war machine, and both sides confirmed the halt on Wednesday after the key transit deal expired.[1]

## Market impact

The stoppage meant that central European countries which had relied on the flows would be forced to source more expensive gas elsewhere, compounding pressure on supplies just as the region depleted winter storage at the fastest pace in years.[1] Although the route accounted for only about 5% of Europe's needs, the continent was still absorbing the aftershocks of the energy crisis triggered by [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|Russia's invasion of Ukraine]], with gas prices up 50% year-on-year and further boosted in the preceding weeks by the looming cutoff.[1] Europe became increasingly exposed to market volatility as it grew more reliant on globally traded liquefied natural gas.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had explicitly warned Europe that this would happen, referencing a widely circulated photograph of Trump confronting former German Chancellor Angela Merkel with the point that Germany had become reliant on Russian natural gas — making its request that the United States fund [[nato]], an alliance ostensibly built to guard against Russian aggression, preposterous.[1] The commentary predicted that Europe was "about to plunge into a deep hole that will be very difficult to climb out from," blaming what it called self-destructive European policies.[1]
