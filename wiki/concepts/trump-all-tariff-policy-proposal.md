# Trump All-Tariff Policy Proposal (2024)

The "all tariff policy" is a proposal floated by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Thursday, June 13, 2024, under which tariffs on imports would generate sufficient federal revenue to allow the United States to eliminate the [[federal-income-tax|federal income tax]] altogether.[1]

## Origin

Trump raised the idea in a private meeting with Republican lawmakers at the Capitol Hill Club in Washington, D.C., held the same day as his return to Capitol Hill; sources in the room described the remarks to CNBC.[1] In the same meeting he discussed using tariffs as leverage in negotiations with bad actors abroad.[1] The remarks indicated that Trump, who had championed tariffs as a foreign-policy multi-tool during his first term, was weighing a substantially more protectionist trade agenda for a second.[1] Spokespeople for the campaign did not immediately respond to requests for comment, and Trump posted on Truth Social later that morning only that there was "lots discussed, all positive" in the meeting.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that campaigning on funding the government through tariffs rather than taxes would force Uniparty candidates to defend taxation itself as a plank of their platforms — a position he called untenable.[1] Regime and controlled-opposition media, he wrote, would have to make affirmative pro-tax arguments, which would inevitably invite scrutiny of the legality and constitutionality of the federal income tax, and would raise for ordinary Americans the question of what it means to learn the government has been robbing them their entire lives.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the proposal as a "pincer move."[1] The standard rebuttal to abolishing taxation, he noted, is the "muh roads" argument — a rhetorical device he attributes to Prussian statecraft, designed to persuade citizens that government must extract revenue to build infrastructure a free market would supply on its own.[1] Trump's framing, he argued, eliminates taxation without triggering that objection, because funding would still flow — from the very globalist trade that critics of protectionism champion — and opponents cannot easily argue that taxes levied on voluntary foreign trading partners are immoral while taxes on Americans are moral.[1]
