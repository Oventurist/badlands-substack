# Ontario Reagan Tariff Advertisement

The Ontario Reagan tariff advertisement was a television spot paid for by the government of [[ontario|Ontario]] that used audio from an address by former President [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] criticizing tariffs, and which precipitated a breakdown in US–Canada trade negotiations in late October 2025.[1]

## Trump's response

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the advertisement "fraudulent," suspended trade talks with Canada, and announced he would impose an additional 10 percent tariff on Canadian goods as punishment.[1]

## Withdrawal

Ontario Premier [[doug-ford|Doug Ford]] announced on Friday, after speaking with Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]], that the ad would cease airing on Monday.[1] The withdrawal was consistent with Canada's declared strategy of removing all irritants from the relationship, an approach that had previously involved rolling back retaliatory tariffs and suspending a June tax on American technology companies.[1]

Carney, meanwhile, adopted a new message to Canadians: that his focus is limited to what the country can control, chiefly the pursuit of additional economic partnerships abroad, particularly in Asia, where he was meeting leaders at the time.[1]

## Termination of trade negotiations

On Thursday night, October 23, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was terminating all trade negotiations with [[canada|Canada]] because the Ontario provincial government had aired the advertisement featuring [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] speaking negatively about tariffs.[2] Trump's announcement accused Canada of trying to influence a pending U.S. Supreme Court case that could doom many of his tariffs, including those on Canada.[2]

His statement followed a declaration by the Ronald Reagan Presidential Foundation and Institute that the ad misrepresented a presidential radio address Reagan delivered in April 1987 and that his remarks had been edited without permission; the foundation did not specify what was misleading.[2] Reporting cited in the brief noted that Ontario Premier [[doug-ford|Doug Ford]] had said the province would spend $75 million on ads running in the United States featuring Reagan criticizing tariffs.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that learning Americans had been propagandized by Canada renewed her resolve for "Maple Liberation," accusing Canada of attempting to manipulate American courts and turn Americans against Trump to avoid paying its fair share.[2] She characterized the tariffs as a corrective measure for a trade imbalance and the ad campaign as information warfare, remarking that if Canada is "Globalism's Alamo, the script writers should win awards."[2]
