# Facebook Files

The "Facebook Files" is the name given by Representative [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-OH) to a cache of internal communications between Facebook and the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]], obtained and progressively released by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] during 2023.[1] The documents were produced only after the committee applied sustained pressure on the company, including a threat to hold [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] in contempt of [[congress]].[1]

## Contents

The release publicized in early August 2023 showed White House digital strategy director [[rob-flaherty|Rob Flaherty]] asking Facebook whether it could change its algorithm so that users were more likely to encounter establishment outlets such as [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] and The Wall Street Journal rather than competitors including Tomi Lahren and the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]].[1] Flaherty also asked whether the company had "a mechanism to check the material impact" of such a change.[1]

The materials therefore went beyond takedown requests for individual posts and touched the ranking and amplification systems that determine what users see by default.[1]

## Interpretation

The disclosures were widely framed as a sequel to the [[twitter-files]], reinforcing the argument that the federal government used private intermediaries to accomplish censorship it was constitutionally barred from performing itself under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1] Commentators at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] accepted the evidentiary value of the files but were sharply skeptical of their practical effect, observing that Republican legislators had spent years denouncing censorship without altering the underlying apparatus.[1] In that assessment, awareness had increased while visibility filtering continued and unjustly banned accounts remained banned, making congressional rhetoric about censorship "noise" absent genuine accountability.[1] The episode is treated as a documentary pillar of the [[censorship-industrial-complex]] thesis.[1]
