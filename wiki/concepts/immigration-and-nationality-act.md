# Immigration and Nationality Act

The Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) is the principal federal statute governing immigration, admission, removal, and asylum in the United States.[1]

## Standard of review for asylum determinations

In a unanimous March 2026 decision, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] held that the INA requires federal appeals courts to defer to immigration agencies' factual findings when deciding whether an asylum seeker's accepted facts amount to persecution.[1] Writing for the Court, Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] explained that under this deferential standard an appellate court may overturn an agency determination only if it is clearly unsupported by the evidence.[1]

The ruling arose from the case of Douglas Humberto Urias-Orellana, a Salvadoran national who entered the country without authorization in 2021 with his wife and child and testified that a hitman had targeted him in El Salvador.[1] An immigration judge credited his testimony but found the facts insufficient to meet the asylum threshold; the Court affirmed, leaving that conclusion undisturbed.[1] The outcome narrowed the scope for appellate reversal of asylum denials and was regarded as a win for the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1]

## Section 221(i) and the Honduran visa actions (December 2025)

The [[state-department|State Department]] invoked Section 221(i) of the Act in December 2025 to justify revoking the visa of one Honduran official and denying a visa to another over alleged interference with a special vote count following [[honduras|Honduras]]'s election.[2] Officials described the section as generally barring entry to those whose entry or proposed activities have potentially serious adverse U.S. foreign policy consequences.[2] Both officials targeted were members of the [[libre-party|LIBRE party]].[2]
