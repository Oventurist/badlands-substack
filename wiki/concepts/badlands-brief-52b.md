# Badlands Brief: Golgotha & The Metaverse

"Golgotha & The Metaverse" is an installment of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated March 20, 2026. The edition pairs news summaries with signed commentary from Badlands contributors, and in this issue covers the closure of the [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|Church of the Holy Sepulchre]] amid the Iran–Israel war, [[meta-platforms|Meta]]'s reversal on winding down its metaverse products, a widening foreign-policy split inside the MAGA coalition, a federal subpoena issued to [[james-comey|James Comey]], and bonus items on U.S.–Japan energy cooperation and a British political platform called [[restore-britain|Restore Britain]].

## Closure of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre

The Brief reports that the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem — traditionally identified as the site of the crucifixion and burial of Jesus — had been closed to the public, with church authorities citing security concerns ahead of Easter. The closure was characterized as rare and precautionary, taking place amid heightened regional instability.

The item situates the closure within an escalating exchange between [[iran|Iran]] and [[israel|Israel]]. According to the source, Iran launched missile and drone attacks on Israeli territory, including strikes on energy infrastructure such as an oil refinery in Haifa, where damage was reported. Israeli defense reporting cited by the Brief indicates that the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] were at times deliberately declining to intercept certain incoming munitions, including cluster bomblets, in order to conserve interceptor stocks. Israeli officials acknowledged sustained incoming attacks and impacts as the conflict expanded across multiple fronts.

In commentary, [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] calls the closure "a crazy development in the weeks leading up the Easter," recalling that the last closure occurred in 2018 as a protest by the church against taxes imposed by the Jerusalem municipal government. He argues that "the Israeli government(s) is totally out of control, drunk on its own hubris," and that if unchecked it has "the ego to take control of the Middle East and create Greater Israel." He frames the conflict as partly spiritual, asserting that "in every facet of this spiritual war, the enemy seeks to remove Jesus from peoples' lives," and cites a recent remark attributed to Senator [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] characterizing the phrase "Christ is King" as antisemitic. He concludes that "it is imperative that we plant our flag and prepare to die on this hill."

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] notes that the church typically draws up to four million visitors annually and quotes John 19:41 — "At the place where Jesus was crucified, there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb, in which no one had ever been laid." She observes that the crucifixion site and the empty tomb sit only a few dozen meters apart inside one complex, that the church is almost never closed, and adds: "Good thing Jesus is alive, otherwise this would be very sad."

## Meta's metaverse reversal

The second segment reports that Meta reversed its decision to shut down [[horizon-worlds|Horizon Worlds]] in virtual reality, having previously announced the product would become inaccessible after June 15. Instead, the company said it would pivot its metaverse experiences toward mobile and toward mixed reality rather than fully immersive virtual worlds, while continuing to fund [[reality-labs|Reality Labs]] for the foreseeable future.

The Brief quotes Meta technology chief Andrew Bosworth: "We will keep Horizon Worlds working in VR for existing games, to support the fans who've reached out... For people who already have games they like that they're using in Horizon Worlds, [they] will be able to download the Horizon Worlds app and use it in VR for the foreseeable future." Bosworth also pushed back on coverage of the earlier announcement: "We announced, 'hey, we're moving away from Horizon Worlds in VR,' and the headline is that Horizon is dead. It's not. And likewise, VR is not dead. We're continuing to invest tremendously." The source notes that Reality Labs has accumulated tens of billions of dollars in losses, which Meta leadership defends as a long-term investment.

Commentator [[matthew-trump|Matthew Trump]] asks whether there was "anything more mock-worthy in the social media era than [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]]'s attempt to stake his entire company's future on a virtual reality avatar world that almost no one wanted to explore?" He suggests the reprieve may have been granted "out of spite for his haters," recalls that few Trump supporters have affection for Zuckerberg given his funding of election administration in swing states in 2020 and his subsequent accommodation with Trump, and predicts the virtual-world concept will eventually return "in a new less repulsive form." He closes by warning that Zuckerberg remains formidable in the artificial-intelligence race because of "the Himalaya Range of personal data at his disposal."

## The Israel war debate and the MAGA coalition

The third segment reports that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] intensified his public rhetoric on the Iran–Israel conflict, signaling support for Israeli military action while warning of broader escalation, and that the war exposed divisions within Trump-aligned political and media circles over U.S. involvement in foreign wars. Citing reporting from The Guardian, Axios, The Wall Street Journal and Politico, the Brief describes a split between factions favoring strong U.S.–Israel alignment and others advocating restraint or non-intervention, contested under the banner of the [[america-first|America First]] doctrine.

The divide sharpened following the resignation of counterterrorism chief [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]] over the U.S. decision to attack Iran, and an attack on an Iranian gas field. At a press conference, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said Israel "acted alone" in striking the field.

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argues that Trump "doesn't smash through the calcified barriers of the Collective Mind alone," and instead "sends cognitive rangers to the outer edges of the Overton window—and follows them partway." He compares the moment to [[operation-midnight-hammer|Operation Midnight Hammer]], during which, in his reading, Trump "publicly blessed the pro-Israel optics every NeoCon, Zionist and legacy hawk demanded while making it crystal clear that he, and only he was calling the shots," a maneuver he termed "Narrative Disarmament." He describes Kent's resignation — timed to the [[south-pars-gas-field|South Pars gas field]] strikes and declaring that the United States entered the conflict "due to pressure from Israel and its powerful American lobby" rather than vital American interests — as "catnip for every anti-Zionist, isolationist and 'betrayed' voice in the extended MAGA coalition," seized on by Politico and the Journal as proof of MAGA fracturing.

Yet, Burning Bright notes, Trump hardened his own stance the same day, stating that Israel acted on the South Pars strike "without American coordination" and warning that further attacks on the field would cease unless Iran again struck [[qatar|Qatar]], with the threat of an overwhelming and "entirely hypothetical" U.S. response on his own terms. Second-order effects followed immediately: [[turkey|Turkey]]'s [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] publicly blamed Israel for the "unprecedented crisis," and the new Gulf alignments Trump has been building showed visible strain. Burning Bright concludes that the episode need not sort Kent into "white hat or black hat camps," only that Trump "knows the core makeup and incentive structures of every character who moves in his narrative gravity well," and is allowing his base "to engage in the spirited—and at times ugly—debate necessary to clarify alignments on the Israel variable."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry reads the sequence as coordinated: Kent resigns with a letter blaming Israel for several historical events; Trump posts disparagingly about Israel over the oil-field attack, saying Israel acted alone; Netanyahu then affirms as much to the press. On [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s podcast, he notes, Kent never attacks Trump, instead repeatedly saying the situation is salvageable and that Trump is uniquely qualified to salvage it — by telling Israel to back down or lose U.S. military support, then partnering with the Gulf Arabs to negotiate a settlement requiring Iran to sell oil in U.S. dollars. Qatar and Turkey subsequently held a joint press conference in which Qatar blamed Israel for the war and called for Arab reconciliation with Iran.

He further recounts that Kent told Carlson negotiations with Iran had been progressing and a deal was possible; that on the same day the British National Security Advisor, who had attended the meetings, described an "unbelievable" Iranian offer; and that the following day the Omani Foreign Minister, also present, said Iran had agreed to "zero enriched uranium stockpiling," a concession few expected. A few hours after those purported terms were agreed, he writes, Tehran was bombed and the war began. ([[oman|Oman]] and the United Kingdom served as intermediaries in those talks.)

Turning to the present, he notes Israel's strike on the large gas field between Qatar and Iran in the Persian Gulf, Trump's Truth Social post blaming Israel, and Netanyahu's confirmation that Israel acted alone and would comply with Trump's request to refrain from further attacks — a pledge he doubts. He points to the same press conference, in which Netanyahu says a ground invasion will occur, and relays an online claim that the video may be AI-generated, with an alleged artifact at timestamp 10:17 of the Israel Press Office's official YouTube upload. He judges it unlikely that Netanyahu is dead, reasoning that Israel's ruthless political class could not all be induced to sustain a cover-up, and speculates that if the video is fake, [[mossad|Mossad]] may be attempting "to bait and/or confuse the truth movement into discrediting itself." He predicts Netanyahu will lead Israel into encirclement by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], "narratively but perhaps also physically," and that Trump will create distance from Netanyahu in the interim.

## Comey subpoena

The Brief reports that James Comey was subpoenaed in a federal investigation examining alleged conspiracy-related matters tied to prior Trump-era probes, part of a broader [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] inquiry into decision-making, coordination and potential misconduct in those earlier investigations. The probe is described as ongoing, with no charges announced, and is characterized as a significant escalation in scrutiny of the origins and conduct of past federal investigations involving Trump.

Ashe in America recalls that Comey was indicted on federal charges of lying to Congress in 2020, but that a judge found prosecutor [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]]'s appointment unlawful under the interim U.S. attorney statute, requiring that all actions flowing from the defective appointment — including the indictment — be set aside, and the case dismissed. The Justice Department appealed. She adds a broader speculation: that the Halligan precedent "could impact entire portfolios of government legal work being tossed out from the previous administration," invoking the [[autopen-scandal|autopen]] controversy, and asks what would follow if it "ultimately, effectively erases the Biden Administration," calling the resulting blast radius "a mandate-creating experience for the American people." She ends with a one-word exhortation: "Accelerate."

## Bonus items

### U.S.–Japan energy and nuclear agreements

The Brief reports that Trump met Japanese Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]] at the [[white-house|White House]] for a bilateral summit on energy, security and economic cooperation. The two governments are advancing a major nuclear energy initiative including small modular reactor development in the United States, with project estimates around $40 billion. [[japan|Japan]] pledged tens of billions of dollars in U.S. energy-sector investment covering nuclear, natural gas and infrastructure projects, alongside cooperation on critical minerals and rare earth supply chains intended to reduce reliance on [[china|China]]. The leaders discussed energy security amid global instability, including oil supply concerns tied to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. Takaichi reiterated Japan's limits on military involvement under its pacifist constitution while affirming continued economic and strategic cooperation.

### Restore Britain

A final item describes a group called Restore Britain circulating a platform that includes mass deportations of migrants, expanded law-and-order measures and sweeping cultural policy changes — among them deporting large numbers of migrants, restricting practices deemed incompatible with British law, and substantially tougher criminal penalties. The Brief notes that some reported positions involve extreme punitive measures that would face significant legal and constitutional barriers under UK law, including human rights protections, and that the platform has drawn attention for pushing beyond traditional boundaries in British politics on immigration and criminal justice.
