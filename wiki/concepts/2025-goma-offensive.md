# 2025 Goma Offensive

The **2025 Goma offensive** was the seizure of Goma, the largest city in eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], by [[m23|M23]] rebels backed by Rwandan troops in late January 2025, followed by a southward push toward the city of Bukavu in South Kivu province.[1]

## Course of the offensive

The United Nations expressed deep concern at reports of M23 rebels and Rwandan troops advancing south toward Bukavu on Thursday, 30 January 2025, as the militants sought to consolidate control over Goma.[1] The seizure of Goma and the ongoing southward offensive constituted the biggest escalation since 2012 of a decades-old conflict that the U.N. warned risked spiralling into another major regional war.[1] A sustained and successful push by M23 into South Kivu would give the group control of territory that previous rebellions had not taken since the end of the two major wars that ran from 1996 to 2003, in which millions of civilians died, mostly from malnutrition and disease.[1] U.N. spokesperson Stephane Dujarric said the absence of U.N. peacekeepers in South Kivu heightened the humanitarian and security risks of an escalation in fighting there.[1]

## South African involvement and the Kagame–Ramaphosa dispute

The war in eastern DRC also revived old disputes between Pretoria and Kigali, which found themselves on opposite sides of the Congolese battlefield by proxy.[1] South Africa suffered heavy losses, with 13 soldiers killed in the battle for Goma on 23 January 2025; the country supplies the largest contingent to SAMIDRC, a regional military force dispatched by the Southern African Development Community.[1] Pretoria also faced domestic criticism, with the South African public questioning the decision to send troops to fight in a distant country, and threats from Rwandan president [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]], who rejected blame for the conflict and regarded the South African intervention as hostile.[1] South African president [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]] was the target of Kagame's anger; Badlands commentary described Ramaphosa as a close ally of Vladimir Putin whose army was deployed in eastern DRC fighting alongside Congolese security forces to drive out the rebels.[1]

## Diplomatic response

On 29 January, Kenyan president [[william-ruto|William Ruto]] organized a virtual conference between eight African countries about the violence, in which Kagame participated; DRC president [[felix-tshisekedi|Felix Tshisekedi]] was absent, reportedly because of a scheduling conflict.[1]

## Resource dimension

Badlands commentary framed the offensive within a longer history of resource capture. Kagame was described as a former rebel leader who sponsored several wars in the 1990s, one of which installed Laurent Kabila as president of the DRC.[1] Kabila's son [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]], who succeeded his father after the elder was assassinated in 2001, befriended Israeli oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] and granted him mineral rights that effectively gave Gertler and his partners a monopoly over the Congo's precious metals market, estimated to be worth in excess of $24 trillion.[1] Joseph Kabila left office at the end of 2018 after Tshisekedi won the election, having declined to seek re-election once his corrupt dealings with Gertler were exposed to the Congolese public.[1] A map circulated in the brief highlighted a [[coltan|coltan]] mine near Goma, and a British court ruling in 2023 that stripped mineral rights from Gertler was followed by a multi-billion dollar agreement between a UAE investment firm and the DRC government to take over eastern mining operations.[1]
