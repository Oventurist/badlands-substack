# 2023 Hamas Delegation Visit to Moscow

The 2023 Hamas delegation visit to Moscow was an unannounced diplomatic trip by senior [[hamas]] figures to the Russian capital, confirmed by the [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]] on the evening of Thursday, October 26, 2023.[1] The visit occurred against the backdrop of the Israel–Hamas war and Russian accusations that the United States was driving escalation in the region.[1]

## Confirmation and composition

Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] told a press briefing, "I can confirm that representatives of the [Hamas] Palestinian movement are visiting Moscow," and promised further details as the talks unfolded.[1] The visit had not been previously announced by either side.[1] The delegation was led by [[moussa-abu-marzouk|Moussa Abu Marzouk]], a senior member of the group.[1] Hamas is designated a terrorist organization by the United States, the European Union, and other countries, but maintains official relations with states including [[iran]], [[turkey]], [[syria]], and — apparently, as of this visit — [[russia]].[1]

## Russian posture

Moscow maintained that it remained willing to talk to all sides of the conflict in pursuit of a ceasefire and peace, a posture consistent with its earlier hosting of a Taliban delegation following the U.S. exit from [[afghanistan]].[1] It was initially unclear whether Russia's top diplomat, [[sergey-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]], would meet the Hamas representatives, as he was reported to be in Minsk at the time.[1] RIA Novosti reported that during opening meetings with Russian officials, Hamas "commended Putin's position and the efforts of Russia's diplomacy."[1]

The Kremlin said it was engaged in crisis diplomacy with both the Hamas and Israeli sides at a moment when more than 220 hostages were still held in [[gaza]]; four had been released at that point, including two Americans, largely through the mediation of [[qatar]].[1] Observers speculated that pressing the delegation for further hostage releases was high on Moscow's agenda.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media]] contributors treated the episode as a test of Western narrative consistency. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the West was escalating toward global war while Russia, at least outwardly, made moves toward peace, and noted the irony that after decades of being told Russia and its allies were "savage world villains," it was the United States pushing for further violence in the region.[1] She characterized Hamas as no victim but rather a party that had made victims of both Palestinians and Israelis, apparently intent on prolonging the conflict until it could reclaim the Holy City and liberate the mosque, and suggested that a Russian-brokered Hamas ceasefire would leave the pro-Ukraine/pro-Palestine factions in the United States rhetorically and psychologically stranded.[1] She also observed that the preceding day marked the anniversary of Israel's 1994 peace agreement with Jordan, which still held, as proof that peace is possible when parties genuinely desire it.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] added that the Western "communist contingent" — described as Hamas superfans — would find itself obliged to praise Russian peacemaking after two years of condemning Moscow and praising "Ukrainian Nazis," calling the resulting cognitive dissonance both predictable and funny.[1]
