# American System of political economy

The **American System** is the nationalist school of political economy — protective tariffs, national banking, and state-directed credit for infrastructure — associated in Badlands Media commentary with [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and identified as the principal threat facing the [[british-empire|British Empire]] at the opening of the twentieth century.[1]

## Global diffusion

In Germany, the American System-inspired Zollverein customs union not only unified a divided nation but raised it to a level of productive power and sovereignty that outpaced the monopoly power of the British East India Company.[1] In Japan, American engineers helped assemble trains funded by a national banking system and protective tariffs during the Meiji Restoration.[1] In Russia, [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]] revolutionized the economy with American-built trains rolling across the [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]].[1]

In France, President Sadi Carnot and foreign minister Gabriel Hanotaux applied the system before Carnot's 1895 assassination, building diplomatic bridges around large-scale rail and industrial projects with Russia, Germany and China; a Russo-Chinese Bank was created to fund rail development from the Trans-Siberian line into China.[1] Russia signed a mutual defense treaty with China in 1895, and Witte pressed to keep Chinese territory intact after the 1894 Sino-Japanese war.[1] The [[berlin-baghdad-railway|Berlin to Baghdad Railway]] extended the same impulse into the [[ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]].[1]

## American System Caucus

Inside the United States, nationalists sometimes dubbed the "American System Caucus" fought to keep the country neutral in 1914, drawing on the non-interventionist tradition of [[george-washington|George Washington]] and [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]], whose [[monroe-doctrine|Monroe Doctrine]] warned that America must never "go about searching for monsters to destroy."[1] They did not win at the federal level until the 1921 inauguration of [[warren-harding|Warren Harding]].[1]
