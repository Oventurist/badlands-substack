# Black Sea Grain Initiative

The Black Sea Grain Initiative was a United Nations-brokered arrangement, administered through Turkey, that established a humanitarian shipping corridor allowing Ukrainian grain to be exported from Black Sea ports to global markets despite wartime hostilities between Russia and Ukraine.[1]

## Termination in July 2023

News broke on Monday, July 17, 2023 that Russia had allowed the grain deal to lapse at midnight, Istanbul time, marking the end of the Turkey-administered corridor.[1] Moscow subsequently confirmed that the deal had been "terminated," and the Kremlin stated that the arrangement was being terminated until Russia's demands were met.[1] Wheat prices jumped on the news, reflecting the corridor's importance to global commodity supply.[1]

## Relationship to the Crimean Bridge attack

Commentary at the time speculated that a fresh attack on the [[crimean-bridge-attack|Crimean Bridge]] had been the last straw prompting the termination, but Moscow downplayed the connection.[1] Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov noted that Russia's willingness to withdraw from the grain initiative had been communicated prior to the attack.[1]

## Badlands framing

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the collapse of the grain deal as one datapoint in a broader unraveling of what he described as the Western globalist proxy war against Russia, in which narrative losses accompany battlefield ones.[1] In that reading, the termination of the corridor and the Kremlin's projection of "stalwart confidence and certitude" indicated that Russian strategy and objectives had not meaningfully changed under Western pressure.[1]
