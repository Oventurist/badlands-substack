# Predictive Programming

Predictive programming is the idea, recurrent in the Badlands Media corpus, that films and television deliberately depict future events or policies in advance so that audiences will accept them when they occur. The concept is developed at length in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s essay [[a-new-hollywood|A New Hollywood]] (February 6, 2025), which states that "Hollywood has been used to condition us and has used predictive programming to prepare us to accept future [[deep-state|Deep State]] narratives and agendas."

The essay's distinctive claim is that the technique is not intrinsically malign and has changed hands. Carlson argues that Hollywood "is now being used to prepare us for what [[donald-trump|Trump]] has in store," offering as evidence a cluster of recent [[paramount-global|Paramount]] and [[showtime|Showtime]] programming: *Lioness*, in which [[cia|CIA]] operatives infiltrate a Mexican drug cartel and recover trafficked children; *The Agency*; *Landman*, which he glosses as "drill, baby drill"; and the [[skydance-media|Skydance]] series *Reacher* and *Jack Ryan*.

His central example is *Mission Impossible: Dead Reckoning* (summer 2023), whose plot turns on saving the world from a powerful [[artificial-intelligence|AI]] system that could be used to take over the world in the wrong hands. Because the film was released only about six months after ChatGPT's late-2022 launch, and production must have begun well before AI became a popular talking point, Carlson reads it as a warning about AI and asks whether "a faction of Hollywood is trying to warn us of possible dangers and opening our eyes to future events."

An earlier historical instance offered by the article is the original *Top Gun* (1986), released under [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] and called "the biggest military recruiting tool ever created" — presented as proof that a president can shape the national imagination through film, and as the template for what the author expects from Trump.
