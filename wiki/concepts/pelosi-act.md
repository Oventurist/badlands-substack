# PELOSI Act

The PELOSI Act — formally the Preventing Elected Leaders from Owning Securities and Investments Act — is proposed United States legislation introduced by Senator [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] (R-MO) that would bar members of [[congress|Congress]], as well as their family members, from holding or trading stocks.[1] Hawley introduced the bill in January 2023, having brought forward similar legislation the previous year, and argued that the practice of congressional stock ownership must end.[1]

## Naming and background

The bill's acronym deliberately references former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] (D-CA), who drew sustained criticism after her husband, [[paul-pelosi|Paul Pelosi]], purchased up to $5 million in stock in a semiconductor company at the time the Senate was passing legislation to heavily subsidize the semiconductor industry.[1] Pelosi was also among a bipartisan group of lawmakers who beat the market in 2021 across hundreds of millions of dollars in stock trades; others who performed especially well included Representatives Austin Scott (R-GA), Brian Mast (R-FL), French Hill (R-AR), John Curtis (R-UT), and Dan Crenshaw (R-TX).[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described the bill as a step in the right direction while arguing that it left the underlying problem untouched.[1] He contended that insider trading and market opportunism at the expense of ordinary Americans and of global economic stability remained unaddressed, because information obtained by members of Congress carries sweeping international implications for economic trends and emerging markets, and a legislator need not personally own stock to exploit it.[1] As an example he cited advance anticipation of the COVID-19 lockdowns, which he said allowed those with foreknowledge to make billions and facilitated what he called the single largest transfer of wealth in human history, with equities forming only a small part of the gains.[1] Deschamps concluded that until the public learns the law and its relationship to the economy and to individual rights well enough to end all forms of unfair advantage and manipulation, governmental corruption and the parasitic character of the economy would persist.[1]
