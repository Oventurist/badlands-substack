# Israel-Turkey Relations

Relations between [[israel|Israel]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] have been fraught in recent years, with the two states maintaining diplomatic ties through a period of sharp friction generated by the war in Gaza and by competing interests in Syria.[1]

## Friction during the Gaza war

The two-year war in Gaza added friction to an already tense dynamic, though the countries maintained diplomatic relations throughout.[1] During the fighting between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]], Israeli officials worked to curb Turkish efforts to expand Ankara's role in ceasefire mediation.[1] Regionally, the conflict widened fault lines and exposed competing Israeli and Turkish interests in Syria.[1]

## Turkey as a guarantor of the Gaza peace plan

In October 2025 Turkey secured a frontline role in Gaza's future by signing on as a guarantor of a US-initiated peace plan for the territory.[1] Alongside President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] signed a joint statement backing an end to the Israel–Hamas war, with Ankara committing to practical steps in the post-conflict phase.[1]

## Military balance

The relationship also has a hard-power dimension. Israeli strikes on Turkey's neighbours Iran and Syria, and on Lebanon and Qatar, unnerved Ankara over the preceding year and exposed vulnerabilities that drove the [[turkish-fighter-jet-procurement-push|Turkish fighter jet procurement push]], as Turkey sought Eurofighter Typhoons and eventually [[f-35|F-35]] aircraft to close the gap with the region's most advanced air force.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that new US–Turkish security agreements appear to concern a balance of power in Syria, with Israel ostensibly representing one side and Turkey the other, and noted that since the overthrow of Assad in December, Trump has given Erdogan narrative credit for "conquering" Syria.[1]
