# United States v. Apple Antitrust Case

The **antitrust action against Apple** is the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] case brought in 2024 against [[apple|Apple]] Inc. On its face, the action concerns Apple's secure communications network: the Justice Department wants the company to share its services with other networks.[1]

## Criticism

The [[brownstone-institute|Brownstone Institute]] argued that, as with many antitrust actions in history, the case is really about the government taking sides in a competitive dispute between companies — here on behalf of Samsung and other smartphone providers, who resent the way Apple products all work together and want that changed.[1]

The institute placed the case in a lineage beginning with [[white-house|White House]] press secretary [[jen-psaki|Jen Psaki]]'s statement of May 5, 2021, in which she pressed platforms to stop amplifying COVID-19 and election "misinformation" while noting that President [[joe-biden|Biden]] supported "a robust antitrust program" — read by critics as a warning that noncompliant companies would face regulatory consequences.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] found it notable that the Biden DOJ was "bullying Apple" just after Supreme Court arguments in [[murthy-v-missouri|Murthy v. Missouri]] reframed government coercion as "encouragement."[1] In her reading, the government is demonizing competition and trampling intellectual property rights in the name of antitrust, and the deeper stake is control of information: "Centralized control of information is critical to control of the collective mind — and the regime is failing in that."[1] She argued that the shadow bureaucracy effectively ran the social media platforms in the run-up to 2020 and has operated openly since, and that the Apple case represents an attempt to extend the level of control it once exercised over Facebook and old Twitter to device hardware and networking infrastructure — concluding, "Danger ahead."[1]
