# Proof-of-Stake

Proof-of-stake (POS) is a blockchain consensus mechanism in which randomly selected validators confirm transactions and create new blocks, in contrast to [[proof-of-work]] chains such as [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]], which rely on competing miners running specialized computers to solve complex mathematical problems.[1]

## Criticism as a centralizing design

Independent journalist [[ryan-matta|Ryan Matta]] told Badlands Media that "there is nothing decentralized about proof-of-stake blockchain."[1] Because validators are chosen based on the number of staked coins they hold, POS can create barriers to fair access for retail investors.[1] Matta added that "when a project launches, the team owns enough stake in the company to where they can control and change the rules of a blockchain" — conduct that in a traditional market would resemble antitrust violations and market manipulation, but which in crypto, the article observed, "is just a Tuesday."[1] Matta explored these dynamics in detail in his October 2022 video "What is Solana?" about [[solana|Solana]].[1]

## Energy trade-off

POS is generally promoted for its speed and lower environmental impact. In September 2022 [[ethereum|Ethereum]] completed its transition from proof-of-work to proof-of-stake — an upgrade dubbed "the Merge" — reportedly cutting direct energy consumption by 99 percent while increasing transaction speed.[1] The article argued that in solving for speed and environmental footprint, Ethereum added greater centralization.[1]
