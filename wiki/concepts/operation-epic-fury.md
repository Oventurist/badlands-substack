# Operation Epic Fury

**Operation Epic Fury** is the name used in Badlands Media commentary for the coordinated United States and Israeli air and missile campaign launched against [[iran|Iran]] in late February and early March 2026.[1] The operation targeted Iranian military leadership, missile systems, and nuclear-related infrastructure, according to US officials.[1]

## Course of the campaign

The strikes were launched on a Friday in coordination with [[israel|Israel]], and US officials confirmed that more than 1,000 targets had been struck since the campaign began.[1] Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] was killed in the attacks.[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the operation was intended to halt Iran's nuclear and long-range missile capabilities and could last several weeks, while indicating he remained open to talks with Iranian officials even as military pressure continued.[1]

Secretary of War [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] said the United States did not intend to deploy ground troops but declined to rule it out if necessary, describing the campaign as focused on military objectives rather than governance change.[1] Iran responded with retaliatory missile and drone attacks against US and allied positions across the region.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

The Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the operation as launching "with the full cinematic weight the Collective Mind has been primed to expect" — precision strikes, retaliatory missiles, and a direct Trump address to the Iranian people — all of which carried the surface optics of a conventional regime change operation.[1] In this reading, however, the campaign actually follows the [[venezuela-model|Venezuela Model]]: the removal of a visible figurehead while the governing structure remains intact and becomes open to negotiated arrangements, rather than an open-ended occupation.[1]

Evidence cited for this interpretation includes the reaction of former national security adviser [[john-bolton|John Bolton]], who warned of "turmoil and bloodshed" and criticized the lack of consultation with Iranian opposition figures — behavior read by Burning Bright as neoconservative frustration that full-scale regime change was *not* being pursued.[1]

## Related developments

The campaign disrupted shipping through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and drew Saudi oil facilities into the conflict.[1] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] later said the United States struck preemptively because it had determined Israel was preparing its own attack that would have triggered Iranian retaliation against American forces.[1]

## The end of OPEC as an objective

Badlands coverage of the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]]'s May 1, 2026 exit from [[opec|OPEC]] framed the "end of OPEC" as a geopolitical objective of Epic Fury rather than merely an energy story: break Iran's leverage, weaken cartel coordination, and drive postwar oil prices lower.[2] Writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the visible narrative war served as cover for the actual operations beneath it — sovereign disentanglement, supply-line realignments, and the quiet cultivation of force multipliers defining the next era of multipolarity — such that short-term disruptions were features rather than bugs, each squeeze exposing the old system's fragility while building public mandate for free-market dynamics.[2] He predicted a cascade in which other Gulf producers, including [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] with its long-reserved spare capacity, would follow once the [[strait-of-hormuz|Hormuz]] theater resolved.[2]

## Trump's "legendary Epic Fury" post and sequenced conclusion

In a Truth Social post on the morning of Wednesday, May 6, 2026, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] wrote that the "already legendary Epic Fury" would reach its end once [[iran|Iran]] agreed to "give what has been agreed to," adding almost in passing that this remained "perhaps, a big assumption."[3] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the post not as hedging but as a public ratification of two premises structuring the campaign.[3]

The first is that the deals are already done: the terminal agreements enabling a multipolar order to emerge from the controlled demolition of the globalist superstructure were, on this reading, negotiated among [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] principals long before the theater opened for public consumption, leaving visible only the managed withdrawal of the old enforcement architecture and the isolation of factions still defending it.[3] Trump's phrasing assumes Tehran already knows the precise final shape of what "has been agreed to," which Burning Bright treated as further confirmation that coordination persists across lines the central narrative presents as adversarial.[3]

The second premise concerns sequencing: calling the episode "legendary" while conditioning its public conclusion on compliance with already-understood terms signals that kinetic pressure and narrative architecture are being deliberately staged apart.[3] The military posture moves on its own timetable while the story moves on the timetable required for mandate cultivation, so the war ends not when battlefield metrics are satisfied but when the global audience has been brought to grant the American President uncontested authority to declare it concluded — or, put differently, when the warmongers lose the public mandate to keep opposing him.[3] Burning Bright described this as [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation]] sequencing at scale.[3]

## Opening phase and the third operation

Writing on March 2, 2026 as the campaign's opening phase unfolded, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Epic Fury as the "Iranian Regime Change Narrative Deployment" and identified it as the third operation of its kind deployed over the preceding nine months, following [[operation-midnight-hammer|Operation Midnight Hammer]] against Iran's nuclear program in 2025 and [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]] in Venezuela.[4] Over that weekend Trump delivered an eight-minute address announcing the start of major combat operations, speaking directly to the Iranian people, urging them to take over their government and seize a generational chance for freedom, and offering immunity to military and [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] members who stood down immediately.[4]

Three U.S. service members were killed and five others seriously wounded on Sunday during U.S. attacks on Iran, the first American fatalities of the conflict; [[us-central-command|U.S. Central Command]] confirmed the casualties and said further losses were possible before the campaign ended.[4] Trump characterized the campaign as "major combat operations" that could continue for weeks.[4] More than 1,000 Iranian sites had been targeted, and Iran responded with missile and drone attacks on U.S. and allied bases across the Middle East — notably at regional bases rather than U.S. soil.[4] In a March 1 interview, Trump said Iranian leaders had expressed interest in resuming negotiations and that he agreed to engage, while criticizing Tehran for delaying and offering few specifics on terms or timelines.[4]

Burning Bright argued that the true adversary of the operation is neither the Iranian people nor "the Regime" as conventionally understood, but the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]] — the network of [[neoconservatives|neoconservative]] warmongers, globalist forever-war planners and their institutional backers.[4] He framed the situation as [[donald-trump|Trump]] moving through a [[kobayashi-maru|Kobayashi Maru]] while placing that enemy inside a no-win scenario with no clean exit, and asserted that little of it beyond forewarned face-saving strikes needed to cross into the "Realm of the Real" to be effective — an application of the [[power-paradigm|Power Paradigm]] and a path toward [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]].[4]
