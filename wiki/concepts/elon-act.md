# ELON Act

The ELON Act was a bill introduced in the U.S. House of Representatives in February 2023 by Representative [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] (R-Colo.), backed by nine other cosponsors, and described as the first legislative attempt by House Republicans to slow federal requests to Big Tech companies to censor online content.[1]

## Provisions

The measure would impose a one-year moratorium on taxpayer payments from the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to social media firms, and would require an audit of how much money had changed hands between the DOJ and Big Tech companies since the beginning of 2015.[1]

## Context

The bill followed revelations that the [[fbi|FBI]] had paid more than $3 million to [[twitter|Twitter]] to compensate the company for processing a large volume of censorship requests dating to the 2020 election, disclosures that formed part of the broader body of evidence about federal pressure on platforms to suppress American speech online.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] situated the legislation within a long historical pattern in which control of information has been the primary instrument of social control, arguing that the apparent liberalisation of the past four centuries masked an evolution rather than an end of that agenda, with Big Tech as its latest vehicle. He characterised the use of taxpayer money to pay private firms to suppress speech as an open and unconstitutional violation of American rights, but argued that its very boldness was useful: it made a long-invisible loss of freedom legible to the public and could help rouse what he called the sleeping giant of the American freedom-fighting populace.[1]
