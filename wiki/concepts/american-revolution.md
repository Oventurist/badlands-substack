# American Revolution

The American Revolution is the founding conflict in which the American colonies broke from the British Crown, and it serves as the historical anchor of [[patriot-chronicles|The Patriot Chronicles]], the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] series by [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|Ghost of Patrick Henry]].[1]

## The colonial crucible

The series' introduction argued that the despotism the colonies faced was not merely political but also cultural.[1] Settlers arriving in the New World were thrown into the tempest of the wilds, forced to endure an unforgiving frontier as well as the hostilities of weary natives, and in the process became proficient survivalists who also learned the nuance of diplomacy in dealing with their native hosts.[1] Natives frequently served as trade partners and as guides, teaching colonists how to live off land whose plant and animal life was unfamiliar to Britain.[1] Most of these interactions occurred organically without formal supervision or blessing from the Crown — an arrangement into which the Crown eventually injected itself, to the detriment of both colonists and natives.[1]

After nearly 170 years far removed from "civilized" European society, the colonists had cultivated a mindset of fierce independence and skills of self-reliance surpassing those of their peers in Britain; unbeknownst to the power-brokers in London, the author wrote, the crucible of the American wilderness had forged a new people.[1] Within that furnace the fires of frontier survival were said to have burned away the shackles of servitude and empowered colonists to shed the psychological paradigm curated by the masters of the Old World: the [[divine-right-of-kings|Divine Right of Kings]].[1]

## Declaration and legacy

The essay quoted at length from [[thomas-jefferson|Thomas Jefferson]]'s [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]], emphasizing the passage holding that when a long train of abuses evinces a design to reduce a people under absolute despotism, "it is their right, it is their duty, to throw off such Government."[1] The Declaration was characterized as more than a letter of intent for self-governance: it was a signal to the world that the Divine Right of Kings was a lie and that the Age of Monarchy must end, because no man needs a master — for which reason, the author argued, America has always been fiercely hated by narcissistic sociopaths with aspirations to rule over others.[1]

The introduction was illustrated with Don Troiani's "Lexington Common, 19th of April 1775" and Luis Arcas Brauner's "The Boston Tea Party," and held that while the term "patriot" has existed in various forms since ancient times, it was American ancestors who came to personify and define it in the modern lexicon, making "patriot" and "freedom" synonymous with the United States.[1] A teaser for the first installment described [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]] and his veterans stopping the most powerful global empire of the age at the most critical moment of the Revolution.[1]
