# Trumponomics

"Trumponomics" is the shorthand used for the economic program of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. In an extensive Bloomberg interview given before the June 2024 presidential debate and before the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|Butler rally shooting]], Trump laid out the core tenets of what commentators dubbed *Trumponomics 2.0*, the framework he intended to govern by in a second term.[1]

## Key pillars

Reporting on the interview identified several commitments. Trump said he would enforce large bilateral sanctions even while claiming "I don't love sanctions," and repeatedly invoked [[william-mckinley|William McKinley]], who he argued raised enough revenue through tariffs during his turn-of-the-20th-century presidency to avoid instituting a federal income tax yet never received appropriate credit for it.[1] He indicated he would allow [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] to serve out his term as chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], which ran through May 2026, rather than firing him.[1] He proposed lowering the corporate tax rate to as low as 15 percent, and said he no longer planned to ban TikTok.[1] He floated JPMorgan chief [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] as a possible secretary of the Department of the Treasury.[1]

On foreign economic policy, Trump was described as ambivalent if not outright hostile toward defending Taiwan from Chinese aggression and toward US efforts to punish Vladimir Putin for the invasion of Ukraine.[1]

## Continuity and execution

Coverage stressed that the broad strokes of the program differed little from Trump's first term; what was new was the speed and efficiency with which he intended to enact it. Trump argued he now understood the levers of power far more deeply, including the importance of personnel: "We had great people, but I had some people that I would not have chosen for a second time. Now, I know everybody. Now, I am truly experienced."[1]
