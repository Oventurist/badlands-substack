# Wonsan-Kalma Tourist Zone

The Wonsan-Kalma zone is a coastal tourism development on [[north-korea|North Korea]]'s eastern coast and one of [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]]'s most talked-about projects since the plans were announced in 2015.[1] North Korea has built luxury hotels and recreational facilities there with the aim of creating a key attraction for international visitors.[1]

## Development and setbacks

The project reportedly suffered setbacks owing to shortages of construction materials caused by toughened UN sanctions and COVID-era restrictions.[1] In July 2024, according to the official Korean Central News Agency, Kim toured the zone with top deputies and discussed preparations for an opening by May 2025, a date set by a ruling party meeting the previous January.[1] Press coverage pictured the North Korean leader grinning as he surveyed the plans and strolling along an immaculate white sand beach situated next to a rocket-testing site.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] presented the resort as an example of what he termed "Multipolar Devolution," a play on Jon Herold's [[devolution|devolution]] theory referring to the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]'s operations against globalist unipolar hegemony.[1] He framed the development as the fulfillment of a promise made by both Kim and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2018, when Kim pledged a "Major Change" to his country after the historic DMZ meeting at which Trump became the first sitting U.S. president to enter North Korea.[1]

Trump, described as a legendary real estate developer, had pledged to help the young leader transform the coastline into a tourist destination, telling reporters: "North Korea has great beaches. You see that whenever they're exploding cannons into the ocean. I said, 'Boy, look at that view. Wouldn't that make a great condo?'"[1] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry, who said he had worked in construction, real estate, and architecture for nearly 20 years, judged the beachfront to have "some of the best potential I've ever seen in a build site."[1] He tied the project to the memoirs of former South Korean president [[moon-jae-in|Moon Jae-in]], which claimed Kim had been "desperate" to rid North Korea of nuclear weapons since the Trump summit, concluding that "peace was always an option, and the carrot that will deliver it to the world is the promise of prosperity."[1]
