# Preemptive Pardons

**Preemptive pardons** are clemency grants issued to individuals who have not been charged with or convicted of any crime, shielding them in advance from prospective federal prosecution.[1]

## Biden's consideration in January 2025

On Friday, January 10, 2025, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] indicated he was still considering pre-emptive pardons for people whom President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had targeted, telling reporters at the [[white-house|White House]] that he was taking into account Trump's stated intentions for retribution.[1] "The idea that he would punish people ... is outrageous," Biden said, adding, "There's still consideration ... but no decision."[1] Trump had repeatedly called for the prosecution of his perceived enemies after defeating Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in 2024.[1]

In the same exchange, Biden rejected the suggestion that his decision to seek re-election had paved the way for Trump's victory, saying, "I think I would have beaten Trump, could have beaten Trump, and I think that Kamala could have beaten Trump."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the prospect as the outgoing administration pardoning itself, arguing that Biden "hasn't been in control, maybe ever" and that clemency for "anyone and everyone that might be impacted by the justice phase" amounted to self-protection.[1] She argued that no pardon could cover the offense of stealing an election, that the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] could not sustain such an act, and that truth and reconciliation for US elections must be pursued regardless.[1] She predicted that any such pardons would come during the week before the inauguration.[1]

## December 2024 White House deliberations

Reporting by [[politico|Politico]] on December 4, 2024 described a vigorous internal debate among President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s senior aides over whether to issue preemptive pardons to a range of current and former public officials who could be targeted after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s return to the White House.[2] Senior Democrats familiar with the discussions said alarm had accelerated after Trump announced the appointment of [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] to lead the [[fbi|FBI]]; Patel has publicly vowed to pursue Trump's critics.[2]

White House officials were described as carefully weighing the extraordinary step of handing out blanket pardons to people who had committed no crimes, both because it could suggest impropriety and thereby fuel Trump's criticisms, and because those offered preemptive pardons might reject them.[2] The deliberations covered officials currently in office, elected and appointed, as well as former officials who had angered Trump and his allies.[2] Names floated included members of Congress' [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] such as Senator-elect [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and former Representative [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]], whom Trump had said "should go to Jail along with the rest of the Unselect Committee," as well as [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]], the former head of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases.[2]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that the "pardon panic" displayed multi-track game theory, contending that the Patel nomination was as much narrative as actual and was the pick on which both sides of the information war had converged in advance.[2] The commentary held that after the pardon of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], preemptively offering pardons to figures such as Cheney and Fauci seeded the justice phase in the public mind and codified years of what had been dismissed as conspiracy narrative.[2] It further noted that recipients must accept a pardon, which the writer framed as a de facto admission of guilt in advance of any charge by the incoming Justice Department.[2]
