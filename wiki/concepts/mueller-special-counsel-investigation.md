# Mueller Special Counsel Investigation

The Mueller Special Counsel investigation was the federal inquiry into alleged collusion between the 2016 Trump campaign and Russia, launched after the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] counterintelligence operation was handed off to a special counsel headed by [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]].[1] In the Badlands Media reading advanced by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], the investigation was not what it appeared: it was an enemy operation that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] anticipated, welcomed, and ultimately turned against its authors through leverage, [[disinformation]], and deception.[1]

## Origins and the enemy's purpose

Lange argues that Trump expected Democrats to demand a special counsel and welcomed the demand, because foreknowledge of the opposition's "playbook" was a decisive advantage.[1] The investigation is described as serving three enemy purposes: acting as a "blockade" to prevent the truth about the origins of Russiagate from reaching the public, influencing the 2018 midterm elections so that Democrats could take the House and shut down inquiries into the Crossfire Hurricane conspiracy, and — most importantly — setting the table for Trump's impeachment and removal from office.[1]

When the Mueller Report was described as having "exonerated" Trump of collusion and obstruction, Lange writes, it cut the legs out from under that plan: the House impeached Trump anyway, but the Senate would never vote to remove him.[1] After more than two years of collusion allegations, the finding flipped the narrative and "the hunters were becoming the hunted."[1]

## Leverage over Mueller and Rosenstein

Central to the thesis is the claim that Trump leveraged both Mueller and [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]], who were connected through their participation in the cover-up of corruption benefiting [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] — a "protection racket."[1] Q drop 3 (October 29, 2017) named Rosenstein and Mueller as key players in the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] scandal, and Q drop 1704 stated flatly that if one was dirty the other must also be dirty.[1] Rather than call for their recusal, Lange argues, Trump allowed the enemy to place its pawns in full control of the special counsel, creating the opportunity to flip them.[1]

Q drop 2512 (November 30, 2018) said Mueller was "removed via DECLAS" and would face charges related to Uranium One while "working to save himself."[1] Lange reads this not as removal from the post but as removal of Mueller's power to pursue Trump; declassification exposed his role in covering up Obama-era crimes, and he was thereafter working to protect himself.[1]

## Giuliani, the "insurance file," and the April 2018 turn

[[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]] joined Trump's legal team in April 2018, telling reporters he wanted to "negotiate an end" to the probe.[1] Q drop 1180, posted the same day, referenced "Rudy. NYC. Relationships High. 'Insurance File.' Quiet until now… Direct discussions avail [now] w/ MUELLER."[1] Lange argues Giuliani had been deliberately held back until the moment was right, and that his New York law-enforcement relationships gave him non-public evidence — including material tied to the [[anthony-weiner|Anthony Weiner]] and [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] laptop, whose email trove reportedly reached back to Clinton's first months at the State Department and therefore touched Uranium One and Mueller's FBI tenure.[1]

Q drop 1273 (April 25, 2018) noted "Rudy met w/ MUELLER today" and asked whether this was the "End of POTUS investigation? Continue w/ other investigations?"[1] Two days later Q drop 1286 recorded that [[james-comey|Comey]], Rosenstein, and Mueller had each told Trump he was not being investigated and was "not a criminal target" — the opposite of the prevailing media narrative.[1] The investigation continued until March 2019, but in Lange's account it had already stopped targeting Trump and had turned toward other targets.[1]

## The Cohen raid and attorney–client privilege

The FBI raid on [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]]'s office and residence, ostensibly over the [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] hush-money payment, is presented as a deliberate part of the plan rather than an attack.[1] Q drop 1287 asked how one legally introduces evidence into an investigation and whether material is "admissible in the court of law," immediately followed by "Insert Rudy."[1] Trump amplified the raid publicly, tweeting "Attorney-client privilege is dead!", which Lange reads as a deliberate effort to set [[attorney-client-privilege|precedent]] destroying that defense for future presidents.[1] Mueller subsequently handed the Cohen matter to [[geoffrey-berman|Geoffrey Berman]] at the [[southern-district-of-new-york|SDNY]], enabling a legal "hand-off" of Giuliani's evidence into ongoing criminal investigations.[1]

## Redirection toward the Podestas

Q drop 1286 also asked why the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]] "closed shop," which Lange treats as the answer to who the special counsel had begun targeting.[1] NBC News reported that [[tony-podesta|Tony Podesta]] and the Podesta Group were subjects of a Mueller-led federal investigation that grew out of the inquiry into the finances of [[paul-manafort|Paul Manafort]].[1] Manafort's indictment identified the Podesta Group as "Company B," hired to lobby for a nonprofit ultimately directed by the Ukrainian president and government — Ukrainian, not Russian, connections.[1] The firm shut its doors after thirty years within days of the indictment.[1]

## Assessment

Lange concludes that the special counsel investigation, intended to end the Trump presidency, "turned into the biggest BOOMERANG in history," leaving the enemy confused when the report exonerated Trump.[1] Q drop 4373 framed the surrounding environment as "the greatest [coordinated] disinformation campaign to ever be launched against the American people," an [[information-war|information war]] and irregular-warfare insurgency.[1] These claims reflect the interpretive framework of Badlands Media contributors and are not established fact.

## The "boomerang" reading

Badlands Media's article "Do You Believe in Coincidences?" presents the investigation as a "blockade" installed by the president's opponents, intended to supply the evidentiary basis for impeachment and removal on obstruction-of-justice charges, and to influence the 2018 midterm elections.[2] Its original mandate, assigned by Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]], was to investigate "any links and/or coordination between the Russian government and individuals associated with the campaign of President Donald Trump."[2] The article says Rosenstein later inserted "special articles" — a second version of the scope document — that expanded the inquiry into a fishing expedition over [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s finances and children, work associated with prosecutor [[andrew-weissmann|Andrew Weissmann]].[2]

Acting Attorney General [[matthew-whitaker|Matthew Whitaker]] is credited with removing those articles and forcing the investigation back to its narrow original mandate, and Attorney General [[william-barr|William Barr]], who assumed supervision on February 14, 2019, with shutting it down entirely on March 22, 2019.[2] The final 448-page report found no evidence of collusion between Russia and Trump or his associates; the convictions [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] secured against a handful of Trump associates concerned unrelated charges.[2] [[carter-page|Carter Page]], the nominal target whose surveillance produced the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|FISA warrants]], was never indicted.[2]

Barr's release of a self-written summary ahead of the full report allowed Trump to declare "complete and total exoneration," which the article characterises as the deliberate destruction of the opposing narrative.[2] Rosenstein signed off on that summary.[2] Badlands concludes that what appeared to be a trap for Trump was in fact "the biggest sting operation in history," exposing participants in [[spygate|Spygate]] and [[obamagate|Obamagate]] rather than the president.[2]
