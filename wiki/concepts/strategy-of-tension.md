# Strategy of Tension

The "Strategy of Tension" is a term for a destabilization doctrine in which violence, unrest, and fear are deliberately fostered or amplified within a target society so that the resulting chaos can be steered toward a desired political outcome — typically the discrediting of an existing government and the legitimation of a replacement.[1]

## Application to Iran, January 2026

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked the concept while analyzing the January 2026 unrest in [[iran|Iran]], suggesting that observers were "about to get a public crash course on the Strategy of Tension and regime change operations."[1] The framing was applied to a situation in which Iranian authorities intensified a crackdown as protests spread, cut nationwide internet access, and accused the United States and [[israel|Israel]] of interfering in Iran's internal affairs — allegations neither government confirmed nor denied.[1]

Elements cited as consistent with the pattern included circulating videos of rioters attacking security guards, the Iranian president's enumeration of destroyed property such as ambulances and hundreds of mosques, reported [[mossad|Mossad]] encouragement of protesters, and the posture of exiled claimant [[reza-pahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]], who characterized reported protester deaths as a necessary cost of a war to "liberate" Iran.[1]

## Analytical significance

Within the Badlands framework, the Strategy of Tension supplies a lens for reading street violence not as a spontaneous expression of popular will but as an instrument in an externally shaped contest over legitimacy — a reading applied in parallel to the domestic anti-ICE protest wave organized by [[indivisible|Indivisible]] in the same period.[1]
