# 2026 Los Angeles Mayoral Election

The 2026 Los Angeles mayoral election is a nonpartisan contest whose June primary sent incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and City Council member [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]] to a November runoff, eliminating former reality television personality [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]].[1] Voters chose from among 14 candidates.[1]

## The primary

Raman entered the race shortly before the filing deadline and campaigned on housing affordability, homelessness, and city services.[1] Pratt's campaign emphasized homelessness, crime, and dissatisfaction with city government, and drew national attention because of his celebrity and his criticism of city leadership after the Palisades Fire.[1] Pre-primary public polling showed all three competing closely; vote counting left Pratt outside the top two.[1]

## Pre-primary campaign (May 2026)

In the weeks before the primary, [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] attracted national coverage for using power washers and stencils to blast clean slogans — "IMAGINE IF THE STREETS WERE THIS CLEAN" and "SPENCER PRATT FOR MAYOR" — into filthy city sidewalks, a tactic framed by Modernity News as a guerrilla campaign ad against [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]].[2] Contemporary coverage showed Pratt as a genuine factor in the race, with polling and fundraising momentum placing him in serious contention for a runoff; Newsweek reported he was dominating fundraising, and a *New York Times*-cited poll put him within three points of Bass, though from a private research firm with no sponsor disclosed.[2] Los Angeles establishment figures pushed back, with Drew Carey calling Pratt a "serial scammer" and critics attacking his residency, hotel spending, and MAGA-adjacent support.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the cycle as a "fake election" that was nonetheless entertaining, arguing that what captures attention in it is "a bit depressing in its reflection of the American people and their collective mind."[2]

## Badlands interpretation

For [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], the result served as proof of a thesis rather than a surprise. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], citing [[canncon|CannCon]], noted that Los Angeles is the only jurisdiction in the United States still using [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] equipment, and used the race as an entry point into a detailed history of Smartmatic, [[sequoia-voting-systems|Sequoia]], [[election-systems-software|ES&S]], [[premier-election-solutions|Premier]], and [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion]].[1] Her conclusion was that "Spencer Pratt and Smartmatic in LA in 2026 might be about a lot more than who is going to run the City of Angels."[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the primary as "the latest, most cartoonish exhibition in a long and accelerating line of successful attempts to subvert American democracy in real time and in full public view."[1] He identified familiar mechanics: a mail-in system with extended windows, results that "evolve" over days and weeks, and "the same counting choreography that has defined contested cycles since 2020."[1] What had changed, he argued, was only the brazenness, occurring under a sitting president who has spent years calling out the same machine.[1] He noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "has called the theft what it is" and that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] "has amplified his signal," while the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] deployed its standard script of "unsubstantiated," "normal process," and "conspiracy theory."[1]

Burning Bright further argued that the fraud "does not require every race to flip; it only requires that enough races remain controllable," that enough citizens keep participating in the ritual, and that enough voices in "awake" spaces keep policing the boundaries of acceptable skepticism.[1] He framed the primary as a dress rehearsal for the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[1]

## Federal voter fraud investigation

In June 2026, federal agents opened an operation on Los Angeles' Skid Row as part of an investigation into alleged voter fraud connected to the mayoral election and the concurrent gubernatorial primary races.[3] Roughly twenty agents, including personnel from [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]], interviewed dozens of homeless residents in the district, and [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] amplified the operation publicly.[3] Allegations that surfaced after Election Day included videos in which Skid Row residents said they had been paid cash to sign registration forms, forge signatures, and complete voter information, and that they had been encouraged to support [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]] while [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] was omitted from the candidates promoted to them.[3]

The timeline assembled by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] ran from the Department of Justice's May 18, 2026 indictment of signature collector [[brenda-lee-brown-armstrong|Brenda Lee Brown Armstrong]], through the June 2 primary and the June 7 announcement that Pratt had "lost" the jungle primary — a call that prompted even mainstream observers to allege fraud — to [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]]'s June 8 re-posting of his March undercover footage.[3] She asked openly whether the mayoral election had been a sting operation, and warned readers about "fraud apologists" arguing the scheme was "not enough to overturn the outcome."[3] Details of the probe are collected at [[skid-row-voter-fraud-investigation|Skid Row voter fraud investigation]].[3]

## Mail ballot reversal and fraud claims

Early returns on election night showed Raman trailing [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] by roughly eight points; as additional mail ballots were counted over the following days the margin reversed, positioning Raman to potentially advance to a runoff against incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]].[4] Registered Democrats outnumber Republicans nearly three-to-one in Los Angeles County, and late mail ballots have consistently shifted results toward Democratic candidates in recent California elections.[4] California permanently adopted automatic mailing of ballots to all active registered voters in 2021 after expanding the practice during the COVID pandemic, and ballots postmarked by Election Day remain eligible if received within seven days, producing a slow multi-day count.[4]

Trump administration officials and Republican allies pointed to the delayed counting and late swings as signs of potential fraud, and the state's top federal prosecutor in Los Angeles said his office had multiple election-fraud investigations underway while accusing California of blocking a federal audit of its voter rolls.[4] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and congressional Republicans used the episode to renew calls for the [[save-act|SAVE Act]] and stricter voter identification.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the race as a live demonstration of the [[red-mirage|Red Mirage]] pattern being rehearsed ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterms]].[4]
