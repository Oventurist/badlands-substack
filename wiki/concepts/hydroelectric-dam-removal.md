# Hydroelectric Dam Removal

Hydroelectric dam removal refers to the policy, described critically in Badlands Media commentary, of demolishing existing dams across North America and Europe in order to "liberate the rivers" and "restore the deserts" to what advocates regard as their natural condition.[1]

## Rationale and criticism

The practice is presented as the practical outgrowth of the belief that it was a mistake to use science and technology to populate the American west, and that the great dams of Franklin Roosevelt's New Deal — which made the Imperial Valley possible — are an abomination of nature.[1] Matthew Ehret characterises the resulting demolitions as the systematic destruction of hydroelectric capacity intended to return river systems to a "supposedly 'natural' dead status."[1]

## Scale

In California alone, half a billion dollars was spent by Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] in 2023–2024 to destroy four strategically important dams on the Klamath in the name of saving nature from humans.[1] In 2021 the Democratic Party allocated the largest budget yet for demolishing hydroelectric dams, 56 in total, and the same logic has produced hundreds of dam demolitions across Europe.[1]

The removals compound the wider [[north-american-water-crisis|North American water crisis]], in which no significant new hydroelectric construction has occurred for decades and aging dams that supply power, flood control and water management are decaying.[1] Ehret treats the removal campaign as an expression of [[zero-sum-thinking|zero-sum thinking]] and as the opposite of the development paradigm embodied by the [[north-american-water-and-power-alliance|North American Water and Power Alliance]].[1]
