# Lewis v. United States

*Lewis v. United States*, 680 F.2d 1239 (9th Cir. 1982), is a decision of the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit cited by Badlands Media as federal case law establishing that the Federal Reserve Banks are private corporations rather than agencies of the United States government.[1]

## Holding as quoted

A February 16, 2023 Badlands Media essay reproduces the court's language: "Examining the organization and function of the Federal Reserve Banks, and applying the relevant factors, we conclude that the Reserve Banks are not federal instrumentalities for purposes of the FTCA (Federal Tort Claims Act), but are independent, privately owned and locally controlled corporations… The Banks are neither listed as 'wholly owned' government corporations under 31 U.S.C. 846 nor as 'mixed ownership' corporations under 31 U.S.C. 856."[1]

## Use in the Badlands argument

The essay pairs the decision with Point 4 of Section 4 of the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]], which provides that each Federal Reserve Bank is to be regarded as a body corporate, to argue that [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] is not a branch of government in the sense most Americans assume and is not an organ of the Treasury or a department of the legislative or executive branches.[1] Federal case law and congressional records together, the article concludes, attest that the Federal Reserve system is a corporate banking system, so skepticism on that point can no longer be entertained.[1] The essay treats this, however, as a surface-level detail betraying a deeper problem in America's financial and legal sectors.[1]
