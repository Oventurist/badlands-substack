# Caffeine and Cardiometabolic Health Study

In September 2024, research reported by [[cnn|CNN]] found that moderate caffeine intake was associated with a lower risk of developing cardiometabolic multimorbidity.[1]

## Findings

The study's lead author, Dr. Chaofu Ke, associate professor of epidemiology and biostatistics at Soochow University in Suzhou, China, defined moderate intake as roughly three cups of coffee or tea a day.[1] Cardiometabolic multimorbidity, or CM, is the coexistence of at least two cardiometabolic diseases such as coronary heart disease, stroke and type 2 diabetes.[1] Ke said that "coffee and caffeine consumption may play an important protective role in almost all phases of CM development."[1] Researchers analyzed data from about 180,000 people in the UK Biobank, a large long-term biomedical database, none of whom had cardiometabolic diseases at the outset.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the study to mock the oscillating character of nutritional science reporting—"Wine is bad, wine is good. Eggs are bad, eggs are good. Coffee is bad, coffee is good"—and the injunction to "trust the science and the experts."[1] She noted that the scientific and media establishment was already hedging and warning readers not to over-trust the appealing result.[1]
