# 2024 Hezbollah Casualty Claims Against Israel

The **2024 Hezbollah casualty claims against Israel** were battlefield loss figures published by [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]'s Operations Room on October 23, 2024, covering the first three weeks of Israel's ground campaign in southern Lebanon.[1]

## The claims

Hezbollah stated that its fighters had killed more than 70 Israeli army soldiers and officers in southern Lebanon and wounded more than 600.[1] The statement said 28 Merkava tanks, four military bulldozers, an armed vehicle and an armored troop carrier had been destroyed by the Lebanese resistance.[1] The toll was said to exclude Israeli losses at military bases, sites and barracks in the north and in the depth of what the group called occupied Palestine.[1]

The statement asserted that [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli forces]] had been unable to establish control over or occupy any villages, advancing only short distances into southern Lebanese villages given the intensity of resistance operations, and it detailed operations along several axes of the Israeli advance.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the figure of 28 tanks in three weeks aligned with reporting from Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]], Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]], [[scott-ritter|Scott Ritter]] and others on the Lebanon conflict.[1] For scale, he noted that in the first four years of the Iraq War 80 U.S. tanks were "put out of action" by enemy attacks, of which 63 were repaired and returned to the battlefield and only 17 counted as destroyed.[1]

He concluded that prior reports of an IDF blitzkrieg-style invasion plan appeared accurate, and that Hezbollah had been trained to defend against blitzkrieg — most plausibly by the Russian military, which he described as present on the ground in Syria alongside Hezbollah and advising Tehran in [[iran|Iran]], and as the world's most accomplished military on that subject following its Second World War experience against Germany.[1] He added that if Macgregor's other statements were accurate, the IDF faced a manpower shortage that would soon prevent it from continuing the war, and expressed concern about what [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] might do with his back against the wall.[1] He characterized the overall storyline as "an Arab militia defeat[ing] an invading Jewish army that is deploying [failed] Third Reich military tactics."[1]
