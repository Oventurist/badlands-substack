# 2025 US–China Geneva Tariff Agreement

The 2025 US–China Geneva tariff agreement was a temporary 90-day de-escalation of the trade war between the world's two largest economies, announced on Monday, May 12, 2025.[1] The joint statement was released in Geneva, where American and [[china|Chinese]] officials had negotiated over the preceding weekend.[1]

## Terms

Under the agreement, the United States would reduce tariffs on Chinese goods from 145% to 30%, while China would lower duties on US imports from 125% to 10%, effective 14 May and lasting for 90 days.[1] Taken together the deal cut reciprocal tariffs by 115 percentage points.[1]

US Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said at a news briefing after two days of talks that the prior high tariff levels would have amounted to a complete blockage of each side's goods, an outcome neither side wanted.[1] "The consensus from both delegations this weekend is neither side wants a decoupling," Bessent said, describing the previous tariff levels as "the equivalent of an embargo" and adding, "We do want trade. We want more balanced trade."[1]

## Market reaction

Wall Street rallied massively on the news: the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 2.8%, or more than 1,100 points, the Nasdaq rose 4.8%, and the S&P 500 gained 3.3%.[1] Analysts also attributed part of the move to President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order on drug prices, which analysts said would raise drug prices overseas to deliver savings for Americans.[1]

Asia-Pacific markets traded mixed the following Tuesday.[1] Analysts at the Japanese investment bank Nomura upgraded Chinese equities to a "tactical overweight," with Asia-Pacific equity strategist Chetan Seth writing that the reduction "came as a significant surprise for markets, and will likely support risk positivity in the near term" and was "much larger than expected."[1] Hong Kong stocks nonetheless fell sharply, with the Hang Seng Index down 1.74% and the Hang Seng Tech Index down 3.06%, while mainland China's CSI 300 was flat.[1] Indian markets fell at the open, the Nifty 50 down 0.34% and the BSE Sensex down 0.39%, reversing the previous session's best one-day gain since February 2021 that had followed optimism over the India–Pakistan ceasefire.[1] In Japan the Nikkei 225 surged 1.71% and the broader Topix advanced 1.28%.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentary treated the Geneva talks skeptically, with [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] questioning why anyone would "go to Geneva to talk to 'China'" and arguing the episode only makes sense if one recognizes that the adversary is not China but "the people in Geneva."[1] He noted the near-identical set of Trump-era outcomes in 2020 and 2025 — a trade deal with China, a deal with Xi to stop the fentanyl trade, and a plan for TikTok — as evidence the conflict was staged.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the episode as the US and China "pausing their kayfabe trade war."[1]
