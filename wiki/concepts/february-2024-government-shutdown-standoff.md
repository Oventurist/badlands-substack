# February 2024 Government Shutdown Standoff

The February 2024 government shutdown standoff was a fight over federal appropriations in which lawmakers raced to avoid a partial shutdown ahead of a funding deadline that Friday, after congressional leaders failed to reach a deal over the preceding weekend and began trading blame.[1]

## Course of the standoff

Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] announced on Sunday, February 25, 2024, that congressional leaders had not yet reached agreement on compromise spending bills, blaming House Republicans for the delay.[1] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] shot back, placing the onus on "new Democrat demands" in the negotiations.[1]

The Senate reconvened Monday and House lawmakers returned to Washington on Wednesday, leaving only a handful of days to resolve differences and approve appropriations bills or clear another short-term stopgap.[1] Four spending measures were due that Friday, with the remaining eight due by March 8.[1] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] hosted the top four congressional leaders at the White House on Tuesday to discuss the funding deadline and the Senate-passed foreign aid package awaiting action in the House.[1]

## White House meeting and optimism

Congressional leaders emerged from their Tuesday, February 27, 2024 meeting with President [[joe-biden|Biden]] saying they were optimistic about averting a partial government shutdown at the end of the week, but they remained short of a concrete plan before the Friday deadline.[2]

Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], under intense pressure from Biden, Democrats and Senate Republicans to agree to a spending deal over the fierce objections of right-wing lawmakers in his own ranks, suggested he might be ready to do so in the coming days.[2] "We have been working in good faith around the clock every single day, for months and weeks, and over the last several days, quite literally around the clock, to get that job done," Johnson said after meeting the president one on one following a group session with Biden and the other three top congressional leaders.[2] "We're very optimistic," he added, calling the prevention of a shutdown "our first responsibility."[2] Senate Minority Leader [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] offered a similarly upbeat assessment at the Capitol, saying "We are making some real headway on the appropriations process."[2]

## Late-February brinkmanship

By February 21, 2024, with a two-phased shutdown set to begin absent a budget or stopgap by March 1, House Republicans were reportedly preparing behind closed doors for failure even as Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] publicly maintained that the deadlines would be met.[3] One GOP lawmaker told Axios, "People are predicting a shutdown even if it's just for a few days," while a subcommittee chair characterized negotiations as either "close to reaching a deal or it's about to blow up."[3] A separate trigger set for April 30 would impose a 1% across-the-board spending cut if no new budget were agreed, a prospect palatable to some House conservatives and therefore a hard ceiling on Democratic support for any stopgap extending past that date.[3]

The structural bind was widely noted: Johnson could either force a shutdown fight with Democrats or pass a stopgap with Democratic votes, the very move that led to [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s ouster.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the more likely outcome was a Republican cave funding a national security supplemental spanning Ukraine, Israel/Gaza, Taiwan, and Sudan, alongside continued domestic spending announcements from Biden surrogates.[3]
