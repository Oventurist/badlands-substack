# Israel–Hamas War

The Israel–Hamas war that erupted in October 2023 is treated by Badlands Media as "the latest narrative in the [[war-of-stories|war of stories]]," serving multiple strategic purposes simultaneously: fuelling the war economy, dividing American society, and providing a pretext to bolster the Western censorship regime.[1]

## The manufactured binary

The article's central claim is that establishment forces want the public hungry for the war and "might not even care which side you are on so long as you do pick a side."[1] What it calls the master psychologists of the deep political establishment are described as masters of creating binaries — in this case, support Israel or support Palestine — and the system will attempt to pigeonhole even those who recognise the trap, on the logic that "if you're not against [[hamas|Hamas]] then you are with them," and vice versa.[1]

## Elite consensus versus public opinion

The permanent political class was in total agreement that the United States should back Israel, despite a majority of American voters opposing US support and many fearing that involvement would lead to broader conflict.[1] Badlands Media frames this as a failure of representation traceable to the donor class and particularly the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]].[1]

## Fracture on the right

[[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] delivered an opening monologue taking an America First line: the attacks were tragic, but did not necessarily require any US action.[1] He played clips of [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] urging Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to "finish them," and of [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] proposing a new war with Iran by bombing its four major oil refineries — positions Badlands Media read as indifferent to collateral damage.[1] The monologue also criticised Texas Congressman [[dan-crenshaw|Dan Crenshaw]] for tweeting that this would be "the war to end all wars," an idea the article calls absurd on the grounds that wars only beget more war.[1] In an interview with [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], Carlson argued the southern border crisis merited more immediate federal attention than a crisis in a far away land — a statement that sent [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] into what the article describes as a full-blown freak-out.[1]

## Fracture on the left

The left fractured too. Badlands Media noted a growing contingent of left-leaning pundits becoming increasingly anti-establishment, exemplified by [[briahna-joy-gray|Briahna Joy Gray]], whose heated exchange with [[rising|Rising]] co-host [[robby-soave|Robby Soave]] over her criticism of the Israeli government became a flashpoint.[1] [[dennis-kucinich|Dennis Kucinich]] stepped down from running [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]'s presidential campaign, a split suspected to stem from irreconcilable differences over Israel–Palestine.[1]

## US policy and blowback

The [[biden-administration|Biden administration]] deployed much of the same rhetoric used to justify support for Ukraine, with multibillion-dollar support packages and access to major military assets expected to follow.[1] The United States was the only nation on the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] to veto a resolution calling for humanitarian pauses to deliver lifesaving aid to millions in Gaza.[1] Badlands Media notes this was not the first such veto — the pattern has held for decades and has produced a perception across the Muslim world that the US is Israel's partner and enabler.[1] Mass protests in Lebanon, Turkey, Yemen, Saudi Arabia and Iran blamed the United States as much as Israel.[1]

## Historical analogy

To assess whether backing Israel would make America safer, the article invites readers to name a post-WWII conflict in which US government actions were not directly responsible for the other nation's hostility.[1] It cites the suppression of [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]] interviews after 9/11 as an attempt to prevent Americans from learning that groups like [[al-qaeda|al-Qaeda]] and the [[taliban|Taliban]] were motivated by US violence in the region rather than by American social freedoms.[1]

## Conclusions

Badlands Media argues that even apparent dissenters such as [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] and "the squad" ultimately serve the same end of building public support for war.[1] The author contends that a peaceful solution to the conflict was never seriously on the table and that events are "right on schedule for an endgame that most folks will never see coming," speculating that the conflict is the beginning of an attempt to foment World War III.[1] The piece closes by urging vigilance and reminding readers that "there is always room on the side of peace."[1]
