# Ballot Harvesting

Ballot harvesting is the practice by which completed absentee ballots are collected by a third party and delivered to a polling office or election authority on the voter's behalf. The practice is strictly prohibited in at least six US states, and many other states impose partial limits on who may submit a ballot other than the voter.[1]

## Legal status

Because election administration is governed state by state, the legality of third-party ballot collection varies widely. Some states have no policy restricting the practice, while others — including Pennsylvania and Alabama — have statutes that expressly prohibit it. Mass collection of ballots by an organization therefore risks violating the law in some jurisdictions even where the same activity is lawful in others.[1]

## 2024 Israel case

Ahead of the November 5, 2024 US presidential election, a [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] investigation reported a "potentially illegal" ballot-harvesting operation in central Israel run by [[ivoteisrael|iVoteIsrael]], a group that promotes absentee voting by American immigrants living in Israel. The report raised the prospect that ballots gathered en masse abroad could have been returned to states where such collection is barred.[1]

Badlands Media commentary treated the episode as a sovereignty question rather than a partisan one, asking readers why an overseas harvesting operation aimed at a US election would be treated with more indulgence than the same conduct by any other foreign country.[1]

## Related election-integrity themes

Discussion of ballot harvesting in Badlands coverage sits alongside broader arguments about the structural vulnerability of US election systems, including [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]]'s call for the incoming administration to reform election systems outright rather than rely on layered "defenses in depth" to protect results.[1]

## Lara Trump's 2024 pledge

In February 2024, ahead of her election as co-chair of the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], [[lara-trump|Lara Trump]] told [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]]: "We need to have the biggest legal ballot harvesting operation this country has ever seen."[2] The report noted that ballot harvesting is legal in many states, allowing people to deliver other voters' absentee or mail-in ballots to election officials, that some fear the practice facilitates election fraud, and that Republicans have long frowned upon it while Democrats have taken advantage of it.[2] Of Democratic practice she said, "Whether or not they do it legally, that's up for discussion," arguing it was time for Republicans to start "attacking the game differently."[2]

## Georgia 2020 allegations

[[true-the-vote|True the Vote]] presented Georgia state investigators with data in May 2021 that it said showed organized ballot harvesting, including "279 cellphones which had made multiple trips to within 100 feet of a voter drop box" and geolocation links between those phones and various organizations.[3] Then-[[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|GBI]] Director [[vic-reynolds|Vic Reynolds]] wrote in a follow-up letter that the data, "while curious," did not rise to probable cause absent witness statements or named potential defendants.[3]

Former U.S. Senator [[david-perdue|David Perdue]] testified before the Fulton County special grand jury that Reynolds had described the evidence — video, cell phone records, testimony and corroborated bank records — as compelling enough to investigate, but declined to open a case because, in Perdue's account, "if the governor doesn't want to investigate, we're not going to investigate."[3] Perdue said two whistleblowers had come forward on Georgia ballot harvesting, one with bank statements documenting payments for the activity, and that Reynolds said the matter could not proceed without the witness.[3]

## Keshel's "actions on the objective"

Election analyst [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] described ballot harvesting as "the most important condition of mail-ballot fraud — actions on the objective," and said his election forecasts now weight it alongside [[automatic-voter-registration|Automatic Voter Registration]] and universal or excessive [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]], noting that its legality varies by state.[4] In his April 2025 essay comparing elections to casino games, he grouped harvesting with drop boxes and Nevada-style universal mail voting as house advantages, and warned that mixing in [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice voting]] as Alaska and Maine do compounds the effect.[4]

## Professional collectors and the time variable

In a February 13, 2025 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] guest essay, [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] listed the legalization or non-penalization of ballot harvesting as a distinct stage in his sequence of manual election corruption, following corrupted [[voter-registration|voter registration]] and the expansion of [[mail-in-voting|mail-in balloting]] toward the universal model.[5] He described the next stage as the hiring of professional ballot collectors to gather mail ballots, especially those landing at designated locations with no real recipient but known to collection agents through compromised and shared data.[5] The final ingredient, he wrote, is time: more [[early-voting|early voting]] gives harvesters more opportunity to collect and turn in every assigned ballot in a precinct.[5] Ballot harvesting is one of his [[eight-cardinal-sins|Eight Cardinal Sins]] of election administration.[5]
