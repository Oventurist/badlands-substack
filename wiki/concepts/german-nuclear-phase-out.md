# German Nuclear Phase-Out

The German nuclear phase-out refers to [[germany|Germany]]'s decision to permanently shut down its civilian nuclear power sector, culminating in April 2023 when the country took its last three nuclear power plants offline — including the reactor at Neckarwestheim.[1] A year later it appeared clear that the plants would never be brought back on line.[1]

## Context: a global nuclear renaissance

The German decision ran counter to a broader global trend. Uranium prices were soaring as countries snapped up nuclear fuel, public support for nuclear power in the United States reached a ten-year high, Russia was expanding its nuclear energy footprint in emerging African economies, and Japan was returning to the carbon-free source thirteen years after the Fukushima disaster.[1] Approximately 60 new reactors were under construction worldwide with another 110 in planning stages.[1]

## Energy-security implications

Germany's stance was described as surprising given that the nation is an outspoken proponent of the green energy transition, yet chose to eliminate one of its most reliable forms of carbon-free generation ahead of transitioning away from coal, the dirtiest fossil fuel.[1] The phase-out also came at a time of acute concern about German energy security: critics had warned for years that Europe, and Germany in particular, was dangerously reliant on Russian energy imports, a warning borne out when sanctions following Russia's February 2022 invasion of Ukraine plunged the continent into an energy crisis.[1] Germany depended on Russia for roughly 50 percent of its natural gas supplies at the time of the invasion.[1]

Rather than extending the life of its nuclear fleet, Germany opted to spend billions on new domestic natural gas plants augmented by significant renewable expansion, and to fall back on coal when supplies run short — a course many energy and climate experts found baffling.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the shutdown left the de facto leader of the EU "all in" on less efficient alternatives including natural gas, solar and wind, and predicted catastrophic effects for the German people in the years ahead.[1] He noted that solar and wind had proven unable to handle grid loads while remaining vulnerable to disruption and costly to maintain, and that as the United States, Russia, China and Global South nations pursued a nuclear renaissance, the EU risked being left behind — a liability [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had pointed out during his presidential world tours.[1]
