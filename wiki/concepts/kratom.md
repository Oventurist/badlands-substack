# Kratom and 7-OH Scheduling

Kratom is a plant whose ground leaves are sold in the United States as a dietary supplement marketed to boost energy or provide pain relief.[1] In July 2025 the compound 7-hydroxymitragynine — commonly abbreviated 7-OH — became the subject of a federal scheduling recommendation, placing kratom-derived products at the center of a drug-policy dispute.[1]

## The FDA recommendation

On Tuesday, July 29, 2025, [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] Commissioner [[marty-makary|Marty Makary]] announced that the Trump administration was recommending that 7-OH be scheduled as an illicit drug.[1] FDA officials said the compound carries a "high risk of addiction" and abuse because of its ability to bind to opioid receptors in the brain.[1]

7-OH occurs in only trace amounts in the kratom plant itself; the agency's action is aimed at products manufactured with concentrated levels of the compound rather than at conventional leaf kratom.[1] Makary said the agency was urging the [[drug-enforcement-administration|Drug Enforcement Administration]] to classify 7-OH as a Schedule 1 substance, the category that includes heroin, lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) and ecstasy.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands host [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] called the move "good news," describing 7-OH as a highly abused, synthesized alkaloid derived from the kratom plant.[1] He cautioned, however, that there is considerable nuance to kratom and that most people do not understand what a plant alkaloid is: kratom can be helpful in the right time and place, while carrying real addiction potential, and not all kratom products contain 7-OH.[1] Sather pointed readers to a Huberman Lab episode featuring a PhD scientist who studies kratom as a source describing those distinctions.[1]
