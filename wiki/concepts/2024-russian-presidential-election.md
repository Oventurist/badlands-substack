# 2024 Russian Presidential Election

The 2024 Russian presidential election, held over three days in March 2024, returned [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to the presidency for a fifth term with a reported 87% of the vote.[1]

## Results and turnout

Putin's 87% share, reported by an exit poll broadcast on state television shortly after voting closed, exceeded the previous high of 77% he received in 2018.[1] Three other candidates, all drawn from parties loyal to the Kremlin, received no more than 5% support.[1] Nearly 4.5 million people voted online through a system used in 29 of Russia's regions for the first time in a presidential election, according to the Interfax news service citing government data.[1]

Preliminary turnout reported by the Central Election Commission and displayed on state television was 74.22% — the highest in more than three decades and the highest since Boris Yeltsin became president in 1991 after the Soviet Union's collapse, well above the 67.5% recorded in 2018.[1] At least six Russian regions claimed turnout above 90%.[1] The commission also reported that, after processing 100% of protocols, Putin received 94.12% of the vote in the Lugansk People's Republic in the contested Donetsk region.[1]

## Significance

The result extends Putin's nearly quarter-century rule; completing the six-year term at age 77 would make him Russia's longest-serving ruler, surpassing Joseph Stalin.[1] The vote came as Russian forces pressed their advantage in the third year of the invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]] — Europe's largest conflict since World War II — while Ukraine struggled to supply its forces with munitions amid delays in military aid from its US and European allies.[1] ZeroHedge characterized the outcome as largely pre-determined "even without mystery sacks of mail-in ballots arriving at 3am."[1]
