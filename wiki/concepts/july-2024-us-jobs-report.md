# July 2024 US Jobs Report

The July 2024 US jobs report, released at 8:30 a.m. ET on Friday, August 2, 2024, was a monthly employment release that abruptly reset market expectations about the health of the American labor market and triggered a broad sell-off in equities.[1]

## Contents of the report

The report showed the US economy added 114,000 jobs in July, far fewer than the roughly 176,000 economists had expected.[1] The unemployment rate rose to 4.3% from 4.1% the previous month; as recently as April it had stood at 3.9%, capping the longest stretch below 4% in decades.[1] The weakness of the release tipped market sentiment and sent stocks into what was described as meltdown mode, with many investors deciding it was time to panic.[1]

## Interpretation and Federal Reserve context

Commentators cautioned that a single data point was not a sound basis for changing an entire narrative, but treated the report as a wake-up call that danger was closer than many had assumed.[1] The labor market had in fact been weakening for some time, and no clear catalyst existed to reverse the trend.[1] Because the release came only days after the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] held interest rates steady rather than cutting them, it also fueled fears that the central bank was behind the curve and that a recession might be approaching.[1] Guy Berger, director of economic research at the Burning Glass Institute, summarized the shift: "There was a lot of data that was sort of in the greenish area, and now there's a lot more data that's flashing yellow lights."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the most implausible element of the mainstream coverage was its premise that everyone had believed the economy was strong before the release, noting that the same reporting recounted months of deteriorating metrics.[1] The commentary rejected the claim that the United States was not yet in recession, contending instead that a recession began in late 2021 and had been obscured by manipulated metrics and redefined terms.[1] It concluded that responsibility for provisioning one's family is individual, that resilience should be the primary goal, and that "the economy is a house of cards, and the wind is blowing."[1]
