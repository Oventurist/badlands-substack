# Badlands Brief: Somali Shatterpoints, Cartel Countermeasures, & Erosion of the World Order

"Somali Shatterpoints, Cartel Countermeasures, & Erosion of the World Order" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily news-and-commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack. Dated January 9, 2026, and recorded with 162 likes, the edition pairs summarized wire reporting with signed commentary from the Badlands contributor collective — [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]. The Brief describes itself as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war."

The issue covers five main segments — US cartel-strike rhetoric toward Mexico and Colombia, unrest in Minneapolis after a fatal immigration-enforcement shooting, protests and an internet blackout in Iran, expectations of Federal Reserve rate cuts, and European complaints that Washington is eroding the postwar international order — followed by two bonus items on federal prosecutorial appointments and a new fraud-focused Justice role.

## Trump's cartel strike comments

The Brief reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters on Tuesday that the United States may begin conducting land strikes against drug cartels operating in [[mexico|Mexico]], expanding on earlier statements about using military force against transnational criminal organizations. Trump characterized cross-border cartel drug operations as a direct national security threat. According to the summary, Mexican officials warned against any violation of national sovereignty, while in [[colombia|Colombia]] President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] criticized US military actions in the region following Washington's recent operation involving [[venezuela|Venezuela]]. No formal military orders or timelines had been announced. Politico is cited for the claim that Trump's rhetoric has put Mexico diplomatically on edge.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that he has argued for months that Petro is "the righteous actor," having spent three years pursuing the cartels, and points to a video Petro posted to X recapping Colombian operations. He notes that Trump posted to Truth Social on Wednesday night that he had spoken with Petro by phone and invited him to the [[white-house|White House]] for a face-to-face meeting — prompting the commentator's remark, "I guess it all was kayfabe after all, huh?"

The commentary recalls that a year earlier Petro disclosed that the [[national-liberation-army|National Liberation Army]] (ELN), active in the region since 1963 and historically linked to [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]] and the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín Cartel]], was in fact a proxy of the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]] dispatched to secure Central American cocaine smuggling routes. The author objects that "NeoCons and Zionists" have accused [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] of heading the ELN without supporting evidence.

On Mexico specifically, the author says he believes Trump "is speaking the truth," calling Mexico an enigma. He describes former president [[andres-manuel-lopez-obrador|Andrés Manuel López Obrador]] as "a hardcore commie" who nonetheless respected Trump and deployed 29,000 Mexican troops to the border. Current president [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]], Obrador's purported protégé, "seems to sing a different tune"; the author senses Trump's frustration given the absence of a finalized bilateral trade deal, and recalls that in May Trump accused Sheinbaum of being afraid of the cartels after she rejected his offer to send US troops. His stated belief is that "Mexico is the real Deep State hub of Latin America, and Claudia Sheinbaum was an agent dispatched to conduct damage control after Obrador gave Trump everything he asked for."

The segment closes on Sheinbaum's defense of Maduro and her public request that he receive a fair trial. The author reports emerging intelligence claims that the US case against Maduro hinges on the testimony of a single Venezuelan general given as part of a plea deal for clemency; if true, he speculates the prosecution could "blow up in court" and Maduro be publicly exonerated of cartel-boss allegations.

## Minneapolis demonstrations and the FBI

The Brief summarizes reporting that protests escalated in Minneapolis on January 8 after the fatal shooting of a 37-year-old woman during an immigration enforcement operation involving [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|US Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] the previous day. Demonstrators gathered near the Bishop Henry Whipple Federal Building, which houses ICE offices; authorities used crowd-control measures including pepper spray and closed highways in part, though no arrests were announced locally. Solidarity actions were reported in other cities. Separately, the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] assumed control of the shooting investigation, and [[minnesota|Minnesota]] investigators claim the FBI is blocking their access to information.

Burning Bright frames the timing as revealing: "The system is panicked, its Hegelian architects flailing for purchase as the Collective Mind begins to map the patterns it once dismissed as paranoia." He argues that "distraction theater" erupting in the same city where the [[minnesota-somali-fraud|Somali-linked fraud]] scandal "has shattered plausible deniability" strips away narrative shielding, producing "cognitive shatterpoints." He connects the "vast NGO protest industrial complex" — unearthed in public consciousness in part through the 2025 [[doge|DOGE]] initiative, which he had argued was "more narrative ignition than immediate structural demolition" — to the same theater where the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] storyline detonated in 2020 and gilded nationwide riots under the "Summer of Love" framing. "Same city. Same networks. Same attempted inversion of justice into justification for engineered unrest." He concludes that the machine recycles playbooks "not because they think the populace is going to fall for it, but rather because they've got nothing left," and that the Minne-Somali storyline appearing days before a mass mobilization of what he calls a domestic insurgency cannot be coincidence.

Ashe in America adds that the episode "all feels very George Floyd," joking that "paid protesters charge more when occupation threatens frostbite." She reports that Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] authorized the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] "to be staged and ready to support local and state law enforcement if needed," quoting Walz: "Minnesotans have met this moment. Thousands of people have peacefully made their voices heard. Minnesota: thank you. We saw powerful peace... We have every reason to believe that peace will hold."

She then quotes [[executive-order-26-01|Executive Order 26-01]] at length: it enables the Guard to protect critical infrastructure and assist local law enforcement, authorizes the Adjutant General to place Guard personnel, equipment and facilities on state active duty, and notes the Minnesota State Patrol mobilized 85 members of its Mobile Response Team in the Twin Cities. She highlights the order's recital that "On January 7, 2026, federal immigration agents shot and killed [[renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]] in south Minneapolis," that the shooting followed "the federal government's surge of masked immigration officials in Minnesota," and that such agents "have indiscriminately arrested people off the street, intimidated our friends and neighbors, and have now caused the loss of life." Her reading is that Walz "appears to be mobilizing the Minnesota National Guard to fight federal agents."

Calling the story "a fun story, but it's a distraction," she pivots to the Minnesota Somali fraud hearings, criticizing the five-minute questioning format as she watched Representative [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]] rush through questions: "Five minutes can only be for show. Campaign clips. Five minutes is an unserious amount of time for the People's representatives' pursuit of truth." The segment ends with her call: "Ban & Deport Somalis. Convict & Exile Complicit Americans…Exile them to Somalia."

## Iran's internet blackout

The Brief reports that protests and labor strikes spread across [[iran|Iran]] on Thursday, with demonstrations in Tehran and other cities driven by economic grievances and political tensions, and that authorities imposed widespread internet restrictions while deploying riot police and plainclothes officers. NGOs reported dozens killed nationwide; Iranian authorities released no independent casualty figures, and state media characterized the protests as limited while blaming foreign actors. The unrest escalated around the anniversary of the killing of [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]], which officials cited as a reason for heightened security.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry contrasts a clip of Trump from the Patrick Bet-David podcast in October of the 2024 campaign, where he was asked whether he would support regime change in Iran, with Trump's recent appearance on the Hugh Hewitt Show, where he said he did not think [[reza-pahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]], son of the deposed Shah, would be an appropriate leader for Iran. This, the author writes, "throws cold water on the NeoCons and Zionists, who have been pushing this guy down our throats for months," noting Pahlavi's meeting with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and repeated trips to [[israel|Israel]]. His prediction is that regime change probably will not occur, though he can envision the religious elements — the Ayatollah, the mullahs, and the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] — being expunged while the socially moderate political administration remains. He expects "every Republicuck congressman" to shill for regime change and forever war.

## Fed rate cuts and the jobs report

The Brief reports that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] is expected to begin cutting interest rates in 2026 as labor-market conditions slow and inflation eases. [[congressional-budget-office|Congressional Budget Office]] projections put the federal funds rate near 3.4% by the end of 2026, with inflation above the Fed's 2% target in the near term and longer-term Treasury yields elevated or rising modestly — keeping mortgage and other long-term borrowing costs relatively high even as short-term rates fall. Ahead of the December jobs report, payroll growth was expected to slow, with unemployment forecast in the mid-4% range and initial jobless claims low but edging higher, indicating cooling rather than downturn. Fed policymakers have expressed differing views, with recent decisions including dissents. Attention also focused on leadership changes: Trump stated a decision had been made on a nominee to succeed Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] when his term ends in May 2026, though no formal nomination had been announced.

Ashe in America argues that the upcoming [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] case involving Trump and Federal Reserve Governor [[lisa-cook|Lisa Cook]] is wrongly framed as a test of Fed independence. "The real question is, in which constitutional box do these authorities reside? Can [[congress|Congress]] bypass the executive and insulate power from presidential control?" She contends the Constitution vests executive power in the president alone, that clear lines of accountability matter more than bureaucratic insulation, and that "nothing in the Constitution creates or protects an 'independent' central bank" — the Fed existing entirely by statute. A "for cause" removal standard that effectively prevents presidential judgment, she writes, undermines Article II: "Serving at the pleasure of the President means cause is whatever he says it is. Loss of confidence is cause." Answering critics who say presidential removal threatens public trust in monetary policy, she replies "there isn't any." She closes: "The originalist position cannot be that the Federal Reserve Act overrides the Constitution... End the Fed. Bring on the Golden Age."

Burning Bright adds that Trump has publicly leaned on the Fed to supercharge the [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]] economy since before taking office and that "the Fed has stalled," predicting Trump's mortgage buy-back program is "the first in a rapid cascade of him demonstrating that he doesn't need Jerome."

## Ukraine, Kadyrov, and the erosion of world order

The Brief reports that Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] said the United States should intensify pressure on [[russia|Russia]] and suggested Washington could pursue "some sort of operation" to remove Chechen leader [[ramzan-kadyrov|Ramzan Kadyrov]], drawing a parallel to the US operation that captured Maduro, as a way to push President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to reconsider the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]]. European leaders publicly warned that recent US actions strain the postwar system: German President [[frank-walter-steinmeier|Frank-Walter Steinmeier]] said US policy under Trump is helping erode the global order, citing the Maduro episode. EU foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] said the bloc discussed how it might respond if the US advances efforts to acquire [[greenland|Greenland]], calling US messaging "extremely concerning," and an EU Commission spokesperson reportedly said the [[european-union|EU]] does not recognize the legitimacy of Venezuela's interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] while maintaining "targeted engagement."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry welcomes the trend: "The chaos generated from President Trump's actions and behavior is beginning to bear fruit—dismantling the '[[rules-based-international-order|international rules-based order]].'" He cites Putin describing the system's malice and deception and argues that international law "is nothing more than an abstract idea, because without a true means of enforcement there is no codified law." The [[great-reset|Great Reset]], he writes, was supposed to establish that enforcement, but the world is instead moving toward decentralization of authority. He notes a commentator characterizing international law as flagrant "law of the jungle" and concedes the point: the United States "does whatever it wants because it can—or at least, could." He suggests EU preparations to confront Trump over Greenland could lead to "a diplomatic divorce between the two continents," meaning "the official end of the ambitions set forth in [[agenda-2030|Agenda 2030]]."

He mocks the EU's refusal to recognize Rodríguez's presidency and its attachment to [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], notes French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] lamenting that the US is turning away from allies, and quotes the German president's warning "against turning global politics into 'a den of robbers,'" replying: "Perhaps, fraulein, it's always been that way? And President Trump is merely pulling back the curtain?" He praises Slovakian President [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] for stating directly that the Maduro operation represented the end of the post-WW2 international order — "the apparatus designed to deliver globalism."

Beyond rhetoric, the author reports that Trump withdrew the United States from 66 international organizations, half of them [[united-nations|UN]] bodies, including the climate change platform, and that UN Secretary-General's spokesman [[stephane-dujarric|Stéphane Dujarric]] is threatening to revoke the US vote over unpaid dues. His verdict: "Accelerate!" ## Bonus items

The first bonus item reports that a federal judge disqualified [[john-sarcone|John Sarcone III]] as US Attorney for the Northern District of New York and invalidated subpoenas his office had issued to state Attorney General Letitia James. US District Judge Lorna Schofield, an Obama appointee, wrote in a 24-page opinion that Sarcone had served unlawfully: "When the Executive branch of government skirts restraints put in place by Congress and then uses that power to subject political adversaries to criminal investigations, it acts without lawful authority." The ruling follows decisions removing US attorneys in New Jersey, Delaware, Nevada, Los Angeles and Virginia, where the dismissal of Lindsey Halligan invalidated indictments against both James and former FBI Director James Comey; attempts to re-indict James in Virginia have failed. Sarcone's authority was challenged after unconventional moves kept him in office past his 120-day term — when local judges declined to reappoint him, Attorney General Pam Bondi named him a "special attorney" with an indefinite term.

The second bonus item reports that Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] said Thursday the administration is creating a new assistant attorney general position focused on fraud tied to federally backed programs, following claims that taxpayers lost billions in Minnesota. The official will coordinate investigations across federal agencies and pursue criminal charges, but will not be housed within the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] — instead operating out of the White House under the president and vice president. Vance said the role will likely last through the end of the administration with nationwide jurisdiction, initially concentrated in Minnesota; it requires [[senate|Senate]] confirmation, a nominee was expected within days, and Majority Leader John Thune pledged swift confirmation. The announcement follows Tuesday's decision to halt more than $10 billion in federal social-services funding to Minnesota, New York, California, Illinois and Colorado, with the Department of Health and Human Services already freezing federal child care funding for Minnesota.
