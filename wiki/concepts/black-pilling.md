# Black-Pilling

**Black-pilling** is a term used within alternative-media and "truther" communities to describe the induced state of despair, cynicism and hopelessness in which a person who is otherwise aware of political corruption interprets every development, however favorable, as evidence of inevitable defeat. In his May 22, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "Gaslighting From All Sides," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated black-pilling as a deliberate demoralization tactic and as the principal internal threat to his own political movement.[1]

## Carlson's characterization

Carlson described black-pillers and "doomers" as people who exploit the heightened alarm generated by [[gaslighting|gaslighting]] campaigns in order to frighten and demoralize those on the political right.[1] He framed this demoralization as the collateral damage of an intentional effort by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and "the Patriots" to wake the political left to the border crisis: while sleeping people on the left were being awakened, already-awake people on the right were being driven into despair.[1]

He illustrated the mindset with everyday analogies — a person given a free house who immediately complains about property taxes and mowing the lawn, or a person given a free car who responds by citing the price of gasoline.[1] In his estimate, a substantial portion of the awake population is genuinely this negative and unable to perceive any good in unfolding events.[1]

## Population estimate

Carlson divided America into four notional groups. A permanently, psychologically damaged group that will never accept truth regardless of evidence, which he said Q had estimated at 4–6 percent of the country; roughly 60–65 percent who are awake; and 30–35 percent still asleep but capable of waking if exposed to enough truth.[1] Of the awake majority, he judged that a large share fall prey to black-pilling, while a much smaller share understand the tactics in play and remain "comfy" — feet up, popcorn in hand, active but unstressed because they see what is going on.[1]

## Examples cited

Carlson offered several 2023 instances of what he considered reflexive black-pilling. When [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] bought [[twitter|Twitter]] and began exposing [[deep-state|Deep State]] corruption while restoring banned accounts, black-pillers replied that he was setting up a larger betrayal.[1] When [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] departed [[fox-news|Fox News]] — which the author called a controlled-opposition network that censored him — the response was that the public would never have access to truth again.[1] When Musk hired [[linda-yaccarino|a new CEO]] to run Twitter advertising, black-pillers alleged she was a World Economic Forum-aligned Democrat who would eliminate free speech, a claim Carlson rebutted by noting Tucker Carlson had just announced he would air his show on the platform.[1]

The clearest example he gave concerned the [[2023-george-soros-death-rumor|false report that George Soros had died]], circulated on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] by a prominent figure in the truther movement on Mother's Day 2023.[1] Reading the comments, Carlson was struck that most respondents who believed the report said "who cares, his son is worse than him anyway" or that someone equally bad would take his place — a reaction he found shockingly devoid of hope, though he allowed that some commenters may have been bots.[1]

Carlson also identified the [[e-jean-carroll-defamation-judgment|defamation verdict against Trump]] and the subsequent release of the [[durham-report|Durham report]] without new indictments as events that black-pilled large numbers of people, even though he read the same sequence as establishing legal precedent Trump could later use in defamation suits of his own.[1]

## The wolf-in-sheep's-clothing metaphor

Carlson likened the black-pilled to sheep lured out of the pen by a wolf in sheep's clothing, which persuades them that it is safer outside the fence and that the shepherd, away searching for a lost sheep, has abandoned them.[1] The wolves, in his reading, are people who appear to be allies but who intentionally work to strip away hope and confidence; the black-pilled are those who follow them, hearts filled with despair.[1] Against this he set his own anchor of religious faith, arguing that acceptance that hard things must occur allows the believer to shake off apparent setbacks.[1]

## As a designed outcome of poisoning the well

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated black-pilling as one of the two intended end-states of narrative contamination in his January 26, 2024 essay "Poisoning the Well."[2] Describing the repeated exposure of implanted traitors within the [[truth-movement|truth community]], he wrote that when this occurs often enough the community becomes fractured and off balance — which he called the best outcome the [[deep-state|Deep State]] Cabal can hope for in many cases.[2] Of those affected, "some become confused, others become black pilled; they become demoralized and lose hope."[2] The consequence he identified in a single line: "They give up."[2]

## Negativity as an engineered condition

In "Stuck in a Groove of Negativity and Hopelessness" (May 4, 2023), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated pervasive negativity among truth seekers not as a reasonable response to events but as the intended product of an agenda of control: the [[deep-state|Deep State]] lies, blocks the truth, and — when truth escapes anyway — overwhelms people into hopelessness.[3] He described the method as using the seeker's own weight against them, laying "trails of breadcrumbs to get us lost" and then pulling the rug out and mocking those who publicly announced a conclusion.[3]

Carlson argued that because the small minority of active truth seekers cannot simply be lied to or starved of information, "black pilling" and [[controlled-opposition|controlled opposition]] become the essential tools against them, with excessive negativity and partial truths designed to confuse and deflate.[3] He drew an analogy to overtraining in weightlifting: the remedy for a body that can no longer recover is rest, reduced volume or more food, and the analogous remedy for the demoralized researcher is to know oneself, recognize when one is being intentionally overwhelmed, and take a break or slow the pace.[3]

He identified two concrete symptoms within his own community: people so convinced the American economy would be destroyed by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's policies that they were rooting for the collapse in order to validate their belief, and people who insisted [[donald-trump|Trump]] could not win in 2024 because elections are too rigged and no protective systems exist.[3] Such readers, he wrote, seek only validation for their hopelessness and refuse messages of hope even when backed by evidence.[3] Against this he set an explicitly religious claim — that human beings are not designed by God to feel hopeless or defeated, and that despair originates with Satan and functions to remove a person from the fight.[3]

## The "Doomer's Refrain" and the barren season

In "Thrive in the Transition" (December 7, 2022), [[pepe-lives-matter|Pepe Lives Matter]] paired the black pill with what he called the "Doomer's Refrain" and plain defeatism, warning that without encouragement and faith these can embed themselves deep in the psyche and, once lodged, often refuse to let go.[4] He argued that the countermeasure is a "Macro Viewpoint" — an eagle-eyed perspective above the daily news cycle — because the noise of day-to-day reporting can otherwise drown out hope until emotional well-being becomes indefensible.[4]

That essay located the source of black-pilling partly in an audience mismatch: the constant "precipice" narratives are, on his account, designed to bring the normie and the half-awake to their own [[red-pilling|red pill]] moment and are not what the already-awakened patriot should consume daily.[4] A reader who is continually swept up emotionally by narratives aimed at other people's awakenings is described as "living on the precipice incorrectly," and inhabiting that emotional state permanently is called a recipe for disaster and heartbreak.[4] The prescribed remedy is a change of mentality and expectation during the transitional "barren season," with hope treated as an art form to be guarded and practised until mastered.[4] See [[thriving-in-the-transition|Thriving in the Transition]].[4]
