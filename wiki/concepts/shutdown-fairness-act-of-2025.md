# Shutdown Fairness Act of 2025

The Shutdown Fairness Act of 2025 was a Republican-sponsored bill, introduced by Sen. [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] of Wisconsin, that would have paid active-duty members of the military and other essential federal employees required to work during the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]].[1]

## Senate vote

On Thursday, October 23, 2025, [[united-states-senate|Senate]] Democrats blocked the measure in a mostly party-line 54-45 vote; it needed 60 votes to advance.[1] Sens. [[jon-ossoff|Jon Ossoff]] and [[raphael-warnock|Raphael Warnock]] of Georgia and [[john-fetterman|John Fetterman]] of Pennsylvania crossed party lines to vote in favor.[1] Fetterman — along with Sens. [[catherine-cortez-masto|Catherine Cortez Masto]] of Nevada and [[angus-king|Angus King]] of Maine — had also been voting with Republicans to reopen the government, while the two Georgia Democrats continued voting against the stopgap funding bill.[1]

## Relationship to the True Shutdown Fairness Act

The vote occurred the same day that Johnson objected on the Senate floor to a unanimous-consent request from Sen. [[chris-van-hollen|Chris Van Hollen]] of Maryland to immediately pass the [[true-shutdown-fairness-act|True Shutdown Fairness Act]], which would have paid all federal workers, military members and federal contractors during the shutdown.[1] Johnson rejected two Democratic-sponsored pay bills on the floor that day, producing a symmetrical standoff in which each party blocked the other's version of shutdown pay relief.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that since legislators could not reach agreement, the matter should go to a popular vote, offering a package that funds the military, eliminates the majority of federal government jobs, and pays [[congress|Congress]] the minimum wage.[1]
