# Rug Pull

A "rug pull" is a form of cryptocurrency fraud in which the creators of a token attract retail buyers, then dump insider holdings or drain liquidity, leaving investors with worthless assets.[1] Badlands Media described the practice as endemic to the [[decentralized-finance|DeFi]] sector, where in 2022 alone $3.6 billion was lost to attacks on all project types — a 47.4 percent increase over the $2.4 billion lost in 2021.[1]

## Mechanics and concealment

The archetypal case documented by Badlands is [[saitama-token|Saitama Token]], which an investigator described as "intended to rug since the beginning."[1] Insiders reserved undisclosed "team tokens," advertised a [[fair-launch|fair launch]] anyway, and later moved the reserve from four main wallets through hundreds of sub-wallets and onto centralized exchanges to obscure the trail, selling the remainder through [[uniswap|Uniswap]] where KYC rules did not apply.[1]

## Community self-policing

Because smaller frauds — those costing individual investors $20,000 to $30,000 — rarely make the news, victims have little recourse.[1] In response, some retail investors have turned investigator, tracking wallets and connecting on-chain transactions, and are finding that many small scams are executed by a well-coordinated network.[1] One such investor, posting as @Dontforgetsauce, wrote: "Done with the eth demons who ain't building shit and using their friends as exit liquidity."[1] The article argued that if such coordination can be proven it constitutes antitrust violations and market manipulation, and that a proven rug pull is a violation of [[securities-exchange-act-of-1934|Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934]].[1]
