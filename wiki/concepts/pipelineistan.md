# Pipelineistan

"Pipelineistan" is a term coined in 2009 by journalist [[pepe-escobar|Pepe Escobar]] in a series of dispatches describing the vast network of oil and gas pipelines that, in his phrase, "crisscross the potential imperial battlefields of the planet."[1] Escobar placed Afghanistan — strategically positioned between the Middle East, Central Asia, and South Asia — "at the core of Pipelineistan," as described in a 2009 article by Conn Hallinan surveying Escobar's writings.[1]

## The thesis

In Escobar's reading, as summarized by Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the conflicts that have unfolded in and around the Middle East are best understood as pipeline wars.[1] The [[2008-russo-georgian-war|2008 Georgia-Russia war]] is treated as a case in point: provoked after President George W. Bush vowed to make Georgia and Ukraine members of NATO, and after what the Brief describes as the use of the [[operation-gladio|Gladio]] playbook to install a Georgian government oriented toward the West and hostile to [[russia|Russia]].[1] The American deep state's interest in Georgia centered on the [[baku-tbilisi-ceyhan-pipeline|Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline]], which reduced US dependence on Middle Eastern oil while circumventing Russia to give Europe an alternative to the Russian pipelines that had supplied it since 1964.[1]

In his 2009 essay "Liquid War," Escobar traced the origin of the US energy war to President Jimmy Carter and his National Security Advisor [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]].[1]

## Badlands extension of the argument

The Brief extends Pipelineistan into a claim about the American domestic energy industry: that [[donald-trump|President Trump]] demonstrated in his first term that America could become not only energy independent but a leading exporter of oil and natural gas, and that part of the reason oligarchic interests suppressed that emergence was to justify meddling in Russia's borderlands in Ukraine and the Caucasus.[1] On this account 9/11 served as the catalyst for inserting the US military into the Middle East at scale in order to control the geography around Iran — Iraq and Afghanistan — and prevent the Russia-Iran-China alignment that Brzezinski identified as the only combination capable of stopping the emergence of the [[new-world-order|New World Order]].[1]

Geopolitical developments since Trump left office, the Brief argues, guaranteed that this alliance would not only emerge but be galvanized by necessity, since all three states face individual threats from the NATO-centered order and cannot afford to face them alone.[1] Invoking the fate of the earlier [[sovereign-alliance|sovereign alliance]] of Gaddafi, Saddam, and the recently overthrown Assad, the piece concludes that any adversary formidable enough to check the globalist cartel must focus on economics rather than kinetic confrontation, bypassing the cartel to negotiate directly and mutually beneficially with the American people.[1]
