# Anti-Weaponization Fund

The "anti-weaponization" fund is a $1.776 billion fund established through President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s settlement with the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], intended to compensate individuals against whom the federal government was weaponized.[1]

## California countermeasure

In May 2026 California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] vowed to impose a 100% tax on any payouts that California residents receive from the fund.[1] Newsom described the fund as a "slush fund" and linked it to Trump's pardons of January 6 defendants, stating that recipients are being "rewarded" after being pardoned for "beating up cops."[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the proposal collapses on its own logic: a Californian compensated for state-federal weaponization would then have the state government weaponized against them to confiscate the payment.[1] She called the measure "petty and punitive and intended to preserve a false narrative," questioned how such taxation "came through representation," and predicted that it would not withstand legal challenge.[1]

The fund is closely related to the wider Badlands treatment of the [[doj-slush-fund|DOJ slush fund]] narrative.[1]

## Origin in the IRS tax-return leak settlement

The fund was established as part of President Trump's settlement of his $10 billion lawsuit against the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] and the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] over the leaking of his tax returns.[2] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] said that under the settlement the "Anti-Weaponization Fund" had been established to review and address claims of alleged government weaponization and lawfare.[2] The fund totals $1.776 billion and is financed through the federal judgment fund, a permanent DOJ appropriation for settlements, and the program allows for formal apologies as well as monetary relief for eligible claimants.[2]

The underlying lawsuit was filed in January alongside Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump and accused the IRS of unlawfully disclosing Trump's tax records from his first term through a government contractor.[2] Trump's legal team filed notice in federal court in Miami seeking dismissal of the case with prejudice, meaning it cannot be refiled; attorneys argued the case remained in its early stages, allowing voluntary dismissal without court approval or IRS consent.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted the apology-plus-compensation structure, asking whether anyone had thought formal government apologies for weaponizing state power, accompanied by monetary settlement, were possible, and said Badlands would cover the fund's deployment "from a place of first hand knowledge."[2]

## Reported 2026 shutdown

On June 1, 2026, [[axios|Axios]] reported that the Trump administration was preparing to eliminate the Justice Department initiative known internally as the "weaponization" fund, a program established to support investigations and legal actions tied to alleged political abuse by federal agencies during prior administrations.[3] According to that reporting, administration officials believed the effort had become politically burdensome and legally difficult to sustain as attention shifted toward broader institutional reforms and budget priorities.[3]

The fund had been associated with investigations into alleged misconduct involving federal law enforcement, intelligence agencies, censorship coordination, and politically motivated prosecutions; critics inside and outside the administration reportedly argued that it created expectations of sweeping criminal accountability that proved difficult to deliver in court.[3] Supporters viewed the program as a symbolic commitment to confronting what they describe as entrenched bureaucratic and prosecutorial abuse.[3] The reported move came amid wider restructuring across the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and renewed debate inside Trump-aligned circles over whether systemic reform or high-profile prosecutorial action should remain the administration's primary strategy.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the report, noting that it rested on two anonymous sources and calling it "statist wishful thinking."[3] She linked the reported cancellation to efforts by Democratic politicians — naming Senator [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]] of [[colorado|Colorado]] and Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] — to tax weaponization disbursements at 100%, a strategy she characterized as weaponizing the anti-weaponization remedy itself, citing [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] as an example of a potential claimant.[3] She predicted that such pressure would instead push Trump to "double the fund" and argued that the fund deals with restitution while accountability remains outstanding.[3]
