# Alfa Bank Hoax

The **Alfa Bank Hoax** is the term used in Badlands Media commentary for the allegation — constructed by cyber contractors working for the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] 2016 presidential campaign — that a secret communications channel existed between the [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] organization and Russia's Alfa Bank.[1] According to evidence surfaced by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]], the allegation was assembled from classified and non-public data pulled out of federal agency databases by private contractors, and was then packaged for delivery to the news media and to federal agencies.[1]

## Construction of the allegation

Durham's court filings and trial presentations established that the Clinton campaign's private cyber operatives were paid to extract classified or non-public data from federal agency databases and to build the Trump/Russia collusion claim that came to be known as the Alfa Bank Hoax.[1] The work was performed under the direction and supervision of the campaign's lawyers at [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]], the firm that also retained [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]].[1] The contractors operated without a warrant and without any law-enforcement or intelligence authority, since a presidential campaign is neither a law enforcement nor an intelligence agency.[1]

The finished product was distributed along two tracks: to the press, and to two specific federal agencies — a law enforcement agency, the [[fbi|FBI]], and an intelligence agency, the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[1]

## The February 2017 revision presented to the CIA

By the time Trump assumed office, the FBI had closed its Alfa Bank inquiry.[1] The Clinton-aligned operatives then attempted to open a second front, revising and expanding the original allegation with newly obtained data and bringing the expanded package to the CIA in February 2017 in an effort to trigger a counterintelligence investigation of the sitting President.[1] Some of the added material was taken from servers inside the Executive Office of the President — that is, from White House systems — after Trump had already been sworn in as the nation's 45th President.[1]

## Relationship to other Spygate strands

The Alfa Bank Hoax was the vehicle for [[michael-sussmann|Michael Sussmann]]'s September 2016 approach to the FBI, and the Sussmann prosecution became the venue in which Durham first placed much of this evidence on the public record.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] wrote that he suspected, without being able to prove, that some of the data stolen from federal databases about Trump associates on the campaign's target list also found its way into the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[1] Together with the dossier, the Alfa Bank allegation forms one of the two principal fabricated evidentiary streams at the heart of [[spygate|Spygate]] and the broader [[russiagate-hoax|RussiaGate hoax]].[1]
