# US-Funded Global Biolab Program

The **US-funded global biolab program** refers to a network of biological research laboratories abroad financed in whole or part by United States taxpayer money, the existence and scope of which became the subject of a formal intelligence-community disclosure in June 2026.

## The 2026 ODNI disclosure

In a statement released on Friday, June 12, 2026, the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], under Director [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], said newly declassified evidence concerned a U.S.-funded global network of more than 120 biological laboratories operating in more than 30 countries. ODNI said the network included laboratories in Ukraine, and asserted that some facilities had conducted research involving hazardous pathogens, including instances the office characterized as [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]]. It further said at least one U.S.-funded laboratory in Ukraine had been assessed as vulnerable to attack, seizure, or damage during the Russia-Ukraine war.

Gabbard directed increased intelligence collection on overseas laboratories and associated facilities, an effort ODNI described as intended to identify laboratory locations, the pathogens held there, and ongoing activities.

Separately, [[rt|RT]] reported that documents released alongside the announcement described 40 laboratories in Ukraine and listed pathogens including anthrax, avian influenza, Ebola, plague, and tuberculosis. RT characterized the documents as substantiating longstanding allegations about U.S.-funded biological research facilities in Ukraine.

## Prior disclosure and the "drip" critique

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded by noting that the underlying facts were not new to the truth community: she dated her own awareness to Under Secretary of State [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]]'s 2022 Senate testimony, in which Nuland expressed concern that Ukrainian biological research facilities might fall into Russian hands. That exchange went viral at the time, which made it striking that the subject continued to be dismissed as conspiracy theory afterward — a failure Ashe attributed to legacy media protecting the narrative even after disclosure had occurred.

She acknowledged that the "breaking news" framing does little to answer criticism of the slow, partial cadence of official disclosure — the "drip drip of disclosure rug pulls" — among those who have known for years, while arguing that the reruns still function as first runs for a large audience encountering the material for the first time, and that mass awakening is worth the redundancy.
