# US-Funded Global Biolab Program

The US-funded global biolab program refers to a network of more than 120 biological laboratories operating in more than 30 countries, financed by U.S. taxpayers, which was the subject of a declassification announcement by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] in June 2026.[1]

## The ODNI disclosure

Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s office released a statement saying newly declassified evidence concerned the network, which included laboratories in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1] ODNI asserted that some facilities conducted research involving hazardous pathogens, including instances it characterized as gain-of-function research, and that at least one U.S.-funded laboratory in Ukraine had been assessed as vulnerable to attack, seizure, or damage during the Russia-Ukraine war.[1]

The statement said Gabbard directed increased intelligence collection on overseas laboratories and facilities associated with the program, with the effort intended to identify laboratory locations, pathogens, and ongoing activities.[1]

## Ukrainian laboratories

RT reported that documents released alongside the announcement described 40 laboratories in Ukraine and listed pathogens including anthrax, avian influenza, Ebola, plague, and tuberculosis.[1] RT characterized the documents as supporting longstanding allegations regarding U.S.-funded biological research facilities in Ukraine.[1]

## Reception

Badlands contributor Ashe in America noted that she had first learned of the U.S.-funded biolabs from [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]]'s 2022 Senate testimony, which went viral at the time, and expressed surprise that the subject continued to be treated as a conspiracy theory afterward — faulting legacy media for protecting the narrative even after disclosure.[1] She acknowledged that the "breaking news" framing did little to calm criticism about the "drip drip of disclosure rug pulls" among those who had known for years, but argued that "reruns are first runs for many" and that new audiences waking up to the material was a net good.[1]
