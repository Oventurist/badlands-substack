# The Werewolf Game

**The Werewolf Game** is a multi-part essay series published at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] by the pseudonymous author [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], which explores what the author describes as core tenets of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s mass mind-control strategy.[1] The series advances the premise that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Kennedy families have been engaged for centuries — and possibly millennia — in a struggle against an ancient, werewolf-themed death cult originating in the region of the world known today as Ukraine.[1]

The fourth installment, subtitled "A New Class of Elites," was published on May 22, 2024, and is written to stand alone without requiring the reader to have read Parts 1 through 3, although the author recommends reading all of them so that ideas may be encountered repeatedly in varying contexts.[1] The author states that the wider subject matter — occultism, secret societies, and [[fifth-generation-warfare|5G warfare]] — extends far beyond the series' central premise, but maintains that the werewolf-cult thesis is an important piece of the puzzle of [[qanon|Q]], Trump, and what the author calls the *narrative battle*.[1]

## Epistemic framing

The author explicitly declines to ask readers to believe the material automatically, observing that "automatic" belief is itself a form of hypnosis, and framing his own project as the inverse of conventional hypnosis: rather than putting audiences to sleep, he describes his aim as waking people up.[1] Research for the series is described as having been both entertaining and fruitful beyond the author's expectations.[1]

## Core narrative

The series holds that a nomadic warrior cult, the [[koryos|Koryos]], practiced ritual violence in the Ukraine–Western Russia–Romania region from roughly 2000–3000 BC and is the true origin of werewolf symbolism.[1] Against this dark lineage the author sets an advanced and possibly benevolent global civilization, provisionally identified with the term "[[tartaria|Tartaria]]," and posits that the Trump and Kennedy families are royal heirs — or something akin to royal heirs — of that positive civilization.[1]

A distinguishing feature of the series is its rejection of the common conspiracy premise that a single "cabal" has monopolized the planet since the beginning.[1] The author writes that he once held that view but now believes humanity is caught between many competing influences running the gamut from malevolent to benevolent, and doubts that the Deep State is more powerful than the force he associates with Trump.[1]

## Part Four: "A New Class of Elites"

Part Four argues that the reaction of the media and political establishment to Trump's 2016 victory is evidence that the Trump and Kennedy families represent a genuine rival elite.[1] It surveys the [[kennedy-curse|"Kennedy Curse"]], catalogues genealogical and social links between the Trump and Kennedy families, and introduces the claim — attributed to the researcher [[erhard-landmann|Erhard Landmann]] — that an ancient form of German was once spoken by all the peoples of the world.[1] The author promises that a subsequent article will use the German origin of Trump's ancestral surname *Drumpf* to decode one of Q's posts.[1]

## Conclusion of Part Four

The article concludes that while many elites are indeed "fat cats and cultists," the establishment's reaction to Trump demonstrates the existence of forces the Deep State fears, and that the Trump and Kennedy families are the most visible members of a counter-elite opposed to the Deep State and the New World Order.[1] The author argues that control of history is an indispensable component of the cabal's system of mental enslavement, because human beings invariably "act out" the identity they ascribe to themselves; recovering true history therefore disarms false history as a weapon.[1] The piece closes with a pointer to Part 5 and the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributor features represent the opinions of their authors rather than the views of Badlands Media itself.[1]

### The game described

The Werewolf Game proper is described as a parlor game in which participants play either as "villagers" or as "werewolves," with the villagers having no idea who among them the werewolves are.[6] Mainstream sources are said to credit its invention to a Russian sociology student who wanted to create a game that would demonstrate to anyone who played it how information warfare actually works.[6] The author argues that the use of the word "werewolf" is itself a *comm*, since vampire, werewolf and zombie symbolism is important to the dark occultists who run the Deep State, and that the game's invention in Russia is likely significant.[6] He contends that the predicament of mankind is strikingly similar to the predicament of the villagers in the game, that essentially any tactic that helps villagers win the parlor version is transferable to real life, and that playing the game regularly with friends would subconsciously train players to spot the werewolves around them in reality.[6]

### Information warfare versus physical warfare

A central claim of Part 1 is that an informed minority almost always defeats an uninformed majority in any information-based conflict.[6] The author observes that most people equate war with standing armies and physical combat, where the quantity of soldiers has historically mattered most, and therefore find it counterintuitive that other forms of war can easily be won by small numbers against large numbers.[6] Fighting effectively in an [[information-warfare|information war]], he writes, requires becoming cognizant of one's own nescience regarding information one does not yet possess.[6]

The essay defines evil as "unbounded parasitism" and describes an *eternal power struggle worldview* — a Darwinist reading of life as a non-stop struggle for domination — which the author identifies as the central belief of Marxism and a perspective shared on some level by all psychopaths.[6] The difference between psychopaths and non-psychopaths is said to be that ordinary people do not define the entirety of existence by such struggles, and the mentality is described as a powerful unifier for the world's slavers and dark occultists.[6] Citing [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]]'s [[shadow-men|*Shadow Men*]] and [[andrew-lobaczewski|Andrew Lobaczewski]]'s [[political-ponerology|*Political Ponerology*]], the author concludes that "the Deep State, in so many words, amounts to organized psychopathy," and that the masses cannot defend themselves against psychopathy they cannot recognize.[6] He recommends [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]]'s [[psychopath-proof|*Psychopath Proof*]] as the single most insightful book he has read on the subject.[6]

### The one-language argument

The installment's stated main point is to present the evidence that all the peoples of the world once spoke ancient German, and then to discuss the implications.[9] It credits [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]] with bringing the discovery to the English-speaking world and [[erhard-landmann|Erhard Landmann]] with originating it, defends reliance on a single source by arguing that the demand for multiple sources mathematically guarantees new discoveries are always discarded, and dismisses peer review where all peers share the same indoctrination.[9] It attacks the [[christopher-columbus|Columbus]] discovery narrative as implausible and probably a psyop, sets out a method of comparison based on resemblance rather than identity, and presents roughly forty [[aztec-german-word-correspondences|Aztec–ancient German word pairs]] drawn from Dodson.[9] Two place-name pairs are singled out: *Sacatlan* as *Saschenland* or [[saxony|Saxony]], and *Ani Sazi* as *Ahnen Sachen*, "ancestors from Saxony," alongside the observation that [[montezuma|Montezuma]] and the Koryos are both described as red-haired.[9]

### Owls and two elites

Under the heading "The Owls Are Not What They Seem," the author notes that many researchers equate the Druids with the [[illuminati|Illuminati]], that the Illuminati reportedly began in Bavaria, and that the ancient German for "Holy Owl" is *Wai Ulu* — offered as a possible decoding of [[q-post-189|Q's post 189]] directive to follow [[y-owl-symbolism|"Y/Owl" symbolism]] worldwide.[9] The closing section argues that the elites' power rests on an information advantage about true history and the nature of reality, that ancient German names such as Drumpf and Schwaub persist among them, and that whoever knows what confounded mankind's languages could hold the upper hand indefinitely.[9] The piece ends with a pointer to Part 6 and the standard Badlands disclaimer.[9]

## Part Three: "Hell On"

Part 3, subtitled "Something Weird is Going on at the Black Sea," was published 22 April 2024 and advances the series' most explicitly metaphysical claim: that the dark occultists running the [[deep-state|Deep State]] are literally attempting to create [[hell|hell]] on Earth and have probably been working at it for millennia, with the author adding that they are going to lose.[10] He clarifies that "hell" is used not to convert readers to any religion but as the best available term for a *veil of evil* enshrouding mankind's collective psyche, one that may literally hail from subterranean realms.[10] Recapping the earlier installments, he notes that the [[koryos|Koryos]] emerged near the [[black-sea|Black Sea]] — the site of a pivotal contemporary battle — and states that he does not regard them as the only origin of the modern Deep State, only as a major hand in forming it.[10]

### Evidence of hell

The installment's evidentiary core is the clinical work of [[jerry-marzinsky|Jerry Marzinsky]], who concluded that the voices heard by schizophrenic patients are parasitic demonic entities feeding on negative emotion rather than hallucinations, and that reciting Psalm 23 drives them off.[10] The author argues that the consistency and uniformly anti-God character of the voices refutes the randomness expected of hallucination, and quotes [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]]'s *Levels of Heaven and Hell* on the cross-cultural ubiquity of hell traditions.[10] He draws a structural parallel to narcissists and psychopaths, whose isolation tactics are said to mirror the spirits' aim of severing a person's connection to God, and contends that the institutional enshrinement of materialism and atheism in Western civilization implies evil within the halls of power.[10] Tibetan Buddhism's realm of hungry ghosts is cited as an example of the widely held doctrine that hell has levels.[10]

### Essential features of the Koryos and 1960s counterculture

Part 3 enumerates twelve indicators of the Koryos cult — ritual violence under the full moon in wolf pelts, child cannibalism, blood ritual, the fusion of sex and death, possession combined with drugs, taboo-breaking, apex-predator self-conception, animal costume, permanent revolution against tradition, the colour red, demonic ancestor worship, and the grooming of children as mercenaries.[10] The author sees the same motifs in contemporary music videos, and compares the cult's unproductive drug-fuelled existence between raids to the drug and music culture promoted after the 1960s cultural revolution, in which [[timothy-leary|Timothy Leary]] is described as a [[cia|CIA]] asset.[10] Leary's colleague [[robert-anton-wilson|Robert Anton Wilson]], chief editor of [[playboy|Playboy]] from 1960 to 1969, and his co-author [[robert-shea|Robert Shea]] supply the link to the [[illuminatus-trilogy|Illuminatus Trilogy]], whose submerged zombie army the author reads as possible disclosure.[10]

### Tartaria and Black Sea anomalies

The essay reports the author's realisation that the Greek Tartarus, the region of hell below Hades, closely resembles [[tartaria|Tartaria]], located where the Koryos emerged in territory once known as [[khazaria|Khazaria]].[10] It catalogues the Black Sea's anoxia, sulphur content, "deep red fluorescence" and meromictic layering as further circumstantial evidence, and, drawing on former satanist [[mark-passio|Mark Passio]], proposes that vampires symbolise the cult's royalty, werewolves its mercenaries and zombies the general population.[10] The author summarises the Koryos as one of "hell's proxy armies," restates that the Trump and Kennedy families may have fought it for centuries, notes a reader's observation that the Prussian army also originated in the Ukraine area, and closes with the assertion that evil depends on human participation and that "their biggest fear is a mass awakening."[10]
