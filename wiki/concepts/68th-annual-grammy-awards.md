# 68th Annual Grammy Awards

The 68th Annual Grammy Awards were held on Sunday, February 1, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles and broadcast live on [[cbs|CBS]].[1] The ceremony drew commentary in the Badlands Brief chiefly for its political content and for a dispute between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and host Trevor Noah.[1]

## Performances and awards

Performers included Lady Gaga, Bruno Mars, Sabrina Carpenter, Justin Bieber, and Tyler, The Creator.[1] Bad Bunny won Album of the Year for *Debí Tirar Más Fotos*, the first Spanish-language album to receive the award. Kendrick Lamar and SZA won Record of the Year for "Luther," Billie Eilish and Finneas won Song of the Year, and Olivia Dean won Best New Artist.[1]

The ceremony included extended tribute segments: an In Memoriam honoring Ozzy Osbourne with performances by Post Malone, Slash, Duff McKagan, and Chad Smith, and tributes led by Lauryn Hill honoring D'Angelo and Roberta Flack alongside Chaka Khan and John Legend.[1] Cher's Lifetime Achievement appearance included a brief misstatement while announcing Record of the Year that was subsequently corrected, and minor in-ear monitor issues occurred during performances.[1] Many acceptance speeches included political remarks, particularly on US immigration.[1]

## Trump–Trevor Noah dispute

Host Trevor Noah drew the ire of President Trump during the event by making comments about the President and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1] Trump responded on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], calling the Grammys "the WORST, virtually unwatchable," comparing Noah unfavorably to Jimmy Kimmel, and disputing the claim that he and [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] had spent time on Epstein Island. Trump wrote that he had "never been to Epstein Island, nor anywhere close," described the remark as false and defamatory, and said he would send his lawyers to sue Noah.[1]

## Commentary

In the wake of the event, a [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]] *Real Time* monologue from January 30 went viral, in which Maher criticized celebrity politicization and said of ICE, "I don't need to wear a pin about it."[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] identified three encouraging moments: Maher openly mocking his own audience's causes — including calling their pain over Renee Good "stupid," something she argued would have been impossible in 2020; Joy Villa's protest outfit targeting Scientology, following her departure from the organization after 15 years; and Jelly Roll using his acceptance speech to declare that "Jesus is for everybody."[1]

Villa, writing in *Evie*, said that from the outside her life inside Scientology looked like a success story while inside it was slowly destroying her, that the organization attributed every achievement to auditing, donations, and loyalty so that "my success became propaganda," and that "silence protects systems of abuse." She described being rebaptized and fully surrendered to Jesus Christ, and committed to helping others recover from cult abuse and coercive control through her nonprofit, The Fearless Joy Foundation.[1]
