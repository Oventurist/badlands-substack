# Second Amendment

The Second Amendment to the United States Constitution, ratified in 1791 as part of the Bill of Rights, provides that "a well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State, the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed." Its scope has been the subject of sustained litigation, particularly since the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] recognized an individual right to keep and bear arms in *District of Columbia v. Heller* (2008) and applied it to the states in *McDonald v. City of Chicago* (2010).

## The Bruen framework

In [[new-york-state-rifle-and-pistol-association-v-bruen|New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen]], the Court replaced means-end balancing with a test requiring that a challenged firearm regulation be consistent with "the nation's historical tradition of firearm regulation." That framework has generated extensive lower-court litigation over which categories of persons may constitutionally be disarmed.

## The drug-user possession ban

The March 3, 2026 Badlands Brief reports as a bonus item that the Supreme Court heard argument on a federal statute barring any person who is an "unlawful user" of a controlled substance from possessing a firearm. Several justices expressed skepticism about the statute's breadth, and multiple members of the Court asked during oral argument whether historical analogues exist that would justify broadly disarming individuals based solely on current drug use. The federal government defended the law, arguing that persons actively using illegal drugs may pose heightened safety risks comparable to groups historically subject to disarmament. The Brief's headline framing — "Crazies Keeping Their Guns (Maybe)" — reflects the expectation that the Court may narrow or invalidate the prohibition.
