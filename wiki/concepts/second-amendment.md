# Second Amendment

The Second Amendment to the [[us-constitution|United States Constitution]] protects the right of the people to keep and bear arms, a right that its defenders describe as inalienable and pre-political rather than granted by government.[1] Within the Badlands corpus the amendment is treated less as a policy question than as a structural safeguard: the right to defend oneself against tyranny.[1]

## The 2023 New Mexico test case

The amendment's limits against state executive action were tested in September 2023 when New Mexico Governor [[michelle-lujan-grisham|Michelle Lujan Grisham]] issued an emergency order suspending the public carrying of firearms across Albuquerque and Bernalillo County for at least thirty days, citing a spate of gun violence including the death of an eleven-year-old boy outside a minor league baseball stadium.[1] The order was enforced as a set of civil violations by state police; Albuquerque police chief [[harold-medina|Harold Medina]] said he would not enforce it, and Bernalillo County Sheriff [[john-allen-sheriff|John Allen]] said the order raised too many questions about constitutional rights.[1] State lawmakers called for the governor's impeachment.[1]

## Constitutional argument

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] grounded the objection in [[marbury-v-madison|Marbury v. Madison]] (1803), invoking the principle that all laws repugnant to the Constitution are null and void, and concluding that a state governor cannot suspend the Second Amendment because states have no power to separate citizens from their constitutional rights.[1]

Notably, prominent figures on the left including Representative [[ted-lieu|Ted Lieu]] and activist [[david-hogg|David Hogg]] agreed that states may not strike down the Second Amendment.[1] Ashe argued their agreement was strategic rather than principled — that such figures reject states' rights generally and prefer restrictions imposed federally, by constitutional amendment or legislation, in the same way the establishment reacted to abortion policy being returned to the states by [[roe-v-wade|the reversal of Roe v. Wade]].[1] The Badlands position was that neither the states nor the federal government may infringe the right: "shall not be infringed."[1]

## DOJ suit against the District of Columbia (December 2025)

The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] established a Second Amendment Section within its Civil Rights Division and used it in December 2025 to file a [[doj-lawsuit-against-dc-firearm-laws|federal lawsuit]] against the District of Columbia and its [[metropolitan-police-department-of-the-district-of-columbia|Metropolitan Police Department]], arguing that D.C.'s firearm registration laws and enforcement practices amount to a de facto ban on AR-15s and other commonly owned semi-automatic firearms in violation of the amendment.[2] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] framed the case as vindicating the rights of law-abiding citizens living in the nation's capital.[2]
