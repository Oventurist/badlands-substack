# D-Exit

**D-Exit** is a term coined in coverage of the November 2024 United States presidential election to describe the exodus of long-rooted constituencies from the Democratic Party toward [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], framed as the American equivalent of Great Britain's [[brexit|Brexit]] departure from the European Union.[1]

## Origins

The label emerged in reporting by Just the News in the immediate aftermath of the election, which argued that Trump's return to the White House was consequential less for his personal comeback than for the once-in-a-generation political realignment it revealed — one described as deeper and more pervasive than his 2016 upset.[1] Trump had prevailed against media, Big Tech and Democratic opposition stretching "from the courthouse to the social media sphere," after two impeachments and acquittals, four indictments, two assassination attempts and an unprecedented volume of lawfare.[1]

## Composition of the shift

According to the analysis, black men, Hispanic voters and young voters turned out more strongly for Trump and less fervently for [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] than they had for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] or Barack Obama, while Arab and Muslim voters also underperformed for Harris.[1] The individual movements were small but cumulatively decisive, crumbling a coalition born in the Kennedy–Johnson era that had underpinned the Obama–Biden dynasty's dominance of twelve of the previous sixteen years.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] accepted the realignment framing but used it to press an election-integrity argument: if Trump genuinely triggered a D-Exit, yet was trending below his own 2020 vote total, the narrative did not match the data.[1] She contrasted the raw vote counts circulating on X — Obama's 69 million in 2008 and 65 million in 2012, Hillary Clinton's 65 million in 2016, Biden's 81 million in 2020 and Harris's 66 million in 2024 — to argue that roughly 15 million votes had materialized only once, in November 2020.[1] Those votes, she contended, would have produced illegitimate outcomes down the entire ballot, including Colorado's 2020 adoption of the [[national-popular-vote-interstate-compact|National Popular Vote Interstate Compact]] and its unrestricted abortion measure.[1] Her conclusion was that legitimate elections are foundational, that without them "the regime fabricates our consent to subjugate and exploit us," and that the corruption must be investigated, exposed and prosecuted.[1]
