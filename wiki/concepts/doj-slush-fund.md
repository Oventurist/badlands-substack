# DOJ Settlement Slush Fund

The "DOJ slush fund" refers to the practice, alleged by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] under [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]], of the Obama-era [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] directing money from legal settlements with financial institutions to third-party activist organizations rather than to the U.S. Treasury.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Findings

Internal DOJ documents confirmed the fund's existence and showed that officials "went out of their way" to exclude conservative groups from the list of approved recipients.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The committee characterized the practice as systematically subverting Congress's budget authority, and found that approximately $1 billion had been routed to outside organizations over a two-year period.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Goodlatte released emails he called a "smoking gun" and stated that only approved left-wing groups were eligible for bank payouts under the settlements.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Legislative response

The disclosures came just ahead of a House vote on a bill that would prohibit government officials — most notably the DOJ — from entering into or enforcing settlement agreements providing payment or loans to any person or entity other than the United States, subject to limited exceptions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The committee had earlier requested that the DOJ preserve all documents and emails relating to settlement money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)
