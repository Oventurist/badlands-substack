# DOJ Settlement Slush Fund

The "DOJ settlement slush fund" refers to a practice, alleged by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] under chairman [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]], whereby the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] during the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration directed money from settlements with financial institutions to outside activist organizations rather than to the United States Treasury. It is treated at length in the Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 2.

## The congressional findings

*Forbes* reported in October 2017 that internal DOJ documents confirmed the existence of the fund and that department officials "went out of their way" to exclude conservative groups. Goodlatte made the claim ahead of a House vote on legislation that would prohibit government officials from entering into or enforcing settlement agreements on behalf of the United States that provide for payment or loans to any person or entity other than the United States, with limited exceptions. He said the legislation became necessary after an extended committee investigation found that the DOJ had engaged in a pattern of systematically subverting Congress's budget authority by using settlements from financial institutions to funnel money to what he described as "left-wing activist groups."

Hot Air reported Goodlatte's floor statement — "It is not every day in congressional investigations that we find a smoking gun" — and his release of emails said to show that only approved left-wing groups were eligible to receive payouts from bank settlements. The committee's investigation found that over two years the Obama DOJ directed approximately $1 billion to various outside organizations from settlements; in January the committee had asked the DOJ to preserve all related documents and emails.

## Framing in the corpus

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] introduces the topic by asking why the Obama DOJ imposed a $780 million penalty on [[ubs|UBS]] if the bank had already purchased protection from the Clintons, and answers that the Clintons were not the only officials using government positions to extract money from corporations. He links the practice to Q drop 36 (November 2, 2017), which asked what "SLUSH FUND" Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] had ended and how George Soros, Obama, [[hillary-clinton|Clinton]], Eric Holder and Loretta Lynch all net many millions of dollars within a single tax year. The article concludes that Sessions terminated the arrangement and that the evidence gathered will feature in a future prosecution phase — a contested claim.
