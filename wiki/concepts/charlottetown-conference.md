# Charlottetown Conference

The Charlottetown convention of 1864 was the founding meeting of the Canadian Confederation project, held while the American [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] was still being fought.[1] Matthew Ehret describes it as a week-long "booze-soaked orgy" that hammered out the resolutions later put into law by the [[british-north-america-act|British North America Act]] of 1 July 1867.[1]

Ehret frames the timing as the essential point: in 1864, as one confederacy founded upon slavery was being shut down, a conference in Charlottetown was preparing the groundwork for a new confederacy founded upon monarchical principles — the outlook expressed by [[georges-etienne-cartier|Sir Georges-Etienne Cartier]] when he insisted Canada must have "a distinct form of government in which the monarchical spirit will be found."[1]
