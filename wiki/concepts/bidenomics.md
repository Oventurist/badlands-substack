# Bidenomics

"Bidenomics" is the branding term used to describe the economic doctrine of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Originally adopted by the White House as a badge of honor, the label came to be treated by late 2023 as a political liability as public sentiment on the economy remained skeptical.[1]

## Retreat from the term

By December 2023 reporting noted that Biden had largely stopped saying "Bidenomics" in public appearances.[1] The word was absent from nearly all of his November appearances, including his prepared remarks in Colorado touting the Inflation Reduction Act and his speeches at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in California.[1] The last occasion on which he prominently used the term was a November 1 speech in Minnesota, where he invoked it four times and equated it with the American Dream, saying "Folks, Bidenomics is just another way of saying 'the American Dream.'"[1]

The branding did not vanish entirely. Biden's re-election campaign continued to deploy it in subtler forms — on signage at the Colorado event, in the titles of presidential events and speeches, and in social media messaging.[1] The [[white-house|White House]] posted on X on November 30 that in Colorado Biden "highlighted how Bidenomics is creating jobs and opportunities – unleashing over $7 billion in new investments across the state."[1] Mainstream outlets including [[nbc-news|NBC News]] noticed and reported on the term's disappearance from the president's own speeches.[1]

## Related economic backdrop

The retreat coincided with market turbulence in which gold reached a record high above $2,130 an ounce and [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] climbed above $40,000, developments Badlands writers read as a verdict on the credibility of fiat monetary policy under [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] and the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1]

## Retail results as counter-evidence (2024)

By May 2024 the term had become a standing rebuttal to official claims of economic strength, invoked whenever consumer data undercut the White House message.[2] [[target-corporation|Target]]'s quarterly earnings miss and weak forecast that month — which sent its shares down 7.7 percent to $143.84, their sharpest one-day drop since November 2022 — were treated in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as a market verdict on the administration's economic narrative.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described enjoying pointing out "Bidenomics" to shoppers complaining about prices such as ground beef at nearly $7 a pound, noting that politicians and news anchors who do not do their own grocery shopping rarely encounter the disconnect.[2] Citing Investopedia's observation that consumer spending accounts for roughly 65 percent of U.S. gross domestic product, she argued that while the [[white-house|White House]] maintains the economy is strong, Target's numbers disagreed, and concluded that "in Bidenomics, the majority of Americans are just trying to survive."[2]

## September 2023 gas prices

During the [[2023-gasoline-price-surge|September 2023 gasoline price surge]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] continued to tout Bidenomics and to insist the economy was good even as pump prices hit a record high for that point in the year, and dismissed his claim of being "laser-focused on cutting energy costs" through clean-energy investment as offering no scalable near-term relief.[3]

## Abandonment of the message (January 2024)

By January 2024 the "Bidenomics" message had deflated to the point that [[joe-biden|President Biden]]'s campaign shifted its emphasis to January 6-related attacks on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], framing him as a threat to democracy in a speech at Valley Forge, Pennsylvania.[4] Senior campaign officials highlighted the location's association with George Washington's willingness to relinquish power, contrasting it with Trump's alleged attempts to cling to office, while campaign manager [[julie-chavez-rodriguez|Julie Chavez Rodriguez]] said that "over the last three years, MAGA Republicans haven't shied away from the Big Lie — they've doubled down."[4]

Badlands writer [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued that the pivot showed the lie becoming so exposed that its perpetrators had no option but to double down, with Biden abandoning claims of an "amazing economy" in the face of what Fleuret called his administration's horrendous economic policies and returning to attacks on Trump.[4] He described the cycle of exposure and re-spun narrative as exhausting and concluded that "this Administration is a cooked goose, and they know it."[4]

## November 2023: an admitted liability

By late November 2023 the term had become openly toxic within the Democratic Party.[5] Washington Post journalist Leigh Ann Caldwell told NBC's Meet the Press that "'Bidenomics' has really become a negative word, especially among Democrats because it's not working."[5] The same week, White House press secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] told [[fox-news|Fox News]]' [[peter-doocy|Peter Doocy]] that prices of basic items "are going down," omitting that Bloomberg data showed typical household items up by double digits between January 2020 and October 2023 — $119.27 now required to buy what $100 once did, with groceries and electricity up 25 percent, used cars up 35 percent, auto insurance up 33 percent and rents roughly 20 percent.[5] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Democratic strategists knew the message was unusable even with their base, that the administration was "all in" on Bidenomics regardless, and that the gaslighting could itself become the trigger for a mass political awakening among Democratic voters.[5]
