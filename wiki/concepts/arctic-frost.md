# Arctic Frost

Arctic Frost is the codename of an [[fbi|FBI]] investigation conducted during the Biden administration that swept up communications records of individuals aligned with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The investigation became a focus of congressional oversight and of conservative claims of government weaponization after the scope of its subpoenas and data requests became public in 2026.[1]

## Phone records of Patel and Wiles

In late February 2026, Reuters reported — citing statements by FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] — that the Bureau had subpoenaed the phone records of Patel and [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] under Arctic Frost while both were private citizens, before they assumed their present roles as FBI Director and [[white-house|White House]] Chief of Staff respectively.[1] Those records were found in files labeled "Prohibited."[1]

## Scope

Congressional oversight disclosures indicated that hundreds of Trump-aligned individuals and groups, including sitting members of [[congress|Congress]], were targeted with subpoenas and data requests under Arctic Frost, drawing scrutiny over the investigation's scope and methods.[1] Materials relating to the matter were published by the Senate Judiciary Committee.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Arctic Frost was "looking more and more like a reckoning," arguing that the secret collection of records from Patel, Wiles, and hundreds of other Trump allies was hard to spin as anything other than political targeting, and predicting that media would frame accountability for that abuse of power as itself an abuse of power.[1] She summarized the reversal of roles with the phrase "the hunted have become the hunters."[1]

## Judicial Watch FOIA litigation (February 2026)

[[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] filed a lawsuit in the US District Court for the District of Columbia against the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] seeking records related to the Arctic Frost investigation, described as the codename for a Washington Field Office Public Corruption Unit inquiry involving President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The suit followed an unanswered Freedom of Information Act request submitted on October 7, 2025, and sought records on investigative approvals, investigative techniques, and communications with Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s office.[2]

## Paid "Sedition Hunters" informants

Memos reviewed by *Just the News* and reported in January 2026 showed that the [[fbi|FBI]] under the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration paid more than $150,000 to members of the [[sedition-hunters|Sedition Hunters]], a group of tech sleuths with anti-Trump leanings and known foreign ties, to help identify suspects in the January 6 Capitol riot and in the Arctic Frost investigation.[3] Some informants used facial recognition and spyware to locate American citizens for potential arrest, and payments included $20,000 in the summer of 2023 approved by a deputy to then-Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] for video analysis linking Trump supporters at the Ellipse speech to the Capitol riot.[3]

FBI officials said all supervisors involved had been dismissed and that an audit was underway, while the arrangement raised concerns that bureau policies on informant bias, foreign influence and secrecy had been violated — the Sedition Hunters having openly acknowledged their assistance online despite the usual secrecy requirement for confidential human sources.[3] FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] said he planned to work with [[congress|Congress]] to create safeguards against similar entanglements in future.[3]

## Jack Smith criminal referral (July 2026)

On July 22, 2026 the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] cited Arctic Frost in referring former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for criminal prosecution over allegedly false statements made in his December 17, 2025 sworn deposition.[4] The committee's letter to Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] restated that during the Biden-Harris DOJ probe investigators unconstitutionally subpoenaed the phone records of over a dozen Republican Members of [[congress|Congress]] and used gag orders to hide that the records had been obtained.[4]

According to the referral, Smith's team proposed and he approved the vast majority of additional toll record subpoenas, and internal DOJ experts warned the actions might be unconstitutional yet prosecutors moved forward anyway.[4] The letter flagged potential violations of 18 U.S.C. § 1001 and of the Speech or Debate Clause protecting congressional communications.[4] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the largely elite victims of Arctic Frost had themselves created, funded and defended the surveillance state and had assumed they were exempt from it.[4]

## October 2025 declassifications

Newly declassified revelations related to Arctic Frost, reported in October 2025, chronicled what Republicans described as the 2022 lawfare assault against then-former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and MAGA world, as criminal inquiries — which would lead to criminal charges — spun into high gear while Trump leaned toward running for president again.[5]

The revelation that the [[fbi|FBI]] had snooped on the phone records of Republican members of [[congress|Congress]] during its January 6 investigation brought greater scrutiny to then-FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]], during whose tenure the bureau effort occurred, and to then-Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], who led the Biden Justice Department's investigation into Trump.[5]

The disclosures also spotlighted former key FBI official [[timothy-thibault|Timothy Thibault]], whom Republicans argue showed extreme anti-Trump bias, demonstrated a willingness to target Trump early in his first term, attempted to slow-walk or block the FBI's investigation into [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], and in April 2022 helped spark the investigation dubbed Arctic Frost — later carried on by Smith — which led to criminal charges against Trump related to the Capitol riot.[5]

## Verizon disclosure and Boasberg impeachment push (November 2025)

On November 4, 2025, [[verizon|Verizon]] acknowledged that it had secretly provided the call records of Republican lawmakers to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] as part of the Arctic Frost investigation, which served as the foundation for the federal indictment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and said it would implement new safeguards requiring top-level review before complying with subpoenas targeting members of Congress.[6] A Verizon spokesman said the company had been ordered in 2023 to turn over the records, that former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] and the Justice Department decided who to target, and that a court order barred the company from telling anyone.[6] Chief Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] approved the orders preventing carriers from notifying the lawmakers, and Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]] introduced articles of impeachment against Boasberg over that role the same day.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as intimidation and coercion falling squarely within the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]].[6]
