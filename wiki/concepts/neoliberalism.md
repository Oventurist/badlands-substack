# Neoliberalism

Neoliberalism, in the account of [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], was another name given to globalization, and its intention was always toxic.[1] With protective tariffs applied across the board by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in April 2025, many proclaimed the age of neoliberalism dead.[1]

## The caste model

Ehret describes the program as stripping the productive industrial powers of each nation-state down to dependent shells, organizing the world around a caste system of first, second and third worlds under a design outlined by [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]]'s [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], which he says took over the US government in 1973.[1] "First world" nations were told to embrace a culture of post-industrial consumerism without the means to produce for themselves.[1] "Second world" nations were to become cheap-labor sweatshops kept too poor to consume the goods they exported, and to receive [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] or [[world-bank|World Bank]] aid they had to accept "conditionalities" preventing large-scale infrastructure building of the kind many assassinated nationalist leaders had pursued.[1] "Third world" nations were denied even factory sweatshops and treated as resource extraction zones run by mineral cartels as vast slave plantations.[1]

## Intellectual and monetary foundations

Ehret dates the model's serious beginning to 1971, when the US dollar was removed from the gold reserve system and fixed exchange rates, and runs it to April 2, 2025.[1] The theories of [[milton-friedman|Milton Friedman]] and Friedrich von Hayek were applied across the trans-Atlantic as a "post-Keynesian" dialectic in which deregulation and open markets defined a trajectory toward nation-stripping and the transfer of power to a small oligarchical class.[1] Economic value itself was redefined as merely financial services devoid of any reality, transforming the trans-Atlantic economy into a time bomb built atop hyperbolically growing speculative bubbles.[1]

## Successor phase

The system that replaced the industrial model was named [[post-industrial-monetarism|post-industrial monetarism]], ushered in by [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]]'s destruction of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods]] fixed exchange rate system.[1] Ehret argues that critics of tariffs such as [[jeffrey-sachs|Jeffrey Sachs]] ignore that in 250 years the only times the United States successfully industrialized and increased the productive powers of labor came through protective tariffs — a fact he says applies to every nation.[1] Where unbounded free trade was the norm, industrial growth diminished, speculation increased, and empires benefited by keeping their victims underdeveloped, corrupt and poor.[1]
