# First Inaugural Address of Donald Trump

The inaugural address delivered by [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] on January 20, 2017 is treated by Badlands Media as a coded declaration of war against the criminal networks that, in the outlet's telling, covertly exert undue influence over free people worldwide.[1] Writing in March 2023, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that on that day the then President-elect "uttered words that would send shockwaves" through a secret group of blackmailers and debt peddlers who weaponize vice, run international banking schemes and manufacture crises to accumulate power.[1]

## The transfer-of-power passage

The line singled out as central is Trump's statement that the ceremony had special meaning "because today we are not merely transferring power from one administration to another, or from one party to another, but we are transferring power from Washington, D.C., and giving it back to you, the people."[1] Deschamps paired it with a second passage — "What truly matters is not which party controls our government, but whether our government is controlled by the people. January 20th, 2017 will be remembered as the day the people became the rulers of this nation again" — and read the two together as an admission that the American people had not, in fact, been ruling themselves.[1]

In the article's framework, "power" means discretion over one's own life: the capacity of a citizen to defend their rights and exercise lawfully-based freedoms, and to have their will represented in decisions that affect them.[1] People with no power over their own lives, it argues, are properly called slaves or prisoners, and a subjected nation is called conquered; the conclusion drawn is that Americans enjoy only pre-prescribed choices produced by globalists, international bankers and their collaborators, and that the American people are therefore "a conquered people."[1]

## The establishment passage

A second passage is presented as prophetic of the [[covid-19-lockdowns|pandemic response]] that followed: "For too long, a small group in our nation's capital has reaped the rewards of government, while the people have borne the cost. Washington flourished, but the people did not share in its wealth. Politicians prospered, but the jobs left and the factories closed. The establishment protected itself, but not the citizens of our country."[1] Deschamps read this as Trump naming the [[two-tier-justice-system|two-tiered justice system]] — one set of rules for average citizens and another for the establishment — years before lockdowns, mandates and pandemic-era [[2020-congressional-insider-trading-scandal|insider trading]] made the disparity vivid.[1]

## Reception within the Badlands framework

The address is used throughout the essay as evidence for its refrain that "Trump knows": that the president understood the mass mind control, [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] and legal manipulation used against the public, and proposed the only real remedy, a genuine return of power to the people.[1] The article concedes the remedy is not risk-free, noting that historically when populations regain power — often through [[color-revolution|color revolution]]-style campaigns run by intelligence agencies — elites have recaptured control quickly, with United States history offered as a leading example.[1]
