# Mockingbird Media

"Mockingbird Media" is the term used across the Badlands corpus for the mainstream press understood as an instrument of state-aligned psychological operations rather than as journalism — a usage derived from the Cold War-era Operation Mockingbird allegations.[1]

## Claimed function

In the analysis of [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], the entire purpose of the Mockingbird Media is to prevent the public from setting down its metaphorical and literal rocks long enough for "the fog of war" to dissipate and for the puppeteers behind the scenes to become visible.[1] Divisive propaganda, on this account, must do two things: activate the amygdala, and provoke a pre-programmed response.[1] It is not sufficient for audiences to be fearful or enraged; the essential ingredient is that they discharge those emotions in a direction chosen by the social engineers.[1] Anger is compared to rocket fuel — abundant but useless if the rocket cannot be aimed.[1] This claimed function is offered as one of many reasons [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the mainstream media the enemy of the people.[1]

The two operational pillars identified are [[nominalization|nominalizations]] and hypnotic repetition: attach as much emotion as possible to a word for which no commonly agreed definition exists, then repeat it until the learned response becomes automatic, however illogical or self-destructive.[1]

## Claimed breakdown

The essay argues that the technology is failing. Citing Rasmussen polling that 58% of American voters believe the media are deliberately misleading them — a figure the author expects to climb — it contends that people are losing sight of *where* to direct their emotions, which would signal a critical breakdown in the Mockingbird social-engineering apparatus.[1] The author cautions that old programming persists even among the 58%, who likely believe their own preferred outlets could not be deceiving them, but holds that the [[israel-palestine-conflict|Israel-Palestine conflict]] is acting as a wrecking ball against tribal loyalty on both right and left.[1] Popular culture is cited as evidence of the shift, including a skit by comedian Ryan Long and a comic strip by the cartoonist Stonetoss.[1]

The author frames the discrediting of the mainstream media not as malice but as a "survival imperative," comparable to the necessary exposure of a pathological liar — though only a first step.[1]
