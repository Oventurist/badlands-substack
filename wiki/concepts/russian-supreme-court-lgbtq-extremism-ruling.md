# Russian Supreme Court LGBTQ Extremism Ruling

On Thursday, November 30, 2023, the [[supreme-court-of-russia|Supreme Court of Russia]] declared what it called the "international LGBTQ movement" an extremist organization and banned all activities associated with it in the country.[1]

## The decision

The court ruled in favour of a motion filed by the [[ministry-of-justice-russia|Ministry of Justice]], which claimed that the LGBTQ community risked "inciting social and religious discord," in violation of Russia's Law on Countering Extremism — a characterization drawn from a United Nations statement condemning the outcome.[1] The operative language, as reported by state news agency RIA Novosti, read: "The claims are to be satisfied: to recognize the international LGBT movement as an extremist organization and to prohibit its activities in Russia."[1]

The four-hour hearing was held behind closed doors, with only the Justice Ministry present and the case materials classified.[1] RIA Novosti reported that the decision was effective immediately.[1]

## Context and effects

The landmark ruling was expected to further erode the rights of Russia's LGBTQ community, which had faced an intensifying crackdown in recent years as President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] sought to shore up his image as a defender of traditional moral values against the liberal West.[1] No legally recognized LGBTQ community exists in Russia under the country's anti-gay propaganda law, a point that made the scope of the ban unusually open-ended.[1] The ruling was reported by [[cnn|CNN]] and carried in the Badlands News Brief bonus items.[1]
