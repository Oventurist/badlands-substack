# Musk–Zuckerberg Cage Match

The **Musk–Zuckerberg cage match** was a repeatedly proposed but never realized mixed martial arts bout between [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], owner of X (formerly Twitter), and [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], chief executive of [[meta-platforms|Meta]].[1] Musk announced on X in August 2023 that he would fight his "arch-nemesis" in an MMA cage match, generating immediate internet enthusiasm.[1]

## Course of the dispute

The excitement deflated a day later when Musk wrote that the exact date was "still in flux" and that he was getting an MRI of his neck and upper back.[1] Hopes faded further when Musk responded to TED Talks host Chris Anderson by agreeing that a "battle of brains" in a cage-match-style debate would be more "noble."[1] The proposal then revived again, with Musk floating an "epic location" in Italy.[1]

Zuckerberg publicly refused to treat the matter as settled, posting on [[threads|Threads]]: "I've been ready to fight since the day Elon challenged me. If he ever agrees on an actual date, you'll hear it from me. Until then, please assume anything he says has not been agreed on."[1] He added that he was "not holding my breath for Elon" and that when he competed he wanted to spotlight elite athletes by working with professional organizations such as the [[ufc|UFC]] or ONE.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dismissed the affair as "bread and circuses," arguing that in a media environment where most mainstream stories function as intentional distractions this one was "a little on the nose."[1] She predicted the fight would never occur, noting reports that Musk would require surgery first.[1] From a business standpoint she read it as a publicity stunt to drive engagement to Meta and X, since the event was to be streamed on both platforms, and compared the proposed staging to the film *Gladiator*.[1] Handicapping the matchup, she picked Zuckerberg on the basis of superior training and experience despite Musk's size advantage — an assessment she noted was uncomfortable given her pro-X and anti-Meta sympathies — and said she would only watch if it were free rather than pay-per-view.[1]
