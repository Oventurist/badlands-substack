# Arizona Senate Bill 1695

Arizona Senate Bill 1695 was a 2023 election-integrity bill introduced in the Arizona Legislature and aimed at combating voter fraud, described at the time as a potential turning point in the state's contentious election disputes.[1] It was sponsored by Republican state Senator [[jake-hoffman|Jake Hoffman]] and was approved by the Senate Government Committee on February 16, 2023.[1]

## Provisions

Under the proposal, an election would not be certified if voters were disenfranchised because election laws had been violated.[1] Where such violations occurred, the bill called for a complete redo of the election.[1] Its terms applied to Arizona counties with more than one million residents — a threshold that in practice targeted the state's largest jurisdictions.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the necessity of such a bill "betrays a much bigger problem": that the American public had lost touch with what a valid election looks like and with its power to hold government accountable.[1] He framed elections as a critical and sacred means by which citizens engage their government, and asserted that officials, employees, and contractors should work to carry out the will of the people even when inconvenient or contrary to precedent.[1] In his view, confidence in the disputed election had collapsed not from frivolous public judgement but because of evidence and admissions of error, malfeasance, and breach of trust across the election apparatus.[1] Deschamps concluded that summary dismissal of citizen concerns without investigation is itself evidence of governmental contempt, and that while the bill was a step in the right direction, much work remained.[1]
