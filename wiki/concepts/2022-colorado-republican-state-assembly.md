# 2022 Colorado Republican State Assembly and Convention

The 2022 Colorado Republican State Assembly and Convention was the candidate-selection gathering held in April 2022 at which the voting members of the [[colorado-republican-party|Colorado Republican Party]] determined which candidates would appear on the party's June primary ballot.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who is not a Republican, attended and covered the event as a journalist, and later used it as a case study in how state party assembly and convention processes can be manipulated.[1]

## Structure of the process

The convention is the stage at which credentialed delegates decide ballot access. Before the full body votes on statewide offices — in 2022 Governor, Secretary of State, and Attorney General, and in a presidential cycle also President of the United States — the congressional districts nominate and vote on candidates for district-level offices, much of that work taking place before the convention proper.[1] Colorado also permits candidates to bypass the assembly entirely by petitioning onto the primary ballot, a route that allows a candidate to avoid facing the party's own voting members.[1]

## District results

On its face the 2022 convention was a win for the [[america-first-movement|America First]] grassroots, who had spent 2021 filling vacant party positions under the [[precinct-strategy|precinct strategy]].[1] [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] swept the third congressional district.[1] In the fourth district, a largely unknown candidate, Bob Lewis, was nominated from the floor to primary incumbent [[ken-buck|Ken Buck]] and took 62% of the vote, appearing ahead of Buck on the primary ballot; Ashe in America characterized Buck as an incumbent who dissents from the establishment only when the establishment has enough votes to win without him.[1] In the fifth district, covering [[el-paso-county|El Paso County]], Representative [[doug-lamborn|Doug Lamborn]] declined to participate in the assembly at all and petitioned onto the ballot instead, a choice she attributed to his unpopularity among Republicans in his district.[1]

## The paper-ballot fight

At the morning read-out of the Rules Committee, several voting members attempted to bar the party's [[edivote100-clickers|electronic "clicker" voting devices]] and conduct the assembly on hand-marked paper ballots.[1] The motion required amending several related rules first, and the resulting procedure was convoluted and confusing to the 3,772 credentialed delegates.[1] Chairwoman [[kristi-burton-brown|Kristi Burton Brown]] — elected to her post in 2021 only with last-minute grassroots support — and the parliamentarian refused to allow debate and did not disclose that the watermarked paper ballots, clear ballot boxes, and other provisions named in the motion were already onsite and ready to use; Brown instead framed the request as an impossibility.[1] One delegate said afterward, "I didn't understand what they were asking me to vote on! Then Kristi said we'd be there until midnight, and we wouldn't know the results."[1] A Weld County delegate reported that only ten minutes total had been allotted for motions from the 3,772 delegates, all of which was consumed by the paper-ballot fight.[1] The motion failed two separate votes and the clickers were used.[1]

## Statewide votes and the governor's race

The first statewide vote, for Governor, shocked the arena. Energy in the room had consolidated around Greg Lopez, Heidi Ganahl, and [[danielle-neuschwanger|Danielle Neuschwanger]], with Neuschwanger showing the most visible support in t-shirts and signs.[1] The reported result was Lopez 34%, Ganahl 32%, and Neuschwanger 27%; with a 30% threshold required for ballot access, Neuschwanger fell roughly 112 votes short.[1] Volunteers at her table were immediately bombarded with claims that the clickers had not worked.[1]

In the US Senate race, State Representative [[ron-hanks|Ron Hanks]] took 39% and was the only candidate to qualify from a crowded field, later facing [[joe-odea|Joe O'Dea]], who had petitioned onto the ballot.[1] For Secretary of State, [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] took a 61% majority of assembled delegates, with lesser-known candidate Mike O'Donnell taking the remainder and also qualifying; Peters faced both O'Donnell and [[pam-anderson|Pam Anderson]] in the primary.[1] With the exception of Neuschwanger, America First candidates won the day at assembly, and candidates who openly campaigned on election integrity outperformed those who straddled the fence.[1]

## Aftermath

Ashe in America's assessment was that the candidates who refused to face Republican voters at assembly, and who petitioned onto the ballot instead, went on to win the primaries despite running little visible campaign against opponents with far greater enthusiasm and public support — after which they lost the general election.[1] The convention and the subsequent [[2022-el-paso-county-recount|El Paso County recount]] formed the core of her warning that the same playbook, exercised in many states in 2022, would likely "rhyme" in the 2024 candidate-selection cycle.[1]
