# Crime Bill of 1994

The Crime Bill of 1994 was federal legislation associated with [[bill-clinton|President Bill Clinton]] and then-Senator [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that, in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s account, increased sentences for non-violent criminals — chiefly drug dealers.[1]

## Alleged motive

Carlson situates the bill within the privatization of the American prison system under Clinton, under which private companies began running prisons for profit.[1] He argues the difficulty was that crime was starting to drop sharply, so those who benefited from full prisons needed the trend reversed, and the Crime Bill supplied the sentencing mechanism.[1]

## Alleged supporting operations

In Carlson's framework the bill did not operate alone. He argues the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[cia|CIA]] engineered [[gangster-rap|gangster rap]] in the late 1980s to steer young Black men toward crime, and that the [[cia|CIA]] imported narcotics from South and Central America for sale in South Central Los Angeles so that there would be drugs to prosecute.[1] He states that by the mid-1990s the stage had been set to fill the private prisons and that kickbacks flowed to the politicians who helped pass the new crime bills.[1] He further ties the period's racial politics — the [[rodney-king|Rodney King]] beating, the [[la-riots-1992|1992 riots]], and the [[oj-simpson|O.J. Simpson]] trial — to the same objective.[1]
