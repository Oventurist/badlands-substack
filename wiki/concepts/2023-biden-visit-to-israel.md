# 2023 Biden Visit to Israel

On October 18, 2023, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] traveled to Tel Aviv to deliver remarks in the aftermath of the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 Hamas attacks]] on [[israel|Israel]], telling Israelis "You're Not Alone."[1]

## Announcement

The trip was announced on Monday, October 16, 2023, when Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] confirmed after more than seven hours of negotiations in Tel Aviv that Biden would travel to Israel that Wednesday.[2] Aides said Biden had expressed strong interest in making the journey after being invited over the weekend by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], whom Biden had known for four decades, and that he spent Monday deliberating over the trip at the White House with his top national security and intelligence advisers.[2] The visit was framed as an extraordinary wartime trip intended to demonstrate staunch support for Israel as it worked to eliminate [[hamas|Hamas]], while also pressing for ways to ease humanitarian suffering in [[gaza|Gaza]] — dueling objectives that carried significant risks as the president sought to prevent the Middle East crisis from widening.[2]

Blinken said the United States and Israel "have agreed to develop a plan that will enable humanitarian aid from donor nations and multilateral organizations to reach civilians in Gaza."[2] Biden was also scheduled to travel to Jordan to meet [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]], Egyptian President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah el-Sisi]], and Palestinian Authority President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], all of whom had condemned the situation in Gaza.[2]

## Remarks

Biden stated during his remarks, "I'm the first US president to visit a nation during a time of war."[1] His speech was heavy on religious imagery and recounted the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] terror incursion into Israel and the number of Jewish victims; early in the address he characterized the resulting death toll, scaled to Israel's population, as akin to "fifteen 9/11's."[1]

He only briefly touched on the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|Gaza hospital bombing]], but stressed that the United States saw the event the same way Israel did: "Based on the information we have seen today it seems to have been the result of an errant missile fired from a terrorist group in Gaza."[1]

Biden also emphasized that "Hamas does not represent the Palestinian people," asserting that the group uses civilians as "human shields" and places command centers and tunnels beneath civilian buildings.[1] While acknowledging the humanitarian crisis in the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]] and the West Bank, he unveiled $100 million in new US assistance for Palestinians, qualifying that aid would flow from Egypt to Gaza under the close watch of inspectors and warning Hamas against thwarting or stealing it: "If it happens it will stop the international community from being able to provide this aid."[1] He demanded that the International Red Cross be given immediate access into Gaza to visit hostages and check on their condition, and renewed a call for a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]].[1] He further affirmed that he would ask [[congress|Congress]] "this week" for "unprecedented" Israel aid.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], had previously described Biden as caught in a political and narrative pincer akin to the "no-win" Kobayashi Maru scenario.[1] He argued that on the first public leg of the president's "super serious peace tour," Biden had reduced what audiences were told was the most catastrophic event of the moment "down to sports analogies," while simultaneously merging the Ukraine and Israel funding narratives through the linked [[2023-supplemental-foreign-aid-request|$100 billion aid package]].[1] He contrasted this with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s brokering of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]], writing that "We're in a War of Stories, and we have better writers."[1]

Ahead of the trip, Burning Bright wrote that the "Potato-in-Chief" was about to attempt to stumble across "one of the most radioactive minefields in the sociopolitical zeitgeist," describing the spectacle as both dark comedy and an "exposure op."[2] He noted polling showing that older American demographics supported Israel while more than half of younger Americans supported the Palestinians, arguing this was the "mass psychological terrain" Biden had to navigate in full view of an increasingly tense public, while Donald Trump commentated from "the most powerful and exclusive peanut gallery in history."[2]
