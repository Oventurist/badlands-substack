# COVID-19 Narrative

The "Covid narrative" is [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s term for the publicly promoted account of the pandemic, which he distinguishes sharply from the pathogen itself.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## Origin claims

Carlson writes that he does believe some sort of bioweapon was released from a lab in Wuhan, China, but that he does not think this is where it originated.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) In his account it originated in a lab in North Carolina and was moved to [[wuhan-lab|Wuhan]] to maintain secrecy and control, a program he alleges was funded and run by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]], who had to move it overseas once [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] came into office.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He further believes other bioweapons were being created in biolabs elsewhere in the world, specifically in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and possibly Taiwan.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Trump, he says, publicly stated that Covid was intentionally created in a lab and released on the world.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## "Chyna virus"

Trump referred to the virus as the "Chyna virus" and was called racist for doing so and for insinuating it came from a Chinese lab.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Carlson argues Trump knows perfectly well where the bioweapon originated, and that the "Chyna virus" formulation is therefore a double meaning pointing at Communism: China is a communist country, and Trump is saying that the CCP is infiltrating America.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) In this reading the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] forced a bioweapon on the world that proved less deadly than hoped, but the communist-controlled media and government officials could achieve the same destructive effect simply by continuing to push the narrative that it was extremely deadly.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## Function

The narrative, rather than the pathogen, is credited with the outcomes: it forced the [[covid-19-lockdowns|lockdowns]], produced an overreaching control of people and an abandonment of human rights, and justified mail-in ballots, by which means Carlson says a duly-elected President was overthrown and removed from power.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He invites readers to consider that what America is experiencing is itself like a man-made virus designed to destroy everything and everyone — narratives and agendas essentially created in a lab and dropped on the population.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## The 2023 scientific mea culpa and media about-face

In late January 2023, [[newsweek|Newsweek]] ran an opinion essay by a medical student and researcher who had supported lockdowns, vaccines and boosters, and who concluded that he and the scientific community had been wrong and that the error cost lives.[2] The essay charged the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]], the [[world-health-organization|WHO]] and the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] with repeatedly overstating the evidence and misleading the public on natural versus artificial immunity, school closures and transmission, aerosol spread, mask mandates, and vaccine effectiveness and safety — errors identifiable at the time, with some obfuscations continuing into the present.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the piece as part of an accelerating about-face by the corporate press on what he called the "Big Lie" of the COVID era, its lockdowns, and its mass medical interventions.[2] He argued that the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] was staging this reversal through opinion pieces functioning as "Narrative Trojan Horses" and as the foundation for a mass "Forgive and Forget" campaign ahead of fuller disclosure of the era's costs, concluding that it was far too late: "We won't forgive. We won't forget."[2]

## Early rejection and the pivot to race riots

In a May 2024 essay, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that had the majority of Americans rejected the Covid narrative from the moment it was imposed, it would never have gained traction and taken hold.[3] Because enough people did not fall prey to it, he wrote, the narrative began to unravel: the hysteria had been intended to last through the 2020 election, but lost so much steam that the planners had to pivot to race riots, which he characterised as a standby tool pulled out of the Deep State's bag of tricks at least once a decade — the BLM riots simply arriving a couple of months earlier than they otherwise would have.[3]

Carlson used the Covid episode as his central case for [[media-distrust-as-default|media distrust as default]], contending that receiving news from a posture of neutrality — extending the benefit of the doubt to the media and government agencies — could have resulted in early vaccination, a decision that later scepticism and fewer boosters would not undo, whereas beginning from distrust would have meant not being injected at all.[3] He noted that many people who believed themselves awake before the Covid narrative was dropped nonetheless feared for their lives, and stated that he personally did not believe the narrative for a second.[3] See also [[fear-as-a-control-mechanism|fear as a control mechanism]].[3]

## Collapse of trust in healthcare (Carlson, 2024)

In a November 2024 essay, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated the Covid narrative as the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s costliest miscalculation, arguing it was a fake pandemic created to keep [[donald-trump|Trump]] from re-election in 2020 that instead destroyed public trust in the healthcare system.[4] He cited a 50-state survey of U.S. adults led by [[northeastern-university|Northeastern University]] professor David Lazer finding that public trust in physicians and hospitals fell from 71.5 percent in April 2020 to 40.1 percent in January 2024 — meaning 31.4 percent of Americans joined the 28.5 percent who already distrusted them, leaving 59.9 percent distrustful, a more than doubling in under four years.[4]

Carlson described a range of public responses to the narrative, from outright refusal of masks and vaccines, to reluctant compliance to keep a job or career, to enthusiastic queuing for vaccination and demonisation of the unvaccinated.[4] He argued that within two months of the pandemic's launch so many Americans were waking up that the apparatus changed direction by fomenting Antifa and Black Lives Matter riots, followed by what he called a staged coup at the Capitol on [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6]], which allowed those questioning the [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 results]] to be labelled "election deniers" and the Deep State to cast itself as the victim.[4] He contended that mandates were subsequently relaxed — no compulsory vaccination, masking or social distancing — because distrust of Covid vaccines threatened to spread to all vaccines, all pharmaceuticals and the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] and [[big-pharma|Big Pharma]] alike, and that under his [[60-40-rule|60/40 rule]] the industry will implode now that a supermajority has withdrawn its trust, particularly with [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] dismantling it.[4]
