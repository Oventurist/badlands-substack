# Corporate Average Fuel Economy Standards

The Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) standards are United States federal regulations that set average fuel economy targets for new vehicles. First established in 1975, they have been tightened over the years with the aim of making vehicles more fuel-efficient.[1]

## Repeal announcement, December 2025

On Wednesday, December 3, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that he was repealing the Biden-era federal fuel economy standards, significantly weakening fuel efficiency requirements for tens of millions of new gasoline-powered cars and light trucks.[1]

The move marked the latest effort by the administration to dismantle pollution regulations and federal support for cleaner-running vehicles and renewable energy.[1] Burning gasoline is a significant contributor to global heating, and transportation is the largest source of greenhouse gas emissions in the United States.[1]

"We are officially terminating Joe Biden's ridiculously burdensome, horrible actually, Cafe standards that imposed expensive restrictions," Trump said in an Oval Office announcement, flanked by top automobile executives including the chief executives of Ford and Stellantis.[1] He added that the standards "put tremendous upward pressure on car prices, combined with the insane electric vehicle mandate."[1]
