# Six-Day War

The Six-Day War was the June 1967 conflict between Israel and its Arab neighbors, after which Israel took control of the Palestinian territories it has occupied ever since.[1] The war is the historical starting point for the occupation addressed by the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] in its July 2024 advisory opinion, which found Israel's presence in the occupied Palestinian territories unlawful and called for it to end, along with an immediate halt to settlement construction.[1]

## Significance in the 2024 ICJ ruling

The ICJ's 15-judge panel described a sweeping condemnation of Israeli rule over lands captured 57 years earlier, pointing to the building and expansion of settlements in the West Bank and east Jerusalem, exploitation of the area's natural resources, annexation and imposition of permanent control, and discriminatory policies against Palestinians.[1] Commentary in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief emphasized that despite Israel having occupied Palestine since the Six-Day War of June 1967, no complaint against Israel had ever been granted standing by the court prior to February 2024 — a record attributed to the political selectivity of the [[united-nations|United Nations]] system rather than to the absence of grievances.[1]

The Brief further argued that Israel had engaged in other acts of aggression against its neighbors over the course of its history without ever being challenged by the UN or its courts, and framed the 2024 ruling as branding the occupied territories an apartheid state and marking a turning point against the globalist consensus.[1]
