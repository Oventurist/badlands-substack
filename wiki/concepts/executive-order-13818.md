# Executive Order 13818

Executive Order 13818 is a sanctions order signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on December 20, 2017, targeting individuals implicated in serious human rights abuse and corruption.[1]

## Gertler designation

The order's accompanying sanctions list names [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] as number three, thrusting the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DRC]] mining assets [[mutanda-mining|Mutanda Mining]] and [[kamoto-copper-company|Kamoto Copper Company]] into the middle of a high-level geopolitical conflict.[1] Assets tied to the designation were frozen, and a series of court cases — largely in foreign jurisdictions — followed, with mineral rights stripped away from the companies involved.[1]

## The Gertler reversal and reimposition

Badlands commentary treats Executive Order 13818 as central to the case of Israeli mining oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]], who was sanctioned under its authority during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term.[2] According to [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], on January 15, 2021, Israel's then-ambassador [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] entered the [[state-department|State Department]] and reversed those sanctions, a maneuver reported by Bloomberg but overshadowed at the time by coverage of January 6.[2] Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] reissued the sanctions in March 2021, after which the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] prosecuted Gertler and stripped him of his African mining rights in 2022.[2]

## Invoked in the DR Congo minerals debate (2025)

Badlands commentary in April 2025 tied the prospective [[us-dr-congo-critical-minerals-deal|US–DR Congo critical minerals deal]] to Executive Order 13818, signed in December 2017, which sanctioned Israeli mining oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]].[3] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the order stripped globalist interests of control over Congolese rare earth minerals, prompting attempts to destabilize the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DR Congo]] through proxy terrorist groups, and that those efforts ultimately positioned the United States to claim a share of the estimated $24 trillion in mineral wealth.[3]
