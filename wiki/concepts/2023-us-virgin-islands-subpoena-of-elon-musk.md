# 2023 US Virgin Islands Subpoena of Elon Musk

In May 2023, the government of the [[us-virgin-islands|U.S. Virgin Islands]] moved to subpoena billionaire [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] for documents in its lawsuit seeking to hold [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] liable for sex trafficking committed by [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## Background

The Virgin Islands, where Epstein maintained an estate, sued JPMorgan in 2022, alleging that its investigation had shown the bank enabled Epstein's recruiters to pay victims and was "indispensable to the operation and concealment of the Epstein trafficking enterprise."[1] Epstein died by suicide in 2019 in a federal jail in Manhattan while awaiting sex trafficking charges.[1]

Musk has never been publicly accused of any wrongdoing connected to Epstein.[1] Over the years there had been unconfirmed speculation — encouraged by Epstein himself in press interviews — that Epstein advised Musk on certain business matters; spokespeople for Musk denied those reports.[1] In a court filing, the Virgin Islands government said it believed Epstein may have referred, or attempted to refer, Musk to JPMorgan as a potential client, which formed the basis for the document subpoena.[1] Lawyers for JPMorgan did not immediately respond to requests for comment, according to reporting by the [[associated-press|Associated Press]].[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] noted the competing readings of Musk's role in the story, writing that some in the Truth Community regard Musk as a possible "white hat" who may hold damaging information about the Deep State and whose purchase of Twitter was part of a larger operation.[1] Deschamps declined to render a verdict, saying he could not say for sure whether Musk was "on the right side of history" but that "there are a lot of reasons to believe he might be," and predicted the subpoena might gain significance over time.[1]
