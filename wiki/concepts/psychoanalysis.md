# Psychoanalysis

Psychoanalysis is a clinical method, developed by [[sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]], for evaluating and treating pathologies explained as originating in conflicts in the psyche, carried out through dialogue between a patient and a psychoanalyst — in other words, treating people for ailments occurring on an unconscious level.[1]

## Origins

Freud was introduced to hypnosis in 1885, which led him toward techniques he regarded as more consistent, and psychoanalysis was born out of that search.[1]

## Arrival in America

Badlands Media commentary treats the arrival of psychoanalysis in the United States during the 1910s as the introduction of "control through the unconscious." Freud's work reached [[james-jackson-putnam|James Jackson Putnam]] of [[harvard-university|Harvard]], whose public endorsement was a breakthrough for the psychoanalytic cause in America, and in 1911 Putnam and [[ernest-jones|Ernest Jones]] founded the [[american-psychoanalytic-association|American Psychoanalytic Association]].[1]

## Application to persuasion

The same understanding of the unconscious mind was carried into commercial and political persuasion by Freud's nephew [[edward-bernays|Edward Bernays]], who became the father of public relations and modern advertising and argued in his 1928 book *Propaganda* that propaganda is not merely beneficial but necessary in governing a nation.[1] In this reading, the 1910s were the decade in which Americans began to be altered and controlled unconsciously — brainwashed.[1]
