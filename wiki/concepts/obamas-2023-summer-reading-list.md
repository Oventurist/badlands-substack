# Obama's 2023 Summer Reading List

Obama's 2023 summer reading list was an annual book recommendation published by former president [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] to his social media following on July 20, 2023.[1] Ordinarily treated as light seasonal content, the 2023 edition drew commentary from [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writers who read it as an act of narrative curation directed at an audience of roughly 132 million Twitter followers, and therefore as a move within the broader [[information-war|information war]].[1]

## Contents of the list

The list comprised nine titles — one biography, one work of social commentary, one historical nonfiction narrative, and six novels.[1] They were: *Poverty, by America* by Matthew Desmond, which argues that poverty persists in the United States because of choices made by more fortunate Americans; *Small Mercies* by Dennis Lehane, a novel in which a teenage girl's disappearance coincides with the death of a young Black man struck by a subway train; *King: A Life* by Jonathan Eig, a biography of Martin Luther King Jr. that confronts King's extramarital affairs and struggles with depression while arguing these failings did not diminish his civil-rights commitment; and *Hello Beautiful* by Ann Napolitano, a novel compared by reviewers to *Little Women*.[1]

Also included were *All the Sinners Bleed* by S.A. Cosby, a thriller centered on a Black sheriff pursuing a serial killer in a combustible small town; *Birnam Wood* by Eleanor Catton, described as a psychological thriller Shakespearean in drama and Austenian in wit; *What Napoleon Could Not Do* by DK Nnuro, a novel viewing America through three characters with ties to Africa; *The Wager: A Tale of Shipwreck, Mutiny and Murder* by David Grann, a nonfiction account of 1740s British castaways whose heroism was later recast as mutiny; and *Blue Hour* by Tiffany Clarke Harrison, narrated by a biracial photographer grappling with motherhood against a backdrop of police brutality.[1]

## Commentary and the shadow-presidency reading

The Badlands commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied the list to a 2020 remark Obama made to Stephen Colbert, in which he mused that he would happily govern through "a stand-in or front-man or front-woman" wearing an earpiece while he worked from his basement in sweats.[1] On that reading, a former president operating a shadow presidency would be expected to appear publicly disengaged while quietly shaping opinion — for instance by running a book club and directing a mass following toward particular stories.[1]

The analysis held that although the literary vehicles varied widely, the thematic content was tightly unified around race and class, and that the release amounted to a rebuke of American race and class arrangements aimed at Obama's fan base.[1] The writer explicitly declined to disparage the individual books, noting that several had been added to her own reading list, and framed the observation as a question rather than an accusation: whether the collection was "innocent or insidious," released by a figure imagined "lounging by the pool on his multimillion dollar Martha's Vineyard estate."[1]
