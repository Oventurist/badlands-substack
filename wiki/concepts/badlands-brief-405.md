# Badlands Brief: Agencies Aflutter, Corporate Compacts & Discombobulated Drones

"Agencies Aflutter, Corporate Compacts & Discombobulated Drones" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on February 12, 2026. Like other editions in the series, it pairs summaries of mainstream news reports with commentary from Badlands contributors, in this case [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. The edition covers a proposed federal compact with artificial intelligence firms over data center energy use, a White House meeting between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], a directive routing 2020 election intelligence to attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]], remarks by [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] on French policy in Africa, a reported cartel drone incursion near El Paso, and two bonus items on congressional testimony by [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and a mass shooting in Canada.

## AI data centers, energy prices and the endangerment finding

The Brief opens with a *[[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]]* opinion piece titled "Even Trump Can't Go Back to the Future," published on Tuesday, which argued that structural shifts in the American energy economy limit how far any administration can reverse current trends. The Brief summarizes the editorial's framing: that US electricity demand is rising, driven in part by data centers and AI expansion; that the energy mix has shifted toward natural gas and renewables over the past fifteen years largely organically; and that coal generation has declined because of cheap gas and plant retirements rather than regulation alone. The piece concludes, in the Brief's characterization, that "nothing can stop what is coming" with the energy transition, because market forces, capital cycles and physical infrastructure constraints shape outcomes more than executive action.

The Brief then reports that *[[politico|Politico]]*, citing two administration officials granted anonymity to discuss private conversations, said on Monday that the Trump administration is courting the world's largest technology companies to publicly commit to a new AI data center compact. *Politico* claimed to have seen a draft of the compact and described commitments intended to ensure that energy-hungry data centers do not raise household electricity prices, strain water supplies or undermine grid reliability, and that the companies driving demand bear the cost of new infrastructure. A related *Politico* report, "Poll shows Democrats hold edge over Trump in energy cost battle," found that 37% of US adults believed Democrats care more about protecting them from spiking natural gas, heating and utility bills, against 25% who said Republicans care more.

Burning Bright's commentary frames energy as the substrate of all historical conflict, writing that "every great conflict in human history has been, at its core, a war over energy (power) projection," and that the "Golden Age War" is fought on both Actual and Narrative fronts as patriots seek to reclaim an Age of Abundance he believes was "stolen from us and reverse-engineered into hard-capped and uneven austerity." He argues that two narratives collided in the same week without notice. On one side, the administration announced what he calls the largest act of deregulation in American history: the full repeal of the Obama-era [[endangerment-finding|endangerment finding]] that branded greenhouse gases a threat to public health and served as the legal cornerstone of decades of [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] climate mandates. Motor vehicle emission standards, compliance programs, credit schemes and reporting obligations were, in his words, "ripped out root and stem."

Burning Bright describes the reaction of what he calls the [[media-protectorate|Media Protectorate]] as immediate and predictable, with environmental groups "wailing about millions endangered" and Democratic strongholds vowing legal warfare, crystallizing a frame of Trump as an existential threat rolling back settled science on behalf of fossil-fuel oligarchs. He labels this the "Hegelian Nightmare in its purest form," in which progress is painted as planetary suicide and abundance recast as greed. Against this, he points to counter-deployments on the same day: reports of a voluntary compact with OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta requiring them to bear 100% of the infrastructure costs of their AI data centers — new generation, transmission upgrades, long-term contracts to shield residential ratepayers, water positivity mandates, grid reliability commitments and community mitigation — designed to preempt an energy crunch as data center demand threatens to triple by 2028. He treats the accompanying poll showing Democrats with a double-digit edge on energy costs as a narrative deployment against Trump's domestic energy acceleration agenda, one that blames a fossil fuel transition and nuclear renaissance for rising bills rather than crediting them as decentralized, sovereign solutions.

Ashe in America responds that requiring corporations to enter compacts "feels right in an age where corporations are more powerful than nation states," especially technology companies. She notes that she and Blue Eyes discussed AI's disruption of every industry on Monday's episode of [[culture-of-change|Culture of Change]] (episode 138). Companies that harness AI intelligently will, she argues, leapfrog competitors into the Golden Age, while those chasing shiny objects will waste money and become irrelevant; AI firms serve both and win either way, and to platform that success and failure they need infrastructure. She characterizes Trump's position as insisting that planned growth be centrally planned, which is why he seeks to prohibit states from acting independently while "luring corporate countries into treaties (essentially)." She writes that she hates the precedent but finds the premise sound, since allowing companies to define the future without accounting for the people produced the "America last dystopia" the country is climbing out of, and compares the moment to the way solar fields reshaped the landscape.

## Netanyahu meeting and Iran negotiations

The Brief reports that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu met President Trump in Washington on Wednesday for closed-door discussions covering [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program and regional security. It was the seventh meeting between the two leaders since Trump returned to office, and no joint press conference followed. Trump posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that the meeting was "very good," that "the tremendous relationship between our two Countries continues," and that "there was nothing definitive reached other than I insisted that negotiations with Iran continue to see whether or not a Deal can be consummated." He said a deal would be his preference, adding that the last time Iran declined a deal "they were hit with Midnight Hammer — That did not work well for them," and that he hoped Iran would be "more reasonable and responsible" this time. He also said the two discussed "tremendous progress being made in Gaza, and the Region in general," declaring "There is truly PEACE in the Middle East."

The Brief notes that US and Iranian officials recently held indirect nuclear talks in Oman, with another round expected, and that Iran maintains its ballistic missile program is non-negotiable. [[ali-shamkhani|Ali Shamkhani]], senior adviser to Iran's supreme leader, is quoted by *Al Jazeera* as saying on Wednesday, during an event marking the 47th anniversary of the Islamic revolution, that "the Islamic Republic's missile capabilities are non-negotiable." Iran holds that it retains the right to enrich uranium under international agreements, while US officials maintain that preventing an Iranian nuclear weapon remains policy. Netanyahu has previously indicated Israel seeks firm measures against Iranian nuclear weapons capabilities, and Israeli officials have raised concerns about Iran's missile program and support for regional armed groups.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry comments that after the build-up nothing emerged from the meeting — no video, photos, announcement or formal statements — calling it a "tough look for Netanyahu," who "flew all that way and walked away with nothing," though he concludes that in this situation "no news is good news."

## Election intelligence directed to Kurt Olsen

The Brief reports that an anonymous US intelligence official and "a person with knowledge of the matter" told *[[nbc-news|NBC News]]* on Tuesday that Trump instructed US intelligence agencies to provide 2020 election-related intelligence to attorney Kurt Olsen, with *Politico* reporting the same story citing "four people with knowledge of the effort." Olsen represented [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] in her 2022 election challenges as well as numerous other election cases, and was appointed a special advisor on elections the previous year. According to the anonymous sources, the directive concerned claims of foreign interference in the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 election]] and was issued to US spy agencies including the [[cia|CIA]]. The six anonymous sources across the two stories reported that intelligence officials raised concerns about sharing classified information with a private attorney; Olsen became a federal "special government employee" in October.

Ashe in America writes that if one person in the United States has a comprehensive, macro-level view of American election issues, it is Olsen. She recounts meeting him in August 2021 at the Cyber Symposium, working with him on several projects over the following year, and interviewing him with [[canncon|CannCon]] both on and off Badlands. She objects to media descriptions of him as a "private attorney," noting he was appointed a special government employee in October and, before his legal career, was a Navy SEAL. She traces his entry into the election narrative to January 2021, when *[[washington-post|The Washington Post]]* reported on [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]] visiting the White House holding papers that photographers glimpsed — the story, she says, of how Lindell and Olsen met, after which they worked together for years on election-related projects. In her opinion, any lawsuit, legislative effort or cyber analysis pursued by grassroots election researchers during the previous administration had at least Olsen's awareness. She argues the significance of Olsen serving as the clearinghouse for the intelligence agencies' election portfolios "cannot be understated," and that as the agencies "try to rewrite their records and roles in stolen US elections, Trump just ordered them to give all their info to the one guy that can informedly fact check them."

## Lavrov on France in Africa

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said in a speech to the Russian parliament on Wednesday that [[france|France]] is pursuing "revanchist" policies in Africa, according to *[[rt|RT]]*. He said France "is attempting to overthrow undesirable nationalist governments in the Sahara-Sahel region and other parts of Africa," and that while African nations "have long since abandoned their ties to their former metropolis," Paris has chosen to undermine them through opposition figures, "outright terrorist groups and Ukrainian militants." The Brief situates the remarks amid diplomatic tensions between Russia and Western nations over influence in Africa, noting that France has stated it respects African sovereignty while expressing concern about unconstitutional changes of government.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that Lavrov confirmed what he has dubbed "The War of Sovereignty," a coordinated subversive campaign orchestrated by what he calls the Unipolar Hegemony, corresponding to "Conflict A" on a map he produced. He argues the campaign involves not only ISIS and coup plotters funded by the [[deep-state|Deep State]] but Ukrainian mercenaries, targeting the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] led by Burkinabè President Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]]. He notes that the Russian military and the Wagner Group trained these forces in Syria, and wonders whether Russia will re-mobilize and redeploy to the Sahel.

## Cartel drones near El Paso

The Brief reports that the administration confirmed cartel drones entered US airspace near El Paso, Texas, and were disabled by the [[federal-aviation-administration|FAA]] and the Department of War. Transportation Secretary [[sean-duffy|Sean Duffy]] posted on X that "the FAA and DOW acted swiftly to address a cartel drone incursion. The threat has been neutralized, and there is no danger to commercial travel in the region. The restrictions have been lifted and normal flights are resuming." The FAA had earlier issued a surprise notice closing airspace above El Paso and halting all flights up to 18,000 feet for ten days for "special security reasons," covering a ten-mile radius; the restrictions were lifted Wednesday night. *[[business-insider|Business Insider]]* cited anonymous officials saying counter-drone measures were used, without the department specifying the technology.

Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] rejected reports of the incursion, telling reporters that "there is no information about the use of drones on the border," according to *Newsweek*, which also quoted Representative [[tony-gonzales|Tony Gonzales]]: "For those of us who live and work along the border, daily drone incursions by criminal organizations are part of everyday life. It's a Wednesday for us."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry connects the episode to his speculation that the military buildup in the eastern Mediterranean may be a head-fake. He recounts Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] visiting Washington the previous week — and nearly being assassinated on the way home — while vowing that he and Trump were working together to bring down cartels and the bankers financing them. He relays a question posed by Eric Rice: who sold Mexico the drones? He cites a 2015 article on the subject, conceding it concerns the Mexican government rather than the cartels, but asking whether there is an actual difference. He concludes that Mexico is "the last bastion of these Hispanic narcotics cartels," that Trump has signaled he considers the Mexican government in bed with the cartels and therefore a threat, and that the airspace closure followed by conflicting reports amounts to "definitely an invasion."

## Bonus items

The Brief notes that Attorney General Pam Bondi testified before [[congress|Congress]] on Wednesday, where lawmakers questioned her contentiously about the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s handling of the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] matter. Epstein survivors spoke publicly following the hearing, according to NBC video reporting, and the hearing also covered broader Justice Department oversight and policy issues.

A second bonus item reports the Tumbler Ridge school shooting in Canada, in which an eighteen-year-old male-to-female transgender person identified as Jesse Strang killed eight people on Wednesday, including five students aged twelve and thirteen and an eleven-year-old stepbrother, with a twelve-year-old girl in critical condition. Citing [[cnn|CNN]], the Brief states the shooter transitioned about six years earlier, at roughly age twelve, that reports varied on whether the suspect was seventeen or eighteen, and that police had visited the residence several times over mental health concerns. Authorities said the investigation is ongoing.
