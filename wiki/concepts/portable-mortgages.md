# Portable Mortgages

A portable mortgage is a home loan structure that would allow a homeowner to transfer an existing mortgage rate, term, and lender from one property to another rather than originating an entirely new loan when moving.[1] In November 2025 the concept entered mainstream U.S. policy debate when [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]], director of the [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]], said the administration was "actively evaluating portable mortgages."[1]

## Policy context

Pulte's statement, made in a social media post on Wednesday, November 12, 2025, came days after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s call for 50-year mortgages fell flat with both industry and consumers; Pulte had been quick to praise Trump's post on the 50-year idea the previous weekend.[1] He gave no details on how a portable-mortgage program would actually work.[1] The proposals emerged as the [[white-house|White House]] sought to address rising voter concerns about housing affordability, an issue widely blamed for Republican losses by wider-than-expected margins in the elections held the previous week.[1]

## Mechanics

In the model described in commentary, instead of taking out a completely new mortgage, a mover uses proceeds from the sale of the current property to pay off the existing loan and then takes out a new mortgage on the same terms with the same provider to finance the new home.[1] Advocates argue this would let borrowers avoid early repayment charges and escape lender fees of roughly three to five points charged on new mortgages and refinancings.[1]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded to the proposal with the sardonic line "If you like your mortgage you can keep your mortgage," invoking the Affordable Care Act promise.[1] [[gmoney|GMONEY]] took the opposite view, calling the potential savings "YUUUUGE" and emphasizing the elimination of origination and refinance points.[1]
