# SMART ePANTS

SMART ePANTS — Smart Electrically Powered and Networked Textile Systems — is a United States intelligence community program to develop computerized clothing. It is the first-of-its-kind effort by the intelligence community to build garments that function as networked sensors.[1]

## Announcement and administration

The [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] announced on August 22, 2023 that the intelligence community's advanced research and development arm, the [[iarpa|Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity]], would develop the program over the following three and a half years.[1]

The [[armed-forces-communications-and-electronics-association|Armed Forces Communications and Electronics Association]] wrote that "SMART ePANTS could revolutionize the Internet of Things by collecting data to help intelligence, medical and sports communities."[1]

## Contracts and contractors

IARPA, in partnership with the Naval Information Warfare Center, Pacific, awarded research contracts totaling more than $22 million to [[nautilus-defense|Nautilus Defense]] and [[leidos|Leidos, Inc.]], according to an August 9 [[pentagon|Pentagon]] announcement. [[sri-international|SRI International]], the [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|Massachusetts Institute of Technology]] and Areté received undisclosed amounts to develop the technology, according to reporting in [[the-intercept|The Intercept]].[1]

Reporting by Suzanne Burdick, Ph.D. in [[childrens-health-defense|Children's Health Defense]]'s publication *The Defender* noted that the investment giants [[vanguard-group|Vanguard]] and [[blackrock|BlackRock]] — which benefited from the sale of COVID-19 vaccines and hold ownership stakes in technology companies developing vaccine passports and digital wallets — are listed among the top investors in both Nautilus Defense and Leidos.[1]

## Criticism

Badlands Media presents SMART ePANTS as evidence that the intelligence community has already begun turning clothing into another avenue for spying on civilians, and situates it within the broader [[smart-clothing|smart clothing]] push described as a bridge between the smartphone and an eventual digital ID chip implanted under the skin — the "surveillance under the skin" concept popularized by [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]], an adviser to [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]].[1]
