# January 2025 Los Angeles Wildfires

A series of dangerous, wind-driven fires broke out on Tuesday, January 7, 2025, across the Los Angeles area, destroying more than a thousand homes within two days and prompting mass evacuations.[1]

## Mayoral absence

When the fires began, Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] was on the other side of the globe, serving as part of a delegation sent by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to Ghana for the inauguration of its new president.[1] Bass, a former Democratic congresswoman who became mayor in late 2022, did not return to Los Angeles until Wednesday afternoon, by which point more than 1,000 homes had burned and 100,000 people across the region had been forced to flee.[1]

The mayor's absence drew criticism from Angelenos, many of whom said there was insufficient warning from officials about the likelihood of devastating fires even as weather forecasts predicted extreme danger.[1] "There was zero preparation. There was zero thought here," said Michael Gonzales, 47, a lawyer whose home burned down in Pacific Palisades; he argued that Bass made a poor decision to remain overseas despite forecasters warning of the most dangerous fire conditions in more than a decade.[1]

## Impact on the film industry

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences delayed its Oscar nominations announcement by 48 hours because of the deadly wildfires raging across vast swathes of the Los Angeles area.[1] The announcement had been scheduled for Friday, January 17, but the nominees were instead to be revealed on January 19, and voting for the nearly 10,000 Academy members, which opened on January 8, was extended by two days until Tuesday, January 14.[1] Former Oscars host Billy Crystal was among the celebrities who lost homes in the blaze, along with Paris Hilton, James Woods, Miles Teller and Julia Louis-Dreyfus.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Burning Bright argued that "Overton's Goalpost" was in full effect, with officials hurriedly — perhaps proudly — declaring themselves incompetent and inept in the midst of the crisis, on the theory that admitting incompetence forecloses the far more damaging finding of malicious intent, which is followed by tribunals.[1] The same writer connected the fires to Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s request for a $25 million infusion to fight the incoming Trump administration, asking whether the sequence represented organic exposure or planned demolition.[1]

## Cost and Casualties

Early estimates indicated the wildfires that erupted across Los Angeles County in January 2025 were on track to be among the costliest natural disasters in United States history.[2] As of mid-January the blazes had killed at least 11 people and incinerated more than 12,000 structures since Tuesday of that week, destroying entire neighborhoods that had been home to multimillion-dollar properties.[2] A preliminary AccuWeather assessment put damage and total economic losses at between $135 billion and $150 billion, a figure that would make the fires the costliest in US history.[2]

## Day Two: Simultaneous Fires and Strained Resources

By Wednesday, January 8, 2025, five simultaneous wildfires were burning across various regions of Los Angeles, spreading due to extreme winds, causing historic destruction of homes and displacing thousands of people; roughly 1,000 structures had been destroyed and two deaths were confirmed.[3] New ignitions continued into the night, including a fire that broke out in Studio City at Vanetta Place and Sunset Drive at 9:24 PM PT, while evacuation areas for the Sunset Fire continued to expand.[3] Some Los Angelenos escaped the smoke and evacuation zones by fleeing to Disneyland.[3]

[[pasadena-fire-department|Pasadena Fire Department]] Chief [[chad-augustin|Chad Augustin]] said his department was mobilizing 60 strike teams with 750 firefighters to battle the [[eaton-fire|Eaton Fire]], with assistance arriving from across California and as far away as Arizona.[3] He described his local firefighters as "exhausted" and said the multiple simultaneous fires had strained the resources Pasadena normally relies on through its partnership with Los Angeles County, creating an unprecedented situation.[3] Augustin estimated 200 to 500 structures had been damaged or lost in the Eaton Fire, whose origins remained undetermined, and said that while the water supply was stretched by demand and power outages, the erratic winds — not the water supply — were the driving cause of the fire's expansion.[3]

## Competing Narratives

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the photographs and video from Los Angeles as depicting "an apocalyptic hellscape," and catalogued the competing explanations circulating online: proof of climate change versus weaponized weather, and tragic forest and water mismanagement versus a plan to rebuild Los Angeles as a smart city in time for the [[2028-los-angeles-olympics|2028 Olympics]].[3] She doubted the public would ever receive actual answers, noting that "we never do," and reported that Badlands' California contributors had checked in safe.[3] Co-host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] offered a wry counterpoint that one half of the country was on fire while the other was covered in ice, joking that he blamed George R. R. Martin.[3]

## Notable Losses

Among the reported casualties of the fires was a trove of nearly 200 artworks by [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], valued at "millions of dollars," which had been in storage near the Pacific Palisades home of his attorney Kevin Morris.[2]
