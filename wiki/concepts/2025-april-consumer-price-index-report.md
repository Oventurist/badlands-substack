# 2025 April Consumer Price Index Report

The April 2025 Consumer Price Index report, released on the morning of May 13, 2025, showed U.S. consumer prices rising at a 2.3 percent annual rate — the slowest pace since early 2021 — against economist expectations of 2.4 percent.[1] Markets rallied on the surprise.[1]

## Context

The reading landed after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had spent April imposing large tariffs on goods from every country in the world, pushing the average American levy on imports to its highest level in roughly a century.[1] Despite those measures, measured inflation slowed.[1] Separately, on May 12 the administration lowered its tariff on Chinese imports from 145 percent to 30 percent for 90 days while the two countries worked toward a permanent agreement, and China cut its retaliatory tariff on American goods from 125 percent to 10 percent — see [[us-china-trade-war-2025|the 2025 US–China trade war]].[1]

Press analysis cautioned that the trade war was still poised to raise prices and slow growth later in the year, while conceding that the risk of a severe economic crisis had fallen significantly.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked the media reversal, noting that economists had assured the public inflation would skyrocket and were now explaining away results they had predicted would be catastrophic.[1] She argued that tariffs were only one component of Trump's economic agenda, citing Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s description of a "three-legged stool": cut spending intentionally while avoiding a recession, deregulate to jump-start the private sector, and reorder international trade to bring back manufacturing.[1] Bessent's summary of the underlying problem was quoted directly: "The U.S. doesn't have a revenue problem—we have a spending problem."[1]

Ashe contended that the Trump economy would not replicate the "decades-long globalist/Keynesian fever dream" because its levers were strategic and grounded in sound money and market economics rather than social justice theory, and predicted that outlets would continue quietly correcting the record much as they had begun doing over the story of Joe Biden's decline.[1]
