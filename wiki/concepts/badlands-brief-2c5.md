# Badlands Brief: Bioweapons Sneak Into the News Cycle as Israeli Shenanigans Ensue

**Badlands Brief: Bioweapons Sneak Into the News Cycle as Israeli Shenanigans Ensue** is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published on May 27, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The edition, which recorded 148 likes, collects five news items with accompanying commentary from Badlands contributors [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], plus a bonus item on the Texas Senate primary runoff. Its recurring themes are biological-materials scandals, the perceived capture of the American justice system by foreign interests, and federal pressure on left-aligned activist networks.

## Internal DOJ doubts about the Mar-a-Lago raid

The first item reports that a senior [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] official raised internal concerns two days after the [[fbi|FBI]]'s August 8, 2022 raid on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] residence. In an August 10, 2022 email to National Security Division attorney [[sophia-brill|Sophia Brill]], [[patty-stemler|Patty Stemler]] — described as a longtime DOJ veteran and ally of then-Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] who had been consulted on Trump-related matters — questioned whether the former president had declassified the seized documents. Stemler noted that although standard declassification procedures exist, "the President as Commander in Chief" is not necessarily bound by them, drawing an analogy to the pardon power.

The article states that the email was obtained by the outlet [[just-the-news|Just the News]] and was discovered during the Justice Department's own review of federal law enforcement actions, and argues that it underscores early internal doubts about the [[mar-a-lago-raid|raid]]'s legal footing. The search, personally approved by Garland, followed prior assertions by Trump's legal team — including a May 2022 letter from attorney [[evan-corcoran|Evan Corcoran]] — that Trump possessed broad declassification authority and had issued a standing order treating documents removed to his residence as declassified.

Ashe in America notes that "the email was reportedly discovered during the Justice Department's investigation into weaponization of federal law enforcement," and comments that a proper investigation of that scope would not be a quick undertaking. She argues that the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]] during the Biden years "was not the work of rogue officials, it was official policy — as evidenced by the resource allocation and the consistency in tactics in field offices across the nation." Periodic reminders that the investigation is ongoing, she writes, are "signal in the midst of an all out assault of noise," and she expresses hope that following the investigation will show that Trump is only "the tip of the iceberg." The section closes with the assertion that "there must be a justice phase before we can have a golden age."

## Dropped federal charges in the Las Vegas biolab case

The second item reports that federal authorities dropped gun-related charges against [[ori-solomon|Ori Solomon]], the property manager tied to an [[las-vegas-biolab|illegal biolab]] discovered in a Las Vegas home in late January and early February 2026. Solomon, an Israeli citizen on a non-immigrant visa, was arrested in February after the FBI and local police searched an east Las Vegas rental property and found lab equipment, test kits, vaccines, influenza samples and other biological materials stored in a locked garage. Federal prosecutors dismissed the illegal firearm possession complaint without prejudice in May 2026, citing the interests of justice after reviewing additional information. Solomon still faced a pending [[nevada|Clark County]] charge for improper disposal of hazardous waste, with a status hearing set for June 4.

The article links the Las Vegas site to Chinese national [[jia-bei-zhu|Jia Bei Zhu]] — also known as Qiang "David" He — who owned the property through an LLC. Zhu was convicted in a separate federal case in California for operating [[universal-meditech|Universal Meditech Inc.]] (later Prestige Biotech) in the Fresno/Reedley area, and was found guilty of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, multiple counts of wire fraud, distributing adulterated and misbranded medical devices, and making a false statement to the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] after fraudulently selling over one million faulty COVID-19 tests imported from China for nearly $4 million between 2020 and 2023. The source suggests materials found in the Las Vegas garage may have been connected to Zhu's California operations, and notes that his former partner remains a fugitive in China.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's commentary emphasizes that Solomon was "an Israeli foreign national (not even an American dual citizen)" running an illegal biolab in the garage of a single-family home. Multiple people with access to the property became "deathly ill" after exposure to the specimens; hundreds of vials of rare and highly infectious diseases were, he writes, "basically just shoved into an old refrigerator," and the house cleaner — who also became gravely ill — said the place reeked of "foul stagnant hospital air." Solomon, who managed 37 properties in the area, was in illegal possession of six firearms when arrested given his nonimmigrant visa.

The commentary adds that Zhu was arrested in 2023 and later convicted, and that according to a report from the [[select-committee-on-the-chinese-communist-party|Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party]] the vials found in the 2022 California lab were labeled as pathogens including "HIV," "tuberculosis," "malaria," "hepatitis" and "Ebola." When Zhu and his wife were arrested in 2023, authorities realized Zhu was already a fugitive in Canada, wanted "for his connection to companies in the Chinese dairy industry." His business partner [[zhaoyan-wang|Zhaoyan Wang]] was also indicted but fled to China and remains a fugitive. The article quotes reporting that "trial evidence showed Zhu and Wang hired inexperienced employees, like cellphone salespeople, supermarket workers and childcare workers, who 'would not ask any questions,'" some of them recruited through the Fresno County Economic Development Corporation, a public organization that helps place unskilled workers and provides subsidies to employers who hire them.

The most contested portion of the item concerns the officials involved. The article names magistrate judge [[elayna-youchah|Elayna J. Youchah]], who released Solomon pending trial in February citing "no concern" that he would fail to return, and federal prosecutor [[sigal-chattah|Sigal Chattah]], the First Assistant U.S. Attorney and Special Attorney to the U.S. Attorney General, who dropped the weapons charges. The commentary asserts that both are Jewish and that Chattah was born in Israel, making her a dual citizen, and treats those facts as "a pretty important detail" given Solomon's Israeli citizenship and the claim that "Israel is notorious for blocking extradition requests by the US for criminals hiding within their borders." The writer concludes that the episode "appears to be more than just a miscarriage of justice" and instead "the control of the US justice system for the benefit of a foreign country," asking repeatedly, "Why do Israeli citizens consistently get away with committing federal (and state) crimes inside the United States?" These are the source's interpretive claims rather than established findings.

## Treasury subpoenas over the Cuba aid convoy

The third item reports that federal investigators subpoenaed streamer [[hasan-piker|Hasan Piker]] and activist [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]] as part of a [[department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] probe into their participation in a March 2026 humanitarian aid convoy to [[cuba|Cuba]]. The [[nuestra-america-convoy|"Nuestra America Convoy"]], organized with help from [[codepink|CodePink]] — the anti-war group Benjamin co-founded — delivered roughly 20 tons of goods to Havana. More than 40 Americans are reportedly under scrutiny by the Treasury's [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] for potential violations of U.S. sanctions law, and the subpoenas served on Piker and Benjamin seek financial, logistical and communications records related to the trip.

The article situates the probe amid heightened Trump administration pressure on Cuba, including a tightened blockade implemented the previous year, the designation of Cuba as a state sponsor of terrorism, and a Justice Department indictment of former Cuban president [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]]. Piker, a 34-year-old democratic socialist streamer, publicly praised aspects of the Cuban regime after the trip and was photographed with CodePink figures in Havana; Benjamin, 73, has long been a prominent critic of U.S. Cuba policy and has faced prior scrutiny over her activism.

Burning Bright's commentary describes Piker as "one of the internet's leading Democratic Socialist voices" who "made his name on anti-Trump, anti-MAGA rhetoric," and characterizes CodePink as an NGO operating "in the same ecosystem of activist NGOs that parallel the [[southern-poverty-law-center|SPLC]] model — leveraging humanitarian framing and narrative positioning to advance anti-American rhetoric." He argues that the subpoenas represent the beginning of a much wider probe into dozens of Americans and of "the public disclosure phase of what we've known all along: what has been presented as organic counter-culture dissent to Donald Trump and the MAGA core is now being exposed as having been engineered from the start." He frames these as "a rapid and seismic progression in the Mind War," suggesting anti-SPLC action could mark "the dismantling of institutional proxies that manufactured conflict and laundered narrative power through the administrative state," and that pressure on culture-war figures under the guise of countering Cuban communist influence extends into "the cultural transmission layer." Each disclosure, he writes, forces more of the "insurgency's" structure into view "without requiring total kinetic confrontation on the home front."

## Ebola outbreak coverage in the DRC and Uganda

The fourth item, headlined as fearmongering by the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] and aid groups, reports on an [[ebola|Ebola]] outbreak centered in Ituri province in northeastern the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]] that aid organizations say is spreading faster than response efforts can contain it. The outbreak involves the [[bundibugyo-virus|Bundibugyo virus]] strain and has expanded into North Kivu and South Kivu provinces as well as into [[uganda|Uganda]], with confirmed cases in major population centers including Goma and Kampala.

The [[international-rescue-committee|International Rescue Committee]] warned that "the warning signs are flashing red," stating the epidemic is outpacing responders. [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]] said the delay in detection means authorities are "playing catch-up with a very fast-moving epidemic." No vaccines or treatments are available for the Bundibugyo strain. Response efforts include intensified surveillance, patient isolation, contact monitoring and scaled-up operations by the WHO and the Congolese and Ugandan governments, with roughly $500 million pledged for response and prevention. The IRC called for relaxing import restrictions on personal protective equipment and for rapid funding for health workers and safe burial teams. Congolese officials described the situation in the affected provinces as "well contained," while aid groups emphasized the heightened risk of further spread.

Ashe in America responds skeptically: "Isn't it weird how we keep having all these potential pandemics after the global think tanks told us we were going to see increasingly frequent pandemics? They're so good at predicting stuff … or they're planning stuff." She adds that authorities "do seem to be throwing all the public health crises at the wall to see what sticks," and that in the wake of the "affirmation" of the lab leak theory such stories are less believable than in prior years.

## Israeli pressure on the Iran negotiations

The fifth item concerns a statement by [[israel|Israel]]'s national security minister that Israel would not let Trump sign a "bad deal" with [[iran|Iran]]. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that "the Israelis clearly believe that they are in control and in the driver's seat as far as 'Peace in the Middle East' is concerned," and suggests that is why they launched another major offensive in Lebanon while Trump is in the middle of a high-stakes negotiation with Iran. He notes unsubstantiated rumors that the deal has already been finalized and awaits only signatures.

The commentary singles out [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and his remarks about Pakistani Defence Minister [[khawaja-asif|Khawaja Asif]] and Pakistanis generally, observing that Trump has had "nothing but high praise" for [[pakistan|Pakistani]] leadership and vice versa. The writer predicts that when the Iran deal is formally announced, "most of the GOP is going to turn on President Trump like a pack of wild rabid dogs" because, in his contested assertion, "those congressmen all are compromised and actually work for the State of Israel, not the American People." He proposes declassifying both the COVID and 2020 election investigations — "specifically any material that implicates the GOP in the coup against President Trump" — and says he would like to see [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] "break all of their toys on her way out" as a way to hurt the Uniparty by exposing GOP traitors.

## Bonus item: Paxton defeats Cornyn in Texas

The bonus item reports that [[texas|Texas]] Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] defeated four-term U.S. Senator [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]] in the Republican primary runoff for Texas's U.S. Senate seat, winning roughly 63% to 37%. The victory followed a competitive March primary in which neither candidate secured a majority, and is described as one of the biggest primary upsets in modern Texas political history. Paxton, a hard-charging conservative and Trump ally known for legal battles against the Biden administration, campaigned as a fighter against the Washington establishment, while Cornyn — a longtime Senate insider and former Majority Whip — was portrayed as out of touch. The race featured heavy spending on attack ads, and Trump's endorsement of Paxton days before early voting is credited with energizing the MAGA base and proving decisive. In his victory remarks Paxton said, "Texas has spoken loud and clear. We're done with business as usual." The article concludes that the outcome signals a further rightward shift within the Texas Republican Party.
