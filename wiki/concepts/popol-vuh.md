# Popol Vuh

The **Popol Vuh** is the Mayan creation narrative, discussed in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — Creation Myth Clues" as a parallel to the founding myth of [[roman-empire|Rome]] and as evidence of ancient global contact.[1]

## Account given in the article

The essay states that the Mayan creation story describes the founding of the nation by a man who travelled with a woman able to shapeshift into a wolf.[1] In the version recounted, the entire world was submerged by a giant flood, survived only by that man and his wolf-woman companion, who taught him how to make sacrifices to appease "god."[1]

The author treats this as remarkable because Rome's own creation story has [[romulus-and-remus|Romulus and Remus]] suckled by a she-wolf, and because the [[koryos|Koryos]] werewolf cult is said to have practiced child grooming while migrating from the Eurasian steppes into Europe before Rome's founding.[1] He asks rhetorically what the odds are that these parallels are coincidental, invoking [[qanon|Q]]'s question of how many coincidences must accumulate before something is no longer a coincidence.[1]

## Linguistic argument

The article notes that *Popol Vuh* translates into English as the "People's Book," and argues that the word *popol* resembles *people*.[1] Because "v" is pronounced as "b" in Spanish, the author transforms *vuh* into *buh*, which he says strongly resembles *book*.[1] This is presented as supporting the broader thesis of the series that the world's languages descend from a single original tongue and that trans-oceanic contact occurred long before mainstream history allows.[1]
