# Chemical Preservatives in Processed Food

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] grouped potassium sorbate, sodium sorbate, sodium benzoate, and potassium bromate together as a family of widely used chemical preservatives whose safety assurances he treated as unreliable.[1]

## Potassium sorbate

Potassium sorbate is described as one of the most prolific preservatives in the food industry, to the point that it is nearly impossible to find packaged candy or ice cream without it.[1] The food industry and its "scientists" offer endless assurances that potassium sorbate poses no health threat, but the article holds that conflicting data suggests otherwise.[1]

## Sodium benzoate and benzene formation

The combination of sodium benzoate — or potassium benzoate — with ascorbic acid in soft drinks may result in the production of benzene, a carcinogen.[1]

## Potassium bromate

Potassium bromate is used to increase volume in white flour and is found mostly in bread and rolls.[1] It is known to cause cancer, and the article states that even small amounts in bread can create problems for humans.[1]

## Sodium sorbate

Sodium sorbate is known to be genotoxic, meaning DNA-damaging.[1] A 2012 study quoted in the article concluded that sodium sorbate "has genotoxic and clastogenic effects in human peripheral lymphocytes," citing Madle et al. (1993) for the proposition that human lymphocytes provide the best results for human mutagenicity studies, and reasoning that on this basis sodium sorbate "may also cause cancer because of its mutagenic and genotoxic effects."[1]
