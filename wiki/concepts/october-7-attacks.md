# October 7 Attacks

The October 7 attacks were an unprecedented multi-pronged assault launched from the Gaza Strip into southern Israel on Saturday, October 7, 2023, by [[hamas|Hamas]] and allied Palestinian factions. Reported in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of October 9, 2023, the assault dominated the news cycle and, in the Brief's framing, overshadowed domestic American controversies that were unfolding simultaneously.[1]

## The assault

The attack combined air, land and sea incursions and constituted the most significant breach of Israel's borders since the 1973 [[yom-kippur-war|Yom Kippur War]].[1] Town-by-town and farm-by-farm fighting was still underway two days later, with [[hamas|Hamas]] and [[palestinian-islamic-jihad|Palestinian Islamic Jihad]] reportedly controlling swathes of southern territory and even military outposts inside Israel.[1]

Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] warned residents of [[gaza|Gaza]] to "leave now, because we will operate forcefully everywhere," and said the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] would use "all its strength" to "destroy" Hamas.[1] Netanyahu and his cabinet were described as contemplating a high-risk hostage rescue operation and a potential ground incursion.[1]

## "Pearl Harbor moment"

Former IDF international spokesperson [[jonathan-conricus|Jonathan Conricus]] characterized the day as Israel's "Pearl Harbor" moment, telling [[cnn|CNN]]: "The entire system failed. It's not just one component. It's the entire defense architecture that evidently failed to provide the necessary defense for Israeli civilians."[1] He added that "there was reality up until today, and then there will be reality after today."[1]

## Alleged Iranian role

The [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] reported that Iranian security officials helped plan the attack and gave the green light at a meeting in Beirut the preceding Monday, according to senior members of Hamas and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1] Officers of the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] had reportedly worked with Hamas since August 2023 to devise the incursions, with details refined during several meetings in [[beirut|Beirut]] attended by IRGC officers and representatives of four Iran-backed militant groups.[1]

U.S. officials disputed the account. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] told CNN, "We have not yet seen evidence that Iran directed or was behind this particular attack, but there is certainly a long relationship," while another U.S. official said, "We don't have any information at this time to corroborate this account."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the entire American establishment had aligned behind Israel within hours, citing [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]]'s description of the assault as "an attack on America," and noted that Iran-sponsored [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] had entered the fray by Sunday.[1] She predicted a long regional conflict from which defense contractors stood to benefit, and criticized Democratic defenses of the $6 billion in Iranian funds released by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration on the grounds that money is fungible and that the U.S. government lacks any reliable mechanism to monitor its use.[1] She noted that [[politico|Politico]] had covered the issue three weeks earlier and that Iran had claimed it would spend the unfrozen funds as it wished, and pointed to a statement posted on X by Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] declaring that "the cancer of the usurper Zionist regime will be eradicated at the hands of the Palestinian people and the Resistance forces throughout the region" — a post X ruled violated platform terms but allowed to remain up.[1]

Ashe stated support for the Jewish people and their right to exist in the land where Solomon's temple stood, while refusing the position that the Israeli government could not be criticized for what she called the worst national security failure in modern history; she reported being labelled both an antisemite and a Zionist over the same weekend.[1] She placed the attacks in the context of establishment war drums, billions in new funding to Israel, and zero tolerance for journalistic inquiry, and declined to "pick a side of who should die."[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a "bicameral" actual-versus-narrative reading: the actual event was a Deep State terror cell going kinetic on the heels of months of fomented [[color-revolution|color revolution]] tactics inside Israel endorsed by the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], suggesting clandestine networks had been forced into the open and were spending ammunition rapidly.[1] The narrative layer, he argued, was a Middle East devolving toward major-power conflict against the backdrop of Biden- and Obama-era funding of Iran — an escalation deliberately contrasted with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]].[1] "We're in a Mind War," he concluded. "And we're winning it."[1]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] situated the conflict within a centuries-long religio-political struggle over the Holy Land, tracing a lineage from [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] through the Crusades, [[sabbateanism|Sabbateanism]], [[jacob-frank|Frankism]] and the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]] to modern [[zionism|Zionism]] and Christian war hawks.[1]

## Hostage deaths reclassified (December 2024)

On December 2, 2024, the Israeli military announced that [[omer-neutra|Omer Maxim Neutra]], a 21-year-old American-Israeli tank platoon commander long believed to be a living hostage, had been killed during the attacks and that his body remained in Gaza.[2] Foreign Minister Israel Katz said Neutra "fell in a heroic battle near Nir Oz," a kibbutz where roughly one in four residents was murdered or kidnapped — among the communities worst affected that day.[2] Neutra was one of seven American citizens held in Gaza, four of whom had been pronounced dead.[2]

The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] used the announcement to revisit reports that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] implemented the [[hannibal-directive|Hannibal Directive]] on October 7, under which Israelis in the process of being captured may be targeted and killed, and to question how many of those described as hostages were in fact killed that day.[2]


## Response-time and friendly-fire claims

Badlands has argued that the Israeli government's seven-hour delay in mobilizing a response to the invasion is difficult to reconcile with the state's demonstrated capacity for elaborate covert coordination, such as the September 2024 [[2024-lebanon-pager-attacks|pager and walkie-talkie operation]] in Lebanon.[3] The commentary noted that Israeli society, organized around mandatory military service since the state's creation in 1948, had been preparing for precisely such a scenario, yet failed to meet it.[3]

Badlands further cited testimony by Israeli helicopter pilots and corroborating investigative reports as confirmation that military personnel were ordered to fire upon Israeli citizens that day, killing hundreds.[3] [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]] and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] covered the pilot's public testimony on [[badlands-daily|Badlands Daily]] in November 2023, during which [[canncon|CannCon]] argued that imagery of the [[nova-music-festival|Nova music festival]] parking lot showed damage consistent with hellfire missiles fired from helicopters rather than Hamas infantry weapons.[3]

## Immediate international and domestic aftermath

Contemporaneous accounts recorded that thousands of attendees at the Tribe of Nova music festival in southern Israel were ambushed on the morning of Saturday, October 7, 2023 by [[hamas|Hamas]] fighters — including attackers arriving by paraglider — who killed hundreds and took others captive, with over 260 deaths attributed to the festival attack alone.[4] Videos of the rapes, murders, attacks and kidnappings circulated widely across news and social media platforms in the days that followed.[4]

The attacks reverberated internationally within days. Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] used them to indict United States Middle East policy and to press for a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]], telling a Moscow meeting with Iraqi Prime Minister [[mohammed-shia-al-sudani|Mohammed al-Sudani]] that the crisis was "a glaring example of the failures of the US Middle East policies."[4] In the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]], Home Secretary [[suella-braverman|Suella Braverman]] wrote to police chiefs asking them to be alert for displays of Hamas flags or logos and to consider whether waving a Palestinian flag might constitute a public order offence in context.[4] In [[new-york-city-police-department|New York City]], Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] warned residents to beware of "lone wolves" radicalized online and said police would monitor social media for threats against religious communities.[4] The Chicago chapter of [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] drew widespread condemnation for posting — and then deleting — a paraglider graphic reading "I Stand With Palestine."[4]

Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], speaking in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, said the United States was "closer to World War III than we've ever been," warning that another conflict could involve "weaponry the likes of which this world has never seen before."[4] Google search interest in the phrase "Will I get drafted to war?" rose above its February 2022 peak from the Russian invasion of Ukraine, reaching its highest level since the 2007 Iraq troop surge.[4]
