# Hannibal Directive

The Hannibal Directive is an Israeli military protocol under which Israeli citizens or soldiers in the process of being taken captive may be targeted and killed, on the reasoning that preventing capture protects national security by denying an adversary both a bargaining chip and a source of sensitive intelligence about the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] and related infrastructure.[1]

## Invocation on October 7

Badlands commentary asserts that it has been widely reported and verified that the IDF implemented the Hannibal Directive during the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 attacks]].[1] Writing for the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the directive as the interpretive key to Israel's later announcement that American-Israeli soldier [[omer-neutra|Omer Maxim Neutra]] — long presumed to be a living hostage — had in fact been killed on October 7.[1]

## Role in hostage-narrative skepticism

The Brief argued that the directive raises the possibility that a substantial number of those described as hostages were killed on October 7 itself rather than taken alive, and questioned the plausibility of [[hamas|Hamas]] retaining a soldier's body through more than a year of intense bombardment in Gaza.[1] The commentary further noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had said on multiple occasions that he did not believe any living hostages remained, and speculated that a continuing hostage narrative served to justify the expansion of the conflict into the West Bank, Lebanon, Syria and Iran.[1]
