# National Firearms Act of 1934

The National Firearms Act of 1934 (NFA) is the foundational federal statute establishing heightened regulation of a defined class of firearms, including short-barreled rifles and sawed-off shotguns, which have been "heavily regulated since the 1930s."[1]

## Modern application

The NFA, read together with the [[gun-control-act-of-1968|Gun Control Act of 1968]], supplies the statutory authority the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives]] invoked when issuing the January 2023 [[stabilizing-brace-rule|stabilizing brace rule]].[1] The agency's Federal Register notice held that under the two statutes, "a firearm equipped with a stabilizing brace or other rearward attachment is considered a 'rifle' or 'short-barreled rifle' if other factors indicate that the weapon is designed, made, and intended to be fired from the shoulder."[1] Because the NFA regime attaches registration and tax burdens to short-barreled rifles, the reclassification had the effect of sweeping large numbers of commonly owned braced pistols into a restrictive category, which prompted the House repeal resolution of June 13, 2023.[1]
