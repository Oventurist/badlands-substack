# Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program

The Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program (CICP) is the federal mechanism, administered by the [[health-resources-and-services-administration|U.S. Health Resources and Services Administration]] (HRSA), through which people injured by government-designated countermeasures — including COVID-19 vaccines — may seek compensation.[1]

## COVID-19 vaccine payouts

In an update reflecting data current as of October 1, 2023, HRSA disclosed that the program had paid two additional people injured by COVID-19 vaccines.[1] Both had suffered myocarditis, or heart inflammation, after vaccination.[1] One person received $4,183 and the other received $4,934.[1] HRSA declined to disclose which shots the recipients had received and did not respond to requests for more information.[1]

## Criticism

Critics said the payments were nowhere near sufficient to satisfy demands for an improved government response to vaccine injuries, and the disclosures came as the agency faced the prospect of being ordered by a court to improve the program for victims.[1] The small size of the awards, relative to the medical and financial burden of vaccine-induced myocarditis, has been a recurring point of contention among those who argue that the CICP is structurally designed to minimize payouts.[1]
