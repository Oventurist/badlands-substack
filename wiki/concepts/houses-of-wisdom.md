# Houses of Wisdom

The Houses of Wisdom were networks of humanist education centers created under the [[abbasid-dynasty|Abbasid Dynasty]], specifically under the leadership of Caliph al-Mahdi, his son [[harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]] and his grandson al-Mamun.[1]

These institutions brought Muslim, Christian and Jewish scholars together to translate ancient works of Greek and Latin and to study astronomy, literature, medicine and engineering.[1] Matthew Ehret situates them within what he calls the Abbasid Renaissance, an "Islamic Golden Age" of bureaucratic, monetary and educational reform carried out under a principle equivalent to the Confucian Mandate of Heaven.[1] Complementing the translation work, paper mills were established in 832 CE in Samarkand, Cairo, Damascus and Baghdad, applying Chinese technology to broaden humanity's access to knowledge.[1]
