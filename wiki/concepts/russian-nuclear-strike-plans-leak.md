# Russian Nuclear Strike Plans Leak

The Russian nuclear strike plans leak refers to an August 2024 report, published by the [[financial-times|Financial Times]] and relayed by ReMix News, that Western sources had leaked Russian plans drawn up between 2008 and 2014 for "a series of overwhelming strikes across Western Europe."[1]

## Reported contents

Citing documents from Western security sources, the reporting stated that [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] had allegedly sought to enable the Russian navy to execute nuclear strikes within [[nato|NATO]] territory were a conflict with the alliance to emerge.[1] The targets described were not solely military: according to William Alberque, a former NATO official, "There could be hundreds, if not thousands, of targets mapped across Europe, including military and infrastructure targets."[1] The report added that [[russia|Russia]] is evidently still capable of transporting nuclear weapons on surface ships, which experts say carries a significant risk of escalation or accident, a possibility that [[start-i|START I]] was supposed to have eliminated.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the leak as "a perfect example of how a faulty premise that could easily be debunked or challenged from the outset can lead to a cascade of absurd claims and even detailed 'actuals' that are really just potentials in a fantasy land constructed entirely within the false reality."[1] He noted that the piece buried an important qualifier — "were a conflict with the alliance to emerge" — and argued that, even assuming the leaks were true, it should surprise no one that Russia maintained contingency plans for a potential and apparently inevitable conflict with a Globalist Bloc that had been steadily encroaching on its borders for the better part of two decades.[1] He further argued that Russian nuclear mongering would be "incredibly useful to prime the mass psychological pump for Scare Events to come," situating the story within the broader [[information-war|information war]].[1]
