# 2024 Libertarian National Convention

The 2024 national convention of the United States [[libertarian-party|Libertarian Party]] concluded on the weekend of May 25–26, 2024 with the nomination of [[chase-oliver|Chase Oliver]] as the party's presidential candidate.[1]

## Nomination contest

The presidential balloting stretched across seven rounds over a single long and contentious day, with party factions feuding simultaneously over the nomination and over internal leadership positions.[1] Fewer than 900 delegates cast votes.[1] Oliver, supported by the left-leaning Classic Liberal Caucus, defeated [[michael-rectenwald|Michael Rectenwald]], the candidate endorsed by the [[mises-caucus|Mises Caucus]] that had run the party since 2022, taking 60 percent against "none of the above" once the last rival was eliminated.[1] Oliver used his acceptance speech to appeal for unity: "I'm extending my hand. Take it and be a part of liberty."[1]

Mises Caucus leader and comedian Dave Smith described the proceedings as "a microcosm of where the country is headed," adding, "Things are so polarized."[1]

## Trump's appearance

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] addressed the convention and negotiated over portions of the party platform in real time from the stage, an appearance that drew whining, booing and profanity from delegates according to Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].[1] That commentary held that Trump had given the minor party its largest spotlight ever and that the party "face planted," and noted that some Libertarians afterward asked Trump to honor the cabinet and senior post offers they had booed at the convention.[1] Ashe in America further argued the weekend proved the party was not composed of "serious change makers," and predicted the November 5 vote totals would be "interesting … and probably hilarious," with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] rather than Trump facing the extra challengers.[1]


## Trump's reception and the Ramaswamy alliance pitch

Donald Trump's Saturday-night address to the convention in Washington, D.C. drew boos, heckling and applause in roughly equal measure, an unusual event that the Washington Post's Aaron Blake noted was remarkable even for the [[libertarian-party|Libertarian Party]] — inviting another party's 2024 nominee to speak while nominating a candidate to oppose him.[2] The invitation was deeply controversial given how much of Trump's record is repulsive to libertarians, and produced a delegate motion against it on the Friday.[2] Trump supporters who had bought spectator tickets periodically chanted "WE WANT TRUMP," and were drowned out by libertarians chanting "END THE FED."[2] Tension preceded the speech: delegates dismissed from convention proceedings entered the hall to find ticket-buying Trump supporters already occupying the front rows despite assurances from party chairwoman [[angela-mcardle|Angela McArdle]] that delegates would get the best seats, and verbal battles escalated into fisticuffs.[2]

Trump surrogate and former Libertarian Party voter [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] told the convention that "the future of this country depends on a libertarian-nationalist alliance," a call that drew mostly boos.[2] He outlined skepticism toward both Democrats and "the establishment wing of the Republican Party," criticizing Republican support for the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, and said that independent candidate [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], who had addressed the convention earlier that day, was "in no sense a libertarian," citing Kennedy's past support for punishing skeptics of the mainstream climate narrative.[2] In his opening remarks and a subsequent debate with the Libertarians' vice presidential nominee Clint Russell, Ramaswamy argued the moment offered libertarian ideals an opportunity to shape Trump's "America First" movement, saying he had had "extensive conversations with Donald Trump about libertarian objectives."[2]
