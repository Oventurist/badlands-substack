# US Sanctions Campaign Against Russian Oligarch Networks

The United States campaign against smuggling and money laundering networks supporting the Russian government refers to the coordinated program of asset seizures, criminal indictments, export controls and financial sanctions pursued by U.S. authorities following the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|Russian invasion of Ukraine]].[1]

## Scope of the effort

Over the year preceding March 2023, the U.S. government seized more than $500 million in assets belonging to Russian oligarchs and to those who had unlawfully supported the Russian regime, and more than 30 individuals and two corporate entities were indicted for crimes including sanctions evasion and money laundering.[1] In the same period the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]] announced a new wave of sanctions targeting Russia's metals and mining sector, intended to hinder resupply efforts in the ground war against Ukraine.[1]

These measures were coordinated with U.S. allies and [[g7|G7]] partners and were framed as an attempt to isolate Russia from the global economy; as a result, Russia's financial sector was reported to have lost hundreds of billions of dollars.[1] Since the beginning of the war, the United States and a coalition of more than 30 allies and partners imposed a range of economic measures including sanctions, export controls and price caps.[1] Thousands of individuals and entities linked to the Russian regime — among them senior government officials and military manufacturing firms — were blacklisted, and more than 1,000 foreign companies scaled back or ceased operations in Russia, stifling investment and industrial growth.[1] The program was reported by the [[organized-crime-and-corruption-reporting-project|OCCRP]].[1]

## Named targets

Badlands commentary highlighted [[viktor-vekselberg|Viktor Vekselberg]], [[roman-abramovich|Roman Abramovich]], [[andrei-derkach|Andrei Derkach]] and [[oleg-deripaska|Oleg Deripaska]] as among the most significant targets of the effort.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] described these figures as "members of the Swamp, the international Swamp," noting that Vekselberg was a Clinton associate and [[clinton-foundation|Foundation]] donor and that Deripaska was a key figure in Spygate.[1] Just Human observed that the fact sheet issued by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] justified the actions as a response to "Russia's unprovoked, full-scale invasion of Ukraine" and pledged use of "all tools at [their] disposal to impose accountability and serious costs on Russia and its enablers."[1] The contributor then asked whether the true object of the campaign was Russia and its enablers or instead the Swamp operating across Russia, Europe and the United States.[1]
