# JFK: What the Doctors Saw

"JFK: What the Doctors Saw" is a documentary built around interviews conducted in 2013 with seven physicians who were present in the Parkland Hospital emergency room when President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] was brought in after being shot in Dallas in 1963.[1] Reporting on the film in November 2023 noted that the doctors raised serious doubts about the official narrative that a lone gunman was responsible.[1]

## Content

Executive producer Jacquelynn Lueth interviewed the seven Parkland doctors for the film and wrote about the project for CBS News, saying their "recollections were precise and clear, as if the intervening decades had melted away."[1] She reported that "each of them reacted strongly when the autopsy pictures were projected on a screen," and that although they did not agree on everything, "it became obvious that the way the president looked at Parkland did not match the autopsy photos taken at Bethesda even before the official autopsy began."[1]

Doctors who saw the wound to the president's neck believed it was an entrance wound, which would contradict the [[warren-commission|Warren Commission]]'s conclusion that [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] fired from behind, and several described a gaping hole in the back of Kennedy's head.[1] Lueth stipulated that the physicians "had no agenda other than trying to save the president's life."[1]
