# 2025 Oman Nuclear Talks

The 2025 Oman nuclear talks were negotiations between the United States and [[iran|Iran]] over Tehran's nuclear program, initiated by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and set to begin in [[oman|Oman]] on Saturday, April 12, 2025.[1]

## Trump's ultimatum

Trump said he had a specific deadline in mind for reaching a deal and confirmed he would "absolutely" consider military action if negotiations failed.[1] He characterized the discussions as direct, while Tehran insisted they would remain indirect, saying Washington cannot be trusted.[1] Asked at the [[white-house|White House]] on April 9 whether he expected "something definitive" from the meeting, Trump described the Oman session as the start of a process: "It's a start. We have a little time, but we don't have much time. Because we're not gonna let them have a nuclear weapon. When you start talks, you know if they're going along well or not. And I would say the conclusion would be when I think they're not going along well. So that's just a feeling."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the episode "the bait" — the shiny object [[neoconservatives|NeoCons]] had chased since the Ayatollah was installed in Tehran in 1979: war with Iran.[1] He had long argued that such a conflict would be manufactured by the [[deep-state|Deep State]], and that with the right villains in key Iranian government and military positions it would be easy to escalate into a full-scale war drawing in every major power — World War III.[1] That this had not happened, and that Tehran had not truly responded to Israel's provocations, he read as a tell that those positions may no longer be occupied by the right villains; Trump's provocative rhetoric was a further tell that he had the situation under control.[1] If the rhetoric was not aimed at Tehran, he reasoned, it was aimed at [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] — "maybe" — and at the NeoCons "most certainly," with Trump chumming the waters to draw out the sharks and let warmongers expose their appetite for calamity, setting the stage for the world's two most powerful leaders to join forces.[1] He estimated the world was "roughly 30 to 40 days from Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] meeting in Riyadh."[1]

## Delegations and format dispute

Trump's envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] was designated to lead the US delegation to the Saturday talks in Oman, according to two sources familiar with the plan cited by *Axios*.[2] Trump had surprised the world on Monday, April 7, 2025 by announcing the high-level meeting between US and Iranian officials, and reporting warned that if diplomacy failed the next stage was likely war.[2] At that point only "negotiations about the negotiations" had taken place, and even those did not appear resolved.[2] Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]], whom Iranian press reports identified as Tehran's chief negotiator, insisted the talks would be "indirect," with Omani mediators passing messages between the sides, while Trump insisted they would be "direct" — a format two US officials confirmed was indeed the plan.[2]

Asked by *Axios* whether he was prepared to use military force to destroy Iran's nuclear program, Trump said: "If the talks aren't successful with Iran, I think Iran is going to be in great danger. You know, it's not a complicated formula. Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon."[2]

## Israeli reaction

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that when Trump announced the direct talks during his Oval Office press huddle with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], "Bibi looked like he had just seen a ghost."[2] Reporter [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] noted Israel had been briefed ahead of time, but the commentary held it remained an embarrassing moment for Netanyahu, described as having pressed for war with Iran for forty years.[2] The same commentary noted Witkoff's track record negotiating with Hamas in Qatar and with [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] in Russia, asked whether fellow envoy [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] would also travel to Oman, and argued that a broader peace with the Muslim world looked close — leaving the question of what Netanyahu would do to subvert it.[2]

## Israeli assessment of a likely agreement (April 2025)

By late April 2025, [[israel|Israel]] estimated that the negotiations between the United States and [[iran|Iran]] were likely to conclude with an agreement, an Israeli official told The Jerusalem Post on Monday, 28 April.[3] The official clarified that Israel did not yet know whether the agreement would align with its demands — namely the complete dismantling of uranium enrichment facilities — or whether it would resemble the previous nuclear deal, which Israel considers a "bad agreement."[3]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the nuclear talks with Iran were going "very well" on the preceding Sunday night, telling reporters: "I think a deal will be reached... We'll have something without needing to start bombing."[3] Another round of talks between Iran and the United States was scheduled for the coming Saturday, and the Israeli official said he believed Trump's 60-day deadline to reach an agreement would eventually be extended.[3]

In parallel, Israeli Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] re-issued a threat that Israel remained ready to attack Tehran's nuclear program, telling the IDF high command that "the central mission is to prevent Iran from getting a nuclear weapon."[3] Top Israeli officials had told the Post for weeks that there was significant concern Trump would block Israel from acting so that he could cut what they viewed as a mediocre deal with the Islamic Republic.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the sequence as evidence that talks between envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and the Iranians were going well, that Trump was not the "Zionist puppet" his critics alleged, and that the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] regime would attempt to subvert the deal.[3]
