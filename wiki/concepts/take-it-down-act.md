# Take It Down Act

The **Take It Down Act** is United States legislation criminalizing the publication of nonconsensual intimate imagery — including AI-generated [[deepfake|deepfakes]] — and requiring technology platforms to remove such material under threat of enforcement by the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]].[1]

## Passage

House lawmakers voted by an overwhelming majority on Monday, 28 April 2025 to crack down on deepfake pornography, passing the bill by a 409–2 margin and teeing it up for the president's signature.[1] The [[united-states-senate|Senate]] had passed the measure by unanimous consent in February, reviving an effort that stalled out in the previous Congress; the bill gathered momentum the following month after First Lady [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] promoted it in [[congress|Congress]].[1]

## Advocacy and endorsements

With its vote, the House delivered on a priority set out by the first lady.[1] "Advancing this legislation has been a key focus since I returned to my role as First Lady this past January. I am honored to have contributed to guiding it through Congress," Melania Trump said in a statement.[1] Major platforms including Meta, X, TikTok and Snapchat endorsed the legislation, as did Republican lawmakers led by Senate Commerce Chair [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] (R-Texas), the bill's GOP cosponsor.[1]

## Free-speech concerns

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] backed the bill, joking during his joint address to Congress that he would use the new law to defend himself against online attacks: "I'm going to use that bill for myself too, if you don't mind."[1] The remark sparked alarm from critics who feared he might use the statute to suppress free speech.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she supported the legislation but was concerned about how it would be enforced, adding that she is "always skeptical when 409 Representatives agree on something" and that unanimity of that scale "doesn't give me warm feelings."[1] She found it notable that the platforms supported the legislation and said she was interested to see whether it would have a meaningful impact on social experiences online.[1] She used the occasion to argue that personal data is what matters and that users should have ownership over it, expressing hope the bill becoming law would move the needle in that direction.[1]

## First Lady's endorsement (March 2025)

In early March 2025, First Lady [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] spoke publicly in support of the Take It Down Act, described as legislation to protect Americans against the nonconsensual sharing of intimate images, including deepfakes.[2]
