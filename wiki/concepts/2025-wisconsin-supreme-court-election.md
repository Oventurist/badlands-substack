# 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court Election

The 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court election, held on Tuesday, April 1, 2025, was among the first major elections of 2025 and was widely treated as an early indicator of the national political mood roughly six months after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 victory.[1] The race was heavily contested and expensive, and featured what The Washington Post described as "a very heavy dose of" [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1]

## Result

Democrats scored a decisive victory in the Wisconsin race; the conservative judge Musk had vocally supported lost the seat by 10 points.[1] The same night, Florida hosted a pair of special elections for U.S. House seats, including one seen as potentially competitive despite Trump having carried the district by 30 points six months earlier; Republicans held both Florida seats by double digits, though by significantly smaller margins than usual.[1]

## Musk's involvement

Musk spent an estimated $25 million on the Wisconsin race, making him by far the biggest spender, a bet The Washington Post characterized as an "unforced error" that backfired.[1] The loss fed into reporting that Trump administration insiders and outside allies increasingly viewed Musk as a political liability, a dynamic tied to reports of his anticipated departure from [[doge|the Department of Government Efficiency]].[1]

## Analysis

The Washington Post's three takeaways were encouraging signs for Democrats, Musk's unforced error, and strong turnout — noting that Democratic overperformance is more meaningful when turnout is high rather than depressed.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded that "the narrative is set in place, no matter what the 'reality' is."[1]

## Out-of-state money

On the eve of the contest, Wisconsin Public Radio reported that unprecedented levels of funding backing Dane County Judge [[susan-crawford|Susan Crawford]] and Waukesha County Judge [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]] had come from across the country, with money continuing to funnel into the state.[2] Both candidates also drew grassroots support from ordinary citizens, but the bulk of their backing came from prominent political groups and megadonors.[2] Campaign contributions to individual state Supreme Court candidates are capped at $20,000 in Wisconsin, but donors with more to spend can give unlimited amounts through political action committees or the state political parties, a structure that helped drive at least $81 million in spending on the race.[2]

Badlands framed the contest alongside the [[florida-6th-congressional-district-special-election|Florida 6th District special election]] as a local race with national impact, and noted that an interview between [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] on [[the-narrative|The Narrative]] suggested a solid chance the race would go to Crawford, while maintaining that "elections are fake" because nothing had been done to fix them.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] urged readers to set emotion aside and vote — "Golden Age or Bust."[2]
