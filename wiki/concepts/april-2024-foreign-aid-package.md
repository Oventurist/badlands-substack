# April 2024 Foreign Aid Package

The April 2024 foreign aid package was a set of bills passed by the [[us-house-of-representatives|US House of Representatives]] on Saturday, April 20, 2024, providing $61 billion in fresh aid for Ukraine and ending a six-month political impasse during which Kyiv's stockpiles had dwindled in its war against Russia.[1] The package also included more than $14 billion in military aid to Israel and $9 billion in humanitarian assistance, much of which was destined for Gaza, alongside Taiwan-related provisions.[1]

## Passage

Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] worked with Democrats and overcame fierce objections from within his own party, pushing the legislation to passage on a 311 to 112 bipartisan vote.[1] The Senate was expected to pass the package, which had been negotiated with the White House, the following week.[1] Bloomberg characterized the vote as a victory for Republican defense hawks against the more isolationist wing of the party led by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

House Foreign Affairs Chairman [[michael-mccaul|Michael McCaul]], a Texas Republican, said: "We cannot be afraid of our shadows. We must be strong. We have to do what's right."[1] Democrats and some Republicans waved Ukrainian flags during the vote, a rare moment of bipartisanship in a bitterly and narrowly divided House.[1] House Democratic leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] said that "traditional House Republicans led by Speaker Mike Johnson have risen to the occasion" and that "we have a responsibility to push back against authoritarianism."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Burning Bright argued that the spectacle was useful, since the image of the vote had been "seared into" the minds of those paying attention to the congressional "clown show."[1] The commentary held that while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took a sledgehammer to the Democratic establishment in his first term, his "second, silent term" — mistakenly called the Biden administration — has been about exposing the [[uniparty|Uniparty]], and that Mike Johnson and Kevin McCarthy before him had done a stellar job exposing the controlled-opposition dynamic.[1] It further asserted that the process conflated what it called the "Globalist Proxy States" of Ukraine, Israel and Taiwan with broader elite interests, and that in a "Mind War" success should be measured solely by the direction the collective mind is moving.[1]

The same package's Israel funding drew attention because it coincided with reported US plans to sanction the [[netzah-yehuda-battalion|Netzah Yehuda Battalion]] of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]].[1]

## Johnson's four-bill plan, April 15, 2024

On the evening of Monday, April 15, 2024, after months of inaction, House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] unveiled an outline of his plan to address the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, defying Republicans who had threatened to oust him from the speakership if he advanced funding for Ukraine.[2] The convoluted plan sought to placate his critics on the right while giving national security hawks a path to move billions of dollars in aid to Ukraine, [[israel|Israel]] and Taiwan, at the risk of angering many members of his conference.[2]

Johnson planned to put four separate bills on the floor, decoupling aid for Israel — which had faced a barrage of missiles and drones from [[iran|Iran]] over the weekend in retaliation for a deadly airstrike on its consulate in Syria — from aid for Ukraine, which was far more contentious within the Republican conference.[2] Separate votes were then to follow on aid to Taiwan and on a measure satisfying Republican foreign policy demands, including the seizure of Russian assets and a House-approved bill that could ban TikTok in the United States.[2] According to five people familiar with the numbers, a draft of Johnson's plan mirrored the Senate bill but might omit humanitarian assistance directed mainly toward Gaza.[2]
