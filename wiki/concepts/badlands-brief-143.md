# Badlands Brief: All the Classic Earmarks of Discombobulation

"All the Classic Earmarks of Discombobulation" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated March 26, 2026. Like other editions, it pairs summarized news items with signed commentary from Badlands contributors, in this instance [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The edition covers five principal stories — a Justice Department records request tied to former CIA Director John Brennan, the retraction of a Telegraph article on Christian–Hezbollah cooperation in southern Lebanon, Russian escalation amid energy and currency realignment, the state of US–Iran peace talks, and new appointments to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology — plus a bonus item on an appellate immigration detention ruling.

## DOJ records request tied to John Brennan

The brief reports that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] requested records from the House Intelligence Committee relating to former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] as part of a reported criminal inquiry. Citing Punchbowl News, the brief notes that Brennan, CIA director from 2013 to 2017, "set up the counterintelligence investigation into Trump in 2016," and that [[donald-trump|Trump]] stripped Brennan of his security clearance in 2018 and again in 2025.

According to the source, the request centers on documents generated or held by the committee during prior intelligence oversight activities, and is being described as a "rare instance" of the executive branch seeking access to legislative branch materials in a prosecutorial context. The brief characterizes the move as signaling potential escalation beyond routine information-gathering, while noting that no charges had been announced and the scope of the inquiry remained unclear. It adds that the request may raise separation-of-powers issues and could lead to negotiations or litigation over compliance and the limits of executive authority.

Ashe in America observes that the committee vote authorizing cooperation "was split along party lines and deeply divided the committee," with Democrats saying they had insufficient time to review material several lawmakers received on a Friday. Her commentary frames the development as a marker of change: two years earlier, in March 2024, the idea that "our government oppressors could face accountability" was, she writes, a fantasy, and Brennan was "still considered untouchable" while legacy media treated him as an American hero rather than what she calls "the subversive traitor – perhaps the worst in US history." She concludes that with Trump in office and Brennan under investigation, the "uniparty appears frustrated that they are unable to stop it," while cautioning "No celebration without bracelets."

## Telegraph retraction and southern Lebanon

The second item concerns The Telegraph's removal of an article that reportedly documented alleged cooperation between Christian villages in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]. The brief, citing RT, states that third-party traces indicate the piece was published March 23 under the headline "Christians and Hezbollah unite against 'Epstein empire'" before being taken down roughly a day later, with no public explanation and no accessible original for independent review. The brief notes that separate reporting from Christianity Today describes Christian communities in southern Lebanon as geographically embedded in Hezbollah-influenced territory and exposed to Israeli military operations, with some residents denying affiliation with Hezbollah even while living within its operational environment.

The article situates this within a sharp escalation of Israel's posture. On March 23, Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] said Israel's border "must be the Litani," signaling support for extending control northward. A day later, Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] stated that Israeli forces would occupy territory up to the Litani River and control bridges and a broader "security zone." The brief reports that the wider conflict intensified after March 2, when Hezbollah launched missiles into [[israel|Israel]], prompting evacuation orders and expanded [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] strikes and ground activity. Lebanese authorities report more than 1,070 deaths and over one million displaced; Hezbollah has described any Israeli occupation south of the Litani as an "existential threat," and the [[united-nations|United Nations]] has called the prospect of a sustained Israeli presence "very much concerning."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's commentary argues that the region has been under Hezbollah control since the 1980s and yet contains entire villages composed of Catholics, many marked by monumental crosses. He cites the Cross of All Nations in Qanat Bakish at over 240 feet, crosses in Rmeim and in Kobayat (over 120 feet), the world's largest inhabitable cross in Ijdabra at roughly 150 feet with an interior church seating 200, and a recently completed statue of Jesus near Al-Qaa described as among the largest in the world. He notes that in the same month the IDF accidentally killed Father Pierre al Rahi with a tank round mistakenly fired in Qlayaa.

Against the conventional characterization of Hezbollah as a group that "seeks to kill Christians around the world," the commentator argues it is "actually a resistance group that has been fighting a legitimate state military (IDF) for decades," and that Hezbollah has defended and supplied Lebanese Christian villages, including providing Christmas trees. He links this to what he presents as a pattern in which Hezbollah was the first and only entity to cross into [[syria|Syria]] to defend Assad after the Arab Spring of 2011, a conflict he says led to the formation of [[isis|ISIS]], which was then used against Israel's perceived regional enemies. He frames the current moment as Christians and Muslims uniting against "the invading imperial army of [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel]]," and criticizes The Telegraph for lacking "the decency or courage" to keep its story published.

## Russia, energy resets and currency settlement

The brief reports that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] said [[russia|Russia]] launched one of the largest aerial attacks of the war, involving hundreds of drones and multiple missiles over roughly 24 hours. It also reports that [[india|India]]'s refiners booked approximately 60 million barrels of Russian crude for the coming month, more than double February levels, following supply disruption concerns tied to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. Indian refiners are increasingly settling Russian oil trades in non-dollar currencies including UAE dirhams and Chinese yuan, with workarounds converting rupees into dirhams or yuan. The Strait of Hormuz, per the US Energy Information Administration, carries roughly 20% of global petroleum liquids consumption, and analysts quoted say the crisis could accelerate a shift away from dollar-denominated oil trade.

Burning Bright's accompanying analysis argues that the Russia–Ukraine story, "once the unchallenged king of the Narrative cycle," faded into background noise as the Iranian theater absorbed attention, and that the apparent pause "reeked of" coordination rather than paralysis. He contends that renewed Russian pressure in the Donbas is being narratively anchored to Trump's recent easing of sanctions on seaborne Russian oil — temporary waivers issued amid energy price spikes from the Strait disruptions — which legacy outlets and the "[[con-inc|Con Inc.]] doom brigade" spin as Trump accidentally fueling [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s war machine. Rejecting that reading bluntly, he describes events as "the methodical unwind of the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]]," the architecture the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] was formed to dismantle. What the [[media-protectorate|Media Protectorate]] brands as incompetence, he writes, is "the controlled release of pressure valves across theaters," exposing that the old sanctions regime "was never about security, but about engineered scarcity and perpetual entanglement." The deeper play, in his framing, is [[sovereign-disentanglement|disentanglement]], and analysis that fails to "invert the Axis and Allies paradigm" is doomed from the start.

## US–Iran peace talks

The brief states that efforts to negotiate an end to the conflict between the United States and [[iran|Iran]] remain active but unresolved. Indirect channels — primarily intermediaries such as [[pakistan|Pakistan]] along with other regional go-betweens — continue to carry proposals between Washington and Tehran, including a US-backed ceasefire framework that mediators said had been relayed to Iran and publicly detailed on March 25. Iran has rejected at least one recent US-backed proposal and offered a counter-proposal with broad conditions. Public statements remain inconsistent: Trump and other US officials point to "productive conversations," while Iranian officials deny direct negotiations or question their value. Earlier in March, Iran's envoy to the United Nations said Iran had not initiated talks and voiced skepticism while strikes continued. The standoff follows nuclear and de-escalation talks that ended without a deal in late February before being overtaken by US and Israeli military action.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that Trump and Iran "continue to play games regarding the true nature of this war." He notes Trump's statement that the Iranians gave him a gift signaling a deal is possible, that Trump expects Iran to deny negotiations are occurring, and that the administration has identified Iranian officials it trusts and would work with in future. He points to the Trump administration removing names of Iranian leaders from its "Kill List" as an indication that talks may genuinely be underway.

## PCAST appointments

The brief reports that the [[white-house|White House]] announced new appointments to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST) on Wednesday. PCAST is described as a federal advisory body providing independent advice on science, technology, and innovation policy, drawing leaders from academia, industry, and research institutions, and tasked with advising on artificial intelligence, national security technology, economic competitiveness, and research priorities. It operates under the Federal Advisory Committee Act framework.

The announced appointees are Marc Andreessen, Sergey Brin, Safra Catz, Michael Dell, Jacob DeWitte, Fred Ehrsam, [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], David Friedberg, Jensen Huang, John Martinis, Bob Mumgaard, Lisa Su, and [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].

Ashe in America predicts "the Thiel Doomers are going to have a field day," counting the list as roughly half "Thiel bros" — Zuckerberg, Andreessen, Ehrsam, Ellison, Friedberg, and Huang — and half not. She says she lives with a Thiel Doomer and hears regularly the argument that Peter Thiel "is shadow running our government through his vast network of insider placements," while noting that having read *1984*, *Brave New World* and other tech-dystopian literature she recognizes "all the classic earmarks of doomer's delight." She singles out Zuckerberg for skepticism, invoking the Center for Tech and Civic Life and asking whether that history is simply being forgotten, and insists that "public trust is conditioned on accountability." Referencing "the whole [[melania-trump|Melania]] Robot thing," she concludes with a dual posture: if power is returned to the People it is up to the People to say no — "we are the plan" — while also believing that saving the nation at the enterprise level requires committed patriots and a sophisticated plan. The "seeming celebration of the birth of Westworld, and its infamous board of governors," she writes, "is definitely part of someone's plan. Trump's? Thiel's? Truth's?"

## Bonus item: Eighth Circuit immigration detention ruling

As a bonus item, the brief reports that a federal appeals court upheld the Trump administration's interpretation of immigration law. The St. Louis-based US Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit ruled 2-1 that federal law not only allows but mandates detention in the majority of deportation cases, reversing lower court decisions that had granted release petitions in Minnesota. The brief notes this was the administration's second appellate victory on the issue, following a similar 2-1 decision from the US Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit the previous month.
