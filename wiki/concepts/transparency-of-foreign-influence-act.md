# Transparency of Foreign Influence Act

The Transparency of Foreign Influence Act is the formal name of the "foreign agents law" passed by [[georgia-country|Georgia]] in May 2024, requiring NGOs, media outlets and individuals that receive more than 20% of their funding from abroad to register as entities "promoting the interests of a foreign power" and to disclose their donors.[1]

## Provisions and controversy

The legislation's reach extends beyond non-governmental organizations to media outlets and individual recipients of foreign money, distinguishing it from narrower comparable statutes.[1] It sparked weeks of protests in the Georgian capital Tbilisi, with critics arguing that the registration requirement could be used as a tool to crack down on political opposition.[1] Western governments and press treated the law as a democratic backslide and as evidence of Russian influence over Georgian policy.[1]

## Government rationale

Prime minister [[irakli-kobakhidze|Irakli Kobakhidze]] defended the law as necessary to curb external influence and avoid repeating the fate of Ukraine, which he said the West had converted into a battlefront against Russia.[1] He described the pre-existing situation as "muddy water," in which foreign influence flows unhindered and those who wish to shape Georgian governance can easily "catch fish" — locating opponents of state policy and uniting them into revolutionary movements.[1] Without the law, he argued, Georgia risked becoming another Western proxy of the kind Ukraine became before the 2014 Maidan coup in Kiev.[1]

Kobakhidze subsequently alleged that an unnamed European commissioner had threatened him over the law by phone, listing measures the West would take and adding "you see what happened to Fico, and you should be very careful," in reference to the May 15, 2024 shooting of Slovak prime minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]].[1] He answered publicly that "both America and the European Union need deoligarchization," branding them the "Global War Party."[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands commentary presented Georgia's law as following the lead of Slovakia, whose own foreign agent law preceded the attempt on Fico's life, and described both as modeled on Russian legislation passed under [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1] The commentary asserted that international NGOs function largely as fronts for Western intelligence agencies such as the [[cia|CIA]], citing groups like the Red Cross as vehicles for infiltrating struggling nations in the aftermath of natural disasters.[1] On this reading, the intensity of the Western media reaction is itself the measure of how threatening funding-transparency laws are to the "rules-based international order," understood in the brief as a euphemism for the [[new-world-order|New World Order]].[1]
