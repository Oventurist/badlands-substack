# Tokenization

Tokenization is defined as the act of replacing sensitive data with unique identification symbols that retain all the essential information about the data without compromising its security.[1] In his April 27, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Economy: Weaponized," [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cited this information-technology definition but argued that it is fundamentally related to a much grander human trait: symbolic representation.[1]

On that reading, [[money|money]] is in all respects and forms merely a token — a symbol.[1] Deschamps wrote that humans are "symbol machines" and that nearly everything they think, feel and experience is a representation of something else; because all people create symbols regardless of their station in life, all people can likewise create money.[1] Money symbolizes monetary value, and monetary value in turn symbolizes objects, things, events or labor valued by a person or group.[1]

He illustrated the breadth of tokenization with non-financial examples, including a coach smacking a football player on the backside after a good play — a token of recognition that the play was valuable to the team, communicated to the player and observed by teammates seeking the same recognition — and a husband kissing his wife after a meal she prepared.[1] Although such gestures cannot be converted into purchasing power at a store, they function as compensation for services rendered and motivate future service.[1] The practical demonstration Deschamps offered was the [[prison-yard-monetary-systems|prison yard monetary system]], in which cigarettes or candy are tokenized into functioning currency.[1]
