# White Rural Rage

***White Rural Rage: The Threat to American Democracy*** is a 2024 book by University of Maryland political science professor [[thomas-schaller|Thomas Schaller]] and op-ed writer [[paul-waldman|Paul Waldman]] that argues rural white Americans constitute a distinct danger to American democracy.[1] The book and its promotional media tour became a flashpoint in the run-up to the November 2024 election.[1]

## MSNBC segment

On Thursday, February 29, 2024, Schaller and Waldman appeared in the [[msnbc|MSNBC]] "Morning Joe" studio to promote the book, in a segment ZeroHedge described as "possibly the most overtly racist segment ever on MSNBC."[1] The two repeatedly characterized whites in the countryside as "racist" and "anti-democracy."[1] Schaller said: "They are the most racist, xenophobic, anti-immigrant, anti-gay, geodemographic group in the country," adding, "Second, they're the most conspiracist group. QAnon support and subscribers, election denialism, COVID denialism instead of scientific skepticism, Obama birtherism."[1] He further claimed of white rural people that "they don't believe in an independent press, free speech."[1]

Online reaction was hostile. One commenter wrote that "this might be the most overtly racist thing I've seen people say on TV," and a commenter identifying as "Educated Hillbilly" observed that the segment was notable for being "far more in your face and blatant," complaining: "I have not seen anyone on TV say all black city people are XYZ this blatantly racist way and be accepted on a mainstream so and get support from everyone while saying it."[1] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] framed the segment as a preview of an avalanche of mainstream media and punditry hostility directed at the rural voting public in the months before the election.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the operation's "actual impact has become staggeringly minuscule in comparison to the effort being put in," pointing to the heavy ratio the author received on his own X post announcing the book.[1] Esler contended that such "propaganda books" are produced in part to provide fodder for news clips, requiring "long mental gymnastics diatribes just to get a single news cycle of interviews that are used against them anyway."[1]

He argued the clips mainly reaffirmed how racist the intellectual left had become as "the tip of the spear for the pre-2024 panic," and that the more subversive element of the campaign was not the attack on white people on the surface but the redirection of public attention toward constant thinking about race.[1] Esler concluded that "these intentionally vague racial categories are against the realization of the sovereign, free thinking individual," and that such campaigns would keep failing because dwelling on racial identity is "a shitty form of anxiety that wastes precious energy needed to build a new society."[1]
