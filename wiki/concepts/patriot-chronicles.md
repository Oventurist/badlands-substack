# The Patriot Chronicles

The Patriot Chronicles is a [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay series by [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|Ghost of Patrick Henry]] devoted to recovering American revolutionary history as a living inheritance rather than an academic subject.[1] In "Horseshoe Theory - Part 1," the author described the series as arguing that this heritage "is our history, and it needs to be reclaimed."[1]

Installments referenced in that article include a piece on Francis Marion, the guerrilla commander known as the Swamp Fox, whose blacksmiths forged crude mill-saw sabers in a makeshift forest forge — an image the author invoked self-deprecatingly to describe his own hand-made graphic for the Horseshoe Theory essay.[1] An introductory installment discussed the mythology of heroes and cultural norms that the ancient Greeks built from the legends, myths and songs attributed to the Muses.[1]

## Relationship to Horseshoe Theory

The author explicitly linked the spirit of the Patriot Chronicles to that of the [[horseshoe-theory|Horseshoe Theory]] series, confirming that readers noticing parallels between the two were correct.[1] Both projects rest on the premise that inspiration — narrative, myth and shared history — rather than argument alone is what moves populations, a claim he traced back to Herodotus and the nine Muses after whom the books of *The Histories* were named.[1]

## Launch and stated purpose (2023)

The series was formally announced on 6 June 2023 in "Introducing 'The Patriot Chronicles': Reclaiming Our Past, Present, and Future," published on the Badlands Substack.[2] The introduction framed the project as a response to an age "of war, and peace, and chaos, and madness," in which the wisdom of ancestors who had defeated an apparently unassailable global tyranny could supply inspiration — with the author suggesting that the same enemy is still being fought today.[2] A recurring theme of the introduction was that victory in those historical struggles came less from individual genius than from bonds of brotherhood among teams of patriots who set aside their differences for a common cause.[2]

Each entry, the author explained, would feature a specific subject — most often an individual historical figure, but sometimes a group or an event — with cross-over cameos by previously established subjects in later installments.[2] The stated goal was a writing style that is captivating, informative and above all inspiring, on the premise that inspiration is the core function of any mythology.[2] Many of the stories, he wrote, would furnish anecdotal proof of Joseph Campbell's Hero's Journey, a framework discussed at length by [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] and [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] on the Badlands show [[knowledge-based|Knowledge Based]].[2]

## "All history is interpretation"

The introduction's central methodological claim, repeated three times as a refrain, is that **all history is interpretation**.[2] Just as commentators inside and outside [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] derive different readings from the same set of current events — the author contrasted [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s economic analysis of BRICS as evidence of the [[sovereign-alliance|sovereign alliance]] with [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s "Righteous Russia" series — academic historians build competing interpretations from the same record.[2] A third category of writers, "those without honor," was said to omit, obfuscate or fabricate facts to manipulate public opinion, and to suppress opposing views because their ideas cannot win in a free marketplace.[2]

The essay set out a conventional distinction between primary sources — government documents, or letters and diaries written by eyewitnesses — and secondary sources created later by parties removed from the event, noting that [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s [[devolution|Devolution Series]] is most convincing where it rests on primary documents.[2] A newspaper article or letter by a contemporary who was not an eyewitness was described as a weaker form of primary source.[2] The author warned that as academic journals increasingly cite only secondary sources, successive generations of scholars can produce layered misunderstandings of the past even in good faith, and argued that history records must therefore be kept decentralized.[2] He praised fellow Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan Delarme]] for appending works-cited sections to his articles, calling the practice an academic polish that is especially effective when debunking purportedly settled science.[2]

Despite this, the author stated plainly that the series is *not* an academic journal: though deeply researched, it would avoid MLA formalism and disputes internal to academia, and should be read as an oral history — stories told by elders around a campfire, an American mythology.[2] The purpose of such a mythology, he wrote, is to reinforce a shared culture and to promote idealized traits of altruism such as courage, morality, honor and loyalty through tales of hardship, peril and triumph against certain defeat.[2]

## Reclaiming the American mythos

The introduction asserted that a coordinated, sophisticated and well-funded effort is underway to strip Americans of their heritage and of the memory of common struggles, so that they may be divided and more easily controlled.[2] It located this within the broader Culture War and asked how the Cultural Marxists could be defeated and the American Spirit revived, answering with the proverb about eating an elephant one bite at a time.[2] The author cited [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s extensive writing on the [[war-of-stories|War of Stories]] and General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]]'s echoing of that sentiment in tweets and in his book on [[fifth-generation-warfare|Fifth Generation Warfare]], holding that false narratives and counter-narratives have escalated the conflict into full-blown information warfare — in academia and in culture alike.[2]

## Relationship to the Garden of Heroes

The author presented the series as "a digital kickstart" for [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s proposed [[garden-of-heroes|Garden of Heroes]], a national park of monuments to American heroes.[2] He said he had been composing such short stories for friends and family by email and social media as far back as 2015, and that the Garden of Heroes was one of the two aspects of Trump's [[1776-commission|1776 Commission]] that most excited him — the other being the restoration of Classical Architecture to federal buildings, his professional background.[2]

## Format notes and first installment

In an author's note, he observed that many of the introduction's images featured firearms and used the coincidence to reassert that firearms and the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] are a cornerstone of freedom and American culture, promising firearms as a recurring theme.[2] He closed with thanks to Jon, Kate and the Badlands crew, expressing the hope of igniting "the brushfires of freedom" in readers' hearts and minds.[2] A teaser headed "Beware of the Swamp…" previewed the first chapter: long before the emergence of the novel [[deep-state|Deep State]], the swamp belonged to "another apex predator," a phantom leading hardened veterans who personified the teachings of Sun Tzu and, armed with muskets and their wits, stopped the most powerful empire of the age at the most critical moment of the [[american-revolution|American Revolution]] — [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]], the subject of Part 1.[2]
