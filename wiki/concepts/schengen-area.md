# Schengen Area

The Schengen Zone is the European area of free movement whose internal borders are normally free of routine checks. It came under strain in September 2024 when [[germany|Germany]] imposed controls at all of its land borders as part of a crackdown on migration, placing restrictions on the free-movement area and stirring anger among its European neighbors.[1]

## The September 2024 German controls

From Monday, September 16, 2024, in addition to existing controls with Austria, Switzerland, the Czech Republic and Poland, Germany introduced internal border controls with France, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Belgium and Denmark.[1] Berlin gained the power to reject people at all land borders, according to a statement from the German Interior Ministry, with the new rules to last six months initially.[1] Reporting by [[cnn|CNN]] framed the move as a marker of how far Germany had shifted on migration, given that the government under [[angela-merkel|Angela Merkel]] welcomed more than one million new arrivals during the 2015–2016 migrant crisis.[1] The decision came days after Germany struck a controlled migration deal with [[kenya|Kenya]] to open its doors to skilled and semi-skilled Kenyan workers.[1]

## Neighbor resistance and burden-sharing

Badlands argued that the criticism of Germany's border tightening was driven less by principle than by burden-shifting: other EU states do not wish to absorb Germany's share of the migration load.[1] [[poland|Poland]] pushed back, and both Greece and Austria warned that they would not accept migrants rejected by Germany.[1] Badlands characterized the growing resistance of EU nation states to what McKinsey and the World Economic Forum have described as "The Great Migration" as well-founded, noting that after more than a decade in which tolerance and empathy governed European migration sentiment, the approach had hardened in light of surging arrivals — particularly from the Middle East and Ukraine — as well as terror attacks motivated by Islamic extremism.[1]

## Media framing dispute

Badlands criticized [[cnn|CNN]]'s framing, mocking the outlet's repeated presentation of resistance to unchecked migration as rooted in racism and its description of the German coalition as seeking to counter the "burgeoning far-right" [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany (AfD)]], "known for its explicitly anti-immigrant and anti-Islam agenda."[1] The commentary predicted that U.S. media would extend the same spin to Germany's immigration decision, as it had in labeling the AfD white supremacists during its earlier 2024 campaign, which produced a globalist panic-inducing electoral result.[1] Badlands also posed the displacement question: if EU countries turn off the migrant faucet, migration will not stop but shift toward more accepting jurisdictions where border security is deemed racist and access to basic income and government services is comparatively easy.[1]
