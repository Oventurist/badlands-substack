# Mossad Media

"Mossad Media" is a pejorative used in Badlands commentary for the network of online pro-Israel activists and commentators who, in the Brief's framing, function as an information-warfare arm advancing Israeli state interests to American audiences.[1]

## July 2026 usage

In the July 6, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the "real story" surrounding the [[funeral-of-ali-khamenei|funeral of Ali Khamenei]] was the reaction of Mossad Media online.[1] [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] was described as spending the Fourth of July rage-posting about [[iran|Iran]] and calling for terrorist attacks against civilians and world leaders attending the funeral, having earlier called the event a "target rich environment."[1] [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] was cited as chiming in.[1]

The commentary contrasted the treatment of Palestinian children who throw rocks at armed IDF soldiers — typically arrested or shot and labeled terrorists — with Israelis openly fantasizing about killing tens of thousands of civilians by bombing a funeral for a deceased world leader, and posted the dictionary definition of terrorism in anticipation of accusations of sympathizing with terrorists.[1]

The stated logic attributed to Mossad Media was that bombing the funeral would drive the Iranian people to denounce the state religion and overthrow the government, disregarding the human desire for revenge and the consequences for the United States and [[israel|Israel]] of killing hundreds of attending world leaders and diplomats.[1] The Brief argued that global hostility toward Israel and its online culture stems not from Jewishness but from the consistent expression of violent extremism with impunity, coupled with demands that such violence be carried out by the U.S. military at U.S. taxpayer expense.[1] When [[donald-trump|Trump]] called Iran a "spoiled child," the author suggested he was in fact referring to Israel.[1]

As additional evidence that Loomer is a [[deep-state|Deep State]] propagandist, the Brief pointed to back-to-back posts promoting [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] and disparaging [[vladimir-putin|Putin]].[1] A separate viral claim circulated by the same milieu was dismissed as fake news, with the Brief noting the 2020 revelation that Mexican cartels were using Israeli-made weapons, a practice said to date to the 1980s.[1]

## June 2026: reaction to the Trump–Netanyahu call

The term was used again in June 2026 when [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] assessed reactions to Axios reporting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had berated Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in an expletive-filled June 1 phone call.[2] He argued that the reaction from "Mossad Media" was itself evidence the call was real: [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] called for an FBI investigation to catch the leaker and [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] denounced disloyalty within Trump's camp, whereas a fabricated story would simply have been brushed off as fake news.[2] The demand for "heads on pikes," he suggested, reflected fear that an audio recording of the call might exist.[2]
