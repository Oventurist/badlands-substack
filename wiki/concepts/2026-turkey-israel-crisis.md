# 2026 Turkey–Israel Crisis

The **2026 Turkey–Israel crisis** refers to the sharp escalation of hostile rhetoric between Ankara and Jerusalem in June 2026, in which Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] exchanged public threats over Israeli military operations in [[syria|Syria]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[1]

## The exchange

Erdoğan criticized Israeli strikes in Syria and Lebanon and warned that Israel's regional actions could eventually threaten [[turkey|Turkey]], accusing Israel of pursuing expansionist policies and renewing calls for stronger support for the Palestinian cause, including the eventual "liberation" of Jerusalem.[1] Addressing the Turkish Parliament, he said Israel's publicly stated aim of establishing [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] through the conquest and annexation of neighboring countries — described by Israel as necessary for its security — must never be allowed to be achieved.[1]

Netanyahu responded on X by branding Erdoğan an "antisemitic dictator" who supports [[hamas|Hamas]], saying Israel would continue acting against security threats across the region and rejecting Turkish criticism of its operations.[1] The friction was driven by Israeli strikes against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]-linked targets in Lebanon and military assets in Syria, which Turkish officials called destabilizing and Israeli leaders defended as necessary to prevent attacks by Iranian-backed groups.[1]

## Background and American posture

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] traced the pattern of escalating rebukes to the October 7 attacks, recalling that [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] had warned Erdoğan at the United Nations he might become the next Saddam Hussein, and that Israeli opposition figures in the [[knesset|Knesset]] had voiced a desire to see Turkey become "the next Iran."[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took a notably different tone, praising Erdoğan as a strong and effective negotiator when asked about the [[turkish-f-35-sale-dispute|withheld F-35 jets]] and emphasizing the importance of US–Turkey relations.[1]

## Strategic analysis

The Badlands assessment held that as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] advances north in Lebanon and takes [[beirut|Beirut]], it moves closer to the Turkish sphere of influence that now extends in practice into Syria, and that Ankara is more concerned about an Israeli advance on Damascus threatening Turkish–Saudi plans to rebuild the country.[1] It speculated that Netanyahu would leverage Israel's recently bolstered alliance with [[greece|Greece]] — forged over shared contempt for Turkey and rooted in a feud dating to the Ottoman conquest of Byzantine Constantinople — to have the Greek navy harass Turkish forces in the Aegean Sea or near [[cyprus|Cyprus]].[1]

The strategic significance offered was that a clash between Greece and Turkey, both [[nato|NATO]] members, would not invoke [[article-5|Article 5]], whereas a direct Israeli attack on Turkey would place Israel at war with the entire alliance.[1] The writer judged a three-way Israel–Greece–Ukraine encirclement of Turkey too ambitious to succeed but plausible for a desperate Netanyahu, warned that a naval conflict would open a western front in the Greater Israel campaign and bring Europe into the Middle East war, and concluded that Erdoğan must work with allies in [[pakistan|Pakistan]], Egypt and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to contain the IDF in Lebanon and fold [[iran|Iran]] into the emerging regional security architecture.[1]
