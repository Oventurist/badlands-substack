# CUT: Daughters of the West

*CUT: Daughters of the West* is a documentary film written and produced by [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], described in Badlands Media coverage as "an explosive documentary that reveals the ugly truth about the effect of Wokism on our daughters."[1] The film was announced with a release date of April 15, 2023, and was promoted through the website daughtersofthewestfilm.com, where readers were directed to pre-order rentals or purchases using the promotional code "BADLANDS."[1]

## Intent and design

Esler described the film as deliberately conceived as a weapon of [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] (5GW), an intention he said had been present throughout its development but which he had rarely discussed publicly.[1] He explained that he withheld those intentions because the elements of the film carrying them were meant to remain covert and camouflaged — not out of deceit, but in accordance with the principles of 5GW, in which operations blend into the ordinary socio-cultural landscape.[1]

## Subject matter

The film's central argument extends beyond politically driven [[gender-ideology|gender ideology]]. Esler said *CUT* explores the exponentially rising rates of teenage girls obtaining breast augmentation and labiaplasty (cosmetic genital surgery) both before and during what he called the "trans-craze."[1] He argued these cosmetic surgery trends were not initiated by a politically charged ideology such as gender theory, yet they encouraged girls to permanently alter their bodies based on trends handed to them by mainstream media and the normalisation of self-diagnosis.[1]

By presenting these cosmetic surgery trends alongside the testimony of [[detransitioners|detransitioners]], Esler framed the film as an invitation to both left-wing and right-wing parents to examine what is happening to the "daughters of the West" outside of partisan framing.[1] He tied this to his broader claim that the adversary behind the phenomenon is apolitical and depends on political division for cover, so that uncovering apolitical elements of the issue allows cross-partisan networks to form.[1]

## Distribution appeal

Esler closed his Badlands feature by asking readers to help "wage 5G warfare" by getting the film into the world, arguing that broad distribution would break the collective into greater awareness beyond the information warfare in which audiences had been entrenched.[1]
