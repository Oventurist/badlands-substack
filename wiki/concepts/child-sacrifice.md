# Child Sacrifice

**Child sacrifice** is the ritual killing or ritual exploitation of children, historically attested in ancient Mediterranean religion and, in the framing advanced by Badlands Media contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], a recurring instrument of elite social control that a modern shadow government works to both conceal and normalize.[1]

## Thesis of cyclical recurrence

Esler frames the subject through the lens of cyclical time, arguing that human collectives pass through repeating cycles in the same way individuals encounter recurring personal challenges.[1] From that premise he poses a series of questions: whether the elites of the past normalized child sacrifice, whether an elite class is normalizing it again today, and whether the cultural weaponry of [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] is being used both to cover for elites who practice actual forms of child sacrifice and to normalize child sacrifice within the wider culture.[1] He argues that of all the ideas suppressed under the label "conspiracy theory," the concept of elite child sacrifice has been the most vehemently outcast.[1]

## Historical precedent: Carthage

The historical anchor of the argument is [[carthage|Carthage]], which Esler describes as perhaps the prime example of a civilization that produced an elite culture normalizing the ritual sacrifice of children to [[baal|Baal]].[1] He notes that the accusation has itself been surrounded by decades of information warfare portraying it as "racist propaganda" recycled from Greek and Roman smears, while archaeological evidence reported by [[david-soren|Dr. David Soren]] and Dr. Lawrence Stager indicates that the Precinct of Tanit contained only cremated children, from stillborn fetuses to four-year-olds, dedicated by markers to Tanit and Ba'al Hammon.[1] Crucially, the practice was "democratized" over time: fifth-century BC offerings came almost entirely from elite parents, but by the third century BC the markers included professionals and artisans, and the ratio of children to animals rose from three-to-one to ten-to-one.[1]

## Modern forms

Esler distinguishes literal ritual killing from what he calls broader "forms of child sacrifice" — the sacrifice of childhood innocence through cultural engineering.[1] He points to the [[balenciaga|Balenciaga]] advertising controversy, in which a prop rendered the brand name as "Baalenciaga," as evidence that modern cultural powerhouses echo Carthaginian invocation of Ba'al directly.[1] He also cites the sexualization of children in schools through warped sex education and [[queer-theory|queer theory]], the repositioning of children's entertainer Lil Nas X toward Satanic imagery, and the sextortion operations attributed to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and [[sean-combs|Sean "P. Diddy" Combs]].[1]

Esler argues that Combs's alleged operation carries "all the basic elements of a ritual sacrifice": a minor is selected for vulnerability, the vulnerability is deliberately arranged, grooming prepares the target, drugs and alcohol are supplied, cameras capture the abuse, and the child's suffering is converted into a token of power held over the compromised celebrity.[1] He states he is aware of no evidence that Combs used explicitly Satanic ritual, but maintains that repeated abuse of minors to acquire and wield cultural power is itself ritual in form.[1]

## Suppression and strategy

The concealment of child sacrifice, in this account, is not merely the product of villains who enjoy suffering but a tactical, operational and strategic necessity for an enemy pursuing global domination, because trauma is the mechanism of [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]]-style compartmentalized mind control.[1] Esler concludes that campaigners may be taking bait by trying to prove Satanic elites are literally killing children to a mind-controlled public, when the more persuasive path is to expose the visible cultural changes that reveal forms of child sacrifice at work.[1] His closing formulation is that "a culture that accepts literal child sacrifice is downstream from a culture that accepts the sacrifice of childhood innocence."[1]
