# Great Replacement Theory

The "Great Replacement" is the claim that native populations of Western countries are being demographically displaced through mass immigration and falling birth rates. Mainstream outlets describe it as a racist conspiracy theory; it recurs in coverage of both online radicalization and platform moderation fights.[1]

## In the Musk coverage

CNN's March 2024 analysis of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s interview with [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] accused Musk of "yet again" giving credence to the racist Great Replacement theory, alongside equating content moderation to censorship, criticizing the press, attacking DEI programs and disparaging advertisers who left [[x|X]].[1]

## In the Buffalo litigation

The theory — referred to in the commentary as "white replacement theory" — was also cited as the alleged motive for the 2022 Buffalo grocery store shooting carried out by [[payton-gendron|Payton Gendron]], the factual predicate of the lawsuit against [[meta-platforms|Meta]], [[google|Alphabet]], [[reddit|Reddit]] and [[4chan]] permitted to proceed by Justice [[paula-feroleto|Paula Feroleto]].[1] [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that treating the theory as uniquely dangerous supports a definition of "extremist" that in practice refers only to right-wing ideas, and predicted that a plaintiff victory would become the pretext for broader crackdowns on conservative political speech.[1]

## Invocation in the 2024 campaign

NPR reported in September 2024 that when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told debate viewers that "our elections are bad, and a lot of these illegal immigrants coming in, they're trying to get them to vote," he was echoing the latest iteration of the great replacement theory — the claim that there is a plan to bring nonwhite immigrants into the United States and other Western countries to replace white voters in service of a political agenda — which NPR described as having moved from the fringe into the Republican mainstream.[2] The outlet noted that no evidence had surfaced of a mass scheme to register undocumented immigrants and that [[noncitizen-voting|noncitizen voting]] in federal elections is illegal and rare.[2] Badlands rejected the framing, pointing to federal data showing only 35 prosecutions of aliens voting between 2001 and 2021 and to Colorado's automatic DMV registration practices as evidence that the question is under-enforced rather than nonexistent.[2]

## Kalergi and the infiltration framing (2025)

The May 2025 Badlands essay "On Infiltration and Israel" advanced a version of the replacement thesis rooted in the [[kalergi-plan|Kalergi Plan]], asserting that [[richard-coudenhove-kalergi|Count Coudenhove-Kalergi]] proposed in the 1910s and 1920s to flood Europe with non-Europeans to create a single "euro-asiatic, negroid race."[3] The author argued that Western governments have facilitated mass migration with such zeal that many Western leaders must subscribe to that vision, and that European countries have been flooded with millions of military-aged men.[3]

The essay framed the mechanism as [[infiltration|infiltration]] rather than mere policy failure: no country would welcome an invading force unless it had first been groomed and demoralized into it by infiltrators who scripted a movie to make European peoples despise themselves and accept their own replacement.[3] It cited the [[grooming-gangs|UK rape gangs]] as evidence and claimed that anyone who notices is labelled a Nazi, with much online pro-Nazi content — and groups such as the [[groypers|Groypers]] — existing to validate that label by giving White advocacy the worst possible optics.[3]
