# Badlands Brief: Trump Conducts the Chaos as the Uniparty Concentrates Fire

"Trump Conducts the Chaos as the Uniparty Concentrates Fire" is an installment of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dated March 4, 2026. The edition pairs summarized news items with commentary from the contributors [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]. Its five segments cover the ongoing United States–Iran war, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s claim that he "forced Israel's hand," the Democratic Party's primary infighting ahead of the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]], a new American insurance guarantee for Gulf shipping, and the Senate testimony of Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]]. A bonus item addresses [[openai|OpenAI]]'s rewriting of a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] contract.

## The war with Iran

The Brief reports that the United States, under Trump, launched military strikes on Iran in coordination with Israel, escalating into a war then in its fifth day. A major weekend attack killed Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]]; the source notes that no official photograph of his body was released although Iranian state media confirmed the death. Missile exchanges and shrapnel damage were said to have affected nearly every Middle Eastern country, and several U.S. service members were killed, including in a drone strike in Kuwait and in friendly-fire incidents.

According to the article, misinformation proliferated online, with AI-generated or recycled images falsely purporting to show Khamenei's body under rubble, destroyed U.S. ships and aircraft, downed jets, soldier casualties and Iranian missile impacts. Many were debunked as old footage, video-game clips or outright fabrications.

The strikes are described as following warnings about Iran's advancing nuclear and missile capabilities. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] stated that the United States preempted attacks on American forces given known Israeli plans and Iran's bad-faith negotiations. Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], previously an anti-interventionist voice, defended the operation as limited and necessary to prevent nuclear development. Trump estimated the campaign would last four to five weeks without ruling out ground forces, while officials insisted it would neither become an endless war nor aim at regime change.

Politically, the Brief records that Democrats largely unified in opposition, condemning the strikes, pushing war-powers restraints and highlighting risks such as rising gas prices before the midterms, though some moderates broke ranks. The aftermath of the Gaza war intensified scrutiny of Democrats' ties to Israel in primaries in Illinois, Michigan and Texas, where pro-Israel positions or links to [[aipac|AIPAC]] were weaponized by challengers amid a party base shifting toward sympathy with Palestinians.

Commenting, Burning Bright argues that Trump "has not merely stepped into the chaos" but has "deliberately placed the entire mindscape into it." In this reading the conflict is not another conventional Forever War but "a new Forever War Narrative" that Trump dares the machine to sustain, signaling that he can extend timelines precisely where his adversaries need them to contract. Legacy outlets, the commentary notes, now concede that reporting out of Tehran is confusing or digitally manufactured, yet cannot call the war fake without shattering the premise "that any American action in the region must devolve into quagmire to be considered legitimate."

The commentary describes the resulting scramble: the institutional Left, having cultivated anti-Zionism in its base after Gaza, is caught in a "self-made narrative pincer" in which supporting the strikes alienates street-level rage while condemning them requires defending the Iranian regime its own activists call existential evil. The [[uniparty|NeoCon establishment]] received its cinematic opening act but has shifted from celebration to concern-trolling because the operation is not trending toward total regime change — "No endless boots on the ground. No new occupation." Even the [[america-first|America First]] contingent aligned with Vance has fallen into "a telling silence," because the Israel question resists simple pro- or anti-Zionist framing and demands evaluation against First Principles.

## Trump's claim to have forced Israel's hand

In an Oval Office press conference with German Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]], Trump said he may have "forced Israel's hand" into war with Iran's "lunatics," claiming Tehran was preparing to attack first and that deadlocked nuclear talks left no alternative. Denying that Israel dragged the United States into the conflict — then in its fourth day — he said he had spoken fifteen times with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in the preceding two months and gave a green light once negotiations collapsed. Trump reported Iran's navy, air force, air defenses, radar and most military capabilities "knocked out," confirmed Khamenei's death in an Israeli strike, and said most potential future Iranian leaders were already dead. He predicted oil prices would spike temporarily before falling below pre-war levels and urged Iranian dissidents not to protest during the campaign.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls this "a hell of a plot twist," wondering whether Netanyahu will end up submitting after biting off more than he can chew, and suggesting the episode would explain a pattern of Trump adopting NeoCon positions in order to bait them into self-destructive action.

## Democratic "civil war" before the midterms

The Brief reports that Democrats are locked in an expensive internal civil war ahead of the 2026 midterms, with more than $64 million already spent on House primaries. Thirty incumbents face well-funded challengers who have each raised at least $100,000, and nearly a dozen incumbents are being out-raised. Party leaders complain the infighting drains resources needed for competitive general-election races. Many insurgent and progressive candidates, including those backed by [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] and [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], have refused to commit to supporting House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]], raising fears of a rebellious freshman class. Early tests came on March 3 in North Carolina and Texas, including challenges to Representatives [[valerie-foushee|Valerie Foushee]] (by Sanders-backed [[nida-allam|Nida Allam]]), [[al-green|Al Green]] (by [[christian-menefee|Christian Menefee]]) and [[julie-johnson|Julie Johnson]] (by former Representative [[collin-allred|Collin Allred]]), driven largely by generational change and anti-establishment frustration.

Ashe in America frames the fight as the end stage of a revolution: the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democrat Socialists of America]] have been "waging a temu revolution on Americans since 2017" and failed to make every city [[zohran-mamdani|Mamdani]]'s New York. In 2020 the DSA "occupied all the cities, carried by the students, the idealists, the restless believers in a new order," but having failed to expand beyond a few blue strongholds they are now inconvenient for the uniparty left's shift to the middle. Because the left must appear "stable and sane" in order to paint Trump and his supporters as extremists, the DSA has become a liability. The commentary invokes the Jacobins, the Bolsheviks and the Red Guard as precedents in which "the idealists get handled when the phase of the revolution changes," observing that "stability demands hierarchy" and "hierarchy demands control," while DSA activists are uncontrollable ideologues. It asks what this means for Mamdani in New York or Johnston in Denver, predicting "entertainment for the rest of us" and possible retribution.

## Tanker insurance and the Strait of Hormuz

Oil prices tumbled on a Tuesday after Trump announced that the United States would immediately provide political risk insurance and financial guarantees, at "very reasonable" prices, for all maritime trade — especially energy — transiting the Persian Gulf, through the [[development-finance-corporation|Development Finance Corporation]]. He added that the U.S. Navy would escort tankers through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] "if necessary." The measure followed Iranian attacks and threats to close the strait, which triggered war-risk policies that froze tanker traffic, sent supertanker rates to all-time highs and spiked oil volatility, and it instantly eased market fears of major supply disruption.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry notes Trump's repeated statements that the insurance industry has exploited the American people, and connects the announcement to Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s claim that he had discussed joint anti-cartel operations with Trump. He points to [[united-states-southern-command|SOUTHCOM]]'s announcement of new operations in Ecuador — the location Petro long described as the new hub of cartel activity — and to Petro's contention that the real cartel leaders are not in the jungles but in major cities working for banks and corporations.

## Kristi Noem's Senate testimony

Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem faced sharp bipartisan criticism at a [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing on March 3, 2026, over the Trump administration's mass deportation operations and [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] spending. Noem defended aggressive [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] actions in Minnesota, including [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]], which led to the fatal shootings of two U.S. citizen protesters — [[renee-good|Renee Good]] on January 7 and [[alex-pretti|Alex Pretti]] on January 24 — amid clashes in Minneapolis and St. Paul. She blamed "violent protesters" for the chaos, while Democrats accused officers of excessive force and Noem of wrongly labeling the victims "domestic terrorists."

Lawmakers of both parties also criticized a $220 million advertising campaign featuring Noem herself and a $143 million no-bid contract awarded to a company created eleven days earlier that subcontracted work to a firm tied to the husband of a former top DHS spokeswoman. Senator [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] called for her resignation over misplaced priorities; Noem denied personal involvement in the contracts and defended the ad spending as effective.

Ashe in America, who watched the nearly five-hour hearing, writes that the real story was not the "arrogant, whiney democrats with fringe examples of alleged overreach" but the Republican attacks on the Secretary and on immigration enforcement. She describes [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]]'s position as a surprise and the most fiery Republican remarks as coming from Tillis, who told Noem he was giving her a performance review and would not permit her to respond, repeatedly throwing shade at Trump allies such as [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] and [[corey-lewandowski|Cory Lewandowski]]. Noting that Tillis's Senate career is over, she attributes his conduct to unresolved conflict with the President. She says she skipped only the questioning by Cory Booker and Mazie Hirono and listened even to "Pencilneck" [[adam-schiff|Schiff]], and that afterward she spoke with a source inside DHS about departmental dynamics. Her closing observation is structural: Noem, who reports to Trump, absorbs the "bipartisan evisceration" before the committee while border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], who also reports to Trump, enjoys bipartisan cover in the field and is cited by senators as the favorable contrast — "all the while, immigration enforcement continues unimpeded."

## Bonus item: OpenAI and the Pentagon

The bonus segment reports that OpenAI rewrote a rushed Pentagon artificial-intelligence contract after backlash over risks of enabling domestic mass surveillance. The deal to supply OpenAI models for classified U.S. military operations was announced on a Friday following the collapse of negotiations with rival [[anthropic|Anthropic]], which walked away over strict red lines banning domestic surveillance of Americans and lethal autonomous weapons. Facing employee protests, chalk graffiti at headquarters and an open letter signed by nearly 900 workers at OpenAI and Google, chief executive [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] called the initial rollout "sloppy" and lacking clear communication. The company added explicit language prohibiting "intentional" use of its systems for domestic surveillance or monitoring of U.S. persons and nationals, including through commercially acquired personal data, and excluded Pentagon intelligence agencies such as the [[national-security-agency|NSA]] pending new agreements. The Pentagon said the tools remain limited to lawful military use via cloud access only.
