# Mystery Cults

"Mystery cults" is the term used by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] for the initiatic religious systems of antiquity — chiefly those of Mithras, Cybele-Attis and Orpheus — which he treats as instruments of imperial governance rather than as spontaneous popular religion.[1] The cults are a recurring subject of his work, including his Revenge of the Mystery Cult trilogy, and they form the analytical backdrop of his November 15, 2025 essay "Don't Be Symbol-Minded."[1]

In that essay Ehret described the [[persian-empire|Persian Empire]] as the "Marcher Lord of Babylon" which managed the global mystery cults, and argued that after the [[ionian-revolt|Ionian Revolt]] of 499–493 BC was crushed the cults of Mithras and Cybele-Attis were infused into the governance of the defeated Anatolian and Ionian population.[1] It was at that point, he wrote, that the [[phrygian-cap|Phrygian cap]] — previously a republican emblem of freedom and honor — was rebranded as a cult attribute; earlier Persian depictions of Mithra carried no such cap.[1] The reworked cults were imported into Rome via Phrygia during the Second Punic War of 218–201 BC, with Mithra's first official Roman appearance dated to the period of the general Pompey (100–48 BC).[1] Ehret described the cults of Mithra, Cybele-Attis and Orpheus as having sought to destroy Christianity in the early years of that movement.[1]

Ehret argued that the cults' presence in the symbolism of the [[2024-paris-summer-olympics|2024 Paris Olympics]] is demonstrable — the games used a Phrygian cap mascot and featured a number of Mithraic themes — but rejected the inference that every appearance of cult-associated symbolism proves cult control.[1] Against the view he attributed to [[peter-duke|Peter Duke]], that satanic cults have steered every side of every battle since ancient times including the American Revolution, he insisted that a battle over the meaning of symbols has been continuous since the most ancient times, and that the same emblem can serve either the oligarchical or the republican side.[1]
