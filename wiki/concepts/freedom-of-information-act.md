# Freedom of Information Act

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) is the United States federal statute establishing a public right of access to records held by executive branch agencies, subject to enumerated exemptions. Agencies are obligated to respond to requests within statutory deadlines; when they fail to do so, requesters may sue to compel production. In the Badlands Media corpus, FOIA functions less as a neutral legal mechanism than as a recurring illustration of how federal agencies manage, delay, and blunt disclosure.

## The FBI records case

The March 24, 2026 Badlands Brief, [[badlands-brief-50d|Alternative Annexations & New(ish) Regime Change]], describes a FOIA lawsuit filed in August 2025 stemming from a June request that the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] failed to fulfill. In response to the litigation, the Bureau identified as many as 1.9 million pages of potentially responsive records — between roughly 950,000 and 1.9 million pages held in multiple safes inside a [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility]] at headquarters, according to a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] filing. The FBI stated the material must be digitized and reviewed before responsiveness can be determined, a process expected to take ten to twelve months, with classification review likely required. Some legacy records fall under the authority of the Department of Justice Office of Inspector General and must be reviewed in consultation with that office, while others may require coordination with additional agencies.

## Interpretive framing

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] presents the case as evidence of asymmetric legal obligation. Her central claim is that the FBI "bought themselves another year of obstruction," and she contrasts this with the consequences a private citizen would face for telling the government it needed an additional year to redact material before complying with a lawful, statute-driven demand: "You'd go to prison." She predicts the agency will receive the extension, retain its secrets, and that the underlying records may surface only decades later, "long after accountability is possible."
