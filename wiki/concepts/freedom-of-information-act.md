# Freedom of Information Act

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) is the U.S. federal statute that allows members of the public to request records held by executive branch agencies, and provides for litigation when an agency fails to produce responsive documents.[1]

## Use in COVID-19 treatment disclosure litigation

FOIA featured centrally in a December 2023 lawsuit brought by [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] against the [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]].[1] AFL had submitted a FOIA request in August 2022 seeking records about [[hydroxychloroquine|hydroxychloroquine]] from March 1 to September 1, 2020, and a further request in September 2022 concerning [[ivermectin|ivermectin]]; both agencies acknowledged receipt and issued tracking numbers but neither produced the requested material, prompting suit.[1] The requests were aimed at establishing when and why officials including [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] discouraged the two treatments.[1]

The episode is cited within the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] corpus as an example of federal agencies using procedural non-compliance to blunt statutory transparency requirements.[1]

## FOIA in the Uranium One dispute

Badlands Media's "[[irregular-warfare|Irregular Warfare]]" treats FOIA as a central front in the fight over the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] record.[3] A request filed during the first Trump administration sought all documents pertaining to the [[fbi|FBI]]'s undercover investigation of the deal; the bureau identified 37 pages and declined to release them, citing national security, law enforcement techniques, individual privacy, and interagency communication.[3] Its declaration and list of withheld records were posted in the FBI's online FOIA vault under the title "Uranium One Transaction," accompanied only by the release of already-public congressional letters.[3] Joe Lange argues the withholding was lawful protection of evidence in an ongoing grand jury and RICO process rather than a cover-up, and that [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] was telling the truth when he declined to comment on ongoing investigations.[3]

The article also identifies FOIA defense as the professional specialty of attorney [[lisa-barsoomian|Lisa Barsoomian]], who with R. Craig Lawrence litigated against FOIA suits on behalf of officials including [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]], [[james-comey|James Comey]], [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and the Clintons, as well as the FBI and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] — a role Lange characterizes as institutionalized concealment.[3]

## Evasion by private email server (Badlands account)

The August 2023 Badlands article "Clowns in America" argues that the Freedom of Information Act is "a huge deal" and instrumental in giving power back to the people, and that "the enemy truly fears it."[2] Writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] asserted that this fear was the reason [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] set up a private email server in the first place — to avoid the FOIA laws that had exposed many of her husband's crimes during his presidency — and that President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] did the same, later getting caught lying about it.[2] Lange added that Obama sought to hide the arrangement by classifying material under state secrets or top secret designations.[2]
