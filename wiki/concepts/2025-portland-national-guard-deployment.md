# 2025 Portland National Guard Deployment

The 2025 Portland National Guard deployment was an attempt by the administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to place federalized [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops in Portland, Oregon, in response to prolonged unrest around federal immigration facilities, and the litigation that followed.[1]

## Background

Portland had, by early October 2025, been the site of anti-[[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agitation for more than 100 consecutive days, with the city described in press accounts as "war ravaged" and under siege by demonstrators targeting federal immigration operations.[1]

## Federal court block and the California workaround

A federal judge in Oregon temporarily halted the Trump administration from sending 200 Oregon National Guard troops to Portland, ruling against the federalization of the Oregon Guard.[1] On Sunday, October 5, 2025, President Trump responded by deploying 300 [[california-army-national-guard|California National Guard]] troops to Portland instead — personnel already under federal control from an earlier federalization — prompting the New York Post headline that Trump had defied the federal court.[1]

California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] announced the move on X, writing that "after a federal court blocked his attempt to federalize the Oregon National Guard, Donald Trump is deploying 300 California National Guard personnel into Oregon," and calling the order "un-American."[1] Newsom vowed to sue, saying: "They are on their way there now. We are taking this fight back to court. The public cannot stay silent in the face of such reckless and authoritarian conduct by the President of the United States."[1]

## Relationship to the California litigation

Badlands commentary framed the Oregon episode as a rerun of the June 2025 Los Angeles immigration riots dispute.[1] Federal Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] in San Francisco had ruled that Trump's federalization of roughly 4,000 California Guard troops for Los Angeles violated federal law — 10 U.S.C. § 12406, which authorizes such deployments only for invasion, rebellion, or inability to enforce the laws — as well as the Tenth Amendment's protection of state sovereignty, and ordered control returned to Newsom.[1]

Immediately after Breyer's June 12 order, the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|Ninth Circuit]] issued a temporary stay allowing Trump to retain control pending review.[1] On June 19 a unanimous three-judge panel held that Trump likely had statutory authority to federalize the Guard, rejected the argument that the decision was unreviewable, and found that the Los Angeles protests — including property damage and interference with federal agents — met the "danger of rebellion" threshold.[1] That opinion kept the troops under federal control during the litigation, with the case, *Newsom v. Trump*, remaining before the Ninth Circuit and open to en banc review or escalation to the Supreme Court.[1]

On September 2, Breyer issued a further ruling enjoining the administration from using the remaining federalized California Guard members — or any military force — for domestic law enforcement inside California, citing the [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus Act]].[1] Badlands characterized this as a partial victory for Newsom that blocked active enforcement in California without fully reversing federalization.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the combined effect of the rulings was that Trump held federalized California troops he was barred from deploying against California unrest while simultaneously being barred from federalizing Oregon's Guard, so sending the California soldiers to Oregon was, in her framing, "a perfect solution to a national security issue" that was also "efficient and avoids waste" — "accelerating with [[doge|DOGE]]."[1]
