# CBP One

CBP One is the U.S. Customs and Border Protection mobile application system that migrants used to schedule asylum appointments at the southern border.[1]

## Shutdown and aftermath

Asylum appointment access through the CBP One system was shut down on inauguration day in January 2025, leaving thousands of migrants stranded on the Mexican side of the border.[1] Mexican shelters and civil society organizations absorbed stranded and deported migrants, with limited official support for non-Mexican deportees and a reduced presence from some international aid groups.[1]

## Context: shrinking immigrant population

The shutdown formed part of a broader enforcement posture under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] credited with shrinking the U.S. immigrant population.[1] Researchers and on-the-ground reporting attributed the drop largely to expanded removals and fear-driven voluntary departures, while the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] offered $1,000 and a paid flight to undocumented people who registered to leave through its voluntary-departure program.[1] The immigrant share of the U.S. workforce fell to 19% in June 2025, about one percentage point lower than in January, equal to roughly 750,000 fewer immigrant workers.[1] The [[pew-research-center|Pew Research Center]] estimated the immigrant population fell by about 1.4 million from January to June 2025.[1]
