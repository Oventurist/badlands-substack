# CHNV Parole Program

The CHNV parole program was a Biden administration immigration initiative that allowed nationals of Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua, and Venezuela to fly directly into the United States under a sponsorship process, receiving temporary legal status, work permits, and deportation protections under the immigration authority known as parole.[1] The program was designed to reduce illegal crossings at the U.S.–Mexico border by creating legal migration avenues, and a total of 532,000 migrants entered the United States under it.[1]

## Termination under the Trump administration

The program was paused soon after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office in January 2025.[1] In March 2025 the administration announced it would revoke the legal status of more than half a million Latin American and Haitian migrants admitted under the process, urging them to self-deport or face arrest and removal by deportation agents.[1] According to a federal notice, the termination of their work permits and deportation protections would take effect in late April 2025, thirty days after March 25.[1] It was not clear at the time how many of the affected migrants had secured an alternative status permitting them to remain in the country lawfully.[1]

## August 2024 pause over sponsor fraud

The Biden administration paused the migrant sponsorship policy in early August 2024 because of concerns about fraud among the American sponsors underwriting arrivals.[2] The policy allowed up to 30,000 migrants a month from Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua and Venezuela to fly into the United States legally if a U.S.-based sponsor agreed to support them financially; it began in late 2022 and was expanded in early 2023 with the stated aim of dissuading migrants from those countries from travelling to the southern border.[2] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] said it had stopped issuing travel documents while it reviewed supporter applications, stating that "out of an abundance of caution, DHS has temporarily paused the issuance of advanced travel authorizations for new beneficiaries" and would restart processing "as quickly as possible, with appropriate safeguards."[2] An internal report cited in subsequent reporting found that application forms contained Social Security numbers, addresses and phone numbers reused hundreds of times in some cases.[2]

Badlands Media commentary connected the pause to President Joe Biden's February 2023 State of the Union claim that unlawful migration from Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua and Venezuela had fallen 97% since the launch of the new border plan, arguing that the decline was an artefact of redefining metrics — making lawful what had been unlawful — in the same manner the outlet alleges for jobs and inflation figures.[2] The commentary stressed that the program was a Biden-Harris initiative never passed or approved by Congress, and that Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], as border czar focused on the "root causes" of migration, therefore owned the resulting fraud.[2]
