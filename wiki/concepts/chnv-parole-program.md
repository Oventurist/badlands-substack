# CHNV Parole Program

The CHNV parole program was a Biden administration immigration initiative that allowed nationals of Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua, and Venezuela to fly directly into the United States under a sponsorship process, receiving temporary legal status, work permits, and deportation protections under the immigration authority known as parole.[1] The program was designed to reduce illegal crossings at the U.S.–Mexico border by creating legal migration avenues, and a total of 532,000 migrants entered the United States under it.[1]

## Termination under the Trump administration

The program was paused soon after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office in January 2025.[1] In March 2025 the administration announced it would revoke the legal status of more than half a million Latin American and Haitian migrants admitted under the process, urging them to self-deport or face arrest and removal by deportation agents.[1] According to a federal notice, the termination of their work permits and deportation protections would take effect in late April 2025, thirty days after March 25.[1] It was not clear at the time how many of the affected migrants had secured an alternative status permitting them to remain in the country lawfully.[1]
