# Khazaria

Khazaria was a medieval empire on the Eurasian steppe, described in Badlands commentary as the only Jewish nation in history aside from Israel, and at its peak the largest empire in Europe during its existence from the late 7th century to the late 10th century AD.[1]

## Fall and Rus connection

Khazaria was defeated in 965 AD by the Russian leader Sviatoslav I, who was Grand Prince of Kiev.[1] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry connected this lineage to the history lesson [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] gave [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]: Sviatoslav's grandfather, Rurik the Varangian, was invited from Scandinavia to Novgorod to become king of the Rus, and Sviatoslav's grandson, Vladimir the Great, converted to Christianity in 988 at the site of modern-day Sevastopol in Crimea, triggering the mass conversion known as the Baptism of Russia.[1] This history, the commentary argued, is why Crimea and Kiev remain central to both Russian identity and Orthodox Christianity — and why Ukraine's bombing of Sevastopol during a Russian national holiday carried symbolic weight.[1]

## Historical absence

The commentary observed that despite Khazaria's size, almost no direct record of it survives — no written texts, artwork, clothing, or architecture — with knowledge limited to accounts by foreign cultures that encountered it, raising the question of whether the civilization was deliberately removed from the written record.[1]

## Speculative questions

The Badlands take raised a series of open questions: what Putin meant by calling [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] and his associates "ethnic Jews," whether he referred to Ashkenazi Jews or to "Khazars," whether the Khazarians were related to the Bolsheviks who emerged from the [[pale-of-settlement|Pale of Settlement]], and whether they were the same people Putin accused of having previously destroyed Russia.[1] It further asked whether this was what Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] referenced when he told Tucker Carlson in August 2023 that [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]]'s grudge against Russia stemmed from an ancient rivalry unrelated to America.[1]

## Matthew Ehret's revisionist account

In Part 7 of his "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler" series, [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argued that readers of alternative media either have never heard of the Jewish Khazar Kingdom that dominated central Europe, southern Russia and the Caucasus in the 7th–10th centuries, or believe it to have been the source of everything evil since, while many mainstream scholars simply deny that the kingdom existed at all.[2] Ehret asserted the opposite: that bountiful evidence establishes the kingdom's existence and that it was a beautiful phenomenon uniting all three major Abrahamic faiths under one ecumenical alliance for well over a century, as well as a keystone of the reborn [[silk-road|Silk Road]] linking Asia with Europe through the Confucian [[tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]].[2] Much of his report drew on the work of historian [[pierre-beaudry|Pierre Beaudry]] in the online book *The Charlemagne Ecumenical Principle*.[2]

## Turkic origins and economy

Khazaria was first established in the mid-7th century by the western Turkish Khaganate, a remnant of the [[gokturk-empire|GokTurk Empire]] that had become independent of its eastern parent after the latter's defeat by the Taizong Emperor of the Tang in 643 CE.[2] The newly independent Turks established a highly developed centralized government whose economy rested primarily on fisheries and agriculture, and Confucianism spread rapidly across the Turkic world.[2] Khazaria became a keystone of the Silk Road, with the Steppe Silk Road running east-west overland from Uygur territory to western Crimea and import-export lines along the Dnieper, Don and Volga rivers feeding into the Caspian and Black Seas.[2] It also held the vital north-south Volga route from Scandinavia through central Russia to Islamic Iran and Azerbaijan, a corridor made indispensable by Venetian-steered wars that had closed Mediterranean trade.[2]

## Ecumenical institutions

[[king-bulan|King Bulan]] converted the kingdom to Judaism around 750 CE, though the majority of the population remained a mix of Christians, Muslims and pagans while the king and his court were Jewish.[2] Al-Masudi reported in *Meadows of Gold* that the Khazars had a military alliance with the [[abbasid-dynasty|Abbasid Dynasty]], which supplied 10,000 Muslim soldiers on the condition that they would fight for Islam should any future Jewish leader declare war on it.[2] The 10th-century Persian historian Abu al-Istakhri described a Khazarian Supreme Court of Justice composed of two Christians, two Muslims, two Jews and one pagan, quoting: "The king has 7 judges [hukkan] from the Jews, Christians, Muslims and Idolators. When the people have a lawsuit, it is they who judge it. The parties do not approach the king himself but only these judges."[2]

## Collapse and erasure

Ehret writes that whatever caused the weakening and ultimate collapse of Khazaria under Kiev Rus invasions in 969 is not clear, but that in the late 10th century Jews were cut off from Khazaria as Genoa and the [[venetian-empire|Venetian Empire]] seized all east-west trade routes.[2] Physical evidence of the kingdom was nearly all destroyed or suppressed, leaving room for what he calls speculative gossip from Arthur Koestler and David Icke, though dozens of Christian and Muslim scholars of the 8th–12th centuries wrote of it and fragments from the [[cairo-geniza|Cairo Geniza]] are finally being made public.[2]
