# Khazaria

Khazaria was a medieval empire on the Eurasian steppe, described in Badlands commentary as the only Jewish nation in history aside from Israel, and at its peak the largest empire in Europe during its existence from the late 7th century to the late 10th century AD.[1]

## Fall and Rus connection

Khazaria was defeated in 965 AD by the Russian leader Sviatoslav I, who was Grand Prince of Kiev.[1] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry connected this lineage to the history lesson [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] gave [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]: Sviatoslav's grandfather, Rurik the Varangian, was invited from Scandinavia to Novgorod to become king of the Rus, and Sviatoslav's grandson, Vladimir the Great, converted to Christianity in 988 at the site of modern-day Sevastopol in Crimea, triggering the mass conversion known as the Baptism of Russia.[1] This history, the commentary argued, is why Crimea and Kiev remain central to both Russian identity and Orthodox Christianity — and why Ukraine's bombing of Sevastopol during a Russian national holiday carried symbolic weight.[1]

## Historical absence

The commentary observed that despite Khazaria's size, almost no direct record of it survives — no written texts, artwork, clothing, or architecture — with knowledge limited to accounts by foreign cultures that encountered it, raising the question of whether the civilization was deliberately removed from the written record.[1]

## Speculative questions

The Badlands take raised a series of open questions: what Putin meant by calling [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] and his associates "ethnic Jews," whether he referred to Ashkenazi Jews or to "Khazars," whether the Khazarians were related to the Bolsheviks who emerged from the [[pale-of-settlement|Pale of Settlement]], and whether they were the same people Putin accused of having previously destroyed Russia.[1] It further asked whether this was what Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] referenced when he told Tucker Carlson in August 2023 that [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]]'s grudge against Russia stemmed from an ancient rivalry unrelated to America.[1]
