# Roe v. Wade

Roe v. Wade was the United States Supreme Court decision that established a federal constitutional framework for abortion, later overturned so that abortion policy was returned to the states.[1]

## As a federalism precedent in Badlands commentary

In the Badlands corpus, the reversal of Roe functions primarily as a reference point for arguments about federalism rather than about abortion itself.[1] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in September 2023 about New Mexico Governor [[michelle-lujan-grisham|Michelle Lujan Grisham]]'s suspension of public firearms carry, invoked the establishment reaction to Roe being returned to the states as evidence of a consistent pattern: according to what she called the regime's puppets, the people do not have the right to decide questions locally and federal legislation is required for everything.[1]

That framing was used to explain why figures such as [[ted-lieu|Ted Lieu]] and [[david-hogg|David Hogg]] insisted that states cannot strike down the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] — not from attachment to states' rights, which Ashe argued they reject, but because they favor changes made at the federal level.[1]
