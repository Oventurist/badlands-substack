# Gold-Backed BRICS Reserve Currency

The gold-backed BRICS reserve currency is a proposed transaction unit, backed by gold, that the [[brics|BRICS]] bloc was reported in July 2023 to be preparing to introduce as an alternative to the U.S. dollar in international settlement.[1]

## Reporting and announcement timeline

In early July 2023 Kitco reported that Russia had confirmed the BRICS states would create a gold-backed currency, with the official announcement expected during the BRICS summit in [[south-africa|South Africa]] in August 2023.[1] Russian state outlet RT ran the story over that weekend under headlines such as "New Money, New World" and "Gold Standard Will Be Of Great Benefit To Strengthening New Singly Currency."[1] Coverage aggregated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] framed the development as the return of the [[gold-standard|gold standard]], with more than forty-one countries described as potentially moving back toward gold-based money.[1]

## Economic assessments

[[thorsten-polleit|Thorsten Polleit]], chief economist at Degussa, said the proposal could represent a major challenge to the dollar's hegemony, but only if the unit were genuinely convertible into gold on demand.[1] He warned that a true gold unit of account could sharply devalue fiat currencies — including those of Brazil, [[russia|Russia]], India, [[china|China]] and South Africa — and act as "a shock to the global fiat money system."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] welcomed the move as BRICS "doing what everyone hoped they'd do—work toward upending the globalist banking system," arguing that if the currency were pegged to a gold price insulated from manipulation it could be a significant step in taking the financial system out of the hands of what he called parasitic bankers.[1]

He cautioned, however, that gold is "not the silver bullet some folks think," pointing to the [[long-depression|Long Depression]] that began in 1873 and lasted twenty years, which he attributed to the abolition of silver money and the resulting de facto gold standard.[1] Any top-down shift in what counts as money, in his account, devalues other currencies and reshuffles valuations across the whole economy, favoring insiders unless everyone is given equal instruction and market access.[1] The chief benefit he identified is that a gold peg makes open-ended money printing untenable, since fiat currencies produced that way could not be sold to BRICS nations.[1]

Deschamps also argued that money functions as a unit of account and must be stable relative to all other valued goods — likening currency to a ruler whose measuring power must be consistent for every user.[1] He held that price instability follows when market participants over- or undervalue transactions, producing inflation, deflation and stagflation, and described the modern stock market, which emerged in the Netherlands shortly after the first modern [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve banking]] system, as partly designed to induce price instability by instilling avarice in the public.[1] He characterized hedge funds as businesses whose chief product is price manipulation and opportunism, and asserted that globalist financiers used "whale" economic warfare to buy up assets and manipulate systems from 1830 to 1913 in the run-up to the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], continuing through 1971 to persuade the public that pure [[fiat-currency|fiat]] money was superior to gold backing.[1]

## Early reporting and expansion context (2022)

Coverage in December 2022 traced the initiative to the aftermath of the 2009 Yekaterinburg summit, at which the original BRIC nations announced the need for a new global reserve currency that would be "diverse, stable and predictable" — a statement that, without naming the dollar, precipitated a fall in its value against other major currencies.[2] [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] announced in June 2022 that [[brics|BRICS]] was already setting up such a currency.[2]

Badlands contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] wrote that numerous news reports pointed to BRICS moving toward a gold-backed global reserve currency that "would totally shake up the world's economies and destroy the American dollar," placing it within a broader worldwide return to asset-based money and the death of [[fiat-currency|fiat currency]].[2] She argued that because one cannot break a piece off a gold bar to buy groceries, any metal-backed system requires a digital representation held in [[digital-wallets|digital wallets]], distinct from a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]], and speculated that a beta version had already been deployed to a small group worldwide.[2]
