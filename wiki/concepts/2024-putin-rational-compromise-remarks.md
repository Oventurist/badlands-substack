# 2024 Putin Rational Compromise Remarks

The **2024 Putin rational compromise remarks** were statements by Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], made in a late-night interview on the sidelines of the [[2024-brics-kazan-summit|BRICS summit in Kazan]] and released by Russian media on Friday, October 25, 2024, indicating Moscow's willingness to negotiate an end to the Ukraine conflict.[1]

## The statements

Asked how his government envisioned the end of hostilities with Ukraine, Putin said that "any outcome should be favorable to Russia, and I say that directly," while adding that Moscow did not rule out compromises on its part so long as they were "rational."[1] He argued that Ukraine's unwillingness to negotiate rendered moot any discussion of what Moscow might offer.[1]

Putin said Moscow had recently received a proposal from Ankara to discuss a possible deal, which an aide to Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] said originated from Kiev.[1] "We agreed to it, and the next day [Ukrainian leader Vladimir Zelensky] publicly announced that he will not negotiate anything," Putin said, calling this "irrational behavior, which is hard to predict," and concluding that "no plans can be based on this foundation."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "isn't the only Peacemaker on the game board," and that some are harder to spot.[1] He noted that in the same week [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had softened his hard line, saying "we do not attack their energy infrastructures; they don't attack ours," and suggesting this could lead to the end of the war's hot phase.[1] Putin's compromise language followed.[1]

While such statements might appear to be ordinary negotiating common sense, Burning Bright argued that timing is everything: [[nato|NATO]] and what he called the Western Globalist Hegemon desire an endless "quagmire" of death in the Donbas, while Trump, Putin and Zelensky all want the conflict ended.[1] On this reading, Trump's claim that "Russia never would have gone into Ukraine" had he been president is correct, because knowledge of the Nazi occupation of the Donbas implies Russia had to intervene and therefore Trump had to remain on the sidelines — a position Burning Bright said Trump would not keep.[1]
