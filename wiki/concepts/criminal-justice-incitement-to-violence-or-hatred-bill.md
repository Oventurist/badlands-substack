# Criminal Justice (Incitement to Violence or Hatred) Bill

The Irish hate-speech bill advanced in late 2023 is legislation criminalizing the preparation or possession of material likely to incite violence or hatred against persons on account of protected characteristics.[1] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] described it as one of the most chilling crackdowns on speech to date.[1]

## Provisions

The bill criminalizes any "preparing or possessing material likely to incite violence or hatred against persons on account of their protected characteristics."[1] Those characteristics include national or ethnic origin, as well as "transgender and a gender other than those of male and female."[1]

## Political context

Turley had previously discussed the growing anti-free-speech movement in [[ireland|Ireland]], and, in his book *The Indispensable Right: Free Speech in the Age of Rage*, argued that such crackdowns tend to arrive during periods of public panic or anger, which anti-free-speech advocates use as opportunities to persuade the public to surrender the right to government censors or prosecutors.[1] He assessed that Ireland's push had arrived "right on schedule."[1]

The proximate excuse for the rollback, in his account, was a riot in Dublin that led to the arrest of 34 people and extensive property damage during anti-immigration protests — protests that had challenged government policies on handling undocumented migrants.[1] See [[2023-dublin-riots|2023 Dublin riots]].[1]
