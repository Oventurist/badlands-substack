# Interpretatio graeca

Interpretatio graeca, or "Greek translation," is a discourse used to interpret or attempt to understand the mythology and religion of other cultures by identifying foreign deities with Greek ones.[1] It is described in [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s February 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] survey of [[hermeticism|Hermeticism]].[1]

Greeks in the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Egypt used interpretatio graeca to recognise the equivalence of the god Hermes and the Egyptian god [[thoth|Thoth]], and the two were consequently worshiped as one — the syncretism that produced [[hermes-trismegistus|Hermes Trismegistus]].[1] DeLarme presents the practice as an expression of the universality of ancient Greek theological vision: unlike their Semitic counterparts, the Greeks claimed no uniqueness for their deities but freely acknowledged that the Olympians often had exact analogs among the gods of other nations, particularly Egypt, whose pantheon they revered as prototypes of their own.[1] The Romans, aware their gods were repurposed Greek deities, followed the same example as their empire expanded across the Mediterranean, including Egypt.[1] The renaming of the Temple of Thoth at Khemenu as Hermopolis in the Hellenistic period is given as a concrete example.[1]
