# Connecticut Digital Asset Ban

The Connecticut digital asset ban is a 2025 state law, signed by Governor [[ned-lamont|Ned Lamont]], that bars any Connecticut state government entity from acquiring, holding, or investing in digital assets, including Bitcoin.[1]

## Provisions and rationale

The legislation, which passed with unanimous approval, prohibits state entities from participating in digital asset transactions of any kind.[1] Its stated purpose is to address the risks associated with the volatile nature of digital assets and to safeguard the stability of the state's financial reserves.[1] Supporters framed the measure as a response to mounting regulatory and security challenges presented by cryptocurrencies, prioritizing the safety of public funds over potential gains from speculative assets.[1]

## Contrast with other states

The ban represents a notable departure from states that have integrated cryptocurrencies into their financial strategies.[1] Texas approved a $10 million Bitcoin purchase for its state reserve around the same period, reflecting a more proactive posture toward digital currencies, whereas Connecticut emphasized regulatory clarity, financial prudence, and risk management.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentators treated the law as a decisive rejection of the state-level Bitcoin reserve trend, noting that Connecticut had officially banned itself from creating a Bitcoin reserve or investing in any digital assets.[1]
