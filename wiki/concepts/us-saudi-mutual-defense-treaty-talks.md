# US-Saudi Mutual Defense Treaty Talks

The US–Saudi mutual defense treaty talks were negotiations, reported in September 2023, in which the United States discussed the terms of a mutual defense treaty with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] modeled on American military pacts with Japan and South Korea.[1]

## Terms under discussion

Under such an agreement, the United States and Saudi Arabia would generally pledge to provide military support if the other were attacked in the region or on Saudi territory.[1] The East Asian treaties used as models are considered among the strongest U.S. commitments outside its European pacts, and the plan to emulate them had not previously been reported.[1]

## Diplomatic context

The talks sat at the center of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s high-stakes diplomacy aimed at persuading the kingdom to normalize relations with Israel.[1] Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], Saudi Arabia's de facto ruler, regarded a mutual defense agreement as the most important element of his talks with the Biden administration about Israel.[1] Saudi officials argued that a strong defense agreement would help deter potential assaults by Iran or its armed partners, even as the two regional rivals re-established diplomatic ties.[1]

Prince Mohammed also asked the administration to help Saudi Arabia develop a civilian nuclear program, which some U.S. officials feared could serve as cover for a nuclear weapons program to counter Iran.[1] Any treaty resembling the East Asian pacts was expected to draw strong objections in [[congress|Congress]], where some senior lawmakers, including top Democrats, view the Saudi government and Prince Mohammed as unreliable partners with little regard for U.S. interests or human rights.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the negotiations as a piece of [[devolution|Devolution]] evidence, arguing that although the Biden administration was ostensibly in control of the game board, its moves — new defense pacts in the Indo-Pacific and now with Saudi Arabia — were "oddly reminiscent" of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s foreign policy.[1] He noted that the Saudis had publicly snubbed Biden on multiple occasions while committing to the expansion of the [[brics|BRICS]] alliance, and concluded that Biden "may be the 'president,' but he is not the leader of the western world, and he's not being treated as such."[1]
