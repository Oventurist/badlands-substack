# 2024 European Farmer Protests

The 2024 European farmer protests were a wave of demonstrations by agricultural producers across the [[european-union|European Union]] against the bloc's climate and trade policies, culminating in Brussels on Monday, February 26, 2024, when farmers encircled the EU's headquarters.[1]

## Brussels demonstration

Hundreds of Belgian farmers on tractors clogged roadways around the European Council building in downtown Brussels, in a scene ZeroHedge described as smelling of "manure, melting tires and malodorous teargas" and likened to "a warzone."[1] The protest was timed to coincide with a meeting of the bloc's agriculture ministers.[1]

Farmers cited bureaucratic hurdles, trade deals, climate-related rules, and EU efforts to help Ukraine sell cheap grain into European markets.[1] Guillaume Van Binst, secretary general of the Federation of Young Farmers, said there was "a clear problem with the reduction of the import tariffs for Ukraine and massive imports of grain and poultry which depresses the prices," adding that the measures proposed by the European Commission were "very weak" and amounted to "passing the hot potato to member states."[1] The Brussels action followed a series of demonstrations in France, Italy and Spain, driven also by imploding incomes and elevated inflation among blue-collar workers.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] responded to Belgian agriculture minister David Clarinval's statement that "aggression has never been a source of solutions" by calling it "hilariously wrong."[1] He argued that the "global, collective and aggressive uprising against a globalist consortium running a massive power grab operation using 'green propaganda'" was entirely correct, and that anything less than an aggressive stance against such operations would likely fail.[1] Distinguishing aggression from violence — citing the definition "done in a determined and forceful way" — he urged readers to be "aggressively (peacefully) against the [[new-world-order|NWO]]," and praised the farmers and truckers rising up globally.[1]

## Commission concessions

In February 2024 the [[european-commission|European Commission]] "bent the knee" to the protesting farmers by dropping key passages from its [[eu-2040-climate-proposal|2040 climate proposal]].[2] While the revised plan still said "all sectors" must contribute, the previously mandated 30 percent cut to agricultural production by 2040 was removed, as were a mandate for citizen lifestyle changes such as eating less meat and a push to end fossil fuel subsidies.[2]

Commissioner [[wopke-hoekstra|Wopke Hoekstra]], unveiling the revision, said a "balanced approach" was needed because citizens who want climate protection are "also worried about what that implies for their livelihood."[2] The EU separately scrapped plans to halve pesticide use across the bloc by 2030 under the Green Deal.[2] The changes were spearheaded by the [[european-peoples-party|European People's Party]], the political family of [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] and the largest group in the EU Parliament.[2]

## France concedes; Ireland joins

On Thursday, February 1, 2024, two of France's main farming unions agreed to suspend protests and lift road blockades across the country after the government announced measures they deemed "tangible progress" in the revolt against EU climate-driven initiatives.[3] The concessions included better control of imports and additional aid to farmers.[3] Prime Minister [[gabriel-attal|Gabriel Attal]] announced the measures, framing them around how Europe could continue to produce more but better, tackle climate change, and avoid unfair competition from foreign countries.[3]

[[fnsea|FNSEA]], France's main farmers union, said it was time to lift the blockades and "go home," a message echoed by Arnaud Gaillot of the Young Farmers' union, though both warned other forms of protest would continue and that they would return if the government failed to deliver.[3] Protests had also been held in Belgium, Portugal, Greece and Germany, with tensions coming to a head in Brussels when farmers threw eggs and stones at the European Parliament building while demanding an end to taxes and rising costs imposed to finance the green agenda.[3] Farmers in Ireland began solidarity protests that same Thursday evening.[3]
