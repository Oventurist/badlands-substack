# Anti-Asian Violence Media Narrative

The "anti-Asian violence" media narrative refers to the concentrated wave of American news coverage of assaults on Asian Americans that Badlands Media commentary treats as an example of narrative manipulation through selective presentation rather than fabrication.[1]

## The argument

In the November 2023 essay "Believe Half of What You See," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] recalled that the narrative of violence against Asians appeared "out of nowhere" a couple of years earlier, with media constantly running such stories, and that in his estimate roughly 99% of the accompanying video clips showed a Black person assaulting an Asian person.[1] He noted that the narrative subsequently disappeared from coverage and asked what had changed.[1]

Carlson's contention was that the underlying incidence of violence had not vastly increased; rather, the media simply showed more of it, selecting images to manufacture division and hatred between Asian and Black Americans.[1] He was explicit that he was not claiming the depicted violence had not occurred, only that the footage was selectively chosen.[1] He placed the episode within a broader pattern in which mug shots and crime videos featuring Black suspects are routinely aired to make Americans view Black people as criminals and for Black people to internalise that image, describing the Black community as an abused stepchild of the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1]

## Illustrative analogy

To explain the mechanism, Carlson offered the example of car accidents: by airing one fatal crash after another on the evening news, a broadcaster can lead audiences to conclude that road deaths are rising even if the statistics show a decline, and without ever asserting the claim directly.[1] This mechanism fits the third category in his taxonomy of media output — total lie, half-truth, or manipulation of the truth — in which real events are presented, or information withheld, in order to advance a narrative.[1]
