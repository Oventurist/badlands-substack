# 2025 Steel and Aluminum Tariffs

In February 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] ordered a 25% tariff on all imports of steel and aluminum into the United States from all countries, restoring a 25% tariff on steel imports and elevating the aluminum tariff to 25%.[1] The White House presented the measure as part of a run of weekly wins for the American people.[1]

## Reciprocal trade framework

Days after the metals duties, Trump signed a measure on Thursday that could lead to the implementation of reciprocal tariffs on U.S. trading partners as soon as April, fulfilling a frequent campaign promise while also raising revenue as Republicans readied a tax and spending bill.[1] He unveiled the plan for "fair and reciprocal trade," making clear that the United States would no longer tolerate being ripped off.[1]

For the time being, Trump had reneged on planned 25% across-the-board tariffs on U.S. neighbours Canada and Mexico, at least until early March.[1] Duties on China had gone into effect the prior week and China retaliated, though Trump was planning to speak with Chinese President Xi Jinping, leaving hope of a compromise.[1] The escalation affected top trading partners while bolstering industries in U.S. states key to Trump's election.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump's most masterful deployments are often the simplest, calling his reciprocal trade stance "quite literally unassailable even by the most skilled narrative setters in the commentariat," because in order to attack the tariffs critics must attack the globalist system that provoked them.[1]
