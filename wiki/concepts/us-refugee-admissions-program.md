# United States Refugee Admissions Program

The U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP) is the federal framework through which refugees are admitted to the United States and resettled with the assistance of funded nonprofit agencies.[1]

## Suspension under the second Trump administration

[[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] suspended USRAP and halted all funding for agencies that support refugees.[1] Although the executive order was blocked by a federal judge in February 2025, local resettlement programs still faced a lack of federal funds in practice, and the case was expected to face an appeal from the Trump administration.[1]

## Effect on local resettlement agencies

The funding halt forced closures at the local level. The Central and Northern Missouri chapter of [[catholic-charities|Catholic Charities]] announced it would end its refugee resettlement program on March 31, 2025, after 14 years, with executive director Litz Main telling supporters the nonprofit had to end the program due to the lack of federal funding.[1] There was no indication when the program would resume.[1]

## Judicial block of the 2025 suspension

On Tuesday, February 25, 2025, U.S. District Judge [[jamal-whitehead|Jamal Whitehead]] in Seattle blocked President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order pausing the refugee admissions system, ruling that while the president may determine who is allowed to enter the country, he cannot invalidate the law enacted by Congress that established the program.[2] Whitehead said the president's actions amounted to an "effective nullification of congressional will" and indicated a written opinion would follow.[2] The order at issue, titled "Realigning the United States Refugee Admission Program," imposed a 90-day suspension of USRAP, asserting that the record influx of migrants under the Biden administration had strained federal resources such that the United States could no longer offer financial assistance and resources without harming American communities.[2]
