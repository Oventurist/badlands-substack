# NSSM-200

NSSM-200 is the National Security Study Memorandum authored under [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] that, in the Badlands account, redirected United States foreign policy away from the genuine industrial development of so-called "third-world nations" toward priorities driven by an obsession with depopulation and resource control.[1]

## Context

The shift is dated to the early 1970s and tied to the usurpation of US military policy-making by figures affiliated with David Rockefeller's [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[1] Plans for nuclear power, hydroelectric dams and general industrial growth of the sort promoted by [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and his brother were scrapped in favor of US and World Bank loans permitted only for "appropriate technologies" — defined as zero-technological-growth forms such as windmills, solar panels and biodiesel — and laced with usurious conditionalities ensuring foreign corporate control of extractive resources and mines in target nations.[1]

Within this structure, private corporate and banking power rigged through the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg Group]] and its junior counterpart the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] is described as becoming more powerful than nation states, with US military and economic power serving to enforce the will of an unelected feudalist class.[1]

## Content and legacy per Ehret (2025)

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes NSSM-200 — formally *Implications of Worldwide Population Growth for U.S. Security and Overseas Interests*, published in 1974 under [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] — as the declassified report that went far to transform US foreign policy from a pro-development philosophy into a new paradigm of population control.[2]

The report warned that "if future numbers are to be kept within reasonable bounds, it is urgent that measures to reduce fertility be started and made effective in the 1970s and 1980s," directed that assistance to other countries consider population growth, called food and agricultural aid "vital for any population sensitive development strategy," instructed that allocation of scarce resources take account of a country's population-control steps, and noted "an alternative view that mandatory programs may be needed."[2]

Thirteen nations were targeted by the memorandum, with China added as a special emphasis: Beijing was permitted to acquire western technology only on condition that it adopt [[one-child-policy|one-child policy]] programmes demanded by Rockefeller–World Bank interests.[2] Ehret invokes the document as the first of five "facts" showing that depopulation remains a driving factor of unipolar policy — an agenda now masked by language such as "Fourth Industrial Revolution," "decarbonized economies" and "Great Resets," and aimed at sovereign nation states and the world's "overpopulated zones," which have long included both China and the USA.[2]
