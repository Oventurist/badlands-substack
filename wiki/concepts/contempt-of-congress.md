# Contempt of Congress

Contempt of Congress is the criminal offense of defying a congressional subpoena for testimony or documents. Its most prominent recent application was the prosecution of [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]], who was convicted in 2022 for defying a subpoena from the House [[january-6-committee|Jan. 6 committee]] and later served a four-month federal prison sentence.[1]

## The Bannon case and its unwinding

On a Monday in April 2026, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] vacated the lower-court ruling that had left Bannon's contempt conviction in place, clearing the way for dismissal after the Trump [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] asked that the indictment be dropped.[1] The high court did not explain its order but returned the matter for further proceedings in light of the government's new request to end the case.[1]

The ruling did not declare Bannon innocent on the underlying facts; it removed the appellate decision upholding the conviction and opened the door for the lower court to dismiss, giving Bannon a major legal win in a prosecution tied to the congressional investigation of the Capitol attack.[1]

## Commentary on precedent and restitution

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Bannon had already served the time, calling the absence of restitution or even acknowledgment of harm one of the woeful features of the US justice system in need of reform.[1] She described the same dynamic as a factor in her decision to fire her attorneys and proceed pro se in the federal case brought against her by the [[naacp|NAACP]], after her lead attorney recounted a case in which a criminal conviction was overturned but the innocent prisoner received nothing beyond release.[1] She argued that political prosecution should never occur and that the entities carrying it out are resourced enough to do so repeatedly, and concluded that while throwing out the conviction benefits Bannon, it also benefits the regime by erasing a precedent.[1]

## Application to ActBlue (2026)

In June 2026, three House committee chairmen — [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], [[bryan-steil|Bryan Steil]], and [[james-comer|James Comer]] — warned [[actblue|ActBlue]] CEO [[regina-wallace-jones|Regina Wallace-Jones]] that the fundraising platform could face contempt of Congress for failure to comply with subpoenas issued on July 22, 2025.[2] The chairmen argued that ActBlue's withholding of records under attorney-client privilege, including at least 420 documents listed in a June 5, 2026 privilege log, was hindering Congress's ability to investigate possible foreign interference in political donations.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] pointed to the Bannon precedent as evidence that a contempt referral can be carried through even where related litigation is ongoing.[2]

## Hunter Biden contempt proceedings (2024)

In January 2024, the House Oversight and Judiciary Committees moved to hold [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] in contempt of Congress after he skipped a closed-door deposition the previous month, at which he would have faced questions about his business dealings in connection with the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into his father.[3] He made a surprise appearance at the January 10 contempt hearing, where Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] accused him of "ignoring a congressional subpoena to be deposed" and said he "should be arrested right here, right now."[3] He left the room when Rep. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] began to speak, having earlier stated he would testify only in a public setting.[3]

## Contempt proceedings against Hunter Biden, December 2023

After Hunter Biden declined on December 13, 2023 to appear for a subpoenaed closed-door deposition — insisting instead on testifying publicly — House Oversight Chair James Comer and House Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan announced they would begin contempt of Congress proceedings against him.[4] Their joint statement declared, "Hunter Biden today defied lawful subpoenas and we will now initiate contempt of Congress proceedings," adding that "we will not provide special treatment because his last name is Biden."[4] Badlands commentary contrasted his treatment with that of an ordinary citizen who defied a congressional deposition subpoena, framing the episode as another instance of two-tiered enforcement.[4]

## Contempt vote against Attorney General Merrick Garland (June 2024)

On June 12, 2024, the House of Representatives voted 216 to 207 to hold Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] in contempt of Congress for refusing to turn over audio recordings of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interviews with former special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], with Rep. Dave Joyce of Ohio the only Republican voting against.[5] The vote escalated a monthslong dispute between House Republicans and the executive branch that followed Biden's assertion of executive privilege over the files.[5] Under the applicable procedure, House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] certifies the contempt report to the United States attorney for the District of Columbia, whom the law directs to bring the matter before a grand jury, though the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] retains discretion over whether to prosecute — a structural tension when the official held in contempt heads that department.[5] Garland characterized the vote as turning "a serious congressional authority into a partisan weapon" and as disregarding the constitutional separation of powers.[5]
