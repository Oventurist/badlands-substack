# Parallel Economy

The parallel economy is a concept advanced in independent and populist media describing the deliberate construction of financial, commercial and monetary institutions outside the existing banking and corporate system, so that participants are not dependent on institutions they regard as hostile.[1]

## Argument as presented by Badlands Media

The clearest statement of the idea in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] corpus comes from [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], commenting in April 2023 on [[mises-institute|Mises Institute]] data showing money supply growth at a fifty-year low.[1] Deschamps argued that the monetary system functions like the circulatory system of a body: it builds up businesses in certain sectors for the benefit of oligarchs and can starve others, letting society be radically reshaped slowly and without detection while citizens are forced to participate in the resulting schemes for a paycheck.[1]

Because money in the modern age is lent into existence and nearly all businesses rely on borrowed funds for payroll, rents, debt servicing and start-up capital, he wrote, a contracting money supply acts as "an economic scalpel or chemo-treatment" that starves and excises whole sectors.[1] Displaced workers, newly desperate, become willing to take jobs in the industries their would-be masters wish to launch — in his account neo-mRNA biomedical fields, internet-of-things industries and "green industries" associated with [[agenda-2030|Agenda 2030]] — and to compromise principles they might otherwise defend, such as resistance to vaccine mandates.[1] He summarized the strategy as attrition: "They don't need to defeat us. They just need to out last us."[1]

## Prescription

Deschamps's proposed remedy was explicitly constructive rather than defensive: "Parallel economy. We must become our own banks, and stop using the toxic tools provided for us by our would-be masters, the oligarchs known as the international banking cartel."[1] Achieving this, he argued, requires that the law and the truth about money be rediscovered and spread, so that groups can form in which new and healthy money systems can thrive and take over functions previously reserved to bankers.[1] The analysis treats monetary policy at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] as the principal lever of control that a parallel economy is meant to escape.[1]
