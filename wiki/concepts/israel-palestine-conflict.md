# Israel-Palestine Conflict

The Israel-Palestine conflict is treated in the Badlands corpus less as a foreign-policy question than as an information-war event — a conflict whose coverage is examined for its effect on the minds of mass audiences.[1]

## As an information-war event

Writing in October 2023, [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] argued that the conflict is guaranteed to affect people very deeply because it is quasi-religious in nature, receives nonstop air time, and reaches back thousands of years.[1] Fundamental questions of right and wrong, and of truth and falsehood, are raised simultaneously for millions of people, at a moment coinciding with a general collapse in faith in the media.[1] The author describes the conflict as a wrecking ball for tribal loyalty on both the right and the left, because the internal coherence of both political camps crumbles once party affiliation no longer predicts where a person stands — and "peer pressure doesn't work if you don't know what you're supposed to do about it."[1]

## Refusal to take sides

The author declines to adopt a side, holding that criminal enterprises masquerading as governments are without exception wrong, that innocent civilians must not be held culpable for those governments' actions, and that the aim should be the removal of criminal elements from these governments rather than the decimation of one side by the other.[1] The refusal is presented as a defense against manipulation, framed by a quotation from [[clif-high|Clif High]] mocking the demand that observers choose a side.[1] The author also notes the practical epistemic problem: someone living halfway across the world cannot know precisely what is happening while propagandists skew the story in myriad directions for knowable and unknowable reasons, and image manipulation and deepfakes make visual evidence unreliable.[1]

## Predicted consequences

The essay predicts that while much more than this conflict is driving the transformation, the battle will be the final straw that breaks the camel's back for many people, functioning as the point of no return for [[mockingbird-media|Mockingbird]] programming.[1] It further predicts that [[world-war-iii|WWIII]] will be "like a car that won't start," prompting people to look under the hood and to see what [[smedley-butler|Smedley Butler]] saw — that war is a racket and the puppeteers play both sides against each other.[1] In this account the conflict also drives people toward the [[q|Q]] framework, which supplies "a place to go when their old narratives break down."[1]
