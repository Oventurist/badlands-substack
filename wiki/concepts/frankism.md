# Frankism

Frankism is an 18th-century heretical religious movement described in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as a pioneer of the doctrine of redemption through transgression.[1]

## Doctrine as described

According to [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], the Frankists held that in order to redeem the fallen sublunary world and restore humanity to a pre-adamic state, the messiah must come either through manifestations of great virtue or of great vice — and they chose the latter.[1] European nobility of the period became enamored with the idea because it allowed their already debauched practices to be justified spiritually.[1]

## Alleged modern legacy

Deschamps argues that prior to the secularization of the West, most cultures were highly resistant to vice-prone behavior, and that blasphemy was largely confined to elites who practiced Neoplatonic and gnostic magical rites in aristocratic halls; what once carried the penalty of burning at the stake has since become common pastime.[1] He describes the mechanism as suppression of normal human behaviors and the healthy cultural systems that satisfy primal urges, followed by the introduction of debauched practices that people first resist and eventually accept as "the new normal."[1] He frames contemporary cases such as the conviction of former [[cnn|CNN]] producer [[john-griffin|John Griffin]] as symptoms of this engineered process, and suggests examining the origins of Romanticism to understand how seemingly harmless cultural practices of the past 500 years shaped modern relationships.[1]
