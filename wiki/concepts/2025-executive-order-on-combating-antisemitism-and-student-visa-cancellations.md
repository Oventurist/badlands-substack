# 2025 Executive Order on Combating Antisemitism and Student Visa Cancellations

On Wednesday, January 29, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order to combat antisemitism, pledging to deport non-citizen college students and others who took part in pro-Palestinian protests.[1]

## Provisions

A fact sheet accompanying the order promised "immediate action" by the Justice Department to prosecute "terroristic threats, arson, vandalism and violence against American Jews," and to marshal all federal resources against what it described as "the explosion of antisemitism on our campuses and streets" since the October 7, 2023 attack on Israel by [[hamas|Hamas]].[1] Trump stated in the fact sheet: "To all the resident aliens who joined in the pro-jihadist protests, we put you on notice: come 2025, we will find you, and we will deport you," adding that he would "quickly cancel the student visas of all Hamas sympathizers on college campuses, which have been infested with radicalism like never before," echoing a 2024 campaign promise.[1]

## Reaction

Rights groups and legal scholars said the measure would violate constitutional free speech protections and would likely draw legal challenges, and the [[council-on-american-islamic-relations|Council on American-Islamic Relations]] said it would consider challenging the order in court if Trump attempted to implement it.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reported that his inbox was flooded with objections and argued that the order appeared on its face to be a draconian shift toward a crackdown on free speech, in line with behavior he attributed to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] on X, calling it a symptom of a disease infecting the Republican Party that he had warned about for over a year.[1] He contended that special privilege and protection is afforded to Israelis in America, that raising the issue can destroy reputations and careers, and that this resembles the cancel mobs of the woke era enforcing identity politics — arguing that "antisemitism," whose definition he said the Republican Party has expanded to include anti-Zionism, is itself identity politics, and that the Republican Party represents the single greatest threat to the MAGA populist movement.[1] He also pointed to an October 7, 2024 interview with [[hugh-hewitt|Hugh Hewitt]] in which Trump said many of the campus rioters were themselves Jewish students, asking what Republicans would do if Jewish students were targeted by the order for engaging in antisemitic behavior.[1]
