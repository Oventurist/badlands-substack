# Michigan Absentee Ballot ID Match Ruling

The **Michigan absentee ballot ID match ruling** was a December 2025 decision by a Michigan judge in favor of the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], the [[michigan-republican-party|Michigan Republican Party]], and Chesterfield Township Clerk [[cindy-berry|Cindy Berry]] in a lawsuit over how the state handles absentee ballots whose identification numbers do not match.[1]

## Background

The case stemmed from a 2024 change to Michigan election law requiring that the unique identification number printed on an absentee ballot stub exactly match the number on the return envelope before a vote may be counted.[1] The dispute centered on what should happen when those numbers do not match.[1]

Republican plaintiffs argued that mismatched ballots must be rejected, while voters should be given an opportunity to cure the defect.[1] Secretary of State [[jocelyn-benson|Jocelyn Benson]] and Director of Elections [[jonathan-brater|Jonathan Brater]] countered that decades-long administrative guidance permitted such ballots to be counted as "challenged ballots" rather than rejected outright.[1]

## Ruling

The court rejected the state's position, holding that election inspectors have authority to count absentee ballots only when the identification numbers match, and that where there is no match there is no legal authority to tabulate the ballot under any category.[1] The judge reasoned that the Legislature had strengthened the matching requirement in order to protect election integrity, and that long-standing administrative practices can no longer override the updated statute.[1]
