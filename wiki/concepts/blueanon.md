# BlueAnon

"BlueAnon" is a pejorative term for left-wing conspiracy theorizing, coined as a mirror image of [[qanon|QAnon]]. It gained wide use in July 2024 after polling showed large shares of Democratic voters embracing the claim that the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] had been staged.[1]

## The 2024 polling

At the height of the QAnon phenomenon in 2021, roughly 23 percent of Republicans told the Public Religion Research Institute that they believed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was fighting a cabal of satanic, sex-trafficking pedophiles.[1] By comparison, a [[morning-consult|Morning Consult]] poll released on Monday, July 22, 2024, found that 34 percent of Democrats — roughly one-third — believed Trump had faked his own assassination attempt the previous Saturday.[1] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] presented the comparison as evidence that conspiracy theorizing had "gone mainstream" on the political left.[1]

## Origins of the staged-shooting claim

Citing the Washington Free Beacon, ZeroHedge reported that the specific claim originated with Democratic powerbroker [[dmitri-mehlhorn|Dmitri Mehlhorn]], an adviser to LinkedIn founder [[reid-hoffman|Reid Hoffman]], who had made at least ten visits to the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]].[1] Hours after the shooting, Mehlhorn circulated a memo to reporters suggesting they portray the event as a false-flag operation drawn from [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s playbook and designed to give Trump an iconic photo opportunity, writing that "this is a classic Russian tactic, such as when Putin killed 300 civilians in 1999 and blamed it on terrorists to ride the backlash to winning power."[1]

Critics noted that the memo did not address the photographic evidence — including an image of a bullet passing near Trump's head and the visible wound across his right ear — or the death of rally attendee Corey Comperatore, who was shot while shielding his family.[1]
