# Badlands Brief: Trump Roasts The Press; Teases 2028 Run

"Trump Roasts The Press; Teases 2028 Run" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on July 27, 2026. The Brief is described by its editors as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war." The edition opens with an editor's note apologizing for a delay, explaining that Substack had deleted the entire post without saving drafts and that the issue had to be reconstructed the following morning while the editor was on a plane. Commentary in the issue is contributed by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].

## The White House Correspondents' Association Dinner

The lead item concerns President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s attendance at the rescheduled White House Correspondents' Association Dinner, held July 24 at the Waldorf Astoria in Washington, D.C. The source material notes that the annual event had been postponed after an April shooting disrupted the original dinner, and that the association intended to complete the interrupted program, including journalism awards and a performance by mentalist Oz Pearlman. The [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] said it had implemented a strengthened security plan following the April attack.

During his remarks, Trump joked that the audience represented "the really largest group of Trump derangement syndrome people ever put together at one time." According to ABC News as cited in the Brief, the address lasted more than an hour, alternating between calls for cooperation with the press and criticism of journalists and political opponents, and closed with the president donning a red "Trump 2028" hat as part of a joke about another presidential campaign.

Burning Bright's commentary treats the evening as an inversion of a long-running ritual. He argues that the dinner, once read as "a good bit of rare bipartisan unity coding for the American plebs," looks in hindsight "like a deeper humiliation ritual (on us) than any of us knew at the time." He recalls having loved Stephen Colbert's 2006 performance roasting President [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] as an indictment of neoconservative policy, but writes that he now regards Colbert as "always cut from the same cloth as the very warmongers and collectivists he purported to oppose" — a dialectic he credits Trump with interrupting over "the most gloriously-disruptive decade in American politics in ... perhaps ever."

The commentary dwells on what it calls the understated portions of the speech, particularly Trump's seemingly somber lament that the media would have nothing left to talk about once he was gone. Burning Bright reads this as both a shot at media incentive structures — describing the press as "an antagonist to the American people masquerading as a guardian of its mindscape" — and as an existential truth, arguing that Trump has allowed the public to diagnose the ills of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] and find them terminal. He speculates that just as there may not be political parties in a future America built through deconstruction, "perhaps there never should have been a class recognized as 'media' in the first place," and that the incentive structures that made the press will unmake it once Trump leaves center stage — a departure the writer hopes will not come until 2032, "a possibility Trump is still doing nothing to distance himself from." The section closes with an analogy to the Joker's dependence on Batman: the Media Industrial Complex, having been tricked into defining itself entirely in opposition to Trump, will lose its meaning after the Age of Trump, and in so doing has revealed that whom it really opposes is the American public.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry supplements the item with a list of highlights from the evening, noting jokes at the expense of [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]], Chris Christie, Kaitlan Collins, [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]], and [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK, Jr.]], as well as Trump making fun of himself when jokes did not land. He singles out "this banger of a joke calling [[bari-weiss|Bari Weiss]] a gay dictator over [[cbs|CBS News]]," and "Trump's epic troll announcing his 2028 candidacy."

## The ICC, Mamdani, and Venezuela's withdrawal

A second item links the narrative around a possible arrest of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in New York to a [[state-department|State Department]] campaign against the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]]. The Brief reports that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] announced a campaign aimed at dismantling the ICC, arguing the tribunal threatens U.S. sovereignty and improperly targets American military personnel and officials, and that the effort includes encouraging other countries to distance themselves from the court.

The announcement coincided with renewed debate over the ICC's 2024 arrest warrant for Netanyahu. Trump said Netanyahu would not be arrested while visiting the United States; [[israel|Israel]] rejects the court's jurisdiction and denies the war crimes allegations underlying the warrant. New York City Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] said his administration had reviewed what authority the city possessed, but later clarified that New York City cannot itself execute an ICC arrest warrant. Reuters is cited for the point that U.S. law provides no domestic mechanism requiring state or local officials to enforce ICC warrants because the United States is not a party to the Rome Statute. Netanyahu separately criticized Mamdani, accusing him of fomenting hatred, and, per the commentary, responded on Fox News accusing the mayor of antisemitism. The United States welcomed [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s decision to withdraw from the ICC and called on other countries to follow suit, according to a State Department statement reported by Anadolu Agency.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry builds a timeline beginning July 13, when Rubio announced a sweeping "whole-of-government" campaign to dismantle the court. He asserts — an interpretive claim advanced by the author — that the ICC was "founded in the year 2000 by [[george-soros|George Soros]] in order to prosecute world leaders like [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] who refused to comply with the [[new-world-order|New World Order]]." He recounts that Mamdani then released a video declaring Netanyahu a war criminal and stating his intent to arrest him should he set foot in the state, and that Trump publicly defended Netanyahu.

The commentary then challenges the premise that Netanyahu is the root of Israeli warmongering. It points to an interview with [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]], an opposition leader described as the favorite to defeat Netanyahu in the upcoming October election. Bennett describes himself as "ten degrees more right-wing than Netanyahu" but also as a secularist with no religious element to his platform and no allegiance to any Ultra-Orthodox faction; the author notes that although Bennett once said he would be willing to sign a two-state solution, he now says that as prime minister he will never allow Palestine to exist and will "erase it from the map and absorb it into Israel." The conclusion drawn is that "there is no 'pro-peace' faction in Israeli politics" with any chance of taking power.

On Venezuela, the Brief reports that a group of "Zionist congressmen" traveled to Caracas over the weekend to meet interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], who then announced Venezuela's ICC withdrawal, prompting allegations that she acted on Israel's behalf. The author offers an alternative reading: the ICC has investigated the Venezuelan government since [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] took office in 2014, opened formal investigations after referrals from "NeoCon-aligned governments" in 2018, and received additional referrals as recently as the prior year from [[ecuador|Ecuador]] — the country Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] has identified as the new home base of the cocaine cartels. The ICC denied all Venezuelan requests to end the inquiries, which the author reads as intent to indict Maduro and probably Rodríguez and to bring Venezuela under UN control. That the State Department is now using Venezuela's withdrawal as a rallying cry, he argues, "demonstrates President Trump's ability to turn the [[deep-state|Deep State]] against itself." The section ends with an open question: whether Mamdani is working with Trump to generate these narrative developments, or whether Trump is "merely playing the Game Theory board like a master."

## Security clearances of the Jack Smith team

A third item, headlined as ironic, reports that the [[center-to-advance-security-in-america|Center to Advance Security in America]] (CASA) asked Acting Director of National Intelligence [[william-pulte|William Pulte]] to review the security clearances of former members of Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s investigative team, citing recently released [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] records. CASA requested that clearances be revoked if an official review determines the allegations warrant it.

The request relies on internal Justice Department communications released by [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] Chairman [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]]. As described in the reporting, the records allege that members of Smith's office granted access to classified information without the required "need-to-know" authorization, and that concerns were raised internally about security procedures involving a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility. The Brief also notes that [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] separately referred Smith to the Justice Department for possible criminal investigation over testimony related to his office's access to members of [[congress|Congress]]' text messages. As of publication, no action by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] had been announced.

Ashe in America's commentary asks why any of these people still hold clearances "in the middle of 2026." She argues that security clearances are of extreme value to government bureaucrats because they lead to "cushy consulting gigs and speaking fees after government service," provide insider access to classified information and national security insights, and "(inexplicably) presume the cleared person is trustworthy," a premise she says "seems to invite foreign influence and espionage." Her position is that a clearance should be granted for a specific purpose and never be an entitlement, that "bureaucrats caught in a treasonous grand conspiracy should have their access to classified information revoked as step one," and that this is "not retaliation, it's consequence."

## Kuznetsova's allegations on Ukrainian children

The fourth item reports remarks by Russian [[state-duma|State Duma]] Deputy Speaker [[anna-kuznetsova|Anna Kuznetsova]], published in a Saturday interview. Kuznetsova alleged that Ukrainian children have been trafficked through the darknet for between $30,000 and $50,000, and that law enforcement agencies in France and other European countries had documented dozens of cases involving the use of Ukrainian children as slave labor. She said preliminary data indicated that more than 60,000 Ukrainian children had been deported without legal representatives, including to [[european-union|European Union]] countries. In a separate report based on the same interview, she said mines she alleged were laid by Ukrainian forces in border areas had killed more than 50 Ukrainian children and injured more than 120, and that more than 15 children in [[russia|Russia]] had been injured by detonations involving Ukrainian antipersonnel mines.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry responds in unusually forceful terms, writing that if there is any truth to the allegations and if any American is involved — "including those from the Trump administration" — they "should be hunted to the ends of the earth like dogs," treated as unlawful enemy combatants, and shown no mercy. He asks to what extent the State Department and Congress are involved and whether this explains why "both political parties have so ardently defended [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s war effort," adding, "You will never convince me that nobody knew." He calls for every resource and constitutional power to be used, including state governors deploying the National Guard or the president deploying the Marines to lock down Washington, and states that he would accept dissolving the federal government and entering "a temporary state of anarchy" if that were required to save the children.

## The American Bar Association and accreditation

The fifth item reports that Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]], R-Texas, intends to pursue legislation removing the [[american-bar-association|American Bar Association]]'s authority to accredit U.S. law schools. Gill chairs the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]]'s Task Force on Defending Constitutional Rights and Exposing Institutional Abuse, and said lawmakers are examining federal legislation that could reduce the ABA's accreditation role while leaving states free to decide whether to work with the organization.

Gill's comments followed a House hearing at which ABA President [[michelle-behnke|Michelle Behnke]] defended the organization's approach to diversity, equity and inclusion, testifying that diversity initiatives strengthen the legal profession and that diversity and excellence are "not mutually exclusive." The Brief reports that the ABA has begun rescinding its DEI requirements for law schools after Trump signed an executive order addressing such mandates. During the hearing Gill criticized the ABA's previous DEI requirements as discriminating against certain students and alleged that the organization has exercised significant influence over law school admissions, curricula and continuing legal education.

Ashe in America recalls that the previous year Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] sent a letter to the ABA stripping its preferred access to the judicial nomination process, quoting: "While the ABA is free to comment on judicial nominations along with other activist organizations, there is no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations and the Department of Justice will not do so." She describes Gill — who "sort of looks like Clark Kent" — as now moving to end the ABA's accreditation role, and argues that "NGO's and Professional Associations are the connective tissue of the corrupt and subversive shadow control, and the ABA is the center of the beast when it comes to controlling lawyers and judges and the institution of law itself." The "long march through the institutions," she writes, "was comprehensive and effective, but it's more exposed than it's ever been."

She pivots to a related resource story, noting reporting by Notus that the [[democratic-national-committee|DNC]] had to mortgage its headquarters to fund operations ahead of the midterms — and had done so two years in a row. She finds this striking given persistent predictions that the midterms will be "a blood bath," asking, "What happened to all the super real and not at all laundered donations?" and concluding: "Dismantling the institutional corruption is fun, and we're just getting started. Accelerate."

## The Caspian Sea strike and naval escalation

The final item concerns Iran's accusation that Ukraine attacked an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea, killing one sailor and wounding another. Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] said Ukraine had conducted long-range strikes against vessels involved in military cargo shipments linked to [[iran|Iran]], as well as a warship. Iran summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Tehran to protest and said it would pursue the matter diplomatically. Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said he raised the issue with EU foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] and Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, describing the strike as a violation of international law and warning that it "cannot go unanswered," according to Iranian statements reported by AFP.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the item with a geography lesson, noting that the Black Sea — where naval conflict between Russia and Ukraine has previously occurred — lies on the opposite side of Russia from the Caspian, meaning Ukraine "likely had to fly their drone OVER Russia" to strike the Iranian cargo ship. He quotes Araghchi's claim that "Ukraine attacked Iran at the behest of Israel in order to draw Europe deeper into their war," and recalls that the previous week Ukraine attacked 13 ships in the Black Sea, at least one of them Turkish, killing a sailor in that attack as well.

During Russia's Navy Day events, President Putin convened his naval commanders and said Russian authorities should respond decisively to attempts to seize Russian vessels while acting within international maritime law, comparing such situations to efforts against piracy. He described the incidents as "attempts by ill-intentioned parties to pressure our fleet, including the commercial fleet," and said: "In such cases, of course, we must act cautiously, within the framework of international maritime law, but decisively – just as piracy and pirates must be fought. I hope for your effective work in this field – protecting our fleet." The commentary notes that in international maritime law pirates were once branded "the enemies of mankind."

Tallying the week, the author observes that Zelensky has bombed cargo ships from Russia, [[turkey|Turkey]] and Iran in the Black, Mediterranean and Caspian Seas, and predicts that "it now seems all but a certainty that we will see a Russia-Turkey-Iran naval alliance," a development that "would really throw a wrench into [[nato|NATO]], the Persian Gulf and [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]." As a postscript, he cites an appearance by [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] — whom he calls a "[[mossad|Mossad]] Media shill" — on Ukrainian television revealing that she had learned she is of Ukrainian descent, which he says "tracks" given his claim that Israel was founded by Bolsheviks "who all came from Ukraine."
