# Courage and Radical Acceptance

**Courage and radical acceptance of the truth** is the coping framework presented by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in "Parenting For Freedom" (May 12, 2023) as, in his subtitle's terms, "the only way out."[1]

## The spilled-coffee illustration

Deschamps illustrates the framework with a scenario: a person about to leave for work trips, spills coffee over a freshly cleaned suit, and realizes they will be late with nothing they can do about it.[1]

In **Response A: Cowardice and Half-Heartedness**, the person collapses in tears, concludes the universe is against them, and experiences panic, existential angst, depression, and disillusionment, reawakening an old wound of feeling tragically victimized.[1] This fearful response accepts the truth of the situation only reluctantly and half-heartedly, leaving the old expectation — that everything was fine and work would begin on time — still buzzing in the mind and stealing whatever joy the new situation offers.[1] The result is a divided self, unable to release what was in order to accept what is, producing demotivation, disillusionment, discouragement, and disempowerment.[1] The subconscious then generates emotions and thoughts validating the conclusion that one is a perpetual victim, yielding depression, lack of ambition, boredom, and a constant flow of distracting and unsettling emotions.[1] Deschamps states that the vast majority of children raised in less-than-ideal conditions experience this way of seeing the world.[1]

In **Response B: Courage and Whole-Heartedness**, the person chuckles at the comic nature of the situation and, recognizing there is no returning to the timeline where they were on time, employs what Deschamps calls the radical acceptance of the truth coping method.[1] The person recalls friends and family who met challenges stoically rather than bitterly, whose role model influence guides them in the moment.[1] Once the quiet inner choice to accept the truth is made, frustration transforms almost instantly into excitement and humorous glee; the person changes outfits, plays a favourite playlist, and drives to work late but in good cheer, better able to take advantage of opportunities they would otherwise have discounted.[1]

## Compounding effects

Courage, Deschamps writes, adds to itself and makes us more courageous; it frees mental and emotional energy for managing and exploiting the opportunities of the moment, and in terms of character it dissolves resentment and bitterness.[1] Conversely, dwelling on tragedy, suffering, and the seeming cruelty of life makes a person more bitter: "Tragedy and hardship are tough pieces of meat; we need to chew thoroughly or choke if we swallow too soon."[1] Bitterness robs a person of joy and emotional solace, makes them quicker to react toxically, and impairs discernment, increasing the likelihood of further mistakes and suffering.[1]

The concept is presented as the psychological expression of [[hormesis]] and the [[zone-of-proximal-development|zone of proximal development]], transmitted to children chiefly through the [[role-model|role model]].[1]
