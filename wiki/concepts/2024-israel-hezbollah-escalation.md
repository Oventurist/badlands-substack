# 2024 Israel–Hezbollah Escalation

In June 2024 the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration grew extremely concerned that escalating violence between [[israel-defense-forces|Israel]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] would deteriorate into all-out war, and scrambled to prevent it, according to US officials.[1]

## The crisis

Coming amid a sensitive push for a ceasefire in Gaza, a full-blown war with the Lebanese militant group would dramatically worsen the regional crisis and draw the United States deeper into the conflict.[1] Behind the scenes, the administration cautioned Israel against the notion of a "limited war" in Lebanon, warning that Iran could intervene and flood Lebanon with pro-Iranian militants from Syria, Iraq, and even Yemen.[1] US officials said they were concerned about Israel rushing into — or being dragged into — a war with Hezbollah without a clear strategy or consideration of the full implications of a wider conflict.[1] The administration sought to contain the fighting while working to secure a hostage-ceasefire deal in Gaza, and the [[white-house|White House]] believed a Gaza ceasefire was the only measure that would significantly de-escalate tensions on the Israeli–Lebanese border.[1] Driving the news was an Israeli airstrike on Tuesday, June 11, that killed a senior Hezbollah commander in the town of Jwaya, roughly six miles north of the border.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] joked that the world might get a full-scale Lord of the Rings battle scene before the conflict ends, and that the Jwaya strike must be one of the "precision strikes" so often advertised.[1] More seriously, he argued Hezbollah is a very different animal from [[hamas|Hamas]]: Hamas is the "JV squad," third-world guerrillas trained up by Langley or one of its affiliates, essentially a street gang given resources to amass an army and wage asymmetric warfare against a fully equipped state military — though he credited them with manufacturing their own sniper rifles and becoming proficient marksmen.[1]

Hezbollah, by contrast, is the "Varsity team," closer to a state military.[1] Formed in 1982 in response to Israel's invasion of Lebanon and backed by the Islamic Republic of Iran, it is alleged to have been trained by the [[cia|CIA]] as part of mujahideen programs that spun off [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]], the project that helped Osama bin Laden build an "Afghan-Arab" army against the Soviets and led to the formation of [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]].[1] The group fields an army larger than the Lebanese national military, with an estimated 30,000 fighters and 20,000 reservists — leaders claim 100,000 — and confirmed reports place its ballistic missile stocks at between 100,000 and 150,000.[1] For a decade it has fought in Syria alongside the Russians in defense of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] and the national government, and it possesses Soviet T-55 and T-72 tanks.[1] Russia's [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] was reported in autumn 2023 to be sending mobile air defense vehicles to Hezbollah, and in June 2024 the group was reported to have fired anti-aircraft missiles at IDF warplanes.[1] Its intelligence network and secret service apparatus have been described as "one of the best in the world," having reportedly infiltrated the IDF.[1]

The commentary concluded that Hezbollah fighters are hardened professionals, far more experienced than Hamas — which, according to Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]], is winning its war against the IDF — and cited reporting by [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]] that the United States funds the IDF, the Lebanese military, and Hamas simultaneously, answering its own question of why with a single word: control.[1]
