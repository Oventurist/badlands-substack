# Peking Man

Peking Man is the name given to a purported new "missing link" between ape and man uncovered in 1926 near Peking (Beijing) by an expedition funded by the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]], which the Jesuit [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] joined during his two decades in China.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] characterizes the episode as "Piltdown Hoax 2.0," noting that suspicion the earlier [[piltdown-man|Piltdown Man]] was a fraud was already spreading across the scientific community when Chardin nonetheless published several scientific papers on the new find, creating a fresh worldwide sensation.[1] The discovery was promoted as final proof that Darwin's theory was true.[1]

Anyone wishing to investigate the claims was out of luck: the hundreds of bone fragments were quickly crated for shipment to the United States for further examination and were then mysteriously lost, never to be recovered.[1] Chardin appeared to feel no remorse over the loss and made no attempt to track down the prized find, telling friends there was no point crying over spilled milk and that everyone should proceed with a renewed faith that Darwinism must be accepted as the essence of Christianity.[1] Ehret notes that scientists to this day treat the find as a fait accompli and continue to write apologetics for the missing bones.[1]

The hoax's cultural durability is illustrated by [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]], who in his 1957 *New Bottles for New Wine* invoked Peking Man as the benchmark of evolutionary distance while declaring his belief in [[transhumanism|transhumanism]].[1]
