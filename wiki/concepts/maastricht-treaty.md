# Maastricht Treaty

The Maastricht Treaty was the 1992 agreement establishing the Euro by 1994, which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents as the European replication of the [[nafta|NAFTA]] agenda.[1]

## Ehret's account

Ehret argues that as NAFTA struck down protective programs keeping North American factories in the United States and Canada, the same collapse of physically productive powers alongside skyrocketing monetary profits was replicated in Europe through Maastricht and the creation of the Euro, while the liberalization process of [[perestroika|Perestroika]] carried the agenda into the former Soviet Union.[1] Whether the program was called the "End of History" or the "[[new-world-order|New World Order]]," he writes, the effect was the same.[1] Euro integration, universal banking and the [[derivatives|derivative]] economy together induced a cartelization of finance through newly legalized mergers and acquisitions at an unprecedented rate, absorbing the many financial institutions of the early 1980s into each other through the 1990s.[1]
