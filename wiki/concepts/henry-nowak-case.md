# Henry Nowak Case

The Henry Nowak case concerns the killing of an 18-year-old man in Southampton, United Kingdom, on December 3, 2025, and the conduct of Hampshire Police in the immediate aftermath. It became a subject of significant public outrage in Britain and, as reported in the June 3, 2026 [[badlands-media|Badlands]] Brief, drew allusions to a "white George Floyd" moment in commentary about differential police and media treatment.

## The killing

Nowak was stabbed five times while walking home after a night out by 23-year-old Vickrum Singh Digwa. The wounds, inflicted with an 8-inch blade, included injuries to his face and legs and a penetrating lung injury. When police arrived, Nowak repeatedly told officers that he had been stabbed and could not breathe. Instead of treating his wounds or immediately securing Digwa — who was carrying two blades, including a kirpan worn around his neck, and had trained with weapons since the age of 12 — officers arrested and handcuffed Nowak after Digwa claimed that Nowak had launched a racist attack that knocked off his turban. Nowak died about an hour later. No independent evidence ever supported the racism allegation, and a visible blood trail was present at the scene.

## Trial and aftermath

Digwa was arrested at the scene and convicted of murder on May 28, 2026. Prosecutors told the court that the case was about murder rather than racism or religion, and Digwa was found to have lied about key events. He was sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term of 21 years.

Nearly six months after the killing, Hampshire Police referred itself for an independent investigation into its handling of the incident. Nowak's family described the treatment of their son as "unbearable," observing that the perpetrator "was believed" while their bleeding son was not. The case prompted broader debate in Britain over police response and media coverage in cases where the victim is white and the perpetrator is from a minority background.
