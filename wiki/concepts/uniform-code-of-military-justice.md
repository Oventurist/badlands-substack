# Uniform Code of Military Justice

The Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) is the body of federal law governing the conduct and discipline of United States military personnel.[1]

In January 2026, the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] invoked the UCMJ in administrative actions against Senator [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]], a retired Navy captain, stating that retired officers remain subject to the code and that Kelly's status as a sitting US senator does not exempt him.[1] Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] announced a formal letter of censure — a permanent reprimand placed in Kelly's military record — for alleged "seditious statements" and "reckless misconduct," along with a 45-day retirement grade determination that could reduce Kelly's retired rank and corresponding retirement pay.[1]

## Invocation in Badlands tribunal commentary

A December 2024 Badlands feature presented the UCMJ as the jurisdictional basis for [[military-tribunals|military tribunals]] said to be addressing crimes such as treason, sedition, and crimes against humanity committed by [[deep-state|Deep State]] actors.[2] The article argued that when an individual is designated an enemy combatant, their conduct is adjudicated under the UCMJ rather than civilian law, and that in that setting presidential pardons — whether preemptive or retrospective — carry no legal weight.[2]

It further claimed that operating under wartime protocols allows tribunals to try cases on the basis of sealed evidence, classified intelligence, and strategic imperatives, and that this framework is uniquely suited to offenses that threaten national security.[2] In practice, U.S. military commissions have jurisdiction over a narrow class of defendants and do not extend to American civilian officials, so these assertions are unsupported.[2]
