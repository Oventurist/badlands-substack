# UK Newborn Whole-Genome Sequencing Programme

The UK newborn whole-genome sequencing programme is a policy announced in June 2025 under which every baby born in the United Kingdom would have their DNA tested as part of a new ten-year plan for the [[nhs|National Health Service]].[1] The plan was made public by Health Secretary [[wes-streeting|Wes Streeting]] in remarks to the Telegraph and reported by [[sky-news|Sky News]].[1]

## Scope and rationale

Whole-genome sequencing would screen newborns for hundreds of diseases, which Streeting said would allow people to "leapfrog" killer illnesses.[1] The programme sits within a broader repositioning of the NHS toward sickness prevention rather than treatment, with personalised medicine — enabled by DNA testing — forming a large part of that shift and £650 million pledged by the government for genomics research.[1] Streeting described the aim as transforming the NHS "from a service which diagnoses and treats ill health to one that predicts and prevents it."[1]

## Criticism

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterised the offer as a bargain in which parents are asked to surrender unrestricted access to their own and their children's genetic information in exchange for a promise of protection from disease, noting sardonically that because the NHS is free at the point of use the public is being told to "just hand over your baby."[1] She emphasised the value of the resulting national genomic data set and argued that "tyranny is always sold with utopian promises that abandoning your rights is the only way to keep you safe. And the herd lines up to comply."[1]
