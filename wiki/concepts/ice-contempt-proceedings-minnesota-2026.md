# ICE Contempt Proceedings in Minnesota (2026)

The ICE contempt proceedings in Minnesota were a confrontation between the federal judiciary and [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] in January 2026, in which Chief U.S. District Judge [[patrick-schiltz|Patrick Schiltz]] ordered acting ICE Director [[todd-lyons|Todd Lyons]] to appear in person in Minneapolis federal court to show cause why he should not be held in contempt.[1]

## The order

Judge Schiltz directed Lyons to appear on January 30, 2026 at 1:00 p.m.[1] The order stemmed from ICE's failure to comply with a January 14, 2026 directive requiring a bond hearing or release within seven days for detainee [[juan-tobay-robles|Juan Tobay Robles]], who had been taken into custody by federal immigration agents earlier that month.[1] Schiltz described the violation as part of "one of dozens of court orders" ignored by ICE in recent weeks, writing that "The Court's patience is at an end... the extent of ICE's violation of court orders is likewise extraordinary, and lesser measures have been tried and failed."[1] The hearing could be canceled if Robles were released beforehand.[1]

## Context

The ruling came amid escalating tensions in Minneapolis over federal immigration enforcement operations, including the fatal shooting of [[alex-jeffrey-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] by [[united-states-border-patrol|U.S. Border Patrol]] agents on January 24, 2026 during anti-ICE protests.[1] The broader uproar over enforcement tactics in Minneapolis also drove the [[january-2026-government-shutdown-standoff|January 2026 government shutdown standoff]] over Department of Homeland Security appropriations.[1]
