# Burr Conspiracy

The Burr conspiracy was the western phase of [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]]'s effort to dismember the United States, exposed in 1807 and treated by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as a formative episode in the birth of America's deep state.[1]

## British recruitment

Three weeks after the July 1804 duel in which Burr killed [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]], British Ambassador [[anthony-merry|Anthony Merry]] — following a meeting with Burr's confidante Col. [[james-wilkinson|James Wilkinson]] — wrote to the British Foreign Secretary that Burr had offered to assist His Majesty's Government "in endeavouring to effect a separation of the western part of the United States from that which lies between the Atlantic and the mountains."[1] Merry judged that Burr's isolation, ambition and "spirit of revenge against the present administration" might make him a faithful servant of the Empire.[1]

## The Louisiana scheme

With the failure of the [[new-england-secessionist-plot|New England secessionist plot]], the design shifted to Burr's dominance of the newly acquired [[louisiana-territory|Louisiana Territory]], sold by Napoleon to America in 1804.[1] Napoleon's willingness to sell the territory surprised everyone, including the Americans, and wrecked British plans to seize the land and suffocate the USA as a closed system locked onto the Atlantic coast, as had been attempted with the 1774 Quebec Act.[1] Burr persuaded President [[thomas-jefferson|Jefferson]] to install his co-conspirators in powerful positions in the new territory, with Wilkinson appointed Governor.[1] British soldiers were to work alongside American mercenaries under Burr's employ to take full control of Louisiana and New Orleans and expand the territory by declaring war on Spain; testimony at Burr's trial held that he would then depose the sitting president and establish himself as monarch of a new British American confederation.[1]

## Exposure, trial and acquittal

The British were caught providing boats and money to the mercenaries — forty British boats and seventy-five mercenaries were apprehended — correspondence was intercepted, and Burr soon faced treason charges before the supreme court.[1] The diplomat William Eaton testified that Burr told him he would "turn Congress neck and heels out of doors, assassinate the President, (or what amounted to that,) and declare himself the protector of an energetic Government."[1] The testimony did not qualify as the hard evidence needed for conviction, and Merry's smoking-gun letter was only discovered decades later, resulting in Burr's acquittal.[1]

## Aftermath

Popular rage made life within the republic impossible for the former vice president, and using a $40,000 "gift" from [[john-jacob-astor|John Jacob Astor]], Burr made his way in disguise to Canada, where his nephew George Prevost served as Governor General.[1] Prevost supplied letters of introduction to Lord Castlereagh, and Burr sailed from Nova Scotia to London, where he lodged for five years at the mansion of [[jeremy-bentham|Jeremy Bentham]] and plotted further schemes for dissolving the Union with the highest echelons of British intelligence.[1]
