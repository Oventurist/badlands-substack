# Capture of Nicolás Maduro

The capture of Nicolás Maduro was a January 2026 US military operation in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ordered a pre-dawn raid on Caracas, [[venezuela|Venezuela]], resulting in the seizure of President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] and his wife [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]].[1]

## The operation and prosecution

The operation led to Maduro's transfer to New York, where he and Flores pleaded not guilty to US federal charges of narco-terrorism conspiracy, cocaine importation and weapons offenses.[1] Maduro declared himself innocent and a "prisoner of war," while his son [[nicolas-maduro-guerra|Nicolás Maduro Guerra]] accused the United States of kidnapping.[1]

## Succession

Former Vice President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] was sworn in as interim president, condemning the raid but calling for "peace and dialogue" with Washington and proposing a "balanced and respectful" cooperative agenda, while insisting Venezuela would "never return to being the colony of another empire."[1] Rodríguez chose to be sworn in atop the sarcophagus of former President [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] at his monument in Caracas, following the ceremony with a speech echoing Maduro's own rhetoric and emphasizing that there was only one true president of Venezuela — Nicolás Maduro.[1]

Trump placed Rodríguez on what he called a "short leash," demanding she crack down on drug trafficking, expel Iranian and Cuban operatives, halt oil sales to US adversaries, release detained Americans and eventually hold free elections before stepping aside — warning she could pay a "bigger price" than Maduro if she resisted, while asserting the United States would "run" Venezuela temporarily to exploit its oil reserves.[1]

## International reaction

The intervention sparked widespread backlash.[1] [[russia|Russia]] denounced it as "international banditry" and a harbinger of global chaos, warning of resurgent neo-colonialism and urging Maduro's release.[1] The UN Security Council held an emergency session, with many nations labeling the raid a crime of aggression.[1] Observers described the event as a sharp return to assertive US interventionism in the region, raising fears of broader instability across Latin America.[1]

## Spillover to Colombia

Trump extended similar threats to [[colombia|Colombia]], calling its president a "sick man" enabling the cocaine trade and suggesting a similar raid "sounds good," prompting President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] to vow he would "take up arms" in defense and to demand loyalty from Colombian officers.[1] Trump reportedly said Petro had better "watch his ass," and Petro responded by calling Trump a coward and saying "Come and get me."[1] Petro also held a rally after Maduro's arrest at which he claimed that "a clan of pedophiles" — specifically "friends of [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]]" — were attempting to take over Colombia and Venezuela and must be stopped.[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the unusual dignity being extended to Maduro and the near-identical rhetoric of Trump and Rodríguez, and pointed to Erik Prince of Blackwater having met Rodríguez in Venezuela six years earlier — a meeting many analysts wrongly treat as the moment she betrayed Maduro.[1] He observed that the arrest turned Maduro into a folk hero across South America as a symbol of defiance against American imperialism, and that Maduro's son now addresses large rallies claiming the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s plan had been to start a civil war in Venezuela to destabilize the government — a pattern reminiscent of the early phase of operations against Assad in Syria.[1] This raised the possibility, in his framing, that the "arrest" actually subverted a civil war plot, consistent with the [[trumpamania|TrumpaMania]] thesis.[1]
