# German Industrial Crisis (2026)

The German industrial crisis of 2026 refers to the accelerating contraction of [[germany|Germany]]'s manufacturing sector, marked by mass job losses, record corporate bankruptcies, and sweeping restructuring across the automotive industry.[1]

## Scale

The [[federation-of-german-industries|Federation of German Industries]] reported in late July 2026 that Germany's industrial sector was shedding roughly 15,000 jobs per month, citing a competitiveness crisis driven by high energy costs, weak investment, geopolitical pressures, and increased competition from [[china|China]]. Three weeks earlier it had been reported that Germany experienced its highest number of corporate bankruptcies in 21 years during the second quarter of 2026.[1]

[[bmw|BMW]] announced it would cut several thousand German jobs by the end of 2027 through voluntary redundancy, with global headcount falling by about 8,000 — roughly 5% of its workforce — concentrated in administrative and development roles rather than production. Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, and other manufacturers announced comparable cost-cutting as the sector faced declining demand, weaker Chinese sales, the electric-vehicle transition, elevated energy costs, and trade pressures.[1]

## Causes and commentary

German industry leaders blamed the energy price shock for the collapsing economy, a trend described as beginning in 2018 and reaching its apex in 2023.[1]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] stressed that Germany is the backbone of the European economy, holding the continent's largest manufacturing base at roughly 28% of its GDP, and that German engineering has been legendary for centuries — many great American inventions of the Industrial Revolution having been developed by first-generation German immigrants.[1]

He argued the driver is not the [[iran-war|Iran war]] but Europe's own elimination of serious domestic energy production, leaving it dependent on [[russia|Russia]] — the country Europe simultaneously seems intent on destroying. Framing the question as "stupidity, or narcissistic sociopathy," he contended that while elites bear blame, the people of any nation, including the United States, must eventually take accountability for the society they have allowed to incubate.[1]

## Bankruptcies and NATO capacity (January 2026)

In January 2026 Germany announced the highest number of commercial bankruptcies in over a decade, concentrated in transportation, storage, hospitality and construction — sectors whose distress signals a potentially severe recession.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited a 74% rise in natural gas prices between 2022 and 2025 as confirmation that Germany was already in severe recession, arguing that without a functioning German economy Europe cannot sustain a continental military and is reduced to "basically a tourism economy."[2] He connected this weakness to [[nato|NATO]]'s thin reinforcement of [[greenland|Greenland]], where Germany contributed thirteen reconnaissance troops.[2]
