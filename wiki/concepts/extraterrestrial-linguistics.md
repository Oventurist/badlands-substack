# Extraterrestrial Linguistics

***Extraterrestrial Linguistics*** is a book by [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]] cited as the principal English-language source for the [[ancient-german-language-thesis|ancient German language thesis]] in [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]]'s [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[1]

## Content and role in the series

The essay "On Languages and Cover-ups" describes the book as going very deep into the comparative-vocabulary material the series summarizes, and tells readers they will have to read it to find out why it carries that title.[1] The author states that he has borrowed heavily from the book in writing the series because he knows of no other English-speaking source on the claimed ancient German connection to all world languages.[1]

## Landmann's research

According to Dodson, the life's work of the German author [[erhard-landmann|Erhard Landmann]] revealed not only the ancient German connection but a great deal about mankind's connection to the stars.[1] The essay reports that on this account the [[pleiades|Pleiades]] play a major role in human history and are often referred to as "Min" or "Maia," and that the vessels which carried mankind's "starry ancestors" to Earth and back were usually egg-shaped — denoted by the syllables *ai* and *eck* — or shaped as a pillar of light, denoted by *sal* or *sul*.[1] The author frames this as a case of following the research where it leads even when the destination is bizarre.[1]

## Applied etymology

Using these syllables the series proposes rereadings of familiar names and words. The name Solomon is said to contain *Sal* and *Min*, which could denote travelling to Earth from the Pleiades on a pillar of light — a reading the author explicitly presents as possible rather than established.[1] Words for "path" are offered as a further test case: Tamil *patai*, where *pat* resembles path and *ai* could refer to the path of the egg; Arabic *masar*, where *ma* could refer to Maia; Amharic *meningedi*, glossed as "go to Min"; Xhosa *umendo*, containing *men/min*; Southern Sotho *tsela*, containing the vessel element *sel*; and Catalan *cami*, which the author identifies as the Hebrew word for the Pleiades.[1] He concludes that this constitutes a great deal of overlap between languages linking paths to the Pleiades and to pillar- or egg-shaped travelling vessels.[1]

## Source of the Aztec word list

Part 5 of the series, "The Ancient Origin of Modern Conflict," states that it borrows heavily from *Extraterrestrial Linguistics* and reproduces from it the [[aztec-german-word-correspondences|list of Aztec words and their ancient German counterparts]] that forms the article's evidentiary core, noting that only a handful of the pairs Dodson lists are given.[2] The author acknowledges that the book's title may throw readers off, and responds by describing Dodson as a multimillionaire success coach with an extensive background in history and linguistics who also wrote *How to Learn a Language in Five Days*.[2] Readers wishing to explore the subject further are directed to *Extraterrestrial Linguistics* together with Dodson's *Levels of Heaven and Hell*.[2]
