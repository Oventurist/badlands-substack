# Executive Order 13850

Executive Order 13850 is a sanctions order issued by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] in November 2018 targeting entities doing business with Venezuela's state oil company, [[pdvsa|PDVSA]], by freezing bank wires and driving the filing of Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs).[1]

## Effect on the PDVSA litigation

Because of the order, defense attorneys for the 42 oil officials sued by [[david-boies|David Boies]] in the Southern District of Florida were able to convince the judge that proceeding with discovery would violate national security.[1] The judge agreed and dismissed the lawsuits.[1]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues that this outcome has been widely misread: rather than ending the case, the sanctions paused it, and the litigation — along with viable discovery into the underlying evidence — would resume once the sanctions were lifted.[1]

## Relevance to sanctions relief in 2026

Under this reading, Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s announced intention to lift Venezuela sanctions would both reopen the dormant discovery track and restart lawful PDVSA-related bank wire transfers.[1] Regulators monitoring those transfers would then notice discrepancies between current and pre-sanctions money flows, triggering fresh SARs and new investigations.[1] Combined with material seized in the [[helsinge|Helsinge]] raids, the result is projected to build a broad conspiracy web implicating individuals and corporations, some with ties to cartels designated as terrorist organizations by the Trump administration.[1]

## Use in the January 2026 oil-belt crackdown

The [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] invoked Executive Order 13850 — formally titled "Blocking Property of Additional Persons Contributing to the Situation in Venezuela" and dated November 2, 2018 — to sanction four companies operating in [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s oil sector: Corniola Limited, Krape Myrtle Co LTD, Winky International Limited, and Aries Global Investment LTD.[2] Four oil tankers were designated as blocked property in connection with the sanctions, which OFAC said targeted firms helping Venezuela evade existing restrictions.[2] Under the order, transactions by U.S. persons involving blocked or designated persons are generally prohibited, and violations can result in civil or criminal penalties.[2]
