# Monosodium Glutamate

Monosodium glutamate (MSG, additive number E621) is a flavor enhancer that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described as "a slow poison" concealed behind numerous alternative names.[1] Because labeling standards do not require MSG to be listed by name, it can appear on ingredient panels as "natural flavoring," "yeast extract," "textured protein," "disodium guanylate," "disodium inosinate," or "hydrolyzed pea protein."[1]

## Use and reported effects

MSG is an amino acid used as a flavor enhancer in soups, dressings, frozen foods, seasonings, cookies, lunch meats, and in many restaurants.[1] Like [[aspartame]], it is characterized as an excitotoxin.[1] Studies cited in the article associate regular consumption with depression, disorientation, impaired vision, fatigue, headaches, and obesity.[1]

The article states that MSG affects neurological pathways in the brain and disengages the "I'm full" response, which is offered as the explanation for rapid weight gain among consumers.[1] The harmful component is identified as the glutamate fraction: glutamic acid found in corn, molasses, or wheat is broken down through processes such as hydrogenation or through fermentation with strong chemicals, bacteria, and enzymes.[1]

## Contested evidence

DeLarme acknowledged that MSG has well-established positive gustatory and psychological effects, as well as reported benefits with regard to hypertension and iron deficiency.[1] At the same time, he pointed to abundant reports of harmful effects including oxidative stress, DNA damage, protein modification, and lysis of stromal cells.[1]
