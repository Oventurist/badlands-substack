# 2017 Trump Twitter Account Deactivation

On **3 November 2017**, the [[twitter|Twitter]] account of sitting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was taken down.[1] The incident is examined at length in Chapter 10 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series, which treats it as a deliberate [[deep-state|Deep State]] action rather than the act of a rogue employee.[1]

## Q's treatment of the event

Drop #73 of 4 November 2017 asks readers when Trump's Twitter account was taken down, and whether anything comparable had ever happened before.[1] The Q for Dummies author answers that it had not, and argues that the attempt was made because hostile actors had realised the [[q-operation|Q Operation]] was real and that measures were needed to slow it down — reading Trump's own remark, "I guess the word must finally be getting out - and having an impact," as a reference to Q.[1]

The date is emphasised: 3 November, rendered 11-3, is tied to the well-known "Military is the only way / 11.3" drop, and the author notes that taking down the Twitter account of a sitting President could be considered an act of war.[1] He states that he holds working theories about the incident, including the possibility that it set off a chain of events leading to the declaration of the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act]] and ultimately to the rollout of [[devolution|Devolution]], while conceding the theory is not yet ready for publication.[1]

## Tracking and physical security

The same drop raises the subject of "tracking," which the commentary connects back to drop #65 and its claim that the Deep State had installed geo-tracking software on Trump's Twitter account, allowing his exact location to be identified at any time.[1] Trump was at that point travelling to Asia with a stop in Hawaii, and Q asks why uniformed military personnel were protecting him.[1] The author offers two possibilities — a credible threat against the President combined with distrust of the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]], or a show of force by Trump ahead of events in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] — while stating the exact reason is not known, and confirms that military guards do not ordinarily assist the Secret Service in presidential protection.[1]

Q further asks what flying object had recently been shot down; the answer given is a missile fired by the [[houthis|Houthi rebels]] toward Riyadh, followed by a question about the precision of non-civilian geo-tracking.[1] The author suggests this may have been an early warning tied to a later incident in the drops in which, in his reading, the Deep State attempted a missile attack on [[air-force-one|Air Force One]] that was thwarted.[1]

## Reinterpretation in Chapter 15: the go-code theory

Chapter 15 of the same series revisits the deactivation and recasts it as an attempt to block a presidential signal rather than merely to silence Trump.[2] Citing drop #66, in which Q states that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] had attacked the Commander in Chief, the author argues that the "rogue employee" blamed publicly for the takedown was in fact a CIA asset, and that the object of the operation was to prevent Trump from sending a coded tweet functioning as the go-code for the removal of the Saudi royal family.[2] On this reading, the [[deep-state|Deep State]] attacked a sitting president to stop him from carrying out the purge of corruption in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[2]

The chapter connects the episode to [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Al Waleed bin Talal]], noting his onetime ownership stake in Twitter and alleging that he financed [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s university education and rise to the Oval Office; the author argues that the [[saudi-purge|Saudi purge]] therefore did not merely clean out Saudi corruption but "cut the strings" of those profiting from ties to the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]].[2]

The author lays out a sequence: the Twitter takedown to block the go-code, Q's identification of it as an attack on POTUS, Trump publicly calling it out, Trump then sending the Saudi go-code tweet, the Saudi purge, the first Q stringer, Obama's coded tweet, a mass shooting, and a second Obama tweet read as a veiled threat.[2]

## The 11.3 marker claim

Chapter 15 dates the takedown to 11.2 and Trump's verifying tweet to 11.3, and argues this satisfies drop #26's condition that "11.3 verifies as first marker."[2] The significance claimed is legal as well as symbolic: because an attack on a sitting president in office constitutes an act of war, the author contends it furnished Trump with the precedent to act as a "war time President," opening options including the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act]], [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]], or [[devolution|Devolution]].[2] This dating differs from the 3 November date given earlier in the series from Chapter 10.[1][2]

## Chapter 16 treatment

Chapter 16 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series revisits the deactivation while unpacking [[q-drop-111|drop #111]], which asks what POTUS's last tweet was before the events in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and why his account was taken down.[3] The chapter asserts that Trump's final tweet before the [[saudi-purge|Saudi purge]] was a coded message giving the green light for the takedown, and that the removal of his account for eleven minutes in the early hours of November 3, 2017 was an attempt to shut him down and keep that message from going out.[3] The "rogue employee" explanation is rejected outright.[3]

The author repeats that taking down the account in order to subvert an operation freeing the United States from foreign shackles could be considered an act of war, and reiterates his speculation from the previous chapter that the tweet may itself have been the "11.3" marker.[3] Q's follow-up question about who was taken down is answered as [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]], the majority stakeholder in [[twitter|Twitter]] and owner of the top floors of the Mandalay Bay hotel from which the [[las-vegas-shooting|Las Vegas shooting]] originated.[3]

## Chapter 8 treatment: the tracking claim

Chapter 8 of the series, covering drops #61–67, presents the takedown as the first concrete strike of the war going hot, noting that a rogue [[twitter|Twitter]] employee removed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s account for eleven minutes and that Twitter's official explanation was that the removal was an accident — an explanation Q rejects.[4] The chapter emphasises that an earlier drop had already anticipated exactly this scenario of platform blackouts.[4]

Reading drop #65, the chapter argues that a three-letter agency, most likely the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], embedded tracking into the President's Twitter account in order to geo-locate him at will, and that the account was taken down precisely so the tracking software could be attached.[4] On this reading the incident was not trolling by a lone employee but an attempt to track the President, and Q's phrase "It has begun" marks the war becoming hot in public view.[4] Drop #66, in which Q answers an anon asking whether POTUS was being attacked by his own intelligence agencies, is read as confirming that white hat elements within the [[national-security-agency|NSA]] detected the attack and immediately informed the President, and that big tech was therefore involved as well.[4]
