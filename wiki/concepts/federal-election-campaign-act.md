# Federal Election Campaign Act

The Federal Election Campaign Act of 1971 is the federal statute regulating the financing of U.S. federal campaigns.[1] Among its provisions are caps on coordinated political spending by national party committees with their own candidates; for the 2025–26 cycle those limits ranged from $61,800 to $123,000 for House races and up to $3.7 million for Senate races.[1]

Those coordinated-spending limits were the subject of [[nrsc-v-fec|NRSC v. FEC]], argued at the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in December 2025, in which Republican party committees and Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]] argued the caps violate the First Amendment.[1] A federal appeals court had upheld the restrictions on the strength of a 2001 Supreme Court ruling before the case reached the justices.[1]
