# Operation Northwoods

Operation Northwoods refers to a declassified Cold War–era U.S. military planning document — often called the Northwoods Memorandum — that proposed staged provocations to build public support for action against [[cuba|Cuba]]. In Truth Community commentary the memorandum is treated as documentary proof that U.S. institutions have formally contemplated false flag operations, and its language is frequently cited as a template for interpreting later events.[1]

## The "generated instances" doctrine

The memorandum describes a false flag operation as being composed of "generated instances," instructing planners to "enable a logical build up of Generated Instances combined with other seemingly unrelated events to camouflage the ultimate objective and create the necessary impressions."[1] That phrasing — the deliberate accumulation of individually deniable incidents to produce a desired public perception — is the element most often invoked by analysts examining clusters of accidents or crises.[1]

## Invocation in Badlands commentary

In the March 19, 2024 Badlands News Brief, [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] applied the Northwoods framework to the wave of aviation incidents involving [[boeing|Boeing]] aircraft and [[united-airlines|United Airlines]], suggesting the pattern resembled a "logical build up of Generated Instances" rather than coincidence.[1] Esler linked the cluster to what he described as a controlled demolition of the airline industry serving the aims of the [[great-reset|Great Reset]], noting Denmark's announced green tax on flights and asking how [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]] could be implemented if people remained free to fly.[1]

## Authorship and rejection

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] emphasized that Operation Northwoods — a plan for a new "Maine incident," a false flag on the model used to justify the Spanish-American war, in order to justify an invasion of Cuba — was proposed not by the Kennedys but by General [[lyman-lemnitzer|Lyman Lemnitzer]], and had the support of the entire [[joint-chiefs-of-staff|Joint Chiefs of Staff]] and the [[cia|CIA]].[2] [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], horrified by the proposal, rejected it and soon fired Lemnitzer, who then took up the leadership of [[nato|NATO]] military forces in Paris.[2] Ehret raised this history against claims, attributed to [[marilyn-monroe|Marilyn Monroe]]'s unseen secret diary, that [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]] had planned an inside job killing American civilians to justify a Cuban invasion.[2] The Northwoods memorandum is dated 13 March 1962.[2]
