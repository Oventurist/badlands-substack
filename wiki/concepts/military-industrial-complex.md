# Military Industrial Complex

The Military Industrial Complex is named in "A Bright Red Line" as the interest that, together with the [[us-security-state|U.S. Security State]], determines which explanation for a war reaches the American public.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) As with Iraq, Afghanistan and nearly every other war of recent decades, the article argues, the only account offered for Russia's invasion of Ukraine is the one that best suits these institutions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Badlands describes the Ukraine war as another conflict instigated by the Military Industrial Complex, in which the public is repeatedly told that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] is a "mad dog" who delights in Ukrainian suffering, while the motivating factors behind the conflict — U.S.-sponsored regime change in Russia's neighbors, arms transfers, and insistence on [[nato|NATO]] expansion to Ukraine — are suppressed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article's broader claim is that propagandized real-life supervillains do not arise for no discernible reason, and that the omitted context is systematically kept from Western audiences.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Advocates of a new world war

In "A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation" (3 September 2025), [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that representatives of the military industrial complex, together with some [[rand-corporation|RAND Corporation]] think-tankers, appear to regard a new world war as an exciting and even winnable prospect on the basis of computer-modelled scenarios.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) He cites the chief executive of [[palantir|Palantir]] telling Americans to prepare for a three-front war with Russia, China and Iran.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) Ehret counters that such a war could never be won, since hypersonic missile technologies and underwater drones unveiled by Russia and China have made a [[nato|NATO]] first-strike monopoly a pipe dream.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Massie's 2023 "quiet part out loud" critique

In a December 2023 interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] argued that the military-industrial complex thrives on the sale of "deadly munitions," a trade that enriches shareholders "some of whom are congressmen."[3] He said the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] had sent Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] a letter urging more Ukraine spending to "revitalize our defense industrial base," accompanied by a list of states such as Alabama, Ohio and Texas that would receive munitions manufacturing work.[3] Massie characterized much of the Ukraine appropriation as being "laundered back to the military-industrial complex," inefficiently but profitably for lawmakers' districts and stock portfolios.[3]

## Ukraine escalation as a profit engine (2023)

In January 2023, as the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] declined to rule out sending F-16 fighter jets to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] following the announcement of 31 Abrams tanks, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the war had exposed a partisan inversion in which the Democratic Party and its progressive base had become the constituency sustaining the war machine.[4] He contended that beyond the money laundering and human trafficking possible under the fog of war, nothing generates more money than war itself, and that the sheer existence of the Military Industrial Complex is evidence enough of the incentive.[4] In his view, MIC leadership loses no sleep over whether a given war is justified or in America's interest, a pattern he described as historically consistent.[4]
