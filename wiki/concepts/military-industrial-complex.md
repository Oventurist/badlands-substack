# Military Industrial Complex

The Military Industrial Complex is named in "A Bright Red Line" as the interest that, together with the [[us-security-state|U.S. Security State]], determines which explanation for a war reaches the American public.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) As with Iraq, Afghanistan and nearly every other war of recent decades, the article argues, the only account offered for Russia's invasion of Ukraine is the one that best suits these institutions.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Badlands describes the Ukraine war as another conflict instigated by the Military Industrial Complex, in which the public is repeatedly told that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] is a "mad dog" who delights in Ukrainian suffering, while the motivating factors behind the conflict — U.S.-sponsored regime change in Russia's neighbors, arms transfers, and insistence on [[nato|NATO]] expansion to Ukraine — are suppressed.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article's broader claim is that propagandized real-life supervillains do not arise for no discernible reason, and that the omitted context is systematically kept from Western audiences.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Advocates of a new world war

In "A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation" (3 September 2025), [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that representatives of the military industrial complex, together with some [[rand-corporation|RAND Corporation]] think-tankers, appear to regard a new world war as an exciting and even winnable prospect on the basis of computer-modelled scenarios.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) He cites the chief executive of [[palantir|Palantir]] telling Americans to prepare for a three-front war with Russia, China and Iran.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) Ehret counters that such a war could never be won, since hypersonic missile technologies and underwater drones unveiled by Russia and China have made a [[nato|NATO]] first-strike monopoly a pipe dream.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Massie's 2023 "quiet part out loud" critique

In a December 2023 interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] argued that the military-industrial complex thrives on the sale of "deadly munitions," a trade that enriches shareholders "some of whom are congressmen."[3] He said the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] had sent Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] a letter urging more Ukraine spending to "revitalize our defense industrial base," accompanied by a list of states such as Alabama, Ohio and Texas that would receive munitions manufacturing work.[3] Massie characterized much of the Ukraine appropriation as being "laundered back to the military-industrial complex," inefficiently but profitably for lawmakers' districts and stock portfolios.[3]

## Ukraine escalation as a profit engine (2023)

In January 2023, as the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] declined to rule out sending F-16 fighter jets to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] following the announcement of 31 Abrams tanks, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the war had exposed a partisan inversion in which the Democratic Party and its progressive base had become the constituency sustaining the war machine.[4] He contended that beyond the money laundering and human trafficking possible under the fog of war, nothing generates more money than war itself, and that the sheer existence of the Military Industrial Complex is evidence enough of the incentive.[4] In his view, MIC leadership loses no sleep over whether a given war is justified or in America's interest, a pattern he described as historically consistent.[4]

## Bernie Sanders and defense manufacturing in Vermont

A 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] as a case study in the political economy of the military-industrial complex, arguing that Sanders rails against big defense corporations at rallies while omitting his own long entanglement with them.[6] Burlington's boom-era economy during his mayoralty was propped up in part by a [[general-electric|General Electric]] plant — GE then among the nation's largest defense contractors — and by the "profligate military spending" Sanders campaigned against.[6] The plant employed three thousand workers, had nearly doubled its defense contracts by 1986, and produced Vulcan Gatling guns for attack helicopters and fighter jets.[6] When his own progressive supporters picketed the facility, Sanders sided with GE executives, denounced the protests, and had some protesters arrested.[6] He later brought a [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] research center to [[burlington-vermont|Burlington]] and secured the basing of 18 [[f-35|F-35]] fighter jets at the city's airport for the Vermont National Guard, leading the World Socialist Web Site to call him a "silent partner of American militarism."[6]

## The 1968 fork

Badlands contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes 1968 as the year in which endless sums of money were absorbed into the American military industrial complex that [[john-f-kennedy|John Kennedy]], [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] and [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert Kennedy]] had died resisting.[7] The complex is presented as one pole of a choice between two futures: a space-based economic system embodied by the [[apollo-program|Apollo program]], and a dystopian path of race riots and an insane war in [[vietnam-war|Vietnam]].[7] With the choice made, the moon program was killed in 1973 and the New Left came to equate the space program, atomic technology and the American constitution itself with the evils of the war machine.[7]

## Operation Cyclone and the War on Terror

Badlands Media argues that when it comes to the Military Industrial Complex, war is good for business, and long, drawn-out wars are preferable to brief conflicts.9 By the mid-1980s it was becoming clear the Cold War would not last forever, and the article frames [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] as the bridge the complex used — alongside the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and the security state — to move from endless Cold War to the endless [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]].9

## Origin of the term and the congressional edit

"War Pigs" traces the phrase military-industrial-congressional complex (MICC) to the farewell address delivered by President [[dwight-eisenhower|Dwight Eisenhower]] from the White House on 17 January 1961, written by Malcolm Moos and Ralph E. Williams; before Eisenhower gave the speech, the reference to Congress was edited out.[13] The same concept is also referred to as the [[permanent-war-economy|permanent war economy]].[13] The warning was not given much attention at the time, presumably because the CIA's [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]] had already given the Deep State a high degree of control over the corporate media, the primary tool used to drum up support for war.[13]

Despite a rise in public awareness of the MICC's exploits, the speech still falls on deaf ears every time a new war arrives with a good marketing campaign; a few traumatic rounds of [[atrocity-propaganda|atrocity propaganda]] are enough to rally the public behind what the article calls a war-economy cash cow.[13]

## Sub-complexes and money flows

The MICC is made up of many moving parts and sub-complexes, one of which is the [[military-industrial-media-complex|military-industrial-media complex]]; the role of commercially controlled media in promoting war and militarism is described as essential to the system's operation.[13] For years [[general-electric|General Electric]] owned 49% of [[nbc|NBC]] while subcontracting on Tomahawk and Patriot II missiles and manufacturing components for the B-2, B-52 and E-3 AWACS aircraft, taking roughly $2 billion in related defence contracts.[13]

When large appropriations bills move taxpayer dollars, "War Pigs" argues, there are six ways from Sunday that money can be skimmed, diverted or appropriated — a fact as true of humanitarian aid as of military support packages.[13] The congressional component of the MICC receives its cut from the donor class, and the donor class gets what it pays for from the politicians it backs.[13] This, the article contends, explains why the complex's propagandists can profitably cultivate support for both sides of a war even when Washington funds only one.[13]

## The 2023 Israel–Palestine application

The article applies the framework to the [[israel-hamas-war|Israel–Hamas war]], arguing that the permanent political class backed Israel in near-unison despite majority public opposition, and that when officials ignore constituents the real employer is the donor class, particularly the military-industrial complex.[13] Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] is singled out as the archetypal congressional shill for the complex.[13]

## Wall Street's calm and defense stocks (October 2023)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] observed that while the world was in chaos after the October 7 attack, the stock market held steady and Wall Street appeared more concerned with 0.25% interest rate increases due to inflation.[15] War and terrorism mean defense stocks go up, he wrote, but given the professed uncertainty the absence of panic suggested investors knew exactly what was going on — "almost like it has been planned, or they at least know it was planned."[15]

He also argued that the Iraq and Afghanistan wars launched after the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]] served the [[cia|CIA]]'s drug running operation and profited the military industrial complex, at a cost of 7,000 US service members killed and countless others dead as a result.[15]

## Ukraine, Israel, and Syria as a single theater (December 2024)

A December 2024 Badlands Media analysis framed the military-industrial complex — the term coined by President [[dwight-d-eisenhower|Dwight D. Eisenhower]] in 1961 — as a set of shadow entities that have for over a century manipulated governments, corporations, and media to maintain global dominance, thriving on war, fear, and control.[12] The piece treated Ukraine, Israel, and Syria not as separate crises but as three theaters of a single century-long agenda, each used to keep conflict profitable and nations locked in the complex's orbit.[12]

The article set out a four-stage playbook. First, the MIC creates or exaggerates threats to justify military action, through orchestrated uprisings, false flags, or media manipulation — with the 1964 [[gulf-of-tonkin-incident|Gulf of Tonkin incident]] and the unsubstantiated weapons-of-mass-destruction claims preceding the 2003 Iraq War given as examples.[12] Second, it manipulates policy through lobbying, think tanks, and compromised leaders, with the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]] named as vehicles that shaped U.S. foreign policy toward perpetual war.[12] Third, it exploits resources after the conflict, as when U.S. oil companies profited from Iraq's oil fields and contractors such as [[halliburton]] earned billions from reconstruction work.[12] Fourth, it relies on media propaganda, with firms like [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] and [[boeing]] exercising influence over mainstream outlets through advertising budgets and strategic partnerships.[12]

Beyond war zones, the complex is described as controlling the global arms trade, accounting for over 40 percent of world arms exports with the United States leading, and counting Saudi Arabia, Israel, and Ukraine among its top clients.[12] U.S. and UK arms sales to Saudi Arabia for the war in Yemen from 2015 onward continued despite condemnation of civilian casualties, and [[nato]] expansion is portrayed as creating new markets for American defense contractors as member states are pressured to buy U.S.-made weapons under the banner of alliance security.[12] Financially, [[blackrock]] and [[state-street|State Street]] are named as giants instrumental in funding the complex, while the rise of [[brics|BRICS+]] is presented as an alternative to the Western financial system that undermines its control of global markets.[12]
