# Maneuver Warfare

**Maneuver warfare** is a military doctrine emphasizing rapid repositioning, flanking and envelopment over frontal attrition. It was fundamentally implemented in Prussian doctrine by the 19th-century field marshal [[helmuth-von-moltke|Helmuth von Moltke]], described as embodying Prussian military organization and tactical genius.[1]

## Moltke's formulation

Unlike [[blitzkrieg]], which seeks total annihilation through rapid brute force, Moltke still valued the prolonged siege; his innovation was recognizing that railways were a unique technology allowing commanders to deploy troops quickly and outflank enemies in a double envelopment, thereby setting the conditions for a longer siege.[1] The Nazis later combined Moltke's maneuver concepts with [[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]]'s [[preventive-war|preventive war]] doctrine to produce blitzkrieg.[1]

## Russian mastery

Badlands analysis holds that the Russians ultimately established themselves as the masters of maneuver warfare, defeating blitzkrieg through the development of elastic "shoulder defense."[1] That lineage was invoked in June 2024 in assessing a possible Israeli mechanized campaign in Lebanon against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], which by then was described as a close ally of both the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] and the Russian military.[1]
