# 2024 Des Moines Register Iowa Poll Miss

The 2024 Des Moines Register Iowa Poll miss refers to the final pre-election survey conducted by pollster [[ann-selzer|J. Ann Selzer]] for [[des-moines-register|The Des Moines Register]], which understated [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s support in Iowa by roughly 16 points and led Selzer to end her election polling operation.[1]

## The poll and the result

The Register's final poll before Election Day showed Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] leading Trump 47–44 percent among likely Iowa voters, a bombshell result that raised speculation about a stunning upset and buoyed Democratic hopes in the closing days of the race.[1] Other surveys had found Trump comfortably ahead in the state, but the Selzer poll was widely amplified by other outlets.[1]

Trump ultimately carried Iowa by 13 points, 56–43 percent, over Harris.[1] Selzer subsequently acknowledged the survey was a "big miss" and suggested it might have "actually energize[d] and activate[d] Republican voters who thought they would likely coast to victory."[1]

Kristin Roberts, chief content officer of Gannett Media, which owns the Register, said the Iowa Poll would "evolve as we find new ways to accurately capture public sentiment and the pulse of Iowans on state and national issues," adding that the paper's mission is to provide trusted news and that "we did not deliver on that promise when we shared results of the last Des Moines Register Iowa Poll."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media characterized the survey as an overt suppression poll that dominated headlines in the days before Election Day, and greeted Selzer's retirement with "good riddance."[1] The commentary observed that a poll long considered "the gold standard" had missed by 16 points, and drew an analogy to Colorado elections, which the outlet said have been called the gold standard for a decade despite, in its view, not producing a verifiably accurate result in a generation or longer.[1] It concluded that the phrase "gold standard" no longer connotes soundness or reliability in modern usage.[1]
