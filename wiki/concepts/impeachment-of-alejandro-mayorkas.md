# Impeachment of Alejandro Mayorkas

The impeachment of Alejandro Mayorkas was a 2024 effort by House Republicans to remove the Secretary of Homeland Security from office over the administration's handling of the southern border.[1] On Sunday, January 28, 2024, House Republicans released two articles of impeachment against [[alejandro-mayorkas|Mayorkas]], accusing him of "willful and systemic refusal to comply with the law" and of "breach of public trust," taking a further step toward a historic removal attempt while Mayorkas denied wrongdoing.[1]

## The articles

House Homeland Security Chairman [[mark-green|Mark Green]] said the articles laid out "a clear, compelling, and irrefutable case," alleging that Mayorkas had willfully and systemically refused to comply with immigration laws enacted by Congress, breached the public trust by knowingly making false statements to Congress and the American people, and obstructed congressional oversight of his department.[1]

## Response from DHS

Mayorkas had long been a focus of Republican criticism of White House border policies and had testified before Congress multiple times.[1] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] maintained that no high crimes or misdemeanors had been committed under the Biden administration, and DHS officials issued a memo dismissing the GOP-led House investigation as unconstitutional and "evidence-free" while rebutting the allegations in detail.[1]

## The February 13, 2024 vote

The Republican-led House narrowly impeached Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Mayorkas]] on the night of Tuesday, February 13, 2024, over what members claimed was his failure to enforce border laws amid a "crisis" of high illegal immigration — a characterization Mayorkas dismissed as "baseless."[2] The vote made him only the second Cabinet officer impeached since the founding of the country, after Secretary of War William Belknap in 1876.[2] It came after an initial attempt the previous week had failed on the same accusations.[2]

Some Republicans and many Democrats criticized the action as resting on policy disagreements rather than allegations of specific crimes, while conservatives also sought to portray Mayorkas as incompetent.[2] The articles were expected to be rejected by the Democratic majority in the [[united-states-senate|Senate]].[2] In a statement after the vote, Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] blamed Mayorkas for "fueling the worst border catastrophe in American history," adding that "[he] deserves to be impeached, and Congress has a constitutional obligation to do so" and that "next to a declaration of war, impeachment is arguably the most serious authority given to the House and we have treated this matter accordingly."[2]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Republicans had campaigned on impeaching Mayorkas in 2022 to such a degree that Republican voters expected articles to drop immediately upon the transfer of the speaker's gavel; the gavel transfer was instead delayed through 15 rounds of voting, and in the distraction the impeachment push fizzled.[1] She argued it was unsurprising that Republicans moved to make good on the prior cycle's campaign promise before the first month of an election year closed, remarking that it was no coincidence the border became important again with the House up for reelection.[1]

She further noted the countervailing Democratic ambition: President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was seeking a deal that would "secure" the border in exchange for Ukraine funding, which she characterized as holding Americans hostage to expand a money-laundering operation.[1] Her conclusion was that nowhere in the moves and countermoves of either party was there consideration of the ongoing harm of open borders on Americans.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the impeachment as the "chaser" to an [[axios|Axios]] report on the administration's border dysfunction published the same week.[2] He traced the border problem to the 2020 televised debates, where he argued [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] baited Biden into openly deriding the idea of a strong sovereign border and inviting migrants to "surge" during his coming tenure.[2] In his reading, the convergence of narratives on the doorstep of the 2024 election left the establishment two unenviable options: get Biden out of the [[white-house|White House]], or go down with the ship.[2]

## Second House vote and context

On Tuesday, February 13, 2024, House Republicans voted to impeach Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] over his border record in a second attempt after an initial failed vote.[3] The impeachment coincided with a fiscal crisis at [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]], where officials drafted plans to release thousands of detainees and cut detention capacity from 38,000 to 22,000 beds after the collapse of the [[bipartisan-border-bill-2024|bipartisan border bill]] that would have erased a $700 million shortfall.[3] Immigration was at the time President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s worst-rated issue in polling.[3] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the impeachment and the ICE budget story functioned together as "Narrative Pincers" in the fight over domestic versus foreign defense funding.[3]

## Committee proceedings begin (January 2024)

On January 3, 2024, a GOP source told CNN that House Republicans would forge ahead with steps to impeach Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] over his handling of the border crisis.[4] A spokesperson for the House Committee on Homeland Security said the panel had conducted a comprehensive investigation for nearly a year into "Secretary Mayorkas' handling of, and role in, the unprecedented crisis at the Southwest border," and that following the bipartisan House vote to refer articles of impeachment to the committee it would conduct hearings and take up those articles in the coming weeks.[4]

The announcement came as immigration shaped up to be a top issue in the 2024 presidential election, with Republicans attacking President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s immigration policies.[4] House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] and Republican colleagues visited one of the busiest sections of the U.S.–Mexico border that same week, days after border authorities contended with a fresh surge of migrants.[4] A senior administration official responded by accusing Johnson of "playing games" and urged him to "stop playing politics" instead of working to pass meaningful reforms.[4]
