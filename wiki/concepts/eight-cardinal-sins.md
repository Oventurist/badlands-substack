# Eight Cardinal Sins of Election Administration

The **Eight Cardinal Sins** is a taxonomy devised by election analyst [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] to catalogue election-administration practices that, in his assessment, degrade election integrity.[1] He selected the eight specifically because they are *not* universally shared among all fifty states — unlike electronic voting systems, which are used everywhere and therefore cannot differentiate states from one another in a comparative model.[1]

## The eight items

Keshel's list comprises [[automatic-voter-registration|Automatic Voter Registration]], [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]], excessive [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]], prolonged counting, [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice voting]], same-day voter registration, "unique corruption," and the absence of [[voter-id|voter ID]] requirements.[1]

## Use as a measurement instrument

Within the [[smart-goal-setting|SMART goal-setting]] framework Keshel applied to election reform in February 2025, the Eight Cardinal Sins serve as the "measurable" component: because there are only so many categories of things that can go wrong in elections, identifying where the demerits are most concentrated relative to other states allows progress to be tracked and reform effort to be prioritized.[1] Keshel has published state-by-state analyses of how these demerits accumulate in both blue and red states in his journal [[captains-corner|Captain K's Corner]], and argues that corruption of [[voter-registration|voter registration]] is the single strongest indicator that a state will vote blue in a given election.[1]
