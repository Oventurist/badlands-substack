# Rupert's Land

Rupert's Land was the vast private territory of the [[hudsons-bay-company|Hudson's Bay Company]], chartered in 1670 by Prince Rupert, which comprised the majority of what is now [[canada|Canada]] in the period before 1867.[1] It separated Britain's eastern North American colonies from the isolated western colony of [[british-columbia|British Columbia]] by roughly 3,000 kilometres of undeveloped wilderness.[1]

Matthew Ehret argues that for nearly two centuries this private land was managed by Jesuits and Anglican agents of the Empire who intentionally kept the North American frontier unexplored, so that Canada would remain a land of furs without industry or infrastructure.[1] Transferring these possessions into federal hands was, in his reconstruction, a central task of British strategy in the 1865-1867 period.[1]

On 18 July 1868 the Hudson Bay territories were sold to Ottawa under an operation led by [[georges-etienne-cartier|Sir Georges-Etienne Cartier]].[1] In the decades that followed, Saskatchewan and Alberta were created as provinces where there had formerly been only Hudson's Bay land.[1]
