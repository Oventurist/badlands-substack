# Motion to Vacate

The motion to vacate the chair is the parliamentary mechanism by which a member of the United States House of Representatives may force a vote on removing the sitting Speaker. Its practical importance rose sharply in the 118th Congress, when the threshold for offering the motion was lowered as part of the rules concessions that secured [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] the Speakership in January 2023.[1]

## Origins in the 2023 Speaker fight

During the January 2023 Speaker election, Representative [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] and roughly twenty other members withheld their votes across fifteen ballots in order to extract commitments on House rules and procedure for the 118th Congress.[1] A central element of that negotiation was the elimination of omnibus bills and [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolutions]], alongside procedural changes that made the Speaker more easily removable; McCarthy accepted the terms and won the gavel.[1]

## October 2023 threat against McCarthy

On October 1, 2023, Gaetz announced on ABC's "This Week" that he would file a motion to vacate that week, after reporting that McCarthy had negotiated Ukraine funding with Democrats without informing his own conference until the 45-day continuing resolution had already passed the House.[1] McCarthy, interviewed the same day on CBS News' "Face the Nation," dismissed the threat as "personal with Matt" and said, "I'll survive."[1]

## Interpretations

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that McCarthy had broken both the January agreement and the House rules, proving that "the machine will do whatever it takes to get whatever it wants: More billions for Ukraine."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the confrontation as [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] — scripted opposition in which both Gaetz and McCarthy occupied roles chosen by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], with the resulting spectacle serving to expose the [[uniparty|uniparty]] rather than to resolve a genuine dispute.[1]

## Origin in the January 2023 concessions

The single-member threshold arose directly from the [[january-2023-mccarthy-concessions|January 2023 McCarthy concessions]]: under the agreement any individual member could call for a motion to vacate the speaker's chair, where the prior rule had required five members to put forth such a vote.[2] Badlands Media contributor Erik Carlson listed this first among the concessions [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] granted to the [[house-freedom-caucus|House Freedom Caucus]] to secure the speakership on the fifteenth ballot.[2]
