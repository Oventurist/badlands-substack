# State of Georgia v. Trump, et al.

State of Georgia v. Trump, et al. was the Fulton County racketeering prosecution brought over the 2020 presidential election results in Georgia. Badlands describes the case as having been manufactured by District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] and her romantic partner, special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[1]

## Course of the case

On August 24, 2023, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] surrendered himself to authorities at the Fulton County Jail after being indicted on racketeering and related charges tied to the 2020 election results in Georgia; the booking produced what Badlands called "the most infamous mugshot of all time."[1] The case was dismissed on November 25, 2025.[1]

## Post-dismissal disclosures

Even after dismissal, the record continued to generate revelations. In January 2026, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] lifted the protective order on the special grand jury transcripts.[1] Among them was the September 6, 2022 transcribed interview of former Senator [[david-perdue|David Perdue]], examined by Wade, in which Perdue alleged that Governor [[brian-kemp|Brian Kemp]] had directed the [[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|Georgia Bureau of Investigation]] not to investigate the election and had invoked being a "team player."[1] Badlands writer [[canncon|CannCon]] published the exclusive report on the transcript.[1]

## Stall of the prosecution (2024)

By August 2024, one year after the indictment, the case had stalled with no chance of going to trial before the end of that year.[2] District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] had used Georgia's anti-racketeering statute to allege that Trump and 18 others participated in a wide-ranging scheme to subvert the will of the state's voters, in what was the fourth and most sprawling of the criminal cases against the former president after his narrow loss of Georgia to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[2] Although Willis's team notched early victories, explosive allegations raised by one of Trump's co-defendants in early 2024 produced delays that reporters said could derail the prosecution.[2] Trump and other defendants appealed McAfee's ruling, and the appeals court barred him from taking further action in the case while the appeal remained pending.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the anniversary coverage itself as the story, writing that "there isn't any news about Fani's RICO because Fani's RICO is stalled indefinitely," and framing the case as part of a broader failed [[lawfare]] campaign against Trump.[2]

## Fundraising effect of the booking photograph

The Atlanta booking produced an immediate and record-breaking fundraising surge for Trump's campaign, which reported raising $7.1 million in the days after he was processed at the jail on Thursday evening, August 24, 2023.[3] On the Friday alone the campaign took in $4.18 million, the single highest 24-hour period of the campaign to that date according to a person familiar with the totals.[3] The haul was powered largely by merchandise sold through the campaign's online store — shirts, posters, bumper stickers and beverage coolers bearing the scowling mugshot and the tagline "NEVER SURRENDER!", priced between $12 and $34.[3]

Trump subsequently marketed posters signed in gold, telling supporters by email that other sellers were "probably Biden cronies just trying to make money off of my sham arrest" and that his official posters carried his signature in gold as proof of authenticity from "the ONLY U.S. President who was so committed to our cause that he was willing to get wrongfully ARRESTED."[3] Ashe in America called it "a hilarious troll" and conceded she "sort of" wanted one, adding that Trump "really is the best marketer."[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the weekend had made the mugshot the most memetic image in American history and had solidified Trump as the ultimate enemy of the establishment and ally of the American people.[3]

## February 2024 disqualification proceedings

The case, brought by Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] and Special Prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and eighteen others, was consumed in February 2024 by defense efforts to disqualify the district attorney.[4] Trump's team fought with Willis over Wade's cellphone records, which could potentially show when their romantic relationship began, filing a reply in Fulton County Superior Court on Monday, February 26 stating that its investigator would only summarize AT&T data obtained under a lawfully issued subpoena and cell tower location information, and noting that the state had not addressed two specific dates.[4] Acceptance of the evidence was expected to extend the evidentiary hearing arising from [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]]'s motion.[4]

That same evening, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] ruled that certain communications between Wade and his former law partner [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] were not covered by attorney-client privilege, meaning Bradley could be compelled to testify about the relationship as defense attorneys sought Willis's disqualification.[4] Summations in Roman's motion were scheduled for Friday, March 1, 2024.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the underlying prosecution as "a novel legal endeavor to persecute, I mean prosecute, a president and others for political speech and association," and argued that the emerging evidence suggested Willis and Wade had perjured themselves before the court.[4]

## February 27, 2024 disqualification hearing

At the hearing on Tuesday, February 27, 2024, key witness [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] — [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]]'s former divorce lawyer and law partner — testified that he did not know when the romantic relationship between Wade and [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] began and had "never witnessed anything," a potential setback for the defense.[5] Defense counsel impeached him with January text messages to [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]] in which he said the romance "absolutely" predated Wade's November 1, 2021 hiring.[5] [[donald-trump|Trump]] attorney [[steve-sadow|Steve Sadow]] led an aggressive cross-examination, and the movants established that Bradley had reviewed and edited Merchant's motion and approved it as "looks good."[5] The hearing was scheduled to resume that Friday at 1 p.m. ET.[5]

## The August 24, 2023 surrender

Trump surrendered at the Fulton County jail on August 24, 2023, an event [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] described as needing no explanatory link because its meaning was self-evident to readers.[6] Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump "was paraded in front of the American people as a criminal. And he has never. Been. Stronger."[6] What occurred "in the realm of the Actual was unprecedented, literally," he argued, but what occurred "in the realm of the Narrative was transcendent," with Trump seizing control of the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]] and converging informational and psychological timelines.[6] In the preceding 24 hours, Burning Bright contended, Trump had done worse than destroy the remnants of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] — he had rendered it irrelevant — and would do the same to the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[6]

Coverage in the same period noted that the arraignment had not damaged Trump politically: his primary polling margins stood at fifty points or more, and outlets including [[cnn|CNN]] and [[time-magazine|Time]] had begun preparing readers for a possible second Trump administration.[6] Commentary published by [[american-greatness|American Greatness]] observed that if Trump's mugshot were taken, "hardly anyone will remember the sideshow in Milwaukee" — the [[first-gop-presidential-debate-2023|first GOP primary debate]] held the night before.[6]

Days later, the case's prosecutor, [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]], came under scrutiny when November 2020 Facebook posts surfaced showing her raising election-irregularity concerns similar to those charged against Trump, prompting [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] to argue that officials who certified the 2020 result while aware of its defects were more plausible defendants than Trump.[6]

## March 2024 pretrial motions hearing

On March 28, 2024, Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] heard a motion by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and two pretrial motions by co-defendant [[david-shafer|David Shafer]], a session centered on technical legal arguments that marked something of a return to normalcy after the case was rocked by allegations that District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] improperly benefited from her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[7]

Trump's lead lawyer, [[steve-sadow|Steve Sadow]], argued that the charges sought to criminalize political speech and advocacy conduct protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]], telling the court, "There is nothing alleged factually against President Trump that is not political speech," and that a sitting president expressing concerns about an election is "the height of political speech" — protected even if what was said proved false.[7] Prosecutor [[donald-wakeford|Donald Wakeford]] countered that the statements were integral to criminal activity and therefore unprotected, arguing that Trump could not "employ his speech and his expression and his statements as part of a criminal conspiracy to violate Georgia's RICO statute, to impersonate public officers, to file false documents, to make false statements to the government."[7] Wakeford noted that similar arguments had been rejected in the federal case brought by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], where Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]] ruled in December that the First Amendment "does not protect speech that is used as an instrument of a crime."[7]

Shafer's attorneys argued that he had acted on advice of counsel in conduct lawful at the time, and separately moved to strike indictment language characterizing guilt versus innocence on the ground that it would taint the jury pool.[7] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the proceedings "a master class in RICO."[7]

## February 2024 disqualification filings

On Tuesday, February 13, 2024, co-defendant [[david-shafer|David Shafer]] filed additional evidence supporting the motion to disqualify Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] and special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] from prosecuting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his 18 co-defendants.[8] The filing accused Wade of lying in his divorce proceedings by initially answering "none" on May 30, 2023 to interrogatories about extramarital sexual relations, then revising those answers in January 2024 — after the "improper relationship" allegations surfaced — to assert privilege under O.C.G.A. Sec. 24-5-505.[8]

## February 2024 disqualification hearing

On Monday, February 12, 2024, Fulton County Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] said it was "possible" that misconduct allegations against District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] "could result in disqualification" if evidence showed she had financially benefited from the case, and confirmed that an evidentiary hearing would proceed as scheduled that Thursday at 9:30 a.m. Eastern.[10] The hearing arose from a motion by Trump co-defendant [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]], filed the previous month, seeking to dismiss the election charges against him and disqualify Willis on the grounds that she had engaged in a "personal, romantic relationship" with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], one of her top prosecutors on the case, allegedly resulting in financial gain for both.[10] Willis, Wade and a number of employees of the district attorney's office were subpoenaed to testify.[10]

McAfee framed the question narrowly: "The state has admitted a relationship existed. And so what remains to be proven is the existence and extent of any financial benefit, again if there even was one. And so because I think it's possible that the facts alleged by the defendant could result in disqualification, I think an evidentiary hearing must occur to establish the record on those core allegations."[10] He cautioned that testimony from Willis herself was not a certainty, adding, "We're not talking about calling Ms. Willis as the first witness, and we need to get over a few procedural hurdles before we can get there."[10]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the RICO prosecution as "very silly" and predicted the hearing would be "popcorn worthy," noting that the previous hearing on co-defendant [[harrison-floyd|Harrison Floyd]]'s evidentiary questions had raised real issues about the veracity of Fulton County's election procedures — issues that might debunk the "big lie" framing of the defendants' intent and show their actions had legitimate, lawful cause.[10] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] separately argued that the Georgia case was "floundering" amid the Willis–Wade allegations, part of a broader war of attrition in which [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might prevail not through vindication but by outlasting prosecutions collapsing under their own corruption and incompetence.[10]

## May 2024 primary results for the case's principals

In May 2024, both principals in the Georgia case prevailed at the ballot box: District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] won the Democratic primary against progressive attorney Christian Wise Smith, and Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] won a nonpartisan election for a full four-year term beginning in January 2025.[11] Willis was set to face Republican [[courtney-kramer|Courtney Kramer]] in the fall general election.[11] The sprawling racketeering case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and others had substantially raised Willis's public profile, and a defense appeal of McAfee's ruling forcing special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] off the case remained pending before the Georgia Court of Appeals.[11]

## Televised arraignments and the first surrenders (August 2023)

On Tuesday, August 22, 2023, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] granted a request from four local television stations to bring live cameras and other recording devices into his courtroom through September 8, making the defendants' arraignments the first of [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s criminal proceedings to be televised.[12] District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] was seeking to hold the arraignments for all defendants the week following Labor Day, which fell within that window; if the timeline slipped, McAfee's order would expire, and the order did not indicate whether cameras would be permitted at trial or in other future proceedings.[12] Willis had charged Trump the previous week alongside 18 co-defendants over their alleged efforts to overturn the 2020 election results, and Trump denied any wrongdoing.[12]

The same day, Atlanta bail bondsman [[scott-hall|Scott Hall]] became the first co-defendant to surrender, turning himself in at the Fulton County jail on charges related to the [[coffee-county-voting-systems-breach|Coffee County voting systems breach]]; he had signed a bond agreement with Willis on Monday, was given an inmate identification number, and was released after about an hour, in a process seen as the template for the remaining defendants.[12] Also on Tuesday, [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]] asked a federal judge to block his arrest while he sought removal of his charges to federal court, and a judge ordered Willis to respond in writing by 3 p.m. Wednesday, with a hearing on removal set for the following Monday.[12]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that while "the Revolution won't be televised," Trump had become "the symbol of that rising sovereign sentiment within the American Mind," and that his revolution would be televised: "The Trial of Donald Trump isn't going to be the end of the story, but the beginning of one."[12]

## The August 14, 2023 indictment

The Atlanta grand jury handed down its indictment on Monday, August 14, 2023, marking the fourth criminal indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; the 98-page charging instrument named him and 18 allies on 13 counts and was issued with the jurors' names unredacted.[13] Prosecutors alleged that Trump and his co-defendants had orchestrated a sweeping criminal enterprise involving more than a dozen felonies as he tried and failed to overturn his defeat in Georgia's 2020 election.[13] Named defendants included [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]], [[sidney-powell|Sidney Powell]] and [[john-eastman|John Eastman]], and the documents additionally listed 30 unindicted co-conspirators.[13]

Earlier the same day, [[reuters|Reuters]] reported that a document had leaked onto the Fulton County court's website showing Trump indicted on racketeering charges before the grand jury had returned its verdict.[13] The Georgia district attorney's office released a statement calling the document "fictitious," a characterisation mocked by Trump's team and much of the internet; his team responded that "this was not a simple administrative mistake."[13]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] recalled [[just-human|Just Human]] saying on DEFECTED that he had tracked the lawfare narrative accurately because "everything that happened to Trump was boomeranging back on Biden" — foreign collusion, quid pro quo, and election fraud alike — and predicted that the public was about to receive a media crash course in racketeering law.[13] He argued that the co-conspirator list should be read as the roster of allies Trump would bring into battle, and that a "Disclosure Defense" strategy "might not just expose election fraud; it could set the template for the exact RICO case that starts the Deep State dominoes falling."[13]

## March 2024 partial dismissal of charges

On Wednesday, March 13, 2024, Fulton County Superior Court Judge Scott McAfee dismissed some of the charges against former President Donald Trump and his co-defendants, while leaving the sweeping racketeering indictment otherwise intact.[14] McAfee quashed six counts, three of them against Trump, the presumptive 2024 Republican presidential nominee, but left in place other counts including ten facing Trump, and held that prosecutors could seek a new indictment to reinstate the dismissed counts.[14] The judge grounded the dismissals in the prosecution's failure to provide sufficient detail about the alleged crimes.[14] It was the first time charges in any of Trump's four criminal cases had been dismissed, and the ruling was described as a blow to District Attorney Fani Willis, who was simultaneously facing an effort to disqualify her over a romantic relationship with a colleague.[14]

Badlands commentary noted that the quashed counts included those relating to the Brad Raffensperger phone call and to the defendants' oaths of office, and argued that the phone call had been arguably the strongest element of the state's case, even as the RICO charge and the prosecution as a whole survived.[14] The ruling resolved a pending motion unrelated to the disqualification question, with a decision on that separate matter expected imminently.[14]

## Bond hearings, August 21, 2023

On Monday, August 21, 2023, Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] set bond for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at $200,000, along with $100,000 each for attorneys [[john-eastman|John Eastman]] and [[kenneth-chesebro|Kenneth Chesebro]], $50,000 for [[ray-smith-iii|Ray Smith III]], and $10,000 for [[scott-hall|Scott Hall]].[15] Trump and the other eighteen defendants were given until noon that Friday to appear at the [[fulton-county-jail|Fulton County Jail]] for processing, and Trump said that evening on his own social media platform that he intended to surrender on Thursday.[15] Eastman was expected to surrender on Wednesday; the California judge overseeing his disbarment proceedings wrote in a Monday filing that "the court is willing to make certain changes in this week's trial to accommodate Dr. Eastman's surrender in Fulton County, Georgia."[15]

Every defendant's bond agreement contained a provision that the defendant "shall perform no act to intimidate any person known to him or her to be a codefendant or witness in this case or to otherwise obstruct the administration of justice," and Trump's agreement specified that this covered "no direct or indirect threat" against codefendants or witnesses — a term widely read as a constraint on his social media commentary.[15] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that on the eve of the public arrest and likely mug shot the mood among Trump's supporters was "excited" rather than reactive, that any anxiety in the air belonged to his political enemies, and invited readers to judge whether the week would make Trump look like part of the Establishment or an existential threat to it.[15]
