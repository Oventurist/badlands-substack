# Decisive Ecological Warfare

**Decisive Ecological Warfare** is the strategic doctrine published by [[deep-green-resistance|Deep Green Resistance]], the movement co-founded in 2011 by [[derrick-jensen|Derrick Jensen]].[1]

## Above grounders and below grounders

The doctrine divides adherents into two tiers. "Above grounders" work to build sustainable and just communities and use direct and indirect action to curb the excesses of those in power, reduce fossil fuel burning, and struggle for social and ecological justice.[1] "Under grounders," meanwhile, "engage in limited attacks on infrastructure, especially energy infrastructure, to try to reduce fossil fuel consumption and overall industrial activity."[1] The stated overall thrust is "to use selective attacks to accelerate collapse in a deliberate way."[1]

## Five-fold strategy

Deep Green Resistance sets out five strategies: (A) direct militant actions against industrial infrastructure, especially energy infrastructure; (B) participation in ongoing social and ecological justice struggles; (C) defense of land against industrial logging, mining and construction so more intact land survives civilization's collapse; (D) building and mobilizing resistance organizations through decentralized training, recruitment and logistical support; and (E) rebuilding a sustainable subsistence base including perennial polycultures and localized democratic communities.[1]

## Military guidance and the Underground Action Calendar

In a 2011 recruitment video, one devotee stated that the movement receives guidance from people involved in the military, who told them that "going after critical nodes of infrastructure is a classic technique that's been used hundreds or even thousands of times throughout history to win large scale conflicts, and especially asymmetric conflicts."[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] asks whether these activists suspect they are being played as pawns on a geopolitical chessboard, drawing a parallel to other militant movements he says have received U.S. training and support.[1]

The movement's news service maintains an "Underground Action Calendar" listing over one hundred cases of eco-terror acts against rail and energy infrastructure across the Americas and Europe.[1] Its opening remarks state that the calendar "exists to publicize and normalize the use of militant and underground tactics in the fight for justice and sustainability," including actions targeting infrastructure "because we believe this sort of action is necessary to dismantle civilization."[1]
