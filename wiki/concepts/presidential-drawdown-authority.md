# Presidential Drawdown Authority

Presidential Drawdown Authority is the mechanism through which the executive branch transfers weapons and equipment directly from existing United States military stocks to a foreign recipient. It became the principal channel for tens of billions of dollars in American assistance to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] following Russia's invasion.[1]

## Use in Ukraine aid

On August 14, 2023, the [[state-department|State Department]] announced a new security assistance package for Ukraine valued at $200 million, including air defense munitions, artillery rounds, mine-clearing equipment and anti-armor capabilities, in a statement issued by Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]].[1] The package was described as the first tranche of $6.2 billion in funds that became available after the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] discovered an accounting error.[1]

## The Pentagon accounting error

In May 2023 the Pentagon acknowledged that it had assigned a higher-than-warranted value to weaponry shipped to Kyiv, because staff had tabulated ammunition, missiles and other equipment at "replacement value" rather than "depreciated value."[1] The revised figure worked to Kyiv's benefit, since more equipment could be shipped under the same accounting ceiling, and Ukraine required materiel deliverable from U.S. stocks within days or weeks to continue repelling the Russian invasion.[1]

## Badlands critique

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the new tranche amounted to throwing good money after bad, coming a day after Badlands had reported that Ukraine's counteroffensive had failed to yield significant gains.[1] Her central objection was structural: because drawdown transfers are distributed without congressional approval while the underlying cost falls on taxpayers, she characterised the arrangement as "taxation without representation."[1]

## June 2023 $500 million package

On Tuesday, June 27, 2023, the Biden administration announced a new $500 million military aid package for Ukraine delivered via Presidential Drawdown Authority, meaning the shipments came directly from [[pentagon|Pentagon]] stockpiles.[2] The announcement came just after the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] reported that Ukrainian forces had failed to capitalise on the weekend turmoil caused by the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] uprising in Russia, with American officials and independent analysts saying there did not "seem to be any immediate defensive gaps to exploit" in Russian lines and that front lines were "likely to remain unchanged."[2] It also followed a tally that roughly 17 percent of the Bradley fighting vehicles already supplied to Ukraine had been damaged or destroyed.[2]
