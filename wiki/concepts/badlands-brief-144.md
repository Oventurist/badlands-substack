# Badlands Brief: MLKKK Day, Peace Boards and Power Paradigms

"MLKKK Day, Peace Boards and Power Paradigms" is the January 20, 2026 edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The edition carries contributions from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and covers four main items: the invocation of the [[ku-klux-klan-act-of-1871|Ku Klux Klan Act]] against left-wing activists in Minnesota, the launch of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]], the transatlantic rupture over [[greenland|Greenland]], and the [[minnesota-somali-fraud|Minnesota Somali welfare fraud scandal]].

## The KKK Act and the Minneapolis church incident

The Brief reports that tensions in the Minneapolis–St. Paul area had surged because of federal [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] deportation operations targeting criminal illegal aliens in the sanctuary-city region, aggravated by the fatal shooting of [[renee-good|Renee Good]], who according to the source was killed by an ICE agent after allegedly attempting to run over a federal officer with her vehicle. The incident sparked ongoing anti-ICE riots and protests against a background of left-wing activism, organized pressure campaigns by nonprofits, and federal law-enforcement responses.

A notable flashpoint, the article states, occurred when dozens of left-wing activists stormed [[cities-church|Cities Church]] in St. Paul during a Sunday service, apparently targeting a pastor believed to be linked to ICE's local field office operations. Former [[cnn|CNN]] host [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] is alleged to have joined the mob, leading the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to place him "on notice" for potential federal charges. Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] stated that the incident could violate the Ku Klux Klan Act, which prohibits conspiracies to intimidate or violate civil rights, and the FACE Act, which protects houses of worship from interference. Dhillon emphasized zero tolerance, noting that disrupting religious services is not protected speech and that the DOJ's Civil Rights Division, along with the [[fbi|FBI]], was investigating. Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] spoke directly with the affected pastor, affirming that attacks on law enforcement and intimidation of Christians would face the "full force of federal law." [[white-house|White House]] officials and religious leaders condemned the actions as a desecration of a sacred space. Lemon dismissed the allegations as "fake news." The broader federal response included the deployment of 3,000 ICE agents to the area, with 1,500 troops on standby, amid warnings of escalating left-wing violence in Democrat-run cities.

Ashe in America frames the story through her own experience: in March 2022 she was sued for allegedly violating the KKK Act, fought back and won, but argues that "the outcome isn't the point. The story is the point." She writes that the Act "makes for sensational headlines" and "carries a unique moral stigma because of its association with the Ku Klux Klan and racial terror," a stigma she says is not accidental. She quotes a 2015 NYU Social Justice Law Review passage cited by the plaintiffs in her own case: "Another reason to bring a KKK Act claim is for the purpose of political messaging. Liability under the Act carries the additional stigma of conspiracy and its association with the KKK's legacy of politicalized racism. …the [KKK] Act creates the opportunity to brand a particular ballot security group or party committee with these deeply embarrassing associations… the threat of such associations could strengthen the law's deterrent effect and help drive settlement."

She argues that in the 21st century the statute "is less about stopping violent conspiracies and more about attaching moral contamination to political opponents," calling it "character assassination with federal branding," and observes that it had not previously been aimed at figures like Don Lemon — "Empathetic mockingbirds rarely get clap backs like 'Klan.'" While saying the Act "should probably be abolished," she concedes that seeing the DOJ "boomerang" it onto the political class that weaponized it "scratches an itch."

## The Board of Peace and the Davos signing

The Brief reports that Trump launched the Board of Peace, a new international body chaired by him, initially established to oversee postwar reconstruction and governance in [[gaza|Gaza]] under a fragile ceasefire following the [[october-7-attacks|Hamas-led October 7, 2023 attack]] and the subsequent war. Backed by a November 2025 [[united-nations|UN]] Security Council resolution, with Russia and China abstaining, the board's mandate was officially limited to Gaza until the end of 2027, supervising a Palestinian technocratic administration and a separate 11-member Gaza Executive Committee handling day-to-day operations. The draft charter, however, omits specific mention of Gaza, criticizes past "failed" institutions — implicitly the UN — and positions the board as a "nimble and effective" alternative for global conflict resolution with potential expansion beyond the Middle East.

A high-profile signing ceremony was scheduled for Thursday at 10:30 a.m. during the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, intended to launch phase two of Trump's Gaza peace efforts. Invitations went to leaders of roughly 60 countries, including France, Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, [[hungary|Hungary]], Poland, [[canada|Canada]], Egypt, Jordan, [[turkey|Turkey]], [[qatar|Qatar]], India, Pakistan, Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Australia, Argentina, Paraguay, Thailand, Vietnam, Morocco, Albania, [[cyprus|Cyprus]], [[greece|Greece]], Singapore and Uzbekistan. Confirmed acceptances included Hungary (Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]], who announced unequivocal support), Kazakhstan (President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev), Belarus (President Alexander Lukashenko), Argentina, Vietnam and Morocco. Italy's Giorgia Meloni and Canada's [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] expressed readiness to contribute in principle, while the United Kingdom under [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] signaled willingness for Gaza's second ceasefire phase. France declined, citing conflicts with UN commitments and the charter's broader scope; Poland raised concerns over [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s inclusion; and many leaders avoided public commitments amid fears the board could undermine the United Nations' authority as the sole universal body for peace and security. Putin received an invitation, with the Kremlin saying it was seeking to "clarify all nuances" with Washington before responding. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was invited and his office confirmed receipt, but he was not attending the Davos ceremony.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry describes Trump as "launching a thousand ships (so to speak) against the World Economic Forum in Davos this week," continuing a "campaign of hyperbolic aggression" that sows "narrative chaos and dread among the global elite." He welcomes accusations that Trump is building his own version of the United Nations — "that sounds pretty awesome to me" — and notes Trump's response to Norwegian criticism over Greenland: because the Nobel Committee in Oslo refused him the Peace Prize after he "stopped eight wars," Trump said he "no longer feel[s] an obligation to think purely of peace."

On Israel, the contributor notes that Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] said "the time has come to dismantle the headquarters in Kiryat Gat," referring to the Civil Military Coordination Centre established by the United States in October as a headquarters for civilian and military personnel from countries participating in the International Stabilization Force. Quoting the Times of Israel, the Brief records Smotrich's claim that dismantling the headquarters would let Israel remove representatives from "countries like Egypt and Britain that are hostile to Israel and undermine its security," while declining to explain why he singled out the United Kingdom. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry observes that those two states are Israel's historic allies alongside the United States: the modern State of [[israel|Israel]] was formed out of British Mandate Palestine, fulfilling a promise made by British Lord Sir Arthur Balfour in 1917 to Sir Walter Rothschild, who inherited the title Baron Rothschild in 1915, and Egypt was the first Arab nation to normalize relations with Israel, in 1980.

He predicts Netanyahu will ultimately accept the invitation, persuading Smotrich that "it's better to be in the room and at the head of the table," while Smotrich uses the issue to catalyze fervor among Israeli settlers — possibly to the point of protests or attacks on the headquarters. He notes that the first high-profile invitee revealed was Putin, whose acceptance Russian Special Envoy to the US Kirill Dmitriev confirmed on X, meaning Trump and Putin would figuratively stand "shoulder to shoulder at the Gaza Border, standing between Palestine and Netanyahu," backed by soldiers from across the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] led by Turkey and Qatar. He compares the situation to a pay-per-view professional wrestling event, with "Trump and Putin headlining against Netanyahu and the WEF."

## Greenland and the transatlantic crisis

The Brief reports that Trump's renewed campaign to bring Greenland under U.S. control — citing national security against Russian and Chinese influence in the Arctic, access to rare earth minerals, and historical U.S. interest dating back over 150 years — escalated into a major rift with European allies, threatening [[nato|NATO]] cohesion and producing the most serious transatlantic crisis in decades. Over the weekend Trump threatened additional 10–15% tariffs effective February 1 on eight countries — [[denmark|Denmark]], Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom — that had deployed a small military contingent to Greenland to bolster Arctic security and demonstrate solidarity.

In a letter to Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, confirmed by Norway and shared by Trump with other NATO leaders, the president linked Norway's refusal to award him the Nobel Peace Prize for allegedly "stopping 8 wars" to a shift in U.S. policy: "Since your country decided not to give me the Nobel Peace Prize... I no longer feel an obligation to think purely of peace... but can now think about what is good and proper for the United States." Støre responded firmly, affirming Norway's support for Denmark's sovereignty over Greenland. The eight affected nations issued a joint statement condemning the tariff threats as undermining transatlantic relations and risking a "dangerous downward spiral," while pledging full solidarity with Denmark and Greenland's self-determination. The [[european-union|European Union]] convened an emergency meeting of its 27 ambassadors to coordinate retaliation, including potential €93 billion in countermeasures. Amid these tensions the Kremlin confirmed Putin's Board of Peace invitation, with spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] saying it was under review pending clarification through U.S. contacts.

Burning Bright reads these events as evidence of "an entirely new Power Paradigm" that "lays bare the fragility of the [[rules-based-international-order|Rules-Based International Order]] the Globalist Hegemon has long presented as an immutable framework." He describes the paradigm as "a profound bicameral cypher," legible whether one views Trump as "the righteous peacemaker restoring balance to a fractured world, or as the unrelenting disruptor pursuing dominance through sheer force of will." In either reading, he argues, Trump's maneuvers force observers to confront the reality that enforcement of any order "has always depended not on moral consensus or institutional pronouncements, but on the raw capacity to project and sustain power." He characterizes the episode as another in a series of "Hegemonic Humiliations," in which Europe's frantic diplomatic gatherings and limited deployments "serve only to underscore their institutional weakness" and confirm that these entities have been "sustained for decades solely by the American backstop."

## Minnesota welfare fraud and the SCAM Act

The final main item reports that the welfare fraud scandal involving Minnesota's Somali immigrant community had escalated into a political firestorm, with Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] alleging a deliberate Democratic strategy to build voting blocs through fraudulent entitlements while the Trump administration pushed citizenship revocation. Prosecutors and investigators estimate that up to half of $18–20 billion in federally funded welfare programs over recent years was lost to fraud, primarily in Medicaid — where 86% of Somalis in Minnesota reportedly receive free healthcare — as well as food stamps, housing, childcare, and the $250 million pandemic-era child nutrition scheme known as Feeding Our Future.

House Oversight Chairman James Comer (R-Ky.), Senate Homeland Security Chairman Rand Paul (R-Ky.) and House Majority Whip Tom Emmer (R-Minn.) are leading investigations, describing the fraud as a "coordinated effort" and "scam" to manipulate elections, congressional apportionment and federal funding in blue states. Whistleblowers, including Minnesota state workers, provided affidavits claiming they had warned Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Attorney General Keith Ellison, both Democrats, about the issues without action being taken; both officials deny the allegations. Probes were expanding to California, New York and Illinois, with the administration freezing funds and targeting high-risk programs.

Senator Eric Schmitt (R-Mo.) introduced the White House-backed Stop Citizenship Abuse and Misrepresentation (SCAM) Act, which would expand denaturalization powers by creating a 10-year post-naturalization window — potentially reducible to five — to revoke citizenship for acts such as defrauding the government of $10,000 or more, aggravated felonies, espionage or terrorist affiliations, retroactively disqualifying "good moral character." Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller hailed the bill and called the Somali scandal "one of the greatest financial scandals in American history," vowing immediate denaturalization and deportation. In a January 19, 2026 [[truth-social|Truth Social]] post, Trump targeted Representative Ilhan Omar (D-MN), a Somali-born naturalized citizen, suggesting she be jailed or "sent back to Somalia" over the "$19 Billion Dollars in Minnesota Somalia Fraud," accusing her of knowing "everything" about it while being a "constant complainer who hates the USA."

Ashe in America restates her position — "deport the Somalis; prosecute the Americans" — and supports the SCAM Act, arguing the culture involved "is incompatible with the American rule of law." She adds that "everyone that enabled this crime, covered it up over the years, and ostensibly took a cut of the spoils is guilty of providing aid and comfort to foreign enemies," and calls to "Pass the SCAM Act and deport the 'Somali-Americans' involved in the fraud. Then prosecute the Americans. End the NGOs. Kraken the Karens," so that the [[rule-of-law|rule of law]] can be restored "equally, without fear or favor."
