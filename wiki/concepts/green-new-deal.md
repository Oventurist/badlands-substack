# Green New Deal

The Green New Deal is described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the framework under which a new system of global government managed by draconian regulation was to be imposed after the fascist "solution" to the crisis of the 1930s was put down during the Second World War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Under this framework the instruments of banking regulation, state-directed capital and centralized government — not evils in themselves — would be directed only toward green, low energy flux density forms of energy, which, in Ehret's argument, inherently lower the population of the earth.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

Ehret contrasts this with the protectionism, bank regulation, state credit and central authority exerted by the United States during the 1930s New Deal, and with the Eurasian New Silk Road policy today.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) The difference, he writes, is that one system empowers sovereign nations and increases the productive powers of labour and the energy flux density of humanity while raising quality of life, whereas the green agenda has the opposite effect because monetary incentives are tied to decreasing the earth's carbon footprint.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) He offers the image of a drug addict being paid in heroin as an incentive to bleed himself to death.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

A prototype for such a policy is identified in Ontario, where [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]] served as President of Ontario Hydro from 1992 to 1994 under Premier [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], a period during which Canada's nuclear sector was nearly shut down.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) The United States' rejection of the Paris Accords and the Green New Deal is presented as signifying a change of orientation away from the Malthusian depopulation agenda to which [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] was devoted.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Burning Bright's assessment

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] describes the Green New Deal era as an especially aggressive acceleration of the [[scarcity-paradigm|Scarcity Paradigm]]'s twin tracks — foreign energy chokepoints and domestic production limits.[2] What was presented as climate and justice policy, he argues, operated in practice as a mechanism for locking in lower energy densities and routing enormous flows of public capital through networks of aligned nonprofits and politically connected entities.[2]

He cites the 2026 criminal referrals by [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] over the [[greenhouse-gas-reduction-fund|Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund]] and related [[inflation-reduction-act|Inflation Reduction Act]] grants — funds moved through thinly-capitalized cutouts to figures with clear partisan ties — as evidence of how those programs functioned once the layers were pulled back.[2]

## European backlash (2024)

Badlands commentary in April 2024 tied the Green New Deal agenda directly to
the 2018 [[yellow-vest-movement|Yellow Vest]] protests and to the later farmer
protests across western Europe, describing governments plotting to requisition
privately-owned farmland and halt food production as orchestrating the
starvation of Europe.[3] Former Macron advisor [[david-djaiz|David Djaïz]]
warned that France's continuation down the green path risked a social revolt
dwarfing the Yellow Vests, even as he argued for building France's industrial
base to compete with China — messaging Badlands read as a concession to
populist nationalism.[3]
