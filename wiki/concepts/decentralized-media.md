# Decentralized Media

"Decentralized media" is the term Badlands Media uses for independent journalism existing outside the handful of conglomerates that own the mainstream press. It is defined in opposition to [[centralized-media|centralized media]]: any media outside the conglomerate umbrellas, "where there are no emissaries of the think tank class telling journalists what to write and how to frame it."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Composition

The sector is populated by [[citizen-journalism|citizen journalists]], Substack writers, independent video networks and outlets such as [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]], [[consortium-news|Consortium News]] and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The article notes an asymmetry of coverage: the Fox News audience is never served the difficult subjects discussed on Badlands, and the "corporate hacks" at CNN will not report what appears in The Grayzone or Consortium News.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Pressures

Journalists in the space have contended with demonetization, deplatforming, public ridicule and even allegations of treason for spreading foreign state disinformation, all while attempting to provide a counterbalance to mainstream narratives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Opposition extends beyond government to Big Tech — including search-engine measures against independent outlets — and to major financial institutions, such as crowdfunding platforms freezing donations to independent publications.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The organized form of this opposition is described as the [[censorship-industrial-complex|Censorship Industrial Complex]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Growth

Despite this pressure, Badlands argues the decentralized sector "continues to grow at a rapid pace—that is the power of the truth, and a testament to how thirsty the American public is for it."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Its rise is set against the simultaneous contraction of the legacy industry, and framed as the fulfilment of [[terence-mckenna|Terence McKenna]]'s 1996 prediction that any-to-any internet communication would finish one-to-many mass media.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)
