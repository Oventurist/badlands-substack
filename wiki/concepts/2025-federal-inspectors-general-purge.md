# 2025 Federal Inspectors General Purge

The 2025 federal inspectors general purge refers to the removal, on a Friday night in late January 2025, of at least fifteen independent inspectors general from across the United States executive branch by the administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The [[white-house|White House]] removed the independent inspectors general of nearly every Cabinet-level agency in what was described as an unprecedented action, one that observers said could clear the way for the president to install loyalists in the role charged with identifying fraud, waste and abuse in government.[1]

## Trump's defense

Speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One on Saturday night while traveling to Florida, in his first comments after the removals, Trump defended the decision as unremarkable: "It's a very common thing to do."[1] He added, "I don't know them," even though many of the officials dismissed had been appointed by him during his first term, and said that "some people thought that some were unfair or some were not doing their job. It's a very standard thing to do."[1]

## Reaction

Lawmakers in both parties raised concerns about the late-night purge and questioned a decision that appeared to violate federal law, which requires advance notice to Congress before an inspector general is removed.[1] The action caused alarm among government watchdogs and members of Congress.[1]

Badlands commentator Burning Bright framed the episode within the outlet's recurring narrative device of a scripted political drama, asking rhetorically whether one feels tension during a movie already seen, and asserting that "The Golden Age is a story. Donald Trump is a character."[1] In this reading the outcomes were already settled — "The deals are done. The future is here. The rest is a matter of timing, and awakening."[1]
