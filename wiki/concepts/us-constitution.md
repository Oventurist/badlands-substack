# United States Constitution

The United States Constitution is treated in Badlands Media commentary as the central legal instrument standing between the American people and their own government. In Erik Carlson's essay "America's Greatest Threat," the founding generation is described as having understood that the gravest danger to the new republic came not from invading armies, naval cannon, or pirates, but from America itself — specifically from the American government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) On this reading the Constitution was written not primarily to defend Americans from foreign adversaries directly, but to protect them from an American government infiltrated by those adversaries.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Purpose and rights

Carlson frames the Constitution as protecting rights that are conferred by God rather than granted by the state: the right to be heard, the right to protect oneself and one's family, and the baseline entitlements of life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) These rights are characterized as inalienable, and the document's purpose is to ensure that nothing and no one — including conspiring Americans within the government — may stand in the way of an individual pursuing them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

The essay notes that the two great oceans flanking the United States provided natural protection from external attack, which made an internal safeguard necessary; the danger, as in Stanley Kubrick's *The Shining*, came from within the isolated household itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Taxation and amendment

Carlson argues that the founders specifically treated a federal income tax as unconstitutional, and that the absence of such a tax was what kept the federal government small — the size the founders intended.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He identifies the ratification of the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]] on February 3, 1913 as the moment the Constitution was altered to permit the government to take from the people, occurring in close proximity to the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] — a coincidence he rejects as such.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Erosion

The essay contends that the American government has convinced the public that the Constitution should be abolished, that free speech is bad, and that the right to bear arms makes people less safe.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) During the events of 2020, Carlson writes, Americans themselves decided that other Americans did not deserve their constitutional rights, enforcing lockdowns, vaccine requirements, and exclusion from public life on one another.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He concludes that until the Constitution is upheld, respected, and treated as holding authority over the American government, nothing will change.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)
