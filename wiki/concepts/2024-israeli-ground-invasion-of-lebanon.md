# 2024 Israeli Ground Invasion of Lebanon

The 2024 Israeli ground invasion of Lebanon was a cross-border military operation launched by [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli ground forces]] into southern Lebanon in the early hours of Tuesday, October 1, 2024, marking a significant escalation of Israel's offensive against [[hezbollah]] and opening a new front in what had by then become a yearlong war against Iranian-backed adversaries.[1] It was the first Israeli ground entry into [[lebanon]] since the 2006 Lebanese War.[1]

## Official framing

The [[national-security-council|White House National Security Council]] characterized the incursion as "limited operations" aimed at destroying Hezbollah infrastructure across the border, and said the action was in line with Israel's right to defend its citizens and to safely return civilians to their homes.[1] The NSC simultaneously warned that expansion of the operation carried risk, stating that "mission creep can be a risk and we will keep discussing that with the Israelis," and insisting that a diplomatic solution was the only route to lasting stability along the Israel–Lebanon border.[1]

The stated Israeli objective, per the official narrative out of Tel Aviv, was the establishment of an eighteen-mile buffer zone north of Israel, explicitly compared to the demilitarized zone between North and South Korea.[1] Badlands commentary argued that such a buffer would in practice be filled by the [[israeli-settler-movement]], which would be expected to begin constructing new settlements as soon as the land was vacated.[1]

## Scope of operations

Arab and Russian media reported that IDF soldiers were present in the suburbs immediately south of Beirut — nearly sixty miles from the border — with Israeli aircraft striking targets in the area, suggesting that [[benjamin-netanyahu]] was moving on Lebanon's capital on the first night of the operation.[1] Israeli media reported that no armor had yet crossed the border, but the tempo of the operation was described as having the character of a blitzkrieg, notwithstanding roughly four months of forewarning in the press.[1] Syrian state media additionally reported Israeli strikes on Damascus resulting in several deaths, including that of news anchor Safaa Ahmad.[1]

## Deception and political dimension

Badlands analysis stressed that Hezbollah is not solely a paramilitary organization but originated as a political movement protesting Israel's prolonged presence in Lebanon after it pursued Yasser Arafat from the West Bank to Lebanon in 1982 and remained for eighteen years.[1] Alongside dozens of Hezbollah military commanders killed in the strike campaign, numerous Hezbollah politicians regarded as legitimate officials within the Lebanese government were also killed.[1]

Reports cited in the brief held that Israel used deception to lull [[hassan-nasrallah]] and his associates into a false sense of security, assuring officials in [[iran]] that new peace terms were imminent as Netanyahu boarded a plane to New York to address the UN General Assembly; Iran was told to restrain Hezbollah pending delivery of those terms.[1] The assurance was a lie, and the commentary argued that while the gamesmanship might be admired in the abstract, the deception would damage Israel's long-term ability to build allies in the region.[1]

## Regional and great-power reaction

At the [[united-nations-general-assembly]], Netanyahu accused the assembly of being antisemitic flat-earthers and displayed a map identifying his "Arab partners," invoking the [[abraham-accords]] and vowing to confront Iran with their backing.[1] Those same partners then took the stage, claiming to speak for all fifty-seven nations of the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation]], and expressed indignation, pleading with the assembly to ask Israel about its "endgame."[1] Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan]] was among the ministers voicing exasperation, lending weight to [[douglas-macgregor|Colonel Macgregor's]] six-month-old contention that the Abraham Accords were dead and unrevivable.[1]

The maps Netanyahu displayed were described as battle maps drawn by neoconservative warmongers who saw the Accords as an opportunity to turn the Sunni Muslims of Arabia against the Shia Muslims of Persia.[1] The move against Hezbollah and Iran was likened to going "all in" at poker, calling the bluff of General [[michael-kurilla|Kurilla's]] warning that the United States would not back Israel in a war against Hezbollah or Iran — the equivalent of throwing oneself into a tiger pit and daring the zookeepers to mount a rescue.[1]

[[russia|Russian]] positioning was read as a counter-signal: [[vladimir-putin]] dispatched his prime minister to Iran to announce increased cooperation across all fields, a show of force signaling that Russia had Iran's back, and separately signed an order conscripting an additional 133,000 soldiers while explicitly stating that the new conscripts would not be deployed to the Ukraine conflict.[1]
