# QAnon Shaman Lawsuit Over Portland Deployment

In late September 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was named as a codefendant in a lawsuit brought by [[jacob-chansley|Jacob Chansley]], the January 6 figure widely known as the "QAnon Shaman," in connection with federal action in Portland, Oregon.[1] Esquire reported the suit as an effort to challenge the validity of Trump's presidency.[1]

## Portland context

Trump gave an interview to NBC News in which he was asked about his description of Portland as a "war-ravaged" wasteland.[1] During the Sunday morning phone interview with NBC White House correspondent Yamiche Alcindor, Trump made remarks that seemed to indicate he might be backing off his military plan for the city, referencing a weekend conversation with Oregon Governor [[tina-kotek|Tina Kotek]]: "I spoke to the governor, she was very nice. But I said, 'Well wait a minute, am I watching things on television that are different from what's happening? My people tell me different.' They are literally attacking and there are fires all over the place. ... It looks like terrible."[1] He then ordered the [[national-guard|National Guard]] in anyway.[1]

Esquire's Charles Pierce argued that the president had admitted making a dangerous and unpopular decision based on old B-roll from 2020 and on the advice of those around him, comparing the episode to "Nixon-talking-to-the-portraits" and claiming that if the same had been done by Joe Biden, [[jake-tapper|Jake Tapper]] "would have a three-book deal by the end of business today."[1]

## Badlands rebuttal

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called Pierce's line a "hilarious historical rewrite," noting that Tapper did not publish his book until after Biden left office and arguing the announcement was intended to defuse or front-run the autopen scandal.[1] She wrote that Tapper "suddenly found journalism" and that the media pretended it was a surprise that the sitting president was incapacitated.[1] She also pointed out that Esquire had itself disparaged Tapper's *Original Sin* the day before publication, on 20 May 2025, in an article by the same writer, Charles Pierce — accusing him of "building new bullshit on top of his old bullshit" and speculating that Esquire and the QAnon Shaman might share the same clandestine employer.[1]
