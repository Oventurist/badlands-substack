# Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act

The **Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act** is the Michigan state statute prohibiting discrimination on the basis of certain protected characteristics, including religion.[1] Implemented in 1977, the act was expanded in 2023 by the state legislature and Democratic Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] to cover gender identity, a change that enshrined in statute a 2022 Michigan Supreme Court interpretation of the law.[1]

## Enforcement

The act is enforced by the [[michigan-department-of-civil-rights|Michigan Department of Civil Rights]], which investigates complaints and may issue formal charges. In November 2023 the department invoked the statute in the [[studio-8-hair-lab-discrimination-charge|Studio 8 Hair Lab discrimination charge]], accusing salon owner [[christine-geiger|Christine Geiger]] of having "openly and repeatedly violated" the act after she stated she would not serve customers who identify as anything other than a man or woman.[1] Critics in the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] characterized the resulting proceeding as an example of [[lawfare|lawfare]], in which the process itself functions as the punishment.[1]
