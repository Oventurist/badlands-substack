# 2024 Israeli Strikes on Dahiyeh

In late October 2024 more than a dozen Israeli airstrikes were reported to have hit Beirut's southern suburbs on Monday night and into the early hours of Tuesday, after the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] accused [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] of storing millions of dollars in a bunker underneath one of Lebanon's largest hospitals amid the country's years-long financial crisis.[1]

## Evacuation warnings and the hospital claim

The strikes followed instructions from the IDF's Arabic-language spokesman, Lt. Col. Avichay Adraee, telling residents of several buildings in Beirut's southern suburbs to evacuate, warning that they were "located near facilities and interests belonging to Hezbollah."[1] The military said earlier that day that it would continue striking Hezbollah targets with a focus on their financial infrastructure, after IDF spokesman Rear Adm. [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] stated that Israel estimated there to be roughly "half a billion dollars in dollar bills and gold" in a bunker beneath the [[al-sahel-hospital|Al-Sahel Hospital]] in the southern Beirut suburb of Dahiyeh, where Hezbollah is largely based.[1]

Hagari said the Israeli Air Force was monitoring the compound but stressed that Israel would not strike the hospital itself, arguing that it was at war with Hezbollah and not with the Lebanese people.[1] The hospital nevertheless evacuated its patients to a safer location, and its director, Fadi Alameh, denied the IDF's allegations and invited the Lebanese army to inspect the site.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the debate over whether gold was buried beneath the hospital was itself the distraction, displacing the more basic question of why the IDF was openly bombing the capital of Lebanon at all, despite prior assurances that military operations would remain within roughly 18 miles of the border — Beirut lies more than 60 miles from it.[1] He described the sequence as a goalpost-shifting exercise reminiscent of the Bush-era march into the Middle East under the banner of pursuing terrorists, and predicted Syria would be next, citing [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s stated ambitions regarding Damascus.[1]
