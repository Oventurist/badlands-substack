# COINTELPRO

COINTELPRO is the name of the FBI's counterintelligence program against domestic political movements, invoked within the "Truth Community" as shorthand for state infiltration and steering of activist causes through witting or unwitting assets.[1]

The term appears in the *[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]]* of April 19, 2023, where [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] applied it to Tennessee state Representative [[justin-jones|Justin Jones]], writing that "everything about this guy screams 'asset' to me, whether knowingly or not," and urging readers unfamiliar with COINTELPRO to look it up on Wikispooks.[1] The framing treats media-amplified activist figures as potential products of an infiltration model rather than organic political actors, with DeLarme predicting that Jones would rapidly rise within the Democratic Party.[1]

## Lineage claimed in Badlands commentary

In August 2023, Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] invoked COINTELPRO as the origin point of the security state's domestic-threat messaging, writing that the constant warnings that domestic terrorists pose a more imminent danger than Chinese infiltration or compromised officials harken back to the program's early days.[2] He described institutions such as the [[fbi|FBI]] planting agents or acquiring informants within groups like the Ku Klux Klan and the Black Panthers and steering them in ways that stoked division and supported the narratives of the day.[2]

DeLarme traced the same method forward through the [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]], when he said the FBI built a network of more than 15,000 informants to infiltrate Muslim communities and facilitate phony terrorist plots, and then to January 6, 2021, alleging the insertion of informants and agent provocateurs into the crowd and the infiltration of the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] and [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]], with Patriot Front and Blood Tribe created "out of whole cloth" and the [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] kidnapping case as a further example.[2]

## Displacement of the civil rights movement

Badlands Media argues that COINTELPRO's decisive achievement was the replacement of [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]]'s civil rights movement with FBI-funded radicals after King's murder in April 1968.[3] In this account, the program's covert intelligence operations transplanted a movement built on effective, peaceful civil disobedience with the "New Left," featuring armed and violence-prone operatives running bomb-creating organisations such as the [[weather-underground|Weather Underground]].[3]

The consequence was ideological as well as tactical: the drug-loving anarchists who rose to prominence in that milieu, many of whom later became leading figures of the establishment, propagated an ethic equating all of Western civilisation — the space programme, atomic technology, the American constitution and Western values generally — with the evils of the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam war]] and the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]].[3] Badlands links the technique to contemporary cases including [[patriot-front|Patriot Front]], the [[heritage-front|Heritage Front]] and the [[atomwaffen-division|Atomwaffen Division]].[3]

## Domestic application of foreign tradecraft

In its account of the [[plausible-deniability-network|plausible deniability network]], Badlands Media presents COINTELPRO as the domestic counterpart to the manipulation of foreign populations pioneered in operations such as the 1953 CIA–MI6 overthrow of Iranian Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh.[4] Described as a covert and often illegal series of operations run by the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], the program allowed the federal government not only to infiltrate and steer organic movements — anti-war protesters, the Black Panthers, the National Indian Movement and others — in a desired direction, but to alter their messaging as well.[4]

The article argues that the usefulness of an unsuspecting American public, particularly once organized into political activist groups, is precisely what made COINTELPRO so effective, and that the same logic was later industrialized through NGO cut-outs and rebranded as "[[nonviolent-action|nonviolent action]]."[4]
