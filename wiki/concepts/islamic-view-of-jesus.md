# Islamic View of Jesus

The article devotes a section to correcting what it calls gaslit Western misconceptions about Jesus of Nazareth and Islam.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Quranic teaching

Islam is the only religion besides Christianity to recognize Jesus — Isa in Islam — as a holy figure.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The Quran explicitly states that Jesus is the penultimate prophet and messenger of God, dedicating one of its four books entirely to Him, the Injīl (The Gospel of Jesus).1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) It affirms that He was born to a virgin mother, Mary, that His Father is God, and that He was aided by disciples who believed His message.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) It credits Him with performing at least six miracles, while clarifying that [[muhammad|Muhammad]] performed none.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

The Quran further states that Jesus was betrayed by the Pharisees, was saved by God and brought to rule the Kingdom of Heaven, and would one day return to earth in a Second Coming to bring peace and justice to all mankind.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The archangel Gabriel who visited Muhammad is identified in the article as the same celestial being who visited Mary on the night of Jesus's birth.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Argument

From this the author concludes that the idea Islam and Christianity cannot find common ground or live together in peace is a devious deception, whose 20th-century proliferation the series promises to explore.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The point complements the [[constitution-of-medina|Constitution of Medina]] as evidence of early Islamic religious tolerance.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
