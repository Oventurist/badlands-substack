# 2025 Trump Tariffs

The 2025 Trump tariffs were the trade measures imposed during the opening months of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term, including a 25% tariff on automobiles built overseas, which became a focal point of public anxiety about inflation and recession in spring 2025.[1]

## Economic context

Writing in early April 2025, Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] noted that the S&P 500 was down only about 1.57% for the year — little more than 1% off an all-time high — yet consumer confidence stood at a twelve-year low.[1] He observed that this pessimism persisted despite inflation at a four-year low, falling gasoline prices, and $3 to $4 trillion already committed to US manufacturing in the administration's first two months.[1]

## Debate over inflation effects

Wall Street commentary and the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] raised concerns that tariffs would push consumer prices higher, and popular sentiment held that the cost of goods might double.[1] Carlson rejected this framing, arguing that no one is compelled to purchase an imported vehicle when American-built alternatives exist, and that the tariffs are designed to be self-cancelling: once foreign manufacturers sign agreements to produce vehicles in the United States, the tariff disappears without waiting for production to begin.[1] He characterized media and left-wing treatment of tariffs as presenting solvable trade questions as unsolvable crises.[1]

## Political fallout

Carlson linked tariff anxiety to a broader collapse of confidence that also drove the [[tesla-vandalism-attacks|Tesla vandalism attacks]] and hostility toward [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[doge|DOGE]].[1] He reported receiving calls and emails from clients convinced the stock market was crashing and a severe recession imminent, a shift he dated to roughly a week after Trump's early-March address to Congress, when previously dormant opposition messaging resumed.[1]
