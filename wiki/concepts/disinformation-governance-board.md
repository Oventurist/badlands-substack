# Disinformation Governance Board

The Disinformation Governance Board was a short-lived body established under the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] and headed by [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]].[1] The board never held a meeting, but it became a durable symbol in American debates over government involvement in the regulation of speech.[1]

## Purpose

The stated purpose of the board was to coordinate disinformation efforts at DHS across topics ranging from migration to addressing plots attributed to Russia and Iran.[1]

## Criticism

The board was heavily criticized by the GOP and regularly covered by [[fox-news|Fox News]].[1] The [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] dubbed it the "Ministry of Truth," invoking George Orwell's novel *1984*, and couched the board's definition of disinformation as "any speech we don't like."[1] Fox News mentioned Jankowicz and her role leading the board more than 300 times in its coverage, with hosts and commentators asserting that she planned to censor speech.[1]

## Legacy

The board's collapse did not end the controversy: Jankowicz subsequently filed a defamation suit against Fox News over its coverage, a case commentators at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] framed as a potential historic precedent for [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections, since a government official was seeking redress for criticism of her official role.[1] The episode is frequently cited in the broader critique of the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]].[1]

## Contemporary commentary

A February 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay listed the short-lived Disinformation Governance Board alongside fact checks and context labels as instruments through which, in the author's view, the government and platforms exercised near-total control over American discourse in the digital public square for two years following the 2020 election.[2]

## A departmental "Ministry of Truth"

An April 2024 article by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the board as an endeavour undertaken by the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to establish an internal departmental "Ministry of Truth," an effort that sparked a significant scandal.[3] The article placed the board within the wider [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]], noting that the individuals who staff that network often first emerge from the U.S. government and typically from Homeland Security itself.[3]

DeLarme argued that the "Scary Poppins" public-relations disaster surrounding [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]] caused the endeavour to be shut down, but that such efforts had only temporarily receded: Western governments were still working with organizations funded by neoliberal billionaires to make an official ministry-of-truth body a reality, while censorship-enabling legal frameworks had already been adopted in Canada through the [[online-streaming-act|Online Streaming Act]], in the United Kingdom through the [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]], and across the European Union through the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[3] He further contended that the media's response to distrust of the board — using that distrust as evidence that [[disinformation-expert|disinformation experts]] are needed more than ever — was reprised in the defence of [[kate-starbird|Kate Starbird]].[3]
