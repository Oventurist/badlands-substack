# An SOS From Ground Zero

"An SOS From Ground Zero," subtitled "Maricopa County Hangs in the Balance," is an opinion article published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on November 22, 2022 and written by the Arizona-based contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]]. Written two weeks after the November 8, 2022 general election and days before the statutory deadline for county canvasses, the piece argues that Arizona's election system is "appallingly broken" and calls on readers to "SAVE. OUR. STATE." The article combines a personal appreciation of Arizona, a critique of state officials, a statistical argument about down-ballot vote totals, and a detailed summary of a letter sent by an assistant attorney general to [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] questioning its administration of the election.

## Framing and personal preamble

The author opens with an affectionate portrait of Arizona, noting that she has lived in the state since "fleeing Chicagoland in 1984" and describing the [[maricopa-county|Phoenix area]] as offering "the best of all worlds" — palm trees, cactus, swimming pools, and weather that "the country covets half the year." She catalogues destinations including the Grand Canyon, Tombstone, Antelope Canyon, Winslow and Route 66, the Lost Dutchman's Gold Mine, Sedona, Bisbee, the Petrified Forest, and Jerome, and observes that Arizona has "no tornados, no hurricanes," only occasional forest fires and the dramatic dust storms locally known as "haboobs." The affection is used as the emotional predicate for the article's distress signal about the state's elections.

## Criticism of county and state officials

The article describes what it calls "two new comedy shows playing on Twitter." The first is a weekend series of explanatory videos posted to the @MaricopaCounty account by [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]], the chairman of the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]], answering voter questions about election administration, including one on the transport trucks running between the county recorder's office and its vendor. The author writes that she "can't watch them all" and that the videos "make my stomach turn." In a sidebar she references her own earlier article of November 14, "The Plot Thickens in Maricopa County," about [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]], and notes that a [[q|Q]] drop dated November 18 also mentioned Runbeck.

The second target is Arizona Attorney General [[mark-brnovich|Mark Brnovich]], whom the author faults for joking on social media "at what could be considered the most critical time in the state of Arizona's history." She notes that Brnovich was described as "acting governor" during a period when Governor [[doug-ducey|Doug Ducey]] was apparently out of state, and points out that under Article V, Section 6 of the Constitution of Arizona the line of succession runs first to the [[arizona-secretary-of-state|Secretary of State]] and only then to the Attorney General. She asks pointedly whether Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] was passed over because she was "TOO BUSY trying to certify her OWN ELECTION," adding: "If we had any real journalists in Arizona, maybe someone could ask that question."

The article recounts Brnovich's tenure as attorney general since 2015, his third-place finish in the August 2022 U.S. Senate primary, and two of his Twitter posts — a May 13, 2019 post about his nunchaku and martial arts training, and an October 15, 2021 video posted after the Arizona audit of the 2020 election with the caption "You want the nunchucks. You got the nunchucks." The author's verdict is that "Brnovich did NOTHING to fight election fraud after 2020," and she speculates that as a Republican he is "either compromised or playing a specific role in this movie," noting that he spent time with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] before the Florence, Arizona rally.

## The down-ballot vote-total argument

Recalling her November 5 article "Why Kari Lake Will Lose in Arizona," in which she warned that "the left can't JUST flip the Governor's race" but "will need to flip the ENTIRE Republican down-ballot ticket in order for the Governor's race results to be believable," the author presents statewide totals drawn on November 21 from the Arizona Secretary of State's "2022 Unofficial General Election Results" page:

- U.S. Senate: 1,321,435 votes cast
- Arizona Governor: 1,287,322 votes cast
- Arizona Secretary of State: 1,320,059 votes cast
- Arizona Attorney General: 1,254,096 votes cast
- Arizona State Treasurer: 1,389,215 votes cast

She argues it is implausible that the low-profile State Treasurer contest, for which she "never even saw an AD or a ROAD SIGN," outdrew the four top-of-ticket races: 67,780 more than U.S. Senate, 101,893 more than Governor, 69,156 more than Secretary of State, and 135,119 more than Attorney General. The proposed explanation is that manipulation did not extend as far down the ballot as Treasurer — a race won by Republican [[kimberly-yee|Kimberly Yee]] with 55.7 percent of the vote. "These people are stupid," the author concludes, adding an aside mocking President Joe Biden's catchphrase, "C'mon, man!"

## Litigation and county responses

The article reports that "lawyers are swarming again" in Arizona, singling out attorney [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] as among the "savage lawyers fighting for election integrity." It cites a November 18 post by the Kari Lake War Room account publicizing the reply a [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] volunteer received from [[tom-liddy|Tom Liddy]], Civil Division Chief of the [[maricopa-county-attorneys-office|Maricopa County Attorney's Office]]. In a sidebar the author confirms that Tom Liddy is the son of Watergate figure [[g-gordon-liddy|G. Gordon Liddy]] and notes that she once worked with him when he was a radio talk show host, offering only "No comment."

## The Jennifer Wright letter

The centerpiece of the article is a summary of a four-page letter dated November 19, 2022 from [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]], an assistant attorney general in the Arizona Attorney General's Election Integrity Unit, to Tom Liddy. Wright writes that hundreds of citizen complaints, including first-hand witness accounts, have raised concerns "regarding Maricopa's lawful compliance with Arizona election law." The author organizes the letter into three "buckets."

### Ballot-on-demand printer configuration settings

The letter states that "according to Maricopa County, at least 60 voting locations had issues related to some ballot-on-demand (BOD) printers having printer configuration settings that were non-uniform, which appeared to have resulted in ballots that were unable to be read by on-site ballot tabulators." The article notes that although the machines had been tested the day before, problems began within thirty minutes of polls opening on November 8. Wright requests "a comprehensive report detailing with specificity" the circumstances at every affected location. The author highlights item seven of the request, which asks for "the method used to update or reconfigure the printer configuration settings at each voting location," including whether technicians were deployed in the field or whether "the network administrator was able to make universal changes" — a phrasing the author reads as implicitly conceding network connectivity, remarking, "I thought none of the machines was supposed to have internet access?"

### Election Day check-out procedures

The second bucket concerns training deficiencies. Voters turned away from a malfunctioning location and directed elsewhere found on arrival that the system already showed them as checked in at the first site, leaving them without a remedy; the article suggests poll workers were inadequately trained in the provisional ballot process. Wright again requested extensive documentation of training and check-out procedures.

### Door 3 ballots and close-out duties

The third bucket concerns the ballots deposited in the container known as [[door-3-ballots|"Door 3"]]. The author recounts that she and her husband were instructed to place their ballots in a container labeled "BOX 3" because their polling place had no working tabulator, and were assured the ballots would be escorted to the [[maricopa-county-tabulation-and-election-center|Maricopa County Tabulation and Election Center]] by both a Republican and a Democrat — reassurance she dismisses given the possibility of "RINOs or DINOs." Wright's letter states that "Maricopa County appears to have failed to adhere to the statutory guidelines in segregating, counting, tabulating, tallying, and transporting the 'Door 3' ballots," that the county has admitted non-tabulated Door 3 ballots were commingled with tabulated ballots at some locations, and that a sworn complaint from an election observer alleges "more than 1700 'Door 3' non-tabulated ballots from one voting location were placed in black duffle bags that were intended to be used for tabulated ballots."

### Deadline and the canvass

Wright's letter closes by asserting that "Arizonans deserve a full report and accounting of the myriad problems that occurred in relation to Maricopa County's administration of the 2022 General Election," and, because the issues bear on the county's "ability to lawfully certify election results," requests a response on or before the county submits its official [[canvass-arizona|canvass]] to the Secretary of State, which under A.R.S. § 16-642(A) must occur on or before November 28, 2022. The article explains that the canvass is the certification process and that the statute requires the governing body to canvass "not less than six days nor more than twenty days following the election." The author draws particular attention to subsection C, which provides that if returns from any polling place are missing at the time of the meeting, "the canvass shall be postponed from day to day until all the returns are received or until six postponements have been had" — after which, she writes, "we're in uncharted territory." She adds a closing joke that "nunchucks were NOT mentioned in the four-page letter."

## Profile of Jennifer Wright

To answer "Who is Jennifer Wright?", the article quotes a [[talking-points-memo|Talking Points Memo]] piece of August 22, 2019 headlined "GOP Voter Fraud Hawk Hired For Arizona AG's New 'Elections Integrity' Unit," credited to a hat tip from Praying Medic. The quoted passage describes Wright as "a Tea Party-linked voter fraud activist known for her vigilante approach to poll-monitoring," a former GOP mayoral candidate who once worked for [[verify-the-vote|Verify the Vote]], and notes that Verify the Vote was a partner of the Houston-based group [[true-the-vote|True the Vote]], itself an outgrowth of the Tea Party organization King Street Patriots and an advocate of voter ID laws. The author's response to this characterization — plainly intended by the original as criticism — is approving: "Sounds like our kind of gal!"

## Conclusion

The article closes by quoting a November 21 message from Kari Lake to "the People of Arizona & the United States": "Attorneys are working diligently to gather information. Whistleblowers are coming forward, and the curtain is being lifted. Whether done accidentally or intentionally, it is clear that this election was a debacle that destroyed any trust in our elections … I will continue fighting until we restore confidence and faith in our elections." The author affirms, "I believe you, Kari. And I'm with you," urges readers to follow independent outlets such as Badlands Media through Thanksgiving week, and signs off with an image from the [[babylon-bee|Babylon Bee]], recently restored to Twitter. A standard disclaimer notes that Badlands Media articles represent the opinions of contributing authors rather than the outlet itself.
