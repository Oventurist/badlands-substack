# Badlands Brief: God Bless The USA (July 6, 2026)

"God Bless The USA" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published July 6, 2026, framed around the United States' 250th Independence Day. The Brief pairs mainstream news summaries with commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. This edition covers President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s anti-communism messaging ahead of the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]], the funeral of Iranian Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]], a July 4 Trump–[[vladimir-putin|Putin]] telephone call, a robotics dispute at General Motors, and riots over discounted air conditioners at Lidl stores in France.

## Trump, communism and the 2026 midterms

The Brief reports that Trump and Republicans are escalating attacks casting progressive Democrats and democratic socialists as communists ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, following a series of left-wing primary victories. In a July 3 speech at Mount Rushmore, Trump warned of a "communist menace" and tied that message to recent progressive wins. Citing [[reuters|Reuters]], the Brief notes that four progressive candidates — three of them democratic socialists — won competitive Democratic primaries in New York City and Colorado, with further progressive victories in Kentucky, New Jersey, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Texas. The [[associated-press|Associated Press]] reported that experts considered Trump's broad claims linking Democrats to communism inaccurate, while [[fox-news|Fox News]], citing [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] senior fellow [[mike-gonzalez|Mike Gonzalez]], reported a warning that socialist electoral gains could produce "communists in double digits" in the House.

Ashe in America responds to the objection "It's not real communism" by arguing that [[communism|communism]] is simply the doctrine that "the community" — meaning the government — owns the means of production and that private property is outlawed, summarized in the slogan "You will own nothing and be happy." She contends that the defense of "not real communism" is unfalsifiable, since communism "is only real communism if it succeeds at utopia; and it never has, so it's never real communism when the starvation and mass murder inevitably begin." More precisely, she writes, today's ideology is "a globalist, collectivist novel authoritarianism enabled by technology and fueled by weaponized empathy," but "it's just easier to say 'communism.'" She quotes [[winston-churchill|Winston Churchill]]'s description of socialism as "the philosophy of failure, the creed of ignorance, and the gospel of envy," treating the socialism–communism distinction as one "without a difference." The commentary argues the ideology now occupies the seats of power, the airwaves, curricula at every level, and federal and state judicial precedent, concluding that "we cannot defeat an enemy that we refuse to acknowledge exists" and that the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]] is at least becoming more visible. It closes: "Accelerate."

## Khamenei's funeral and reaction from pro-Israel media

The Brief summarizes reporting that Iran began a funeral procession for late Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, with ceremonies in Tehran before continuing in other cities. Arab News, citing AP and AFP, reported that Khamenei's coffin and those of family members killed in a February 28 airstrike were carried through the capital toward Mehrabad International Airport, with thousands gathering at Tehran's Grand Mosalla. Events were scheduled to continue in Qom, in the Iraqi holy cities of Najaf and Karbala, and to conclude with burial in Mashhad; Iranian authorities presented the ceremonies as a major national event with crowd-control measures.

Activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] posted on July 2 that the funeral was a "target rich environment," accompanied by a bomb emoji. Trump told [[axios|Axios]] that he was monitoring the funeral and that U.S.–Iran talks would pause for about a week, with neither side engaging in hostilities. He said: "They are all there. One shot [and we can take them all out], but we are not going to do that because then we would have nobody to negotiate with." Trump added that he was surprised to see Iranians crying at the funeral, having thought people hated Khamenei, and said of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], "We get along very good. He knows who the boss is." [[rt|RT]] reported that [[ali-abdollahi|Ali Abdollahi]], commander of Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, warned the United States and Israel against attacks during the ceremonies, and that Israeli Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] said [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] was "marked for death."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls the "week off" a moment of levity only Trump could land, but also a signal "that maybe this war isn't what it seems," asking why one would grant an enemy a pause to mourn the leader one purportedly killed while also championing total war. He notes the symbolism of Khamenei being buried on the Fourth of July, and reads Trump's remark to Barak Ravid of Axios about crying Iranians as a deliberate use of the Streisand effect to draw attention to the possibility that Western audiences have been misled about the Ayatollah's relationship with the Iranian people — adding that "we Americans are more heavily psyop'd by our own government than even North Korea."

The commentary's principal target is what it calls "Mossad Media": Loomer is described as spending the Fourth of July rage-posting about Iran and calling for terrorist attacks against civilians and world leaders attending the funeral, with [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] chiming in. The author contrasts outrage over Palestinian children throwing rocks at IDF soldiers with open fantasies about bombing a funeral, and posts the dictionary definition of terrorism in anticipation of accusations of sympathizing with terrorists. He argues the intended outcome imagined by such voices — Iranians denouncing the state religion and overthrowing the government — ignores the desire to avenge thousands killed and the consequences of murdering hundreds of attending world leaders and diplomats. The world's hostility toward Israel, he writes, stems not from Jewishness but from a "rotten disposition" and "violent extremism with total impunity," including demands that such violence be carried out by the U.S. military at taxpayer expense. He suspects that when Trump called Iran a "spoiled child," he was actually referring to Israel.

The Brief notes that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi]] representatives, historic enemies of Iran who had announced no plans to attend, showed up unexpectedly to pay respects. The author restates a standing Badlands hypothesis that the Ayatollah died years earlier of natural causes and that the "new" Ayatollah is not a real person, with the martyrdom story serving as a face-saving exercise allowing Iranian leadership to end its theocratic age on a high note. He cites Iran's reported [[continuity-of-government|continuity of government]] protocol implemented the previous August, which removed the Ayatollah from the chain of command and effectively dissolved the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] over concerns of [[mossad|Mossad]] infiltration and an Israeli decapitation attempt. The Brief adds the aside that Russia is not a communist country but Israel is — citing [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]]'s campaign for Israeli prime minister on a platform of opening the marketplace from government control — and that Soviet oligarchs fled to Israel when the USSR collapsed. ## Putin's invitation to Trump

Russian officials said Trump and Putin held a roughly 90-minute telephone conversation on July 4. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Trump expressed willingness to work toward ending the fighting in Ukraine, described the call as "business-like and quite constructive," and said Russia continues to seek a political-diplomatic resolution while maintaining its stated positions. Putin renewed an invitation for Trump to visit Russia, a detail attributed to Axios. Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] separately said on Telegram that his conversation with Trump was "very good," that there was a "real prospect" of ending the war, and thanked the United States for military and political support including Javelin and Patriot systems.

Burning Bright writes that such a headline confuses normies and fills the globalist Deep State with existential dread, while this audience merely shrugs, familiar with the contours of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] he first named in 2022 and which he says the Q drops hinted at years earlier. His central observation is that Trump, Putin and Zelenskyy have all stated the war will end only at an agreed-upon time — which he reads as evidence that the deals have already been made in the shadows and that what plays out on the world stage is a show with multiple purposes. Putin's invitation, he argues, is not deal-making but "a signal flare to the Invisible Enemy that the Sovereign Alliance is alive and well." Timelines cannot be guessed temporally, only in narrative time, but a Trump visit to Moscow is "a foregone conclusion."

## Robots at General Motors and the limits of organized labor

The Brief summarizes a July 4 Futurism report, citing Crain's Detroit Business, that a labor dispute emerged at General Motors' Factory Zero electric-vehicle plant in Detroit after the installation of 50 AI-integrated manufacturing robots. The [[united-auto-workers|United Auto Workers]] contends that more than 1,000 employees at the plant were idled once the robots began operating; UAW Local 22 President James Cotton told Crain's the union was concerned about the introduction of robots following workforce reductions. The machines are collaborative robots, or "cobots," designed to work alongside humans and, per Autoblog, used primarily to attach body panels to electric vehicles. GM's response was not included in the material reviewed.

Ashe in America recalls the Brief's extensive 2023 coverage of cross-industry strikes and quotes her October 2023 article "[[a-time-to-strike|A Time to Strike]]," which argued that Trump was correct that the auto industry was being assassinated by optimization and global sourcing policies, that global corporations had invested in [[automation|automation]], robotics and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] for over a decade, and that the "future of work" discussions reflected a designed outcome in which "there are going to be fewer jobs than people." She notes the 2023 UAW deal was meant to run to 2028 but that the transformation is ahead of schedule, and repeats her earlier judgment that the strikes "never stood a chance," being "self-interested and delusional, driven by greed in denial of industry talent models that no longer exist." Her earlier advice to "learn to code" is retracted — "AI can code now, too" — and she closes by questioning how long it will take workers to realize their organizing entities cannot stop what is coming, and that they are still paying dues.

## The French air-conditioning riots

Crowds seeking discounted air-conditioning units at Lidl stores across France on July 2, 2026 formed long lines and in some locations came to blows as the country prepared for another forecast heatwave. Lidl had offered approximately 200,000 units in the promotion; Lidl France said it "deplores the incidents" and that employees had to manage tensions in a difficult environment, attributing shortages to its sales cycle and inventory planning. BFMTV reported about 100 people gathered at a store in Nanterre with disputes over a limited number of units; La Voix du Nord reported roughly 60 customers competing for four units in Hazebrouck, and Ouest-France reported police intervention at stores in Trélazé and near Angers. Fnac Darty CEO Enrique Martinez told BFM Business that customers had waited outside stores from as early as 4 a.m. during the heatwave and that some incidents involved physical confrontations.

Burning Bright writes that "social engineering goes both ways," finding it convenient that millions of European tourists flooded the United States during the World Cup and produced viral videos praising America just as the rest of the Global West visibly declined — but welcoming it if the effect is awakening. He argues the "French Air Conditioning Riots of 2026" bear the three markers of memetic significance in the digital age: the story is funny, the story is scary, and the story communicates a core truth. He describes the participants as "third-world invaders masquerading as Europeans," links the scenes to a broader trend of immigrant-on-native crime, and reads the episode as simultaneously a humiliation ritual and a motivating factor for native-born French and Europeans, who "SHOULD be angry," embarrassed, envious of the American position, and indignant that air conditioning is a rare luxury rather than an everyday convenience.
