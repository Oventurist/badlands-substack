# The Opposition

"The Opposition" is the term adopted after the November 2024 election by Democratic-aligned operatives and non-governmental organizations to describe the successor formation to the 2017-era "resistance" against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Where the earlier movement was framed as a temporary holding action, the Opposition is explicitly conceived as a permanent, institutionalized counter-structure.[1]

## Origins

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that after Trump's 2024 victory many rank-and-file participants in [[the-resistance|the Resistance]] were burnt out from eight years of activism and were tuning out of politics altogether, while professional operatives — figures such as [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]] and [[david-brock|David Brock]] — could not afford to retreat and instead reorganized under the new label.[1]

The clearest programmatic statement came from Elias, writing at [[democracy-docket|Democracy Docket]]. He recounted that after 2016 the hope had been that if Trump "could simply be held back — resisted — for four years, politics would go back to normal," and that Trump would be remembered as an aberration.[1] Rejecting that framing as insufficient, Elias wrote that "hoping that Trump fails is not a plan" and called for new movements, structures, tactics, platforms and leaders.[1] He argued that in most democratic systems this role is called the opposition, which is "more comprehensive and durable" than a resistance because "there are no time limits to the effort," and noted that in some systems the opposition entails "an entire shadow government."[1]

## Institutional machinery

DeLarme characterized the Opposition as reactivating an existing engine of consensus-shaping built from prominent NGOs including [[media-matters-for-america|Media Matters]], the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]], the [[southern-poverty-law-center|SPLC]] and the [[american-civil-liberties-union|ACLU]].[1] In his framing, large international think tanks act as the head of the apparatus while the NGO layer functions as its arms and legs.[1] He identified [[rachel-maddow|Rachel Maddow]] and Chris Hayes as the principal media pundits sustaining the Opposition's morale and inadvertently telegraphing its plans through guest bookings.[1]

## Tactics

Two tactical pillars were identified from an MSNBC interview between Maddow and ACLU executive director [[anthony-romero|Anthony Romero]]: litigation and the mobilization of sub-federal officials.[1] Romero conceded that the movement "can't run the same playbook" but insisted "litigation is going to be key," which DeLarme read as an intention to deploy [[lawfare|lawfare]] against a second Trump administration.[1] Romero also described a [[firewall-for-freedom|"firewall for freedom"]] — a plan to enlist state attorneys general, governors, mayors and city councils to withhold police cooperation and jail capacity from federal immigration enforcement, with the ACLU "preparing executive orders" and "organizing our folks to put pressures on elected officials" so they would sever immigration-enforcement relationships with the federal government.[1]

Both Maddow and Romero suggested that the absence of large-scale street protests after the 2024 election did not indicate demobilization; rather, resources were being marshaled for the most opportune moment, expected to be the onset of mass deportations.[1] DeLarme interpreted this as evidence that protest infrastructure comparable to 2016 and 2020 had already been prepared and was being held in reserve.[1]

## Assessment

DeLarme argued that the Opposition intends to leverage residual public belief that Trump is a bigot in order to obstruct his policies, and that the [[time-magazine|TIME]] and [[meet-the-press|Meet the Press]] interviews appeared designed to elicit inflammatory statements on transgender bathroom access or the deportation of Dreamers, pitfalls he judged Trump to have sidestepped.[1] He concluded that the first twelve months of the second term would be the most consequential period of the confrontation.[1]
