# 2025 Russia–Azerbaijan Rift

The **2025 Russia–Azerbaijan rift** was a period of sharply cooled relations between Moscow and Baku in mid-2025, driven by law-enforcement actions on both sides and by [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]]'s public alignment with [[ukraine|Ukraine]] against Russia.[1]

## Kremlin response

On Tuesday, July 22, 2025, [[kremlin|Kremlin]] spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said Russia disagrees with Azerbaijan's stance on the Ukraine conflict but hopes the divergence will not hinder efforts to restore bilateral ties.[1] Peskov acknowledged the differences in the two countries' positions but insisted this had not been an obstacle in relations, adding that Moscow seeks "to move past what we hope is a brief period of cooling in bilateral relations and avoid sacrificing mutually beneficial interests for the sake of a short-term situation."[1] The remarks were reported by [[rt|RT]].[1]

## Aliyev's position

Azerbaijani President [[ilham-aliyev|Ilham Aliyev]] had recently urged Ukraine to "never agree to occupation."[1] Speaking at the Shusha Global Media Forum, Aliyev encouraged Kyiv to consider Azerbaijan's approach to resolving its territorial dispute with Armenia over Nagorno-Karabakh — a longstanding conflict that ended in 2023 after Azerbaijan retook the region by force, prompting the departure of its remaining ethnic Armenian population.[1]

## Arrests and reprisals

The week prior, the Kremlin detained the leader of the Azerbaijani diaspora in Russia, an action connected to a crackdown on Azeri criminal organizations.[1] The move provoked Azerbaijan into arresting and imprisoning several Russian journalists working for Sputnik, accusing them of being agents of the Kremlin.[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that Azerbaijan "continues to serve as the accelerant that will merge the Ukraine and Israeli Wars, while Russia continues to try and be the adult in the room."[1] He described Azerbaijan as a small but strategic piece of land sitting directly between Russia and Iran while maintaining a deepening relationship with Israel, noting that the [[baku-tbilisi-ceyhan-pipeline|Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline]] remains a critical conduit between Azerbaijan and Israel through Turkey, even as the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]]'s [[north-south-transportation-corridor|North–South Transportation Corridor]] solidifies the Caspian Sea as a major energy corridor with Baku as the central node.[1]

He identified Turkey as the decisive variable, noting Turkey's historic ties to Azerbaijan and the likelihood that President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] was being pressured by [[nato|NATO]] to help the Azeris "twist the knife" on [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and Iran.[1] Against that, Erdogan had been flirting with BRICS and the SCO, making peace with Iran and the Arabs, and facing a collapsing situation in Syria as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] recklessly pushed to expand Israel's borders.[1] Putin and Erdogan had also announced a third round of Russia–Ukraine peace talks to be hosted in Istanbul.[1] The expectation offered was that Erdogan would play his cards close to the chest while continuing to antagonize the Zionists.[1]
