# 2021 DOJ School Board Memo

The 2021 school board memorandum was a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] directive that focused federal law-enforcement attention on threats against school board members and educators.[1] It became a major political controversy during the Biden administration because critics argued it chilled the speech of parents objecting at local school board meetings.[1]

## Origins

Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] signed the memo in October 2021, citing a "disturbing spike in harassment, intimidation, and threats of violence against school administrators, board members, teachers, and staff" amid broader disputes over COVID-19 policies and the treatment of race and gender in schools.[2]

## 2023 House Judiciary subpoenas

In early February 2023, the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] under chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-Ohio) issued its first subpoenas of the new Congress over the memo, targeting Garland, [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]], and Education Secretary [[miguel-cardona|Miguel Cardona]].[2] The subpoenas followed more than 100 letters that Judiciary Republicans had sent about the 2021 memo, and were reported by [[the-hill|The Hill]].[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] asked whether the House GOP was finally ramping up to take on the [[deep-state|Deep State]], and whether the officials would comply.[2] He noted that [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] had ignored a congressional subpoena for over two years, and cited [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]'s claim that if subpoena recipients fail to obey, the Sergeant at Arms has an avenue to request the military to arrest them — a route the House GOP would have to pursue vigorously.[2] DeLarme questioned whether it would come to that, or whether the individuals were "merely players on a stage" in a movie the public was watching, with the effort amounting to placation leading nowhere.[2]

## Internal warnings disclosed in 2026

Newly released DOJ documents reported in June 2026 show internal concerns among department officials about the memorandum at the time it was issued.[1] The records indicate that some officials raised questions about how the memo could affect parents speaking out at school board meetings.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the disclosure to a similar "internal warnings were raised and ignored" pattern reported in recent weeks concerning the Mar-a-Lago raid, suggesting the public is being prepared to recognize [[weaponization-of-government|weaponized government]] as a sweeping and long-running phenomenon rather than a series of isolated incidents.[1]
