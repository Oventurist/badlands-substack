# Solar Radiation Management

Solar radiation management (SRM), also described as solar radiation modification, is a class of proposed geoengineering interventions intended to cool the planet by deflecting a portion of incoming sunlight.[1] Proposed techniques include spraying reflective particles high into the atmosphere and spreading glass beads over polar ice to achieve a similar cooling effect.[1]

## United Kingdom rejection

In September 2025 Britain announced it had no plans to use the controversial technique of blocking some of the sun's rays to cool the planet, stating that the Antarctic was not the place to experiment with risky interventions aimed at slowing rising sea levels.[1] "The government is not in favour of solar radiation management," British authorities said in response to a report by parliament's Environmental Audit Committee, adding that "many parties considered Antarctica was not the place for experimentation given the potentially significant environmental implications."[1]

The announcement followed a warning by a team of more than three dozen polar scientists that proposals to artificially cool the world's polar regions were expensive, potentially dangerous, and unfeasible.[1]

## Debate

Some scientists and entrepreneurs argue that surpassing potential warming "tipping points" that could threaten life on Earth justifies exploring all options.[1] Others fear unintended side effects and argue that such interventions distract from adaptation strategies and from reducing emissions through a transition to a greener economy.[1] Reporting noted that "a number of moonshot solutions to cool the planet have gained traction in recent years amid faltering efforts to stem global warming by cutting emissions."[1]
