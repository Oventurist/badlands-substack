# The Pied Pipers of Babylon

The Pied Pipers of Babylon is a book by the legal researcher [[verl-k-speer|Verl K. Speer]], archived at the Internet Archive, which Badlands Media has credited as the inspiration for its article series on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1]

## Content cited

The passage Badlands quotes, from page 168, addresses the creation of trusts by implication. Speer writes that it is not necessary for the word "trust" to be used, and that if the language of an arrangement fairly interpreted means that the one to whom property is transferred is legally bound to use it for the benefit of others, then a trust arises.[1] He goes on to describe trusts implied in fact, sometimes called resulting trusts, which rest on an intention of the parties expressed not in words but in acts and surrounding circumstances.[1]

## Use in the Federal Reserve series

The Badlands author presents Speer's account as the clearest available statement of how unnamed arrangements can carry legal trust obligations, and applies it to argue that the American people function as trustor and nominal beneficiary of a credit trust administered by the Federal Reserve.[1] The book's framing of [[trust-law|trust law]] as fundamental to all other law underpins the article's argument that legal literacy is a form of situational awareness in [[fifth-generation-warfare|5G warfare]].[1]
