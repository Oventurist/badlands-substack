# 2024 Trump–Zelensky Meeting

The 2024 Trump–Zelensky meeting was a September 27, 2024 encounter between Republican presidential nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], described in contemporaneous reporting as opening "a new chapter in a fraught relationship roiled by skepticism from the Republican presidential nominee and his party over U.S. involvement in the Russia-Ukraine war."[1]

## Background

The meeting was the two leaders' first since 2019, and came in the final stage of a U.S. presidential race in which partisan divisions over American funding of the war in Ukraine had become a central issue.[1] The 2019 relationship had been dominated by the so-called "perfect phone call" between the two men, which became the predicate for Trump's first impeachment.[1]

## The meeting

Trump said it was an "honor" to meet with Zelensky, while the gathering also illustrated both leaders' cautious approach to one another amid their differing views of the United States' role in the conflict.[1] Before the meeting began, Trump said he had a "very good relationship" with the Ukrainian president and that he also had a "very good relationship" with Russian President Vladimir Putin; Zelensky interjected, "I hope we have more good relations."[1] After the meeting ended, Trump predicted a "very fair and rather rapid deal" to end the war if he won in November, but offered no specific details.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media writer Burning Bright treated the appearance as heavy "SIGNAL," arguing that Trump's statements alongside Zelensky confirmed long-running theories held since before Russia's "Special Military Operation," and describing the remarks as "yet another masterclass in asides" best approached with "Bicameral thinking."[1] Burning Bright highlighted Trump's comment that the Ukrainian president "could have played cute and he didn't play cute," reading it as a reference to the 2019 call and the impeachment episode, and Trump's claim that Ukraine had been through "more than almost any country," which he read as a veiled reference to the [[euromaidan|Maidan]] events and subsequent violence in the Donbas.[1] In this reading, Trump's invocation of his relationship with Putin was the point of the exercise: "Peacemakers are on deck," with the public performance serving to "engender the mandate for peace."[1]
