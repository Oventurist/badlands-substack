# October 2020 Intelligence Officials' Letter on the Hunter Biden Laptop

The October 2020 intelligence officials' letter was a controversial public statement alleging that the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] bore "all the classic earmarks of a Russian disinformation operation."[1] The statement, signed by former intelligence officials, falsely implied that the New York Post's reporting about [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] was the product of Russian disinformation.[1]

## Congressional pressure in 2023

By February 2023, pressure was ratcheting up on the signers of the statement to agree to on-the-record transcribed interviews with investigators for the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] and the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]].[1] In letters dated February 13, 2023 and obtained by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]], Judiciary Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-Ohio) and Intelligence Chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] (R-Ohio) wrote that their committees were conducting oversight of federal law-enforcement and intelligence matters within their respective jurisdictions.[1]

The chairmen noted that the Judiciary Committee had made a prior request for documents and information about the October 2020 statement, and that the request — including a request for a transcribed interview before the committees — remained outstanding, with the documents and testimony described as necessary to further the oversight.[1] "You have been on notice about our oversight request—and aware the request is outstanding—for months. For your convenience, we have attached the letter from the Judiciary Committee dated April 6, 2022. To date, you have not complied with this request. Accordingly, we reiterate our requests and ask that you comply promptly," Jordan and Turner wrote.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that requests had previously been made to these former officials by GOP committee members and were ignored, but that this was when the House was run by Democrats.[1] With the GOP-run House and its committees on offense, he expected the recipients would either comply or face subpoenas, adding that the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] had set a precedent for enforcing such demands.[1]

## Clapper's February 2023 walk-back

On February 13, 2023, [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] — a key signer and former Director of National Intelligence — accused Politico, the outlet that first reported the letter, of distorting "what we said," more than two years after publication.[2] The letter had been signed by fifty-one former intelligence officials days after the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] first revealed the existence of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s laptop in October 2020, and was subsequently amplified by then-candidate [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[2] By early 2023 it was under investigation by the Republican-led [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary]] and [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence]] committees.[2]

## Badlands analysis of the letter's wording

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the laptop was "the gift that keeps on giving," and that if [[james-comer|Representative James Comer]] was to be believed the fifty-one officials might be summoned before the Judiciary Committee for questioning comparable to the recent hearing with Twitter executives.[2] He said he remained eager to learn how a letter could have been drafted and agreed upon by fifty-one experts only five days after the Post story broke.[2] DeLarme highlighted that the operative sentence claimed the emails "has all the classic earmarks of a Russian information operation," which in his view gave the signatories an out: they could split hairs and argue they never said the material was Russian disinformation, only that it bore indicators of such an operation.[2] He nonetheless predicted an explosive hearing that would force even fence-sitters to acknowledge the affair's irregularity.[2]
