# Insurrection Act

The Insurrection Act is the body of United States law that permits the president to deploy federal military forces domestically to suppress insurrection, rebellion, or civil disorder, and to enforce federal law when ordinary judicial proceedings are obstructed. It functions as a statutory exception to the general prohibition on using the armed forces for domestic law enforcement.

## January 2026 Minneapolis consideration

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said in January 2026 that he was considering invoking the Insurrection Act in response to the situation in Minnesota, posting the statement to [[truth-social|Truth Social]].[1] The remarks followed a second shooting involving [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]], continued protests, and the looting of federal vehicles during the unrest.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the circumstances met the plain meaning of insurrection, writing that it "sure seems to be an insurrection when federal officers are being attacked and federal property is being destroyed and the state is enabling it."[1] The framing tied state-level non-cooperation with federal immigration enforcement to the statutory predicate for federal deployment, and connected the Minneapolis unrest to the earlier [[minneapolis-ice-shooting-of-renee-nicole-good|shooting of Renee Nicole Good]] on January 7, 2026.[1]


## Border Assessment Under Trump (2025)

On his first day back in the White House in January 2025, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] signed an executive order declaring an emergency at the US southern border and directed senior officials to evaluate whether to invoke the Insurrection Act of 1807, which allows the president to use active-duty military personnel for law-enforcement duties inside the United States.[2] Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] were tasked with the assessment and were reported to be preparing recommendations for release in late April 2025.[2] Border crossings had reached a quarter-century low by that point, but the statute would further expand presidential power; Badlands commentary dismissed alarm over invocation as less far-fetched than "the TV narrative that nothing is going on behind the scenes."[2]
