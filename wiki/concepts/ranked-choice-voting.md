# Ranked Choice Voting

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is an electoral method in which voters rank candidates in order of preference and lower-ranked preferences are redistributed through successive tabulation rounds until a candidate reaches a majority.[1] In Badlands commentary it is treated as a mechanism that obscures accountability rather than improving representation.[1]

## Criticism in Badlands commentary

Writing in March 2026, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described ranked choice voting as "un-auditable, unverifiable" and cited Senator [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]]'s support for the system in Alaska as an example of an incumbent insulated from her own electorate.[1] Because of RCV, the commentary argued, Alaskan voters would be "hard pressed to hold 'Murkowski' accountable" for positions such as her refusal to support the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]].[1]

The broader claim advanced was that structural reforms to tabulation cannot be evaluated in isolation from the integrity of the underlying process: "We can't have real elections until we are honest about how fake they've been."[1]

## Relationship to election-integrity legislation

RCV appears in this commentary as the counterpoint to documentary proof-of-citizenship measures such as the SAVE America Act, which [[donald-trump|President Trump]] named a top priority ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]].[1] Opposition to citizenship and voter identification requirements on disenfranchisement grounds, and support for ranked choice voting, are presented as two features of the same "progressive" posture toward elections.[1]

## Louisiana prohibition (2024)

On March 20, 2024, the Republican-controlled Louisiana Senate advanced [[louisiana-senate-bill-101|Senate Bill 101]], legislation providing that a "ranked-choice voting or instant runoff voting method … shall not be used in determining the election or nomination of any candidate to any local, state, or federal elective office in this state," while permitting the method for absentee voting by military and overseas voters.[2] The measure passed 29–9, with two Democrats joining 27 Republicans in support and all opposition coming from Democrats.[2]

Bill sponsor and Republican Senator Blake Miguez, pressed on the need for a ban when no Louisiana locality used RCV, said the aim was to prevent future adoption of a "chaotic system" that could disenfranchise voters: "America is about … one person, one vote, and to have these complex tabulated ballots is un-American."[2]

## Use in the 2024 US Virgin Islands caucuses

Ranked-choice voting was used in the [[2024-us-virgin-islands-republican-caucuses|2024 US Virgin Islands Republican caucuses]] on February 8, 2024, where territorial GOP Chairman Gordon Ackley promoted it as producing "an unrigged caucus" that gave "every candidate a fair and equal playing field."[3] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the promotion sarcastically, writing that "the regime is pushing RCV like they pushed the vaccines" and advising readers to "just say no."[3]

## 2025 New York City Democratic mayoral primary

The June 2025 New York City Democratic mayoral primary was conducted under ranked choice rules, and as first-choice votes were tallied no candidate was set to secure a majority in the first round.[4] Former Governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] nevertheless conceded and declared state assemblyman [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]], who led the first-choice count, the winner before later rounds were run.[4] See [[new-york-city-mayoral-election-2025|the 2025 New York City mayoral election]].[4]

## As a cardinal sin of election administration

Election analyst [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] includes ranked choice voting among his [[eight-cardinal-sins|Eight Cardinal Sins]] of election administration — practices selected because, unlike electronic voting systems, they are not shared by all fifty states and can therefore distinguish jurisdictions in a comparative integrity model.[5] In his February 2025 [[smart-goal-setting|SMART goal-setting]] essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], the concentration of such demerits across states supplies the measurable component of his proposed reform program.[5]
