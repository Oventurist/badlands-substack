# Weaponization of the Federal Government

"Weaponization of the federal government" is the term used by the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and its allies for the use of federal investigative, prosecutorial and regulatory machinery against political opponents. Trump signed an executive order directed at ending the practice, supplemented by a related presidential memorandum on preventing abuses of the legal system and federal courts.

The Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026 records the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s statement that its complaint against [[district-of-columbia-bar|District of Columbia bar]] disciplinary authorities — filed on behalf of former Assistant Attorney General [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] — advances both the executive order and the memorandum. In that framing, attorney discipline proceedings against federal lawyers for internal executive branch deliberations are themselves an instance of weaponization, extended into the professional-ethics apparatus rather than the criminal law.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterizes the dispute as "weaponized ethics… being adjudicated," welcoming the government's argument that rights protected under law are at stake and hoping the resulting precedent might eventually be applied at all levels of government. She closes the segment with the single word "Accelerate," a recurring motif in Badlands commentary on [[accelerationism|accelerationist]] dynamics in institutional conflict.
