# 2024 Democratic National Convention

The 2024 Democratic National Convention, held in Chicago, functioned as a handoff ceremony from President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] after Biden's withdrawal from the presidential race.[1]

Biden planned to address the convention on its opening night, touting his partnership with Harris — a speech Axios described as "a changing of the guard ceremony for Democrats," kicking off a convention Biden had intended to close as his party's nominee.[1] It was a goodbye he had resisted for weeks as pressure built within the party following his debate performance against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in June.[1] As a sitting president who wanted another term, Biden was placed in the unusual position of celebrating his own accomplishments while urging Americans to elect a different Democrat.[1]

Party planners sought to focus his remarks on legislative legacy items including the bipartisan infrastructure law, the climate and health care reconciliation bill, and the [[chips-and-science-act|Chips and Science Act]].[1] Conventions normally take months to choreograph; after Biden's sudden July 21 withdrawal, organizers had four weeks to rework four nights of programming to honor Biden and reintroduce Harris.[1] Former Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was slated to speak on the opening night, with former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] taking the prime-time slot on Tuesday.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the convention was ostensibly meant to be the coming-out party for the left-wing agenda, anointing "the newest fake candidate for the Globalist Regime," but that the establishment could not project a unified narrative or brand heading into the coronation.[1] He argued Biden's lingering presence made what should have been a celebration double as "a state funeral," set against a Trump movement he judged never stronger, citing Trump's billion-view conversation with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1] The scheduled appearances of Clinton and Obama — in his estimation "THE two names most synonymous with the US contingent of the Globalist [[deep-state|Deep State]]" and Trump's two original adversaries — were, he wrote, the real signal in the noise as the "War of Stories" approached a series of inflection points.[1]

## Pre-convention replacement speculation

Months before the convention, the February 2024 release of the [[hur-report|Hur report]] made the July gathering the focal point of speculation over whether President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would remain the nominee.[2] [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]] predicted that Democrats would "ditch him before the Democrat convention takes place in July" because he was "just too embarrassing," citing plummeting approval ratings.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disagreed with that timeline, leaning instead toward a contested convention producing a [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]], [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]], or [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] candidacy, while noting that other Badlands writers expected the party to remain stuck with Biden — an outcome they read as [[devolution|devolution]] proof alongside Pence having been Trump's VP pick.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that a successful ouster would cost Democrats the last of their faux legitimacy and elevate figures such as Newsom and [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]], whose COVID-era records made them among the most reviled politicians in the country.[2]

## Mpox and calls to delay the convention

In the days before the convention, some officials called for the DNC in particular to be delayed or cancelled altogether for the betterment of public health, amid the propaganda push around monkeypox that accompanied the [[2024-mpox-global-health-emergency|2024 mpox global health emergency]].[3] The anonymous researcher [[julians-rum|Julian's Rum]], responding to a viral [[wall-street-silver|Wall Street Silver]] post about that push, resurfaced a 2020 [[qanon|Q]] drop concerning a last-minute change to the Democratic convention made ostensibly under the guise of COVID-19 measures.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] praised the connection as strong anon work but said he personally would not expect the convention to be delayed or cancelled, reading the narrative instead as evidence of "a true panic pattern developing for the [[deep-state|Deep State]]" ahead of what should have been its coronation of the next candidate.[3]

## Nights one and two

[[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] appeared briefly on the convention's first night, and Badlands commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked the appearance, describing repeated "good evening" greetings, a softened version of her trademark laugh, word salad, and a "Thank you and good night!" closing delivered in the middle of the evening's programming before she joined the crowd to watch the remaining speeches.[4] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] gave an emotional late-night farewell address on Monday, August 19, pushed out of primetime, and then left Chicago for a five-day vacation at the California ranch of donor [[joe-kiani|Joe Kiani]] rather than attending the rest of the convention.[4]

On Tuesday, August 20, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] returned to his hometown of Chicago to headline Day 2 alongside [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]], with the aim of transferring the energy of his 2008 campaign to Harris's bid.[4] Reporting noted Obama's balancing act between his partnership with Biden — whose departure from the race he had helped engineer with other party leaders — and his friendship with Harris.[4] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the return of the Old Guard undercut the ticket's progressive branding and asked whether the Obamas and Clintons were endorsing Harris ahead of an election steal or preparing the ground for an October surprise.[4]

Harris entered the convention leading [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] 46.6% to 43.8% in the FiveThirtyEight polling average, though [[chauncey-mclean|Chauncey McLean]] of [[future-forward|Future Forward]] warned publicly the day before that the super PAC's internal numbers were "much less rosy."[4] While Democrats gathered in Chicago, [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaigned in Michigan and [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in Kenosha, Wisconsin, both focused on crime and public safety.[4]

## Opening-day protests and security

Badlands coverage of the convention's opening day, August 19, 2024, drew on an Associated Press report that dozens of protesters broke through a security fence near the convention site as thousands marched to voice opposition to the war in Gaza.[5] Families with strollers, students and elected officials joined a largely peaceful march to the United Center to call for a ceasefire in the Israel–Hamas war, while a few dozen who broke away tore down sections of the outer security fence.[5] Several protesters who made it through were detained and handcuffed, and officers donned gas masks as demonstrators attempted to bring down a second fence; authorities said the inner security perimeter was never breached and that attendees faced no threat.[5] Chicago Police Superintendent [[larry-snelling|Larry Snelling]] said some of those who took down the fence threw water bottles and other objects at officers, and that police de-escalated without resorting to batons or chemical agents.[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the disorder as comedy at the party's expense, noting leftist protesters threatening police and a party activist crashing the stage before security intervened.[5] She highlighted a New York Post report that attendees could obtain free abortions or vasectomies blocks from the venue, with vasectomy appointments filling fast, quipping that one should never interfere with an enemy that is sterilizing itself.[5] She also flagged the convention's ID-check requirement for entry — Jesse Watters remarking on air, \"Walls and IDs? We were told that's racist\" — and pointed to Craigslist advertisements recruiting non-union actors to play \"Happy Democrats,\" which she read as objective evidence against the party's insistence on joy.[5]

## Night one commentary

Reviewing night one on Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[the-alphawarrior-show|The AlphaWarrior Show]] concluded that the dominant impression was desperation, with speakers shouting at the audience while insisting they were joyful in a manner she called forced and fake.[5] She again flagged as odd that [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], the nominee, spoke mid-evening on the first night and then remained, spotted in the crowd at the close of the program.[5] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] separately dissected the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] speeches on [[baseless-conspiracies|Baseless Conspiracies]].[5]

Burning Bright framed the convention through a boxing analogy, comparing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to [[floyd-mayweather|Floyd Mayweather]] — a counter-striker who made himself the story and forced opponents to fight on his terms — and arguing that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had no story to sell that did not involve Trump.[5]

## Coverage of the Biden-to-Harris handoff

ZeroHedge coverage cited in the brief noted that as delegates gathered in Chicago, the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] columnist [[maureen-dowd|Maureen Dowd]] wrote that \"a coup is still a coup,\" describing the removal of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] from the 2024 ticket as a behind-the-scenes operation orchestrated by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] and [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]], leaving the president feeling betrayed by his allies.[5] What was meant to be a triumphant gathering was, in that telling, a hotbed of tension and betrayal within the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]].[5]

## Night two: flag burning and the Obama speeches

On the evening of Tuesday, August 20, 2024 — the convention's second night — Chicago police officers in riot gear clashed with a crowd of anti-Israel protesters outside the venue while other demonstrators set an American flag on fire.[6] A crowd chanting "Free, free Palestine" gathered around the protester who lit the flag; when Real America's Voice reporter Ben Bergquam stepped forward and tried to pull the flag out of the flames, a protester appeared to shove him and told him, "Fuck this country! Burn it down!"[6] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that chants to burn down the country and eliminate the state of Israel were ubiquitous outside the hall and that "peacekeepers failed to keep the peace by every metric."[6]

Inside, state delegates shouted their support for [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in what Ashe called a bizarre roll call vote, after which [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]] took the stage to demand unity, mock their political adversaries and rebuke Democratic voters for a "Goldilocks" complex.[6] Barack Obama praised Harris as someone who "won't be focused on her problems, she'll be focused on yours" and who would not "punish those who refuse to kiss the ring or bend the knee," while Michelle Obama issued a direct call to action: "consider this to be your official ask: Michelle Obama is asking you, no I'm telling y'all, to do something."[6] Michelle Obama's 2016 formulation "when they go low, we go high" was widely invoked, with ABC News observing that on this night the couple did both.[6]

Ashe in America argued that the contrast between the contrived messaging inside the convention and the hate and violence outside was working against the party, noting that most major pollsters reported an increase in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s support since the convention began, and pointing to an [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] poll on X that drew over four million responses.[6]

## The Obama deployment as a signal

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the Obamas' appearance as "almost surreal on a few levels," arguing that the couple's long absence from the political spotlight until that moment revealed "an overarching thread of desperation that now hangs over the establishment."[6] In his account the speeches attempted to resuscitate the 2008 "Hope and Change" aesthetic for the final push of the 2024 cycle, an appeal he judged exhausted among centrists and left-leaning voters who had "already took the cheese once."[6] He further argued that the Obamas were advocating full-scale change on the back of an administration they had themselves endorsed, so that "the Old Guard is acknowledging how bad things ARE by promising to return to how bad things WERE."[6]

## Harris acceptance speech (August 22, 2024)

[[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] closed the convention on the night of Thursday, August 22, 2024 with an acceptance speech framing the election as a "fight for America's future" and depicting a second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] presidency as chaotic and dangerous.[7] She promised to be "a president who leads and listens, who is realistic, practical, and has common sense," pledged to push through the derailed bipartisan immigration bill, and affirmed goals such as ending the housing shortage and supporting [[nato|NATO]], while leaving most implementation questions to after Election Day.[7] [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] reported that across four days among Democrats in Chicago its correspondent never heard the words "white paper" or "study," and that speakers dwelt more on the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]]'s [[project-2025|Project 2025]] than on Harris's own program.[7] Badlands commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the proceedings as an astroturfed joy campaign and the speech as a word salad.[7]
