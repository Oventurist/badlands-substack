# Trans-Eurasian Belt Development

The **Trans-Eurasian Belt Development** is a multi-trillion-dollar Russian development policy for transcontinental transport and industrial corridors, described publicly by former Russian Railways president [[vladimir-yakunin|Vladimir Yakunin]] on March 23, 2015.[1] Yakunin argued that the programme must be an "inter-state, inter-civilization, project" and "an alternative to the current (neo-liberal) model, which has caused a systemic crisis," to be "turned into a world 'future zone'" based "on leading, not catching, technologies."[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents the programme as the international complement to Canada's sabotaged Arctic development plans, and links it to the Bering Strait rail corridor proposed by the governments of Russia and China, which would extend transportation corridors from the Eurasian New Silk Road through the Siberian development belt and into the Americas, connecting into the west coast and New York.[1] Since Yakunin's remarks, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Far East development has been unveiled as a "21st century national priority" for Russia, encompassing new cities, mining, transport corridors, oil and natural gas, and already featuring partners from China, Japan, South Korea, India and other APEC nations.[1]

Ehret argues that Putin's 2018 proposal that the United States join this win-win project matters not only because it would build trust, create business opportunities and re-establish the lost art of long-term thinking, but because it would link western business into the Asia-Pacific development process being shaped by China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and especially the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]].[1] Although tensions have been inflamed to schism China and India from cooperating directly on the Belt and Road Initiative, India's embrace of Russian Far East development investments has, in his view, created a non-linear flank capable of bringing the two Asian giants into harmony.[1]

## Yakunin's 2015 formulation
Former Russian Railways president [[vladimir-yakunin|Vladimir Yakunin]] said on March 23, 2015 that the programme must be an "inter-state, inter-civilization, project" and "an alternative to the current (neo-liberal) model, which has caused a systemic crisis," insisting the project "should be turned into a world 'future zone', and it must be based on leading, not catching, technologies."[2] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] repeated this framing in his August 2025 essay on the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin Alaska summit]], presenting the multi-trillion-dollar programme as the complement to Canada's [[frobisher-bay-domed-city|Frobisher Bay]] plan and to Russia's Far East development, now declared a "21st century national priority."[2]
