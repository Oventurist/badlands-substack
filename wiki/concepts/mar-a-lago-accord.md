# Mar-a-Lago Accord

The "Mar-a-Lago Accord" is the informal name given to a proposed American plan to restructure the global monetary system, discussed intensively in financial circles in early 2025.[1] It takes its name from [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]], echoing historical currency agreements such as the Plaza Accord.

## Content of the proposal

As described by the [[financial-times|Financial Times]], the concept derives from a detailed discussion paper by Council of Economic Advisers chair nominee [[stephen-miran|Stephen Miran]].[1] Under the scheme, America's trading partners would help weaken the dollar and commit to providing low-cost, long-term financing to the United States government, with compliance enforced by the threat of higher tariffs or the withdrawal of American security guarantees.[1]

The Financial Times noted a complication: because foreign sales of US Treasuries and the prospect of dollar losses could push up US interest rates and jeopardise the financing of federal budget deficits, foreign governments would be expected to extend the duration of their remaining Treasury holdings — even purchasing 100-year zero-coupon bonds from the US government, described by the paper as "in essence, free financing for a century."[1]

No formal announcement of such an initiative came from the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, and the president did not post about it; nonetheless Miran's paper, together with various remarks by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]], led Wall Street observers to conclude that something of the kind was in the offing.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers asked whether Trump's pressure to push Speaker Mike Johnson's large budget package through Congress was intended to create precisely this dynamic.[1] They also observed that the Financial Times considered the article important enough to publish outside its paywall, and argued that when "the mouthpiece of the Regime" warns that a coming change to global trade and the monetary system is "very, very, very bad," the warning itself is informative.[1]
