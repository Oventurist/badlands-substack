# Arizona Proof-of-Citizenship Voter Registration Law

The Arizona proof-of-citizenship voter registration law is a 2022 Arizona statute imposing new restrictions on voting, portions of which the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] allowed the state to enforce ahead of the November 2024 election.[1]

## Supreme Court order

On Thursday, August 22, 2024, the Supreme Court allowed Arizona, at least for the time being, to toughen some voting requirements, ruling that people registering to vote before the coming election must show proof of citizenship.[1] The decision was issued in a terse, unsigned order and handed a partial victory to Republicans who supported the 2022 law.[1] The Court declined, however, to allow Arizona to put into effect another part of that law, which could have prohibited tens of thousands of already-registered voters from participating in the presidential election or casting any ballots by mail unless they provided proof of citizenship.[1]

## Division among the justices

The decision did not include any legal reasoning, which is common in such emergency applications, but there were signs that the Court was divided, and that Chief Justice John G. Roberts Jr. and Justice Brett M. Kavanaugh may have split their votes between two factions.[1] The order named four justices who wanted to keep the state from enforcing both measures — Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan, Amy Coney Barrett, and Ketanji Brown Jackson — and three who wanted to let the state put both provisions into effect: Clarence Thomas, Samuel A. Alito Jr., and Neil M. Gorsuch.[1]

## Practical effect

Under the mixed decision, Arizonans newly registering to vote for the coming election were required to provide copies of one of several documents, including a birth certificate or a passport, in order to prove their citizenship.[1]
