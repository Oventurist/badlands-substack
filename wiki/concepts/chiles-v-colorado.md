# Chiles v. Colorado

Chiles v. Colorado is a United States Supreme Court decision, announced at the end of March 2026, in which the Court ruled 8-1 in favor of a Christian mental health counselor and struck down Colorado's 2019 law banning so-called "conversion therapy" for minors as a violation of the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]

## Holding

Writing for the majority, Justice [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]] held that the statute unconstitutionally discriminated on the basis of viewpoint.[1] The law prohibited licensed counselors from providing talk therapy aimed at helping clients reduce unwanted same-sex attractions, change sexual behaviors, or align their gender identity with their biological sex, while expressly permitting those same counselors to offer "acceptance, support, and understanding" to clients exploring gender transition or identity.[1] The opinion emphasized that speech does not lose constitutional protection simply because the government labels it "treatment" or a "therapeutic modality," and reversed the ruling of the Tenth Circuit.[1]

## The plaintiff

The plaintiff, a licensed professional counselor named Chiles practicing in Colorado Springs, provides exclusively talk therapy with no medications or coercive techniques, and works with clients — including minors — who seek faith-integrated counseling aligned with her Christian beliefs.[1] Some of her clients request affirmation of their current identity, while others ask for help pursuing goals such as reducing same-sex attractions or living in alignment with their biological sex.[1]

## Dissent

Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] filed the lone dissent, warning that the ruling "opens a dangerous can of worms" and could impair the ability of states to regulate medical and mental health care, potentially allowing licensed professionals to provide what she described as "substandard medical care."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the 8-1 outcome as evidence that eight of nine justices agree the government cannot engage in viewpoint discrimination, "even against Christians," and placed the decision within a long run of First Amendment victories from what she called "Trump's Supreme Court."[1] The line of precedent she cited runs from NIFLA v. Becerra and Janus v. AFSCME (2018) through Iancu v. Brunetti (2019), Espinoza v. Montana Department of Revenue (2020), Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta and Mahanoy Area School District v. B.L. (2021), Shurtleff v. Boston, Carson v. Makin and Kennedy v. Bremerton School District (2022), 303 Creative LLC v. Elenis (2023), NRA v. Vullo and Moody v. NetChoice (2024), and Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton (2025).[1] She referred to the dissenting justice as "Autopen Jackson" and noted how many such First Amendment cases originate in [[colorado|Colorado]].[1]
