# Speculation

**Speculation**, in the sense argued by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], is the act of forming a theory without firm evidence to back it up — a practice he defends as normal, necessary, and unjustly stigmatised in contemporary political discourse.[1] His February 7, 2023 essay "When Did 'Speculation' Become a Dirty Word?" (subtitled "When Did 'Hope' Become Weakness?") treats the term as the central battleground between independent thought and narrative compliance.[1]

## The argument

Carlson opens by disclaiming any insider information, then recounts a face-to-face conversation about classified documents in which his interlocutor unexpectedly proved knowledgeable.[1] Carlson argued that it would be foolish to believe the classified documents found in [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s possession were random rather than part of a larger plan, reasoning that missing documents are always noticed and that someone had known about them all along while choosing to do nothing.[1] The same conversation turned to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], with the other party voicing the common complaint that Trump should "keep his mouth shut"; Carlson replied that Trump is "literally a genius" and that everything he says is planned for effect, offence included.[1]

Carlson compares the repetition of that complaint to local news anchors reciting identical scripts — a phenomenon some attribute to the [[4-am-talking-points|4 AM talking points]].[1] He extends the standard claim that the Left has been brainwashed by the mainstream media, arguing that parts of the Right have also been conditioned — specifically, conditioned to lose hope and to see only a bleak present and bleaker future.[1]

## Hope, inflation and the debt thesis

The essay ties one's outlook to one's reading of the preceding two years.[1] If the reader believes that period represented a process put in place by Trump and his people to dismantle a corrupt system, hope follows; if the reader believes it was simply the product of the Biden administration's damaging policies, pessimism follows.[1] Carlson advances the speculative thesis that Trump deliberately jump-started record inflation to force higher interest rates, and that the combination would destroy the corrupt world banking system.[1]

He draws the analogy of a household with heavy credit-card debt and an adjustable-rate mortgage that is pushed into bankruptcy when rates jump.[1] With US government debt at $31 trillion and growing, he argues, inflation plus rate increases by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] create a situation in which the corrupt system cannot even service the interest on its debt and therefore implodes — leaving the world banking system on the hook.[1] Trump, in his framing, is "the judo master" using the [[deep-state|deep state]]'s own weight against it.[1]

## Speculation as an ordinary human act

Carlson asks who decides what counts as "firm evidence" — the mainstream media, history books, Pfizer scientists, "the experts" — and concludes none of these can be trusted.[1] Given that, he argues, no theory can be advanced without some degree of speculation.[1] He catalogues everyday examples: choosing a spouse speculates on fidelity and endurance; starting a family speculates on being a good parent; buying a house speculates on rising value and the absence of fire, earthquake or mudslide; buying stock speculates on appreciation and accepts risk.[1] Even religious faith qualifies, he writes: Christianity involves speculating that repentance and giving one's life to Christ leads to Heaven, and ultimately that God exists and His grace suffices.[1]

From this he derives the essay's sharpest formulations: "To not speculate is to be a mental slave. Slaves don't speculate; their decisions are made for them," and "To follow a narrative is to be a mental slave."[1] Those who accept media narratives, he argues, are choosing not to speculate — treating what they are told as firm evidence.[1] He cites vaccination as the paradigm case: people took "the jab" because remaining unvaccinated felt like speculation to them, on the reasoning that if the vast majority agree, the vaccines must be safe.[1] The same majoritarian logic, he says, produces the conclusions that Trump is bad, that walls are racist, and that Biden won the election fairly.[1]

## Conclusions

Carlson asserts that the media, the medical industry, Big Pharma, most politicians, Wall Street and Hollywood are all not telling the truth, leaving the public needing to speculate more often than at any point in history.[1] "Preparation is speculation," he writes, and asks who has the right to tell him to be pessimistic or to live in fear, and whom his cautious optimism harms even if it were delusional.[1] He poses the rhetorical question "Does extinguishing optimism validate pessimism?" and reads the instruction "stop speculating" as meaning "stop planning, stop preparing mentally and physically — just give in to what the media tells you."[1]

As a practical rule of thumb amid propaganda, he recommends waiting 24 to 48 hours before openly speculating on most things, while maintaining that even a fully informed opinion remains speculation — "and that's not a bad thing."[1] The piece carries Badlands Media's standard disclaimer that contributor articles represent the author's opinions, and links to Carlson's personal Substack.[1]
