# Systemic Racism and Urban Wildlife Study

The **systemic racism and urban wildlife study** was a paper by Chloé Schmidt and Colin J. Garroway of the University of Manitoba, Winnipeg, published in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, arguing that systemic racism shapes the demography of urban wildlife populations.[1]

## Findings as reported

The authors wrote that "systemic racism alters the demography of urban wildlife populations in ways that generally limit population sizes and negatively affect their chances of persistence."[1] Examining 39 terrestrial vertebrate species across 268 urban locations in the United States, they reported "generally consistent patterns of reduced genetic diversity and decreased connectivity in neighborhoods with fewer White residents."[1] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] summarized the result as the claim that white neighborhoods have greater abundance and diversity of animal life and that racism is to blame.[1]
