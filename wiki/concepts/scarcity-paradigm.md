# Scarcity Paradigm

The Scarcity Paradigm is a term used by [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] to describe what he argues is a deliberately constructed narrative of physical limits and inevitable decline, rather than a neutral observation about resource constraints. In his framing, scarcity has functioned as "the central operating lie" of the globalist [[deep-state|deep state]]'s [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian]] machinery — a macro-narrative refined over decades and then reinforced by engineered conditions on the ground.

## Structure of the argument

Burning Bright describes the paradigm as running on two parallel tracks. The first is external: destabilization operations in the Middle East and other energy-producing regions that punish sovereign peoples by making them dependent on adversarial or unstable suppliers. The second is domestic: corporate and regulatory architectures that constrain American energy production and distribution while routing political and financial power through the administrative state.

The [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] era is presented as an especially aggressive acceleration of both tracks. What was framed publicly as climate and justice policy operated, in this reading, as a mechanism for locking in lower energy densities while channeling enormous flows of public capital through networks of aligned nonprofits — a dynamic he sees confirmed by the criminal referrals issued by EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] over [[inflation-reduction-act|Inflation Reduction Act]] grants moved through thinly capitalized entities such as [[power-forward-communities|Power Forward Communities]] and the [[greenhouse-gas-reduction-fund|Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund]] intermediaries.

## Reverse indicators

Burning Bright argues that two years of executive orders dismantling energy limits and reopening pathways in nuclear power and supersonics amount to a restoration of "energetic sovereignty" rather than mere regulatory housekeeping. He treats mainstream media coverage as an involuntary confirmation: outlets that once celebrated offshore wind commitments now lament halted projects in states such as Massachusetts, where promised long-term industrial footprints delivered only temporary construction booms before cost realities and investor flight intervened, while the same channels issue near-daily warnings that data center expansion threatens grids, water, and rural communities. His summary maxim: "the enemy always attacks what he fears most."
