# The Innocence of Muslims

"The Innocence of Muslims" is a short anti-Islam video trailer produced by [[nakoula-basseley-nakoula|Nakoula Basseley Nakoula]], a Coptic Christian, and posted online under the pseudonym "Sam Bacile." The trailer mocked the Islamic prophet Mohammad, depicting him as everything from a bozo and womanizer to a predator and homosexual.[1]

## Use as the Benghazi explanation

The video became the centerpiece of the Obama administration's initial public explanation for the September 11, 2012 [[benghazi-attack|attack on the U.S. diplomatic compound and CIA annex in Benghazi]], in which Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|J. Christopher Stevens]], Foreign Service information management officer Sean Smith, and CIA contractors [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]] were killed.[1] According to Fox News reporting on [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s memoir *Hard Choices*, Clinton was the first member of the administration to publicly link the anti-Islam video to the attack, in a State Department press release issued in her name at 10:07 p.m. on September 11, 2012 — before she called President [[barack-obama|Obama]] to provide an update.[1] The release said some had "sought to justify this vicious behavior as a response to inflammatory material posted on the Internet."[1]

[[susan-rice|Susan Rice]], then U.S. ambassador to the United Nations and Obama's national security adviser, subsequently made the rounds of the Sunday news shows to push the claim that the attack was a protest over the video that got out of hand.[1]

## Contradicting evidence

Sources told Fox News in late September 2012 that U.S. officials knew the assault was terrorism within 24 hours, and personnel on the ground in Libya reported a direct assault rather than a protest gone awry.[1] A mortar strike that killed Woods and Doherty placed three of five rounds on target from more than half a mile away in darkness in under a minute, an accuracy that multiple military and intelligence professionals said required military training and premeditation.[1] [[kris-paronto|Kris "Tanto" Paronto]], one of the CIA security contractors who survived the attack, said he first heard of the film or of protests roughly 36 hours afterward, while recovering in Germany, and that the movie played no part in Benghazi.[1] House Select Committee hearings later established that Clinton was aware the attacks were well-crafted and not spurred by the film.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands Media argues the video narrative was not a hasty cover story but a pre-planned pretext, chosen because it allowed the administration to link Benghazi to the broader [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]] uprisings and to justify prosecuting the filmmaker — an application in practice of the speech restrictions embodied in [[un-resolution-16-18|UN Resolution 16/18]] and, in this reading, a step toward normalizing [[sharia-law|Sharia]]-style blasphemy limits in the United States.[1]
