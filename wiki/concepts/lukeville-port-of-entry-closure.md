# Lukeville Port of Entry Closure

The Lukeville Port of Entry closure was the December 2023 federally ordered shutdown of an Arizona border crossing, undertaken so that personnel could be redirected to processing migrants, and which became a flashpoint in the state's dispute with the Biden administration over border costs.

The port was closed at the federal government's order on a Monday in early December 2023, forcing all American and Mexican residents to drive several hours to the next closest port of entry at Nogales, Arizona, where travelers faced lines up to five hours long according to Rep. [[juan-ciscomani|Juan Ciscomani]].[1]

Gov. [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]], a Democrat, called the situation an "unmitigated humanitarian crisis" that had "put Arizona's safety and commerce at risk," writing to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that "for far too long, Arizona has continued to bear the burden of federal inaction in managing our southern border."[1] She demanded the reassignment of military personnel to the area and reimbursement of $512 million the state had spent responding to the border crisis, and traveled to the border with the state's top [[national-guard|National Guard]] official.[1]

The closure coincided with a sharp rise in crossings through the Tucson sector, where weekly arrests climbed from about 12,000 to 17,500 in the week ending November 30, 2023.[1]
