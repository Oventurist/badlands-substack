# Federal Advisory Committee Act

The Federal Advisory Committee Act is a 1972 federal law that governs bodies that provide advice to the executive branch. It requires entities meeting the definition of a "federal advisory committee" to follow rules on public disclosure, hiring and other practices, so that the government receives advice that is transparent and balanced rather than the product of closed, unaccountable panels.[1]

The statute returned to prominence on January 20, 2025, when the public interest law firm [[national-security-counselors|National Security Counselors]] filed a 30-page complaint alleging that the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] — the nongovernmental cost-cutting panel that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] created and handed to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] — is an unregistered federal advisory committee operating in violation of the 1972 law. The filing, made within minutes of Trump's inauguration, opened a legal battle over one of the administration's signature initiatives.[1]
