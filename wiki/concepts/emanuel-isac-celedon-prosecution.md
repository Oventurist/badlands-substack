# Emanuel Isac Celedon Prosecution

The prosecution of Emanuel Isac Celedon was a federal corruption case against a former [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] officer who allowed aliens and cocaine to cross the southern border in exchange for cartel bribes.[1]

## Charges and sentence

Celedon, 37, of Laredo, Texas, pleaded guilty on March 11, 2024 to his role in illegally smuggling illegal aliens into the United States through the Lincoln Juarez Port of Entry in Laredo.[1] He also admitted to bribery and attempted importation of cocaine for accepting money to allow what he believed was cocaine to cross into the United States from Mexico.[1]

US District Judge Diana Saldana imposed a total of 117 months in prison across the two cases, to be followed immediately by four years of supervised release, and ordered him to pay a money judgment of $17,980.[1] At the hearing the court noted that Celedon's job was to protect the United States from the introduction of controlled substances and from people not authorized to be in the country, and that he had failed in both regards; Judge Saldana added that he was deeply involved in the organization and appeared to want to go even deeper.[1] The sentencing was announced by US Attorney Nicholas J. Ganjei and reported by the Department of Justice.[1]
