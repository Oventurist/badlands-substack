# Debt Slavery

"Debt slavery" is the term Erik Carlson uses in "America's Greatest Threat" to describe the condition he believes the American tax and monetary system deliberately imposes on ordinary citizens. In his framing, taxes are not principally a revenue mechanism but "a key component of creating and maintaining debt slaves," a form of servitude that keeps the population in line and discourages pushback against those in power.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## The arithmetic of the claim

Carlson works through an illustrative calculation of what remains of a dollar earned. Assuming income and payroll taxes take roughly 30 percent, inflation another 10 percent, property tax 10 percent, and sales tax 10 percent, forty cents remain; subtracting a further five percent for miscellaneous levies such as car taxes and gas taxes leaves thirty-five cents.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) Setting aside ten percent in savings for eventual retirement reduces the figure to twenty-five cents — the portion left to cover food, shelter, health insurance, car insurance, clothing, fuel, electricity, and internet, before any interest on mortgages, student loans, car loans, or business loans.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He acknowledges the numbers are not run perfectly but insists the point stands: the majority of what Americans earn is taken from them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

A recurring theme is layered taxation of the same money — a dollar is taxed when earned, when spent, and when saved.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) Because the levies do not all arrive at once, Carlson argues, they are easier to hide and therefore easier to tolerate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Inflation as the largest tax

Carlson identifies [[inflation]] as "the biggest tax of all," produced by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] printing money out of nothing, backed by nothing, and diluting the purchasing power of existing money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He estimates real inflation since Joe Biden took office may total around 30 percent, averaging roughly 10 percent per year — considerably above official reporting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

This raises what he presents as an obvious question: if the government can print money at will, why tax at all? His answer is that it does not need to; taxation persists because its purpose is control rather than funding.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Enforcement and compliance

The essay is candid about the author's own compliance. Carlson states that he believes taxes are theft yet files and pays before the deadline, unwilling to risk jail and leave his family destitute.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He can refuse vaccination, refuse to believe media narratives, and speak against the system, but is, in his words, "afraid to leave the plantation" — the government retaining complete control through debt slavery.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He poses the counterfactual directly: absent the threat of the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], who would stay in line, and absent the funding of the system, there would be no system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

Freedom from taxation, in this account, would broaden individual choice — how much a person works and what they do for a living being heavily shaped by the need to stay financially afloat.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Dismantling the system

In "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", Joe Lange describes what he calls the global financial debt slavery system as ending, destined to collapse under hundreds of trillions of dollars of debt whose interest can no longer be serviced.[2] He argues the [[cabal|cabal]] already controls everything financially, and that what his financial series documents is the complete dismantling of a debt system controlled for many generations.[2]

The dollar, he writes, was the Keystone: established as the world's reserve currency and welded to oil through an agreement with Saudi Arabia, it forced central banks to hold dollars and let the cabal dictate foreign policy and punish non-compliant countries.[2] He predicts the coming crash will exceed the 2008 credit crisis and even the Depression because the world carries far more debt and the system is centralized around the dollar, and presents [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDCs]] as the tool for moving quickly out of debt slavery into honest money while limiting the duration of suffering.[2]
