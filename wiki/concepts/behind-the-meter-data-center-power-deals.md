# Behind-the-Meter Data Center Power Deals

Behind-the-meter data center power deals are arrangements in which large technology companies contract with the owners of power plants to plug their facilities directly into generation, bypassing the public electric grid.[1] Seeking a quick fix for fast-growing electricity demand, tech giants increasingly pursued such deals in preference to the longer and more expensive process of hooking into what has been described as a fraying grid serving everyone else.[1]

## Controversy

The practice raised questions over whether diverting power to higher-paying customers would leave enough for other users, and whether it is fair to excuse very large consumers from contributing to grid costs.[1] Utilities objected that the arrangements are not fair, while federal regulators moved to determine a policy quickly.[1]

The leading test case was the data center [[amazon|Amazon]]'s cloud subsidiary [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]] is building beside the Susquehanna nuclear plant in eastern Pennsylvania — the first behind-the-meter connection to come before the [[federal-energy-regulatory-commission|Federal Energy Regulatory Commission]], which rejected a deal that could eventually have routed 960 megawatts, roughly 40 percent of the plant's capacity, to the facility, enough to power more than half a million homes.[1]

## Drivers

The rapid growth of cloud computing and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] has fueled demand for data centers requiring power for servers, storage systems, networking equipment and cooling systems.[1] That demand has spurred proposals to bring retired nuclear plants back into service, to develop small modular reactors, and to build utility-scale renewable installations or new natural gas plants.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Ashe in America framed the fight as one between utilities determined to remain the gatekeepers of energy access and capacity management and technology firms that see those gatekeepers as unnecessary, predicting the latter would win: "Nothing can stop the Golden Age. Especially not dinosaur utility companies."[1]
