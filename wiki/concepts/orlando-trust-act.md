# Orlando Trust Act

The Trust Act is a municipal ordinance passed in 2018 by city authorities in Orlando, Florida, that prohibits city employees and law enforcement officers from inquiring about an individual's immigration status.[1] It functions as a local [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary]] measure, limiting municipal cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.[1]

## 2025 confrontation with the state of Florida

In April 2025 the act became the focus of a clash between Orlando's Democratic mayor, [[buddy-dyer|Buddy Dyer]], and the state government.[1] Dyer reportedly told a meeting with the Orlando Police Department that law enforcement should resist immigration enforcement and should adhere to the Trust Act.[1]

Florida Attorney General [[james-uthmeier|James Uthmeier]] responded with a letter, posted on X, warning Dyer that he could face removal from office if he implemented a policy of protecting illegal immigrants, and stating that Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] would reserve the right to remove him.[1] The letter said that "Sanctuary policies are not tolerated or lawful in Florida," and that failure to abide by state law "may result in the enforcement of applicable penalties, including but not limited to being held in contempt, declaratory or injunctive relief, and removal from office by the Governor pursuant to section 908.107, Florida Statutes and the Florida Constitution."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the episode to question why gubernatorial removal powers are so rarely exercised elsewhere, suggesting that the general non-enforcement of such statutes implies officials in other states tolerate open violations of law because they are "part of the same system."[1]
