# Durham Special Counsel Investigation

The **Durham Special Counsel investigation** was the inquiry led by [[john-durham|John Durham]] into the origins and conduct of the federal investigations of the [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] campaign — the scandal known in alternative media as [[spygate|Spygate]].[1] Durham was appointed as United States Attorney to examine the matter in 2019 and later elevated to Special Counsel by Attorney General [[william-barr|William Barr]].[1]

## Scope and the two halves of the mandate

Badlands Media contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] argued that Durham's mandate had two halves: the prosecutions actually brought against comparatively low-level figures, and a second half addressing the felonies committed when a warrant application built on fabricated material originating with the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] campaign was approved and renewed three times to surveil the Trump campaign during the 2016 election and into the Trump administration.[1] Much mainstream commentary in October 2022 held that Durham would ignore the second half, bring no further indictments, and spend his remaining months writing a final report; Cates disputed that reading.[1]

## The "counter-programming" strategy

Cates's thesis was that Durham was prosecuting the RussiaGate "joint venture" in reverse: using the most minor charges available against ground-floor participants as courtroom venues to surface evidence held back for half a decade.[1] The reason, in this account, was that a fair jury pool could not be assembled while a years-long media campaign — encompassing the New York Times, the Washington Post, CNN, ABC, NBC, CBS, Fox News, Time, Newsweek, NPR, and the major social platforms — continued to saturate the public with the collusion narrative.[1] Durham, Cates wrote, had to counter roughly six years of what he termed mass formation psychosis and relentless gaslighting before he could seek fair trials.[1]

Cates stipulated that, absent the propaganda campaign, enough evidence existed by June 2019 — and certainly after DOJ Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael E. Horowitz]] released his FISA abuse report in December 2019 — for Durham to have unsealed major indictments by early 2020.[1] But Durham was a realist who understood what would have happened had he moved early, while Trump was still in the White House and Barr still Attorney General.[1]

## Evidence surfaced at the two trials

The [[michael-sussmann|Sussmann]] trial placed on the record the existence of a private Clinton campaign spy ring operating through cybersecurity contractors embedded with federal agencies; its surveillance of Trump and his associates at Trump Tower and later at the White House; the campaign's provision of a target list; the trawling of federal databases; the theft of classified and non-public data used to build the [[alfa-bank-hoax|Alfa Bank Hoax]]; the delivery of that product, through [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]], to the media, the [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]; the February 2017 approach to the CIA using data taken from White House servers after the inauguration; testimony that FBI personnel were themselves subjects of investigations; and Durham's demonstration that more than 80 percent of the attorney–client privilege claims asserted by the campaign, Perkins Coie, [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]] and [[rodney-joffe|Rodney Joffe]]'s contractors were facially bogus.[1]

The [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] trial repeated the template, establishing that the FBI verified none of the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]] allegations used in the [[carter-page|Carter Page]] FISA warrant; that it offered [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] up to $1 million in October 2016 to corroborate the dossier, which he could not do; that Steele had not identified his primary sub-source when the warrant issued; that the Bureau knew by January 2017 that the sub-source, Danchenko, had been the subject of a Baltimore field office espionage investigation; that the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team and later the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel]] nonetheless renewed the warrant three times; that the FBI paid Danchenko as a confidential human source from March 2017 to October 2020; and that Democratic operative [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles H. "Chuck" Dolan]] was among the dossier's sources.[1]

## Potential charges

Cates concluded that, on the public record alone, Durham could charge Clinton campaign personnel and their contractors with espionage and theft of classified information, and could charge federal law enforcement and intelligence officials who continued illegally predicated spying after Trump's inauguration with treason.[1] Whether Durham would follow through, having already exposed much of the joint venture, remained the open question as the Danchenko trial wound down in Alexandria, Virginia in October 2022.[1]

## Cited as a Sequencing Precedent (2025)

An August 2025 Badlands Media essay compared Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]]'s litigation sequencing to the Durham investigation, arguing that both were long-game efforts rather than attempts at a single dramatic reveal.[2] The author wrote that Durham's indictments, depositions, and final report amounted to "laying stone after stone" until the foundation could bear the weight of full exposure, constructing an evidentiary record that later state and federal cases could stand on without collapsing.[2] In this reading, both campaigns aim to ensure that when the existing structure of control finally falls, a lawful framework already exists to keep the republic standing.[2]

## The Danchenko verdict and the long game

The [[trial-of-igor-danchenko|trial of Igor Danchenko]] ended in October 2022 with Judge [[anthony-trenga|Anthony Trenga]] dismissing Count One and the jury acquitting on Counts Two through Five, but the Badlands account argued that a conviction was never the true measure of the case's value.[3] By its reckoning, the prosecution placed the [[fbi|FBI]] itself on trial: [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] endured two days of hostile questioning and was revealed to be under Office of the Inspector General investigation and recommended for suspension over the [[carter-page|Carter Page]] FISA applications, while Danchenko's handler [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] was likewise identified as an OIG subject.[3] The case also generated an extensive evidentiary record — interviews and testimony from Spygate figures major and minor, plus phone, email, social media and travel records — that the Special Counsel could carry into future prosecutions.[3]

The investigation of [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles "Chuck" Dolan Jr.]], opened on the strength of information Danchenko gave the FBI and pursued through search warrants for Dolan's email, phone and social media records, was later transferred to the Durham Special Counsel, where Dolan remains a subject.[3] Special Agent [[ryan-james|Ryan James]], pulled into the Durham team in May 2021, assembled the records evidence used at trial.[3] The author likened Durham's approach to a mob prosecution — working upward through grand juries, witness stands and court filings — and argued that dismantling a criminal syndicate requires many investigations and trials, of which Danchenko was only one.[3]
