# Navarro Contempt of Congress Trial

The Navarro contempt of Congress trial was the September 2023 federal criminal proceeding against [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]], former White House trade adviser to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], for refusing to comply with a subpoena from the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]].[1]

## Jury selection and proceedings

A jury was seated on Tuesday, September 5, 2023, in a process observers expected to produce a rapid verdict; CNN described it as "what's expected to be a speedy trial for the one-time aide to former President Donald Trump."[1] Presiding judge [[amit-mehta|Amit Mehta]] had characterized Navarro's legal arguments as "pretty weak sauce" during pre-trial hearings the previous week, and rejected the executive privilege defense, ruling that "there was no formal invocation of executive privilege by [Trump] after personal consideration nor authorization to Mr. Navarro to invoke privilege on his behalf."[1]

## Charges and exposure

Navarro faced two counts of [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]], carrying a potential sentence of anywhere from ten days to one year in prison.[1] He stated that in the event of a conviction he intended to ask higher courts to review the legal merits of the case.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] placed the trial within a wider pattern of "politically persecutory" litigation, arguing that Navarro's appeal was a near certainty given what she described as demonstrated judicial bias, and that "no one actually believes a conservative can get a fair trial in DC."[1] She also drew a contrast between the prosecution of Navarro and what she characterized as the Biden Justice Department's obstruction of Republican congressional investigators after the GOP assumed House leadership.[1]
