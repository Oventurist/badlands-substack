# Badlands Brief: ICE Getting Boiled, Chinese Coup Chaos, & Carney Bends The Knee

"ICE Getting Boiled, Chinese Coup Chaos, & Carney Bends The Knee" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published on January 26, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The Brief describes itself as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war," pairing summarized news items with commentary from named Badlands contributors. This edition carries commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and covers five main subjects — the fatal shooting of a U.S. citizen during a federal immigration operation in Minneapolis, Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s conduct at the World Economic Forum, the aftermath of the U.S. capture of Venezuelan president [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], Canada's trade posture toward China, and reports of an attempted military coup in China — followed by two bonus items on Italy and Gaza and on European funding for Ukraine. The post carries the standard disclaimer that opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## Minnesota Madness and Fraud Cascade

The Brief's lead item reports that federal immigration agents fatally shot 37-year-old U.S. citizen [[alex-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] in Minneapolis on January 24 during an enforcement operation, igniting protests and political controversy in Minnesota and beyond. According to the summary, the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] said Pretti was armed with a handgun and resisted efforts to disarm him before a [[united-states-border-patrol|Border Patrol]] agent fired, killing him at the scene. The Brief notes that bystander videos and witness accounts suggest a different sequence: that Pretti was initially filming officers and was pepper-sprayed and pinned down before the shots, with some footage showing an agent taking his weapon before others fired.

Border Patrol Chief [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]] is described as defending the officers, characterizing them as victims and asserting that Pretti had interfered with their actions. The encounter drew sharp rebukes from local leaders, including Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]], who decried the federal operation as excessive and called for the withdrawal of federal agents and for accountability. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] used his Truth Social platform to support the federal agents, question the absence of local police involvement, and accuse Minnesota officials of "inciting insurrection." In a series of posts, Trump also linked the situation to alleged "theft and fraud" involving billions of dollars tied to state officials, framing the unrest in broader partisan terms. The Brief concludes that the incident has underscored growing tensions over immigration enforcement and federal–state relations.

Ashe in America's commentary is built around the slogan "No one is above the law." She argues that the phrase was repeated throughout the Biden administration while, in her telling, "the State destroyed every precedent and standard and norm in their pursuit of 'getting Trump.'" She quotes Trump's November 2023 Iowa remarks, repeated throughout the 2024 campaign: "And by the way, I've been indicted more times than Alphonse Capone. He's the most vicious of all gangsters. If he took you to dinner, and if you didn't look proper, if you laughed a little bit, he might think you're laughing at him, it's dangerous to laugh – he'd kill you immediately. He was indicted one time. I was indicted four times." She observes that the previous day had been the anniversary of [[al-capone|Al Capone]]'s death, calling him public enemy number one during Prohibition — "perhaps the greatest season of federal civil overreach before COVID."

Turning the slogan back on its originators, she asks whether state governments are above the law given that [[sanctuary-city|sanctuary]] policies "openly violate federal law"; whether city officials are above the law given municipal "newcomer playbooks" that she says provide aid, comfort, and access to entitlement programs to foreign criminals; and whether local businesses are above the law when brick-and-mortar establishments close during reported ICE activity, "ostensibly because they employ illegals." She predicts federal immigration enforcement in sanctuary jurisdictions will continue for the foreseeable future and endorses it. Against what she calls a "whole-of-legacy-press effort" to claim Americans oppose the administration's enforcement, she cites Rasmussen polling showing opinion divided along party lines and essentially unchanged since the previous summer, and argues that Trump won the popular vote on a mandate to remove "criminal invaders."

## Bessent Talks Smack in Davos; Bankers Freak Out

The second item reports that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent played a central diplomatic role at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in January 2026, attempting to steady global markets and allied partners amid a political storm triggered by Trump's aggressive foreign and trade policy initiatives. Bessent repeatedly urged European leaders and markets not to retaliate against Trump's threatened tariff measures linked to the administration's pressure campaign over [[greenland|Greenland]], the semi-autonomous Danish territory, arguing that escalation would harm long-term relations and citing the importance of established trade frameworks. He also sought to defuse speculation that European governments might sell U.S. Treasury holdings as leverage, dismissing such scenarios as exaggerated and not widely supported. On the forum sidelines he described U.S.–Europe relations as stronger than perceived and encouraged counterparts to "take a deep breath" about Washington's hard line on Greenland.

The Brief notes that Bessent engaged in sharply worded exchanges with domestic political figures, publicly mocking California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s criticism of Trump's diplomacy as lacking economic credibility. In remarks that reverberated beyond Europe, Bessent suggested that the western Canadian province of [[alberta|Alberta]] could serve as a "natural partner" for the United States, referencing discussions about the province's political identity and autonomy. The broader controversy eased mid-week when Trump announced what he described as a "framework of a future deal" with [[nato|NATO]] Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]], temporarily pausing tariff actions that had rattled global markets. The summary adds that Trump's statements about Greenland, including repeated inaccuracies about the island's legal status, drew scrutiny from independent fact-checkers, with NATO allies warning that such rhetoric undermines long-standing security arrangements.

Burning Bright's commentary argues that Trump "didn't go into Davos alone," bringing "two of his most potent (and understated) soldiers into enemy territory" and letting them take the lead for much of the week. He welcomes the wider MAGA world finally giving Bessent his due as "an underrated (and ultra dry) comedian" and "an absolute wolf at the global finance table," recalling that Bessent said in early 2024 that he envisioned a "Global Economic Re-Ordering" and wanted to be part of it. In Burning Bright's framing, Trump is engineering exactly that, "turning the planned Great Reset into a 'Good' One," an argument he says he has advanced since 2022. Trump, he writes, "walked into the den of vipers with two lions beside him" — Bessent and Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] — names "the enemy knows well," and letting them off the leash signals that "the Golden Age is accelerating, perhaps ahead of schedule."

Burning Bright describes Bessent as not merely the prime architect of Trump's Golden Age agenda but "the prime harbinger of the old System's demolition," arguing he may be better suited to demolition than construction because "he's done it before." He quotes his own 2025 long-form essay: "Bessent, in my estimation and close study over the last few months isn't some Hollywood caricature, but rather a battle-hardened operative whose trajectory from complicit insider to insurgent demolitions expert makes him the ultimate harbinger of the central bankers' downfall. And I would bet they know it … which is why many of Scottie's former 'friends' in high places are feeling a bit low these days, and casting more blame on him for the current state of their stateless apparatus—the Hegelian Hydra that is Globalism itself—even than his boss at present. No. They hate and fear him because he's been in the house, he's been in the machine … hell, he's made it run, at least in part, and so, who better than him to author a core piece of its destruction?" He directs readers to his essay "The Wolf of Main Street" for the fuller argument.

## Trump Flexes the Discombobulator

The third item reports that U.S. forces carried out a military operation in Venezuela on January 3 that led to the capture of President Nicolás Maduro and his wife [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]] and their transfer to the United States to face charges. In a January 25 interview, Trump said American forces employed a classified device he called the "[[discombobulator|Discombobulator]]" to disable Venezuelan defense equipment and prevent the launch of Russian and Chinese missiles during the raid, though details about the technology remain unclear. Trump asserted the weapon rendered enemy systems inoperative and ensured no rockets were launched in response.

In the political aftermath, Venezuela's legislature began debating a major overhaul of the oil sector aimed at ending decades of rigid state control. Lawmakers approved initial measures allowing private and foreign companies greater operational autonomy, lowering royalty and tax requirements, and providing for international arbitration — a significant shift from policies established under [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]]. Acting President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] has championed opening the oil industry to foreign investment since Maduro's ouster. A leaked recording of a post-operation meeting captured Rodríguez saying U.S. forces gave members of Maduro's cabinet "15 minutes to comply or they would kill us."

The Brief also reports that a group of U.S. lawmakers expressed support for opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] as a potential future leader of Venezuela, with members of the [[house-foreign-affairs-committee|House Foreign Affairs Committee]] said to have unanimously endorsed her after a closed-door meeting on Capitol Hill, despite Trump having previously dismissed her prospects. Separately, Trump suggested that NATO members could demonstrate the alliance's value by sending troops to help secure the United States' southern border, musing on social media that invoking NATO's mutual defense obligations might compel allied forces to assist with border security.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry reads the episode as coordinated messaging. He argues Acting President Delcy "Kokomo" Rodríguez "is playing her role to perfection," deploying public narratives in concert with Trump that hype cutting-edge U.S. military technology in order to elevate American power projection. He notes that a video of Rodríguez was leaked to a local journalism collective called La Hora de Venezuela, in which she claims that right after Maduro's capture she received a phone call telling her the remaining government leadership had 15 minutes to comply or be killed, and that leadership was initially told Maduro and his wife had been killed rather than captured. He calls this theater, pointing out that Rodríguez has already affirmed Maduro signed a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] order in December in anticipation of his "departure," and that her compliance with Trump's vision suggests the dramatic account is an embellishment "if not a flat out fabrication."

The purpose, in his reading, is twofold: the narrative gives Rodríguez credibility as an earnest opponent of unipolar hegemony while disclosing to the world that the U.S. military is already fielding new weapons technologies, helping reassert American military dominance and sparing her the label of a puppet of the [[state-department|State Department]]. He criticizes the "habitually-treasonous GOP" for continuing to champion Machado as Venezuela's rightful ruler despite Trump's dismissal of her, calling it another demonstration of Republican Party subversion. Trump's response, he writes, was to troll NATO by asking why the alliance has not sent soldiers to defend its leader from foreign invasion at the southern border, threatening in "a brilliant moment of TrumpaMania" to invoke Article Five.

He then turns to the commercial dimension: Trump has called on two of the world's largest commodity trading firms, [[vitol|Vitol]] and [[trafigura|Trafigura]], to begin brokering deals with potential buyers of Venezuelan oil. Both were named as defendants in a lawsuit filed by Maduro in U.S. and Swiss courts in March 2018, and both have had executives arrested for corruption; the commentary states that Trafigura's former CEO, Mark Wainwright, was sentenced in Swiss court the previous February to 32 months in prison after being found guilty of paying over $5 billion in bribes to oil officials in Africa to secure contracts, all connected to Maduro's 2018 suit. He speculates that once these firms and their banks begin circulating money for Venezuelan oil deals, banking regulators could be tripped into filing Suspicious Activity Reports if funds flow in patterns differing from those described in the lawsuit — a situation that, per "lengthy conversations with [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]]," has the potential "to bring the entire transnational central banking system to its knees." He closes with a single word: "Accelerate."

## Oh, Canada…

The fourth item reports that Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] said on Sunday that [[canada|Canada]] has no plans to pursue a free trade agreement with [[china|China]], responding to Trump's threat to impose a 100% tariff on Canadian imports if Ottawa finalized such a deal. Carney said Canada's recent agreement with China simply reduces tariffs on a limited number of products and does not constitute a comprehensive free trade pact, and reiterated that Canada will honor its commitments under the [[usmca|United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement]], which restricts negotiating free trade treaties with non-market economies such as China without notifying partners. The preliminary Canada–China arrangement announced earlier in the month includes tariff reductions on Chinese electric vehicles and lower duties on Canadian agricultural exports such as canola, following a period of elevated tariffs; China's foreign ministry said the arrangements were not aimed at third parties.

Ashe in America's accompanying essay responds to a viewer "Badlands Boost" read on Friday's episode of OnlyLands, which she says was for one cent as a protest and claimed she hates Canadians. She sets the record straight: "I do not now nor have I ever hated Canadians." Her argument is structural rather than ethnic. "Canada is a colony of the British Crown," she writes; the Prime Minister swears allegiance to the British Monarch, and Canada is "a significant beneficiary of and advocate for globalism — the real enemy in the shadow war." Therefore "Canada isn't real. Its power projection isn't real. It projects power on behalf of another. Canada is projecting Britain's power." She states that she hates scammers, thieves, "entitled free loaders," and governments, but not people groups, and that she stands with the people of all nations "to cast off foreign chains and govern themselves without interference," noting she has advocated for the truckers, for Alberta, and for liberation.

She lists three ways Canada's leaders are, in her framing, openly hostile to America: choosing to remain subjects of the monarchy Americans died to expel; giving "belt-and-road imperialist communists a foothold on our continent, right up on our border"; and "choosing to white knight for Denmark, preserving globalist control of northern shipping lanes." She argues Canada "might be our greatest national security threat due to their distance and disdain." She extends the argument to public health via [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]]'s agenda, noting that Canada created [[canola-oil|canola oil]] — "Canadian Oil, Low Acid" — a highly processed seed oil she links to chronic inflammation and obesity, and that Canada is the largest global exporter at 75–80% of supply. "What did they know about canola oil, and when did they know it?" she asks, concluding that RFK's war on highly processed seed oils "is an economic war on Canada," which she supports. On Carney's mixed messaging on China she asks: "Did Carney change his mind, or did [[king-charles-iii|King Charles]]?" Her summary: "I don't hate Canadians. I hate globalism," with Canada in 2026 serving as "the face of globalism, sewn onto the crown like a silence of the lambs skin suit."

## Big Trouble in Little China

The fifth item, written by GhostofBasedPatrickHenry, reports that social media accounts broke news on Friday of an alleged military coup in China. He quotes an X account cited by General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]]: "Latest news confirms: Zhang Youxia, Liu Zhenli, and Zhong Shaojun have been arrested simultaneously, and all of Zhang Youxia's family members have also been taken into custody. The news has been circulated among the top echelons of the CCP leadership. In addition to Zhang Youxia's absence from the opening ceremony of the seminar on studying and implementing the spirit of the Fourth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee of the Communist Party of China for provincial and ministerial-level leading cadres, he also missed the originally scheduled annual event at the National Defense University. More importantly, following the death of former Deputy Chief of the General Staff Wei Fulin, the funeral wreath sent by Zhang Youxia was also notified to be temporarily removed."

A further post from the same account is quoted: "The more rumors about Zhang Youxia being arrested spread, the more sensational and terrifying they become. I just heard that it was Wang Xiaohong's special service bureau that arrested military personnel, including Zhong Shaojun, who had only just been released, and he was arrested again. Seventeen people were taken in one go this time. Since the source of the information cannot be verified, we might as well let the bullet fly a little longer and see whether Zhang Youxia shows up at the Spring Festival group meeting or not. If Zhang Youxia has really been arrested, that would mean Xi Jinping and the red families have completely broken off and reached the point of no return. The big drama left to watch is how the red families will make their move at this critical juncture. As long as the red families join forces to counterattack, it's estimated that very few of Xi Jinping's close confidants would dare to resist to the end."

The commentary states that confirmation followed of a total of 17 officials arrested, including the three named generals — [[zhang-youxia|Zhang Youxia]], [[liu-zhenli|Liu Zhenli]], and [[zhong-shaojun|Zhong Shaojun]] — who are described as coming from the "red families," the original founding families of the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] that control most state-owned natural resources and infrastructure. The arrests are attributed to a special service bureau under [[wang-xiaohong|Wang Xiaohong]]. The Brief reports that the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] has accused Zhang Youxia of leaking classified intelligence on China's nuclear weapons technology to the United States.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry offers a "Good Twin and Evil Twin" framework, credited to Chris Paul: [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and Trump represent the Good Twin in their respective countries, while the Uniparty/Deep State and the arrested Chinese military leaders represent the other. He says he has found no direct connection between the Chinese deal Carney was pursuing and the failed coup, but considers the timing unlikely to be coincidental. His reconstruction is that Carney was dealing with Chinese technocrats — a group including central bankers, distinct from both the red family aristocracy and the military command, though with cross-factional alliances — and that the technocrats were the faction that brought China into the Western banking system and "opened up its economy for exploitation" before being effectively overthrown when Xi became General Secretary in 2012.

He quotes reporting attributed to the Washington Post and the Journal: "Since the summer of 2023, the party has unseated top officers in China's army, air force, navy, strategic-missile force and paramilitary police, as well as major theater commands—including the one focused on Taiwan. More than 50 senior military officers and defense-industry executives have been placed under investigation or removed from office in the past 2½ years, according to official disclosures reviewed by the Journal." And: "The [[central-military-commission|Central Military Commission]] had six professional military members when its current term started in 2022. Now it has just one active uniformed officer, Gen. [[zhang-shengmin|Zhang Shengmin]], who was promoted to vice chairman only in October after the purge of another general who held that role." He notes the [[financial-times|Financial Times]] of London has reported that the move consolidates Xi's power, which he argues is necessary "to stave off calamity," adding that many expect Trump to wield emergency executive authority against the [[color-revolution|color revolution]] in the United States and that "President Xi is simply ahead of the game."

## Bonus items

The first bonus item reports that the United States has formally invited Italy to become a founding member of the [[international-stabilization-force|International Stabilization Force]] for Gaza, according to a Bloomberg report cited by The Jerusalem Post. The invitation, delivered that week to Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]]'s office and Italy's Foreign Ministry, would not obligate Italian combat troops but would rely on Italy's earlier commitment to help train a future Gaza police force and leverage its political connections in the region. Italy's government declined to comment, and a White House spokeswoman did not confirm the offer, saying only that "announcements on the ISF will come soon." The administration has encountered difficulty securing troop pledges, prompting increased outreach. Separately, Meloni has asked Trump to amend the governance terms of the U.S.-led [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]], the body intended to oversee post-war governance in Gaza, citing constitutional issues with its current structure.

The second bonus item reports that Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] used a Davos speech to criticize the [[european-union|European Union]]'s handling of security and its support for Ukraine, saying the bloc has been indecisive and must learn to better defend itself, without specifically thanking member states for nearly €193 billion in military and financial assistance provided since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022. Italian lawmaker [[rossano-sasso|Rossano Sasso]] of [[lega-nord|Lega Nord]] said on social media that the EU should stop sending military aid to Ukraine, writing that the bloc now "deservedly accepts the insults" and asserting that Italy should provide no more weapons or money for a war that is not Italy's. Italian Foreign Minister [[antonio-tajani|Antonio Tajani]] called the address "not generous" given European backing, while European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] said the EU stands by Ukraine and that its actions "speak louder than words." The item closes with a statement from [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] responding to a Trump lawsuit over post-January 6 debanking: "While we regret President Trump has sued us, we believe the suit has no merit. We respect the president's right to sue us and our right to defend ourselves — that's what courts are for."
