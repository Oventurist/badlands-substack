# Polar Silk Road

The **Polar Silk Road** is the northern extension of China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] into the Arctic, which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes as having merged since 2018 with Russia's eastern development ambitions.[1]

## Convergence with Russia's Far East vision

Russia's 21st-century "Manifest Destiny" aims to extend civilization into the Siberian Far East and Arctic and beyond Central Asia, Mongolia, Japan and China.[1] Since 2018 these ambitions have increasingly merged with the Polar Silk Road, amplifying the growth of railways, roads, telecommunication hubs, ports, energy projects and sea corridors through regions long thought inhospitable to human civilization.[1]

## Related programs

The associated Russian rail expansion involves the 9,300 km Trans-Siberian Railway and the 4,300 km southern Baikal-Amur Mainline, both being modernized, double tracked and integrated more deeply into Mongolia, China and Japan.[1] This dovetails with the International North South Transportation Corridor from Moscow to India via Central Asia and Iran; freight traffic along these lines was projected to increase from 120 million tons per year to 180 million tons per year in 2024.[1]

Rail expansion is tied to Russia's Development Plan for the Northern Sea Route, adopted in 2019, which sought to increase annual shipments to 80 million tons by 2024 and includes ports, new Arctic mining hubs, the construction of 40 new vessels including nuclear icebreakers, railways and northern seaports — cutting ten days of shipping time from goods moving between China and Europe.[1] The [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] would complete the link to North America.[1]

## Integration with the Belt and Road and Arctic development

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that China's Polar Silk Road has extended the traditionally east-west development corridor into the Arctic, and that China and Russia have increasingly merged the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] with the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]].[2] Combined with Russia's revival of the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait crossing]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order reviving the [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska-Canada Railway]], he argues this development dynamic offers economic opportunity to all Arctic nations and an escape from military confrontation, in opposition to closed-system warhawks promoting a militaristic program against Russia and China in the Arctic.[2]

## Announcement and Arctic integration

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] dates the Polar Silk Road to 2018, when it was announced as the Arctic extension of the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], following Chinese state media promotion of the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] beginning in 2014.[3] He argues that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s 2018 proposal that the USA join Russia's Far East development would link western businesses into the Asia-Pacific development process shaped by the Belt and Road Initiative and especially the Polar Silk Road, and notes that India's embrace of Russian Far East investment has created a non-linear flank capable of harmonizing the two Asian giants.[3] By contrast, he writes, the North American Arctic remains an underdeveloped, barren tundra where no roads, rail or other development have been permitted to extend northward for decades.[3]
