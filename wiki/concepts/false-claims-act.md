# False Claims Act

The False Claims Act is a United States federal statute, dating to the Civil War era, that imposes liability on persons and companies that defraud government programs, including through false statements about goods or services provided to federal agencies. It is the legal basis of the Justice Department's [[civil-cyber-fraud-initiative|Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative]] against contractors who misrepresent their cybersecurity.

## The Microsoft liability question

In the May 2026 Badlands Brief, the [[american-accountability-foundation|American Accountability Foundation]] asked DOJ inspectors general to investigate whether [[microsoft|Microsoft]] could face liability under the False Claims Act for representations made to federal agencies about the security of its systems, given multiple cyber intrusions between 2019 and 2023 tied to Russian and Chinese hackers that reportedly compromised sensitive US government systems. The referral against [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]] alleged that while she oversaw the Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative, the DOJ pursued other contractors for less serious alleged misconduct but brought no comparable case against Microsoft.
