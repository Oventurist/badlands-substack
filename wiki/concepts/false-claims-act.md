# False Claims Act

The False Claims Act is the principal United States statute used to impose civil liability on parties that make false representations to federal agencies in connection with government contracts and payments.[1] It underpins the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s [[civil-cyber-fraud-initiative|Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative]], which applies the statute to contractors accused of misstating the security of systems sold to the government.[1]

In May 2026 the [[american-accountability-foundation|American Accountability Foundation]] asked Justice Department inspectors general to consider whether [[microsoft|Microsoft]] could face False Claims Act liability for representations made to federal agencies about the security of its systems, as part of a broader referral concerning former Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]].[1]

## Healthcare enforcement and qui tam

The statute is also the principal federal tool against healthcare billing fraud. In January 2026 affiliates of [[kaiser-permanente|Kaiser Permanente]] agreed to pay $556 million to resolve False Claims Act allegations that they submitted unsupported diagnosis codes to Medicare Advantage, producing higher payments from the federal government.[2] The settlement resolved civil claims only and included no admission of liability.[2]

The Justice Department said the Kaiser case was initiated in part through whistleblower lawsuits filed under the Act's qui tam provisions, under which private relators may sue on the government's behalf and receive a portion of any recovery.[2] Proceeds from such settlements are distributed among the affected federal programs, in that case including the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]].[2]
