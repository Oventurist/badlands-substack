# Trans-Siberian Railway

The Trans-Siberian Railway is the 9,300 km rail line spanning Russia from its western population centres to the Pacific, and the backbone of Russian eastward development.

## Modernization program

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s Avoiding WWIII describes plans to expand and improve both the Trans-Siberian and its 4,300 km southern counterpart, the Baikal–Amur Mainline, which is being modernized, double-tracked and integrated ever more deeply with Mongolia, China and even Japan. As the program advances, freight traffic along these lines was projected to increase from 120 million tons a year to 180 million tons in 2024.

The article links this expansion to the International North–South Transportation Corridor running from Moscow to India via Central Asia and Iran, which it argues should be seen as a dimension of both the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s Far East Vision, and to the [[northern-sea-route|Northern Sea Route]] plan and the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]].

## Obstacles

Ehret argues that realizing this vision is harder in Russia than in China because Russia's central bank, privatized in its current form in 1990, remains shaped by monetary protocols associated with the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] and the [[world-trade-organization|WTO]], favouring balanced budgets and free markets over the emission of productive credit. Despite these blocks, he cites Sergei Shoigu's "grand masterplan for Siberia," beginning with the construction of five new cities housing between 500,000 and one million citizens each.
