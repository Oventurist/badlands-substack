# Trans-Siberian Railway

The Trans-Siberian Railway was Russia's continental rail project, described by Matthew Ehret as American-modeled and largely American-built.[1] Its intellectual sponsors were students of [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]]'s program among the Russian intelligentsia, led by Count Sergei Witte and the chemist Dimitri Mendeleyev.[1]

Ehret situates the railway alongside Lincoln's Trans-Continental Railway — begun in 1863 at the height of the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] and nearly complete by the 1867 [[alaska-purchase|Alaska purchase]], destroying the British monopoly over maritime shipping routes.[1] With both continental systems advancing, the linking of rail across the two continents through the Bering Strait was discussed as a real possibility by republican visionaries the world over, and was mapped by [[william-gilpin|Governor William Gilpin]] in his 1890 [[cosmopolitan-railway|Cosmopolitan Railway]].[1]

Czars [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] and Alexander III vectored Russia's application of the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System]] around the Trans-Siberian line.[1] The same continental-connection logic underlies the modern proposal for a [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] rail corridor.[1]

## American contribution and aftermath

The Trans-Siberian Railway, built between 1890 and 1905, benefited immensely from Baldwin Locomotives made in Philadelphia and from the support of hundreds of American technicians and engineers, giving Russia good reason to trust the United States.[2] Its completion in 1905 coincided with American engineers finalizing plans for the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait]] rail project under Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] and [[sergei-witte|Sergey Witte]].[2] In that same year [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] financed Japan's victory in the [[russo-japanese-war|Russo-Japanese War]], crippling Russia and derailing the rail program.[2]
