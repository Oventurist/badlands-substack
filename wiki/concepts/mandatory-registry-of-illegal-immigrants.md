# Mandatory Registry of Illegal Immigrants

The mandatory registry of illegal immigrants was an online registration system the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] planned to launch in late February 2025, requiring undocumented migrants to submit personal information or face fines and arrest.[1]

## Requirements and penalties

According to U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, the registry would require illegal immigrants, including minors over the age of fourteen, to submit fingerprints and home addresses.[1] The *Wall Street Journal* reported that migrants who failed to register could be fined as much as $5,000 and sentenced to up to six months in prison.[1]

Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] framed the policy in a memo: "Aliens in this country illegally face a choice. They can return home and follow the legal process to come to the United States or they can deal with the consequences of continuing to violate our laws."[1] The move was described as part of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s broader effort to bolster the nation's immigration laws and enforcement.[1]

## Context

The registry was reported alongside a federal court ruling in Seattle by Judge [[jamal-whitehead|Jamal Whitehead]] blocking the administration's suspension of the [[us-refugee-admissions-program|U.S. Refugee Admissions Program]], illustrating the parallel tracks of executive action and litigation in the administration's immigration agenda.[1] Badlands writer Ashe in America argued that the resulting court fights over constitutional authority were producing binding precedents that would ultimately favor the administration, and that the volume of suits — more than seventy-five against the president's agenda — would strategically exhaust opponents deprived of dark-money funding.[1]
