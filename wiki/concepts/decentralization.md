# Decentralization

Decentralization is the dispersal of authority, access, and control away from a single central body toward individuals or peer networks. In the Badlands Media essay "Revolution Founded at the Crossroads," published October 31, 2024 by the pseudonymous contributor PiP of [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]], decentralization is presented not merely as a technical property of computer networks but as the defining characteristic of genuine, durable revolution.[1]

## Theological framing

The author argues that decentralization is a recurring divine pattern rather than a modern invention. Speaking on the "Individual Sovereignty" panel at Badlands Media's Great American Restoration Tour 5 in Deadwood, South Dakota, he asked why [[jesus|Jesus Christ]] was hated so intensely, and answered that "He was one of those decentralizing forces in society, in human nature, in human evolution."[1] The core of that teaching, in the author's rendering, was that "you don't have to come to a central body to find God" — access is universal, "decentralized, and open."[1] The tearing of the Temple veil is cited as the symbolic act that decentralized access to God, replacing an intermediated priesthood with direct individual access and the peaceful, non-kinetic "spiritual power projection" of the Holy Spirit.[1]

Because decentralization strips intermediaries of their monopoly, the essay holds that centralized power hierarchies inevitably resist it with maximum force: local religious factions crucified Jesus, Saul was dispatched to persecute the early church, and most of the apostles were executed.[1] The author characterizes this dynamic as a "nothing can stop what is coming" type of event — opposition that fails because the change is structural rather than personal.[1]

## Monetary and technological decentralization

The essay maps the same pattern onto [[bitcoin]]. [[satoshi-nakamoto|Satoshi Nakamoto]] is described as having decentralized control of a monetary network, separating money and state and giving individuals "digital power projection," while remaining anonymous precisely because central banks, financial institutions, and governments profiting from centralized currency debasement would seek to destroy both him and the network.[1] The author explicitly declines to deify the technology — "I will never say Bitcoin is God" — while insisting that "Bitcoin is absolutely changing the world like God did."[1]

Historically, the essay argues, every physical money (seashells, glass beads, silver, gold, paper currency) and every property class (stocks, bonds, real estate) eventually becomes centrally controlled and exploited by what the author calls god-kings, because "Cantillionaires" uncover asymmetric advantages in physical power projection by exploiting humanity's money- and property-related trust.[1] Decentralization through [[proof-of-work|Proof of Work]] is therefore framed as the first effective mitigation available to humanity, establishing "a fixed set of rules, changeable by no one, and accessible to everyone."[1]

## As revolutionary method

Within the essay's [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] frame, decentralization is the mechanism by which a peaceful, non-violent revolution succeeds without kinetic conflict. The author compares Bitcoin to a medieval siege: surrounding the castle so that nothing enters or leaves until the occupants are "starved of its power, starved of its ability to project power against the people."[1] Removing control actions from incumbent institutions — rather than seizing those institutions — is presented as the revolutionary act, an echo of the claim that "Jesus Christ returned power back to, We, the People."[1]

## Decentralization of banking (Lange, March 2023)

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] applied the concept directly to finance in a March 2023 article, arguing that the entire financial system has become centralized and controlled from top to bottom by a cabal of elite bankers who own the currency, own governments, and use puppet politicians to pass self-serving laws — the reason, he noted, that the institutions with power over the system are called "central" banks.[2] He defined centralization as concentrating power and authority in a central organization, and pointed to the decades-long decline in the number of banks nationwide as leaving small businesses, farmers and first-time home buyers with fewer choices.[2]

The prescribed remedy is to decentralize the financial system, create competition and give people the power to choose.[2] Lange identified trust as both the requirement of any financial system and the banks' greatest vulnerability, since its absence produces bank runs and failures.[2] He argued that [[financial-technology|financial technology]], [[peer-to-peer-payments|peer-to-peer payment services]], [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and [[blockchain|blockchain]] recordkeeping, and above all [[web5|Web5]]'s restoration of personal data ownership together constitute a paradigm shift transferring power over personal finances from banks to individuals through their smartphones.[2] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s plans for [[twitter|Twitter]] and [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]]'s work at [[block-inc|Block]] are offered as the leading practical examples.[2]
