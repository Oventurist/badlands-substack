# Washington DC Search Trend Panic (2025)

The Washington DC search trend panic refers to a February 2025 surge in internet searches in the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area for terms associated with evidence destruction, legal exposure and capital flight, interpreted by ZeroHedge and Badlands commentators as evidence of growing alarm within the federal bureaucracy.[1]

## Search trends

Internet search trends in the D.C. metro area were described as "nothing short of stunning," reflecting what appeared to be growing panic within the federal bureaucracy as [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] worked to root out corruption in non-governmental organizations and federal agencies.[1] Earlier in the week, searches for "Criminal Defense Lawyer" and "RICO Laws" had gone viral on X, fueling speculation that Washington's political elites were in panic mode; those searches coincided with DOGE's efforts to cut off [[usaid|USAID]] funding of NGOs alleged to prop up a shadow government, and with the cutting of tens of thousands of workers from federal agencies.[1]

As DOGE efforts intensified at the end of the week, further search spikes emerged. WikiLeaks wrote on X late Thursday that "Washington DC searches soar for 'Swiss bank', 'offshore bank', 'wire money' and 'IBAN'."[1] The brief's title referenced "BleachBit," the disk-wiping utility associated with the destruction of records, alongside "wipe hard drive" searches.[1]

## Housing market effects

The Trump administration's return-to-office mandate and layoffs also disrupted housing markets with large federal footprints, as workers searched for closer commutes and struggled with job insecurity; Trump said federal employees who opted to stay remote would be dismissed.[1] A Kansas City-based Redfin agent, Jo Chavez, reported meeting clients selling specifically because of anticipated return-to-office orders, including one who cancelled plans to upgrade to a larger home out of fear of losing his job to government restructuring.[1]

For the four weeks ending February 9, 2025, new listings were up 7.4% year over year — the highest level since 2022 — while pending sales were down 6%, according to Redfin.[1] Supply stood at five months, the most since early 2019 and up from 4.4 months a year earlier, and the typical home took 57 days to go under contract, the longest span since the early pandemic period of March 2020.[1]

No empathy and no deals."[1] The reporting was carried by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]].[1]
