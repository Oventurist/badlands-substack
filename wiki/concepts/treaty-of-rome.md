# Treaty of Rome

The Treaty of Rome is the agreement that brought the European Common Market into being, the institutional forerunner of the [[european-union|European Union]].[1]

In "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 4," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cites the treaty as a concrete instance of the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg Group]]'s influence on European policy, quoting Bilderberger and former U.S. ambassador to West Germany [[george-mcghee|George McGhee]]'s acknowledgement that "the Treaty of Rome, which brought the European Common Market into being, was nurtured at Bilderberg meetings."[1]

DeLarme frames the treaty within a longer narrative in which the medieval [[knights-templar|Knights Templar]] aimed for a unified Europe — and eventually a unified world — under centralized control, an ambition he argues is well on its way to becoming reality through groups such as the Bilderbergers.[1] He pairs the point with [[jack-sheinkman|Jack Sheinkman]]'s testimony that the idea of a common European currency was discussed at Bilderberg years before it became policy.[1]
