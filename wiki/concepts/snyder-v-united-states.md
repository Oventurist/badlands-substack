# Snyder v. United States

Snyder v. United States is a decision of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] issued on Wednesday, June 26, 2024, interpreting the scope of federal anti-corruption statute 18 U.S.C. § 666.[1] The Court held that "Section 666 prescribes bribes to public officials but does not make it a crime for those officials to accept gratuities for their past acts."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated Snyder as one of two opinions released that Wednesday — alongside [[murthy-v-missouri|Murthy v. Missouri]] — that "appeared to weigh in favor of corruption."[1] She noted with dark humor the statute's numbering ("Of course it's Section 666") and summarized the majority's holding as saying, in effect, "You can't bribe public officials, but you can tip them."[1] She also expressed discomfort at finding herself aligned with the dissenting position of Justices Jackson, Kagan, and Sotomayor.[1]
