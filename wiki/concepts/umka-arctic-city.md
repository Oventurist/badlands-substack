# Umka Arctic City

**Umka** is a proposed Russian nuclear-powered domed city on the island of Kotelny, only 1,000 km from the North Pole, cited by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the model from which Canada and the United States must learn if the Arctic is to be opened as the new frontier of human development.[1]

To meet the challenge of housing over 5,000 workers and their families in a harsh Arctic climate — requiring internal climate control, waste evaporation, internal agricultural capacities and reliable energy — Russian designers used the International Space Station as their model.[1] Ehret argues that this design lineage is important because the Arctic will play a vital role in the investigation of cosmic radiation, a galactic and intergalactic phenomenon driving the evolution of both living and non-living material on Earth.[1]

Umka is presented alongside [[sergey-glazyev|Sergey Glazyev]]'s programme for five advanced Russian Arctic cities and Canada's earlier [[frobisher-bay-domed-city|Frobisher Bay]] plan as expressions of a forward-thinking paradigm that Ehret says the west lost decades ago.[1]

## Restatement in the Alaska summit essay

In his August 2025 essay written ahead of the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin Alaska summit]], Ehret repeated that opening the Arctic as the new frontier of human development requires Canada to learn from Russia's plan for the nuclear-powered domed city of Umka on Kotelny island, and paired it with [[sergey-glazyev|Sergey Glazyev]]'s programme to construct five advanced cities in the Russian Arctic as expressions of a forward-thinking paradigm the west lost decades ago.[2] He again stressed the International Space Station lineage of the design and the Arctic's role in the investigation of cosmic radiation.[2]
