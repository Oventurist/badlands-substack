# Wall Street Trilogy

The "Wall Street Trilogy" is the collective name given to three books published in the 1970s by [[antony-sutton|Antony Sutton]]: *Wall Street and the Bolshevik Revolution*, *Wall Street and Hitler*, and *Wall Street and FDR*.[1] Badlands Media commentary presents the trilogy as essential reading for anyone seeking to understand what the author calls the "ziobankster" class and its activities across the twentieth century.[1]

## Thesis as summarized by Badlands

According to the Badlands article "On Infiltration and Israel," the titles alone convey the argument: Wall Street provided substantial funding to communists, Nazis, and numerous other parties that were subsequently instrumental in subverting Western civilization.[1] The author extends this into the claim that much of the twentieth century was "a theatrical production staged by Wall Street," and that Wall Street itself operated merely as a front for a larger cabal.[1] Wall Street, the author notes, profited handsomely from these ventures.[1]

## Reception and interpretation

The trilogy is invoked to support the aphorism "all wars are bankers wars," which the Badlands author regards as an oversimplification that is nonetheless largely accurate.[1] A central inference drawn from the work is that apparently antagonistic ideological camps — communists, Nazis, capitalists, and socialists — are all "played like puppets," including by their own sincere adherents' belief in the reality of their conflicts.[1] Within the article the trilogy functions as the evidentiary bridge from twentieth-century finance to the founding of [[israel|Israel]], the [[balfour-declaration|Balfour Declaration]], and the [[kalergi-plan|Kalergi Plan]].[1]
