# FAANG Stocks

FAANG is the acronym for the five large-cap American technology stocks that dominated market returns during the 2010s: Facebook, Amazon, Apple, Netflix and Google.[1] It was later displaced in market usage by the broader [[magnificent-seven-stocks|Magnificent Seven]] grouping.[1]

## Badlands commentary

In an August 2024 essay on [[big-tech|Big Tech]]'s political realignment, contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that the [[obama-administration|Obama administration]] grew the FAANG names into mega tech companies, and that during that administration they received preferential treatment over their competitors which ensured they would monopolize their markets.[1] The result, in his account, is that these companies are now invaluable and irreplaceable to the American economy.[1]

Carlson placed this claim inside a wider argument that the sector's origins are inseparable from state funding, contending that without [[darpa|DARPA]] and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] the United States would probably not have Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Netflix, or perhaps even Tesla.[1] He said he did not resent the founders who accepted that funding, since many may have believed they were doing something noble by partnering with the intelligence community before realizing they were at the CIA's beck and call.[1]

## Carlson's CIA claim (2023)

In an earlier essay, "Does the CIA Still Control Iran?" (July 2023), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] recalled how often the FAANG acronym — Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Netflix and Google — was heard during [[barack-obama|Obama]]'s presidency, joking that it should not be confused with Fang Fang, Eric Swalwell's alleged Chinese spy girlfriend.[2] He argued that these five companies dominated the stock market during Obama's time in office and kept it afloat, providing cover for the damage he believed the administration was doing to the economy.[2]

What they share besides being tech stocks, in Carlson's view, is that they are all "CIA-run, CIA-funded, CIA-controlled companies" that collect users' information and were given preferential treatment and unfair advantages over competitors by the [[obama-administration|Obama administration]], and that all are used to influence the public while most have been used to censor it.[2] He added that the smaller group of companies keeping the market afloat at the time of writing — some of them FAANG members — are, in his argument, all connected to the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] as well, past or present.[2]
