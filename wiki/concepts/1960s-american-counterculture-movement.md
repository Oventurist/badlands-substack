# 1960s American Counterculture Movement

The 1960s American counterculture movement was the youth-driven cultural upheaval of the Vietnam War era, characterized by drug use, sexual liberation, communal living and anti-war protest. In Badlands Media commentary it is reinterpreted as a deliberate intelligence operation rather than a spontaneous social phenomenon.[1]

## Alleged origin as a CIA operation

In the March 2024 article "The CIA is Being Thrown Under the Bus," Erik Carlson describes the counterculture as probably one of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s most crowning achievements: a drug-induced, free-love, long-haired, jobless subculture manufactured in order to make any dissenting view of the war machine look insane.[1]

The operational logic Carlson attributes to the agency is one of discrediting by association. Because the counterculture was cast in the public imagination as cartoon-like, anyone who spoke up against the Vietnam War was folded into that caricature and dismissed along with it. Opposition to the war was thereby neutralized without needing to be answered.[1]

## Wider cultural argument

Carlson places the counterculture within a much longer list of cultural currents he attributes to CIA manipulation, including the feminist movement, the normalization of abortion, the erosion of the traditional family, the civil rights movement, the trafficking of people and drugs, the creation of cults and serial killers, the manipulation of major religions, pornography, the transgender movement and climate change. In his summary, no aspect of American culture has gone untouched by the agency.[1]

Counterfactually, he argues that had the CIA never been formed in 1947, or had John F. Kennedy succeeded in his stated intent to splinter it into a thousand pieces, the cultural landscape would look very different — religion would retain a place in public schools and society, the traditional family would be more prevalent, and the divisions that structure American life would be far less pronounced.[1]
