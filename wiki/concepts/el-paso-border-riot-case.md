# El Paso Border Riot Case

The El Paso border riot case refers to the prosecution and subsequent court-ordered release of migrants accused of participating in a "border riot" in which a stampede overwhelmed [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops along the Rio Grande in El Paso, Texas.[1]

## Charges

The [[texas-department-of-public-safety|Texas Department of Public Safety]] booked about 220 people on riot charges.[1] Of those, 39 had been processed for release to [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and remained jailed on immigration detainers, according to a spokeswoman for the El Paso County Sheriff's Office.[1]

## Release order

On Easter Sunday, March 31, 2024, El Paso Presiding Magistrate Judge [[humberto-acosta|Humberto Acosta]] ordered the release of the accused migrants during an online teleconference bond hearing, accusing the El Paso District Attorney's Office of not being ready to proceed with detention hearings for each defendant.[1] "It is the ruling of the court that all the rioting participation cases will be released on their own recognizance," Acosta ordered.[1] Court officials noted that undocumented migrants would remain jailed where a federal immigration hold blocked their release, and another hearing for additional defendants was expected the following Monday.[1] The ruling was reported as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of April 2, 2024.[1]
