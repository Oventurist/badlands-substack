# Saudi F-35 Deal

The Saudi F-35 deal refers to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s November 2025 announcement that the United States would sell F-35 warplanes to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], a move that immediately raised questions about [[qualitative-military-edge|Israel's qualitative military edge]] because [[israel|Israel]] had been the only Middle East operator of the aircraft.[1]

## Israeli response

Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] publicly brushed off the deal, saying Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had assured him by phone that the United States would continue to preserve Israel's legally guaranteed edge in weapons and military systems supplied to Middle Eastern countries, including specifically with respect to F-35 supply.[1]

## Normalization context

The aircraft sale sits within a wider bargaining set that also includes a nuclear deal and a defense agreement sought by Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1] In the same interview cycle, Netanyahu declared that "there will not be a Palestinian state" even at the cost of normalization with Riyadh, which insists on a credible pathway to [[palestinian-statehood|Palestinian statehood]] in exchange for ties.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu and his cohorts tried to leverage the F-35s, the nuclear deal and the defense agreement to force Saudi capitulation on normalization without Palestinian statehood, and that for years analysts assumed the Saudis would eventually sell out the Palestinians.[1] He said the Saudis instead practiced [[first-principles|First Principles]] and negotiated steadfastly enough to obtain their goals without compromising their core condition, and that Netanyahu's bravado rhetoric reflects his recognition of that outcome.[1] He also expressed skepticism that the U.S. would truly degrade the Saudi jets to preserve Israeli superiority.[1]

## Pentagon review of the 48-jet request, November 2025

Ahead of the announcement, [[reuters|Reuters]] reported in early November 2025 that the Trump administration was weighing a Saudi request to buy as many as 48 F-35 fighter jets, a potential multi-billion-dollar deal that had cleared a key [[pentagon|Pentagon]] hurdle in advance of a visit by Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] to Washington.[2] Sources said a sale would mark a significant policy shift, potentially altering the military balance in the Middle East and testing Washington's definition of maintaining Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|qualitative military edge]].[2] Saudi Arabia had made a direct appeal earlier in the year to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and had long been interested in [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]]'s fighter; the size of the request and its status had not previously been reported.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Mohammed bin Salman had pursued F-35s since his tenure as Defense Minister from January 2015 until his elevation to Crown Prince in June 2017, after which he pressed to join the F-35 program.[2] He wrote that Washington lawmakers grew reluctant to approve Saudi arms sales following the murder of [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]], which both MBS and Trump maintain was not ordered by the crown prince and for which they say he was framed.[2] In his reading, congressional reluctance after what he called the Khashoggi "psyact" supports the theory that MBS was framed in order to damage his reputation and hinder his agenda by ostracizing him internationally.[2] With MBS scheduled to visit the [[white-house|White House]] that month, he judged the likelihood that Trump would push the F-35 deal through — notwithstanding Israel's qualitative military edge — to be extremely high.[2]
