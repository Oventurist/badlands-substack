# Wahhabism

Wahhabism is the name given by the outside world to the orthodox interpretation of Islam developed by [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab]] in 18th-century Arabia; neither he nor his followers ever attached a specific moniker to their own ideology.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Doctrine

The movement arose as a claimed restoration rather than an innovation. In al-Wahhab's assessment, Arabians of the early 1700s largely identified as Muslims but had drifted from the hadiths, worshipping hundreds of secular idols and practicing ancient occult magic and mysticism — effectively a return to pre-Islamic idolatry roughly 1,100 years after the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s death.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Against this, Wahhabism rejected taqlid (conformity or group-think) and called for ijtihad, independent legal reasoning grounded in direct research of scripture.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

In practice the doctrine's early implementation in the oasis town of Uyayna involved destroying a venerated tomb of one of the Prophet's companions, felling trees held sacred by locals, and instituting the stoning of women who confessed to adultery.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Political fusion

Wahhabism acquired durable political force through the [[pact-of-diriyah|Pact of Diriyah]] of 1744, in which al-Wahhab agreed to serve as religious leader while [[mohammed-bin-saud|Mohammed bin Saud]] served as imam and political chief, in exchange for an oath to wage jihad against unbelievers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The doctrine appealed strongly to the [[bedouin|Bedouin]], and al-Wahhab recognized that without a strong political backer he and his followers would face perpetual persecution.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The combination is described as "might, reason, and faith."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Later trajectory

The article notes that Muhammad's successors, especially under Wahhabism, adopted a far less tolerant disposition than the Prophet had shown in the [[constitution-of-medina|Constitution of Medina]], which guaranteed religious freedom to Jews and other non-Muslims.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) A promised sequel connects Wahhabism's 20th-century exportation across the Middle East, via British sponsorship of Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman Al Saud and the creation of the Ikhwan (Brotherhood), to the rise of radical Islamic terrorism.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
