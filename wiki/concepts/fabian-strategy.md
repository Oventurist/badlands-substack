# Fabian Strategy

The Fabian strategy is a method of warfare that avoids decisive pitched battle in favor of attrition and indirection, and is identified in the Patriot Chronicles as the template [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]] provided to his colleagues for fighting the most powerful army on earth.[1]

## Application in South Carolina

Marion succeeded in containing [[charles-cornwallis|Cornwallis]] in South Carolina long enough for the French fleet to arrive and reinforce [[george-washington|George Washington]] in Virginia.[1] He did so through irregular warfare and the Fabian approach, which the article characterizes as Sun Tzu-esque and paradoxical: a war of attrition is normally waged by the side with greater resources in order to bleed out a smaller army, yet here the weaker force imposed it on the stronger.[1] The article titles this section "Death by a Thousand Papercuts."[1]

The complementary element was psychological. By spreading his few men over an enormous area, Marion caused Cornwallis to vastly overestimate the size and resources of the partisan forces, making the British commander far more conservative — an effect deepened after Marion seized a wagon full of Cornwallis's personal writings and effects.[1] Marion also directed his men to use their first shot from cover against British officers riding in file; challenged on this during a parlay, he answered that so long as the British burned homes and harmed women he would continue to assassinate British leadership, rejecting the British insistence that the two matters were separate issues.[1]
