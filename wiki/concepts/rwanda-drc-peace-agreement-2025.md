# Rwanda–DR Congo Peace Agreement (2025)

The **Rwanda–DR Congo peace agreement** was signed on Friday, June 27, 2025, in Washington by the foreign ministers of [[rwanda|Rwanda]] and the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] alongside U.S. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], ending — at least on paper — a decades-long conflict.[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who brokered the deal, said it gives the United States rights to local mineral wealth.[1]

## Terms

The agreement pledges a 90-day Rwandan troop withdrawal from the DR Congo, outlines disarmament and reintegration steps for armed groups, and establishes a joint security mechanism.[1]

## Background

Congolese officials have long accused Rwanda of backing militant groups in the mineral-rich eastern DR Congo and of smuggling resources out of the country.[1] Tensions escalated after [[m23|M23]] militants seized mining hubs including Goma and Bukavu, reportedly killing thousands; Kigali denied supporting the militants despite UN and international backing for Kinshasa's claims.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the signing as a possible bookend to a story he had covered for over a year.[1] He traced it from Trump's [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], signed December 20, 2017, which sanctioned Israeli Diamond Cartel scion [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]]; through the replacement of Gertler's longtime business partner, DRC President [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]], by [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]]; and through successive attempts to overthrow Tshisekedi's government — first by Gertler and former Mossad director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]], then by American-Congolese operative [[christian-malanga|Christian Malanga]], and then by Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] and his proxy M23.[1] He noted that the affair also involved arrests of high-profile figures at the world's top three commodity trading houses, including [[glencore|Glencore]].[1]

In his account, all of these attempts trace back to Israel and the Netanyahu network, motivated by roughly $24 trillion of rare earth minerals that slipped out of the control of Kabila, Gertler and their associates — a supply chain he alleges relied on child slavery while feeding Fortune 500 manufacturing of smartphones, laptops and ballistic missiles.[1] He concluded that he does not believe the story is over, but that "it's only getting started."[1]
