# 2024 North Korean Trash Balloon Campaign

In late May 2024, [[north-korea|North Korea]] dropped at least 260 balloons carrying rubbish into South Korea, prompting authorities to warn residents to stay indoors.[1] South Korea's military cautioned the public against touching the white balloons and the attached plastic bags because they contained "filthy waste and trash," and said the balloons had been found in eight of South Korea's nine provinces and were being analysed.[1] The military had earlier said it was investigating whether the balloons carried North Korean propaganda leaflets.[1]

## Background

North and South Korea have both used balloons in propaganda campaigns since the Korean War in the 1950s.[1] The incident came days after North Korea said it would retaliate against the "frequent scattering of leaflets and other rubbish" in border areas by activists in the South.[1] North Korea's vice-minister of defence Kim Kang Il said in a statement to state media that "mounds of wastepaper and filth will soon be scattered over the border areas and the interior of the ROK and it will directly experience how much effort is required to remove them."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the [[bbc|BBC]] buried in its 17th paragraph the fact that a South Korea-based activist group had claimed earlier that month to have sent 20 balloons carrying anti-Pyongyang leaflets and USB sticks with Korean pop music across the border — context indicating that the North's response was provoked.[1] She also observed that Seoul's parliament passed a law in December 2020 criminalising the launch of anti-Pyongyang leaflets, though critics have raised freedom-of-speech and human-rights concerns about it.[1] The commentary questioned whether the Biden administration would engage in diplomacy over the balloon exchange, and whether it possessed effective diplomats at all, suggesting Korea might be better served by outside envoys such as [[dennis-rodman|Dennis Rodman]] than by the [[state-department|State Department]].[1]
