# Badlands Brief: White Knuckling Weaponized Government

"White Knuckling Weaponized Government" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on June 5, 2026. The Brief is described by its publishers as a daily digest of Badlands commentary on "the narratives dominating the info war," pairing excerpted news reports with signed analysis from Badlands contributors. This edition carried five main items and a bonus item, with commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The masthead notes that the opinions expressed belong to the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## Arizona electors case

The Brief reports that the [[arizona-supreme-court|Arizona Supreme Court]] rejected an appeal in the 2020 electors case involving allies of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and that Attorney General [[kris-mayes|Kris Mayes]] responded by announcing plans to seek a new indictment in order to restart the prosecution. Mayes's spokesperson Richie Taylor is quoted saying, "The Arizona Attorney General's Office will return this case to the grand jury," and declining further comment. A judge had previously dismissed the indictment after finding that prosecutors failed to present the grand jury with the precise statutory language tied to the alleged conduct.

Ashe in America's commentary reads the move as self-incriminating: "It's hard to see this as anything other than a regime puppet outing themselves." She argues that "the vibe of the nation has changed" and that a majority of the electoral franchise believes the 81 million votes recorded for Joe Biden in the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]] were not real — from which, she contends, it follows that the actions of the so-called "fake electors" were justifiable. In her reading, Mayes is "doubling down on weaponized government at a time when weaponized government is more acknowledged than ever," which she calls both hilarious and a bold move.

## Bering Strait tunnel agreement

The second item reports that Kremlin special envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]] said Russia and the United States would sign an agreement on June 5 to advance the design phase of a proposed [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] linking Russia's Chukotka region with Alaska. Dmitriev is quoted: "Regarding the tunnel. We will have news tomorrow: we are signing an agreement to continue the tunnel design. The tunnel will be built." The proposal envisions a fixed land link between Asia and North America and has been promoted by Russian officials as a symbol of expanded economic cooperation with the United States. Dmitriev first floated the idea in October of the previous year, estimating that a conventional build could exceed $65 billion but suggesting costs could fall below $8 billion if construction methods associated with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s The Boring Company were used.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the tunnel within a long-arc political theology. He states his opinion that the United States government "was formally seized by a hostile insurgency on November 22, 1963," when President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] was assassinated in Dallas, and that Lyndon B. Johnson was part of the coup along with the [[cia|CIA]], the [[mossad|Mossad]] and "Israeli diaspora" interests. He further believes the same group killed [[john-f-kennedy-jr|JFK Jr.]] when he attempted to begin a Senate career, and that Trump is "bringing the saga full circle, leading a counterinsurgency against these pirates." He notes that Kennedy and Nikita Khrushchev had once attempted to bridge the continents, a project shuttered by the insurgents.

The commentary argues that an alliance between the Russian and American peoples is feared by these insurgents "because they despise Jesus Christ," and that a Russo-American alliance would represent a resurgence of Christianity against forces seeking to erase it in [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and elsewhere. He describes the signing as "marking the formal emergence of the Sovereign Alliance in the public zeitgeist," notes the irony that many who will object are themselves Christian, credits [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] with restraint and patience, and concludes that "the Putin–Trump Tunnel will forever change the world."

## Republican defections and the 2026 midterms

The Brief reports that Trump criticized a small group of Republican lawmakers who opposed portions of his agenda, calling them "unpatriotic" and "grandstanders" after [[senate|Senate]] debates over administration-backed legislation, with several Republicans joining Democrats on select votes related to spending and executive authority. It notes coverage in *The Atlantic*, including a podcast highlighting Republican officials who have broken with the president over spending and redistricting, while observing that most congressional Republicans continued to support the administration's major priorities and that recent Senate votes showed only limited defections.

Burning Bright's accompanying essay argues that what is unfolding in the summer of 2026 "is not the external assault we were long conditioned to monitor" from legacy media, institutional holdouts, or lawfare, but a breach "from within the very walls of the MAGA coalition." He traces the pattern to the 2022 midterm cycle, when voices positioning themselves as guardians of the movement — including those orbiting the [[ron-desantis|DeSantis]] operation — declared Trump's 2024 announcement premature and destabilizing. In his reading, the early declaration functioned as "a disarmament operation against a weaponized unity play already in motion," intended to sideline the one figure preventing the movement from being herded back onto partisan rails.

He contends that many of the same unelected figures, secured in positions during that cycle, are now exploiting the "public discombobulation" generated by Trump's ongoing [[5th-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] campaign to break ranks again, because the movement threatens "the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] architecture they were cultivated to protect." Turning to the Truth Community itself, he writes that those who continue to point out "the structural falsity of American elections" have been mocked and attacked for refusing to outsource discernment to partisan outcomes, and that the claim the movement's future rests on capturing the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]] is "the same trap as 2022, repackaged" — one that reduces American sovereignty to electioneering and hands initiative back to the mechanisms that produced systemic tyranny.

## John Bolton plea deal

Citing CNN, the Brief reports that former Trump National Security Adviser [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] is expected to plead guilty later in the month to a single count of illegally retaining sensitive national security documents. Under the anticipated agreement Bolton has agreed to pay a fine exceeding $2 million; the charge carries a potential sentence ranging from no incarceration to five years, with a plea hearing scheduled for June 26. Federal prosecutors had charged Bolton earlier in the year, alleging he kept diary entries and other records from his time in the first Trump administration at his residence. The original indictment included eight counts related to transmitting national defense information and ten counts tied to retaining such material. Investigators also alleged Bolton shared more than 1,000 pages detailing his daily activities through a personal email account with two unauthorized individuals — allegations not included in the count to which he is expected to plead.

Ashe in America responds by asking, "In which job(s) did Bolton help plan coup d'état?" and reproduces a career chronology: general counsel and then assistant administrator at [[usaid|USAID]] (1981–1983); partner at Covington & Burling (1983–1985); Assistant Attorney General for the Office of Legislative Affairs at the Department of Justice (1985–1989); Assistant Secretary for International Organization Affairs at the [[state-department|State Department]] (1989–1993); partner at Lerner, Reed, Bolton & McManus (1993–1999); vice president of the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]] (1997–2001); Under Secretary of State for Arms Control and International Security Affairs (2001–2005); U.S. Ambassador to the [[united-nations|United Nations]] (2005–2006); of counsel at Kirkland & Ellis and foreign policy adviser to Mitt Romney in 2012 (2007–2018); and National Security Advisor to the President (2018–2019). She asks whether the case is really about the contents of Bolton's book, or whether there will be accountability for a portfolio of work that "sold out the American people so the rest of the world could rise," which she calls "literal treason."

## Trump on enriched uranium and the Ayatollah

The Brief reports that on June 4 Trump said the United States does not need a formal agreement with [[iran|Iran]] to obtain the country's enriched uranium, asserting the US could access it if it chose but that there is currently "no need" because the material is effectively "entombed" at damaged nuclear sites. Trump said the US is closely monitoring the sites and that any recovery operation would be difficult and risky. The Brief characterizes this as a notable shift from earlier negotiations, in which the transfer or surrender of Iran's enriched uranium stockpile had been reported as a central element of a potential deal. Trump also struck a conciliatory tone toward Iran's leadership, saying he is not currently seeking a meeting with Supreme Leader [[mojtaba-khamenei|Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei]] but would consider one if a deal is reached and would approach it respectfully; he has also said Iran already agreed not to obtain nuclear weapons.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls this "a remarkable shift in rhetoric in the past 48 hours," moving from a paraphrased position that "the Ayatollah is a crazy religious zealot who cannot be reasoned with and therefore must be destroyed" to Trump's remark that "he's probably a professional; in some circles, he has a very good reputation." He describes it as "a rebranding exercise at Warp Speed," noting that Trump was answering a question from Fox News correspondent Peter Doocy about willingness to meet, began with "I don't *want* to meet," then said he would be "honored" to meet the Supreme Leader — which the commentator calls "a jaw-dropping breakthrough in public diplomacy" and an olive branch. After months of exchanging disparaging memes and AI videos with the Iranian government on social media, Trump's stated willingness to be respectful reinforces, in this reading, that there will be no formal attempt to overthrow Iran's system of government.

The commentary then turns to reactions in what it calls "Mossad Media." Checking [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]]'s X feed, the author found no response to Trump's comments but noted a repost from Danny Danon, Israel's Ambassador to the United Nations. The author objects to a suggestion attributed to Danon that a list be compiled of Americans who criticize the Israeli government, comparing it to "the lists that the Nazis used to make," and condemns former National Security Advisor Mike Waltz for nodding along on stage. He writes that Waltz "is a traitor—like so many others in our government—and is fully prepared to support the targeted persecution of American citizens by a foreign government," calling the targeting of Americans for exercising their [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] right to criticize a foreign government "perfect symbolism" for the corruption of the political class, and expressing relief that Waltz was fired from the cabinet. He closes by arguing that "the world isn't turning on [[israel|Israel]] because they are Jewish; the world is turning on Israel because they are behaving like a bunch of bloodthirsty sociopaths."

## Bonus item: Israel–Lebanon ceasefire framework

The bonus item reports that the United States convened a fourth round of high-level trilateral talks between Israeli and Lebanese representatives, producing a ceasefire framework tied to new security conditions. Under the US-led agreement, Israel and Lebanon committed to a ceasefire contingent on a full halt to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] fire and the removal of all Hezbollah operatives from the South Litani sector. The deal establishes pilot zones where the [[lebanese-armed-forces|Lebanese Armed Forces]] would assume exclusive control, excluding all non-state armed groups. The framework builds on [[pentagon|Pentagon]] talks held May 29 and centers on reinforcing sovereignty, border stability, and the dismantling of non-state armed organizations, envisioning expanded Lebanese Armed Forces responsibility across national territory with US support. The parties agreed to resume political and security talks the week of June 22, with Washington continuing to facilitate negotiations toward a broader settlement.
