# Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act

The Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act was a $78 billion bipartisan tax package passed by the U.S. House of Representatives on 31 January 2024 in a 357–70 vote, sending the bicameral legislation to the Senate for consideration.[1] The bill boosted the child tax credit and reinstated business deductions that had been rescinded during the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Passage

The measure was crafted by House Ways and Means Committee Chair [[jason-smith|Jason Smith]] (R-Mo.) and Senate Finance Committee Chair [[ron-wyden|Ron Wyden]] (D-Ore.), and its passage marked a rare show of bipartisanship in a Congress otherwise defined by bitter partisan clashes and widely labeled highly unproductive.[1] "The numbers speak for itself, it shows that when you're trying to deliver for the American people, people will join together and that's what we saw today," Smith told reporters after the vote.[1]

The bill was also notable as one of the few instances in the session in which a nonessential measure — legislation not required to keep the government running — had a realistic chance of enactment.[1] Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] (D-N.Y.) said he supported the tax bill and was working with Wyden to determine the best path forward.[1]
