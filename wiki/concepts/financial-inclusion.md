# Financial Inclusion

Financial inclusion is an effort to make everyday financial services available to more of the world's population at a reasonable cost.[1] Advancements in [[financial-technology|fintech]], such as digital transactions, are described as making financial inclusion easier to achieve, although the [[world-bank|World Bank]] estimates that some 1.7 billion adults worldwide still lack access to even a basic bank account.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] links financial inclusion to the broader question of lifting populations out of poverty, arguing that this is done by creating the ability for people to control their own financial destiny and make their own choices, which in turn requires internet access so that they can participate in the global economy regardless of where they live.[1] He presents [[starlink|Starlink]]'s provision of high-speed internet to remote and rural locations as a direct contribution to that goal, and as the opposite of a centralizing agenda that would concentrate populations in large, controllable cities.[1]

On the institutional side, [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]]'s [[block-inc|Block]] is cited as pursuing inclusion through community lending: it invested £2m in ART Business Loans, a Birmingham-based community development finance institution serving businesses that cannot meet banks' full requirements, and was looking to invest in a not-for-profit lender or social enterprise in Ireland.[1] [[square-inc|Square]]'s mission statement — "No one should be left out of the economy because the cost is too great or the technology too complex" — is offered as a summary of the same principle.[1] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s stated ambition to make [[twitter|Twitter]] "the people's financial institution" is presented as a mass-scale version of the idea.[1]
