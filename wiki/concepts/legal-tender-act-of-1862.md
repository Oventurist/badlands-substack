# Legal Tender Act of 1862

The **Legal Tender Act of 1862** was the U.S. statute under which President [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] began issuing [[greenbacks|greenbacks]], a national paper currency issued directly by the U.S. Treasury.[1]

In the account of Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the act was the most important step in Lincoln's fight for sovereign control of credit issuance, a power that under Article 1, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution can only be effected through the U.S. Treasury.[1] The greenbacks authorized by the act could only be issued against U.S. government bonds, tying the new currency to the credit of the nation rather than to private bank notes or foreign gold.[1]

The act formed one component of a three-step operation that also included the Banking and Currency Acts of 1862 and 1863 and the [[national-bank-act-of-1863|Bank Act of 1863]], together restoring the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System]] of national banking during the height of the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]].[1] By the beginning of 1865, $450 million in greenbacks issued under this framework made up over half of all currency in circulation.[1]
