# Ukraine Nuclear Transfer Proposal

The Ukraine nuclear transfer proposal refers to a reported discussion among European Union and American officials, surfaced by the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] in November 2024, that outgoing U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] could provide [[ukraine|Ukraine]] with nuclear weapons as a "security guarantee."[1] The Times article, bylined by four reporters and citing anonymous sources, asserted that such a step "would be an instant and enormous deterrent" while acknowledging it would be "complicated and have serious implications."[1]

## Kremlin response

Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said the idea came from the "extremist flank" of Kiev's Western backers, who had lost touch with reality: "even the most provocative line aimed at escalating tensions has a fringe extremist flank. This idea probably comes from this fringe extremist flank."[1] He characterized the suggestion of transferring nuclear weapons to Ukraine as "absolutely irresponsible deliberations by people, who probably have a poor understanding… of reality, and who do not feel a shred of responsibility" for the consequences, and pointed out that "all of these statements are anonymous."[1]

Former Russian president [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]], deputy chair of the Russian Security Council, warned in a Telegram post that Moscow would treat any threat of nuclear arms being supplied to Ukraine as preparation for direct war with [[russia|Russia]], and that an actual transfer would be tantamount to an attack on the country under Russia's new nuclear doctrine.[1] He said his "sad joke about crazy senile Biden, who's eager to go out with a bang and take a substantial part of humanity with him, is becoming dangerously real," and argued that giving nukes to a country at war with the greatest nuclear power was so absurd that anyone considering it must have "massive paranoid psychosis."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the exchange as evidence that a [[nuclear-scare-event|nuclear scare event]] was on the immediate horizon, and situated it within what he called a new phase of narrative escalation playing out around the [[donald-trump|Trump]] cabinet nominations.[1] He noted that even experienced intelligence analysts such as [[scott-ritter|Scott Ritter]] appeared genuinely fooled, citing Ritter's assessment to Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]] that Trump had "played" the American people by campaigning as a peace-seeking president and then nominating rabid warmongers.[1]

In his own framing, the most logical play before inauguration was for Trump and the White Hats to orchestrate narrative escalation so severe that it outpaces reality, so that public mandate for narrative resolution forms before actual kinetic escalation can manifest — which entails appointing "television characters who represent a desire for death and destruction, in order to project narrative chaos."[1]
