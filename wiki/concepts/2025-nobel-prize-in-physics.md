# 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics

The **2025 Nobel Prize in Physics** was awarded to three United States–based scientists for experiments performed on a chip that revealed quantum physics in action, work described as opening opportunities for developing the next generation of quantum technology, including quantum cryptography, quantum computers, and quantum sensors.[1]

## Laureates

The laureates were [[john-clarke|John Clarke]] and [[john-martinis|John M. Martinis]], associated with the University of California campuses at Berkeley and Santa Barbara, and [[michel-devoret|Michel H. Devoret]] of Yale University in New Haven and the University of California, Santa Barbara.[1] The trio share prize money of 11 million Swedish kronor.[1]

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced that the prize had been awarded to Clarke, Devoret, and Martinis "for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit."[1]

## Scientific basis

Quantum mechanics allows a particle to pass straight through a barrier by a process called tunnelling.[1] As soon as large numbers of particles are involved, quantum mechanical effects usually become insignificant — making the laureates' demonstration of such effects at macroscopic scale in an electrical circuit notable.[1] The finding connects directly to the engineering foundations of [[quantum-computing|quantum computing]].[1]
