# Senior Executive Service

The Senior Executive Service (SES) is the upper echelon of the United States federal civil service, comprising the career executives who sit immediately below presidential appointees across the government's departments and agencies. Because SES members persist across administrations and control much of the day-to-day machinery of the executive branch, the corps is a central object of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's campaign against what the [[white-house|White House]] describes as an unaccountable administrative state.[1]

## The 2025 constitutional training requirement

In May 2025, the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] developed an intensive new training program for federal employees aspiring to join the Senior Executive Service.[1] According to an OPM memo obtained first by *RealClearPolitics*, the syllabus is grounded in the U.S. Constitution, the "Founding ideals of our government," and Trump's own executive orders.[1]

The development program requires 80 hours of video-based training and culminates in two days of in-person instruction in Washington, D.C.[1] It was set to go live in September 2025 and to affect government employees across the administration and its disparate agencies.[1] The program's stated goal is to "Ensure that SES officials uphold the Constitution and the rule of law and effectively serve the American people."[1]

Press coverage characterized the initiative as the latest step in an ongoing campaign to crush what the White House regards as an unaccountable administrative state — the same target set that produced the reorganizations pursued by [[doge|DOGE]] and by [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] at the [[state-department|State Department]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded to the training requirement with a characteristic caveat, remarking that he hoped the new executives would be "learning about the original Constitution and not the thing we're using now" — a reference to the recurring Badlands theme that the operative constitutional order diverges from the founding document.[1]

## Sudan ambassadorship example

Badlands commentary highlighted the Senior Executive Service background of [[john-godfrey|John Godfrey]], the SES official Joe Biden dispatched as ambassador to [[sudan|Sudan]] in 2022, as part of an argument that the [[state-department|State Department]] bureaucracy pursued a destabilization campaign to block a [[russian-naval-base-in-sudan|Russian naval base]] at Port Sudan.[2]
