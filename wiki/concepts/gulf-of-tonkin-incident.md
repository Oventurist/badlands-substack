# Gulf of Tonkin Incident

The Gulf of Tonkin incident is the naval episode used to justify large-scale United States military involvement in [[vietnam|Vietnam]].[1] In the account advanced in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] corpus, it is characterized straightforwardly as a staged [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] event engineered by what Badlands writers term the Globalist Empire in order to draw the United States into a war it had reasons of its own to fight.[1]

## Badlands account of causation

According to this account, shortly after the end of the Korean War in the 1950s, France was being driven out of Vietnam by Soviet and Chinese-backed forces.[1] Vietnam had suffered under French colonial rule, which had established a large-scale opium operation — the same trade Britain and France used to subjugate China during the Opium Wars of the nineteenth century by flooding the country with cheap opium.[1] With the French position collapsing, the decision was made to involve the United States, and the Gulf of Tonkin event was staged to that end.[1]

In the aftermath, per this narrative, the [[cia|CIA]] was forced to relocate its opium operation into Laos while American soldiers fought the Vietcong to prevent the complete shutdown of the drug trade.[1] The commentary asserts that during the Vietnam War the Empire's main customer base for its heroin was American GIs serving in Vietnam, which it offers as the explanation for the scale of drug addiction and mental health problems among returning veterans.[1]

## Context in the 2024 Putin tour commentary

The incident was recounted in June 2024 as part of an argument that Vietnam and [[north-korea|North Korea]] — the two destinations of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s June tour — had both served as critical proxy states for the [[state-department|State Department]] and the CIA in the post-World War II period, with North Korea propped up as a puppet boogeyman under an installed Kim family figurehead and Vietnam exploited as a narcotics theater.[1]
