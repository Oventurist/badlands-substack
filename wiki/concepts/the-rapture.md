# The Rapture

**The Rapture** is the belief that both living and dead believers will ascend into heaven to meet Jesus Christ at the Second Coming.[1] In "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?", the Badlands Media contributor writing as [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] challenges the widespread pre-Tribulation reading of the doctrine.[1]

## Terminology

The article notes that the term "rapture" appears nowhere in the New Testament.[1] The concept derives from the Apostle Paul's phrase "be caught up" — harpazō in Greek, rapio in Latin — in 1 Thessalonians 4:17, a term meaning "to seize, carry off by force," "to seize on, claim for one's self eagerly," or "to snatch out or away."[1] The article lists other occurrences at Matthew 11:12 and 13:19, John 6:15, 10:12 and 10:28–29, Acts 8:39 and 23:10, 2 Corinthians 12:2 and 12:4, Jude 1:23, and Revelation 12:5, with 1 Corinthians 15:51–52, Matthew 24:40–41, and Luke 17:34–36 commonly adduced in support.[1]

## The author's revision

The author accepts that Paul described a real coming event but is not confident it is synonymous with the church disappearing, primarily because the church is still present amid conditions he reads as [[the-tribulation|Tribulation]]-grade.[1] Reading eschatological scripture as a whole, he finds believers pictured in the world at various points and receiving encouragements and warnings about unfolding events.[1]

Examining Matthew 24:36–44, he observes that the passage does not specify who is "taken" — believer or non-believer — while stating plainly that the end times will resemble the days of Noah before the flood, when it was the evil that was removed from the world.[1] He therefore proposes that the rapture is instead the process or event by which believers successfully endure the Tribulation, resist the [[mark-of-the-beast|Mark of the Beast]], and enter the [[millennial-reign|Millennial Reign]] — that is, the rapture removes the evil.[1]

## Framing

The essay's origin is described as a season of seeking God and studying scripture in an attempt to reconcile the scale of evil in the world — compared to biblical Sodom and Gomorrah — with commonly taught doctrines such as the pre-Tribulation rapture.[1] The driving questions were why the church has not been raptured if the judgements of [[book-of-revelation|Revelation]] are being poured out, how much worse conditions would need to become, and whether prevailing interpretations are simply wrong.[1] The author repeatedly stresses that these matters are inconsequential to salvation.[1]
