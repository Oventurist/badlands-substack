# High Fructose Corn Syrup

High Fructose Corn Syrup (HFCS) is a highly refined artificial sweetener that, according to Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], has become the number one source of calories in America.[1] It appears in nearly all processed foods and was described as packing on weight faster than any other single ingredient.[1]

## Health claims

The article attributes a broad range of harms to HFCS: raising LDL cholesterol, contributing substantially to the development of diabetes and tissue damage, promoting cancer, stimulating fat accumulation in the liver, increasing the risk of heart disease, and increasing mercury intake.[1] It is also said to cause leaky gut syndrome.[1]

Statistics cited as of September 2016 include: Americans consume an average of 50 grams of HFCS daily; HFCS represents more than 40 percent of caloric sweeteners added to foods and beverages and is the sole caloric sweetener in U.S. soft drinks; consumption rose more than 1,000 percent between 1970 and 1990, far exceeding the intake change of any other food or food group and serving as a main factor in the obesity epidemic; HFCS can contain up to 570 micrograms of mercury per gram; and the average 20-ounce soda contains 15 teaspoons of sugar, all of it HFCS.[1]

## Production

To create HFCS, caustic soda is used to strip the corn kernel from its starch, producing corn syrup; enzymes — commonly derived from genetically modified organisms — are then introduced to convert the syrup's sugars into super-sweet fructose.[1] The finished product contains no enzymes, vitamins, or minerals, only sugar and calories.[1] The article rejects the argument that HFCS is "natural" because it originates in corn, noting both the extent of chemical processing involved and the fact that much modern corn is itself genetically modified by growers seeking larger yields and higher profits.[1]
