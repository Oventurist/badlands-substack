# Permanent Revolution

Permanent Revolution is the doctrine associated with [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]], described in "Shadows, Sovereigns and Civil Wars – Part 2" as having been inspired by the thinking of [[alexander-parvus|Parvus]], with whom Trotsky was closely associated through the 1905 revolution and after.[1] Trotsky credited Parvus' early studies with transforming "the conquest of power by the proletariat from an astronomical 'final' goal to a practical task for our own day."[1]

Matthew Ehret argues that the doctrine's logic of permanent war and permanent revolution did not die with Trotsky but was revived in a right-wing register — first by the ex-Trotskyists who founded [[neoconservatives|neo-conservatism]], and later by the alt-right organizing of [[otto-von-habsburg|Otto von Habsburg]] and [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]].[1] The article observes the irony that the Jesuit-trained Bannon, frontman of an organization descended from an anti-Bolshevik Catholic body, has said he models his tactics on those of Lenin.[1]

Whether expressed as Trotsky's effort to integrate Darwinism with Marxist dialectical materialism or as the neoconservative commitment to a survival-of-the-fittest ethic merged with a gnostic Christian end-times doctrine, Ehret contends the effects are largely identical: global chaos with a supposed point of rapture or synthesis to resolve it.[1]
