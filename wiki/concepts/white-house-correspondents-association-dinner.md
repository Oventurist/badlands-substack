# White House Correspondents' Association Dinner

The White House Correspondents' Association Dinner is the annual Washington gathering of the political press corps and the president. The 2026 edition was rescheduled to July 24 at the Waldorf Astoria in Washington, D.C., after the original dinner was disrupted by a shooting in April.[1] The association said it intended to complete the interrupted program, including journalism awards and a performance by mentalist Oz Pearlman, and the event was held under enhanced security after the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] implemented a strengthened security plan following the April attack.[1]

## Trump's 2026 address

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] joked that the audience represented "the really largest group of Trump derangement syndrome people ever put together at one time."[1] According to ABC News, his address lasted more than an hour, alternating between calls for cooperation with the press and criticism of journalists and political opponents, and ended with the president putting on a red "Trump 2028" hat as part of a joke about another presidential campaign.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] catalogued the highlights: jokes at the expense of Adam Schiff, Chris Christie, Kaitlan Collins, [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] and RFK Jr., self-deprecating asides when jokes didn't land, and a line calling [[bari-weiss|Bari Weiss]] "a gay dictator" over CBS News.[1]

## Inverted humiliation ritual

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the dinner had historically functioned as a humiliation ritual directed at the American public rather than the bipartisan unity it appeared to represent, citing his own former admiration for Stephen Colbert's 2006 roast of George W. Bush as an indictment of NeoCon policy.[1] In his reading Colbert was always cut from the same cloth as the warmongers he purported to oppose, and Trump has inverted the evening into a reverse humiliation ritual on the Media Industrial Complex.[1]

Burning Bright singled out Trump's somber lament that the media would have nothing left to talk about once he was gone, treating it as both a shot at media incentive structures and an existential truth: that once Trump leaves center stage — which the writer hopes will not be until 2032, a possibility Trump is doing nothing to distance himself from — the incentive structures that made the media class will unmake it.[1] He compared the dynamic to the Joker's existential collapse were he ever to triumph over Batman, arguing that the media defined itself entirely in opposition to Trump and in doing so revealed that its real opposition is the American public.[1]


## The April 25, 2026 shooting

Shots were fired at the dinner at the Washington Hilton on April 25, 2026, prompting Secret Service agents to evacuate President Donald Trump, who was unharmed according to the Associated Press.[3] The suspect, identified by AP as [[cole-thomas-allen|Cole Thomas Allen]], was reported by Reuters to have been armed with a shotgun, handgun and knives, to have fired at a Secret Service agent, and to have been subdued and arrested.[3] CBS News reported that an agent was struck by a round while wearing a bulletproof vest and was expected to be OK, and that the suspect left writings describing a plan to target administration officials "prioritized from highest-ranking to lowest."[3] Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] said the suspect appeared to be targeting Trump administration officials, potentially including Trump.[3] Trump wrote on Truth Social that the Secret Service and law enforcement did a "fantastic job" and that the dinner would be rescheduled within 30 days.[3] See [[2026-white-house-correspondents-dinner-shooting|the 2026 dinner shooting]].[3]

## Prosecution of the alleged attacker

In May 2026 the man accused of attempting to attack President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the dinner pleaded not guilty to federal charges including attempted assassination and assaulting a federal officer, with an attorney entering the plea on his behalf.[2] His legal team sought to remove Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and US Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]] from the prosecution because both attended the event and could be treated as victims or witnesses, a request that defense counsel Eugene Ohm suggested might extend to Pirro's entire office; Judge [[trevor-mcfadden|Trevor McFadden]] deferred ruling.[2] Details appear at [[whca-dinner-assassination-attempt-prosecution|the WHCA dinner assassination attempt prosecution]].[2]

## The 2025 dinner and the Washington party circuit

The 2025 edition was marked by the absence of both the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] and the president himself, who made clear they had no intention of participating.[4] The scheduled host, comedian Amber Ruffin, was subsequently dropped after the association said it wanted to focus not on "the politics of division" but on celebrating journalism.[4]

Despite the diminished main event, the surrounding social calendar expanded rather than contracted, proliferating into a morning-to-midnight array of breakfasts, brunches and boozy parties.[4] Media consultant Tammy Haddad, who helped host two events, noted there were more parties than in previous years, observing that "politics have taken over media" as new outlets gained ground on established publications: "There's all these opportunities for regular people, people that have a political opinion, to elevate themselves. So that's why they're here."[4] Attendees included the usual mix of television anchors, reporters and commentators.[4]

Badlands commentary read the New York Times account as throwing shade at new media, and summarized the weekend under the headline "boozing and brunching while Rome burns."[4] Contributor Ashe in America argued that by withholding his attendance President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had turned the dinner into "a hilarious humiliation ritual for legacy media," and mocked the position that Ivy-educated journalists had simply missed the sitting president's apparent dementia — arguing they should instead stand on their accomplishment of "deceiving the world for years."[4]
