# Strawman Theory

**Strawman theory** is the belief that government registration of a birth creates a separate legal person — a "Strawman" — distinct from the living human being, and that legal obligations attach only to that fiction. The doctrine is central to the Badlands Media essay [[soul-governance|Soul Governance]], which treats it as the operative mechanism of modern control.[1]

According to the essay, birth certificates "were not records; they were bonds," legal names "were not identities; they were contracts," and social contracts "were not agreements; they were surrender."[1] At the moment a person is documented rather than simply born, the article says, a "shadow twin" is created: a phantom made "of ink and assumption" that can be taxed, surveilled, fined, regulated, and detained.[1]

The essay maintains that the living soul remains outside that jurisdiction unless the person forgets the distinction and, in ignorance or fear, declares "I am that name. I consent."[1] It summarizes the claim in the formula "you were not ruled — you were misnamed," and casts the reader not as the Strawman but as "the sovereign holding the pen."[1]

The doctrine is tied in the essay to the [[cestui-que-vie-trust|Cestui Que Vie trust]] and to the jurisdictional claim of [[unam-sanctam|Unam Sanctam]], and is said to be extended into the present through biometric identification, where each checkpoint restates the presumption that "you belong to the system unless you prove otherwise."[1] Its proposed remedy is the revocation of "all claims made on your name, your mind, your body, your frequency," accomplished through remembrance rather than violence.[1]

Strawman theory circulates widely in sovereign-citizen and freeman-on-the-land movements; courts in the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom have uniformly rejected it as having no basis in law.[1]
