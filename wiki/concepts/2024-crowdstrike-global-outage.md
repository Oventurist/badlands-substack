# 2024 CrowdStrike Global Outage

The 2024 CrowdStrike global outage was a worldwide software failure that began on Friday, July 19, 2024, when a faulty update from the cybersecurity firm [[crowdstrike|CrowdStrike]] brought computer systems across the business, healthcare, technology and government sectors "to a screeching halt in under 12 hours."[1]

## Timeline

CNN reconstructed the collapse in Eastern Time. Late Thursday night and early Friday, 911 emergency centers and transportation services were disrupted. Between 6 and 7 a.m., the [[white-house|White House]] began investigating and global banks reported problems. Between 8 and 9 a.m., some flights resumed while airports braced for a difficult day. Between 9 a.m. and noon, federal agencies became involved and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was briefed. From late morning through early afternoon, doctors' and DMV appointments were canceled, with further medical disruptions in the late afternoon. Massive flight delays continued into Saturday morning.[1]

## Interpretation in alternative media

Writers at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the outage as instructive rather than accidental. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized it as "a massive red pill" on smart devices and on elections, pointing to reports of internet-connected refrigerators locking shut and arguing that the event undercut official assurances that electronic voting equipment is "air gapped" from the internet — a theme of the Badlands program [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]].[1] She linked the episode to the broader question of whether the convenience of smart thermostats, doorbells and voice assistants justifies risks such as government-mandated thermostat caps or social credit scoring.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the central worldwide debate was whether the outage was a cyber attack, which mainstream reporting denied, and connected the event to a Washington Post report that an obscure Florida company began announcing management of a large unused block of internet address space formerly owned by the U.S. military on January 20, 2021, as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] left office.[1] He also noted that cyber security had become a mainstream talking point with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[starlink|Starlink]] again in the spotlight, and that the [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] network was completely unaffected while banking networks failed — a point he framed as an argument for decentralization.[1] Both writers connected the outage to the possibility of an election-season false-flag cyber attack.[1]
