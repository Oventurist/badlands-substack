# Ukrainian desertion crisis

The Ukrainian desertion crisis refers to the sharp rise in soldiers abandoning their posts in Ukraine's armed forces during 2024, documented in prosecutions brought by the Ukrainian prosecutor's office.[1]

## Scale

The Ukrainian prosecutor's office opened 51,000 cases of desertion in the first nine months of 2024, a pace on track to roughly double the previous year's total.[1] *The Times of London* reported Ukrainian government data showing that "51,000 criminal cases were initiated for desertion and abandonment of a military unit between January and September of this year."[1] *El País* had earlier reported 45,000 prosecutions from January through August, while Al Jazeera put the figure at at least 30,000 desertions.[1]

## Context

At the start of 2024 Kyiv was estimated to field between 500,000 and 800,000 active-duty soldiers plus roughly 300,000 reservists.[1] Ukrainian forces sustained further casualties defending against Russian advances and during the Kursk incursion.[1] Struggling to replace losses, Ukraine lowered its conscription age from 27 to 25, and American politicians — [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] prominent among them — pressed Kyiv to lower the draft age to 18.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] juxtaposed the desertion figures with [[rand-corporation|RAND Corporation]] analyst [[michael-bohnert|Michael Bohnert]]'s argument that Western-supplied F-16s and Abrams tanks should be expended to destruction, treating the pairing as evidence that Western commentary had lost touch with Ukraine's manpower reality.[1] The commentary argued the desertion data undercut any expectation that the war could be sustained indefinitely and criticized Graham's push to expand the draft.[1]
