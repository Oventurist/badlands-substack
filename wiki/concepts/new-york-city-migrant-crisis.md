# New York City Migrant Crisis

The New York City migrant crisis refers to the strain placed on the city's shelter, housing and budget systems by the arrival of more than 100,000 migrants since April 2022, and the political backlash against Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]]' handling of it.[1]

## Scale and municipal response

By August 2023 the influx had overwhelmed the city government's capacity to house and feed arrivals.[1] Measures approved by Adams that drew resident anger included removing wedding parties from hotels to free up rooms, shipping migrants to suburban counties, and housing adult men in the gymnasiums of actively used elementary schools.[1] The latest flashpoint was the housing of 3,000 migrants in a tent city on Randall's Island, an expanse of parkland and recreational facilities along the East River between northern Manhattan and Queens.[1] Taxpayers already angered by the conversion of youth soccer fields and other leisure assets to migrant housing then learned that the city was spending $20 million per month on the Randall's Island encampment.[1]

## Gracie Mansion confrontation

On Sunday, August 27, 2023, police were called to the mayor's mansion when black-clad [[antifa|Antifa]] counterprotesters arrived at a demonstration against Adams' migrant policies, producing fistfights and bodies slammed to the ground.[1] The rally was headlined by Guardian Angels founder [[curtis-sliwa|Curtis Sliwa]], who told the crowd the migrants had "jumped the queue" and would keep coming so long as they were given hotels and three meals a day, more than homeless Americans and struggling veterans receive.[1]

Protesters carried signs reading "STOP REWARDING, START DEPORTING" and "AMERICANS OVER MIGRANTS," and the *New York Post* quoted one Trump supporter shouting "No migrants on Long Island! We pay a lot of property taxes!"[1] Counterprotesters held signs reading "NO ONE CHOOSES TO BE A REFUGEE" and "HUMANITY HAS NO BORDERS," with some yelling obscenities at the NYPD.[1]

## Badlands framing

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], argued that each four-year cycle brings both an "election variant" of COVID and a social cause used to ignite street violence, and that immigration appeared to be the successor to the George Floyd protests.[1] She noted that the counterprotest came not from migrants but from Antifa agitators, described as cheap and easy to mobilize, generating imagery reminiscent of the 2020 "Summer of Love," and concluded that the clash was "right on cue to lay the groundwork for social chaos next summer."[1]

## James Madison High School relocation (January 2024)

In January 2024, students at a Brooklyn high school were removed from their classrooms and shifted to remote learning to make room for nearly 2,000 migrants evacuated from a controversial tent shelter ahead of a major storm bearing down on New York City.[2] The city acted amid concerns that the massive migrant tent at Floyd Bennett Field would collapse under torrential rain and gusting winds, and packed the evacuees instead into the second-floor gymnasium at [[james-madison-high-school|James Madison High School]], five miles away.[2]

The school's neighbors objected to the last-minute decision.[2] One local resident, identifying himself only as Rob, called the move "a litmus test," saying: "They are using a storm, a legitimate situation, where they are testing this out. I guarantee you they'll be here for the entire summer."[2] He added that "there's 1,900 people getting thrown into my neighborhood, half a block from where I live and we don't know who they are."[2]

## April 2024 City Hall protest

In April 2024, hundreds of migrants flooded the grounds of City Hall in Lower Manhattan to protest the scaling down of their hotel accommodations, which [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized as luxury rooms funded by taxpayers and a sign that New York City could be on the cusp of social unrest.[3] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] responded to footage of the demonstration with "Wow," while another user of X posted video of angry migrants surrounding the City Hall complex as security was reinforced and the situation remained tense.[3]

The protest came one day after pro-Palestinian groups shuttered critical infrastructure in metropolitan areas nationwide, including the Brooklyn Bridge.[3] ZeroHedge argued that the risk of nationwide social instability was elevated because the Biden administration, through open southern borders, together with what it called a shadowy network of NGOs, had facilitated the largest inflow of illegal immigrants in the nation's history.[3]
