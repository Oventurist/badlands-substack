# Iraqi Invasion of Kuwait

The Iraqi invasion of Kuwait began on 2 August 1990 and marked the beginning of the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]].[1] After defeating the State of Kuwait on 4 August 1990, Iraq militarily occupied the country for the next seven months.[1]

## Aftermath

The subsequent conflict pitted Iraq against a 42-country coalition led by the United States, carried out in two phases: Operation Desert Shield, the military buildup from August 1990 to January 1991, and Operation Desert Storm, which opened with aerial bombing on 17 January 1991 and closed with the American-led liberation of Kuwait on 28 February 1991.[1]

## Badlands Media interpretation

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues the invasion's timing was decisive for the media narrative in the United States. The [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scandal — the biggest banking scandal in history, implicating the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and the [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] — was reaching its regulatory climax in 1990–1991 while [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was president.[1] In Lange's account, Desert Shield filled the headlines for months and Desert Storm erased BCCI from public memory altogether: "the biggest bank scandal in history was forgotten because of a war."[1] Invoking the Q line "Public truths of some events force WARS," he contends Bush feared full exposure of BCCI and therefore started a war that became a massive money laundering operation in its own right.[1]
