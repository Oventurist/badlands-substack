# Hunter Biden Investigation Whistleblowers

In May 2023, [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] and [[fbi|FBI]] personnel came forward alleging irregularities in the handling of the federal investigation into [[hunter-biden]].[1] Rep. [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-OH) alleged that both agencies were attempting to "crush" those whistleblowers in retaliation for their disclosures.[1]

## Jordan's allegations

Speaking on Fox News Channel's "Hannity" on Tuesday, May 23, 2023, Jordan told host [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] that the agencies were retaliating against employees who had come forward, and that Democrats' professed support for whistleblowers applied only when the target was [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — noting that an anonymous whistleblower had been behind Trump's first impeachment.[1] He argued that the whistleblowers were exposing "the double standard in the attack on President Trump but the attack on we the people."[1]

Jordan contrasted the present cases with events seven years earlier, when [[james-comey|Jim Comey]], [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] and [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] pursued Trump and his campaign with, in his telling, "no probable cause, no predicate, no evidence whatsoever" — a characterization he attributed to [[john-durham|Special Counsel John Durham]]'s report released that week.[1] He said the same pattern was visible in the prosecutions brought by [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] in Georgia and [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] in Washington.[1] Hannity framed the matter as a failure of whistleblower protection laws.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] acknowledged the widespread expectation that no charges would reach [[christopher-wray|Wray]], [[merrick-garland|Garland]] or the Bidens, but argued that the hearings were nonetheless "a battle for hearts & minds" rather than a realistic path to convictions while the DOJ and a Senate of career politicians remained unfavorable.[1] Invoking the phrase "the military is the only way," he theorized that mass public demand for the abolition of the FBI would raise the perceived necessity — and therefore likelihood — of military intervention.[1] On this view, the whistleblower hearings, like the Durham report, function primarily to brief the public on [[deep-state]] activity, with each new bombshell about institutional corruption activating more citizens and making them more comfortable with the conclusion that the system will not fix itself.[1]
