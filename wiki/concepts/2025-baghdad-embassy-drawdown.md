# 2025 Baghdad Embassy Drawdown

The 2025 Baghdad embassy drawdown refers to the United States decision, reported on June 11, 2025, to partially evacuate its embassy in Baghdad, Iraq, amid rapidly rising tensions with Iran. Non-essential US embassy staff and their dependents were ordered out of the country because of heightened security risks, according to US government sources.[1]

## Background and trigger

Officials declined to specify publicly what prompted the removal, but reporting by CBS News — the BBC's US partner — indicated that Israel was prepared to launch an operation into Iran, and that this was part of the reason some Americans were advised to leave the region. Officials said they anticipated that Iran could retaliate against certain US sites in Iraq.[1] The drawdown coincided with a period in which US talks over Iran's nuclear programme appeared to have stalled.[1]

A [[state-department|State Department]] official told the BBC that the department was "constantly assessing the appropriate personnel posture at all our embassies," and that "based on our latest analysis, we decided to reduce the footprint of our mission in Iraq."[1] In parallel, the State Department authorized the departure of non-essential personnel and family members from Bahrain and Kuwait, giving them the choice of whether to leave.[1]

## Diplomacy in parallel

Despite the evacuation posture, US Middle East envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] was still planning to hold talks with Iran on its nuclear programme that Sunday, officials told CBS. Witkoff was reported by Axios to be meeting Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] in Muscat, Oman.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributors read the escalation as narrative management rather than a straightforward slide into war. Contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] highlighted remarks attributed to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] framing a possible strike as surgical: "we can blow up a lab but nobody's going to be in the lab."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump had told the public he wanted peace in the Middle East, including with Iran, had told Israel not to escalate, and had signalled that if bombs fell "nobody would be in the labs," concluding that audiences were "being told a story to cultivate a mandate for the new, multipolar world."[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] warned that establishment "ConInc" figures appeared "giddy," and suggested Trump had accelerated an inevitable confrontation rather than allowing it to fester, so that the public could see "who the true warmongers are."[1]

Reporting on the same day also noted that Trump announced China would make it easier for American industry to obtain magnets and rare earth minerals, clearing the way for continued trade talks, and that the US would stop efforts to revoke the visas of Chinese nationals on American college campuses.[1]
