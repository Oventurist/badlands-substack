# November 2023 US Emergency Visits to Ukraine

In November 2023, two of the most senior US national-security officials — Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] and [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] — made separate unannounced "urgent" trips to Ukraine as the country's counteroffensive faltered and its war effort appeared to be imploding.[1]

## Austin's visit

Austin arrived in Kiev on Monday, November 20, 2023 during an unannounced visit, at an awkward moment for US–Ukraine relations: President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had been publicly complaining about a decrease in artillery shell supplies, as those munitions were now going to Israel for its war in Gaza.[1] "I was honored to meet with President @ZelenskyyUa in Kyiv today to reaffirm the United States' steadfast support for Ukraine," Austin wrote on [[x-corp|X]], adding that "we, along with our allies and partners, will continue to support Ukraine's urgent battlefield needs and long-term defense requirements."[1] It was Austin's second trip to Kiev since the Russian invasion, and he said he was there "to deliver an important message – the United States will continue to stand with Ukraine in their fight for freedom against Russia's aggression, both now and into the future."[1]

Observers noted that the shift by Washington and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] toward talking mainly about "long-term defense" began as it became clear Ukraine's counteroffensive was failing.[1] Meanwhile [[congress|Congress]] had been dragging its feet on passing President [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s latest supplemental funding requests, amid wrangling over what would be sent to Israel versus what would be set aside for Ukraine.[1]

## Burns's visit

The week before Austin's trip, CIA Director William Burns made a secretive, unannounced visit to Ukraine to hold urgent meetings with Zelensky and top military and intelligence officials.[1] Commentary in Asia Times concluded that the flurry of activity was a response to how badly things were going on the battlefield, with Washington having invested many tens of billions of dollars into what was clearly a proxy war against Russia.[1]
