# 2024 USA Today/Suffolk University Poll on Minority Voters

A *USA Today*/Suffolk University poll published at the start of January 2024 found a pronounced shift of Hispanic and black voters away from President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and, in the Hispanic case, toward former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The findings were carried by ZeroHedge under the headline "Sea-Change: Most Hispanics Now Prefer Trump — Biden's Black Support Plummets From 2020" and reproduced in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of January 4, 2024.[1]

## Findings

Trump held a five-point lead among Hispanic respondents, with Biden trailing 39 percent to 34 percent — an outcome the report described as an absolute sea change from 2020, when Biden's Hispanic share had beaten Trump 65 percent to 32 percent.[1] Among black voters, Biden had received 87 percent of the vote in 2020 but only 63 percent intended to vote for him in November 2024.[1] Trump's black support was holding steady at the same 12 percent he received in 2020; rather than shifting to Trump, 20 percent of black respondents planned to vote for someone other than Biden or Trump, with many eager to back a third-party candidate.[1] Commentators noted the findings were likely to compound [[democratic-party|Democratic]] worries about the 2024 election, confounding years of media portrayals of Trump as a racist.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that the minority vote was swinging heavily toward Trump as predicted, and that combined with rising black support the trend laid out "a story which will almost certainly end in doom for the Democrats."[1] He argued that most Hispanics who immigrate legally from Central and South America are fleeing communism and are therefore unlikely to vote for it, and that black voters were increasingly recognizing the ways the left had used them for its own ends.[1] An awakening among the minority population, in his framing, remained the establishment's biggest fear and was happening in real time.[1]
