# 2024 Kerman Bombings

On January 3, 2024, at least 95 people were killed in explosions in Iran near a public ceremony commemorating the death of [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]], the senior Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps officer killed in 2020 by an American airstrike.[1] Iranian officials said the blasts were the work of terrorists.[1]

## Casualties and response

Some 211 others were wounded by the explosions, which occurred as crowds gathered near Soleimani's tomb, according to Iran's government-run Islamic Republic News Agency.[1] [[iran|Iran]] said it was investigating the cause of the blasts, and officials revised the death toll downward after earlier reporting that 103 people had been killed.[1]

## Islamic State claim of responsibility

[[islamic-state|Islamic State]] claimed responsibility on Thursday, January 4, 2024, for the two explosions, which it said were carried out by two IS members who detonated explosive belts in the crowd gathered at the cemetery in the southeastern Iranian city of Kerman.[2] The claim was posted in a statement on the group's affiliate Telegram channels.[2] The memorial was marking the fourth anniversary of the death of [[qassem-soleimani|Soleimani]], who was assassinated in Iraq in 2020 by a U.S. drone.[2] In Washington, [[white-house|White House]] spokesman [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] told reporters the United States was in no position to doubt Islamic State's claim of responsibility.[2]

Reuters reported the twin blasts as having killed nearly 100 people and wounded 284 others, including children — the bloodiest such attack since the 1979 Islamic Revolution.[2] Tehran vowed revenge, with Iran's First Vice President [[mohammad-mokhber|Mohammad Mokhber]] telling reporters in Kerman that "a very strong retaliation will be meted out to them by the hands of the soldiers of Soleimani."[2]

## Regional context

The bombings came at a moment of heightened tensions across the Middle East, with war raging between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]], an Islamist militant group that had moved closer to Iran in recent years.[1] The day before, senior Hamas leader [[saleh-al-arouri|Saleh al-Arouri]] was killed in Beirut in what Lebanese officials described as a suspected Israeli attack; the Israeli military declined to comment on whether it was responsible.[1] The episode was carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief of January 4, 2024, sourced to *The Wall Street Journal*.[1]
