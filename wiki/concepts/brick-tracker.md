# Brick Tracker

Brick Tracker was the code name given by the office of Special Inspector General [[stuart-w-bowen-jr|Stuart W. Bowen Jr.]] to its secret investigation into a cash-filled bunker in rural [[lebanon|Lebanon]] holding money stolen from Iraq during the American occupation.

## Origins

After the 2003 defeat of Saddam Hussein's government, $12 billion to $14 billion in shrink-wrapped $100 bills was airlifted from Andrews Air Force Base to Baghdad aboard Air Force C-17s, with a further $5 billion sent by electronic transfer; the airlift was ordered by American proconsul [[paul-bremer|Paul Bremer]]. Much of the money could not subsequently be accounted for. Bowen's investigators eventually determined that between $1.2 billion and $1.6 billion had been stolen and moved to the Lebanese bunker for safekeeping.

## Obstruction and closure

Bowen kept the discovery and the Brick Tracker investigation secret, never discussing it publicly until a series of interviews with the Boston Globe. He said he did not know how the money reached Lebanon and that the case could not be closed "for reasons beyond our control." His investigators briefed the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[fbi|FBI]], but neither the [[george-w-bush|Bush]] administration nor the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration pursued the lead; Bowen believed one reason was the official view that "it was Iraqi money stolen by Iraqis." When his staff attempted to extend the search into Lebanon, they met resistance from the U.S. embassy in Beirut: Bowen himself was not allowed to travel there, and two investigators who did were denied permission to see the bunker. Lebanon's prosecutor general initially agreed to cooperate before reversing.

## Interpretation in the corpus

The Badlands Media article "Bad Actors" treats the Lebanon destination as significant because Lebanon is the base of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and the place where [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] was raised and retains family connections, presenting the episode as evidence that U.S. policy was funding the very terrorist networks it claimed to be fighting.
