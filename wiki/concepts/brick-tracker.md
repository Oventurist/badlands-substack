# Brick Tracker

Brick Tracker was the internal code name given by the Office of the Special Inspector General for Iraq Reconstruction to its secret investigation into $1.2–1.6 billion in stolen Iraqi reconstruction funds that had been traced to a bunker in rural Lebanon.[1] It is described in the Badlands Media article "Bad Actors" as the closest anyone came to following the money that vanished during the American occupation of Iraq.[1]

## Origins

After the 2003 invasion, between $12 billion and $14 billion in shrink-wrapped $100 bills was flown from Andrews Air Force Base to Baghdad aboard Air Force C-17s, with a further $5 billion transferred electronically, on the authorisation of [[paul-bremer|Paul Bremer]].[1] Much of the money could not subsequently be accounted for. [[stuart-w-bowen-jr|Stuart W. Bowen Jr.]], appointed special inspector general in 2004, concluded that much of it was probably used by the Iraqi government in some way, but for years could not trace billions more until his investigators achieved the Lebanon breakthrough.[1]

## Secrecy and obstruction

Bowen kept both the discovery and the investigation secret, never discussing Brick Tracker publicly until interviews given to The Boston Globe.[1] "I don't know how the money got to Lebanon," he said, adding that if he did, more progress would have been made on the case.[1] His investigators briefed the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[fbi|FBI]], but neither the Bush nor the Obama administration pursued the lead; Bowen was told one reason was that "it was Iraqi money stolen by Iraqis."[1] When his office attempted to extend the search into Lebanon it met resistance from the US embassy in Beirut: Bowen was barred from travelling, two investigators who did travel were denied permission to view the bunker, and Lebanon's prosecutor general reversed an initial agreement to cooperate.[1] The Iraqi government itself never sought to retrieve the funds and kept the bunker's existence secret.[1]

## Significance in the Badlands account

Joe Lange treats the Lebanon destination as the decisive clue, Lebanon being the base of Hezbollah and, in his account, the childhood home and family power base of [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1] He argues that the shutdown of Brick Tracker demonstrates that American foreign aid and reconstruction money has long been diverted to fund the enemies the United States claims to be fighting, a pattern he identifies as one of the recurring themes of the Q drops.[1] By 2011 the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and the Iraqi government claimed to have accounted for all but $6 billion of the missing money.[1]
