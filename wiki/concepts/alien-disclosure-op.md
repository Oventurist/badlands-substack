# Alien Disclosure Op

The **Alien Disclosure Op** is the term used by the Badlands Substack team for a disclosure initiative on extraterrestrial matters associated with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], characterised on the program as "Fake, but kind of Real."[1]

## Formulation

Episode 7 of [[badlands-blitz|The Blitz]], published February 21, 2026, introduced the subject as "Donald Trump's Fake, but kind of Real Alien Disclosure Op," a phrasing that holds two claims at once: that the disclosure event is stage-managed and therefore not a straightforward revelation, and that it nonetheless carries a genuine component that makes it consequential.[1] The construction mirrors the outlet's habitual distinction between narrative deployments — messaging operations aimed at shaping perception — and "Actuals," the underlying verifiable developments.[1]

## Placement within the week's coverage

The segment occupied the middle of Episode 7, following the discussion of the Epstein Web's expansion and the resulting [[prince-andrew|Prince Andrew]] inflection point, and preceding the closing treatment of the anticipated United States–[[iran|Iran]] convergence.[1] Its inclusion alongside those subjects situates alien disclosure not as a fringe topic but as one element in a coordinated sequence of narrative activity for the week.[1]

The episode was co-hosted by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and recorded 252 likes on Substack.[1]

## Bank of England warning, January 2026

In January 2026 the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] suddenly announced a warning that financial markets could collapse if the U.S. government were to announce the existence of aliens, a warning attributed to senior analyst [[helen-mccaw|Helen McCaw]].[2] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry found the timing conspicuous, noting that the article reporting the warning itself acknowledged that the disclosure trend had been years in the making, and asked why it should seem prudent to panic over the development at that exact moment.[2] He linked the episode to the simultaneous lifting of Venezuela sanctions and the [[safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue-executive-order|executive order shielding Venezuelan oil revenue]] from bank creditors, concluding that "it can't be a coincidence."[2]

## Obama remarks and the Trump declassification pledge (February 2026)

The disclosure sequence accelerated in February 2026 after [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] went viral for conflicting statements about extraterrestrial life, prompting [[fox-news|Fox News]] correspondent [[peter-doocy|Peter Doocy]] to ask President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for comment during an Air Force One press gaggle.[3] Trump deflected the substance — "Well, I don't know if they're real or not" — and instead accused Obama of having given out classified information: "He made a big mistake. He took it out of classified information."[3] When Doocy observed that a president can declassify anything he wants, Trump replied, "Maybe I'll get him out of trouble. I may get him out of trouble by declassifying."[3]

Hours later Trump posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that, "based on the tremendous interest shown," he would direct the Secretary of War and other relevant departments and agencies to begin identifying and releasing government files related to alien and extraterrestrial life, unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), unidentified flying objects (UFOs) "and any and all other information connected to these highly complex, but extremely interesting and important, matters."[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the exchange as Doocy and Obama running a controlled-opposition dynamic to trap Trump in a dialectic over classified material at a moment of maximum public interest — "right on the heels of the Epstein File deployment" — and argued that rather than allowing "Obama's aliens psyop" to ruminate, Trump was "rugging it by declassifying everything."[3] He added that the obvious implication of this particular declass exercise is that new technologies will be revealed as part of the "alien culture," technologies he says have been suppressed for generations and that will transform civilization and launch humanity into the next epoch, the [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]].[3]

## The Grusch disclosure as staged conditioning (2023)

An earlier articulation of the thesis appeared in June 2023 following whistleblower [[david-grusch|David Grusch]]'s claim that the U.S. government holds retrieved craft of non-human origin.[4] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] laid out a five-stage build-up toward public acceptance: fictional media placing extraterrestrials in the public mind since the 1950s; [[predictive-programming|predictive programming]] of the kind he says preceded 9/11 and Covid; a globalist objective of dethroning theism by reframing past prophetic activity as extraterrestrial contact; the release of the Tic Tac footage moving the acceptance window; and finally an authenticated whistleblower providing the first official confirmation of contact.[4] He argued that if this is a limited hangout preparing a false flag, the story would hold center stage long enough to shift public acceptance before a further disclosure or event capitalized on the conditioning.[4]

Deschamps grounded the thesis in the reported claim of [[operation-paperclip|Operation Paperclip]] scientist [[wernher-von-braun|Wernher von Braun]] that a global false flag involving sky projections of extraterrestrial craft, gods and mystics had been planned since at least the 1960s.[4] He also noted the alternative reading that the Grusch story was well-timed distraction from an FBI Biden bribery story that broke the same day, and that [[unacknowledged-special-access-programs|unacknowledged special access programs]] make independent verification effectively impossible.[4]
