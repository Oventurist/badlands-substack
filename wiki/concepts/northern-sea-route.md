# Northern Sea Route

The Northern Sea Route is the Arctic shipping corridor along Russia's northern coast linking Europe and Asia, and the object of a Russian federal development plan adopted in 2019.

## The 2019 development plan

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s Avoiding WWIII reports that Russia's Development Plan for the Northern Sea Route seeks to increase annual shipments to 80 million tons by 2024. Beyond ports and new Arctic mining hubs, the plan includes the construction of 40 new vessels — among them additional nuclear icebreakers — along with railways and northern seaports. The article states that the route will cut ten days of shipping time from goods moving between China and Europe.

## Relation to other corridors

The plan is presented as closely tied to the expansion and modernization of the 9,300 km [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] and its 4,300 km southern Baikal–Amur Mainline, and as one component of the wider [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]] and [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]]. Ehret situates it within [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Far Eastern Vision and the broader Eurasian development paradigm he contrasts with Western post-industrial decline.
