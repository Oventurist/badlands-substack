# Oregon Fourteenth Amendment Ballot Challenge

The Oregon Fourteenth Amendment ballot challenge was one of the state-level efforts in late 2023 and early 2024 to remove [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from a presidential primary ballot under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].[1]

## Supplemental briefing, January 2024

In the week before January 10, 2024, parties in the Oregon challenges filed supplemental briefing on two questions: whether the plaintiffs had standing to bring the challenge, and whether the Oregon Secretary of State possessed the authority to remove Trump from the state's ballot.[1] The Oregon proceeding ran in parallel with the [[maine-ballot-disqualification|Maine disqualification]] appeal and with [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]], the Colorado case the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] agreed on January 5, 2024 to review.[1]

## Assessment

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] grouped the Oregon case with the other multistate ballot challenges as "overt, direct election interference by uniparty government officials and progressive lawfare NGOs," and argued that progressives in Oregon and Maine had been emboldened by the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s affirmation of the trial court's finding that January 6 was an insurrection.[1] On her analysis, the durability of the Oregon and Maine efforts depended on whether the Supreme Court disturbed that underlying insurrection finding rather than merely resolving the Section 3 question.[1]
