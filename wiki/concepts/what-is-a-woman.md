# What Is a Woman?

"What Is a Woman?" is a documentary film released by [[daily-wire|The Daily Wire]] on June 1, 2022 and hosted by commentator [[matt-walsh|Matt Walsh]], built around Walsh's repeated posing of the title question to therapists, physicians, academics, politicians and members of the public.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reviewed the film a year after its release, writing that its promotional materials had presented it as "a high budget troll" — clips of interviewees walking out mid-interview and replays of Walsh's Dr. Phil confrontation — which had led her to dismiss it as an opportunistic marketing stunt from "Conservative, Incorporated" rather than a serious endeavor.[1]

## Content and scope

On finally watching the film, the reviewer reported being "pleasantly surprised by the seriousness" with which Walsh treats the subject, noting that it covers a wide range of ground: pronouns and non-surgical affirmation, women's sports and women's single-sex spaces, [[puberty-blockers|puberty blockers]] and surgeries, and ultimately the nature of truth itself.[1] Walsh interviews both advocates and critics of modern [[gender-ideology|gender theory]], and most of the advocates in the film threaten to end their interviews, accusing him of hatred and bigotry for asking simple questions.[1]

The film is credited with unwinding several claims made by gender theory advocates, among them the assertion that pronoun changes and cross-dressing have no lasting effect on a child's mental health and development, and the claim that pausing puberty with Lupron — a drug also known for its use in the chemical castration of sex offenders — is completely reversible.[1] In one of the film's most affecting moments, detransitioner [[scott-newgent|Scott Newgent]] held up a mutilated forearm with tears in his eyes and said, "We have five children's hospitals in the United States, promoting that," and elsewhere put the cost of surgery at "$70,000 a pop."[1]

The documentary also examines the impact of transgender inclusion on [[transgender-athletes-in-womens-sports|women's sports]], interviewing elite female athletes who lost life-changing opportunities to male competitors, while gender theory advocates in the film deny biological athletic differences on the grounds that transgender athletes have also lost contests — an argument presented without evidence.[1] Cases involving women's spaces range from spas and locker rooms to the [[loudoun-county-school-bathroom-assault-case|Loudoun County, Virginia]] assaults, in which the school board and education system were said to have covered up sexual assaults committed in female spaces.[1]

## The truth theme

At the film's heart, according to the review, is a search for truth: critics of gender theory rely on science and common sense, while advocates repeatedly become trapped in logical fallacies.[1] The point was made most sharply when Walsh asked a self-described "social scientist" whether he could define "woman" without using the word "woman," leaving the interviewee staring slack-jawed into the distance.[1] Walsh's line from his Dr. Phil appearance — "I care about the truth … Basic truth matters" — delivered in response to therapist [[sophie-denbeau|Sophie D'Enbeau]]'s question of why he cared so much, is quoted as the film's animating principle.[1]

## Criticism

The reviewer's principal criticism was that Walsh and his team stopped short of indicting the global order: the film explored neither the international network propagating gender ideology nor the financial establishment recruiting sterilized patients for life, and made no mention of the United Nations' [[sustainable-development-goals|Sustainable Development Goal #5]], which she argued explicitly enshrines gender theory as a global imperative and is showing up in American classrooms.[1] She characterized this omission as a classic earmark of Daily Wire reporting.[1] A second criticism was that the film underexplored parental rights: although Walsh spoke with an anonymous Canadian father under fire for misgendering his daughter and revisited Loudoun County, the retelling foregrounded Walsh's own publicity stunt of renting a basement to establish residency so he could speak at a school board meeting, presenting him as the hero and glossing over the rights and roles of parents.[1]

## Twitter controversy (June 2023)

A year after release, the film returned to public attention when [[daily-wire|The Daily Wire]] prepared to stream it on Twitter and the platform limited its reach.[1] Walsh tweeted that the film "got 3,500 retweets in two minutes and then Twitter flagged it as hate speech and shut down retweets," with a notice reading "Visibility Limited: This Tweet may violate Twitter's rules against Hateful Conduct" — a bad look for a platform marketed under new ownership as a free speech venue.[1] Within a day the suppression was lifted and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] himself tweeted the film and urged people to watch it; Walsh called it "A huge win."[1] The reviewer agreed, observing that "you can't buy that kind of publicity," and credited the episode with reminding her the film existed.[1]
