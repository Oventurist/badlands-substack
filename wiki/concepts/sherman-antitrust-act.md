# Sherman Antitrust Act

The Sherman Antitrust Act is the foundational United States competition statute, prohibiting monopolization and anti-competitive arrangements such as unlawful tying and exclusionary conduct against rivals.[1]

## Rumble v. Google

In May 2024, the video-sharing platform [[rumble|Rumble]] filed suit in the Northern District of California against [[google|Google]] and its parent [[alphabet|Alphabet]], alleging that Google's digital advertising policies violate the Sherman Act.[1] Rumble argued that "Google exploits significant conflicts of interest that stem from its multiple roles in this electronically traded marketplace," enabling it to "pocket a supra-competitive portion of every advertising dollar that passes through the Ad Tech markets it controls" — revenue that Rumble contends should have flowed to publishers and content creators.[1] The complaint accused Google of "exclud[ing] competition by engaging in conduct unlawful under settled antitrust precedent, including through unlawful tying arrangements, a pattern and practice of exclusionary conduct targeting actual and potential rivals," and sought injunctive relief and damages in excess of $1 billion.[1]
