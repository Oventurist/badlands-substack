# California Assembly Bill 1840

California Assembly Bill 1840 is a 2024 state measure that would make illegal immigrants eligible for the same first-time homebuyer assistance available to U.S. citizens, generating national controversy in the spring of that year.[1]

## Provisions and progress

The bill was introduced in mid-January 2024 by Assemblyman [[joaquin-arambula|Joaquin Arambula]], a Democrat from the Fresno area.[1] It would change existing law so that undocumented applicants qualify for the [[california-dream-for-all|California Dream for All Fund]], which supplies interest-free loans covering a down payment on a home for first-time buyers.[1] After several rounds of amendment, AB 1840 advanced in late February 2024 to the Assembly Committee on Housing and Community Development, where it awaited further action.[1] Some lawmakers registered opposition as the measure moved closer to becoming law, objecting to granting benefits to people who entered the country illegally.[1]

Arambula defended the bill on the grounds that homeownership has been the primary mechanism of generational wealth accumulation in the United States and that "the social and economic benefits of homeownership should be available to everyone."[1]

## Commentary

In the Badlands News Brief, [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the compassion framing surrounding the bill was a façade, writing that "the hordes of single military aged men from China are not going to settle down and create multigenerational American families."[1] He read the measure as part of a longer project of degrading American ideals — "the perversion of ideals is the point" — and found it fitting that the program invoked the *California* dream, likening its sponsors to the figure in "California Dreamin'" who kneels in a church and pretends to pray.[1]
