# California Budget Deficit (2023)

In December 2023 California's nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office projected that the state faced a record $68 billion budget deficit, attributable to a "severe revenue decline," with cumulative shortfalls potentially exceeding $155 billion over the following five years.[1] The projection raised the prospect of a formal fiscal budget emergency.

The shortfall followed a 25 percent drop in income tax collections in fiscal year 2022–2023, which ended June 30, compared with the prior year.[1] Compounding the problem, tax receipts normally due in April were delayed until October that year because of federal and state filing exemptions granted after severe winter storms, which made it difficult for state officials to gauge the scale of the deficit early enough to set budget priorities accordingly.[1]

A spokesperson for Gov. [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] suggested some of the roughly $24 billion the state held in reserves could be used to address the gap—an approach analysts agreed would be necessary.[1] Erin Mellon, communications director for Newsom's office, told The Epoch Times that "the Governor has maintained strict fiscal responsibility since taking office, building up the state's reserves to historic levels reaching the maximum allowed by the state constitution to be put in reserves and paying down debts—putting California in a strong position to deal with budget shortfalls."[1]
