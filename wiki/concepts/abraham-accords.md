# Abraham Accords

The Abraham Accords are the normalization agreements between Israel and a set of Muslim-majority states, originally brokered during the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration. By May 2026 they had become the framework Trump was publicly promoting as the vehicle for a comprehensive Middle East settlement that would include [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Expansion push, May 2026

In a Truth Social post, Trump said that following discussions with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud, UAE President Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, Qatar's Emir Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, Pakistani Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi, Jordan's King Abdullah II, and Bahrain's King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa, it "should be mandatory" that all of these countries simultaneously sign onto the Accords.[1] The countries named were Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain (already members), Qatar, Pakistan, Türkiye, Egypt, and Jordan; Trump allowed that one or two might have reason not to sign.[1]

Trump argued the Accords had produced a "Financial, Economic, and Social BOOM" for existing members — listed as the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Morocco, Sudan, and Kazakhstan — none of which had suggested leaving even during active conflict.[1] He tied the expansion to ongoing negotiations with Iran, warning that talks would end in "a Great Deal for all or, no Deal at all — Back to the Battlefront."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] connected the moment to an argument first made in November 2023: that Trump's "Impossible Task" was peace itself, and that the Middle East forever war — with the Israel–Iran conflict at its heart — was the Gordian knot through which he would codify his mandate.[1] In this reading, Trump is calling for a "Macro Region of Responsibility" in the Middle East that includes the Iranian regime he is ostensibly fighting, while the MAGA coalition has been split from what the author terms Congress-aligned Zionists, NATO warmongers, and "Faux-MAGA Grifters," and public appetite for war has been exhausted.[1]

## Nuclear linkage with Saudi Arabia, July 2026

In July 2026 President Trump said the United States was prepared to move forward with a [[us-saudi-civilian-nuclear-cooperation-agreement|civilian nuclear cooperation agreement]] with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] only if the kingdom eventually joined the Abraham Accords and normalized relations with [[israel|Israel]], linking two initiatives that had previously been discussed on separate tracks.[2] Saudi officials responded by reiterating their longstanding position that normalization remains contingent on meaningful progress toward a Palestinian state and an end to the conflict in Gaza, and Riyadh maintained that its conditions for joining the Accords had not changed.[2]

The exchange underscored the administration's continued effort to expand the Accords while pursuing broader security and energy partnerships in the Middle East, and highlighted the difficulty of advancing Israeli-Saudi normalization and a US-Saudi nuclear agreement simultaneously amid the Gaza war.[2] A Saudi source quoted in Badlands commentary said of the existing nuclear framework, "It was signed. So nothing to renegotiate... Tweets don't overturn signed agreements," and the Saudi Energy Ministry issued a statement in response.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued Trump did not intend to enforce the ultimatum and was posturing for Zionists in his coalition, noting that Saudi Arabia already held proposals from South Korean contractor KEPCO and a Japanese firm and intended to build two plants regardless.[2]

## Arab reluctance amid the 2026 Iran talks

During the May 2026 [[2026-iran-deal-negotiations|U.S.–Iran negotiations]], Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] highlighted a report that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had asked Arab states to join the Abraham Accords if he succeeded in making peace with [[iran|Iran]].[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the overture was met with "crickets," summarizing the Arab response as a polite refusal.[3] He attributed the reluctance to [[israel|Israel]]'s standing as what he called an international pariah, arguing that no state was eager to sign an agreement that might constrain its sovereignty should Israel later turn on it.[3] Separately, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Trump's peace overtures included references to Iran's eventual inclusion in the Accords.[3]

## Strain with the UAE (2024)

By April 2024, [[israel|Israel]]'s ties with key Gulf countries such as the
[[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] were described as near breaking point, only a few
short years after diplomatic normalization was hailed through the Abraham
Accords.[4] The UAE announced a suspension of diplomatic coordination with
Israel and a halt to humanitarian aid coordination following the deaths of
seven [[world-central-kitchen|World Central Kitchen]] workers in Gaza.[4]

## Disputed 2025 expansion reporting

In late June 2025, _Israel Hayom_ reported "leaked details" of an expanded version of the Abraham Accords that had been teased by Steve Witkoff on CNBC, claiming that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] had agreed to cede parts of the West Bank to [[israel|Israel]].[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed that reporting as false after the Saudi foreign ministry condemned Israeli Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]]'s July 2 call for Israeli sovereignty over the occupied West Bank as a violation of international law.[5]

## June 2025 expansion push

Immediately after the American assault on Iran's nuclear installations, a four-way telephone conversation took place between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], and Minister of Strategic Affairs [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]].[6] A source familiar with the discussion told Israel Hayom that all participants expressed extreme enthusiasm about the results of the B-2 bomber mission, describing the mood as "euphoria," and that Trump and Netanyahu aimed to move swiftly toward fresh peace arrangements with Arab states as part of an expansion of the Accords.[6]

Badlands analyst GhostofBasedPatrickHenry conducted a live assessment during coverage of a [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] White House press conference and expressed skepticism that the five-point plan described in the report was the agreement [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] had alluded to, because it neither required Israel to recognize Palestinian statehood nor restored Israeli-Palestinian borders to 1967 conditions — the sticking points he identified as non-negotiable for [[saudi-arabia|Saudi]] accession.[6] He noted that as the story published, US Special Envoy to Syria [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] was meeting Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] in [[riyadh|Riyadh]], suggesting the deal's details or optics were still being worked out, and predicted a deal would be announced but not the one leaked to Israeli media.[6]

## Trump's January 2024 Breitbart interview

In a more-than-two-hour exclusive interview with Breitbart News at Mar-a-Lago on January 10, 2024, former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said that had the 2020 election not gone the way it did, every Arab country and "possibly" [[iran|Iran]] would by then have signed the Abraham Accords.[7] "You would have had every country sign, including possibly Iran, into the Abraham Accords if the election wasn't rigged," he said. "By this time, you would have had every country—Saudi Arabia, and maybe even Iran—signed."[7] The countries that joined during his administration were the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Morocco and Sudan; no new country joined during President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s entire presidency.[7]

Trump argued that his approach to the Iranians — ruthless financial pressure through sanctions and the ordered killing of [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]] — was going to force the Islamic Republic to the bargaining table.[7] "Iran, as you know, was broke with me. Now, they've got $250 billion," he said, adding that when Tehran was broke there was "no money for Hamas, no money for Hezbollah."[7] He also maintained that the October 7 attack on Israel by Hamas, largely funded by Iran, "never would have happened" under his presidency, and that "we would have taken much more decisive action if it did."[7]

## Role in the April 2024 Iranian Barrage

Media coverage of Iran's April 13, 2024 missile and drone barrage credited the alliance forged through the Abraham Accords with helping to bring down nearly every projectile fired at Israel.[8] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted the shifting official numbers — from roughly 125 missiles to 300 — and the accompanying commentary mocking Iran's technology, arguing the barrage was never a genuine offensive.[8] He framed Saudi Arabia's insistence that Israel "sign the peace deal" through every escalation as the accords' logic asserting itself, and read the White House push for Saudi–Israeli normalization as its continuation.[8]

## Strain at the 2024 UN General Assembly

Addressing the [[united-nations-general-assembly]] in late September 2024 as Israeli forces prepared to enter Lebanon, [[benjamin-netanyahu]] held up a map identifying his "Arab partners," invoked the Abraham Accords, and assured delegates those partners would back him in confronting [[iran]].[9] Foreign ministers claiming to speak for all fifty-seven nations of the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation]] then rebutted him from the same podium, expressing indignation and demanding to know Israel's "endgame," with Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan]] among them.[9]

Badlands commentary took the exchange as vindication of [[douglas-macgregor|Colonel Douglas Macgregor's]] six-month-old assertion that the Accords were dead and would not be revived, even though Arab partners had continued to keep Israel provisioned and to intercept incoming missiles.[9] The maps Netanyahu displayed were described as neoconservative battle maps drawn by planners who saw the Accords as an opportunity to turn the Sunni Muslims of Arabia against the Shia Muslims of Persia.[9]

## Saudi normalization pressed at the November 2025 Trump–MBS meeting

Axios reported on November 25, 2025 that the November 18 White House meeting between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] grew tense over the possibility of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] joining the Abraham Accords and normalizing relations with [[israel|Israel]].[10] With the war in Gaza over, Trump had hoped the meeting would produce a breakthrough, and White House officials had signalled that expectation to the crown prince in advance; Trump raised the issue himself and pressed hard, while MBS pushed back, saying that Saudi public opinion is highly anti-Israel in the aftermath of the Gaza war and that Saudi society is not ready for such a move.[10]

## January 2025 expansion plans

On January 15, 2025, President-elect Donald Trump said he would use the momentum of the newly agreed Gaza ceasefire deal to expand the Abraham Accords, the US-backed agreements struck during his first term that normalized Israel's relations with several Arab countries.[11] Writing on Truth Social, Trump said his national security team, through Special Envoy to the Middle East [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], would continue working closely with Israel and allies to ensure Gaza never again became a terrorist safe haven, and would keep "promoting PEACE THROUGH STRENGTH throughout the region, as we build upon the momentum of this ceasefire to further expand the Historic Abraham Accords."[11]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry offered a speculative reading of the Accords as a diplomatic weapon: not, as marketed to the American public, an instrument to protect Israel from the Arabs, but a mechanism designed to protect the Arabs from what he called the "evil twin" in Tel Aviv by exposing and destroying it.[11] He noted that when the Emiratis and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] assembled the Accords they intentionally left out Saudi Arabia and Palestine, and that the moment they were signed on September 15, 2020, Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] dispatched the recently promoted Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] to begin work on an Israel–Saudi normalization deal that would fold Saudi Arabia into the Accords and represent a symbolic peace between Israel and Islam.[11] In that framing, the prospect of a genuine regional stabilization — which would render future plans for the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel Project]] all but impossible — is why the [[october-7-attacks|October 7th attacks]] happened, with Trump "chumming the waters" via the Accords and MBS "poking the sharks" with the normalization track.[11]

## June 2025 expansion signals

Middle East envoy Steve Witkoff told CNBC on June 25, 2025 that the White House anticipated "big announcements on countries that are coming into the Abraham Accords."[12] He characterized expansion of the accords as one of President Donald Trump's key objectives and said his team was coordinating with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and the State Department to bring additional states in.[12] Witkoff said the administration hoped for normalization "across an array of countries that people never would have contemplated would come in," and argued that broader accession would act as a stabilizer in the region.[12] The accords originated during Trump's first term in 2020, when the UAE, Bahrain, Morocco, and Sudan agreed to normalize relations with Israel.[12] Badlands commentary speculated about the possibility of Iran itself joining in the wake of the June 2025 strikes and ceasefire.[12]

## Leverage behind the agreement

Badlands commentary, drawing on Israeli journalist [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]]'s reporting and his December 2021 interview with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], holds that Trump's blockage of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s planned Israeli settlements in the [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank]] in May 2020 is exactly what leveraged Netanyahu into agreeing to the Accords.[13]

Netanyahu nonetheless attempted to withdraw at the last moment: twenty-four hours before the signing, Israeli ambassador to the US [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] called the Trump White House to say Netanyahu would not sign, citing "new elections" as the excuse.[13] The account likens this to Netanyahu's backing out of the Soleimani strike the day before it occurred in January 2020.[13] Trump refused to let the deal fall through and pressed Netanyahu until he relented.[13]

## Aftermath

Badlands commentary notes that after the 2020 US election, Netanyahu was the first foreign leader to call [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and congratulate him — a move that deteriorated his relationship with Trump and prompted years of subsequent efforts by Netanyahu's allies to repair the rift.[13]

## Invoked during the April 2024 escalation

As [[israel|Israel]] prepared for possible Iranian retaliation and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said the country was readying for "scenarios in other areas" beyond Gaza, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] asked "What happened to the Abraham Accords?" and whether "the Sons of Abraham" would unite against wider war.[14]

## Saudi Accession Push (May 2025)

At the [[saudi-us-investment-forum|Saudi-US Investment Forum]] in Riyadh on May 13, 2025, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said it was his "fervent hope" that Saudi Arabia would soon sign its own normalization agreement with Israel and join the Accords, while conceding, "But you'll do it in your own time."[15] He told the audience, "It will be a special day in the Middle East, with the whole world watching, when Saudi Arabia joins us, and you'll be greatly honoring me and you'll be greatly honoring all of those people that have fought so hard for the Middle East."[15] Observers noted that while arms sales and tech investment deals were likely to be concluded during the [[2025-trump-middle-east-tour|Middle East tour]], Saudi accession to the Accords remained a tougher sell.[15]

Badlands hosts treated the moment as vindication of a long-running prediction: [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that CannCon's newsletter and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] had raised Saudi accession at least weekly since before the election, and that [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] had gamed out the same outcome for years.[15] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry offered his own label for the emerging arrangement — "Welcome to the JACOB Accords. (Just A Couple Of Bros)" — and argued that "the Old Guard is done."[15]

## Interpretation as a counterinsurgency instrument (2024)

Writing in the July 2, 2024 Badlands News Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] restated his view that the Accords were wielded as a political weapon intended to ignite a peaceful counterinsurgency across the region and within Israel against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the Israeli Deep State, and argued that this was visibly happening.[16] In his account the Accords got the Arab states "all playing the same sheet of music" after decades of infighting over what to do about Israel and perceived Zionist aggression.[16] He cited as evidence Arab states working to keep Israel well-provisioned while Iran-linked proxy militias attacked Red Sea shipping, Arab participation in shielding Israel from Iran's missile barrage, and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] issuing specific advance warnings about which targets it would strike if the IDF invaded Lebanon — behavior he called a bizarre strategy for anyone actually intending to fight.[16] He credited Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] with running the diplomacy, working every channel and speaking daily with members of the so-called Sovereign Alliance including Iran, China, and Russia, and predicted that peace would break out but only after further heat.[16]

## Cited as at risk from West Bank annexation (September 2025)

Ahead of a September 2025 meeting between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and leaders of eight Arab countries at the United Nations, Arab leaders reportedly sought to stress that any Israeli move into the West Bank could jeopardize the Abraham Accords, described as Trump's signature foreign policy achievement of his first term.[17] Trump used the meeting to assure them he would not allow [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to annex the territory.[17]

## Trump's November 2025 remarks

In a Friday interview with [[fox-news|Fox]] in November 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described broader regional shifts in the Middle East, commenting that the list of those interested in signing onto the historic Abraham Accords normalization program with [[israel|Israel]] "keeps growing."[18] He hailed what he called an unprecedented opportunity for peace, noting that the crisis with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon was abating and that [[iran|Iran]] now wanted a nuclear deal.[18]

## Invoked after the April 2024 Iranian attack on Israel

Badlands commentary on the April 2024 Iranian attack held that the Arab nations — principally the Hashemites of [[jordan|Jordan]] — defended [[israel|Israel]] from the barrage in affirmation of the terms of the Accords, while [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], not yet an official member, put out mixed messaging on its level of involvement.[19] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that because the Accords are about bringing peace to the region, and peace cannot hold with missiles flying overhead, the "Peacemakers of the Middle East" would swat down missiles travelling in either direction — meaning the same Arab states that shielded Israel from [[iran|Iran]] might turn around and shield Iran from Israel should [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] retaliate.[19] He described the House of Saud and the Hashemites cooperating to preserve that fragile peace as poetic, given that the Saudis had driven the Hashemites from Mecca and taken their Hejaz Kingdom along the Red Sea 99 years earlier under British influence.[19]

## Alleged Israeli attempt to withdraw

Badlands' July 2024 commentary alleged that the day before the Abraham Accords were scheduled to be signed in 2020, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] dispatched ambassador [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] to inform the White House that Israel would be backing out of the deal.[19] According to the account, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] telephoned Netanyahu directly and pressured him into proceeding, after which the Israelis signed.[19] The episode was presented as part of an alleged pattern of Netanyahu subverting U.S.-led Middle East peace efforts.[19]

## Alleged Deep State repurposing of the Accords

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry recalled speculating two years earlier that the Accords may not have been designed to protect Israel from the rest of the Middle East, but rather to protect the rest of the Middle East from Israeli expansionism.[20] Addressing the recurring question of why so many Deep State figures support an initiative spearheaded by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the analysis argued that the Deep State saw the Accords as an opportunity to unite the Sunni Islamic world against the Shia Islamic world, with the Persian Gulf as the geographic dividing axis.[20]

On this account Shia Islam comprises only 10–15% of Muslims worldwide, concentrated in Iran and surrounding areas including half of Iraq, parts of Syria and Yemen, most of Lebanon, and pockets of Turkey and Saudi Arabia.[20] The alleged plan — traced to [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s [[grand-chessboard|The Grand Chessboard]] — was to build Iran into a legitimate military power, rally the Sunni Arab states around Israel, escalate Israel–Iran tensions into kinetic conflict, and draw in the United States, NATO, and eventually Russia, producing a world war.[20] The commentary credits [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Trump with derailing that design, and holds that the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] will not get the Sunni–Shia war it had forecast.[20]
