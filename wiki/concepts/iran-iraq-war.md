# Iran-Iraq War

The Iran-Iraq War is described in Badlands commentary as running from 1980 until 1984 and as arguably the most gruesome war of the twentieth century, employing the same mass-slaughter tactics that had characterized the First World War.[1]

## Chemical weapons dimension

Antiwar historian [[scott-horton|Scott Horton]], in an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] summarized by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], said that in 1980 the United States sold illegal chemical weapons to [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] and encouraged him to use them against Iran during the conflict.[1] Those same weapons were later cited by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and the [[cia|CIA]] as justification for the 2003 invasion of Iraq and Saddam's overthrow, on the grounds that they were banned under the [[chemical-weapons-convention|Chemical Weapons Convention]].[1]

## Funded on both sides through BCCI

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" states that the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]] was used to fund both sides of the Iran/Iraq war, along with [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]], the drug cartels and the Afghan [[mujahideen|Mujahideen]].[2]

The article situates this within a broader pattern: the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] had been sponsoring terrorism throughout the Middle East since [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] cut a deal to sell weapons to Iran, and having funded both belligerents in the Iran-Iraq conflict, it turned to using BCCI to arm the mujahideen in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] — with all of the resulting unrest directed through [[pakistan|Pakistan]].[2]
