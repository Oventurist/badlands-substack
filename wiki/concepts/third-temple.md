# Third Temple

The **Third Temple** refers to a prospective rebuilt Jewish Temple in Jerusalem, successor to the Second Temple destroyed by Roman forces in 70 AD. Efforts and expectations surrounding its construction form a central theme in Badlands commentary on the religious dimensions of the Middle East conflict.[1]

## Badlands framing

Following [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s March 2026 claim that the [[chabad-lubavitch|Chabad-Lubavitch]] movement was a "hidden hand" behind the Iran war and was pushing for a broader religious war connected to rebuilding the Temple, Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry said he had been writing and speaking about the Third Temple effort for nearly three years and welcomed the topic reaching a mass audience.[1]

His theological argument holds that Jesus himself brought about the destruction of the Second Temple when he condemned Jerusalem and the Sanhedrin for rejecting him as Messiah, calling them the "Synagogue of Satan" and vowing that every stone would be torn down — a prophecy fulfilled by the Romans in 70 AD.[1] From this standpoint there is no need to rebuild the Temple, because Jesus Christ *is* the Third Temple and the fulfillment of prophecy.[1]

## Christian Zionism and the Mashiach

The commentary criticizes a large contingent of [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionists]] who support rebuilding, some believing it will precipitate mass Jewish conversion to Christ.[1] Rabbi [[yosef-mizrachi|Yosef Mizrachi]] is cited mocking that expectation.[1] In the Badlands reading, the Temple in the eyes of its Jewish proponents is intended for the Mashiach — described not as a divine being but as a mortal man who will rule the world and fill it with God's knowledge, imagery the author associates with Eden and the Tree of Knowledge and identifies with the [[antichrist|Antichrist]].[1]

The piece closes by pointing to figures such as [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]] of [[lehava|Lehava]] — arrested in 2015 for calling for the expulsion of Christians and burning of churches — and asks whether Christians understand with whom they are joining forces in Jerusalem.[1]
