# Knesset Ritual Abuse Testimony (2025)

In June 2025, a special joint session of the [[knesset|Knesset]] heard testimony from survivors describing a network of childhood sexual abuse carried out during religious ceremonies in Israel.[1]

## Testimony

Several women, mainly from the ultra-Orthodox and religious Zionist communities, came forward to describe years of childhood abuse suffered during "sadistic sexual rituals" performed in religious ceremonies.[1] Their accounts also indicated repeated neglect by the authorities, and formed part of a broader pattern documented by journalists, trauma experts and NGO leaders.[1] "You have no idea what ritual abuse is," survivor Yael Shitrit told committee members. "The human brain cannot process it. You cannot imagine what it is like to program a 3-year-old girl through rape and sadism so that they can do whatever they want without anyone knowing."[1]

A number of members of the Knesset appeared visibly shaken.[1] Likud MK Eli Dalal said, "This is horrifying. The time has come to move from listening to action," calling for immediate steps by the police, the relevant government ministries and the justice system, and adding, "There are helpless children here, and we need to take initiative now."[1] MK Naama Lazimi of the Democrats party, who chairs the Youth Affairs Committee, said she "couldn't breathe" upon learning of a network of ritual abuse of girls and that no action was being taken to stop it.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that the ultra-Orthodox community — the constituency [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had turned to for political support in his hour of desperation — "has some real skeletons," and that the testimony demonstrated that even the most pious corners of the holy land can harbor demons.[1] He argued that the Republican Party's timidity in criticizing anything related to the government or society of Israel is an effect of idolatry, and that placing human beings on a pedestal and treating them as deities — which is, he wrote, precisely how the American Evangelical community treats the nation of Israel — will eventually lead to acts of evil.[1]
