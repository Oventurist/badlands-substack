# Wagner Group Mutiny

The Wagner Group mutiny was the short-lived June 2023 armed revolt launched by [[wagner-group|Wagner]] chief [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]] against the Russian military leadership.[1]

## Course of the revolt

The episode lasted slightly less than twenty-four hours, beginning with Prigozhin's seizure of a Russian military command headquarters and a tank column's run toward Moscow on the Friday and Saturday of 23–24 June 2023, and ending with the abrupt recall of his fighter columns to their bases.[2] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] described the affair as "entirely bizarre" and noted that the terms of the resulting truce made it stranger still.[2] The revolt concluded with no significant bloodshed and, in ZeroHedge's assessment, no further immediate danger of civil conflict in Russia.[2]

## Terms of the truce

The [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]], citing Kremlin statements, reported that under the agreement Prigozhin would leave Russia for Belarus and that criminal charges against him would be dropped, according to [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]].[2] Russian state broadcaster [[rt|RT]] added, on Peskov's account, that Wagner fighters would not be persecuted in light of their service on the front lines of the Ukraine conflict, and that Putin's team "have always respected their exploits."[2] Contractors who had refused to take part in the mutiny — and, Peskov stressed, whole units had refused — were to be permitted to sign contracts with the [[russian-ministry-of-defense|Russian Defense Ministry]].[2] Fighters who did participate faced no charges, and no changes to Defense Ministry leadership were announced.[2]

ZeroHedge questioned whether the outcome could meaningfully be called "exile," observing that Kremlin statements avoided the punitive word altogether and that harsher punishments were routinely handed out in Russia for offences as minor as possession of cannabis vape pens.[2]

## Aftermath

One month after the failed mutiny, Prigozhin was photographed at a [[russia-africa-summit-2023|Russia-Africa summit]] in St. Petersburg hosted by President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], greeting visiting African officials on the sidelines of the event.[1] His public reappearance undercut Western expectations that he had been removed or eliminated following the revolt.[1]

## Military deception interpretation

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the "mutiny" was best understood as military deception (MILDEC): Prigozhin and Putin had, in his account, publicly engaged in very obvious MILDEC just one month prior during the defense of Bakhmut, a reading circulated across [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] for weeks beforehand.[1] He drew a parallel to the perennial mainstream claim that "the walls are closing in" on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], suggesting that audiences whipped into a frenzy about Putin's imminent downfall should check their underlying premises.[1]

In a longer treatment published immediately after the revolt, Burning Bright framed the events as either "the shortest-lived and strangest massive coup attempt against one of the world's superpowers in the history of the world, or a classic case of MILDEC."[2] He pointed to the episode three weeks earlier in which Wagner had threatened to abandon Bakhmut and publicly decried Kremlin military leadership while claiming to be losing ground to the [[azov-battalion|Azov]] forces; commentators at the time read this as Russian maneuver defense designed to bait an unwise Ukrainian counter-offensive into Bakhmut, which is what followed, and Putin subsequently commended the contractor unit for its effectiveness.[2] Burning Bright judged it implausible that the same undermanned and underarmed force could weeks later muster a march on Moscow and then abandon it within a day with zero bloodshed.[2]

He listed four advantages the deception would have yielded: it ensnared the attention of what he called the Media Industrial Complex, caught out after going "all in" on the walls-closing-in narrative about Putin; it exposed [[antony-blinken|Blinken]], the [[state-department|US State Department]] and the [[g7|G-7]] defense ministers as waiting to see whether the Kremlin would fall and monitoring for "opportunities" along the Ukrainian line; it delivered Putin the most effective loyalty test he could have hoped for, with Wagner leadership largely unpunished though "some" would be; and it drew Azov into more precarious positions in the Donbas while Putin-aligned Chechen forces moved much closer to the Donbas under the pretext of responding to Wagner.[2] "In other words, we're watching a movie, and sometimes that movie has subtitles," he concluded.[2]

## US foreknowledge

The [[washington-post|Washington Post]] reported that US spy agencies picked up intelligence in mid-June 2023 indicating that Prigozhin was planning armed action against the Russian defense establishment, which he had long accused of bungling the war in Ukraine, and urgently informed the [[white-house|White House]] and other agencies so they would not be caught off guard.[2] Officials speaking anonymously said the exact nature and timing of the plans were unclear until shortly before the command-center takeover, but that "there were enough signals to be able to tell the leadership ... that something was up."[2]

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that while no concrete reporting linked the $6.2 billion in newly "discovered" Ukrainian aid funds to the Wagner episode, the foreknowledge reporting made the theory plausible: the US government knew a coup was coming, briefed [[congress|Congress]], worried about Russia's response and potential nuclear fallout, and days before Wagner advanced "found" an extra $6.2 billion for Ukraine.[2] She called the "$6.2B coup" theory one of the least silly of the weekend, contrasting it with a twenty-four-hour Twitter Space hosted by [[mario-nawfal|Mario Nawfal]] in which, she wrote, a stage full of neocons quoted alleged Wagner-run [[telegram|Telegram]] chats and Nawfal himself predicted around 11am ET that "within the next few hours, we could see the Kremlin fall."[2] Dubbing the episode "the coup d'nah," she argued that warmongering neocons had embarrassed themselves and could not hide their disappointment when Wagner turned around.[2]

## Putin's address to the nation

Ahead of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s speech on the evening of Monday, June 26, 2023, the Kremlin touted several "major" announcements, but the short address largely reiterated prior Kremlin statements and did not appear especially tough.[3] Putin said "traitors will face justice" and that "we defeated a colossal threat," while simultaneously extending an olive branch: "we knew and know that the vast majority of the fighters and commanders of the [[wagner-group|Wagner]] group are also patriots of Russia, devoted to their people and the state."[3] He offered mutineers the option of signing contracts with the Ministry of Defense or other law enforcement agencies, returning to their families, or going to [[belarus|Belarus]], adding, "the choice is yours, but I am sure it will be the choice of Russian soldiers who have realized their tragic mistake."[3]

Even so, the [[federal-security-service|FSB]] and the Russian Prosecutor's Office made statements implying that the treason case against [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]] — presumed by then to be in Belarus — remained open.[3] Putin claimed the mutiny had been "resolutely rejected by society," said he had directly ordered that "steps were taken to avoid a lot of bloodshed," praised Russian pilots apparently shot down by Wagner mercenaries for having "saved Russia from tragic devastating consequences," and thanked the military personnel, law enforcement officers, and special services who stood in the rebels' way.[3] Observers described him as looking very serious and angry throughout.[3]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Putin's remarks did nothing to undermine the view held in the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] that the Wagner "coup" was staged, and that its net effects favored the Kremlin: it put the Western media through the narrative wringer, provoked Ukrainian forces into disadvantageous positions, and exposed who inside and outside Putin's chain of command was disloyal, as well as the extent of backing from Russia's supposed Eurasian allies.[3]

He further noted that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had been forced to stress that the United States was "not involved," while Putin charged that the West "wanted Russians to kill each other."[3] To what Burning Bright called the Collective Mind, the American president was being made to deny that he tried to foment rebellion against a nuclear superpower; Putin, he argued, was forwarding rhetoric parallel to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s, with both framing Biden as a warmonger and themselves as peacemakers.[3]
