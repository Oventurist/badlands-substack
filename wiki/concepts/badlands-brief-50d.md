# Badlands Brief: Alternative Annexations & New(ish) Regime Change

"Alternative Annexations & New(ish) Regime Change" is an edition of the *Badlands Brief*, the daily commentary digest published by Badlands Media on Substack, dated March 24, 2026. Like other editions, it pairs excerpted mainstream news summaries with interpretive commentary from Badlands contributors — in this instance [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The edition covers five main items and a bonus item: a mass of undisclosed FBI records surfaced through Freedom of Information Act litigation, contradictory claims about United States–Iran peace talks, rising American gasoline prices, an Israeli cabinet minister's call to annex southern Lebanon, and electoral gains by Germany's Alternative for Germany party, closing with reporting that the United States may be preparing regime change in Cuba.

## FBI records discovered in a secured room

The lead item reports that the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] identified as many as 1.9 million pages of records in response to a [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]] lawsuit, materials reportedly stored inside a secret room at FBI headquarters. According to a filing by the [[department-of-justice|US Department of Justice]], the documents are housed in a [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility]] (SCIF) where multiple safes contain between roughly 950,000 and 1.9 million pages. The FBI stated that the records must be digitized and reviewed before it can determine what is responsive to the request, a process expected to take ten to twelve months, with classification review likely required given the nature of the storage.

The filing indicates that the materials include legacy records tied largely to two closed investigations, along with more recent files connected to active probes and prior Special Counsel matters. Officials noted that many of the legacy records fall under the authority of the Department of Justice Office of Inspector General and must be reviewed in consultation with that office before any release, while other documents may require coordination with additional federal agencies. The August 2025 lawsuit stems from a June FOIA request the FBI failed to fulfill. The case also seeks internal FBI communications involving Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and former Deputy Director [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] regarding the discovery of the records, along with directives on how the materials were handled.

Ashe in America responds with sarcasm — "How many secret rooms are there at the FBI?" — noting that apparently no one thought to check the safes in the secret SCIF before fulfilling FOIA requests. Her key takeaway is that the Bureau "bought themselves another year of obstruction," and she contrasts this with the treatment an ordinary citizen would receive for telling the government it needed another year to redact material it did not want disclosed: "You'd go to prison." She predicts the agency will get the additional time, keep its secrets, and that the truth may surface only decades later, "long after accountability is possible." She closes by quoting the line "For too long, a small group in our nation's capital has reaped the rewards of government while the people have borne the cost," and asking when that will change.

## Contradictory accounts of US–Iran talks

The second item reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States is engaged in talks with Iran to end the ongoing conflict, citing recent "productive" discussions and delaying planned strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure for five days. Iran immediately denied that any negotiations were taking place, with officials calling the claims false and accusing Washington of attempting to influence markets. Despite the conflicting accounts, military operations continued, and the war significantly disrupted global oil flows through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. Markets reacted to Trump's comments with oil prices dropping and stocks rising on expectations of de-escalation, while regional actors pursued indirect diplomatic efforts.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the episode as "Trump-a-Mania," noting that when asked about opening the Strait of Hormuz, Trump suggested that he and the Ayatollah might control it together — after which the Iranian government posted a picture of a child's toy steering wheel alongside Trump's quote and emojis. He calls it a "strange war" that has left observers "discombobulated," and argues that analysts are beginning to notice a pattern in how Trump handles ostensibly hostile nations, a dynamic he credits Burning Bright with identifying earlier. He points to an Associate Editor at the Financial Times reaching a similar conclusion.

## Gasoline prices and the energy realignment thesis

The third item reports that US gasoline prices are approaching a "psychological threshold" of around $4 per gallon, the level at which consumer driving demand historically begins to decline. Fuel prices have risen since the beginning of the joint military conflict with Iran, which increased supply risk in energy markets. US oil executives are engaging with the Trump administration to advocate for policies favorable to domestic production and energy stability, and Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] has called for increased domestic output to offset global supply disruptions. Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] instructed major energy companies to use increased revenues from higher oil prices to pay down debt, describing it as the mature choice; higher prices tied to the conflict are raising Russian revenues while pressuring consumer fuel costs globally. Analysts note that sustained high prices could reduce US fuel demand and alter consumer behavior.

Burning Bright's commentary treats the price surge as a deliberate inflection point. He observes that gasoline and diesel have seen one of the sharpest monthly surges in decades, pushing the national average within a few cents of the $4 barrier, and that the short-term "release valve" is being presented as a straightforward return to Trump's campaign promise of "Drill, Baby, Drill." He notes Wright's appearance in Houston at [[ceraweek|CERAWeek]] urging oil and gas leaders to increase production immediately, and direct [[white-house|White House]] meetings with executives and diplomats to coordinate higher domestic output amid the Iran tensions. In his reading, the "Collective Mind" is being primed to accept expanded production as necessary, handing Trump fresh political capital.

That mandate, he argues, is only the first stage. The "true logical direction on the back of the cutting of the Iranian Knot" is sovereign energy independence and rapid acceleration of nuclear power that will anchor what he calls the Age of Abundance. The dynamics in the Strait of Hormuz, he contends, are designed not to deepen foreign dependence but to break it, creating conditions for "clean disentanglement from Middle Eastern energy leverage." As supporting signal he cites the unusual caution of American oil executives, who by his account should be celebrating $100-plus oil and expanding margins yet instead spoke in Houston about longer-term volatility — because, he suggests, they recognize that the Hormuz situation is forcing permanent sovereign disentanglement of energy flows and a multipolar realignment in which no nation remains vulnerable to single chokepoints, ultimately squeezing global oil conglomerate margins.

He identifies the strongest "Sovereign Signal" in the fact that one of the biggest beneficiaries of the worldwide price surge has been the Russian Federation. Having written the previous week about Trump's sanctions relief on trapped Russian energy, he notes that Putin is now directing Russian energy companies to apply windfall profits to domestic bank loans. Trump's "supposed Iran War," he concludes, is delivering billions in additional revenue to Moscow while Europe and Ukraine watch their American partner inadvertently strengthen the very power they have tried to weaken — ending with the wry question, "Ever the pragmatist, that Putin. Or was it Trump?"

## Call to annex southern Lebanon

The fourth item reports that Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] called for Israel to annex parts of southern Lebanon, saying the territory should be permanently held as a security measure amid ongoing fighting with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]. His remarks came as cross-border violence intensified, though the Israeli government has not formally adopted annexation as policy. The brief notes the proposal is likely to draw international opposition, since annexation of territory by force is widely considered illegal under international law and risks further escalating an already volatile regional conflict.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argues that "just as President Trump is stepping off the rug and exiting the stage, the Israelis are hitting their stride and escalating to the next phase," citing [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] confirming that while the United States negotiates with Iran, Israel will continue to bomb both Iran and Lebanon. He notes that Smotrich's call would extend Israel's northern border to the [[litani-river|Litani River]], bringing the highlighted territory into "[Greater] Israel," and that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] has bombed seven bridges crossing the Litani, cutting off access from Beirut and central Lebanon. He describes the resulting Lebanese narrative as one of the most consequential "since the War of Terror psyop began decades ago," warning that what the adversary "can't have is the two largest demographics in the world — Christians and Muslims — unite against a common threat." He adds a postscript asking whether CNN is becoming "more based than Fox News."

## AfD gains and the establishment firewall

The fifth item reports that Germany's [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD) party posted gains in recent state elections, particularly in eastern regions, while remaining excluded from governing coalitions as mainstream parties enforce a longstanding refusal to cooperate with it. Domestic intelligence authorities have classified the AfD as "right-wing extremists," and mainstream German parties have maintained a "firewall" policy refusing coalitions or governing alliances with the party despite its growing popularity. Even so, its vote share continues to rise.

Ashe in America opens by noting that the previous day was the anniversary of [[benito-mussolini|Benito Mussolini]] founding the Fascist Party in Italy, quoting his remark that "Fascism should more properly be called corporatism, since it is the marriage of government and corporate power," and asking how that differs from the current global order — answering, "It's not." She argues that labeling the opposition as radical is effective "until it isn't," comparing the AfD story to Joe Biden's "red sermon," which in her account single-handedly increased MAGA credibility and was followed by an increase in MAGA's vote share. She contends that whether the AfD is genuinely radical is irrelevant, adding that "elections are fake all over the world," and that the operative story is that attacks on the party are not neutralizing its popularity. "Far right," she concludes, is "the globalists' pejorative for nationalists — and nationalism is more popular than ever, around the world, as the little people wake up to the fact that the globalists are exploiting all of us for the benefit of the few." She closes with a one-word exhortation: "Accelerate."

## Bonus item: possible regime change in Cuba

The bonus item reports that a Russian oil tanker, the *Anatoly Kolodkin*, is approaching the Caribbean carrying crude oil to [[cuba|Cuba]] as the [[united-states-navy|US Navy]] increases efforts to restrict fuel shipments to the island. Cuba is facing a deepening economic crisis marked by widespread blackouts, shortages, and migration, while its government has acknowledged ongoing discussions with the United States over potential reforms. About 10 percent of Cuba's population has left the country since COVID-19. According to [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]], officials familiar with internal planning say the United States is considering legal and strategic measures that could escalate pressure on Cuban leadership, including possible indictments and broader contingency plans. Anonymous officials told the outlet that regime change in Cuba is "lined up."
