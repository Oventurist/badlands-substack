# First Impeachment of Donald Trump

The first impeachment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arose from a 2019 whistleblower complaint alleging that Trump had hijacked U.S. [[ukraine|Ukraine]] policy for political gain. Declassified [[intelligence-community-inspector-general|Intelligence Community Inspector General]] memos released in 2026 reopened questions about the complaint's origins and the credibility of its author.[1]

## The whistleblower

The August 2019 memos, declassified by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] at the request of [[just-the-news|Just the News]], record that the whistleblower acknowledged potential perceptions of political bias, including prior work with then–Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and registration as a Democrat.[1] He told investigators he had worked closely with Biden on Ukraine policy and had traveled with him to Ukraine.[1] The complaint rested on secondhand information rather than direct knowledge of the events described, yet the inspector general assessed that it "appears credible."[1]

A second official, "Witness 2," reviewed the Trump–Ukraine call transcript for situational awareness and assisted the whistleblower out of a stated "moral and patriotic duty"; the memo notes this official had previously worked with former [[fbi|FBI]] agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]].[1]

The whistleblower's name and face were never shown to the public, and his written complaint was heralded by Democrats to launch impeachment proceedings.[1]

## Reception in Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the underlying testimony as fraudulent and framed the impeachment as one phase of a continuous operation against Trump that he traced from RussiaGate in 2016 through the 2020 election and COVID period.[1] He argued that both prison sentences and executions for treason should be pursued through due process, and that the episode justifies restructuring or disbanding intelligence agencies implicated in it.[1]


## 2026 ODNI declassification

On 14 April 2026 the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], under Director [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], released documents it said exposed a coordinated effort by elements within the Intelligence Community to manufacture the conspiracy that served as the basis for the impeachment.[2] ODNI stated that former IC Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]], investigating [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s July 2019 call with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, departed from standard inspector-general procedure and interviewed only four people: the whistleblower; the whistleblower's friend, a co-author of the January 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] and colleague of former [[fbi|FBI]] agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]]; and two character references with no firsthand knowledge of the call.[2]

ODNI further alleged that Atkinson exceeded his statutory jurisdiction by ignoring Department of Justice guidance and relying on second-hand testimony to ensure the complaint reached [[congress|Congress]], the FBI, and the press, after which [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|HPSCI]] Chairman [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] used the narrative to launch impeachment proceedings in December 2019.[2] Gabbard said the whistleblower process had been politicized by a former [[cia|CIA]] employee "working hand in glove with Democrats in Congress."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described watching the impeachment in real time and concluding that its architects "have to know this is bullshit," writing that the declassifications more than six years later confirmed the hoaxes were intended to thwart the President's agenda "to the benefit, at least in part, of foreign nations and interests" — behavior she called treason.[2]

## OCCRP funding revelation

In December 2024 it emerged that the [[organized-crime-and-corruption-reporting-project|Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project]], an international NGO of journalists in six countries described as intimately involved in the first Democrat-backed impeachment, received 52 percent of the funds it spent between 2014 and 2023 from the U.S. federal government, chiefly the [[state-department|Department of State]].[3] U.S. taxpayer dollars likely contributed to OCCRP's investigation into former New York Mayor [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]]'s political work in Ukraine, activities cited no fewer than four times in the whistleblower letter that sparked the impeachment in 2019.[3] Trump was ultimately acquitted of the charges by the U.S. Senate.[3]

## 2024 anniversary and the second Trump-Zelensky call

When [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] spoke by phone on July 19, 2024, Politico noted that the conversation came just a few days shy of the five-year anniversary of the call between the two leaders that led to Trump's first impeachment, after he asked Zelensky to consider investigating [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on what the outlet described as discredited allegations.[4] Unlike that earlier exchange, both sides said they were satisfied with the 2024 call.[4]

## The 2023 Just the News memos

In August 2023, [[just-the-news|Just the News]] reported on U.S. government memos that it said undercut the Biden–Ukraine narrative Democrats had sold during the 2019 impeachment scandal.[5] An Interagency Policy Committee memo dated October 1, 2015 recorded that "Ukraine has made sufficient progress on its reform agenda to justify a third guarantee," contradicting the claim that then–Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s threat weeks later to withhold a $1 billion loan guarantee unless Prosecutor General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] was fired reflected official U.S. policy.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] drew several conclusions from the trove: that [[burisma|Burisma]] used [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] to pressure his father into removing Shokin and that U.S. officials in Kyiv warned Ambassador [[marie-yovanovitch|Marie Yovanovitch]] that Hunter had "impacted the U.S. anti-corruption agenda in Ukraine"; that Yovanovitch and State Department official [[george-kent|George Kent]] lied under oath during the first House impeachment inquiry; that exculpatory evidence was withheld from [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s defense team during the inquiry and the subsequent Senate trial; and that the [[state-department|State Department]] and the broader federal establishment had covered the matter up for years.[5]

## The "walk through the front door" thesis

In "Setting the Stage," Joe Lange presents the first impeachment as a hastily improvised fallback after the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller investigation]] failed to produce the collusion evidence intended to justify removal.[6] [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] coordinated the effort, recruiting the whistleblower — reported to be [[eric-ciaramella|Eric Ciaramella]] — and helping him file the complaint, then using the protection of the whistleblower's identity to force testimony behind closed doors while telling the media the evidence was overwhelming.[6]

The complaint rested on the call between [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], though Ciaramella, a CIA analyst, was not on the call and lacked the clearance to access it, meaning whoever gave him its contents was illegally disclosing classified information.[6] Red State's review of the released transcripts indicated the source was [[alexander-vindman|Alexander Vindman]], after Schiff and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] blocked [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] from asking Vindman whom outside his chain of command he had spoken to.[6] Other testimony proved thinner than advertised: Bill Taylor's supposed confirmation of a quid pro quo rested on fourth-hand assumption and a New York Times report, and Gordon Sondland swore he was relying on presumption rather than any direct order.[6]

Lange argues Trump knew there was no evidence and deliberately let the enemy "walk through the front door," waiting until they had gone all in before declassifying the full transcript of the call, which showed no crime and led the Senate to acquit him quickly.[6] On this reading the impeachment was itself a trap that captured additional participants in what Lange characterises as treason.[6]
