# Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act (RICO)

The Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act, commonly known as RICO, was signed into law in 1970 by President Richard Nixon and is the primary vehicle through which organized crime is prosecuted in the United States.[1] It supports both criminal prosecution and civil suits by private plaintiffs.[1]

## Trump's civil RICO suit against Hillary Clinton

In September 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] moved to revive a civil RICO case against [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] before a federal appeals court.[1] On Friday, September 20, a federal judge gave Trump until September 27 to file a brief of up to 10,000 words explaining why the case should be heard.[1] The suit alleged that Clinton and more than two dozen other defendants attempted to rig the 2016 election against him; Trump sought over $70 million in compensation, claiming he had spent more than $24 million defending himself against false accusations of Russian collusion.[1]

## Badlands Media analysis

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that RICO had come into unusually sharp public focus, citing matters involving [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], Sean "P. Diddy" Combs and New York City Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]], in addition to Fulton County District Attorney Fani Willis's faltering RICO case in Georgia against Trump and others.[1] She reasoned that if government actors were engaged in organized crime, RICO would be the natural legal mechanism for accountability.[1]

She emphasized that in 2016 Clinton was not only the Democratic nominee for the presidency but had earlier served as United States Secretary of State, with the power and resources of that office — accompanied by a published correction noting that a prior brief had wrongly placed Clinton at the [[state-department|State Department]] during Obama's second term, when the officeholder was in fact John Kerry.[1] She also noted that the appeals court could have declined to hear the case outright but instead permitted Trump to file, calling that procedural choice notable.[1] Finally, she argued that widely followed pop-culture RICO cases were training a general audience to process political organized-crime charges, suggesting the symmetry was "by design."[1]


## Use Against Tren de Aragua (2025)

In April 2025 the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] said its two New York indictments charging 27 alleged [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] and Anti-Tren associates were the first brought against the Venezuelan gang under RICO.[2]

## Invocation in Badlands "Biden, Inc." commentary

A December 2024 Badlands feature argued that the Biden family's alleged activities were not isolated incidents but a broader criminal enterprise violating the RICO statutes.[3] The connections it enumerated were [[burisma|Burisma Holdings]] as a money-laundering conduit through Ukrainian energy deals, [[ftx|FTX]] as a political slush fund, [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] influence purchased through business dealings, and links between pharmaceutical companies and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]]'s [[national-institutes-of-health|NIH]] over gain-of-function funding and treatment suppression.[3]

The article claimed that the [[white-hats|White Hats]] are using RICO statutes to expose these connections, focusing on patterns of corruption that transcend borders and institutions, and argued that the approach is aimed at systemic change rather than prosecution alone.[3] No such racketeering case exists in the public record.[3]
