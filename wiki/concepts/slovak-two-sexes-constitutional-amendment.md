# Slovak Two-Sexes Constitutional Amendment

The Slovak two-sexes constitutional amendment is a change to [[slovakia|Slovakia]]'s constitution, passed by its parliament on Friday 26 September 2025, recognising only two sexes — male and female — and restricting adoption rights to married couples.[1]

## Provisions

Beyond its definition of sex and its adoption restriction, the amendment guarantees equal pay for men and women and requires parental consent for sexual education.[1]

## Passage

The measure was introduced by the government of Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] and passed with 90 votes in the 150-seat National Council, clearing the three-fifths majority the constitution required.[1] Twelve opposition lawmakers from right-leaning parties supported it.[1] Justice Minister Boris Susko stated that the amendment aims to strengthen traditional values.[1]

## Opposition

Leftist human rights organisations condemned the move, with [[amnesty-international|Amnesty International]] declaring that "Today, the Slovak government chose to follow the lead of countries, such as Hungary, whose policies have led to an erosion of human rights."[1] The [[council-of-europe|Council of Europe]]'s Commissioner for Human Rights, Michael O'Flaherty, also criticised the amendment on the grounds that it denies "the realities of trans and intersex people" and could violate Slovakia's international obligations, raising particular concern about access to legal gender recognition.[1]
