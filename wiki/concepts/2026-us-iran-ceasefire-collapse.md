# 2026 US–Iran Ceasefire Collapse

The 2026 US–Iran ceasefire collapse refers to the breakdown in July 2026 of the interim ceasefire agreement between the United States and [[iran|Iran]], following Iranian attacks on commercial shipping in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

## Collapse and escalation

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said in July 2026 that the ceasefire was "over" after Iranian attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz and subsequent US military strikes.[1] Speaking at the [[2026-ankara-nato-summit|NATO summit in Ankara]], Trump said the United States had already carried out a new wave of strikes and warned that additional military action was likely if Iran continued its attacks.[1]

The escalation saw the United States strike more than 80 targets inside Iran and revoke a license permitting Iranian oil sales.[1] Iran responded with missile and drone attacks targeting US facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait.[1] Global oil prices climbed roughly 5% on concerns that renewed fighting could further disrupt energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.[1] As of the time of reporting no new agreement had been reached, and the administration indicated future military action would depend on Iran's next moves.[1]

## Allied response

[[nato|NATO]] Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] defended the latest US strikes as "absolutely necessary," saying Iran had violated the ceasefire while emphasizing that NATO was not a party to the conflict.[1] [[spain|Spain]] opposed the US campaign, prompting Trump to call the country "bad people" and order a halt to US trade with Spain; Prime Minister [[pedro-sanchez|Pedro Sánchez]] maintained his government's opposition.[1]

## Doha talks and the June 29 de-escalation

Following several days of renewed hostilities, the United States and [[iran|Iran]] agreed to halt further military strikes and were expected to meet in Doha, [[qatar|Qatar]], to address disputes surrounding the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], according to senior U.S. officials cited by [[axios|Axios]].[2] Both sides agreed to suspend "kinetic activity" and continue technical discussions as part of efforts to stabilize the situation.[2] The Guardian reported that differing interpretations of the earlier memorandum of understanding, particularly regarding the Strait, contributed to the escalation, and that an Iranian drone struck a residential building in Muharraq, [[bahrain|Bahrain]].[2] [[reuters|Reuters]] reported that oil prices rose on June 29 as reciprocal strikes slowed shipping, with attacks on vessels resuming, including an incident involving a Qatar-linked oil tanker.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the collapse was triggered deliberately by the tanker Ever Lovely, which departed the [[international-maritime-organization|IMO]]-brokered two-lane traffic pattern and switched off its AIS transponder before being struck; a second vessel, the Kiku of [[evalend-shipping|Evalend Shipping]], was hit days later.[2] He tied both to the sanctioned network of [[salim-ahmed-said|Salim Ahmed Said]] and suggested Iran was functioning as an enforcer for [[donald-trump|Trump]] against [[deep-state|Deep State]] efforts — including a [[marco-rubio|Rubio]]-led meeting with the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]], Bahrain and [[kuwait|Kuwait]] — to derail the [[steve-witkoff|Witkoff]]–[[jared-kushner|Kushner]] peace deal.[2]
