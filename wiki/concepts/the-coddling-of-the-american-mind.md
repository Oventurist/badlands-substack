# The Coddling of the American Mind

*The Coddling of the American Mind* is a documentary film about the effects of contemporary campus ideology on the mental health and intellectual development of college students, notable both for its content and for being distributed through [[substack|Substack]] as the platform's first feature documentary venture.[1]

## Content

The film chronicles the journeys of college students who, in the framing of sympathetic commentators, fell victim to the [[woke-mind-virus|Woke Mind Virus]] and later emerged from its grip through resilience and self-discovery.[1] Their accounts are supported by research and animated sequences, and are used to argue that the ideological pattern is pervasive rather than anecdotal.[1]

Central to the film is the concept of the "three great untruths": the belief that what doesn't kill you makes you weaker, the mandate to always trust one's feelings, and the notion that life is a battle between good and evil.[1] Professors attending screenings have reported these untruths matching what they observe in students, alongside a surge in requests for special accommodations backed by letters demanding preferential treatment.[1]

One student in the film describes how her autism was reframed by neo-Marxist ideology, recounting in detail how other autistic students indoctrinated her into the belief that people seeking to "cure autism" are engaged in an "autism genocide."[1] Commentators have held this narrative up beside the parallel claim of a "trans genocide," arguing that the staged escalation toward the language of genocide is the same in both cases.[1] All of the students profiled described being extremely depressed and unhappy while under the influence of these theories, and happier after leaving them.[1]

## Distribution and significance

The film represents Substack's entry into documentary distribution, a development treated as significant for dissident filmmaking because mainstream distributors frequently reject such work as "dangerous misinformation" or "too controversial" — as [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] reported happening with his own film [[cut-film|CUT]].[1] The film's animators, present at a Toronto screening, confirmed that Substack was taking a minimal cut of profits compared with other distributors, a fact cited as evidence of the emerging parallel economy for censorship-free distribution.[1]

## Toronto screening

A screening organised by the Toronto chapter of the [[heterodox-academy|Heterodox Academy]] was attended by Esler alongside professors, teaching assistants and students, including [[leigh-rivers|Leigh Rivers]].[1] Esler described the evening as symbolic of the local academic class beginning to recognise the scale of the problem, and argued that the students' accounts of escaping ideological capture constitute tactically valuable information on the [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] battlefield — comparable to, though less traumatic than, the testimony of [[detransitioners|detransitioners]].[1]
