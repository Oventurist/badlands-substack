# Make America Healthy Again

Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) is a public-health movement championed by the Trump administration and Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], who has long criticized substances commonly added to processed food and drinks.[1]

## State-level implementation

West Virginia Governor [[patrick-morrisey|Patrick Morrisey]] explicitly invoked the MAHA mission when signing legislation in March 2025 banning the sale of food products containing seven listed food dyes and two preservatives effective January 1, 2028, with a school-meals ban taking effect that fall.[1] Morrisey argued that West Virginia's poor ranking across public health metrics made it the natural place to lead the effort.[1]

## Related federal legislation

Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]]'s [[nutritious-snap-act|Nutritious SNAP Act of 2025]] pursued an adjacent objective, seeking to stop federal food assistance dollars from subsidizing soda and junk food on the grounds that the program had become a driver of obesity and diet-related chronic disease.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary linked the movement to a broader argument that America's uniquely high rates of chronic disease and obesity — the highest rate of multiple chronic conditions in the world and an obesity rate nearly twice the OECD average — are not accidental, and called for accountability for what it described as exploitation and poisoning of the population.[1]

## Origins in the 2024 HHS nomination

The slogan entered the mainstream with President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s November 14, 2024 announcement that [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] would be his nominee to lead the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]].[2] Trump wrote that HHS would help ensure Americans were protected from "harmful chemicals, pollutants, pesticides, pharmaceutical products, and food additives that have contributed to the overwhelming Health Crisis in this Country," and that Kennedy would restore the agencies "to the traditions of Gold Standard Scientific Research, and beacons of Transparency, to end the Chronic Disease epidemic, and to Make America Great and Healthy Again!"[2] Badlands framed the appointment as the beginning of MAHA and as an assault on one of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s most lucrative systems of power projection.[2]

## President's Make America Healthy Again Commission (February 2025)

On February 13, 2025, the [[white-house|White House]] issued a presidential action establishing the President's Make America Healthy Again Commission, published the same day the [[us-senate|Senate]] confirmed [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] as Secretary of Health and Human Services.[3]

The order opened with a statistical indictment of American health: pre-COVID-19 US life expectancy averaged 78.8 years against 82.6 years in comparable developed countries, a gap the document equated to 1.25 billion fewer life years for the US population.[3] It noted that six in ten Americans have at least one chronic disease, four in ten have two or more, and an estimated one in five US adults lives with a mental illness.[3]

The action placed particular emphasis on children's health, stating that in 2022 an estimated 30 million children (40.7 percent) had at least one health condition such as allergies, asthma, or an autoimmune disease.[3] It cited autism spectrum disorder now affecting 1 in 36 US children — a staggering increase from rates of 1 to 4 per 10,000 identified during the 1980s — along with 18 percent of late adolescents and young adults having fatty liver disease, close to 30 percent of adolescents being prediabetic, and more than 40 percent of adolescents being overweight or obese.[3] It declared it the policy of the federal government "to aggressively combat the critical health challenges facing our citizens, including the rising rates of mental health disorders, obesity, diabetes, and other chronic diseases."[3]

Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] tied the commission to Kennedy's confirmation, quoting his argument that Trump had promised to restore America's strength but that "we can't be a strong nation if we have a weak citizenry."[3]


## Autism initiative, September 2025

The September 22, 2025 [[2025-white-house-tylenol-autism-announcement|White House announcement]] on the causes of autism came as the Make America Healthy Again movement pushed for answers on the disorder.[4] AP News described the diverse coalition of supporters of Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] as including several anti-vaccine activists who have long spread debunked claims that immunizations are responsible for rising autism rates.[4]

## SNAP junk-food waivers (August 2025)

The granting of waivers to six additional states — West Virginia, Florida, Colorado, Louisiana, Oklahoma and Texas — allowing them to bar soda, candy and other high-sugar foods from purchase under [[snap|SNAP]] was reported as a win for MAHA advocates, with the restrictions set to take effect in 2026.[5] The waivers brought to twelve the number of states restricting junk-food purchases, following earlier approvals for Nebraska, Iowa, Indiana, Arkansas, Idaho and Utah.[5]

## Casey Means nomination backlash (May 2025)

The MAHA movement fractured publicly in May 2025 over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nomination of [[casey-means|Casey Means]] as Surgeon General, with many adherents objecting that Means had not completed her surgical residency and was not a practicing clinical physician.[6] Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] defended her, saying the backlash revealed "just how far off course our healthcare conversations have veered" and that she was the ideal replacement because she had left the traditional medical system rather than in spite of it.[6] Kennedy's former running mate [[nicole-shanahan|Nicole Shanahan]] went so far as to imply that Kennedy was lying or being handled.[6]

Badlands writer Ashe in America noted the irony of a movement built on rejecting the established system appealing to that system's credentialing, and restated MAHA's core problem statement as a "bundle of perverse incentives" — a business transformation project rather than a medical one, requiring the weight of the federal government to force change across multiple industry verticals against their profit motivations in pursuit of health solutions that treat the whole human.[6] On that reading, making America healthy again "doesn't need the right doctor in a bureaucratic seat," but "the right leader with a stomach for change."[6]
