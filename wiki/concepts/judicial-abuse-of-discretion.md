# Judicial Abuse of Discretion

Judicial abuse of discretion refers to a trial judge's exercise of discretionary authority — over evidence, procedure, or sentencing — in a manner so unreasonable that an appellate court will reverse. Badlands Media treats it as a recurring theme in coverage of the New York courts, arguing that discretion can compound into outright abuse of power when its net effect is manifest injustice.[1]

## The Weinstein decision as illustration

The April 25, 2024 decision of the [[new-york-court-of-appeals|New York Court of Appeals]] overturning [[harvey-weinstein|Harvey Weinstein]]'s 2020 rape conviction rested on precisely this ground: the trial judge had allowed testimony about allegations that were not part of the charged case, and the appellate court held that this unfairly prejudiced the defendant and required a new trial.[1] Badlands commentary described this as the factual foundation of the decision and as a live issue in a number of other cases then working through the New York appellate process.[1]

## The Trump parallel

The commentary extended the principle to the New York prosecutions and civil actions against [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], asserting that judges including [[lewis-kaplan|Kaplan]], [[arthur-engoron|Engoron]], and [[juan-merchan|Merchan]] had exercised broad discretion in a coordinated effort to "get Trump."[1] The argument holds that trying cases in the court of public opinion may succeed for a time — influencing the district court, tainting the jury, and even surviving at the appellate level — but that reality eventually reasserts itself, as it did seven years after the initial allegations against Weinstein and four years after his conviction.[1] The underlying principle offered is that due process matters and that the role of the court is to ensure the ends of justice are met, regardless of how much the public detests the accused.[1]
