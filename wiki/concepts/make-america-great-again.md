# Make America Great Again

"Make America Great Again" (MAGA) is the political slogan most closely associated with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the movement of supporters who wear it on red baseball caps. Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that the phrase did not originate with Trump at all: it first came about during [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s 1980 presidential campaign, in which Reagan used the slogan "Let's make America great again."[1]

## As evidence of Reagan–Trump continuity

Carlson treats the shared slogan as the first and most visible of a long list of similarities and connections between Reagan and Trump, and as a reason that supporters of one cannot coherently vilify the other.[1] In his account the display of MAGA insignia by Trump supporters elicits scorn, ridicule and disgust from liberals, but the underlying appeal is the same common-sense, anti-establishment message Reagan carried into the Oval Office in 1980.[1]

Trump's own February 6, 2025 message marking Reagan's 114th birthday made the lineage explicit, praising Reagan as "a giant of American history, a lion of freedom and liberty," crediting the Reagan Revolution with an era in which "the economy boomed, confidence soared, Communism collapsed," and framing the present moment as "America's Golden Age" that carries Reagan's torch forward.[1]

## MAGA as memetic activator

The June 26, 2025 Badlands essay "Signal Collapse" by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]] reinterpreted MAGA as something other than a campaign slogan, arguing that "the phrase wasn't the power — the frequency of memory was," and describing it as a "spellbreaking technique" and "activation code for every nation that still carries memory."[2] On that reading MAGA "was never about America alone," and its echoes were said to include [[miga|MIGA]] for Iran, MCGA for Canada, MEGA for Europe and MHGA for humanity, each treated as "a carrier signal for mythic restoration."[2] The essay associated the slogan family with four archetypes — the Sovereign, the Healer, the Warrior and the Rememberer.[2]

## As counterinsurgency political strategy

In "What's Taking So Long?" (December 28, 2022), [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] identified "Make America Great Again" as the political strategy of what he describes as an American [[counterinsurgency|counterinsurgency]] campaign, the [[counterinsurgency-guide|Counterinsurgency Guide]] holding that political strategy is the key function around which all other programs are organized.[3] He argued the slogan focused public attention on the failures of the [[barack-obama|Obama]] years and on what he called the insurgency-driven enfeeblement of the country, while placing opponents on the defensive by forcing them to campaign against popular MAGA policies and to defend the Obama-Biden record.[3]
