# 2023 US Senate Delegation Visit to Israel

The 2023 US Senate delegation visit to Israel was a bipartisan congressional trip conducted in late October 2023, weeks after the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] assault, by five Republican and five Democratic senators.[1]

## Purpose and rhetoric

The delegation had originally been planned to promote Israeli–Saudi normalization, but the trip's public messaging was dominated instead by the war in Gaza.[1] South Carolina Republican Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], who led the group, told a press conference in Tel Aviv on Sunday that "destroying Hamas is non-negotiable," and threatened to open a war front on [[iran|Iran]]'s borders if Tehran moved to broaden the conflict.[1]

Asked to define victory, Graham said: "What is winning? For the terrorist organization to be destroyed, for the people of Israel to have the sense of peace they've lost, for the Palestinian people to have hope they don't have, and for the Arabs and Israelis to end a conflict that would make the world better."[1] The senators stressed a positive postwar vision for the region — "Israel thrives, they survive, Hamas is destroyed, the Palestinian people have a better life."[1] The delegation framed the war as having been opened specifically to prevent Saudi–Israeli peace.[1]

## Meetings

The group met Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] on the Saturday of the trip and urged him to offer funds to sustain the Gazan population until Hamas was destroyed.[1] The senators also met families of hostages taken by Hamas, which killed over 1,400 people on October 7 — most of them civilians slaughtered in their homes or at a music festival near the Gaza border — and took more than 200 hostages.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor Ashe in America described the visit as part of a "reboot of the war on terror," with US senators antagonizing Iran while the [[uss-dwight-d-eisenhower-carrier-strike-group|USS Eisenhower carrier group]] was re-routed into the Persian Gulf.[1] She argued that the mission carried the outward form of normalization diplomacy but that diplomacy had ceased to be a priority after October 7, and criticized Graham — whom she nicknamed "Grambo" — as never having encountered a region where he was not keen to send Americans to die.[1] She also questioned the cost of dispatching legislators abroad "to advertise for war" while Americans struggled under "Bidenomics," concluding that weakening America was the objective.[1]
