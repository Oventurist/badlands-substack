# Benghazi Non-Disclosure Agreements

The re-signing of non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) by Benghazi survivors is presented in the article as a key instrument of the cover-up that followed the [[benghazi-attack|attack]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Fox News reported that [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] told a congressional hearing that security operators involved in Benghazi rescue efforts were required to re-sign non-disclosure agreements because the documents were being updated.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Brennan was pressed on the point by Rep. Devin Nunes of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article rejects the "updating" explanation, describing the NDAs as intimidation of the same kind as the repeated lie detector tests imposed on personnel involved in the Benghazi operation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The Weekly Standard reported that two former CIA officials who fought in Benghazi were asked to sign additional NDAs more than six months after the attacks, and that the documents were presented during a May memorial service at CIA headquarters in Langley honoring [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]] — with the two officials due to testify before a House Intelligence subcommittee that week.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article calls the setting the worst detail of the episode and notes that the officials testified anyway, helping expose lies told by the Obama administration.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) It sums up the agency's posture with the mantra "admit nothing and deny everything."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)
