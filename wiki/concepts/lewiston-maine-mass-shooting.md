# Lewiston, Maine Mass Shooting

The Lewiston, Maine mass shootings of October 2023 killed 18 people and triggered a two-day manhunt for the gunman, 40-year-old [[robert-card|Robert Card]], who was ultimately found dead of a self-inflicted gunshot wound.[1]

## Course of events

On the night of Wednesday, October 25, 2023, at least 16 people were killed and dozens more injured in multiple shootings in Lewiston, with officials warning the toll was expected to rise.[2] The attacks occurred at two locations, Sparetime Recreation and Schemengees Bar & Grille.[2] Maine State Police searched overnight for [[robert-card|Robert Card]], 40, of Bowdoin, a trained firearms instructor believed to serve in the U.S. Army Reserve out of Saco who had recently reported hearing voices and had threatened to shoot the National Guard base in Saco.[2] [[mike-sauschuck|Michael Sauschuck]], commissioner of the state Department of Public Safety, held a press conference around 11:30 p.m. at Lewiston City Hall, refused to confirm the number of deaths and injuries, and would say only that Card was a person of interest.[2] Androscoggin County Sheriff Eric Samson and a Lewiston city councilman at one point suggested the death toll could be as high as 22, though counts shifted through the night, and Sauschuck said "literally hundreds of police officers" across Maine were working the case.[2]

The Androscoggin County Sheriff's Office confirmed on Friday that Card had been found dead, with his body discovered near a recycling plant in the Lisbon area.[1] Mike Sauschuck, commissioner of the Maine Department of Public Safety, announced the death at a Friday night news conference, saying the body was located at about 7:45 p.m. local time near the Androscoggin River in Lisbon, roughly eight miles southwest of Lewiston.[1] The suspect's white Subaru Outback had earlier been found abandoned at a boat launch on the river.[1]

Officials later specified that the body was in the back of a tractor-trailer in the overflow parking lot of the Maine Recycling Corporation, a business where the gunman had worked at some point and which authorities had already searched twice without checking the overflow lot; the property owner contacted police and asked them to look.[1] The first three people to contact authorities identifying the man in released photographs were members of the gunman's family.[1]

## Contested interpretation

### Thursday manhunt and arrest warrant

By Thursday evening, October 26, 2023, the manhunt for the suspect had surpassed 24 hours, and authorities twice converged that day on Card's last known address in Bowdoin to execute search warrants.[3] The second convergence produced tense moments as officers surrounded the home and one was heard calling for the occupant to "come out with your hands up," but police left after about two hours and Maine State Police said they were done at the site for the night; a law enforcement official said investigators believed they had detected something inside or near the home.[3] Maine Department of Public Safety spokesperson Shannon Moss described the activity as due diligence in executing search warrants.[3] Governor Janet Mills said Thursday that the shooting at the bowling alley and restaurant had killed 18 people and injured 13 others, and Maine State Police Col. William Ross announced that an arrest warrant accusing Card of murder had been issued.[3]

Contemporaneous commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] held that the failure to capture the suspect within 24 hours, given the level of public surveillance in U.S. communities, was anomalous, and that Card exhibited "the classic earmarks of a mentally unstable person who was MK-Ultra'd into committing atrocities."[3] She argued that the disarmament messaging surrounding the event had been prepared from the moment the news broke and that the proposed remedies would only create more vulnerable, unarmed people, urging Americans to keep their firearms while offering prayers for those affected.[3]

Same-day commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] framed the event as another "Crisis Event" declared to terrorize the western populace, while conceding that if the facts were as reported it was a horrible tragedy unfolding inside American borders.[2] He listed three reasons the timing was "tremendously suspect": it arrived amid a resurgence of "War on Terror" narratives in the western zeitgeist; [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had warned only the previous Tuesday that the Biden administration's handling of the US border could enable fresh attacks from within; and the administration was simultaneously embattled by both left and right over its handling of events at home and abroad.[2] He urged readers to keep asking the questions that recur after every domestic terror event.[2]

She argued that every element of the official portrait served establishment interests: it supplied a "radical far right extremist" villain, gave cover to open-border advocates, chilled criticism of mass migration, and intensified disarmament demands at a time when American gun owners represented, in her framing, the greatest check on establishment power in modern history.[1] The gunman's death foreclosed questioning about his reported voices or any relationship with federal handlers.[1] She stressed that "false flags often have real victims" and that the loss of life was a genuine tragedy, while maintaining that a story so beneficial to the regime warranted critical examination.[1] Related: [[false-flag-operation]].
