# Armenta v. Deadspin

Armenta v. Deadspin is a defamation lawsuit filed in Delaware against the online sports magazine [[deadspin|Deadspin]] over an article that accused a nine-year-old boy of wearing "blackface" to a National Football League game.[1] The suit was brought by Raul Armenta Jr. and his wife, Shannon, on behalf of themselves and their son, Holden, who is of Chumash Indian descent.[1]

## Background

According to the complaint, Holden Armenta painted his face black and red — two of the colors used in the Kansas City Chiefs logo — and wore a Native American headdress to a November 26, 2023 game between the Chiefs and the Las Vegas Raiders.[1] He was shown briefly during the television broadcast of the game with his red-and-black face paint visible.[1] The following day, Deadspin writer Carron Phillips published an article calling out the boy's attire; the featured image cropped the child so that only the side of his face covered in black paint was visible.[1]

## Ruling on the motion to dismiss

In an October 7, 2024 ruling, Judge Sean Lugg of the Superior Court of Delaware refused to dismiss the lawsuit, rejecting Deadspin's argument that the article in question was opinion and therefore protected from defamation liability.[1] "Deadspin published an image of a child displaying his passionate fandom as a backdrop for its critique of the NFL's diversity efforts and, in its description of the child, crossed the fine line protecting its speech from defamation claims," Lugg wrote.[1] The decision was carried by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] and highlighted in the Badlands News Brief as a notable check on media conduct.[1]
