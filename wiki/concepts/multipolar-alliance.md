# Multipolar Alliance

The "multipolar alliance" is the term used in Badlands Media commentary for the grouping of nations — chiefly Russia, China, India and their partners — organized around national sovereignty and win-win cooperation as an alternative to a unipolar order.[1]

## Principles

The alliance's discussions are described as shaped around the common good, common security, and the coordination of action toward improving the conditions of life of peoples and sovereign nations, in line with the [[united-nations|UN Charter]] rather than the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]].[1] Its members are said to have made clear that they would prefer cooperation with the trans-Atlantic community as allies over war, although events have forced them to prepare for war in ways few thought possible only a few years earlier.[1]

## The choice presented

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] frames the situation as a binary. One option would be for Russia, China, India and other multipolar states to abandon their sovereignty and their desire for a new win-win system, accept a [[great-reset|Great Reset]] managed by technocratic unipolar administrators above elected regimes, agree to erasing or rewriting the UN Charter, and submit to "draconian population reduction under the rubric of 'decarbonization'" in exchange for a promised utopian end of history.[1] Based on the profiles, words and performance of multipolar leaders over the past decade, the article concludes they will not do this.[1]

The alternative is a cooperative emergency summit convened before nuclear war, oriented on objective problems all peoples can agree on and on Franklin Roosevelt's [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]].[1]

## Institutional expression

The alliance's productive financial architecture, led by China, has already unleashed over $3 trillion in large-scale infrastructure investment through the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], increasingly tied to the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]] and to proposals for a new financial architecture founded on real-world values advanced by Sergey Glazyev.[1] The 2025 [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|SCO]] summit and the [[tianjin-declaration|Tianjin Declaration]] are presented as the alliance's most recent milestone.[1] Ehret observes the irony of FDR's Four Freedoms now being advanced by Eurasian powers widely considered the most "un-American" groups on earth.[1]
