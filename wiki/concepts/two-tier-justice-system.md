# Two-Tier Justice System

The **two-tier justice system** is the contention, central to Badlands Media's editorial framing, that federal law enforcement applies criminal law leniently to politically connected figures of the governing establishment while pursuing its opponents aggressively.[1] Badlands writers described the theme as "THE storyline of 2023," arguing that it was firmly established by two news items that broke within hours of one another on June 20, 2023.[1]

## The June 2023 test case

The first item was the [[hunter-biden-plea-agreement|plea agreement]] under which [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] would plead guilty to two tax counts and enter pretrial diversion on a federal gun charge, with [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] recommending probation.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued this constituted proof of unequal treatment, contrasting the probation recommendation with the $250,000 fine and five-year mandatory sentence nominally attached to possession of a firearm by an unlawful drug user, and noting that ordinary citizens with even a single [[suspicious-activity-report|Suspicious Activity Report]] would expect investigation.[1]

The second item was the setting of an August 14, 2023 court date in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[2023-trump-classified-documents-indictment|classified documents case]], one week before the first Republican primary debate.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the pairing of the two stories framed the Bidens as the establishment and Trump as its opposite, and that the perceived leniency toward Hunter Biden was metastasizing into a scandal larger than the underlying conduct.[1]

## Arguments advanced

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the Hunter Biden resolution as "performance art" by [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]]'s [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] that provided narrative cover for the administration without addressing any of the alleged 459 U.S. Code violations documented on the [[hunter-biden-laptop|laptop]].[1] She emphasized the procedural asymmetry described by [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] — "no predawn raid carried live, simultaneously on CNN. There was no perp walk, no handcuffs, no press conference. Above all, there was no felony" — and argued that no ordinary American could obtain such a deal for tax evasion and discarding an illegally possessed firearm in a dumpster near a school.[1] The same writer argued that the prosecution of Trump inverted the pattern, contending that the [[presidential-records-act|Presidential Records Act]] governed his retention and declassification of records and that the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]] was substituted precisely because the records-act precedent favored him.[1]

Polling was cited in support: a Harvard/Harris survey finding 55% of likely voters believed the Trump indictment was politically motivated and 56% that it amounted to 2024 election interference, and a CNN poll finding 71% believed it at least partly politically motivated.[1]
