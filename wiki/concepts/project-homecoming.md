# Project Homecoming

**Project Homecoming** is the formal name of the second [[donald-trump|Trump administration]]'s "self-deportation" program, a federally funded initiative that pays unauthorized immigrants to depart the United States voluntarily rather than be removed through conventional enforcement proceedings.[1]

## Scale and cost

According to internal data shared with [[axios|Axios]], the program was on pace to drive more than 200,000 departures of unauthorized immigrants.[1] Through mid-July 2026 the nearly $600 million initiative had facilitated roughly 132,000 exits, with about 70,000 additional people signed up and expected to leave.[1] Participants receive free travel plus a stipend that had risen to $2,600, having briefly peaked at $3,000 during the preceding Christmas period.[1]

Each voluntary departure was reported to cost the government about $2,500 plus the stipend, compared with roughly $18,245 for a traditional removal, producing projected savings of approximately $2.5 billion.[1] The program is described as a central component of the administration's goal of removing one million unauthorized immigrants annually, pairing a limited financial incentive with aggressive [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[united-states-border-patrol|Border Patrol]] enforcement.[1]

## Operation inside detention facilities

Roughly half of the self-deportations recorded under the program involved people already in ICE detention, an acceleration that reportedly began after a [[doge|DOGE]] staffer brought the program into detention facilities.[1] Case managers now operate in 40 detention sites; detainees who opt in spend an average of 17 days in custody versus a typical 50 days, freeing bed space that had constrained broader removal efforts.[1]

## Contracting and criticism

The contract underpinning the program was set to expire at the end of August 2026, with the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] seeking a replacement.[1] Officials describe the effort as highly efficient, while critics note that it has narrowed to little more than arranging flights.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] calculated a per-migrant cost of roughly $2,970 assuming the additional 70,000 departures occur, with the incentive payment representing about 34% of the cost and the remaining $1,970 covering airfare, travel, and program overhead.[1] Ashe argued that she had never favored paying migrants to leave and characterized the program as "a carrot that should now be followed by a stick," calling for the overhead to be audited and for all government spending to be placed on an immutable, continuously audited ledger.[1] She also noted that because the incentive cost per person is fixed, the per-deportation cost of government overhead falls as the total number of removals rises.[1]
