# Taiwan Strait

The Taiwan Strait is the body of water separating mainland China from self-ruled Taiwan and is one of the most contested maritime flashpoints in global geopolitics.[1]

## 2023 escalation

In April 2023, China concluded large-scale air and sea drills in the strait, staged in retaliation for Taiwanese President [[tsai-ing-wen|Tsai Ing-wen]]'s April 5 meeting with U.S. House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] in California.[1] Beijing said the exercises, which simulated an encirclement of Taiwan, were intended as a "serious warning" to pro-independence politicians on the island and their foreign supporters.[1]

On April 16, 2023, the [[uss-milius|USS Milius]] transited the strait — the first known U.S. warship passage since the encirclement drills.[1] The [[us-7th-fleet|U.S. 7th Fleet]] described the transit as routine and said the ship passed "through a corridor in the Strait that is beyond the territorial sea of any coastal State."[1] China protested the next day, with the [[eastern-theater-command|Eastern Theater Command]] calling the transit "public hype."[1]

## The midline and sustained pressure

China has steadily increased military pressure on Taiwan in recent years, sending fighter jets and naval vessels toward the island on a near-daily basis.[1] Following former Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s August 2022 visit to Taiwan, Chinese vessels increasingly crossed the midline of the strait — an unofficial boundary respected for decades — and that mounting pressure drew greater international attention to Taiwan.[1]

## "Gulf of Taiwonkin" analysis

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] analyzed the strait through the lens of [[scare-event-theory|Scare Event theory]], arguing that a Scare Event can simultaneously function as a unifying event and that a nuclear scare is only one possible template.[1] Citing Chinese aircraft buzzing Taiwan, U.S. warship posturing, and a resurgent Japan, Australia and India encroaching on the region, the commentary asked whether a "Gulf of Taiwonkin" near-miss — an analog to the [[gulf-of-tonkin-incident|Gulf of Tonkin incident]] — was coming.[1]

Some in the community objected that such an event would contradict the view that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] is working to usher in a [[multipolar-world|multi-polar world]] on behalf of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[1] Burning Bright disagreed, arguing that focusing on net effects suggests Xi could act as a catalyst for solidifying a major regional Region of Responsibility by consolidating Japan, Australia and India as powers in the region — a matter, in this reading, of optics.[1]

## 2022 "intermittent escalation"

Writing in October 2022, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the strait as a site of "intermittent escalation," escalating and seemingly de-escalating so often as to produce [[narrative-whiplash|Narrative Whiplash]].[2] He argued that ratcheting tension in the strait, coming while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was out of office, formed part of a broader [[crisis-cascade|Crisis Cascade]], and that a kinetic outcome there would serve globalist agendas by embroiling the United States in another proxy war and propping up the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]].[2] Applying [[bicameral-thinking|Bicameral Thinking]], he simultaneously held that such narratives had been turned back on their authors through what he called a [[reverse-hegelian-dialectic|Reverse Hegelian Dialectic]].[2]
