# DOJ Voter Registration List Lawsuits

The DOJ voter registration list lawsuits are a series of federal suits brought by the [[justice-department|Justice Department]]'s Civil Rights Division against states and local governments that failed to produce their voter registration lists when requested. By mid-December 2025 the division had filed 18 such lawsuits as part of the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s effort to ensure states adhere to federal election laws.[1]

## Defendants

The Civil Rights Division announced on Friday, December 12, 2025 that it had filed new lawsuits against four states — Colorado, Hawaii, Massachusetts, and Nevada. Those four joined twelve others: six states sued in September — California, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, New Hampshire, and Pennsylvania — and six more on December 2 — Delaware, Maryland, New Mexico, Rhode Island, Vermont, and Washington. The division additionally sued one locality, Fulton County, Georgia, for records related to the 2020 election.[1]

## Rationale

Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet K. Dhillon]] said that "States have the statutory duty to preserve and protect their constituents from vote dilution," adding that the department would "not permit states to jeopardize the integrity and effectiveness of elections by refusing to abide by our federal elections laws. If states will not fulfill their duty to protect the integrity of the ballot, we will."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the litigation to parallel findings of DMV "glitches" in the [[commercial-drivers-licenses-for-immigrants|commercial driver's license]] crackdown, arguing that highly complex public-private partnerships generate plausible deniability when officials are caught encouraging ineligible people to register to vote, and that the same class of "data coding mismatches" is dismissed as immaterial in elections.[1]
