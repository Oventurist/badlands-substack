# Public Banking

**Public banking** refers to banking institutions owned by a government or by the public collectively rather than by private shareholders, operated to serve depositors and the local economy rather than to maximise profit. In [[a-seismic-shift|A Seismic Shift]], [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the "public banking option" as one of the two great constitutional safeguards a nation can adopt, alongside citizen-only voter identification.

## The Costa Rican model

The article's framing derives from [[jose-figueres|José "Pepe" Figueres]] of Costa Rica, who according to Lange created a permanent public banking option that prevented private banks from controlling the government and protected the people's savings from being stolen or controlled by private bankers. Lange argues this has gone a long way toward preventing the capture of Costa Rica's government by a global cabal.

## The American record

Two American precedents are examined. The [[bank-of-north-dakota|Bank of North Dakota]], founded in 1919 and owned by the residents of the state, remains the only publicly owned bank in the continental United States and has survived the Great Depression, the 1980s Savings and Loan scandal and the 2008 credit crisis. The [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]], which operated nationally from 1911 to 1967, gave all Americans no-cost, interest-bearing savings accounts at post offices and proved "uniquely safe and reliable" when private banks collapsed in the 1930s.

[[ellen-brown|Ellen Brown]], chair of the [[public-banking-institute|Public Banking Institute]], is quoted from her book *Banking on the People* arguing that a public banking system "can fund the goods, services and infrastructure required to satisfy the needs of the people and the economy without unsustainable debt, taxation or environmental degradation." In These Times is cited for the argument that rather than having public funds extracted from local communities to fuel Wall Street speculation, public banks can stabilise local economies and support local public priorities.

## The contemporary movement

Vox is cited for the observation that North Dakota's success has drawn attention in California, where a public bank movement has gained prominence since 2018, and that efforts have been made in nearly two dozen other states — with New York, New Mexico, New Hampshire and Massachusetts all introducing bills in a single year, none of which passed. Lange characterises the conditions driving the movement as a recurrence of those of the original gilded age: corporate domination of government, an economy strangled by debt, extreme inequality, and ineffective or bought politicians.

## Obstacles and future proposals

The article identifies [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|federal deposit insurance]] as the mechanism that historically neutralised public banking's competitive advantage, and identifies the [[american-bankers-association|American Bankers Association]] as the industry's present-day lobbying vehicle against a public option. The forward-looking proposals it endorses are a revival of postal banking through the [[united-states-postal-service|United States Postal Service]] and the creation of [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]], which Lange links to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s alleged plan to restore a redeemable [[gold-standard|gold standard]] and end taxpayer-funded bank bailouts.
