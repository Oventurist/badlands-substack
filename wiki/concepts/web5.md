# Web5

Web5 is a proposed decentralized version of the internet in which users store their own personal information and can revoke access to it at will.[1] Described as the brainchild of [[twitter|Twitter]] co-founder [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]], it functions as a peer-to-peer system built on top of [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] infrastructure and is offered as an alternative vision to the blockchain-based Web3.[1]

## Design

According to a Builtin.com explanation quoted by Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], Web5 aims to secure personal data by restoring ownership of digital identity to individual users, leveraging Bitcoin's Lightning Network — a protocol operating off the main blockchain to allow rapid network functionality independent of tokens, transaction "gas" fees, trusted validators or additional consensus mechanisms — in order to create a peer-to-peer infrastructure of personal servers.[1] By positioning users as the sole managers of their data, encoded in a wallet stored on a node or device, the choice to make personal information public or to revoke it rests with them.[1]

Lange summarizes Web5 as a decentralized, peer-to-peer network specifically intended to create an internet free from centralization and censorship, in which the people rather than corporations control the servers that store data.[1]

## Components

- **Decentralized Identifiers:** unique, self-generated and self-owned identifiers allowing identity authentication and routing.[1]
- **Verifiable Credentials:** proofs or certificates secured by cryptography and created by multiple users, verifying a claim or interaction.[1]
- **Decentralized Web Nodes:** the computers in the peer-to-peer network that act as servers storing data, relaying messages and supporting decentralized applications.[1]

## Wallets

Lange contrasts the two generations of wallet: a Web3 wallet holds cryptocurrency, NFTs or digital assets secured by encrypted private and public keys, but still allows companies to collect consumer data with an attendant loss of privacy; a Web5 wallet holds a user's identity, data and application authorizations on the user's own nodal server, making the user the gatekeeper and protecting privacy.[1]

## Significance

Michael Pierce, chief executive of NotCommon, is quoted saying that "the fundamental purpose of Web5 is to give users control over their data" and that the concept "attempts to democratize the web by eliminating gatekeepers that effectively centralize technology."[1] Like a globally unique username or digital passport, the protocol supports easy sign-in authorization across applications while the log-in information — often including last names, phone numbers and addresses — stays with the user.[1]

Lange argues that this amounts to a complete revolution in how the financial system works and who controls it: because banks currently control financial data and use it to determine creditworthiness within a global debt system, and because such data has repeatedly proved vulnerable to hackers, shifting data ownership to individuals is presented as the decisive step in the [[decentralization|decentralization]] of banking.[1]
