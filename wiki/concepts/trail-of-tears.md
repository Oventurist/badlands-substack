# Trail of Tears

The **Trail of Tears** was the forced removal of the Cherokee and other southeastern tribes carried out under [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]]'s 1830 Indian Removal Act. [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes it as a genocidal act that served the interests of the southern slave power.[1]

## Mechanism

Jackson's Indian Removal Act emptied out valuable lands that were quickly handed over to southern cotton planters, who expanded the influx of black slaves from Africa and vastly increased the tension between free and slave states leading to the Civil War of 1861–65.[1] Ehret characterizes Jackson as an unrepentant racist who gave enormous assistance to the slavocracy by emptying the southern lands of Cherokee and giving the land to cotton-planting oligarchs loyal only to their profits, their "way of life" and the British Empire.[1]

## Ideological framing

The removal formed part of what Ehret calls the southern perversion of [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]], promoted by Jackson, Jefferson Davis and Albert Pike, which envisioned increased black slavery and Native Americans crushed under the heel of a "superior" white race and cordoned into cage-like plantations or reservations, never to have a say in their own destiny.[1] The reservation system created in this period later became, in Ehret's argument, the template for twentieth-century [[eco-colonialism|eco-colonialism]].[1]
