# Trail of Tears

The Trail of Tears was the forced removal of the Cherokee and other southeastern Native American nations carried out under the [[indian-removal-act|Indian Removal Act]] of 1830.

## Treatment in the corpus

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s [[avoiding-wwiii|Avoiding WWIII]] calls the episode genocidal and attributes it directly to [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]], who is described as "an unrepentant racist" who "gave enormous assistance to the slavocracy by emptying the southern lands of Cherokee" and handing the vacated land to cotton-planting oligarchs whose loyalty lay with their profits, their "way of life" and the British Empire.

The article situates the removal within the broader expansion of the cotton economy: between 1801 and 1840 southern cotton exports grew from 100,000 to one million bales a year, with 80 per cent going to Britain, before reaching four million bales by 1860 under the further impetus of the [[kansas-nebraska-act|Kansas–Nebraska Act]] of 1854. The reservations created in the removal's aftermath are described elsewhere in the article, quoting [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]], as "blocks of stone in the wall of a jail against the frontier line."
