# We The People AZ v. Runbeck

We The People AZ v. Runbeck is an Arizona public-records lawsuit brought by [[we-the-people-arizona-alliance|We The People Arizona Alliance]] against [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]], the private vendor contracted by [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] for ballot printing and handling.[1]

## Origins

The case arose after Runbeck refused to comply with a lawful public records request for "exterior video of all loading dock locations which clearly shows any item(s) being delivered and/or picked up" and corresponding "interior video of all loading dock locations/warehouse space" for the period following Election Day.[1] The Alliance sought two days of surveillance footage, contending that the video was essential to establishing chain of custody for ballots moving between the county and the vendor.[1]

## First hearing

The first hearing was held on Wednesday, September 20, 2023, and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] provided live special coverage of the proceeding on Rumble.[1] The question before the court was whether Runbeck, functioning as a government contractor, is subject to Arizona's public records law.[1]

Counsel for Maricopa County and Runbeck argued that the company is no different from a Staples or a UPS Store, and that the product of Runbeck's work is no different from the toilet paper filling the county's bathrooms.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], reporting for Badlands, remarked that the attorneys really did make that argument.[1]

The counterargument she highlighted was that the county's Runbeck contract is sole source — a designation that waives competitive bidding requirements precisely because the vendor is considered so critical to the election process that no alternative provider exists.[1] A vendor indispensable enough to justify a no-bid contract, in this framing, cannot simultaneously be an ordinary commercial supplier beyond the reach of public records obligations.[1] The plaintiff's attorney advanced this point, though it was not the primary argument and its delivery was described as muddled.[1]

The judge committed to ruling on the matter expeditiously, and Badlands said it would report the decision when issued.[1]
