# Illusion of Democracy

"Illusion of democracy" is the framing used by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in his February 14, 2023 essay "Balance, Necessary for Control" to describe what he presents as a staged electoral process in which outcomes are predetermined while voters are persuaded that their ballots decide who governs.[1]

## The balance thesis

Carlson opens by noting that "balance" is almost never used in a negative sense — a balanced diet, a balance between family and work, a diversified investment portfolio, physical balance — and that no one ever complains of having too much of it.[1] He then inverts the connotation, arguing that balance is also the precondition for political control: maintaining and enforcing a narrative requires that a population be divided somewhere near the middle, because otherwise "the narrative — and control — starts to fall apart."[1]

The mechanism he proposes rests on herd behavior. It is human nature, he writes, to want to belong to the majority, especially a significant one; people are conditioned to "run with the herd" and regard standing alone as dangerous.[1] When a clear majority forms, or appears to form, those in the minority rush to the other side. To the [[cabal|Cabal]], he argues, that stampede is like someone shouting "fire!" in a crowded theater — the frantic rush to the exits causes the "system" to fail.[1]

## The 60/40 threshold

Carlson assigns numbers to the thesis. A roughly even split between Democrats and Republicans, liberals and conservatives, makes a population easier to control; a slight lean is needed to elect politicians at all, and "52/48 works exceptionally well," while 51/49 invites suspicion of voter fraud.[1] Beyond a 60/40 range, he argues, the arrangement collapses, because the 40 begin defecting to the other side out of fear of belonging to a significant minority.[1] He invites readers to notice how often the popular vote in American elections is decided by a couple of percentage points, "even in obviously-rigged elections."[1]

## Convergence of the two parties

A second stabilizing device, in Carlson's account, is minimizing the actual philosophical distance between the two options on offer. Genuinely opposed left-wing liberals and right-wing conservatives would produce a divided nation even if numerically balanced, so the preferred configuration is moderate liberals against moderate conservatives.[1] Every four or eight years, he writes, the people controlling the country — "if not the entire world" — determine which side will be voted in and which way policy will lean.[1] For decades the appearance of balance in the United States was maintained by pairing [[rino|RINOs]] on one side with moderate liberals on the other, though "both sides work for the same people."[1]

## Kayfabe and predetermined outcomes

Carlson applies the professional-wrestling concept of [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] to this arrangement: two sides pretending to be at odds to generate drama within a storyline, with the Cabal acting as the match-maker who determines the winners of elections.[1] The wrestlers are the RINO and liberal Democrat politicians; whether a voter reads a given RINO as the "babyface" or the "heel" depends only on which way that voter naturally leans.[1] Either way, the outcome of the match is already decided, because the [[deep-state|Deep State]] Cabal cannot afford to leave decisions affecting its control in citizens' hands.[1]

The show exists, in this reading, so that people believe a democratic process is taking place. Without the belief that their votes count, the system fails and the Cabal loses control; it "controls the world through illusion," and when the illusion breaks its power ceases to exist.[1]

## The Q drop origin of the phrase

The phrase itself is drawn from [[q-post-2807|Q drop #2807]], which lists "Trillions of dollars at stake," "Controlled by group of special interests," and "Illusion of democracy," followed by the bracketed line "[Welcome to the Real World]" and the exhortation that "WE, THE PEOPLE, MUST RECLAIM WHAT IS RIGHTFULLY OURS."[2] Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] built an entire article, "Illusions of Democracy," around that drop, concluding that "the 'real world' today is an 'illusion of democracy.'"[2]

## Lange's version of the thesis

Lange's account locates the illusion in money rather than in electoral kayfabe. He argues that the United States was founded as a constitutional republic in which the people hold all the power, but that a global cabal of elites took control of the government by controlling the banks and therefore the economies, using politicians as personal puppets.[2] The same drop asks who controls, accounts for and has access to public funds, and whether they return as political donations, donations to foundations and institutes, or offshore accounts for future payoffs — concluding that there is no honest control, no checks and balances and no safeguards in place.[2] Lange reads the drop's insider-versus-outsider framing, in which [[john-f-kennedy|Kennedy]] was assassinated and Reagan survived an assassination attempt, as showing that [[taxpayer-theft|taxpayer theft]] is protected by a [[two-tier-justice-system|two-tier justice system]] created by a [[shadow-government|shadow government]].[2]

His proposed remedy is structural rather than electoral: **safeguards** of the kind the [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]] calls "new Guards for their future security," modelled on those [[jose-figueres-ferrer|José "Pepe" Figueres Ferrer]] installed in [[costa-rica|Costa Rica]] — abolition of the standing army, non-consecutive term limits, the [[cedula|cédula]] citizenship voter ID, and a permanent [[public-banking-option|public banking option]].[2] Lange expects [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the military to hand control back to the American people, after which it will be up to the people to maintain it.[2]
