# Time Person of the Year

**Time Person of the Year** is the annual designation issued by [[time-magazine|Time magazine]] recognizing the person judged to have most influenced the events of the preceding year, for better or worse. The honor carries no cash award and is explicitly not an endorsement, but it functions as one of the most visible annual markers of political and cultural salience in American media.[1]

## 2024 selection

On Thursday, December 12, 2024, Time named President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] its 2024 Person of the Year, citing what the magazine described as a political comeback of historic proportions during a year in which he weathered assassination attempts and felony convictions.[1] In its citation the magazine wrote that Trump was recognized "for marshaling a comeback of historic proportions, for driving a once-in-a-generation political realignment, for reshaping the American presidency and altering America's role in the world."[1]

The magazine added that "since he began running for President in 2015, perhaps no single individual has played a larger role in changing the course of politics and history than Trump," and that Trump was "once again at the center of the world, and in as strong a position as he has ever been."[1]

## Pattern of presidential selections

The 2024 choice was widely anticipated. Time has bestowed the honor on the winner of that year's presidential election in every race since George W. Bush in 2000, and Trump had previously been named Person of the Year in 2016 when he first won the White House.[1] To mark the 2024 occasion, Trump was scheduled to ring the opening bell at the New York Stock Exchange on the morning of the announcement.[1]

## Accompanying interview

The designation came packaged with a lengthy Time interview that itself generated downstream news cycles. In that interview Trump claimed that the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had lost track of more than 300,000 unaccompanied migrant children, a claim subsequently subjected to fact-checking by CBS News.[1] Asked in the same interview whether he trusted Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], Trump replied, "I don't trust anybody."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the award less as an establishment honor than as a move within an ongoing information war, arguing that Trump was giving "characters, cyphers and coalitions on all sides of the info war just enough of a platform to elevate themselves, and just enough rope to hang themselves."[1] Burning Bright interpreted the "I don't trust anybody" line as a statement that Trump distrusts the other players not because he does not know them but because he does, having been "born into the game, raised in the game, and forged in the game."[1] The piece concluded with the inversion that Trump is "not trapped in the story with them" — rather, "they're trapped in the story with him."[1]

## 2024 selection

Ahead of the December 12, 2024 announcement, Politico reported that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was expected to be named Time's Person of the Year, and that to mark the cover unveiling the president-elect would ring the opening bell of the New York Stock Exchange that Thursday morning.[2] The previous year's honoree was the pop superstar Taylor Swift, whose cover reveal was marked by Time chief executive Jessica Sibley ringing the opening bell.[2] Trump had also been named Person of the Year in 2016 after winning the presidency, joining 13 other U.S. presidents to receive the recognition, including [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[2]

The short list, announced on NBC's "The Today Show," included Trump, Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], Kate Middleton, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[2] Time had already named NBA star Caitlin Clark Athlete of the Year, Elton John Icon of the Year and Lisa Su of Advanced Micro Devices CEO of the Year, and a spokesperson said the magazine does not comment on its annual choice prior to publication.[2] Politico noted that the incoming president has long been fixated on magazine covers, especially Time's.[2]
