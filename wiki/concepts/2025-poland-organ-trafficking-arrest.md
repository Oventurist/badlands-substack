# 2025 Poland Organ Trafficking Arrest

The 2025 Poland organ trafficking arrest refers to the detention by Polish border guards, announced on March 11, 2025, of a Ukrainian woman convicted in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] of participating in an organized criminal group that trafficked human organs and sold 56 kidneys.[1]

## The arrest

The 35-year-old woman, referred to only as Ksenia P. under Polish privacy laws, was detained at a railway crossing between [[poland|Poland]] and Ukraine under an [[interpol|Interpol]] red notice, according to Marta Petkowska, a spokeswoman for the prosecutor's office in Przemyśl.[1] She had been sentenced in Kazakhstan to twelve years in prison and had been sought by Interpol since November 2020.[1] Prosecutors did not say why she was not in Kazakh custody at the time of her detention, nor when she had been convicted.[1] The case was reported by [[reuters|Reuters]].[1]

## The underlying conviction

The woman was convicted of participation in an international organized criminal group that illegally collected tissues and organs from people between 2017 and 2019 and sold them on the black market.[1] She was further convicted of illegally obtaining "kidneys from 56 injured parties in Kazakhstan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Ukraine, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan and Thailand" for financial benefit, and of "making the crimes committed a permanent source of income."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the Kazakh element of the conviction as affirming a theory previously advanced in the newsletter — that a Black Sea organ smuggling ring may operate between Turkey and Ukraine, potentially involving Kazakhstan.[1] He argued that [[israel|Israel]], given what he characterized as lax banking regulations and a disposition against extraditing its own citizens regardless of the offense, would be an ideal home base for such a cartel, and pointed to an Israeli organ smuggling ring reportedly broken up in Turkey the previous May.[1]

He further invoked the late investigative author Gordon Thomas's book *Robert Maxwell: Israel's Super Spy*, which recounts that as the Soviet Union's collapse became undeniable in the 1980s, [[semion-mogilevich|Semion Mogilevich]] — described as the crime boss of the Ukrainian Mafia — briefly relocated his operation to Israel.[1] As a longtime business partner of [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]], Mogilevich is said to have learned money laundering from the man who pioneered the craft in the modern monetary system, with Israel offering the most favorable and low-risk environment given its banking system.[1] Mogilevich was placed on the FBI Wanted List in 2009 and removed in 2016 amid suspicion that he was living in a country without a US extradition treaty.[1] Regardless of whether Mogilevich was connected to this particular case, the commentary held that Thomas documented the laundering of billions of dollars by the Ukrainian and Russian mafias through Israel, taught to them by [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]]'s father.[1]
