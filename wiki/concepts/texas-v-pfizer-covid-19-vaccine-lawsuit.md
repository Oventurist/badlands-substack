# Texas v. Pfizer COVID-19 Vaccine Lawsuit

On Thursday, November 30, 2023, Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] announced that his office was suing the pharmaceutical company [[pfizer|Pfizer]], alleging that the company had "misrepresented" the effectiveness of its widely administered COVID-19 vaccine.[1]

## Allegations

Paxton accused Pfizer of violating the [[texas-deceptive-trade-practices-act|Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act]] through "false, deceptive, and misleading acts and practices by making unsupported claims regarding the company's COVID-19 vaccine."[1] The suit alleges five violations of that statute in total.[1] Its central claims are that Pfizer gave the impression its vaccine would end the COVID-19 pandemic, and that the company's assertion that the shot was 95 percent effective was misleading.[1]

More than 366 million doses of Pfizer's original coronavirus vaccine were administered in the United States according to federal data.[1] The efficacy analysis released in November 2020 determined the injection was 95 percent effective against COVID-19 twenty-eight days after the first dose; Paxton's lawsuit contends those results were achieved in a "technical, and artificial way."[1]

"We are pursuing justice for the people of Texas, many of whom were coerced by tyrannical vaccine mandates to take a defective product sold by lies," Paxton said in a statement.[1] "The facts are clear. Pfizer did not tell the truth about their COVID-19 vaccines," he added, charging that "the [[joe-biden|Biden]] Administration weaponized the pandemic to force illegal public health decrees on the public and enrich pharmaceutical companies," and pledging to use every tool available to protect citizens misled and harmed by Pfizer's actions.[1] The suit was reported by [[the-hill|The Hill]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that for nearly four years the key issues and arguments presumed true — those that led to mass lockdowns and forced vaccinations — had never been properly debated or evaluated, a fact he took as supporting the view that the COVID pandemic response was a sophisticated criminal operation involving a partnership between big tech, big med, big media, and big government.[1] He held that Paxton's lawsuit would force an honest assessment of the presumed facts about the vaccine's effectiveness, and that once "the inquisitiveness sleeping giant" is roused it will be difficult to put back in its cage.[1]

Deschamps further argued for a valid, objectively verifiable method of evaluating vaccine and medical intervention efficacy, comparing the need to a carpenter checking level to ensure a house will last: as medical consumers forced by time pressure to accept the dictates of officialdom, the public should have a way to measure official claims and see whether they are on the level.[1]
