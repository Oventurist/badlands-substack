# Waxhaws Massacre

The Waxhaws massacre was the May 1780 killing of surrendering American troops by [[banastre-tarleton|Banastre Tarleton]]'s dragoons at Waxhaw, South Carolina, the atrocity that produced the phrase "[[tarletons-quarter|Tarleton's Quarter]]" and made Tarleton the most hated British officer in the colonies.[1]

## Events

As the [[siege-of-charleston|siege of Charleston]] began, [[henry-clinton|Sir Henry Clinton]] ordered Tarleton to cut off all escape routes from the city and ride down any fleeing military units.[1] One such unit was a body of Virginia militia that had marched down from North Carolina to reinforce Charleston; they encountered [[continental-army|Continental]] dragoons riding north escorting South Carolina Governor [[john-rutledge|John Rutledge]], who intended to raise more militia as he retreated from the state.[1] The combined force turned north toward Charlotte and made camp at Waxhaw, sending the governor on to safer ground.[1]

Tarleton came upon them riding full tilt, and the battle was short.[1] As casualties mounted among the inexperienced militia, their leader told them to throw down their arms and raised the white flag; multiple observers confirm that Tarleton then drew his saber and led a charge through the ranks, cutting them to pieces.[1] Tarleton later claimed his horse had been shot from under him and that his men, believing their commander had fallen, retaliated out of revenge — a defense the article notes would still constitute a war crime.[1]

In a matter of minutes, 113 men were killed, 150 severely wounded, and roughly 200 taken prisoner.[1] The story spread widely and the resulting bloodlust became known as "Tarleton's Quarter."[1] The conduct of Tarleton and the British Legion during the southern campaigns of 1780–1781 was later examined as a war-crimes question in a 2012 Military Law Review article by Major John Kiel of the Office of the Judge Advocate General.[1]
