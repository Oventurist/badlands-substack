# Stanford Prison Experiment

The **Stanford Prison Experiment** is cited by the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] as evidence for his account of identity-driven behaviour.[1] In his May 6, 2024 essay "The Hidden Hypnotic Factor," he wrote that the famous experiment demonstrated that human beings have a remarkable tendency to act exactly as their sense of identity dictates.[1]

The reference appears in the essay's discussion of [[cultural-hypnosis|cultural hypnosis]], where culture is presented as a meta-identity whose constituent narratives supply the raw material for individual identities.[1] The experiment's implication — that assigned roles rapidly govern conduct — underpins the author's related claim that human beings assign characteristic behaviours to identities and then unconsciously act them out, which he treats as the basis of the [[trance-state|trance state]].[1] As far as the subconscious mind is concerned, he argues, identity, beliefs and perception *are* reality.[1]
