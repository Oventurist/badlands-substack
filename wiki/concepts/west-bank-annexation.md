# West Bank Annexation

West Bank annexation refers to the February 2026 package of measures approved by the Israeli Security Cabinet that, in the assessment of both supporters and critics, would establish Israeli sovereignty over the West Bank without formal legislation.[1]

## The measures

The Security Cabinet approved the steps by executive power rather than legislation.[1] They included shifting building authority in Hebron, facilitating settler land purchases, repealing restrictions on non-Muslim real estate transactions, and expanding Israeli enforcement into Palestinian-controlled areas.[1] The measures allow members of the Israeli public to purchase tracts of land in the West Bank and register those properties with the Israeli government rather than the Palestinian Authority, placing the land under Israeli jurisdiction in the eyes of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s Security Cabinet.[1]

Likud Energy Minister [[eli-cohen|Eli Cohen]] praised the package as establishing "de facto sovereignty" that rules out a Palestinian state.[1] The actions violate the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] and drew U.S. concern over regional stability.[1]

## Trump's opposition

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] explicitly opposed the moves, telling journalist Barak Ravid: "I am against annexation," and adding, "We have enough things to think about now. We don't need to be dealing with the West Bank."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the annexation measures were designed as a deliberate provocation, noting that Muslim leadership across the Middle East was enraged and that [[hamas|Hamas]] was calling for kinetic escalation against Israel — which, in this reading, is precisely what Netanyahu wants.[1] The timing, just days before Netanyahu's Washington trip, was read as an attempt to shape the meeting agenda.[1]

## July 2025 sovereignty call and Saudi response

On July 2, 2025, [[israel|Israel]]'s Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]] declared that "the time has come" for Israel to apply sovereignty across the occupied West Bank.[2] [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned the statement the same day, saying the imposition of sovereignty over Palestinian territory would violate international law, rejecting settlement expansion, and reiterating the Kingdom's insistence that Israel abide by international resolutions.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted the Saudi statement with earlier _Israel Hayom_ reporting claiming that, under an expanded [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] framework, Saudi Arabia had agreed to cede parts of the West Bank to Israel — a claim he dismissed as fake news.[2]

## Knesset non-binding motion (July 2025)

An earlier step toward annexation came on July 24, 2025, when [[knesset|Knesset]] lawmakers voted 71–13 in favor of a non-binding motion for the agenda supporting annexation of the West Bank.[3] Knesset Speaker [[amir-ohana|Amir Ohana]] declared after the vote that "the land of Israel belongs to the people of Israel" and that "Jews cannot be the occupiers" of their own ancestral homeland, adding that in 1967 "the occupation did not begin; it ended."[3]

Badlands commentary treated the motion as a decisive falsification of official Israeli framing: contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Israel had claimed to be the party pushing the two-state solution and that everything since October 7 was about stopping Hamas, yet the annexation declaration targeted the West Bank — not Gaza — where Hamas holds no political power or formal presence.[3]

## Smotrich Washington visit (March 2025)

On March 4, 2025, Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] travelled to the United States ahead of an expected statement by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the future of the West Bank, saying he would meet US officials including Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] to strengthen economic cooperation and deepen the strategic alliance.[4] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that annexation of the West Bank would likely mean a world war and would certainly foreclose a trillion-dollar Saudi investment, since Iran and the Arab states, backed by Russia and China, would go to war with Israel — leading him to say he would be stunned to see it happen.[4] He linked the timing to reports of [[russian-mediation-of-us-iran-nuclear-talks|Russian mediation of US–Iran nuclear talks]].[4]


## UAE "red line" warning (September 2025)

In September 2025 the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] delivered its most explicit warning to date against annexation, two days before Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was slated to hold a major ministerial consultation on whether to advance the move.[5] Emirati special envoy [[lana-nusseibeh|Lana Nusseibeh]] told The Times of Israel in an interview at the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Abu Dhabi: "Annexation would be a red line for my government, and that means there can be no lasting peace. It would foreclose the idea of regional integration and be the death knell of the two-state solution."[5] The intervention came just before the fifth anniversary of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], which the UAE initiated by becoming the first Arab country to normalize relations with Israel in over a quarter-century.[5]

Badlands commentary noted that the UAE had played this restraining role before.[5] When Netanyahu announced in May 2020 that Israel would resume construction of new West Bank settlements, UAE Ambassador to the US Yousef Al Otaiba published an open letter in Israeli media warning that the settlements would prevent the Accords from being finalized — prompting Netanyahu to back down and the terms to be settled verbally in mid-August 2020.[5] The day before that finalizing call, Israeli Ambassador to the US [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] reportedly told the Trump White House that Israel would hold off signing because of the political environment and upcoming election, after which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is said to have called Netanyahu personally to insist he appear and sign.[5] Netanyahu's Likud subsequently won the most seats but failed to assemble a 61-seat Knesset coalition, and opposition leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] formed a government with [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] as prime minister and Lapid as foreign minister; it was Lapid who inaugurated the first Israeli embassy in the UAE on the first official visit there by an Israeli public official.[5]
