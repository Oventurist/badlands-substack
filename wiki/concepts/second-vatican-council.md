# Second Vatican Council

The Second Vatican Council, commonly called Vatican II, was launched by Pope John XXIII in 1962 and is described by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the vehicle of the Catholic Church's decentralization and liberalization.[1]

Ehret argues that over the decades following the death of [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] in 1955, his followers played a major role in shaping the outcome of Vatican II.[1] The logic advanced by Chardin's disciples against the dogmatic traditionalists of the [[vatican|Vatican]] was that since the times were changing, so too must religion: the world of the nation state, industrial growth and individualism was held to be an outdated conservative era, superseded by a post-nation-state world of collective planetary consciousness moving toward a mystical [[omega-point|Omega Point]].[1] Christianity, on this view, had to evolve with the times like any creature wishing to avoid extinction in a Darwinian struggle for survival.[1]

Ehret links Vatican II to the subsequent emergence of [[liberation-theology|Liberation Theology]] among networks concentrated in Ibero-America, and to what he characterizes as a full Jesuit takeover of the Church.[1]
