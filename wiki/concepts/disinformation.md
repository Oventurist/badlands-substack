# Disinformation

Disinformation, as defined by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], is the art of blending truth and falsehood into a dizzying web designed to stir the passions of the masses and lead them toward specific actions.[1]

## Distinguishing feature

The definition emphasizes that disinformation is not simple falsehood but a mixture: the presence of genuine truth is what makes the web dizzying and difficult to unravel.[1] The essay's framing premise is that "the world we live in is fake" and that the hard part is figuring out specifically how it is fake — a task made harder by the fact that everyone is yelling.[1] Audiences know the media lie but do not always know how to sift the true from the false.[1]

## Relation to hypnosis

Because heightened emotion is the precondition for accepting a hypnotic suggestion, disinformation works by arousal rather than by argument, and the defense proposed is to remain as emotion-neutral as possible amid chaos.[1] Related techniques catalogued in the same essay include the exploitation of [[nominalization|nominalizations]] and hypnotic repetition by the [[mockingbird-media|Mockingbird Media]], and the manipulation of images — a lesson the author dates to a 2015 Photoshop course and the realization that "anybody can make anything look like anything," now compounded by deepfakes.[1] The author concludes that conditions warrant near-categorical skepticism of everything found online or offline, and that being legitimately "awake" means recognizing a world of fake news designed to hijack cognition by first hijacking emotions and needs.[1] Coverage of the [[israel-palestine-conflict|Israel-Palestine conflict]] is offered as the working example.[1]
