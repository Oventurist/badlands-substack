# Boasberg Memorandum on D.C. Judges

The Boasberg memorandum is a document obtained exclusively by [[the-federalist|The Federalist]] in July 2025 revealing that federal judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] advised Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] and some two dozen other judges that his colleagues on the [[dc-district-court|D.C. District Court]] were "concern[ed] that the Administration would disregard rulings of federal courts leading to a constitutional crisis."[1]

## Significance

The Federalist argued that it was shocking for Boasberg and his fellow D.C. District Court judges to discuss how a named defendant in numerous pending lawsuits might respond to an adverse ruling.[1] It further characterized as outrageous those judges' clear disregard for the presumption of regularity — the presumption that requires a court to presume public officials properly discharged their official duties.[1] Because [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is not merely the president but also a defendant in scores of lawsuits, including multiple cases in the D.C. District Court, the reporting framed the conversation as concerning a specific litigant before the same judges rather than generic concerns of the judiciary.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] praised the piece by Margot Cleveland at The Federalist and argued that Boasberg "and friends need to be handled," meaning that [[congress|Congress]] should reform the district courts and depose judges who show disdain for the Constitution, starting with Boasberg.[1] She noted that Cleveland's article details how Boasberg's own conduct has repeatedly undermined his stated concerns about the rule of law, and characterized it as a bad look for justice when judges are openly biased about specific defendants and lament those biases to the Chief Justice of the U.S. Supreme Court.[1]
