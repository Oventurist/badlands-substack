# North Carolina Voter ID Law

The North Carolina voter ID law is a state statute passed in 2018 to implement a voter-approved constitutional amendment requiring photo identification at the polls, an amendment that received support from a majority of North Carolina voters.[1]

## Litigation history

US District Judge [[loretta-biggs|Loretta Biggs]] blocked the requirement ahead of the 2020 election, but the US Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit overturned that injunction, and the North Carolina Supreme Court separately upheld the law.[1] In a 2026 ruling Biggs reversed her earlier position and upheld the statute, allowing the ID requirement to remain in place before the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[1] She found that voting rights groups had failed to prove the law was intentionally discriminatory, rejecting arguments that it would disproportionately affect Black and Latino voters, and stated that existing precedent required the court to defer to the legislature's stated intent.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the ruling in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], writing that it was good to see a federal judge reverse course and uphold voter ID, while arguing that a legitimate and accountable Congress would already have solved election fraud.[1] She tied the ruling to a broader discussion with Seth Keshel on her program Culture of Change about his book *The American War on Election Corruption*, in which they covered fake voters, fake ballots, and fake counts, focusing most on voter registration.[1] Her thesis was that fake voters are the foundation of the entire corrupt election machine — "the fake voters get the fake ballots which are fake counted for the fake election results" — and she cited a poll by [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] in which most respondents said accountability is what is needed, adding that critical thinking would produce accountability naturally.[1]

## February 2023 rehearing decision

On February 3, 2023, the newly seated 5–2 Republican majority on the [[north-carolina-supreme-court|North Carolina Supreme Court]] voted to rehear the voter ID case along with the partisan gerrymandering case [[harper-v-hall|Harper v. Hall]], signaling openness to overturning both rulings altogether.[2] The decisions being reheard — one finding that the voter ID law discriminated against voters of color, the other reaffirming the court's order to redraw election maps — had been issued only about six weeks earlier by an outgoing Democratic majority.[2] Rehearing is rare in the state: such a petition had been granted only twice in the previous thirty years.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] argued that the outgoing justices had legislated from the bench against the wishes of North Carolina residents and that the likely reversal showed the practical consequence of the November 2022 judicial elections.[2]
