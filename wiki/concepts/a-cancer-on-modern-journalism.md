# A Cancer on Modern Journalism

*[[a-cancer-on-modern-journalism|A Cancer on Modern Journalism]]* is a November 29, 2023 Badlands Media article by Ryan DeLarme that frames the practices of left-leaning media watchdog organizations — particularly [[Media Matters]] — as a central pillar of a Western censorship regime that emerged as a direct result of both the Brexit vote and the rise of [[Donald Trump]]. The article's subtitle is "The Deceitful and Manipulative Practices of Media 'Watchdogs.'"

## Core Thesis

The source argues that the censorship regime that has emerged in the West is "among the most powerful weapons utilized by the ruling class," and that Media Matters is "one of the primary aspects of this journalistic cancer." The article asserts that the regime aims to "control and manipulate public discourse by suppressing alternative viewpoints and dissenting voices," not only stifling free speech but also "perpetuating a narrative that serves the interests of the deep political establishment, further consolidating their power and influence."

A network of billionaire-funded information gatekeepers, the source contends, has "thoroughly and disastrously marred civil discourse in the West, creating a climate of fear around acknowledging the truth."

## David Brock's Transformation

A significant portion of the article is devoted to the biography of [[David Brock]], Media Matters' founder. The source traces Brock's journey from a conservative operative who defended Clarence Thomas and instigated "Troopergate" while criticizing the Clintons, to a steadfast Clinton supporter whose conversion the article describes as "astounding, yet confounding." The piece examines Brock's books *The Seduction of Hillary Rodham* and *Blinded by the Right* (2002), as well as the skeptical reception his transformation received from conservative outlets such as National Review.

## Correct the Record and Digital Trolling

The article documents Brock's creation of [[Correct the Record]], a superPAC that spent approximately $1 million to "find and confront social media users who post unflattering messages about" Hillary Clinton. The *Los Angeles Times* is quoted as describing the effort as one that "aims to spend a large sum of money to increase the amount of trolling that already exists online."

## Media Matters' Methodology and the 2023 Smear Campaigns

The source focuses extensively on Media Matters' November 2023 study claiming that X was placing ads for major brands next to neo-Nazi and antisemitic content. The article alleges that Media Matters staff achieved this by going to "the most remote fringes of the platform" and refreshing feeds until ads appeared beside obscure posts, then taking screenshots. The source notes that the obscure posts had virtually no views, and that "the only human who saw the instance of this video with the Netflix ad" was a Media Matters employee, with an average watch time of one minute and ten seconds.

The article draws a parallel to an earlier Media Matters campaign against [[Rumble]] in March 2023, which claimed Netflix ads were appearing next to Holocaust denial videos. Traffic data is cited showing that the video in question received zero views until the Media Matters article was published, at which point views rose to five and peaked at thirty — all of which the source attributes directly to the Media Matters smear.

## The Thermonuclear Lawsuit and Investigation

The article reports that [[Elon Musk]] filed a "thermonuclear lawsuit" against Media Matters in response to the November 2023 study. Rumble founder [[Chris Pavlovski]] announced that Rumble would "go thermonuclear as well." Texas Attorney General [[Ken Paxton]] opened an investigation into Media Matters for potential fraudulent activity.

The source expresses hope that other institutions — including the Poynter Institute, the [[Anti-Defamation League]], the Southern Poverty Law Center, and the Center for Countering Digital Hate — will "all receive their own day of legal judgment."

## Framing Within the Censorship Regime

The article positions Media Matters as part of a broader network that includes the ADL, the International Fact-Checking Network, and legacy media outlets such as the Washington Post and the New York Times. The source argues that these organizations have shifted from being accurately described as "liberal or Democratic party activist groups" to being treated "as if they were the preeminent scholars of our time, incapable of folly in their relentless pursuit of objective truth."

This shift is framed as a direct result of the advent of Donald J. Trump and the America First contingent, which prompted the International Fact-Checking Network and legacy media to enter an "unholy merger" designed to combat anti-establishment populism. The article identifies supporters of [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], Cornel West, and Vivek Ramaswamy as part of the expanded pool of targets.

## The Playbook and Its Consequences

The source identifies two primary tactics used by media watchdogs: labeling anything that detracts from establishment liberalism as racist, homophobic, antisemitic, white-nationalist, or transphobic; and conducting dubious "studies" that are then used to support partisan claims. The article argues that these organizations "capitalize on" the fact that "a large bulk of the population will believe anything you write so long as it's preceded by the words 'study finds.'"

The article concludes that Media Matters and similar organizations are "symptoms of journalism's slow, agonizing death" and "late-stage liberalism at its finest," whose orthodoxy "seeks to liberate us from the burden of having our own thoughts."
