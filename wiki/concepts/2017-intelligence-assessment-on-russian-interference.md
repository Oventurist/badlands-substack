# 2017 Intelligence Assessment on Russian Interference

The 2017 intelligence assessment on Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. election concluded that [[russia|Russia]] interfered in order to help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defeat [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[1]

## Production and disputed content

The assessment was produced under then-[[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], and the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] was briefly referenced in it, with a summary attached to the classified version.[1] Brennan has said the CIA opposed including the dossier and that it was added only as a compromise with the [[fbi|FBI]].[1]

## Criminal investigation

By May 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] was investigating Brennan's role in producing the assessment, and the FBI had begun interviewing current and former CIA officers about it.[1] Agents from the Miami field office questioned roughly a dozen CIA employees who worked on the assessment at CIA headquarters in McLean, Virginia, with more interviews expected in following weeks.[1] The probe examines whether Brennan made false statements to [[congress|Congress]] in 2023 testimony about the assessment.[1] The matter was referred to the DOJ by Republican Representative [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] in October and is overseen by [[joe-digenova|Joe DiGenova]] after the previous lead prosecutor was removed.[1]

## Miami grand jury investigation (December 2025)

By December 2025 the assessment was the focus of a criminal grand jury investigation in Miami, with U.S. Attorney [[jason-reding-quinones|Jason Reding Quinones]] informing lawyers for former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] that the grand jury was examining Brennan's role in producing it.[2] The probe centers on the assessment's claim that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] sought to help President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a conclusion later challenged by whistleblower evidence released by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], which showed internal dissent and questioned the report's rushed release.[2] Those disclosures also raised concerns about Brennan's push to include the discredited [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] in the assessment.[2]
