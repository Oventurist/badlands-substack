# 2023 Car Dealer Letter on EV Mandates

In late November 2023, nearly 4,000 United States car dealers asked President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to slow proposed emissions regulations designed to ensure that two-thirds of new passenger cars sold would be all-electric by 2032, arguing that consumers were simply not interested and that electric vehicles were stacking up on their lots.[1]

## The letter

A total of 3,882 franchised car dealers from all 50 states sent the letter on Tuesday, November 28, 2023, urging the administration to slow down its EV mandates.[1] The signatories organized under the name "EV Voice of the Consumer," and argued that neighborhood dealers are the best barometer of consumer sentiment and were sounding the alarm about a widening mismatch between EV supply and customer demand.[1]

"Last year, there was a lot of hope and hype about EVs," the dealers wrote, describing demand from early adopters, "but that enthusiasm has stalled. Today, the supply of unsold BEVs (battery electric vehicles) is surging, as they are not selling nearly as fast as they are arriving at our dealerships — even with deep price cuts, manufacturer incentives, and generous government incentives."[1] They added that "with each passing day, it becomes more apparent that this attempted electric vehicle mandate is unrealistic based on current and forecasted customer demand," with unsold inventory serving as their best indicator of demand in the marketplace.[1] The letter was reported by [[axios|Axios]].[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that in a free market EVs would be tanking and automakers would be scrambling to serve their customers, but that under what she called "global corporate communism" the market does not have the right to say no to EVs.[1] In her framing, the "carbon monster" gives policymakers a manufactured mandate to force consumers into their market, removing any incentive to compete in a free one.[1]

She predicted the [[white-house|White House]] would spin the data and change the metrics to claim EV demand is growing and that green is the future, while argued that discerning consumers do not want their transportation dependent on the electrical grid.[1] She further speculated that government at every level would begin buying up EVs to inflate demand figures, and advised readers to secure an older, reliable gasoline-powered used vehicle before such vehicles are banned.[1]
