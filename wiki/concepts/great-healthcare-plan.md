# Great Healthcare Plan

The "Great Healthcare Plan" is a healthcare policy proposal announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in January 2026. The [[white-house|White House]] described it as a plan to lower healthcare costs, increase price transparency, and deliver direct financial benefits to individuals, including through mechanisms that would return savings to patients rather than to insurers or intermediaries.[1]

## Core proposal

The central mechanism described by Trump was direct payment of federal healthcare money to individuals rather than to corporate intermediaries. "Instead of putting the needs of big corporations and special interests first, our plan finally puts you first and puts more money in your pocket," he said, adding that "the government is going to pay the money directly to you. It goes to you, and then you take the money and buy your own healthcare. Nobody's ever heard of that before."[1]

Trump claimed the approach had eluded four decades of prior attempts, saying that "for 40 years they've been trying to do it, but they never were able to do it. No other president was able to do it. I got every other country to approve it," and attributing the result to leverage from tariffs and "other things."[1]

## Drug pricing

On prescription drugs, Trump said prices "will come way, way down" and that Americans would pay "the lowest price in the world," contrasting this with a prior situation in which "you were paying the highest price in the world by far, and the politicians did nothing about it."[1]

## Criticism of the Affordable Care Act

Trump framed the plan against the Affordable Care Act, asserting that "Obamacare was designed to make insurance companies rich." He referred to it as "the Unaffordable Care Act, with billions of dollars in taxpayer subsidies that helped their stock prices skyrocket over 1,700% as you paid more money for healthcare every single year."[1]

## Status

The White House article announcing the proposal did not include legislative text, regulatory filings, or a timeline for congressional consideration.[1] Badlands coverage grouped the announcement under the "MAHA" banner associated with the administration's broader health agenda.[1]
