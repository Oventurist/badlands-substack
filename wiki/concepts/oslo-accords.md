# Oslo Accords

The Oslo Accords are the framework agreements governing Israeli and Palestinian Authority jurisdiction over the West Bank, and they remain the legal baseline against which Israeli administrative changes in the territory are measured.[1]

## 2026 relevance

The Israeli Security Cabinet's February 2026 [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank measures]] — shifting building authority in Hebron, facilitating settler land purchases, repealing restrictions on non-Muslim real estate transactions, and expanding Israeli enforcement into Palestinian-controlled areas — were described as violating the Oslo Accords and drew U.S. concern over regional stability.[1] Under the Accords' division of authority, land in the West Bank is registered with the Palestinian Authority; the new measures would instead route registration through the Israeli government.[1]

## Origins and subversion

The Oslo Accords were signed in 1993 as a framework for a two-state solution: the Palestinian side would immediately recognize the State of Israel, and in exchange the Israel Defense Forces would implement a five-year plan to incrementally withdraw from Gaza and the West Bank, culminating in recognition of Palestine as a nation.[2] The Israeli prime minister who signed the agreement, [[yitzhak-rabin|Yitzhak Rabin]], a retired IDF general, was assassinated by a Jewish extremist, a killing that Badlands commentary credits with enabling the rise of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[2] Netanyahu has said that after coming to power in 1996 he intentionally subverted the Accords and ensured that the promised troop drawdown never occurred.[2]

## Smotrich's cancellation push (February 2026)

In February 2026 far-right Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] announced that the next Israeli government must cancel the Oslo Accords outright, extend Israeli sovereignty across the West Bank, and encourage Palestinians to leave both the West Bank and the Gaza Strip.[2] Speaking at a gathering of his [[religious-zionism-party|Religious Zionism party]], he described these steps as essential to foreclose any possibility of a Palestinian state and insisted no other lasting solution to the conflict exists.[2] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the announcement as a moment when "the mask has finally been lifted," presenting it as evidence of a supremacist current in Israeli politics operating alongside inflammatory rhetoric from allied American politicians such as [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]].[2]

## Signing and the Abbas gesture

Badlands commentary published in May 2024 recounts that the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] was established in 1993 with the signing of the Oslo Accords — carrying the promise of a two-state solution — between Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and Palestine Liberation Organization leader [[yasser-arafat|Yasser Arafat]], with the peace talks first initiated by the then-new Russian Federation.[3]

While Arafat is given the credit for the Accords, the account notes that footage of the signing ceremonies at the Clinton White House shows that it was not Arafat but a young [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] who signed on behalf of Palestine.[3] This was read as a symbolic gesture by Arafat and the Arabs toward the West, promising that the PLO — after decades of bloody conflict with Israel — would be riding off into the sunset, allowing the new, peace-seeking Palestinian Authority to govern Palestine and make peace with its neighbors.[3] In the 31 years since, Abbas and the [[fatah|Fatah]] faction maintained a consistent message of a two-state solution.[3]

## Netanyahu's subversion and its consequences

Badlands commentary holds that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was pushed out of office as Israeli prime minister in the late 1990s largely because of his subversion of the Oslo Accords, and that [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] began working with him in 2000 to plot the political comeback that followed.[4]
