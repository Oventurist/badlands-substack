# 5:20 Bonds

The **5:20 bonds** were U.S. government bonds issued during the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] under the financial program of President [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], so named because of their five-to-twenty-year maturation.[1]

Citizens purchased the bonds as investments in their nation's survival. Uniting "personal self interest" with the general welfare of the nation, the bonds provided loans to manufacturing and also served as the basis for the issuance of more [[greenbacks|greenbacks]].[1] They were organized by Lincoln's ally [[jay-cooke|Jay Cooke]], a patriotic Philadelphia banker, who sold them in small denominations to average citizens so that ordinary people had a vested interest in directly participating in saving their nation.[1]

Between 1862 and 1865, the bonds accounted for $1.3 billion.[1] Lincoln described the result by saying that the patriotism of the people had placed at the disposal of the government the large means demanded by the public exigencies, with much of the national loan taken by citizens of the industrial classes whose confidence in their country's faith had induced them to contribute the whole of their limited acquisitions.[1]

Together with the greenbacks, the 5:20 bonds financed not only the arming, feeding and payment of soldiers but also the large-scale industrial and rail programs begun during the war, including the [[first-transcontinental-railroad|transcontinental railway]].[1]
