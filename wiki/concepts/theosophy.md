# Theosophy

Theosophy is a modern esoteric movement that [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies as a key vector for the spread of [[protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion|*The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion*]] in late Imperial Russia and beyond.[1]

The Russian mystic [[yuliana-glinka|Yuliana Glinka]] was a Theosophist and a close colleague of the occultist [[gerard-encausse|Gerard Encausse]] (Papus), himself an early member of the Theosophists as well as of the Hermetic Order of Light, the Rite of Memphis-Misraim, and the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn.[1] The Russian publicist [[mikhail-menshikov|Mikhail Menshikov]], who first published the Protocols in 1902, recounted that Glinka inducted him into "the mysteries of Theosophy" before initiating him into the mysteries of the Protocols — placing the forgery's transmission squarely inside a Theosophical milieu.[1]

Ehret situates Theosophy within a broader current of pagan occultism hostile to Judaism and Christianity, arguing that occultists have felt immense hatred toward Jews "from the most remote evidence of ancient antiquity to the present day" precisely because the Old Testament is replete with interventions against occultism and human sacrifice.[1] Theosophically-influenced writers such as [[alice-bailey|Alice Bailey]], [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]], and [[nesta-webster|Nesta Webster]] are named among the promoters of "Jewish bankers' conspiracies" narratives.[1]
