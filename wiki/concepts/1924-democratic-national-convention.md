# 1924 Democratic National Convention

The 1924 Democratic National Convention is referred to in the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series by its popular nickname, the "Klan Bake."[1]

In Chapter 20, published October 5, 2024, the author reproduces a graphic pairing an image from the 1924 convention with a photograph of [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] signing a law honouring the Confederacy, and characterises the convention as "basically a huge KKK Rally."[1] The juxtaposition is offered in support of the chapter's reading of Q drop #122, which asks why liberals "prefer racism w/o proof": the author's answer is that the accusation is a projection, and that the Democratic Party formed the [[ku-klux-klan|Ku Klux Klan]] and the Confederacy while Republicans freed the slaves.[1] The same passage identifies [[robert-byrd|Robert Byrd]] as [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s mentor, presenting the lineage as the point Q was making.[1]

The chapter argues that the practice of "playing the racist card" functions to deflect attention from what the author regards as the accusers' own record.[1] It further contends that overt segregation of restaurants, schools and buses has been replaced by a [[deep-state|Deep State]] strategy of classism, citing accounts of crack cocaine and weapons being funnelled into low-income neighbourhoods by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] during the early 1980s in order to keep that section of society at war with itself.[1] The author adds that similar methods of division have been applied to other populations, but that many people are now waking up to the deceit.[1]
