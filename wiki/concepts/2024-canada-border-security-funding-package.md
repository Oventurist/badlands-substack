# 2024 Canada Border Security Funding Package

The 2024 Canada border security funding package was a commitment by the government of [[canada|Canada]] to spend C$1.3 billion (about US$913 million) over six years to strengthen its border, announced in December 2024 after President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened to impose a 25% tariff on all Canadian imports for allowing migrants and fentanyl to flow into the United States.[1]

ZeroHedge framed the move as the "Trump Effect," reporting that Canada's government was simultaneously imploding — Finance Minister Chrystia Freeland had quit while warning Prime Minister Justin Trudeau that Trump's tariffs represented a "grave challenge."[1]

The funds were directed to Public Safety Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency, the Communications Security Establishment, and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.[1] Few specifics were disclosed, though roughly C$81 million was to be spent on border security measures before the end of March, with that amount tripling annually by 2027 according to Global News.[1] The government had previously promised to expand the number of personnel, helicopters and drones assigned to monitor the border but provided no detailed plan at the time of the announcement.[1] More than 23,000 migrants were apprehended by U.S. Border Patrol along the northern border in the twelve-month period ending in October 2024, more than double the previous year.[1]
