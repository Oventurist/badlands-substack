# Colorado BIOS Password Scandal

The **Colorado BIOS password scandal** refers to the exposure of more than 600 BIOS-level passwords for voting equipment in 62 of Colorado's 64 counties on a public website maintained by the Colorado Department of State (CDOS) under Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]]. The exposure spanned the 2024 primary and was still live during early voting in the 2024 general election.

## The cover-up allegation

According to [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who has reported on the episode at length, a deputy secretary of state was recorded saying the department would not inform county clerks about the exposure because doing so would cause a media storm. CDOS instead told both clerks and the public that no breach had occurred.

Her argument turns that assurance against the state's own security model. Colorado's official account of election integrity holds that a breach requires physical access to machines, and the machines are held in the counties. If CDOS never worked with the clerks to investigate — which the deputy secretary's recorded remarks concede — then the department could not have known whether physical access occurred, unless it possessed some remote means of determining machine status. Remote determination would in turn contradict the air-gap story on which the security model rests. The remaining possibility, in her framing, is simply that officials lied and mounted a cover story once caught, prioritizing the appearance of "gold standard elections" over election security itself.

## Relation to the declassified NIC memo

The Badlands Brief of April 22, 2026 paired the Colorado episode with the declassified January 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo on voting-infrastructure vulnerabilities, arguing that officials issuing reassurances in 2020 and afterward would have known their talking points to be false. In this reading the two episodes describe one pattern: documented vulnerability internally, categorical denial publicly.
