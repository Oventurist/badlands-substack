# Nvidia H20 China Sales Resumption

The Nvidia H20 China sales resumption refers to the July 2025 reversal of a U.S. export restriction that had barred [[nvidia|Nvidia]] from selling its H20 artificial intelligence graphics processing unit to Chinese customers, and the linkage of that reversal to American negotiations over Chinese rare earth exports.[1]

## Background

The export restriction was imposed in April 2025 and was designed to keep the most advanced AI chips out of Chinese hands on national security grounds — an objective that had attracted rare bipartisan support in Congress.[1] The H20 was a China-specific variant engineered to fall below earlier performance thresholds set by U.S. export controls.

## Reversal and rare earths linkage

Late on Monday, July 14, 2025, Nvidia said it was filing applications with the U.S. government to resume H20 sales to China and had been assured it would receive the licenses soon.[1] The announcement came days after Nvidia's chief executive met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] said on Tuesday, July 15 that the planned resumption was part of U.S. negotiations on rare earths.[1] "We put that in the trade deal with the magnets," Lutnick said, referring to an agreement Trump had reached to restart rare earth shipments to U.S. manufacturers, though he did not provide additional detail.[1] The reversal drew swift questions and criticism from U.S. legislators.[1] The episode is tied to the broader dispute over [[china-rare-earth-export-restrictions-2025]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the move as part of a deliberate rebalancing of global trade in favor of American workers while simultaneously igniting domestic innovation and manufacturing, calling it "a double whammy for globalists" and arguing that Trump "isn't reacting to the market" but "creating a new one."[1]
