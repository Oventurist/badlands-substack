# White House Election Integrity Portal

The White House Election Integrity Portal is an online repository launched by the [[white-house|White House]] on Thursday, July 16, 2026, containing declassified intelligence assessments, investigative materials and other documents related to election security, voter registration and foreign threats.[1]

## Contents
According to Badlands' summary of the release, the documents show that Intelligence Community officials knew of significant vulnerabilities in electronic voting infrastructure, foreign cyber capabilities targeting election systems, blackmail schemes, and more, but that intelligence agencies withheld the information from both President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the public.[1] Among the released materials are documents concerning China's acquisition of large amounts of U.S. voter data, alleged foreign cyber threats to election infrastructure, and FBI materials relating to a Michigan voter-registration investigation.[1]

## Announcement and reception
Trump announced the declassification effort during a much-anticipated televised primetime address that network television refused to air, arguing that the documents demonstrate a broader pattern of intelligence suppression surrounding election security issues.[1] Administration officials, including in prior statements from the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] under [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], have separately alleged that earlier Intelligence Community processes were influenced by politicized or flawed intelligence products in other matters, including the 2019 impeachment inquiry.[1] Critics dispute the White House's interpretation, arguing the released material does not establish that foreign actors altered vote totals or changed the outcome of the 2020 election.[1]

## Badlands commentary
[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said the primetime address likely felt like reruns for the Badlands audience but described herself as extremely encouraged, noting that [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] was posting findings from his document review to social media.[1] Herold's first-glance summation described intelligence held by the [[cia|CIA]] detailing how China tried to defeat Trump: paying U.S. journalists to write more negative stories about him; inflicting negative financial consequences on states that voted for him by levying tariffs on their exports to China; inviting people on all-expenses-paid trips to China to get them "hooked on China" so they could later be hit "in the wallet" and pressured to lobby Trump on tariffs; and applying pressure to financial supporters of his re-election campaign.[1]

Ashe wrote that the Chinese influence operation aimed at the business community was new information to her, and connected it to Molly Ball's TIME account of how "corporate America turned on" Trump after the 2020 election, with hundreds of major business leaders who had backed his candidacy calling on him to concede.[1] She argued the public-private campaign of narratives, networks, corporations, NGOs and government officials described in that article was neither virtuous nor organic.[1] Framing the stakes, she asked what it means for the Intelligence Community to withhold foreign threat information from the President during a crisis Trump described as worse than Pearl Harbor and 9/11 involving that same foreign threat, and concluded that there can be no real elections without honesty and accountability for how fake they have been.[1]
