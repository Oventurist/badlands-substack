# White House Election Integrity Portal

The White House Election Integrity Portal is a public document repository launched by the [[white-house]] in July 2026 containing declassified intelligence assessments, investigative materials, and other records relating to election security, voter registration, and foreign threats to American elections.

## Launch

As reported in the Badlands Brief of July 17, 2026, President [[donald-trump]] announced the declassification effort during a televised prime-time address that network television declined to carry. The administration presented the portal as evidence that officials within the Intelligence Community had knowledge of significant vulnerabilities in electronic voting infrastructure, of foreign cyber capabilities targeting election systems, and of blackmail schemes — and that these agencies withheld the information from both the President and the public.

## Contents

Among the released materials are documents concerning [[china]]'s acquisition of large volumes of US voter data, assessments of alleged foreign cyber threats to election infrastructure, and [[fbi]] materials relating to a Michigan voter-registration investigation. Administration officials, including the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence]] under [[tulsi-gabbard]], have separately alleged that prior Intelligence Community processes were shaped by politicized or flawed intelligence products in other matters, including the [[first-trump-impeachment|2019 impeachment inquiry]].

Reviewing the documents, [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] highlighted [[cia]] reporting on Chinese efforts against Trump's re-election: paying US journalists to write more negative stories; levying tariffs on exports from states that voted for Trump in order to inflict targeted financial pain; offering all-expenses-paid trips to China to get influential figures "hooked" so they could later be squeezed "in the wallet"; and applying pressure to financial backers of the Trump campaign.

## Dispute

Critics dispute the White House's interpretation, arguing the released material does not establish that foreign actors altered vote totals or changed the outcome of the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election]].

## Badlands framing

[[ashe-in-america]] connected the Chinese pressure campaign on the business community to the [[time-magazine]] account by Molly Ball of the post-2020 "shadow campaign," in which hundreds of corporate leaders who had backed Trump publicly urged him to concede. Where that account presented the public-private coalition of corporations, NGOs, and officials as organic and virtuous, the Badlands reading treats it as coordinated and, in light of the declassified material, at least partly foreign-influenced. Her conclusion: "We can't have real elections until there is honesty and accountability for how fake they've been."
