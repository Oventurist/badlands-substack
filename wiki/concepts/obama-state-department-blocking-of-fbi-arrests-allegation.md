# Obama State Department Blocking of FBI Arrests Allegation

The **Obama State Department blocking of FBI arrests allegation** refers to whistleblower claims, reported in May 2024, that the Obama-Biden [[state-department|State Department]] "actively interfered" to prevent the [[fbi|FBI]] from executing arrest warrants against individuals unlawfully present in the United States who were allegedly supporting Iranian financial efforts to develop weapons of mass destruction.[1]

## Congressional letters

Whistleblowers brought the claims to Sens. [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] (R-Iowa) and [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] (R-Wis.), who sent letters on the matter to Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]], and Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]]; Fox News Digital obtained the correspondence.[1]

## Context

The Obama-Biden administration began its Joint Plan of Action, the negotiating process that produced the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action]] — the Iran nuclear deal signed by President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] in 2015.[1] At the time Obama stated that broader sanctions would remain in place and that his administration would "continue to enforce... vigorously."[1] For decades under both Republican and Democratic administrations, the United States had imposed sanctions on "Iranian individuals, companies, and organizations for involvement in nuclear proliferation, ballistic missile development, support for terrorist groups, and human rights abuses."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the United States built [[iran|Iran]]'s first nuclear reactor in 1967 — the five-megawatt Tehran Research Reactor, initially fueled with roughly 93 percent enriched uranium supplied by the US and converted to 20 percent in 1993.[1] He added that in 1974 Shah [[reza-pahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]] established the Atomic Energy Organization of Iran and announced plans for roughly 23,000 megawatts of generation over 20 years, including 23 nuclear plants and a full fuel cycle, with full public US support.[1]

He characterized the Iran nuclear deal as "more of a money-laundering operation than anything else," arguing that pallets of cash were shipped under cover of darkness precisely to avoid the transaction logs a bank wire would generate, and that Obama's circle blocked FBI arrests because the targets were their Iranian accomplices in looting the US Treasury.[1]

The commentary highlighted an email quoted in the reporting stating that a Trump election win was affecting business in Iran — a Japanese bank had backed off handling funds for a $5 million licensing agreement, unofficially citing the coming Trump presidency, with European banks reportedly feeling the same way — and noted that former FBI official [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] was copied on that email.[1] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry further observed that Strzok grew up in Iran while his father served three tours there with the Army Corps of Engineers.[1]
