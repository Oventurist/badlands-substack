# 2025 Supreme Court EPA Venue Cases

On March 25, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] heard oral arguments in a set of cases addressing where challenges to federal environmental rules must be litigated.[1] The dispute concerned whether certain [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]] regulations should be reviewed exclusively in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit — described in reporting as having more liberal judges — or in regional circuit courts, where certain states and industry groups would prefer to argue.[1] The Court heard two oral arguments covering three EPA cases, with two of the cases consolidated into a single argument.[1]

## Oral argument

Justice Neil Gorsuch questioned the premise that states should bring challenges in regional courts, observing that "[air pollution] crosses the country in ways that don't respect our jurisdictional boundaries between circuits."[1] He warned that regional venue could produce conflicting rulings: "We're going to have different interpretations of the statute with different — different circuits and all these terrible splits and — and, gosh, we won't have the immediate resolution of the D.C. Circuit that we could have."[1] Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson emphasized that state-specific factors informed each EPA denial, noting "It matters that you are Denver versus, you know, Arizona or whatever in terms of how the modeling works."[1]

## Political context

The arguments coincided with Republican calls to rein in what critics described as rogue courts, and with congressional discussion of reforming the district courts by statute — a power grounded in Congress's authority to create and abolish lower federal courts.[1] See [[mike-johnson-proposal-to-eliminate-federal-courts]].

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that all of the arguments focused on the authority of the D.C. courts in matters of national interest, and observed that several justices engaged with a "concerning level of support" for D.C. venue, leaving the outcome uncertain.[1] The same coverage placed the EPA arguments alongside the consolidated Louisiana redistricting cases and [[riley-v-bondi|Riley v. Bondi]] in that week's Supreme Court docket, and previewed the following day's argument on [[universal-service-fund|FCC universal service]] subsidies and the [[nondelegation-doctrine|nondelegation doctrine]].[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried live coverage of the hearings on its Rumble channel.[1]
