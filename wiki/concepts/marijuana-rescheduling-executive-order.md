# Marijuana Rescheduling Executive Order

On Thursday, December 18, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order downgrading cannabis from the most restrictive category of federally controlled drugs, easing some limitations and allowing for expanded research.[1]

## Provisions

The order moves marijuana from Schedule I — the same category as heroin — to Schedule III, the category that includes ketamine.[1] It does not decriminalize or legalize marijuana as some states have done, and according to senior administration officials speaking on condition of anonymity, it would not affect law enforcement's approach to making arrests connected to marijuana.[1] The order additionally authorizes a pilot program to reimburse Medicare patients for products made with CBD, the non-psychoactive cannabis compound.[1]

Announcing the order from the Oval Office, Trump said, "We have people begging for me to do this, people that are in great pain for decades," adding that "It doesn't legalize marijuana in any way, shape or form, and in no way sanctions its use as a recreational drug."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[canncon|CannCon]] welcomed the order enthusiastically and dedicated the moment to the late veteran advocate [[boone-cutler|Boone Cutler]], writing "I wish Boone Cutler were here to see this one. Fair winds, brother."[1]

[[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] framed the reclassification as a convergence of the MAGA and MAHA movements, quoting the administration's argument that "The facts compel the federal government to recognize that marijuana can be legitimate as far as medical applications," alongside a request that [[us-congress|Congress]] reconsider its classification of hemp-derived CBD products.[1]
