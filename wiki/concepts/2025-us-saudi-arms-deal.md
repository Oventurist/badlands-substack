# 2025 US–Saudi Arms Deal

The 2025 US–Saudi arms deal is a landmark $142 billion defense sales agreement between the United States and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], unveiled by the [[white-house|White House]] on May 13, 2025 and described by the administration as "the largest defense sales agreement in history."[1]

## Contents

According to a White House fact sheet, the United States would supply Saudi Arabia with "state-of-the-art war-fighting equipment and services" drawn from more than a dozen American defense contractors.[1] The [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration did not detail the agreement beyond sketching five key areas: advancing air force and space capabilities; strengthening air and missile defense systems; bolstering maritime and coastal security; modernizing border protection and ground forces; and upgrading information and communications technology.[1]

## Constraint by Israel's Qualitative Military Edge

Analysts told The Times of Israel that the deal's potential to reshape Middle Eastern defense would likely be limited by a longstanding US doctrine safeguarding [[israel|Israel]]'s regional military superiority.[1] Speculation about the package has centered on Saudi requests to buy [[f-35|F-35]] fighter jets, but such a sale would run afoul of the US commitment to Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|Qualitative Military Edge]] (QME), which has long curtailed transfers of certain advanced weaponry to the region.[1] A similar dynamic followed the roughly $110 billion arms agreement of 2017, which took the form of commitments rather than concrete deals; Riyadh obtained substantial munitions but no F-35s.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the episode highlights an inconvenient old Washington commitment, tracing the QME to the Foreign Military Sales Act of 1968, the [[arms-export-control-act|Arms Export Control Act]] of 1976, and the 2008 amendment enacted via the Naval Vessel Transfer Act.[1] He contended that the special US–Israel relationship now impedes American diplomacy and underwrites a de facto unipolar order requiring global US military presence, and that a genuine multipolar policy of bringing troops home would require repealing the QME and empowering allies such as Saudi Arabia to police their own region.[1]
