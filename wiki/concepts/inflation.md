# Inflation

In Badlands Media commentary, inflation is described not as a naturally occurring economic phenomenon but as "the biggest financial scam of them all" — a deliberate transfer of purchasing power created by money printing at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The housing example

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] illustrates the mechanism with a home purchase. The Federal Reserve prints money and gives it to banks to lend; this dilutes the purchasing power of existing money, driving house prices up; the bank then lends that money to the buyer at interest.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The politicians are paid by the banks, the banks receive free money to loan out, and the home buyer pays for both — through the higher price caused by inflation and through the loan interest.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Banks are also able to write larger loans precisely because the Fed's printing pushed prices higher.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Carlson concludes that "Bernie Madoff is a lightweight compared to these Ponzi schemers."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Total tax burden

Counting inflation as "the biggest tax of them all" alongside income, property, sales and capital gains taxes, Carlson estimates the average American is left with roughly 30 to 40 cents of every dollar earned to spend as they wish — an effective burden nearer 60–70% rather than the 15% people believe they pay.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The Covid-era printing

Carlson argues that when the Covid lockdowns were imposed in early 2020, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] forced the Fed to print money at an unprecedented rate to save the economy, knowing this would create massive inflation, and that a large portion of subsequent inflation traces to that decision.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He frames it as the lesser of two evils and as "fighting the Cabal with their own weapon," given a Cabal plan to destroy the American economy and keep the country locked down for years.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Why Trump is not blamed

Carlson observes that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] does not seriously accuse Trump of creating the inflation, because doing so would draw attention to the underlying scheme and reveal where inflation actually comes from.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Instead the media downplayed inflation, telling the public "they weren't really experiencing what they were experiencing."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The [[cabal|Cabal]] requires the public to believe inflation is naturally occurring and not the product of printing money backed by nothing: "Who is going to continue to play at a roulette table if they know the wheel has a magnet, or play craps if they know the dice are loaded?"[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Estimates under Biden

Returning to the theme in April 2024, Carlson called inflation "the biggest tax of all" — the tax that comes from the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] printing money out of nothing, backed by nothing, diluting the purchasing power of money already in circulation.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He assumed that real inflation since [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took office was far higher than reported, possibly topping out near 30% cumulatively, which he averaged to about 10% a year for the purpose of estimating how little of a dollar survives taxation.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) Because the government can print money whenever it wishes, he argued, it does not need tax revenue at all and can simply pass the resulting inflation on to the public.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)
