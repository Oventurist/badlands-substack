# Four Freedoms

The Four Freedoms are the universal principles outlined by Franklin Roosevelt in 1941: freedom from want, freedom from fear of war, freedom of speech, and freedom of conscience.[1]

## Role in the post-war order

The Four Freedoms are described as setting the backbone of the spirit of the Atlantic Charter — in its form prior to later Biden and Johnson edits — of the [[united-nations|UN Charter]], and of the original designs of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]].[1] The same universal freedoms were restated in the UN Declaration of Human Rights of 1947 and in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence that emerged from the 1955 Bandung Conference, which set the tone for the Non-Aligned Movement.[1]

## Contemporary relevance

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] proposes that an emergency summit convened before any nuclear war should be shaped on solving objective problems all people can agree on, as expressed in the Four Freedoms, rather than on an anti-humanist [[great-reset|Great Reset]] or transhumanist new world order.[1] He notes the irony that FDR's Four Freedoms are now being advanced by Eurasian powers of the [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]], considered by many to be the most "un-American" groups on earth.[1]
