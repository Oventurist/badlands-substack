# Four Freedoms

The Four Freedoms are the universal aims articulated by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] in 1941. As enumerated in the Badlands Media article A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation, they are freedom from want, freedom from fear of war, freedom of speech and freedom of conscience.

## Significance in the source

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that these freedoms are universal and set "the backbone" of the spirit of the [[atlantic-charter|Atlantic Charter]] in its original form, the [[un-charter|UN Charter]], and the original design of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]]. He adds that the same freedoms were restated in the UN Declaration of Human Rights of 1947 and in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence that emerged from the [[bandung-conference|Bandung Conference]] of 1955, which in turn set the tone for the [[non-aligned-movement|Non-Aligned Movement]].

The article proposes the Four Freedoms as the proper agenda for an emergency world summit convened before rather than after a nuclear war, in place of what it calls an anti-humanist [[great-reset|Great Reset]]. Ehret concludes that the irony of the Four Freedoms now being advanced by the powers of the [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]] "should not be lost on anyone."
