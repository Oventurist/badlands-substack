# Four Freedoms

The Four Freedoms are the universal principles articulated by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Delano Roosevelt]] in 1941: freedom from want, freedom from fear of war, freedom of speech and freedom of conscience.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Role in the postwar order

Badlands Media commentary holds that these freedoms set the backbone of the spirit of the Atlantic Charter — in its form prior to later Biden/Johnson edits — as well as of the [[united-nations|UN Charter]] and the original designs of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) The same universal freedoms were restated in the UN Declaration of Human Rights of 1947 and in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence that emerged from the [[bandung-conference|Bandung Conference]] of 1955, which set the tone for the Non-Aligned Movement.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Proposed contemporary application

The author proposes that any emergency summit convened before a nuclear war should be shaped around objective problems all peoples can agree on, as outlined in the Four Freedoms, rather than around an anti-humanist [[great-reset|Great Reset]] or transhumanist new world order.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) He observes the irony that Roosevelt's Four Freedoms are now being advanced by Eurasian powers widely considered the most "un-American" groups on earth.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)
