# 2024 HTS Aleppo offensive

The **2024 HTS Aleppo offensive** was the late-November 2024 assault by jihadist insurgents led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS) on government-held territory in northern [[syria|Syria]], which captured the central and northwestern parts of Aleppo, the country's largest northern city.[1]

## Course of the offensive

After taking central Aleppo, HTS forces were reported to be advancing on the west-central city of Hama, with HTS sources claiming to have captured six towns and villages in the countryside including Morek, which lies on an important highway connecting central Syria to the north.[1] The Syrian government denied that many of these localities had fallen, amid conflicting social media reports.[1] The insurgents also claimed control of Aleppo's international airport, long a key regional hub, while Russian and Syrian airstrikes pounded the occupied central districts — the first major aerial bombardments of the city since anti-Assad insurgents were driven out in 2016.[1]

HTS, formerly known as Jabhat al-Nusra, attacked alongside a collection of allied militias, breaking a fragile truce brokered by [[russia|Russia]] and [[turkey|Türkiye]] in 2020.[1]

## Iranian response

Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] called the offensive "an American-Zionist plot" in a phone call with his Syrian counterpart Bassam al-Sabbagh, holding the United States and Israel responsible for the resurgence of Salafist terrorism in Syria.[1] According to Iran's PressTV, Araghchi noted the attack came immediately after Israel struck a ceasefire deal with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon, suggesting Washington and West Jerusalem were using HTS as proxies against a Syrian government that supports the Palestinian cause, and said [[iran|Iran]] would continue to support the "Syrian government, nation, and army towards fighting terrorism."[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued Syria would be the stage for the "final battle" of both the escalating Middle East conflict and possibly the Russia–[[ukraine|Ukraine]] war, citing claims that Ukrainian special forces were in-country training [[islamic-state|ISIS]] to operate against [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]]'s government.[1] He treated the Iranian statement as reinforcing the long-standing claim — voiced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] among others — that ISIS was a creation of the U.S. [[deep-state|deep state]], and highlighted the report that while Washington placed a $10 million bounty on al-Qaeda's Syria commander in 2013, then–State Department staffer [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] had written to Secretary of State Hillary Clinton months earlier that "AQ is on our side in Syria."[1]

He characterized the timing as opportunistic: Assad was on a planned trip to Moscow, and Hezbollah — which he said had spent twelve years in Syria protecting Assad from [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and [[nato|NATO]] — had just ratified a ceasefire with Israel, when NATO-funded proxy groups moved to overthrow the government.[1] He compared the sequencing to the pager attack, in which Netanyahu allegedly feigned a ceasefire with Hezbollah through Iranian channels before striking, and argued the move against Assad was driven by the Netanyahu regime seeking Damascus as a symbolic step toward [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]].[1]
