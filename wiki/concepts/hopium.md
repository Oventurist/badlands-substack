# Hopium

**Hopium** is a pejorative used within online political and "truth seeker" communities for optimism regarded as unfounded — the accusation that a person is intoxicated by hope rather than reasoning from evidence. In [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary the term appears chiefly as a charge levelled *at* writers who argue that favourable operations are underway behind the scenes, and it is in that adversarial sense that [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] engaged it in his May 4, 2023 essay "Stuck in a Groove of Negativity and Hopelessness."[1]

## Carlson's engagement with the charge

Carlson opened the essay by adopting his critics' vocabulary ironically, describing himself as "one of those obnoxious, uneducated and deceived fools" who believes good things have been happening behind the scenes and were accelerating in 2023.[1] He wrote that "some would say I'm full of 'hopium' for thinking [[donald-trump|Trump]] put in place a system that has the power to step in before permanent damage occurs," and accepted the label rather than disputing it.[1]

His defence was framed as a question of plausibility rather than sentiment: that Trump had not spent four years fighting the [[deep-state|Deep State]] with everything he and his family had only to hand over "the car keys to a drunk driver who also has dementia" on January 20, 2021.[1] He offered as corroborating evidence the report that China had declared [[george-soros|George Soros]] a "global terrorist" and "the son of Satan," and the apparent disintegration and infighting of the Deep State since 2021 — asking how such disarray would be possible if its control were complete.[1]

## Hopium as the inverse of black-pilling

The structural argument of the essay is that the accusation of hopium and the condition of [[black-pilling|black pilling]] are two poles of the same manipulation. Carlson argued that excessive negativity is itself engineered, and that portions of his community had been manipulated into "a rut of negativity they can't get out of," seeking validation for hopelessness and ignoring evidence of hope.[1] He described people so committed to the belief that the American economy would be destroyed by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration that they were rooting for the destruction to occur, and others who insisted Trump could not win in 2024 because elections are too rigged.[1] Told that conditions are bad and will worsen but that the damage is not permanent, such readers, he wrote, treat the qualification as a message they do not want to hear.[1]

Carlson also cited his own revision on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] as a case where a fixed negative conviction had to be set aside: long convinced the Fed was irredeemable and should be abolished, he came to accept that it is likely being used to dismantle the foreign-controlled cabal banking system, comparing the reversal to Darth Vader turning good.[1] He read the [[brics|BRICS]] grouping the same way — as creating options and loosening European control of the United States rather than destroying it.[1]

## Religious framing

Carlson grounded the defence of hope in Christian theology rather than in political forecasting, asserting that human beings are "not designed by God to feel hopeless or defeated" and that despair originates elsewhere and functions to take a person out of the fight.[1] He described the Bible as "the ultimate expression of hope" — an account of how sin corrupted a perfect world followed by the terms of escape from it — and defined repentance as requiring change rather than merely knowing the truth or feeling sorry.[1] The essay's closing injunctions are "Find hope" and "Be a beacon of hope," with the blunt corollary that a person who does not "is useless in the fight to save the world."[1]
