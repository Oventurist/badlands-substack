# Trump Digital Trading Cards

**Trump Digital Trading Cards**, marketed as "Trump Cards," were a collection of non-fungible tokens (NFTs) unveiled by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on December 15, 2022, and sold through the website collecttrumpcards.com.[1] The launch followed Trump's December 14 video post on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] promising a "major announcement," a practice the Badlands Media commentary described as his standard operating procedure of "announcing the announcement."[1] Overnight speculation held that the announcement would concern his 2024 campaign, a running mate, or a run for Speaker of the House; when the announcement proved to be a digital trading-card collection, many supporters and opponents alike characterized it as a "nothingburger."[1]

## Reaction

Coverage of the launch was uniformly derisive across outlets including Decrypt/Crypto.com, RawStory, and Rolling Stone, and the reaction on Twitter from crypto-market commentators such as Autism Capital, "The Wolf Of All Streets" (Scott Melker), and yzy.eth was similarly mocking.[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] argued that this derision was the intended effect, describing the day as a "bait-and-switch" in which Trump "reeled in libs, RINOs, many of the party faithful," and reportedly even segments of the military into believing he had lost his political judgment.[1]

## The "look here, not there" reading

Gillespie framed the episode as a deliberate misdirection operation she called "LOOK HERE/NOT THERE," in which the NFT collection was the "sizzle" and the same-day [[digital-bill-of-rights|Digital Bill of Rights]] free-speech policy rollout was the "steak."[1] She noted that left-leaning media fixated on the NFTs while largely ignoring the free-speech proposal, which was published in an exclusive video obtained by [[jack-posobiec|Jack Posobiec]].[1] In her reading, the pro-Trump commentators [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and [[steve-cortes|Steve Cortes]], who publicly attacked the Trump team for releasing both items on the same day and demanded firings, were themselves participants in the misdirection, tasked with amplifying scorn for the NFT drop.[1]

## Crypto payment and the digital-wallet thesis

The purchase instructions on collecttrumpcards.com allowed buyers to "pay with crypto," specifically ETH (WETH), at checkout, and stated that purchased cards would be "minted and delivered to your digital wallet," accessible by email login.[1] Gillespie considered this notable because Trump had previously disparaged cryptocurrency, telling Fox Business in August 2021 that crypto was "potentially a disaster waiting to happen" and that he preferred "the currency of the United States," and telling Maria Bartiromo in December 2021 that crypto was "very dangerous" and could produce "an explosion someday — the likes of which we've never seen."[1]

From this apparent contradiction she drew the article's central claim: that the Trump NFT drop — and [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]]'s "Melania's Vision" NFT launched on December 16, 2021, one day shy of a year earlier — were engineered primarily to normalize [[digital-wallets|digital wallets]] among ordinary Americans, with further releases promised "regularly" to steadily increase wallet creation.[1] The collection sold out within hours.[1]

## Lincoln and Washington remark

In the unveiling video Trump described himself as "hopefully your favorite President of all time. Better than Lincoln. Better than Washington."[1] Gillespie interpreted the pairing as monetary signalling rather than vanity: [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] representing U.S. coinage and [[george-washington|George Washington]] representing the U.S. dollar, together indicating that [[fiat-currency|fiat currency]] is finished and that a new currency will be associated with Trump's presidency.[1]
