# Voodoo

**Voodoo** is treated in Part 8 of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series as the paradigm case of dark occult practice: the imposition of one's will upon reality through the use of symbols and effigies.[1]

## Metaphysical basis proposed

The essay grounds voodoo in the claim that the universe is a living language that creates itself by talking to itself, a proposition it attributes to [[chris-langan|Chris Langan]]'s [[cognitive-theoretic-model-of-the-universe|Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe]].[1] If the universe is symbolic and linguistic in nature, the author argues, then words and symbols are conduits of power and energy, and voodoo becomes intelligible.[1]

Reality is divided for this purpose into physical reality, in which objects and events influence one another by direct local impact, and metaphysical or subjective reality, in which concepts and ideas influence one another by likeness and resonance.[1] These are identified with Descartes' extended matter and non-extended mind and said to be reconciled as a single monic substance in the CTMU.[1] The consequence drawn is that it is impossible to avoid affecting reality with one's thoughts, because physical reality is an outgrowth of the metaphysical realm in which thought occurs.[1] The author explicitly presents this as a highly simplified model rather than a complete proof.[1]

## Dark occultism

Dark occultists are said to engage in ritualized symbolic acts to influence how reality unfolds, which the essay identifies as voodoo by another name.[1] Whatever the justifications or paraphernalia involved, the practice is reduced to a single principle: imposing one's will on God's creation rather than allowing that creation to grow of its own accord.[1] The author adds that needless, bewildering complexity is in his opinion a defensive tactic used by dark occultists to conceal their true aims.[1] He argues that studying occult symbolism is not dangerous but necessary, comparing it to learning gang signs so that criminals — in this case "spiritual criminals" — can no longer coordinate over everyone's head, and citing [[qanon|Q]]'s statement that the cabal's need for symbolism will be their downfall.[1] These premises supply the framework for the article's concept of [[cyber-necromancy|cyber-necromancy]].[1]

## Teleology

Related to the argument is the essay's defence of [[teleology|teleology]]: the notion that God has intrinsic purposes for created things, which mainstream academia and science, steeped in secular materialism, are said to deny altogether.[1]
