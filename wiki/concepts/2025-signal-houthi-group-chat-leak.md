# 2025 Signal Houthi Group Chat Leak

The 2025 Signal Houthi group chat leak was a national security controversy that erupted on March 24–25, 2025, after *The Atlantic* journalist [[jeffrey-goldberg|Jeffrey Goldberg]] reported that he had been inadvertently included in an encrypted group chat in which senior members of the second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] administration discussed forthcoming U.S. military strikes against Yemen's [[houthis|Houthi]] movement.[1]

## The chat

Goldberg said he obtained access to a chat on the encrypted messaging application [[signal|Signal]] after being added by a user identified as "Mike Waltz." The group, reportedly styled the "Houthi PC small group," included Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]], Secretary of Defense [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], and other cabinet-level officials.[1] According to the reporting, participants detailed the targets, sequencing, and weapons involved in a U.S. air attack on the Houthis before the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] launched the strikes on March 15, 2025.[1]

## Congressional reaction

The disclosure landed a day before top intelligence officials delivered the annual worldwide threat assessment to the [[senate-select-committee-on-intelligence|Senate Select Committee on Intelligence]], where Democrats used the hearing to attack the administration's handling of classified material.[1] Senator [[mark-warner|Mark R. Warner]] of Virginia, the committee's vice chairman, said that a military or intelligence officer who behaved the same way "would be fired," noting that the text chain also included the identity of an active CIA officer, and characterized the episode as "sloppy, careless, incompetent behavior."[1]

Ratcliffe confirmed at the hearing that he had been on the chat, stating that Signal "is permissible to use to communicate and coordinate for work purposes" and that "my communications were entirely permissible and lawful and did not include classified info."[1] Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] observed that Ratcliffe conspicuously limited his defense to his own messages rather than vouching for the entire group, and noted that [[cisa|CISA]] had itself urged government professionals to use Signal in 2023.[1]

## Pressure on Waltz

Reporting by Politico, summarized by [[rt|RT]], indicated that national security adviser [[mike-waltz|Michael Waltz]] — or another senior official — was believed responsible for the inadvertent addition, and that White House insiders were pressing for his ouster. One anonymous source told Politico that "everyone in the White House can agree on one thing: Mike Waltz is a f***ing idiot," while another said half of the staff believed he would not or should not survive the episode.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributors treated the affair skeptically. [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called it ironic that the regime's "leading narrative setter," *The Atlantic*, ran an almost unbelievable story sourced through an app literally named Signal, suggesting the episode was either gross incompetence or deliberate bait.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the leak was an intentional operation that sent a message to the cartel controlling Western hegemony, relieved [[china|China]] and [[russia|Russia]] of the diplomatic burden of confronting the Houthis themselves given their extensive Suez Canal and Red Sea interests, and demonstrated that the United States was not acting on behalf of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Israel by striking before the IDF did.[1]
