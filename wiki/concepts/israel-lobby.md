# Israel Lobby

"Israel lobby" is the term used by critics for the network of advocacy organizations, donors and media figures said to shape United States policy in favor of [[israel|Israel]]'s strategic preferences.[1]

In February 2026, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked the concept in response to reporting that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was gaming out a sustained, weeks-long campaign against [[iran|Iran]].[1] He wrote that critics who allege Israeli influence over the U.S. government are labeled antisemitic, yet events such as the Iran planning keep supplying support for the allegation.[1]

He questioned why Iran requires a new government, arguing that even if its rulers were tyrannical toward Iranians, that would not constitute an American problem, and asserted there is no evidence Iran poses a threat to the United States or to Israel — dismissing what he called consternation from figures including Laura Loomer, Mark Levin and Senator Lindsey Graham.[1] He concluded by asking why the United States was allowing the Israel lobby to drag it into the situation.[1]

## October 2024 Badlands critique

Responding to reporting on the disparity between U.S. air defense support for [[israel|Israel]] and for [[ukraine|Ukraine]], Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] rejected the official nuclear-risk explanation and argued instead that the United States defends Israel against its own interests because an Israeli oligarchy effectively owns the American government.[2] As evidence he pointed to state-level antisemitism speech measures adopted in roughly 40 states, most of them Republican-controlled, which cite the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|IHRA]] definition to classify criticism of the Israeli government as hate speech.[2]

He argued that the lobby's central error was conflating a political entity — a government — with a religion, a move he attributed to fear that support for Israel could not be sustained without penalizing criticism.[2] He characterized Israel as a fascist-authoritarian state presenting itself as a liberal democracy, founded by Bolsheviks and National Socialists fleeing the poverty of the Pale of Settlement, with a political spectrum entirely socialist and a "right wing" embracing corporatism — and therefore not an extension of American values.[2] He argued the same political forces controlling Israel control the United States, and asked how long before the precedent is applied to protect American governments from criticism.[2]
