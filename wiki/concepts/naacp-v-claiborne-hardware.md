# NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware

**NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.** (458 U.S. 886) is, together with [[brandenburg-v-ohio|Brandenburg v. Ohio]], one of the two seminal [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] cases of the latter half of the twentieth century that [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited in his critique of the [[colorado-ballot-disqualification-case|Colorado ballot disqualification case]].[1] The case is invoked for its protection of political advocacy and association even where some participants in a movement subsequently commit unlawful acts.[1]

## Relevance to the Colorado ruling

DeLarme argued that to criminalize [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s January 6, 2021 speech — that is, to treat it, legally speaking, as anything other than free expression — a court would have to violate both Brandenburg and Claiborne.[1] In his account, the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s conclusion that Trump "engaged in insurrection" through his speech required exactly that departure from settled precedent, since the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] has defined the outer limits of protected political speech principally through those two decisions and their progeny.[1]

He further contended that the absence of any charge against Trump under the federal rebellion-or-insurrection statute, [[18-usc-2383|18 U.S.C. § 2383]], meant that no adjudicated finding displaced the First Amendment presumption that his remarks were protected advocacy.[1]
