# Avdiivka

Avdiivka is a city in the Donetsk region of Ukraine captured by Russian forces in February 2024, becoming a marker of Ukraine's deteriorating battlefield position.[1]

## Fall of the city and its aftermath

Russia captured Avdiivka on Saturday, February 17, 2024, after Ukraine had spent the better part of a decade building extensive fortifications around it.[1] The New York Times observed that without dominant hills, larger rivers or comparable fortifications, "Ukraine will probably have to cede more ground to hold back Russian units," and quoted an analyst saying Ukrainian forces "don't have a well-established secondary line to pull back to."[1]

The retreat in the areas outside Avdiivka was cited alongside troop losses described as long past crisis levels as the backdrop for President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s February 2024 decree opening the [[national-guard-of-ukraine|National Guard of Ukraine]] to foreigners and stateless persons rather than pursuing a politically fraught domestic mobilization drive.[1] The reporting, published by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], was carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief of February 23, 2024.[1]

## Ukrainian withdrawal, February 2024

Following a year in which control of territory changed little, Russia secured a major victory when Ukrainian forces retreated from Avdiivka in February 2024.[2] Shortly after midnight on Saturday, February 17, Ukraine's newly installed top commander General [[oleksandr-syrsky|Oleksandr Syrsky]] announced he had ordered troops to evacuate, saying the decision was taken to avoid encirclement and preserve the lives and health of servicemen and to move to defense on more favorable lines.[2] Ukrainian soldiers in the city were surrounded on three sides.[2]

The capture was Russia's most significant triumph since taking Bakhmut in May 2023.[2] Avdiivka lies nine miles northwest of the Russian-held city of Donetsk; it was briefly controlled by pro-Russian separatists in 2014 before Ukraine retook it, and had a pre-war population of 32,000.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the fall of the city as the proxy war's "grandest defeat yet" for globalist interests, arguing that it helped finalize a land bridge from eastern Russia to Crimea and consolidated Russian control over the territory Moscow had said it intended to annex, and noting that it came within days of the [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] interview with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and the death of [[alexei-navalny|Alexei Navalny]].[2]
