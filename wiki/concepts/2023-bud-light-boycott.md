# 2023 Bud Light Boycott

The 2023 Bud Light boycott was a sustained consumer backlash against the [[anheuser-busch-inbev|Anheuser-Busch InBev]] beer brand [[bud-light|Bud Light]] following a promotional partnership with transgender TikTok influencer [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]] in the spring of 2023.[1]

## Marketing shake-up

Roughly three weeks after the brand attempted to position itself as the "queen" of "woke" beers through the Mulvaney partnership, Anheuser-Busch InBev abruptly changed Bud Light's marketing leadership.[1] Ad Age reported on Friday, April 21, 2023 that vice president of marketing [[alissa-heinerscheid|Alissa Heinerscheid]] had taken a leave of absence and that she would be replaced by [[todd-allen|Todd Allen]], recently the global marketing vice president for [[budweiser|Budweiser]].[1] To prevent a market disaster similar to the one sparked by Heinerscheid, a company spokesperson said top-level executives would closely monitor the brand's marketing activities, framing the aim as maintaining "focus on the things we do best: brewing great beer for all consumers, while always making a positive impact in our communities and on our country."[1]

## Commentary

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] covered the leadership change under the headline "Bud Light Marketing Executive Goes On Leave After Disastrous Trans-TikTok Stunt," and the item was excerpted in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief of April 25, 2023.[1] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that in the [[information-war|Information War]] one day's sensationalist, paradigm-shifting headline is often the next day's footnote, yet the Bud Light controversy had persisted well over a week and a half in the national news cycle, drawing coverage from far more outlets than those tangential to the culture war.[1]

He argued that the most interesting perspectives came from the legacy financial media, which was grudgingly admitting that Anheuser-Busch InBev might have made a catastrophic marketing blunder that would show red in its fiscal ledgers for several quarters and more.[1] He closed by invoking the movement's long-standing slogan "Get Woke, Go Broke," suggesting the company was competing for a gold medal in that race.[1]

## Damage control statement (April 2023)

On Friday, April 14, 2023, before the marketing shake-up, [[anheuser-busch-inbev|Anheuser-Busch InBev]]'s damage-control team responded to the mounting backlash with a press release from CEO [[brendan-whitworth|Brendan Whitworth]] that [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] described as carefully crafted and heavily focus-grouped, and which failed to mention [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]], Bud Light, or transgender issues at all.[2] "We never intended to be part of a discussion that divides people," Whitworth said. "We are in the business of bringing people together over a beer."[2] He added that he cared deeply about "this country, this company, our brands and our partners," spent much of his time traveling across America listening to customers and distributors, and would "continue to work tirelessly to bring great beers to consumers across our nation."[2] ZeroHedge summarized the message as: "Let's all forget about this over a beer."[2]

The backlash had already produced concrete commercial effects, including reports of bar customers refusing to order Bud Light after the Mulvaney campaign.[2]

## Badlands reaction to the statement

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded that "Anheuser-Busch is as American as Barack Obama," noting that the brewer is owned by a multinational conglomerate based in Belgium.[2] He observed that executives were being quoted saying the Mulvaney campaign had not been approved by senior management, and asked whether there were "ghosts in the machine in more places than we know."[2] Either the executives were lying, he argued, "in which case they're woke idiots," or they were telling the truth and someone had gone rogue with a move that cost the company billions in stock valuation and far more in brand damage.[2]

## Market impact in the first week

In the six days after the Mulvaney partnership was announced on April 2, 2023, [[anheuser-busch-inbev|Anheuser-Busch InBev]] lost more than $6 billion in market capitalisation, falling from a three-year high of $66.73 per share as bar owners and distributors reported a sharp drop in [[bud-light|Bud Light]] sales.[3] The company's market capitalisation declined from $132.8 billion to as low as $125.7 billion, more than a five percent fall.[3]

The campaign featured custom cans carrying Mulvaney's face and pro-LGBTQ language marking a year of Mulvaney publicly identifying as a woman, and opened with a video of Mulvaney drinking Bud Light in a bathtub.[3] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], citing the Daily Wire, reported that company executives had never signed off on the campaign even as the company publicly defended it as one of many influencer partnerships used "to authentically connect with audiences across various demographics."[3]

## Boycott as withdrawal of participation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that while much of the transgender angle was "low-hanging fruit," the significant net effect of the backlash was that ordinary consumers came to understand they could have a massive impact on a Fortune 500 company not by taking action but by withholding it, concluding that to destroy a [[system-of-systems|System of Systems]] "all you have to do is not take part in it."[3] The episode became a reference point in [[parallel-economy|parallel economy]] arguments within the [[truth-community|Truth Community]].[3]
