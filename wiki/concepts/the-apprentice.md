# The Apprentice

The Apprentice, later rebranded as The Celebrity Apprentice, was [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s NBC reality television program, read in Badlands commentary as a recruiting instrument disguised as a game show.[1]

## The Badlands reading

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], researching for his show The Book of Trump, argued that the contest was never really about business acumen but about the ability to handle intense pressure and public scrutiny while still performing and delivering.[1] In the first few seasons Trump brought in MBA candidates and successful business owners, reinforcing the business premise; after the rebrand he assembled what the commentator called "an island of misfit toys" — washed-up superstars, D-list celebrities, aspiring comedians.[1]

The stated pattern was strategic targeting of people with some public clout, and thus access in the celebrity world, but not enough prominence to attract scrutiny in any given environment — people rivals would undervalue.[1] The example given is Dennis Rodman, whom adversaries saw as a liability while Trump saw a five-time NBA champion from the 1990s Chicago Bulls, a team worshipped at the highest levels of the North Korean government, a fact reported during the Clinton administration when North Korean diplomats still visited the United States.[1] A startling number of such characters resurfaced in Trump's orbit before and during his first term.[1]

Contestants were subjected to humiliating tasks such as selling cupcakes in Times Square, and Trump exacerbated drama by probing observable tensions during his formal evaluations.[1] He escalated this by using [[omarosa-manigault-newman|Omarosa]] as both contestant and consultant, an arrangement the audience was fully read in on, while Trump repeatedly narrated that he sought "employees" who performed well under pressure.[1]

## The Trump Dialectic

The commentator concludes that the world is now watching the newest season of The Celebrity Apprentice executed at the largest scale imaginable, with agent provocateurs — wittingly or unwittingly — instigating conflict so that Trump can force resolution.[1] [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]]'s break with [[israel|Israel]] is offered as the current instance.[1]
