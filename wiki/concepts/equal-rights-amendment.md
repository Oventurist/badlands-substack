# Equal Rights Amendment

The **Equal Rights Amendment (ERA)** is a proposed amendment to the United States Constitution guaranteeing equality of rights regardless of sex. It was first introduced in the 1920s and finally passed by Congress in the 1970s, but was never ratified by the required 38 states, which is why it has never taken effect as constitutional law.[1]

## Biden's January 2025 declaration

On Friday, January 17, 2025 — three days before leaving office — President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] declared that he considered the Equal Rights Amendment to be "the law of the land," a surprising statement that carried no formal force of effect but was celebrated by ERA supporters at a rally held in front of the National Archives.[1]

The executive branch has no direct role in the constitutional amendment process. The White House told reporters on a conference call that Biden was not going to order the archivist to certify and publish the ERA, and a senior administration official characterized the archivist's role as "purely ministerial" in nature — meaning the archivist is required to publish an amendment only once it has been properly ratified.[1]

Asked by NPR whether the archivist would take any new action, communications staff at the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] pointed to a December statement holding that the ERA cannot be certified as part of the Constitution because of established legal, judicial, and procedural decisions. "This is a long-standing position for the archivist and the National Archives. The underlying legal and procedural issues have not changed," the archives' statement said.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied the episode to remarks House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] had made to Bari Weiss the previous week, in which Johnson said Biden did not know what he was signing and was not the one running the country. Around the same time that video circulated, Biden declared the ERA to be the 28th Amendment, and Democratic elected officials — who had sworn an oath to the Constitution — celebrated the declaration as though it were legally operative, which the Brief characterized as "weird." Legacy news coverage, by contrast, was described as more measured.[1]

The Brief also noted the ERA's cultural afterlife, pointing to the Prime Video limited series *Mrs. America* (2020), in which Cate Blanchett played Phyllis Schlafly, whom the series largely credited with killing ratification — an entertaining if one-sided portrayal of the amendment's embattled history. Ashe in America described Biden's announcement itself as "belligerent and hilarious" and argued that those who celebrated it should be "repeatedly mocked into the golden age."[1]
