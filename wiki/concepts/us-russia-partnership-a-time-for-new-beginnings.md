# US-Russia Partnership: A Time for New Beginnings

*US-Russia Partnership: A Time for New Beginnings* was a policy report commissioned by Republican congressman [[curt-weldon|Curt Weldon]] of Pennsylvania and endorsed by more than 100 elected representatives.[1] Published in early 2001, it presented a coherent vision for a new paradigm in US–Russian relations that the article describes as unmatched in over a decade.[1]

## Contents

Cultural diplomacy, the teaching of Russian in American schools, agricultural assistance, full-spectrum energy development, space exploration, defense cooperation, asteroid defense and fusion research all figured prominently in Weldon's dossier.[1]

The report's opening remarks warned that "America and Russia must forge an alliance beneficial to both, or face the near certainty that historical suspicions will reassert themselves and plunge the world into a new Cold War," adding that such an eventuality would be especially tragic since the two nations "have more in common than not," and that terrorism and WMD proliferation as their gravest common threats should make them natural allies.[1] It argued that the Cold War era model of bilateral relations and arms control, predicated on mutual antagonism and nuclear threats, was unacceptable as the basis for 21st-century relations, and urged US policy to encourage Russian cooperation in counter-terrorism, non-proliferation and missile defense.[1] The key to forging the alliance, it concluded, was "to do it now, before U.S.-Russian relations deteriorate further."[1]

## Fate

It was this spirit of goodwill within the leading strata of American policymakers that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] addressed when he made known his interest in Russian participation in [[nato|NATO]].[1] The vision was displaced, however, by the unipolarist current within the incoming administration of [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]], which carried forward the Clinton–Gore–Talbott Russia policy of the 1990s while adding an obsessive militaristic drive for global supremacy.[1]
