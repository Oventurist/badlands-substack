# Zarqa Al Yamama

*Zarqa Al Yamama* is the first grand opera produced in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], which had its opening performance in Riyadh in the spring of 2024 as part of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s ambitious plans to shift the kingdom into a new economic and social era.[1]

## Story and production

The work tells the story of a blue-eyed tribal matriarch in pre-Islamic Arabia who possesses the gift of foreseeing the future, and whose warnings of imminent danger are ignored by her tribe.[1] Ivan Vukcevic, head of Arabian Opera — a company established in Switzerland to help develop the performance and teaching of classical music in the Middle East — described the tale as having universal resonance and as an epic tragedy familiar to many in the Arabic-speaking world.[1] "At its core lies the central female heroine with an extraordinary gift of foresight, advocating for reason and love in the face of adversity, even at the expense of her own life," he said.[1] Vukcevic and his company were initially taken aback and puzzled when the idea of a lavish new Arabic-language opera was first presented to them two years earlier.[1]

## Commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the premiere as a rebuttal to the recurring claim that Islamic societies would inevitably erase Western culture through the spread of Shariah.[1] Noting that opera, along with ballet, represents the pinnacle of high European culture, he observed that the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia — whose king holds the title "Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques" — was importing that culture into Arabia, calling the phenomenon "inverse Shariah."[1] He suggested his earlier speculation that cathedrals in Riyadh and Catholic mass in Arabic might follow no longer seemed far-fetched, closing with Kanye West's post-2016 line, "It's a new world, Hillary. It's a new world, Barack."[1]
