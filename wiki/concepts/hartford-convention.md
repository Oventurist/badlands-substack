# Hartford Convention

The Hartford Convention of 1814 was a gathering of Federalists during the War of 1812 that has been invoked in contemporary commentary as a historical parallel for political factions that turn against the system they built once they lose power.[1]

## Background

By 1814 the Federalists had lost four consecutive presidential elections — two to Thomas Jefferson and two to James Madison — and had come to detest the federal government they had fought tooth-and-nail to establish. The convention was called so that they could air grievances about their loss of public support.[1]

The main topic of discussion was a plan to gather what states they could and secede from the Union, potentially allying with the British, who were at that moment fighting the United States in the War of 1812. Commentators have drawn the ironic lesson that even the champions of strong centralized government hate it when they are not the ones on the throne.[1]

## Consequences

The convention was cited as one of the reasons the [[federalist-party|Federalist Party]] was abolished after the war ended in 1815. It was also likely one of the reasons that Anti-Federalists frequently accused their rivals of being traitors in incendiary rhetoric.[1]

In October 2024, Badlands Media contributors invoked the Hartford Convention while discussing [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s warnings that authorities would "lose total control" if social media platforms stopped censoring content — framing establishment panic over the loss of narrative dominance as a modern echo of Federalist bitterness.[1]
