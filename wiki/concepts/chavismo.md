# Chavismo

**Chavismo** is the political movement and governing ideology built around [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] and continued under his successor [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1]

## Bolivarianismo

Chávez had not publicly declared himself a socialist when he swept to the presidency in a 1998 landslide, yet Venezuela's entrenched rulers — drawn from both dominant parties — viewed his embrace of *Bolivarianismo*, which invoked [[simon-bolivar|Simón Bolívar]] to champion vague domestic reforms and anti-imperialism, as a mortal threat.[1]

## Governing record

Chavismo's material base was petroleum. After surviving the [[2002-venezuelan-coup-attempt|2002 coup attempt]] and the ensuing owners' strike, Chávez redirected [[pdvsa|PDVSA]]'s revenues into health, education, and housing programs.[1] Electorally the movement was dominant: Chávez won the August 2004 recall referendum with 58 percent, his coalition took 20 of 22 state governorships and 270 of 337 municipalities in the following regional elections, and he was re-elected in 2006 carrying every state with more than 62 percent of the national vote.[1]

## Opposition and decline

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that critics of opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] fault her free-market zeal and U.S. ties for alienating the Chavista base — the slum-dwellers Chávez once lifted with petrodollar largesse.[1] The 2025 Nobel Peace Prize awarded to Machado is framed as the West's anointing of a spearhead for a final push against Chavismo.[1]
