# BHA and BHT

Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA) and butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT, additive number E320) are synthetic preservatives used in common household foods that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] identified as known toxics.[1] Any processed food item with a particularly long shelf life is often filled with BHA.[1]

## Function and exposure

The two compounds keep foods from changing color, changing flavor, or becoming rancid.[1] They are found in cereal, chewing gum, potato chips, vegetable oils, frozen sausages, enriched rice, lard, shortening, cake, candy, and jello.[1] They are also oxidants, which form potentially cancer-causing reactive compounds in the body and can affect the neurological system of the brain and alter behavior.[1]

## Propyl gallate

Propyl gallate is another preservative often used in conjunction with BHA and BHT.[1] It is sometimes found in meat products, chicken soup base, and chewing gums.[1] Animal studies have suggested it could be linked to cancer growth, though other studies claim it can induce cell death in cancer cells.[1] Propyl gallate can also cause stomach and skin irritation, liver damage, and kidney damage.[1]
