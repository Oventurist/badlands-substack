# Ban the Jab Resolution

The "Ban the Jab" resolution is an 83-page measure adopted by Republican executive committees in nine Florida counties during 2023, calling on Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], county sheriffs and the Florida Legislature to prohibit the sale and distribution of [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] and all other mRNA vaccines within the state.[1]

## Contents and demands

The resolution asks state lawmakers to bar the vaccines outright and asks Florida Attorney General Ashley Moody to immediately seize all remaining vaccine supplies in the state and subject them to forensic analysis.[1] It includes more than 140 exhibits of evidence which its authors say reflect the independent findings of biomedical professionals and other parties concerned about the vaccines.[1] The text was written by psychotherapist Joseph Sansone.[1]

The Lee County Republican Party, the first body to adopt it, framed the vote as being taken "on behalf of the preservation of the human race," calling upon DeSantis and the legislature to prohibit "Covid injections and all mRNA injections" in Florida and for the attorney general to seize and forensically analyze them.[1]

## Adoption

The resolution was adopted first by local GOP officials in Lee County, which includes Fort Myers, and subsequently by the executive committees of Collier, Lake, Santa Rosa, Seminole, St. Johns, Hillsborough and Brevard counties.[1] Franklin County became the ninth county to pass it on August 19, 2023.[1] None of the county executive committees responded to requests for comment from [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]], which reported the campaign.[1]

## Badlands framing

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] presented the county-level resolutions as a template for citizen action, arguing that local bodies can preempt "globalist" measures before they take effect and that authorities historically retreat rather than confront the open will of the people.[1] He tied the effort to his broader claim that COVID-era health protocols were never validly debated in a legislature and therefore rest on fraud, so that demanding proof of key facts forces officials either to expose themselves or withdraw.[1]
