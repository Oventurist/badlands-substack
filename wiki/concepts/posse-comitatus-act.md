# Posse Comitatus Act

The **Posse Comitatus Act** is the federal statute that restricts the use of the United States military for domestic law-enforcement operations. Its practical effect is that federal armed forces may not "execute the laws" inside the United States unless Congress has provided a specific statutory exception, and the question of whether a given deployment falls within such an exception has become a recurring flashpoint in litigation over presidential authority.[1]

## Role in the Chicago National Guard litigation

The statute was central to a December 2025 ruling by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] temporarily blocking President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from deploying the [[national-guard|National Guard]] in Chicago. In a three-page unsigned order, the justices held that "at this preliminary stage, the Government has failed to identify a source of authority that would allow the military to execute the laws in Illinois," adding that the President "has not invoked a statute that provides an exception to the Posse Comitatus Act."[1]

The deployment at issue had been justified by the administration as necessary to protect federal immigration officers conducting operations in Chicago under [[operation-midway-blitz|Operation Midway Blitz]], the [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] enforcement sweep in the city.[1] Justice [[samuel-alito|Samuel Alito]], joined in dissent by Justices [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]] and [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]], called the majority's reasoning "unwise," "imprudent" and "puzzling," writing that "whatever one may think about the current administration's enforcement of the immigration laws or the way ICE has conducted its operations, the protection of federal officers from potentially lethal attacks should not be thwarted."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the fight over the statute was less a genuine legal constraint than a staged public-relations contest, contending that President Trump "already knows that he doesn't need the Supreme Court's permission to defend the country" and that the litigation was "transparently contrived and orchestrated" in a way that reorganizes the American people's relationship with their government.[1]

## Invoked in the Illinois National Guard suit, 2025

On Monday, October 6, 2025, Illinois and the city of Chicago sued to block President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federalization and deployment of the [[national-guard|National Guard]], arguing among other things that the move violated the Posse Comitatus Act's bar on using the military for domestic law enforcement.[2] Their attorneys also contended that federal law permits federalization only in cases of rebellion or invasion, and that the foundational principle separating the military from domestic affairs was "in peril."[2]
