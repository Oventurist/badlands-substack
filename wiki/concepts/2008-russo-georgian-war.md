# 2008 Russo-Georgian War

The 2008 Russo-Georgian War was a brief conflict in the South Caucasus that returned to the news in August 2025 when the United States, for the first time, refused to endorse a condemnation of Russia over the war.[1]

## Course of the war

On August 7, 2008, under orders from staunchly pro-Western President Mikhail Saakashvili, the Georgian army invaded the breakaway region of South Ossetia, shelled its capital Tskhinval, and attacked a base of Russian peacekeepers.[1] Russian forces pushed the Georgian troops back, and a French-mediated ceasefire was reached on August 16.[1] That same month, Russia recognized the independence of South Ossetia and of Abkhazia, another of Georgia's former regions.[1]

## 2025 US policy shift

Following a closed-door session of the UN Security Council in August 2025, Denmark, France, Greece, the United Kingdom, and Slovenia issued a statement denouncing Russia for the "brutal invasion" of Georgia seventeen years earlier.[1] The United States did not sign the statement, despite having done so in the past.[1]
