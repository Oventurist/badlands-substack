# Anthony Fauci: Soldier for the Communist Cause

"Anthony Fauci: Soldier for the Communist Cause" is an opinion column published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on December 20, 2022, written by the contributor [[j-b-white|J.B. White]], who writes under the handle RattlerGator. Presented as the author's first written submission to the outlet, the piece frames [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] as the public face of what it calls an anti-American governmental apparatus, and situates his conduct during the COVID-19 response within a broader argument about censorship, narrative control, and ideological subversion. The article carried 177 likes on the platform and closes with the standard Badlands Media disclaimer that features represent the opinions of contributing authors rather than the views of the outlet itself.

## Occasion and framing

The author states that the ongoing revelations of the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] made the moment appropriate to begin regular submissions, describing the disclosures as exposing "remarkable wrongdoing by a RICO-worthy collection of fascistic rascals unworthy of American public service." He characterises what he sees as the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s "massive efforts to prevent competition for their preferred narratives." A second prompt for the column was [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s tweet announcing that his pronouns were "Prosecute/Fauci," which the author reads as a sign that accountability for "Public Enemy Number One" was near. The piece is explicitly written to accompany any future Twitter Files instalment dealing with Fauci.

## Argument about Fauci's formation

White recounts that casual research indicated Fauci was educated in the Jesuit tradition of "men for others," and argues that, like many Catholics and other Christians, Fauci "has evolved into a 'humanist.'" The author treats this evolution as instructive: when Catholic training becomes unmoored from "men for God," he writes, the transition "down Crazy Street" can be very easy. He draws an analogy to the rise of liberation theology in South America, with a pointed aside directed at "Pope Che." In the author's view humanists are "most often indistinguishable from Communists," and the label humanist is "just word warfare" — a judgement he offers as the reason Fauci angered him from the beginning of what he calls the Scamdemic.

## Cases versus deaths

A central thread of the column is the author's objection to the pandemic-era emphasis on case counts. He recalls the hysteria of "Cases, cases, cases!" and says he could never determine why Americans should be concerned about claims of cases — "claims, mind you, not confirmed incidents verified by any known reliable testing method." His own standard, he writes, was "Deaths, not cases," and he maintained on Twitter at the time that until something credible was presented about extraordinary deaths he would not credit hysteria "amplified by a remarkably coordinated range of organizations, corporations, and governments." He expresses surprise that, judging by social media in 2020, few people in the United States or abroad shared that approach, and he attributes this to the patient and skilful application of tools of subversion wielded "in plain sight, but through hidden means" against what he regards as the default American mindset of healthy skepticism.

## Censorship and gaslighting

The article's theoretical core is a lengthy quotation from a column by [[j-e-dyer|J. E. Dyer]] published on December 16, 2022. Dyer is quoted as writing that "the goal of censorship is never just to silence speech. It's to prevent competition for someone's preferred speech, which is to be favored, fostered, enshrined, proclaimed and insisted to be valid and true, regardless of evidence," a process that "routinely devolves into a series of 'Big Lies'" and constitutes "the profile of gaslighting." She continues: "The two always go together. The more you censor, the more latitude you have to gaslight. The purpose of a campaign of political censorship is gaslighting." Dyer also notes communication through media purporting to come from government agencies that "offers no proof whatsoever" of an evidentiary basis, alongside "lies of omission" and "word salads that leave an unmistakable impression" without any basis for accepting it.

White applies this analysis to what he describes as 21st-century warfare as practised by the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] and those who serve it, previously identified merely as useful idiots, for whom lies of omission are "no big deal whatsoever." He cites the "cases, cases, cases" hysteria and "the ridiculous narrative surrounding the January 6th event" as examples from the preceding seven years.

## The Daniel Levitt tweet

The column recounts that on September 16, 2020, a Twitter user named Daniel Levitt, posting as @daniellevitt22, published a recap of "what we know about COVID" listing eight points: that lockdowns increase deaths; that Sweden was right; that Neil Ferguson was wrong; that masks are ineffective; that 95% are asymptomatic; that up to 80% may be immune; that it stops at roughly 20% antibodies; and that more cases do not equal more deaths. When the author saved the tweet on September 17, it had 136 replies, 2,053 retweets and 4,085 likes, which he takes as evidence of broad agreement. He notes that both the tweet and Levitt's account have since been erased, and argues that Twitter "either harassed him into ghosting himself, or they've disappeared him themselves" — a fate he describes as the normal result for many on the conservative side. He stresses the timing: September of election year 2020, by which point "the basic facts were absolutely known."

## Election, thread, and prescriptions

The author refers to "the stolen presidential election of 2020" and "obvious ballot stuffing to steal an election in furtherance of a coup," and expresses shock at what he calls the ho-hum acceptance of the steal by family and friends. He mentions a 70-post Twitter thread of his own that was read aloud by his future Trash Talk co-host [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] in the Reading Epic Threads series, producing roughly 26 minutes of video hosted on Odysee.

Turning to politics, White writes that in the 2016 election cycle the Republican base gave the GOP establishment — the "GOPe" — a test which it failed miserably, and that the incoming Trump administration "put together an invasion force to root out secret adherents to a theology (Communism) masquerading as an ideology (Socialism)." He argues that all three branches of government must re-embrace inalienable individual rights with citizens as "First Sovereigns," and recommends two essays to that end: "The Tipping Point: Douglas MacArthur versus the ruling class" in the Claremont Review of Books, and "Abolish FISA, Reform FBI, & Break Up CIA" at The American Mind.

## Bottom bitches and Katie Hobbs

Declaring that the applicable exclamation is no longer "cases, cases, cases" but "Fauci, Fauci, Fauci," the author calls Fauci "the face of anti-American governmental overlords and the face of the willing executor of the Deep State's bidding," asserting that this "man for others" overstepped his legal authority decades ago while leaving that discussion to others. He argues Fauci does not act alone for what he terms the criminal syndicate of syndicates: he and others in governments, corporations and groups employ "Bottom Bitches" — a term borrowed from an American Greatness essay — across the United States, people who disseminate preferred narratives and insist on partisan censorship among other unconstitutional actions. As an example he names [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] in Arizona.

## Closing

White promises a future column on Fauci, specifically a Florida example of how he believes Fauci had a direct hand in "quite curious reporting" on cases within America and beyond. He closes with an exhortation to remain optimistic and patient while persevering with local action, and with the maxims he once used to end his email correspondence: "Patience. Preparation. Perseverance. Attention to Detail. Flexibility. Resiliency. Never think that God's delays are God's denials. Hold on; hold fast; hold out. Patience is genius." The piece signs off "Later, Gators," with a pointer to the author's own RattlerGator's Newsletter.
