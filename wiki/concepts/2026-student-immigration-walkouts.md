# 2026 Student Immigration Walkouts

The 2026 student immigration walkouts were a wave of high school and school-district walkouts protesting federal immigration enforcement that spread across Pennsylvania, Colorado, Illinois, Maryland, Florida and other states in early 2026.[1]

## Scope and school response

Hundreds of students walked out in some districts, often leaving campus without authorization, which prompted disciplinary warnings for attendance and policy violations.[1] Participating students reportedly opposed deportations and feared the impact of enforcement on immigrant families.[1] Critics of the protests noted the presence of professionally produced signage and apparent professional coordination, a hallmark cited elsewhere in Badlands coverage of [[color-revolution|color revolution]]-style mobilization.[1]

## Palm Beach incident

In Florida, a student near Palm Beach Lakes High School was struck by a vehicle and suffered non-life-threatening injuries; the driver cooperated with police.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that student protest is a normal part of growing up in America, recalling a walkout at her own public high school more than 30 years earlier over the forced early retirement of a beloved football coach.[1] She said she had no problem with students protesting "even for stupid causes like sanctuary for criminal invaders," because the right to advocate publicly is strongly protected in the United States, but objected strongly to students being struck by cars while doing so.[1] She declined to blame the driver, instead faulting first the schools — praising one Pennsylvania principal's handling while predicting parents would demand his removal — and second the parents, arguing that with math and reading scores falling nationally, parents were "more interested in their kids signaling virtue in the streets than developing virtues through scholastic pursuits of excellence."[1]
