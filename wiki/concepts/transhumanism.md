# Transhumanism

**Transhumanism** is characterized in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary not as a technological program but as a religion to be combated.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s feature "Combating the Transhumanist Religion," published 1 November 2025 and highlighted in the 7 November 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] retrospective, is the second installment of a series.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) Its first part introduced readers to the "above ground" and "below ground" movements that Ehret contends have been carefully sculpted to induce as many humans as possible to participate, quite literally, in the willful self-destruction of industrial civilization.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) The distinction between overt and covert movements is central: the transhumanist religion is presented as operating simultaneously through publicly respectable institutions such as the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and the [[great-reset|Great Reset]], and through subterranean currents including [[eco-terrorism|eco-terrorism]] and the anti-industrial philosophy Ehret associates with [[ted-kaczynski|Theodore Kaczynski]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

## Esler's convergence argument (2024)

In February 2024, commenting on the [[google-gemini|Gemini]] image-generation failure, Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the AI issue, gender ideology and the normalization of virtual and augmented reality all intersect in transhumanism.[2] Outsourcing too much of human life to AI is, in his account, a transhumanist problem; gender ideology serves as a primer for transhumanist philosophy by promoting the idea that the human body is an obstacle to "finding the self"; and normalizing immersive technology on trends set by a ruling corporatocracy is likewise transhumanist.[2] Esler wrote that people are being groomed, intentionally and unintentionally, for a transhumanist future, and warned against surprise "when they suddenly collide to form a generation of transhumanist kids."[2] He cited [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s brain-chip ventures as an example of the merger of human thought and technology proceeding relatively unchecked, and urged working now to preserve values that guide culture and technology with future generations in view.[2]

## Ehret's critique: transhumanism as a closed-system religion (2025)

In November 2025, [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] published the third installment of a series describing transhumanism as a "sick and distorted religion" and a psycho-spiritual disease requiring an antidote.[3] He argued that where technological progress was once understood as a liberating process bringing the fruits of mental labor to the service of humanity's needs, transhumanists have turned the philosophy of technological progress inside out, holding that technology itself produces "useless eaters".[3]

The doctrine, in Ehret's account, denies that mind is anything more than the sum total of the non-living atoms composing the physical brain, and asserts — following the [[cybernetics|cybernetics]] revolution of the 1960s — that technology grows with a life all its own, acting as a synthetic and deterministic *élan vital* without regard for human thought or free will.[3] Its lineage runs through Marshall McLuhan, [[julian-huxley|Sir Julian Huxley]], [[norbert-wiener|Norbert Wiener]], the Jesuit transhumanist [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] and his intellectual heir [[ray-kurzweil|Ray Kurzweil]], described as the priests of the [[fourth-industrial-revolution|Fourth Industrial Revolution]].[3]

Ehret characterizes the creed as neo-Darwinian, resting on the assumption that random stochastic processes at small scale drive an inexorable growth of "complexity", while denying that any moral direction shapes evolution and dismissing morality, God, patriotism, soul and freedom as human-made abstractions with no ontological existence.[3] Despite this professed randomness, its adherents believe in rigid deterministic laws under which each technological upshift irreversibly reduces human mental powers, with AI replacing obsolete organic life forms as mammals replaced dinosaurs.[3] The framework treats [[entropy|entropy]] as the only law governing every closed system, and parallels the zero-sum Hobbesian doctrine that all wholes are merely the sum of their parts.[3]

### Harari as figurehead

[[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] is presented as the movement's leading spokesman, quoted as saying that with enough data and computing power one can understand and manipulate people better than they understand themselves, that humans are now "hackable animals", and that the idea that humans have a soul, spirit or free will is "over".[3] As a leading figure of the [[world-economic-forum|WEF]] Great Narrative Project, Harari also said new religions are likely to emerge from Silicon Valley promising happiness, justice and eternal life through technology rather than through a supernatural being — leading Ehret to describe him as being sold as a "Moses" of the post-human age.[3] The transhumanist answer to the resulting [[useless-eaters|useless class]] is a combination of drugs and computer games, following [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]]'s 1962 "Ultimate Revolution" lecture.[3]

### The humanist antidote

Ehret's counter-argument holds that the thesis survives only if consideration is restricted to information technologies; productive technologies such as higher-efficiency energy sources, as described by [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]], break it down.[3] He extends the definition of technology to forms of government and political economy, invoking [[friedrich-schiller|Friedrich Schiller]]'s 1791 rule that institutions are good only insofar as they let all forces inherent in persons flourish, and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]]'s conception of government as an applied "science of happiness" embodying constant self-perfectibility in the phrase "a more perfect union".[3] The anti-Malthusian economics of [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]] and the vision of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] are offered as the American alternative, while Ehret points to Russian president [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s St. Petersburg remarks on a technology-based economy and [[multipolar-world-order|multipolarity]] as the Eurasian defense of natural law against a unipolar order of stasis.[3]
