# NOAA California Sea Level Rise Projection

The NOAA California sea level rise projection is a mapping product from the [[national-oceanic-and-atmospheric-administration|National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration]] showing which California cities would be inundated under a ten-foot rise in sea levels.[1]

## Findings and expert commentary

The projection map indicates that California's coastline would be considerably impacted if sea levels rose by ten feet.[1] J. Derek Loftis, a professor with the Center for Coastal Resources Management at the Virginia Institute of Marine Science, College of William & Mary, told [[newsweek|Newsweek]] that a ten-foot rise would "render significant portions of one-third of coastal cities in the U.S. uninhabitable."[1]

Loftis said the "compounding impacts" of storm surge from hurricanes and nor'easters, combined with sea level rise, would be "the driving force behind abandonment of less resilient coastal communities."[1] He added that even without such a large rise, the issue is already directly affecting coastal cities across the United States, with many dedicating significant monetary resources to resiliency plans in response to transportation interruptions caused by nuisance flooding during the fall tidal flooding season.[1]

## Badlands reaction

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the coverage as recycled alarmism, asking whether people were "still being manipulated by 'California is going to be under water' climate change fear porn" and remarking that such stories made her "root for the meteor."[1]
