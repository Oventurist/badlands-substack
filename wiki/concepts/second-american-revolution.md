# Second American Revolution

The "Second American Revolution" is a phrase popularized in July 2024 by [[kevin-roberts|Kevin Roberts]], president of the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], and adopted and reinterpreted by Badlands Media commentators.[1]

## Roberts's formulation

Speaking on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s "War Room" podcast, Roberts argued that the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s ruling on [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]] — which largely shields former presidents from criminal prosecution for actions in office — would reinforce a "second American Revolution," which he said would "remain bloodless if the left allows it to be."[1] "In spite of all this nonsense from the left, we are going to win. We're in the process of taking this country back. No one in the audience should be despairing," Roberts said, adding that "in spite of all of the injustice… we are going to prevail."[1] Calling the ruling "vital," he pointed to Federalist No. 70, attributed in the source report to Alexander Hamilton, in which the case is made for a strong executive leader.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The Badlands commentary by [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treats the subject as "radioactive" and redirects it away from retribution.[1] It argues that many participants in the movement crave catharsis after what the writer describes as a serious collective trauma, and counsels patience, promising that catharsis will come and "will give you your life back."[1]

The essay insists the process "isn't about justice" alone — although accountability for alleged traitors and pedophiles is said to be necessary for a legitimate government — but is instead about the society, culture and "more perfect union" that participants will build for their children and for posterity.[1] Above all, the writer argues, it concerns "the mentality that we will all adopt," requiring an internal transformation in each participant if the revolution is to be a lasting success.[1]

## Digital 1776

The commentary cites [[gmoney|GMONEY]]'s framing of the moment as a "Digital 1776" — a revolution against the banking system waged with Bitcoin — noting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had just called Bitcoin a "strategic reserve asset."[1] The writer declines to make the argument about Bitcoin itself, instead holding up Gmoney as an exemplar of total sovereignty: a man who once favored gold and silver but has since emotionally disengaged from the system entirely, adopting a "First Principles mindset" and becoming, in the writer's account, a free man who will never be subservient to another earthly master.[1]

The section closes by framing the peaceful revolution as an exercise in believing in oneself and in one another as the precondition for self-governance, and calls on readers to "finish what the Founding Fathers started in 1775," which the writer identifies as "a different way of thinking."[1]
