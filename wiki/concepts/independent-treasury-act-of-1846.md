# Independent Treasury Act of 1846

The **Independent Treasury Act of 1846** was a United States law that, in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], prevented the federal government from influencing economic affairs, leaving currency issuance entirely to private banks.[1]

Together with the 1836 killing of the national bank under [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]], the act produced a highly de-regulated monetary system in which every private bank could issue currency with no federal authority.[1] By the time of the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] there were over 7,000 recognized bank notes in the United States, issued by more than 1,496 banks each circulating multiple notes.[1]

Under this breakdown of finances no national projects were possible, international investments were scarce, and free-market money worshipping ran rampant; manufacturing collapsed, speculation took over, and the slavocracy grew in influence between the bank panic of 1837 and 1860.[1] The system was reversed only when [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] restored federal authority over banking through the [[national-bank-act-of-1863|Bank Act of 1863]] and issued [[greenbacks|greenbacks]] under the [[legal-tender-act-of-1862|Legal Tender Act of 1862]].[1]
