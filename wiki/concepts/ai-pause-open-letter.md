# AI Pause Open Letter (2023)

The AI pause open letter was published on March 22, 2023 and called "on all AI labs to immediately pause for at least 6 months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4."[1] Signatories cited by Badlands Media include [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]], Aza Raskin (rendered "Ava Raskin" in the article), [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]], and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1]

## Criticism

Badlands' assessment is that a pause cannot work if the first of [[mo-gawdat|Mo Gawdat]]'s "three inevitables" holds — namely that [[artificial-intelligence|AI]] is happening, that it is becoming far smarter than humans, and that really bad things will happen.[1] Former Microsoft chief executive [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] made the same argument to ABC News in May 2023: "If you just pause the good guys and you don't pause everyone else, you're probably hurting yourself." The article's author says she did not expect to agree with Gates but concludes he is right, and that one only hurts oneself by complying.[1]

A second objection is asymmetry of capability. If AI capabilities exist for actors such as the drug cartels, law enforcement must counter them, requiring AI policing; and unlike nuclear weapons, which cannot make more nuclear weapons, AIs can create more AIs.[1] A third is that the capabilities the public is permitted to see — the [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]]s of the world — are toys compared with what governments, tech R&D firms, and defense contractors are developing, and none of those programs will stop, since doing so would be competitive and national-security suicide.[1]

Finally, the article notes that the open letter itself provides caveats for research, so the calls for a pause relate to the commercial side of these capabilities and really just to public access.[1] Meanwhile there is no evidence of any pause among institutional actors: the [[united-nations|United Nations]], the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]], and [[pwc|PwC]] all continued to promote AI development through mid-2023.[1]
