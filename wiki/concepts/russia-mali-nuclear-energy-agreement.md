# Russia–Mali Nuclear Energy Agreement

The Russia–Mali nuclear energy agreement is a cooperation deal on the peaceful use of nuclear energy announced by the Russian state nuclear corporation [[rosatom|Rosatom]] on Tuesday, June 24, 2025.[1]

## Terms

The agreement covers infrastructure development, joint projects in healthcare and agriculture, and the training of specialists from the West African country.[1] Rosatom head [[aleksey-likhachev|Alexey Likhachev]] said that "signing this agreement marks a significant milestone in the strategic partnership between Russia and Mali," adding that the deal would support [[mali|Mali]]'s energy and scientific development while "opening up wide-ranging opportunities for both countries."[1]

## Diplomatic context

The agreement followed a meeting the previous day between Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Mali's interim president General [[assimi-goita|Assimi Goita]], at which Putin highlighted promising areas for future cooperation including geological exploration, natural resources, energy, logistics, and humanitarian fields.[1] The two countries had been strengthening ties since an initial memorandum on nuclear cooperation was signed in October 2023, which paved the way for the 2025 deal.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that roughly 45 percent of Mali's population lacks access to electricity, and that Russia had signed a similar agreement the week before with Mali's neighbour [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], where 80 percent of the population is without electricity.[1] He framed these moves as "the essence of what we expect from the Golden Age," in which cheap, affordable energy creates an abundance capable of lifting the world out of poverty and elevating humanity into a new epoch, and suggested the Sahel states might become the definitive case study in how quickly a nuclear energy program can transform a society.[1] The framing aligns with the broader Badlands narrative of a [[nuclear-energy-pivot|nuclear energy pivot]].[1]
