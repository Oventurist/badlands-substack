# A Journey of Ever-changing Truths

"A Journey of Ever-changing Truths," subtitled "How Can Anyone Believe They Have All the Answers?", is an opinion essay published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on April 15, 2025 and written by contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]. The piece is structured as an intellectual autobiography: the author traces how successive rounds of research repeatedly overturned conclusions he had previously believed to be final, and uses that arc to argue that the most effective form of information control is not outright lying but the strategic release of partial truths. The article carries the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributors' views do not necessarily represent those of Badlands Media itself, and it recorded 190 likes at the time of capture.

## The argument from partial truths

The essay opens with the claim that there are "truths, there are lies, and there are partial truths," and that the last category is the most dangerous. According to the source, a partial truth that appears to satisfy a person's need for an explanation causes them to stop searching, making the dissemination of half-truths "an effective way of keeping curious minds from finding the full truth."

Carlson illustrates the point with a hypothetical: describing a man who "shot and killed another man in his home" sounds like cold-blooded murder, but adding that the deceased had broken in and was holding a knife to the shooter's wife's throat transforms the shooter from killer to hero without altering a single fact of the killing itself. The article generalizes this into a media critique, asserting flatly that "the media makes a living off of pushing half-truths."

The author offers an audience-segmentation model of propaganda: he estimates that roughly 80 percent of Americans "will accept anything they are told if it comes from a man with nice hair sitting behind a newsroom desk, or a person wearing a white lab coat and a stethoscope," while the remaining 20 percent are more skeptical, and it is for that skeptical minority that half-truths — statements that are technically true but stripped of context — are manufactured. Finding the truth, he writes, is "like peeling an onion."

## Race riots on a thirty-year cycle

The essay's extended case study begins with the 1991 beating of [[rodney-king|Rodney King]] by LAPD officers. Carlson recounts his own initial reaction of moral outrage, then notes the question he says he never thought to ask at the time: who was recording the beating, and why did that person happen to have a camcorder ready in the era before cell-phone cameras? He judges the perfectly framed nighttime balcony footage "not impossible, but rather unlikely" to have occurred organically, and states that he now believes the recording was staged.

The article then recounts the 1992 acquittal of four officers and the resulting Los Angeles riots, including the beating of a white truck driver pulled from his vehicle and Korean convenience-store owners defending their shops with rifles from rooftops. The riots, the source argues, destroyed whatever sympathy the black community had gained from the King beating.

From this Carlson advances his central hypothesis: that the "[[cabal|Deep State Cabal]] playbook" requires "man-made, manipulated race riots to occur every thirty years or so," on the theory that without a periodic racially divisive incident "racism could possibly start to disappear." He offers a chronology as evidence — the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] incident and BLM protests of 2020, the King beating of 1991 and the riots of 1992, the Watts riots of 1965, and the Harlem race riots of 1935 — and asserts that the pattern is deliberate. Of the Floyd case he writes that many readers now believe the killing was "completely staged, or at least, that there's something not right about it."

## Prisons, the 1994 Crime Bill, and O.J. Simpson

The article situates the early 1990s push for racial division within what it describes as the privatization of the American prison system under President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]]. With private companies newly positioned to run prisons for profit, the source argues, falling crime rates threatened the business model, and so Clinton and then-Senator [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] produced the Crime Bill of 1994, which increased sentences for non-violent offenders — "aka drug dealers." Kickbacks, the article claims, flowed to the politicians who passed the new crime bills. See prison privatization.

Carlson notes that 1994 was also the year [[oj-simpson|O. J. Simpson]] allegedly killed his ex-wife and a man he believed to be her lover. He describes the white Ford Bronco slow-speed chase across the Los Angeles freeways and the round-the-clock coverage that followed — adding the aside that more pizzas were delivered that day than any other day in American history — and characterizes the saturation coverage as "like mind control; check that, it literally WAS mind control." The verdict, delivered by a jury with one white juror, produced no riots but, in his telling, a "deep-seated anger" that split black and white opinion.

## Gangster rap, the CIA, and the drug trade

Looking back to the late 1980s, the essay describes a shift in rap music away from themes of positivity, unity, and ending violence toward "shooting cops and selling drugs" — the birth of gangster rap. The article states that this apparently organic change was in fact "spurred on by the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[cia|CIA]]," which it says were "running record labels," such that hip-hop artists who did not rap about crime had no place in the genre. The purpose, per the source, was to influence young black men toward criminality and thereby fill the newly privatized prisons.

To supply the drugs needed for drug convictions, the article continues, the CIA smuggled narcotics from South and Central America into South Central Los Angeles, where Rick Ross — described as an aspiring young tennis player who could not read — became "fabulously rich." Carlson summarizes the scheme as trading guns in Central America for drugs, selling those drugs on American city streets, and filling private prison cells in the process, concluding wryly that "no one can accuse the CIA of not being good at their job."

## Organized crime and the Godfather thesis

The second half of the essay applies the same layered-truth method to organized crime. Carlson describes believing as a child, on the strength of Hollywood films such as *The Godfather*, *The Godfather Part II*, *Goodfellas*, and *Casino*, that the Italian Mafia ran organized crime in America. He states that he later concluded the Jewish Mafia sat above it, headed by [[meyer-lansky|Meyer Lansky]], with [[mickey-cohen|Mickey Cohen]] running Hollywood and [[bugsy-siegel|Bugsy Siegel]] running Las Vegas until Lansky had Siegel killed after discovering Siegel's girlfriend was skimming money.

Peeling further, the article notes that the novel *The Godfather* was published by a Jewish man, the film produced by a Jewish man, and the studio executive who greenlit it also Jewish, and claims that Mickey Cohen's men staged a shotgun attack on the producer's car to create the impression that the Mafia opposed the film. Carlson reasons that had the Mafia genuinely opposed the production, neither the film nor its sequels would have been made and the producer "certainly wouldn't have survived the shotgun attack unscathed." The film's real function, he argues, was to allow the Jewish Mafia to "disappear into the shadows" by making the Italian Mafia synonymous with organized crime in the public mind. Above the Jewish Mafia, in his pecking order, stands the CIA — which, he writes, could not allow the public to know that "their guy, Meyer Lansky," ran the agency's drugs out of the Golden Triangle during the Vietnam War. He notes the timing: the novel appeared in 1969 and the film in 1972, "in the heat of the Vietnam War."

## From Israel as culprit to Israel as scapegoat

Carlson recounts a subsequent phase of his thinking in which he attributed global power to Jewish overrepresentation, citing the heads of most movie studios, the CEOs of media companies and of large American corporations across sectors, and figures in Washington, and asking how a group constituting two percent of the American population could be so heavily represented in positions of power and influence. For a few months, he writes, he was content to conclude "that Jews ran the world, that Israel ran the world."

He then rejects that conclusion by the same logic he applied to the Italian Mafia: that a visible group may be a scapegoat propped up to conceal a higher one. "I'm not saying some aren't willing participants," he writes, "but what if there is a group above them."

## The City of London thesis

The essay's terminal claim is that the [[city-of-london|City of London]] — the one-square-mile financial district — sits atop the global organized crime syndicate and is where the [[cabal|Cabal]] resides. According to the source, the City of London controls the United States, Israel, Iran, and China alike, and sits at the top of the Deep State. Above the CIA and [[mossad|Mossad]], he writes, is British [[mi6|MI6]]; above Tel Aviv and Washington is the City of London. The Cabal, in his description, consists of Jews and non-Jews alike — including "Jews who masquerade as Jews, but who are really Satanists," fake Christians, fake Muslims, and atheists — bound together not by ethnicity or religion but by Satan worship. Criminals running the United States and Israel are, in this framing, "merely middle management."

The article offers Russia as its historical proof. Russia, it argues, has been cast as the perpetual enemy because it has stood up to the City of London for centuries: during the American Revolutionary War it rebuffed England's demand for an alliance against the Americans, and during the American Civil War Russian ships blocked American ports to keep Britain from intervening on behalf of the South. Communism, the source claims, was "unleashed" on Russia to bring the rebellious nation to heel; the Bolshevik Revolution delivered that control, which lasted until the early 1990s. As evidence of Russian lives being treated as expendable, the article contrasts the 450,000 English dead of the Second World War with 24 million Soviet dead — "50 times more."

## Trump versus the City of London

The essay closes by reframing current events as a covert war between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the City of London rather than between the United States and Russia, China, or Iran. Since early in his first presidency, the source claims, Trump has replaced the LIBOR system with SOFR, transferring control of the American banking system from the City of London to the [[federal-reserve|American Federal Reserve]]. In his second term he is onshoring manufacturing out of Communist China — described as itself run by the City of London — and back to America. The article reads the fall of the Soviet Union in the early 1990s alongside Japan's subsequent thirty-year economic stagnation and China's rise as evidence that the City of London simply "pivoted from controlling the Soviet Union to Communist China." Tariffs, in this account, "aren't just about creating fair trade; they're about removing the Globalist power that stems from the City of London."

Carlson borrows two images from the film *Office Space* to make the point. Israel, Iran, and China are like the manager wandering cubicle to cubicle with his coffee mug: the recognizable face, but not the decision-maker. DOGE is like the two "Bobs" brought in to interview employees about what they actually do, exposing redundancy and triggering mass layoffs and company-wide fear. He concludes that while much of MAGA demands to see middle management "hanging from the gallows," Trump is pursuing the puppet masters: "While we see an ant in our kitchen and think to step on it, Trump is laying out poison, so they might bring it back to the rest of the colony."
