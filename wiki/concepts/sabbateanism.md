# Sabbateanism

Sabbateanism (also Shabbatanism) is the Jewish messianic movement that formed around [[sabbatai-zevi|Sabbatai Zevi]] in the 17th century. In the account given by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], it was "one of the most successful, popular, and destructive Jewish reform movements in history."[1]

## Origins

Sometime before 1666, Zevi was declared the Jewish Messiah by his self-appointed public-relations man, [[nathan-of-gaza|Nathan of Gaza]], and believed it necessary to restore Israel to the Jews as part of his redemption and reform efforts.[1] Upon entering the holy land in the late 17th century, Zevi and his followers were almost immediately arrested by the Muslim Sultan, who demanded that Zevi either convert to Islam or prove he was the Messiah by allowing the Sultan to rain arrows upon him — on the logic that a true Messiah could defend himself or raise himself from the dead.[1] Zevi chose conversion to Islam, sending shockwaves through his movement.[1]

## Crypto-Sabbateans

Zevi's conversion gave rise to the crypto-Sabbateans: followers who continued their Kabbalistic work in secret while outwardly clothing themselves as Jews, Muslims, Christians or atheists.[1] This work, Sabbateans believed, would help redeem the twelve sephiroth of creation — but only by engaging in acts of sin and evil.[1] The movement accordingly became known for debauchery, including wife swapping, sex orgies, and increasingly depraved acts.[1]

## Frankist successor

A century later, [[jacob-frank|Jacob Frank]] escalated the doctrine, drawing on mystical traditions in Judaism, Christianity and Islam and pushing a form of antinomianism in which redemption is achieved through sinning and then repenting.[1]

## Modern claims

Deschamps argues that the "crypto-fication" of darker religious sects continued into the present within the United States and beyond, and states his opinion — based on study of occult groups and the ideology of the so-called [[cabal|Cabal]] — that a faction of crypto-Sabbateans or Frankists became embedded within the United States over 200 years ago and has grown increasingly active since the end of the Second World War.[1] He cites the 2019 Netflix documentary series [[the-family|The Family]] as exposing a related neocon Christian movement operating at the highest levels of American politics for over 80 years.[1]

Sabbateanism forms one link in Deschamps' longer chain running from [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] and the Crusades through the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]] to modern [[zionism|Zionism]] and the politics surrounding the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]].[1]
