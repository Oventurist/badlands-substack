# 2023 US Arms Surge to Israel

The 2023 US arms surge to Israel refers to the rapid American effort, beginning in the days after the 7 October 2023 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack on southern [[israel|Israel]], to move additional weapons and munitions into Israeli hands as Israel declared a "complete siege" of [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## Announcement and initial shipments

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] confirmed in a speech on Tuesday, 10 October 2023, that the United States would begin "surging" defense aid to Israel.[1] A senior defense official said the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was "surging support" to its closest Middle Eastern ally in response to what US officials described as the brutal new tactics of the weekend assault, with the combined death toll exceeding 1,500 and Israel's government having ordered a full-scale siege of the Gaza Strip on Monday.[1] A Pentagon official told [[politico|Politico]] that planes carrying new military aid had "already taken off," with the United States sending air defense equipment and munitions.[1] Israel was reported to have requested precision-guided munitions, small-diameter bombs, and interceptors for its [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] missile defense system.[1]

## Funding mechanisms

Israel receives $3.8 billion in military aid each year from the United States, including $500 million to fund air defense development and $3.3 billion in Foreign Military Financing, which provides foreign governments money to purchase US arms.[1] Neither category permits direct shipments from US military stockpiles; however, according to Politico, roughly $100 million remained available under the [[presidential-drawdown-authority|Presidential Drawdown Authority]], which does allow such transfers.[1] The Pentagon worked to determine which stockpiled arms could be made available while pressing the arms industry to expedite deliveries of weapons Israel had already purchased.[1]

## Competition with Ukraine

Analysts noted that Israel could begin competing with Ukraine for scarce US munitions.[1] Israel's initial focus on airstrikes against Gaza required munitions Ukraine does not use, but an anticipated ground invasion would require 155mm artillery rounds, which were in short supply after the United States shipped millions of shells to Ukraine.[1] The [[washington-post|Washington Post]] reported that the Biden administration was considering combining its request for additional Ukraine war spending with new aid for Israel, an approach described as intended to "jam the far right" — a reference to Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] who oppose the Ukraine proxy war but are staunch supporters of Israel.[1] No final decision had been made at the time, and the United States was already shipping arms under existing authorities.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that "narrative convergence is becoming actual convergence," contending that openly tying the two aid packages together acknowledged both that Ukraine fatigue had reached an all-time high and that support for Israel was being pursued because emotional momentum favored it — a tacit admission, in his framing, that foreign aid is driven more by public-relations campaigns and social media trends than by justice.[1] He linked the episode to the broader warmongering of what Badlands writers call the Establishment [[uniparty|Uniparty]].[1]
