# Executive Order 6102

Executive Order 6102 is the April 5, 1933 order by which US President [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]] forbade "the hoarding of gold coin, gold bullion, and gold certificates within the continental United States."[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] characterizes the order as unconstitutional and, for those able to grasp what was happening, an open display of tyranny.[1] He notes that it was issued under the authority of several other acts he describes as equally repugnant to the spirit of freedom woven into the founding documents of the United States: the [[trading-with-the-enemy-act-of-1917|Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917]] and the [[emergency-banking-act-of-1933|Emergency Banking Act]], signed in March of 1933.[1]

The order is presented as a case study in a recurring pattern: when economic trouble brews, whether manufactured or organic, the ruling class imposes sanctions on using gold as money.[1] In this reading, monetary authorities — banks, a reigning king, or a commander-in-chief — have several times in history decreed that gold cannot be used as money, and financial chaos usually follows, with the less well-off hit hardest while elites, typically tipped off in advance, benefit from the resulting transfer of wealth.[1] The episode is offered as evidence that a [[gold-standard|gold standard]] alone cannot protect citizens, because a government can simply prohibit gold's monetary use.[1]

Deschamps adds the constitutional objection that a government cannot lawfully decide unilaterally to change monetary policy — such as ending the gold standard — unless the citizens have granted it that power, and that in the United States successive "emergencies" have been used to claim a near-total control over commerce that the government never lawfully had.[1]
