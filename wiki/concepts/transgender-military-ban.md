# Transgender Military Ban

The **transgender military ban** is a policy of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration that presumptively disqualifies transgender people from service in the United States armed forces.[1]

## Supreme Court order (2025)

On a Tuesday in May 2025 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] allowed the administration to enforce the ban while legal challenges proceeded.[1] The court acted in a dispute over a policy that presumptively disqualifies transgender people from military service and could lead to the expulsion of experienced, decorated officers.[1] The court's three liberal justices said they would have kept the policy on hold; neither the justices in the majority nor those in dissent explained their votes, which is not uncommon in emergency appeals.[1]

## Executive action

Just after beginning his second term in January, Trump moved aggressively to roll back the rights of transgender people.[1] Among his actions was an executive order claiming that the sexual identity of transgender service members "conflicts with a soldier's commitment to an honorable, truthful, and disciplined lifestyle, even in one's personal life" and is harmful to military readiness.[1]

## Commentary

*Badlands News Brief* contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] commented on the Associated Press account, noting that one service member described in the piece as a decorated military veteran transitioned after Trump's ban took effect.[1]

## Reversal and the 2023 DOD care memo

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] ended [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ban on transgender military service at the beginning of his tenure, after which the administration progressively expanded benefits for service members who identify as transgender.[2] A 34-page [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] memo obtained and published by The Dossier in July 2023 documented those benefits, including deployment waivers and indefinite physical fitness waivers.[2] Badlands contributors [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the policy created a permanently non-deployable cohort and applied a double standard relative to the COVID-19 vaccine mandate, which had been justified on [[military-readiness|military readiness]] grounds.[2]

## January 2025 executive order on accommodations

In late January 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was reported to be preparing an executive order prohibiting taxpayer-funded accommodations for trans-identifying people in the military, covering hormone replacement drugs, cosmetic genital surgery, cosmetic breast removal, and mixed-sex housing.[3] The Federalist argued that no one was being banned from the military and that only radical transgender accommodations were affected, while noting that media coverage framed the order as a civil-rights setback — a CNN screen graphic during a Monday interview segment described it as "banning transgender service members."[3]

According to the New York Post, a White House document previewing the order set out the administration's reasoning: "It can take a minimum of 12 months for an individual to complete treatments after transition surgery, which often involves the use of heavy narcotics," it said, adding that "during this period, they are not physically capable of meeting military readiness requirements and require ongoing medical care. This is not conducive for deployment or other readiness requirements."[3]
