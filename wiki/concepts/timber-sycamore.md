# Timber Sycamore

Timber Sycamore was a [[cia|CIA]] program through which the agency armed and trained militant groups aimed at pressuring the government of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in Syria.[1]

Badlands Media cites the program as public evidence that both the CIA and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] were involved in funding and bolstering Syrian proxy groups, while the Pentagon's own efforts were focused more on fighting [[isis|ISIS]], particularly through support for the predominantly Kurdish [[syrian-democratic-forces|Syrian Democratic Forces]].[1] The termination of the CIA's program in 2017 under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was seen as a shift in U.S. policy toward counter-terrorism rather than regime change.[1]

The article invokes Timber Sycamore to argue that the rebel forces who toppled Assad in December 2024 were backed by foreign money, arms, and motives, situating the program within a longer lineage of proxy warfare that runs from CIA support for the [[mujahideen|Mujahideen]] against the Soviets forward to [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]].[1]

## Invoked at the Gabbard confirmation hearing

Operation Timber Sycamore resurfaced publicly in February 2025 when [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], under questioning from Arizona Senator [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]] at her [[director-of-national-intelligence|Director of National Intelligence]] confirmation hearing, used the program to explain her opposition to the Obama-era CIA campaign against [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[2]

Badlands Media summarized the program as an Obama/Clinton-era CIA effort to arm Sunni Islamist mercenary rebels including the Free Syrian Army, the Al Qaeda affiliate Al-Nusra Front, and ISIS, conducted in alliance with Gulf Arab partners such as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates as well as Turkey and Jordan, with weapons supplied by Saudi intelligence and the CIA's Special Activities Division.[2] Over roughly four years, nearly 100,000 Syrian Arab Army soldiers were killed or wounded, which the publication says prompted Russia's intervention in the Syrian war in the autumn of 2015 and its bombing of NATO-backed Al Qaeda mercenaries.[2] Kelly did not dispute Gabbard's account but objected that the same criticisms had appeared in Russian and Chinese state media.[2]
