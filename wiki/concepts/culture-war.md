# Culture War

The **culture war** is the term used for the recurring set of social conflicts — over religion, sexuality, gender identity, race, and national symbolism — that have enveloped the American political process and electoral cycles.[1] In the framing used by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writers, the culture war is not simply an organic clash of values but an arena that can be deliberately provoked and harvested by political and media actors.

## The 2024 Easter episode

A representative flashpoint occurred at the end of March 2024, when President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s proclamation recognizing March 31 as [[transgender-day-of-visibility|Transgender Day of Visibility]] fell on Easter Sunday.[1] Conservatives accused the president of stifling Christian values; House leadership and the [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign demanded an apology from the [[white-house|White House]]; transgender advocates called the controversy manufactured.[1] [[politico|Politico]] explicitly framed the story with the line "Cue the culture war."[1]

## Analysis as an information operation

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] described the episode as an instructive case of the "online outrage machine" and its use for emotional manipulation.[1] He identified a two-sided mechanism: conservatives are drawn into divisive social-media eddies where they reinforce a collective feeling of being attacked, producing reactionary behavior and fear of the erasure of Christian identity, while the resulting reaction becomes propaganda fodder fed to left-wing audiences.[1] Esler argued that curated outrage is one of the adversary's principal advantages precisely because the outraged party is often morally and objectively correct about the nature of the attack, and that the strategic cost is the diversion of emotional energy away from the physical domain and away from building a parallel society.[1]

Related commentary in the same brief treated the escalating contrast between Biden and Trump as another front of the same conflict, describing the juxtapositions as "cartoonish" as the [[information-war|Info War]] accelerated toward the November 2024 inflection point.[1]

## As an electoral strategy

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s December 2023 [[deep-state-spotlight|Deep State Spotlight]] profile of [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] presented the culture war as a career strategy increasingly popular in both parties: focusing on divisive cultural conflict that appeals to voters' emotions while keeping a politician's true goals hidden.[2] Newsom, DeLarme wrote, has for years used culture-war obsession to bait Republican leaders, repeatedly raising firearms, immigration, race, abortion and contraception — issues quite popular with the Democratic base — while framing his advocacy as an existential struggle to preserve American democracy.[2]

The function of these incursions, in DeLarme's reading, is concealment: they mask a record that is scarcely revolutionary, persuading voters to overlook corruption and administrative failure by persistently invoking basic liberties that are rarely genuinely at issue in today's California.[2] He likened the resulting persona to [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]]-style content personified as a politician, but representing the more divisive aspects of leftist ideology, and observed that a culture-war onslaught was sufficient to eradicate genuine electoral opposition in Newsom's 2022 re-election.[2] The same dynamic was visible in the [[great-red-vs-blue-state-debate|Great Red vs. Blue State Debate]], where DeLarme argued outlets such as [[con-inc|Con Inc]] contribute to and exacerbate the perpetual left-right divide instead of emphasizing common ground.[2]
