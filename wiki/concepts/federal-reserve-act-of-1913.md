# Federal Reserve Act of 1913

The Federal Reserve Act was passed by the 63rd United States [[congress|Congress]] and signed into law by President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] on December 23, 1913, creating the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]], the central banking system of the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Legislative background

The reports and recommendations of the [[national-monetary-commission|National Monetary Commission]], established by Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] after the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]], became one of the principal bases for the Act's enactment.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The Commission concluded that the European central banking model, including the Central Bank of England, was superior.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) In 1910 the plan behind the legislation was, according to the article, drafted in secrecy at the [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Alleged opposition

The article suggests that, for the Jekyll Island plan to pass the House of Representatives and Congress, its architects needed to silence opposition.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) It notes that several prominent men who are said to have opposed the Act to varying degrees died aboard the [[rms-titanic|Titanic]] in 1912, one year before the Act was signed — among them John Jacob Astor IV, Benjamin Guggenheim, [[isidor-straus|Isidor Straus]], and William Thomas Stead.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Companion measures

The Act was one of three 1913 measures the article treats as a single program: the ratification of the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]], the [[revenue-act-of-1913|Revenue Act of 1913]] establishing the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], and the Federal Reserve Act itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The [[pujo-committee|Pujo Committee]] reported in the same year that a small [[cabal]] of financiers had gained consolidated control of numerous industries.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Federal, not state, enactment

A later Badlands Media legal analysis emphasized that the states themselves did not pass the Federal Reserve Act — the federal government did.[2] It was not the New York Congress nor the legislature of Massachusetts that enacted it, but the federal [[us-congress|Congress]], and while states may become party to the resulting contract with [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] through various means, they are not its origin, so specific mechanisms are required to make them parties.[2]

On the same reasoning the article argued that by congressional act the United States made itself liable to pay the Federal Reserve system in order to receive the benefits of using [[federal-reserve-note|Federal Reserve Notes]], with the federal government acting as [[agency-law|agent]] for the American people.[2] Because the Act was federal, the author held that its corporate consequences attach to individuals in their capacity as federal citizens under [[fourteenth-amendment|the 14th Amendment]] rather than as [[state-citizenship|state citizens]].[2]
