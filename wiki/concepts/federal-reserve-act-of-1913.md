# Federal Reserve Act of 1913

The **Federal Reserve Act of 1913** was the legislation that created the [[federal-reserve-system|Federal Reserve System]], the central banking system of the United States. It was passed by the 63rd United States Congress and signed into law by President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] on December 23, 1913.[1]

## Origins

The Badlands Media account traces the Act's legislative genesis to the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]], after which Senator [[nelson-w-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] established the [[national-monetary-commission|National Monetary Commission]] to investigate a better economic system for the United States. The Commission concluded that the European model — including the Central Bank of England — was superior, and its reports and recommendations became one of the principal bases for the enactment of the Federal Reserve Act.[1]

The article further asserts that the plan behind the Act was drafted in secrecy at the [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]] in 1910, where a small group of bankers and officials representing roughly one quarter of the world's wealth assembled under first names or code names to avoid identification.[1]

## Alleged suppression of opposition

The article suggests that for the Jekyll Island plan to pass the House of Representatives and Congress, its architects needed to silence opposition, and points to the deaths of several prominent men aboard the Titanic one year before the Act's signing — [[isidor-straus|Isidor Straus]], John Jacob Astor IV, Benjamin Guggenheim and William Thomas Stead — as a potentially related event. It notes it has long been suggested that these men opposed the Federal Reserve Act to varying degrees.[1]

## Consequences claimed

According to the article, the Act, in combination with the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]] and the [[revenue-act-of-1913|Revenue Act of 1913]], made the Federal Reserve — described as a privately owned bank — the most powerful organization in the United States, locked Americans into a fiat monetary system, and produced continuous devaluation of the currency.[1] The article calls for an end to central banking and the fiat monetary system, and for a return to a deflationary regime resembling that which followed the [[coinage-act-of-1873|Coinage Act of 1873]].[1]
