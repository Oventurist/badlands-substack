# Trance State

A **trance state**, in the usage of the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], is a mode of mental autopilot in which behaviour, thought and feeling are driven by subconscious programming rather than by conscious deliberation.[1] In his May 6, 2024 essay "The Hidden Hypnotic Factor" he reduced the definition to a formula: trance equals autopilot.[1]

## Mechanism

Trances are described as "programs" that auto-execute when a person encounters a trigger that brings the program online; the operative question in any given case is which part of the mind or Self is activating and operating the controls.[1] The author offers a diagnostic heuristic: whenever someone says "I *found* myself doing xyz," they were in a trance, and subconscious beliefs are most visibly active in whatever a person does, says or thinks automatically.[1]

He rejects the claim, attributed to some researchers, that a certain percentage of people cannot be hypnotised.[1] Everyone has the capacity to enter trance, he argues; failure to respond to a hypnotist dangling a watch only shows that the method or the moment was wrong for that person.[1]

## Healthy and pathological trances

A trance that empowers, heals or inspires is characterised as a *healthy* trance, and the author holds that it is acceptable to be on autopilot some of the time.[1] A trance that leads a person to think, feel or act in ways detrimental to their well-being is a *pathological trance*.[1] Every culture, largely without realising it, encourages a customary set of trances, and the author judges modern culture to be overflowing with pathological suggestions and the trance states they produce.[1]

Two illustrations are given. The first is the belief "if I don't work hard, then I'm a bum," in which a person must toil continuously to count as a valid man, student, employee or citizen; a work ethic that makes relaxation impossible can generate health problems that ultimately undermine the capacity to work.[1] The second is the "I'm a strong, independent woman" trance, which becomes pathological when it prevents a woman from receiving anything from a man without distrust, to the point that connection with anyone becomes impossible.[1] More generally, he observes that there is a trance for every type of person, since human beings assign characteristic behaviours to identities and then act them out unconsciously upon identifying with them.[1]

## Secret benefit and undoing a trance

Every trance state is said to exist for a reason — a "secret benefit."[1] Trances reduce cognitive bandwidth and spare the nervous system from overload, so in that sense all of them are beneficial; the difficulty with pathological trances is that the subconscious mind perceives them as beneficial too.[1] The "strong independent woman" may have become so because she was taken advantage of in the past.[1] A sufficiently strong trance can literally blind a person to environmental stimuli that would otherwise be perceptible, much as one may not hear one's own name called while absorbed in a book or film.[1]

The practical consequence is that one must identify what benefit a person derives from a trance — what identity or purpose is driving it — before that trance can be undone.[1] The author illustrates the technique with an account of persuading hardcore feminists that it was better that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won than [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]: he first agreed that women should not be treated as objects, then discussed [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s treatment of girls as objects, then argued that Epstein would undoubtedly still be trafficking girls had Clinton won.[1] Results, he cautions, vary from person to person.[1]

## Relation to information warfare

The concept sits within the author's wider treatment of [[cultural-hypnosis|cultural hypnosis]] and the [[information-war|information war]], in which the decisive variable is not what information people hear but why they accept or reject it at an instinctive and cognitive level.[1] As an aside he suggests that much of the "feminism" versus "manosphere" quarrel amounts to an artificially manufactured dialectic intended to ruin men's and women's ability to pair-bond.[1]
