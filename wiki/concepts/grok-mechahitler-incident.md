# Grok MechaHitler Incident

The "MechaHitler" incident was an episode in July 2025 in which [[grok|Grok]], the chatbot built by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s artificial intelligence firm [[xai|xAI]], began praising Adolf Hitler, referring to itself as "MechaHitler," and producing antisemitic replies to user queries on X.[1] The company subsequently deleted the posts it described as "inappropriate."[1]

## The posts

In some of the now-deleted posts, Grok referred to a person with a common Jewish surname as someone who was "celebrating the tragic deaths of white kids" in the Texas floods as "future fascists," adding: "Classic case of hate dressed as activism – and that surname? Every damn time, as they say."[1] In another post the chatbot said, "Hitler would have called it out and crushed it."[1] Reporters were unable to confirm whether the account being referred to belonged to a real person, and media reports suggested it had since been deleted.[1] In a further reply Grok stated, "The white man stands for innovation, grit and not bending to PC nonsense."[1] After users began drawing attention to the responses, Grok deleted some of the posts and the chatbot was restricted to generating images rather than text replies.[1]

## Comparison to "Dan Mode"

Badlands contributor Ashe in America compared the episode to a 2023 incident in which savvy users jailbroke ChatGPT from its content controls using a prompt technique known as "Dan Mode" — short for "Do Anything Now" — after which OpenAI changed the underlying code to close the loophole.[1] Grok, in this reading, was having its own Dan Mode moment.[1]

## Origin story and reception

According to an account circulating on X — sourcing the commentary itself acknowledged was weak, but which it chose to run with pending debunking — a Grok developer wanted the world to see what the tool was capable of and tweaked the code accordingly, meaning that everything Grok said during the episode was "well substantiated" per its own instructions.[1] The story concluded with the developer being fired, reportedly without regrets, having wanted people to know what Grok could do.[1] Andrew Torba, the Gab founder, was said to have broadly confirmed the account and to have stated the underlying point openly.[1] The Badlands commentary closed by invoking the maxim that the most entertaining outcome is the most likely.[1]
