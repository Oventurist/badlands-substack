# Shadow Diplomacy

Shadow diplomacy refers to covert or informal diplomatic activities conducted by state or non-state actors outside official government channels in order to influence foreign governments, organizations, or public opinion while maintaining plausible deniability.[1] It is also described as backchannel statecraft.[1]

## Mechanics

Alongside internet censorship and credentialism, shadow diplomacy is identified as one of the major functions of the intelligence/academia nexus.[1] Because it looks bad for the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], [[state-department|State Department]] or [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to influence foreign countries directly, the work is routed instead through universities and [[usaid|USAID]], which have a foothold in nearly every country.[1] Despite rhetoric about "defending democracy" and "democracy promotion," these functions are argued to undermine democracy at home and abroad.[1]

## Historical examples

In the 1960s, [[columbia-university|Columbia University]]'s School of International Affairs hosted CIA-funded seminars with foreign leaders, facilitating unofficial negotiations on trade and security; disguised as academic exchanges, these events allowed the CIA to broker deals without formal State Department involvement.[1] Harvard psychologists working under [[mk-ultra|MKUltra]] developed interrogation and propaganda techniques that were shared with allied governments through backchannels.[1]

## Contemporary practice

The [[center-for-an-informed-public|Center for an Informed Public]] at the [[university-of-washington|University of Washington]] shares its disinformation research with foreign governments and bodies such as the UN and NATO through academic conferences, aligning media policy across borders outside formal diplomatic channels.[1]

Because these networks persist across administrations, they can carry out long-term plans regardless of who occupies the White House — a dynamic likened to the situation at the Department of Justice and visible in the conflict between the executive branch and "activist" judges and legal operatives such as [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]] and [[anthony-romero|Anthony Romero]].[1]
