# Information War

"The info war" is the term used across [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary for what its writers describe as an ongoing contest over public perception waged through mass media, institutional authority and narrative management. The concept is developed at length in the August 30, 2024 essay [[a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi|"A Mysterious Masterclass in Multi Sensory Deception"]], which treats a South Dakota gravity-hill attraction as a working model of how such deception operates.

## Anatomy of a deception

The essay argues that effective narrative manipulation follows a repeatable pattern. It begins with "a little bit of truth" — in the Cosmos case, the genuine existence of magnetic anomalies such as the South Atlantic Anomaly and seafloor anomalies used to establish plate tectonics. Because science concedes that such phenomena can occur, the article contends, non-specialists become more willing to believe that one is occurring, a disposition it summarizes as "Trust the experts." An authority figure then repeats the claim throughout the experience, a technique the essay illustrates with a line attributed to [[william-james|William James]]: "There is nothing so absurd that it cannot be believed as truth if repeated often enough." Finally, involuntary bodily and emotional responses appear to confirm the claim, making intellectual scepticism insufficient to dispel it.

The author writes that audiences are "bombarded with 'explosive' stories and brazen emotional pleas, all of Hegel's best shots," most of which mix "a tiny bit of truth with a whole lot of deception," and that these narrative deployments land differently on each person depending on circumstance and disposition.

## "Instruments" and triangulation

Drawing on [[steven-kwast|General Steven Kwast]]'s account of pilot training and the FAA concept of [[spatial-disorientation|spatial disorientation]], the essay asks what the instruments are that a person should trust when the senses mislead. Kwast's answer, as reported, is "the trust between each of us, and our ability to use our individual skills and knowledge, working together, to triangulate what's true." The author adopts "triangulate what's true" as a governing phrase, concluding that separating facts from engineered emotional reactions and cross-checking them collectively is the essential toolset for navigating and winning the information war.

A companion quotation from an attendee identified as Diane extends the analogy to political speech and the mainstream press, comparing statements by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] to the Jedi mind trick line "these aren't the droids you're looking for" and describing "inner instruments" of instinct, intuition, discernment, intelligence and faith.
