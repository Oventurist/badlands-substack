# Obamacare Subsidy Lapse

The Obamacare subsidy lapse refers to the expiration at the end of 2025 of the enhanced premium subsidies for health insurance purchased through marketplaces created by the [[affordable-care-act|Affordable Care Act]], after [[congress|Congress]] declined to renew them before adjourning for the year.[1] The lapse exposed tens of millions of Americans to sharply higher health insurance costs.[1]

## State responses

At least a dozen states moved to shield residents from the resulting increases, with actions taken by state leaders in California, Colorado and Maryland among the most prominent.[1] In nearly every case, however, the state programs came with a significant caveat: they could only help a portion of the people whose coverage would become unaffordable without the enhanced federal subsidies.[1]

New Mexico was the only state that had, at the time of reporting, moved to cover all of the lapsed subsidies.[1] Javier Martínez, speaker of the New Mexico House, said, "We can carry the cost for a little bit, but at some point, we will need Congress to act," adding that "No state can withstand to plug in every single budget hole that the Trump administration leaves behind."[1]

Reporting by [[politico|Politico]] framed the speed with which the mostly Democratic states acted as evidence of mounting national anxiety about the medical and political consequences of the subsidies' expiration.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for the [[badlands-media|Badlands]] News Brief, dismissed Politico's framing as an exercise in portraying blue states as uniquely concerned for the insured, arguing that such actors display authentic outrage only when their own interests are threatened and that "the only time government moves quickly is when it's afraid of exposure."[1] She raised the question of whether kickbacks and fraud were embedded in the Obamacare subsidy structure itself, calling for it to be investigated.[1]
