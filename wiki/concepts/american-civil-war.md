# American Civil War

In Matthew Ehret's account, the American Civil War was not merely a domestic conflict but the decisive theatre of a global fight between the British imperial system and the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System]], with Russia intervening decisively on the Union side.[1]

## British and Canadian involvement

By 1862 the war was in full force, and with British corporations and the Foreign Office supporting the Confederacy, the tide had turned against [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]].[1] The British possessions of Toronto and Montreal served as Confederate bases from which dozens of terrorist attacks, raids, espionage operations and war financing were launched against the Union from the north, while Canada officially maintained an air of neutrality.[1] Citing [[barry-sheehy|Barry Sheehy]], Ehret states that the largest Confederate Secret Service base outside Richmond was in Montreal, under the direct control of Confederate Secretary of State [[judah-benjamin|Judah Benjamin]].[1] Over 10,000 British troops were stationed in Canada with orders to prepare a full invasion of the USA, while in Mexico Napoleon III's forces under Emperor Maximilian stood ready to invade in support of the Confederacy from the south.[1]

Ehret further asserts that [[john-wilkes-booth|John Wilkes Booth]] was deployed from Montreal to kill Lincoln, having spent more than five weeks in Canada receiving payment, training and directions from British-sponsored Confederates, and that Union officers found among his possessions a $500 cheque signed by Ontario Bank President [[henry-starnes|Henry Starnes]].[1]

## Russian intervention

Czar [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]]'s deployment of the Russian navy to the Pacific and Atlantic coasts of America in 1863 kept British and French forces from openly assisting the Confederacy.[1] Russian sailors were celebrated by crowds of thousands in San Francisco and New York.[1] The Czar later explained that he had answered the Anglo-French proposal to recognize Confederate independence by declaring such recognition a *casus belli* for Russia.[1] Ambassador [[eduard-de-stoeckl|Eduard de Stoeckl]] wrote to Foreign Minister [[alexander-gorchakov|Gorchakov]] that the disintegration of the United States would be deplorable, since the union acted as a counterpoise to British power.[1]

## Aftermath

The war's northern dimension shaped what followed: the [[british-north-america-act|British North America Act]] of 1867, prepared at the 1864 [[charlottetown-conference|Charlottetown conference]]; the [[alaska-purchase|Alaska purchase]]; and the [[alabama-claims|Alabama Claims]] arbitration over Britain's construction of Confederate warships.[1] [[jefferson-davis|Jefferson Davis]], Judah Benjamin and other Confederate rebels lived out their days in comfort in Canada and Britain.[1]

## Financial dimension

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that 150 years of revisionist historians have obscured the true nature of the Civil War as a British-run operation to undo the revolution of 1776.[2] Beyond secession, [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]] had to contend with Wall Street financiers and anglophile families who worked tirelessly to sabotage his ability to acquire the funds necessary to execute the war, while the [[city-of-london|City of London]] manipulated gold prices and orchestrated the buyout of U.S. gold reserves; loans were offered only at interest rates of 20 to 25 percent.[2] Russian Ambassador to London de Brunow reported in January 1861 that the English government desired the separation of North America into two republics that would counterbalance one another so that England might dominate both.[2]

Lincoln's response was the revival of the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System]] through the Banking and Currency Acts of 1862 and 1863, the [[national-bank-act-of-1863|Bank Act of 1863]], the issuance of [[greenbacks|greenbacks]] under the [[legal-tender-act-of-1862|Legal Tender Act of 1862]], and the sale of [[five-twenty-bonds|5:20 bonds]] organized by [[jay-cooke|Jay Cooke]].[2] These instruments financed soldiers' pay and supply as well as the [[first-transcontinental-railroad|transcontinental railway]] begun in 1863.[2] Contemplating the war's international scope in 1862, Lincoln told Congress that "we cannot escape history" and that in giving freedom to the slave "we assure freedom to the free," saving or meanly losing "the last, best hope of earth."[2]

## Russian intervention and the postwar vision

Russia under Czar [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] intervened to protect the Union against aggressive intervention by English and French imperial powers supporting secession.[3] In 1863 the Czar sent Russian battleships to New York and San Francisco as a message of solidarity with [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and a threat to the imperial forces of Europe, having told France and Britain that joint recognition of Confederate independence would be a casus belli for Russia.[3]

In the wake of the Union victory, a potential alliance of sovereign nation states was emerging globally, expressed in Lincoln's 1864 support for a Bering Strait telegraph, the 1867 Alaska purchase and [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]]'s [[cosmopolitan-railway|Cosmopolitan Railway]] vision.[3] The article frames the subsequent age of bankers' wars, anarchist assassinations and economic turmoil as the deliberate destruction of that emerging cooperation, and warns that a similar formula is being applied today to set the stage for new civil wars.[3]
