# Pezeshkian Resignation Hoax (2026)

The Pezeshkian resignation hoax was a false news story circulated at the end of May 2026 claiming that Iranian President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] had submitted a letter of resignation.[1]

## The claim

The story reported that Pezeshkian had offered his resignation "citing a lack of authority inside the country's ruling system," and accused the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] of excluding him from major and vital decision-making, creating a vacuum that allowed hardline IRGC factions to take control of affairs.[1] The claim was later proven false by the Iranian government.[1]

## Origin and spread

The story was originally published by [[iran-international|Iran International]] under the headline "Iran's president offers resignation, citing total takeover by IRGC commanders," and was cited by all subsequent outlets as their source.[1] [[fox-news|Fox News]] ran it as the lead story on its website under the headline "Iran's president reportedly submits resignation letter amid claims of deepening internal divisions," later amending the headline to "Iran president's request adds new twist to US-Iran talks as Trump weighs peace proposal" — while leaving the original article body and the accompanying "REGIME RIFT" image caption unchanged.[1] The [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] and other outlets left their original articles posted.[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he encountered the story as he was going live on The Narrative with [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and that after checking Pezeshkian's X account and finding no mention of a resignation, his instinct that the story was wrong was later vindicated by the Iranian government's denial.[1] He argued that Fox News's decision to change the headline while leaving the article intact indicated the outlet knew the story was propaganda and was managing embarrassment rather than issuing a correction.[1]

He traced Iran International to its UK parent [[volant-media|Volant Media]], owned by British-Saudi executive [[adel-abdulkarim-alabdulkarim|Adel Abdulkarim Alabdulkarim]], whom a 2018 Financial Times report accused of receiving Israeli funding — an accusation Alabdulkarim denied.[1] He characterized a UK-based "Middle East" outlet that beams its signal directly into Iranian televisions as an instrument of [[british-intelligence|British intelligence]].[1] In his assessment, the operation's goal was to gaslight audiences inside and outside [[iran|Iran]] into believing that hardliners in the IRGC had seized the civilian government, in order to build support for resuming hostilities between the United States and Iran.[1]
