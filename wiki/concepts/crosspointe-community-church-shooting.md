# CrossPointe Community Church Shooting

The CrossPointe Community Church shooting was an attempted mass shooting on Sunday, June 22, 2025, at a church in Wayne, Michigan, roughly 25 miles west of Detroit, in which the gunman was killed by church staff before he could enter the sanctuary.[1]

## The attack

Police identified the gunman as [[brian-anthony-browning|Brian Anthony Browning]], 31, of Romulus, Michigan, who was killed while trying to shoot worshippers.[1] Browning was seen at about 11 a.m. driving a silver SUV erratically in the church parking lot.[1] He parked and emerged clad in camouflage and a tactical vest, "armed with an AR-15 style rifle with more than a dozen fully loaded magazines, a semi-automatic handgun with an extended magazine and hundreds of rounds of ammunition," according to a statement the Wayne Police Department provided to the New York Daily News.[1]

Several staff members approached Browning as he began shooting, and a parishioner rammed him with a Ford F-150 pickup truck "as the gunman shot the vehicle repeatedly," Wayne Police Department Chief Ryan Strong said.[1] The church security team locked the church doors.[1] A security guard was shot in the leg before two staff members shot and killed Browning; none of those involved wanted to be publicly identified.[1] The wounded guard was in stable condition following surgery.[1]

## Investigation

Police said Browning had no criminal history or prior police contact that would have indicated a turn toward violence.[1] "His motivations are unknown, but at this point it appears he was suffering from a mental health crisis," Chief Strong told reporters at a news conference on Sunday evening.[1] Investigators said Browning's mother was a member of the congregation but was not inside the building at the time, and that Browning himself had attended a few services in the previous year without becoming a member.[1]

## Commentary

In the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that video of the incident was striking, noting that early reporting had wrongly described the truck ramming as the shooter's action when in fact it was a heroic parishioner.[1] She praised the quick action of the men in the congregation for saving lives, observed that elderly congregants and children were visible throughout the footage, and argued that churches need better active-shooter training and a greater sense of urgency in evacuating.[1] She also asked readers to pray for the shooter's mother.[1]
