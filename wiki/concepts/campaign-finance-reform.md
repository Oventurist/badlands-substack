# Campaign Finance Reform

Campaign finance reform was the signature legislative cause through which Arizona senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] built his public reputation, and which Badlands commentary characterizes as a scam designed to fool the American people.[1]

## The loophole argument

Foreign campaign contributions to U.S. elections are banned under federal law, but there is no restriction on donations to nonprofits with ties to candidates or sitting politicians.[1] Companies and foundations, some with business before [[us-congress|Congress]], contributed at least $100,000 each to the [[mccain-institute|McCain Institute]], according to *USA Today* reporting in 2014.[1] Ethics watchdog groups criticize the practice and the laws that allow it, saying such donations provide a means for foreign governments and special-interest groups to exert influence over politicians.[1]

The Badlands account argues that McCain's reform limited money given to political campaigns by Americans while doing nothing to prevent money — including foreign money — from flowing into politicians' personal institutes and foundations.[1] The concrete illustration offered is *Bloomberg*'s report that the Saudi government donated $1 million to the McCain Institute's fundraising arm, a disclosure McCain responded to by claiming he had "nothing to do with" the institute.[1]
