# Las Vegas Shooting

The Las Vegas shooting of October 2017 occupies a pivotal place in the article's narrative, both as a national moment of somber reflection and as the immediate precursor to the appearance of the anonymous poster known as [[qanon|Q]].[1]

The author, then searching inward for answers a few months after his brother's death, describes the rest of the world joining him in that reflection when the shooting occurred.[1] Just weeks later an Anon appeared on 4chan offering cryptic musings in a style not entirely unique to the board.[1]

Speculation across the internet held that the young Crown Prince — [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] — was somehow connected to the tragedy, perhaps as the intended target.[1] The author also refers to "inexplicable datapoints surrounding the Vegas Shooting that were being wiped from the internet," which together with the [[sword-dance|Sword Dance]] drew him toward research on Saudi Arabia.[1]

## Incident command and the cover-up thesis

The October 2017 attack, also known as the Route 91 shooting, is described as the worst mass shooting in American history, and the incident commander during the response was [[john-pelletier|John Pelletier]].[2] Pelletier later became police chief of Maui and led the response to the [[2023-maui-wildfires|2023 Maui wildfires]], an overlap that alarmed conservative commentators in August 2023.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the [[stephen-paddock|Stephen Paddock]] lone shooter story is "a cover up of epic proportions," pointing to 911 recordings from that night indicating shootings across the Las Vegas strip and a helicopter hijacking, alongside other suppressed evidence.[2] She added that many people investigating the incident subsequently turned up dead or went silent in the days after the tragedy, and urged readers unfamiliar with "the real story" to get up to speed.[2]

## Cited as an unresolved FBI case

In July 2024 [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used the Las Vegas Massacre of October 2017 as a benchmark for the [[fbi|FBI]]'s selective certainty, noting that the Bureau still had no clarity on the events in Las Vegas years later yet was able to declare within a day that the gunman in the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|Butler, Pennsylvania attempt on Donald Trump]] had acted alone.[3] Unlike Las Vegas, he predicted, the details of the Butler shooting would not take long to emerge because those responsible were "making it obvious to us."[3]

## Q drops #91–95 and the Vegas questions

Chapter 12 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series, covering drops #91 through #95 posted on November 5, 2017, treats the Las Vegas shooting as the hinge connecting [[seth-rich|Seth Rich]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[ms-13|MS-13]].[4] In drop #92 Q stated that Rich was mentioned only because the matter ties directly to Saudi Arabia and Las Vegas, which the author read as an indication that Rich had discovered a link between [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and the Kingdom, and that MS-13 operatives were used both in Rich's killing and in Vegas.[4]

Q's questions about the hotel — who owns it, which floors, who owns the top floors, and why that is relevant — are answered in the chapter by identifying the venue as the Mandalay Bay, whose top floors were said to be owned by [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] (called "Prince Dopey" by [[donald-trump|Trump]]) and [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], with Gates buying out the prince's stake after the shooting.[4] Ownership of the upper floors is presented as control over the high ground required to carry out an attack of that scale, and both men are characterised as possible [[deep-state|Deep State]] assets.[4]

The chapter stresses the timing: the shooting fell after Trump's visit to Saudi Arabia but before Q's first drops and before the [[saudi-purge|purge of corruption]] in the Kingdom.[4]

## Forensic objections to the official narrative

Drawing on research by [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots In Progress]], the chapter argues that the attack lasted roughly 600 seconds, during which [[stephen-paddock|Stephen Paddock]] is said to have fired about 1,100 rounds from 23 weapons at a 78 percent hit rate while reloading repeatedly, with five of the firearms carrying no optics — a performance the author regards as physically implausible for one man of Paddock's background.[4] A second tabulation of the weapons, their capacities, optics, accessories and rates of fire is cited to the same effect.[4] The author concludes that MS-13 likely supplied additional shooters, which he reads as the reason Q repeatedly asked why the gang was relevant.[4]

The chapter places the event in a lineage of killings attributed to a "lone assassin" — those of John F. Kennedy, Robert F. Kennedy, Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X — and asserts that "lone gunman" functions as an official euphemism for a covered-up clandestine operation.[4]

## Witnesses and other figures

Security guard [[jesus-campos|Jesus Campos]], whose account is said to have changed several times, disappeared shortly before scheduled television interviews.[4] The chapter also reports that eight eyewitnesses whose accounts contradicted the official narrative died soon after the attack, allegedly while planning to organise as a group and publicise their version of events.[4]

Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] is said by many researchers to have been in Las Vegas that weekend and possibly the intended target, having reportedly moved from the hotel owned by Prince Alwaleed to the Tropicana before the attack — a relocation the author finds suggestive of advance warning.[4] Q further asked who else was in Las Vegas that weekend and whom they met, and appeared to indicate that Trump himself had been present; while no proof exists, the chapter notes that Trump's official schedule was clear for the morning and afternoon of October 1, 2017.[4]

## Drop #97 and the "lone gunman" template

Chapter 13 of the Badlands Media "Q For Dummies" series addressed the portion of Drop #97, posted November 5, 2017, that returned to the Vegas shooting, noting that Q asked about the shooter and his family before listing "Force, Narrative, Race, Background" and asking why that information was relevant.[5] The author's answer was that nearly every mass-shooting scenario is presented as the work of a lone gunman: a complete loner with no apparent motive who nonetheless achieves marksmanship inconsistent with any documented history of firearms proficiency.[5] He characterised the recurring official profile as an independently wealthy white male labelled a "conspiracy nut."[5]

The chapter's conclusion was that it is never a lone gunman and the official narrative is never true, and that Q's questions amounted to a crash course in searching out details — a capability the author said now allows independent researchers to dismantle false-flag narratives within hours.[5]
