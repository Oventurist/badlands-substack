# Collective West

**"Collective West"** is a term of Russian diplomatic and rhetorical usage denoting the United States, the European Union, [[nato|NATO]] and their aligned states treated as a single political bloc acting in concert rather than as independent sovereign nations. In the Badlands corpus it is used more expansively, as a name for the transnational governing apparatus variously called the [[cabal|Cabal]], the Globalist Hegemon, or the [[deep-state|Deep State]].

## Putin's June 12, 2026 statement

On June 12, 2026 — Russia Day, the anniversary of the Russian Federation's 1990 declaration of sovereignty — President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] declared that "Russia is standing against the so-called Collective West single-handedly." He asserted that no external force could impose a strategic defeat on Moscow, described the war in Ukraine as a highly technological conflict, and said that all NATO nations were uniformly intensifying their efforts against Russia.

Putin maintained that Russia had not started the conflict: he claimed Western powers had orchestrated a coup in Ukraine (see [[maidan-coup|the Maidan coup]]), that Russia was thereby compelled to protect the population of Crimea, and that Ukrainian forces initiated large-scale hostilities by bombing Donetsk from the air. He drew historical parallels to the united European coalitions assembled against Russia under Napoleon and Hitler, framing the present war as a continuation of a recurring pattern of external pressure.

## Badlands reading

The Badlands writer Burning Bright treated the phrase as personally significant, noting that "Collective West" was the term he had used for the Globalist Hegemon in his *Righteous Russia* series. He argued that Russia represents not merely a component of the theorized [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] but perhaps its core animating engine, and read the June 12 date as signal in itself — the anniversary of Russian sovereignty, at the start of the decade that produced the Russian oligarchy and the leader who would later break it, a purge he compares to [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s anti-corruption campaign and to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s parallel effort in the West.

Quoting his June 2022 essay "Righteous Russia VII – Power is Power," Burning Bright described the West's response to Russia as a [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian]] problem–reaction sequence: Putin cast as the Problem, and the combined weight of the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] and the banking nations wielded as the Reaction. In his telling the maneuver backfired, exposing the Collective West's aggression and weakness to its own populations as energy deprivation bit from Poland to Britain. He notes that Trump has mirrored Putin's rhetoric during his disentangling of the Iranian knot, aiming public ire at the Collective West — "a paper tiger," in Trump's phrase — rather than at the Iranian government itself.
