# Collective West

"Collective West" is a term used in Russian official rhetoric — and adopted widely in alternative-media geopolitical analysis — to describe the United States, NATO members, and allied European states acting as a single bloc rather than as independent nations.[1]

## Putin's June 2026 statement

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said on June 12, 2026 that "Russia is standing against the so-called Collective West single-handedly" in the Ukraine conflict.[1] Speaking at a meeting coinciding with Russia Day, which commemorates the 1990 declaration of sovereignty, Putin asserted that no external force can impose a strategic defeat on Moscow.[1] He described the war as highly technological and said all NATO nations were uniformly intensifying efforts against Russia.[1]

Putin maintained that Russia did not start the conflict, claiming that Western powers orchestrated a coup in Ukraine which forced Russia to protect Crimea's population, and that Ukrainian forces then initiated large-scale hostilities by bombing Donetsk with warplanes.[1] He drew historical parallels to earlier united European coalitions against Russia under Napoleon and Hitler, framing the present situation as a continuation of long-running external pressure.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that he had used the phrase "Collective West" for the Globalist Hegemon in his *Righteous Russia* series, and treated Putin's usage as convergence between Russian rhetoric and the Badlands analytical frame.[1] He argued that Russia is not merely a component of a theorized Sovereign Alliance but perhaps its "core animating engine," noting that the June 12 anniversary marked the start of the decade that produced the Russian oligarchy and the leader who later purged it.[1] He compared that anti-corruption campaign to Xi Jinping's later effort and to what he described as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s parallel process in the West.[1]

Quoting his June 2022 essay "Righteous Russia VII — Power is Power," Burning Bright described the [[deep-state|Deep State]] as having offered the public a simple problem — Putin with Russia behind him — and a simple reaction of panic, fearmongering, and financial pressure via the petrodollar and allied banking nations.[1] He argued that by depriving Deep State nations of Russian energy, Putin exposed the weakness of the Western bloc and triggered a counter-dialectic that unmasked its aggressors.[1]

He further observed that Trump has directed the bulk of his public ire at the Collective West — which Trump calls a "paper tiger" — rather than at the Iranian government, during what Burning Bright called the disentangling of the Iranian Knot.[1] The open question, in his framing, is when the Sovereign Alliance consummates long-prepared deals and strips the "Collectorate" of its remaining legitimacy in the global mind.[1]
