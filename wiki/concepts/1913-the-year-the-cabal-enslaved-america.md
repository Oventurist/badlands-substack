# 1913: The Year the Cabal Enslaved America

"[[1913]]: The Year the Cabal Enslaved America" is a Badlands Media article (published November 29, 2022) that frames the year [[1913]] as the moment a small circle of financiers — which the article calls the "[[cabal]]" — consolidated permanent control over the American monetary and tax systems. The piece traces a narrative from the Founding Fathers' "hard money" tradition through the creation of the [[Federal Reserve]], the federal income tax, and the "Money Trust" documented by the [[Pujo Committee]], arguing that these developments deliberately transferred power from citizens to bankers.

## The hard-money tradition and its enemies

The article argues that prior to [[1913]], the United States operated under a "hard money" system of tradable coins established by the Founders, in which "the value of money was worth astronomically more than it is today, and few had to worry about loans or debt." It characterizes the 1800s economy as a battle between this hard-money tradition and the bankers' "paper money" system. Banks, the article explains, began by letting their own bank notes trade openly, creating a parallel economy to the official hard-money one and "paving the way for what we refer to today as fractional reserve lending." In the article's telling, the only way to stop this was for citizens to demand their gold back en masse, making the abolition of the [[gold standard]] a Bankers' priority.

## Andrew Jackson and the Second Bank

The article presents [[Andrew Jackson]] as the archetypal foe of central banking. Jackson, it says, recognized that the bankers of the [[Second Bank of the United States]] were acting "as a fourth branch of government run by the elite," and was "the only US President in history to completely pay off the US National Debt." He favored hard money over paper and, according to the source, the [[cabal]] later "added insult to injury by putting Andrew Jackson on their $20 bill." [[Donald Trump]] is described as Jackson's admirer and imitator: the article claims Trump nominated [[Judy Shelton]] — an advocate of returning to the [[gold standard]] that spanned 1873 to 1933 — to the Fed, and that Jackson was Trump's favorite president.

## The road to the Federal Reserve

Following the Panic of 1907, Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] established the National Monetary Commission, which concluded that the European model centered on the Central Bank of England was superior; its reports became a principal basis for the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve Act]]. In 1910, the article says, Aldrich and a group of bankers secretly drafted the plan at the [[Jekyll Island meeting]], traveling by train from Hoboken, New Jersey and dropping their surnames for first names or code names. The attendees and their affiliations, as listed by the source, were:

- [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] — his daughter married a Rockefeller
- A.P. Andrews — Assistant Secretary of the U.S. Treasury
- Paul Warburg — employee of Jacob H. Schiff, with family ties to the Rothschilds
- Frank A. Vanderlip — President of the National City Bank of New York
- Henry P. Davison — Senior Partner of J. P. Morgan & Co.
- Charles D. Norton — President of the Morgan-dominated First National Bank of New York
- Benjamin Strong Jr — representing J. P. Morgan

Together, the article states, these men "represented about one quarter of the world's wealth at the time."

## The 1913 package: the Fed, the IRS, and the income tax

The [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve Act]] was passed by the 63rd United States Congress and signed into law by President [[Woodrow Wilson]] on December 23, 1913, creating the [[Federal Reserve]] System. The article frames this as one part of a simultaneous [[1913]] "package":

- The [[Sixteenth Amendment]] was ratified in [[1913]].
- The [[Revenue Act of 1913]] dramatically lowered tariffs and implemented a federal income tax. Representative Oscar Underwood moved the bill through the House; it won Senate approval after extensive Wilson-administration lobbying. Wilson signed it into law on October 3, 1913, establishing the [[Internal Revenue Service]] (IRS).
- The article argues that together, the Fed removed citizens' ability to redeem currency for gold while the income tax gave the state a permanent claim on earnings — a combination it describes as having "enslaved every American to the [[fiat-currency|fiat monetary system]]."

## The Titanic connection (as framed by the source)

The article notes that several prominent men died aboard the *Titanic* in 1912, one year before the Federal Reserve Act, and suggests they opposed the Act to varying degrees. Those named are John Jacob Astor IV, industrialist Benjamin Guggenheim, Macy's owner Isidor Straus, and journalist William Thomas Stead. Isidor Straus — a Director of the Mutual Alliance Trust Company and one of thirteen founding directors alongside Emanuel Lehman, William Rockefeller, and Cornelius Vanderbilt — had published "The Question of Reserves" in the *New York Times* on October 25, 1911. The article presents the idea that their deaths removed opposition to the plan as something "long suggested" rather than established fact.

## The Pujo Committee and the "Money Trust"

Arsène Pujo formed a special committee in 1912–1913 to investigate the monopoly of J. P. Morgan and New York's other powerful bankers, contemporaneous with the enactment of the Fed and the IRS. The committee found, in language the article quotes, that "a small CABAL of financiers had gained consolidated control of numerous industries through the abuse of the public trust in the United States" (Bruner & Carr 2007, p. 148). The [[Pujo Committee]] Report further concluded that no fewer than eighteen major financial corporations were controlled by a cartel led by J. P. Morgan, George F. Baker, and James Stillman, and identified over **$22 billion** in resources and capitalization controlled through **341 directorships held in 112 corporations**. According to the article, the [[cabal]] "squashed" the findings and, controlling the media, kept them from the public.

## Interpretation and call to action

The article characterizes the [[Federal Reserve]] as "a privately owned bank" that became "the most powerful organization in the US," and calls for ending central banking and the [[fiat-currency|fiat system]] in favor of a return to hard money and a "new deflationary period" mirroring 1873. Badlands Media appends a disclaimer that the piece "represents the opinions of the contributing authors and does not necessarily represent the views of Badlands Media itself."
