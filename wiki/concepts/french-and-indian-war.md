# French and Indian War

The **French and Indian War** was the mid-eighteenth-century conflict in colonial North America during which the [[battle-of-the-monongahela|Battle of the Monongahela]] was fought on July 9, 1755, in the area of present-day Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.[1] It is referenced in Badlands Media commentary principally as the setting for the early military career of [[george-washington|George Washington]], who served in the war as a colonel at the age of 23 and carried the general's orders across the Monongahela battlefield while Native American combatants allied against the British specifically targeted officers.[1]

The war supplies the context for the account of Washington's survival that later commentators treat as miraculous: of 86 British and American officers engaged at the Monongahela, 63 became casualties, and Washington alone among mounted officers was not shot down, afterwards finding four bullet holes through his coat but no wounds.[1] [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used this wartime episode as the historical precedent for his reading of the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] as divine protection.[1]
