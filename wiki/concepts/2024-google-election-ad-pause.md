# 2024 Google Election Ad Pause

The 2024 Google election ad pause was a policy under which [[google|Google]] blocked election advertising across all of its platforms after the last polls closed on November 5, 2024, disclosed in a memo sent to advertising partners and obtained by [[axios|Axios]].[1]

## Policy terms

First introduced during the 2020 election, the policy was described as a measure to prevent misinformation about voting, including candidates prematurely claiming victory before a race is called.[1] A Google spokesperson said the company was acting "out of an abundance of caution and to limit the potential for confusion, given the likelihood that votes will continue to be counted after Election Day."[1] The pause applied to any U.S. election ads, or ads referring to U.S. elections, their processes or outcomes, served to people in the United States, across Google Ads, DV360, YouTube, and Google's Ad Exchange and Shopping ads.[1] Axios framed the move within a broader pattern of tech companies operating on high alert after the contested 2020 cycle.[1]

## Badlands critique

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the policy as a restriction on how candidates and activists amplify their messages precisely when the outcome is being determined, noting that the regime anticipated needing several days to call races.[1] She highlighted the carve-out exempting "public information campaign ads run by state or federal governments responsible for administering election processes, such as a State Secretaries' office," arguing that this exposed the arrangement as public-private collusion: government could not impose such a restriction openly, but private partners provide plausible deniability, leaving the official narrative as the only narrative.[1] The critique invoked the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] and the absence of any accountability for the previous cycle's censorship arrangements.[1]
