# Babylon (Book of Revelation)

**Babylon** is the corrupt world city whose fall is announced in the later chapters of the [[book-of-revelation|Book of Revelation]]. The Badlands Media essay "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?" reads the passage as a more detailed look into [[the-beast|the Beast]] system operating during [[the-tribulation|the Tribulation]].[1]

## The announcement of the fall

The angel who announces Babylon's fall attributes it to Babylon rejecting God and becoming a dwelling place for demons, a prison for foul spirits, and a cage for birds.[1] The author reads this as Babylon falling from within due to unchecked, festering evil, with the angel clarifying that Babylon is the vehicle through which the entire world is corrupted.[1]

## Resemblance to the fiat system

The subsequent description is said to bear an uncanny resemblance to the modern [[fiat-currency|fiat]] system, which the author calls the fiat slave system.[1] The text highlights the kings of the earth who sleep with the Beast, furthering Babylon's enslavement of humanity while enriching themselves; when Babylon falls these kings weep and lament, and as God's economy is established their earthly treasures are rapidly rendered worthless.[1] The author connects this to the observation that everything has so far gone to zero against [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] over time, and asks whether readers understand the rationale for expecting the trend to continue.[1]

He also notes the explicit mention of Babylon's destruction occurring in a single hour, asking whether this is where the concept of a "god-candle" — a sudden vertical price move — comes into play.[1]

## Marketplace of bodies and souls

The last feature the author highlights is Revelation's description of Babylon as a global marketplace for every kind of food, drink, trinket, commodity, and service — even bodies and souls — which he leaves to the reader as a rhetorical question.[1]

## Treasure in heaven

Adjacent to the Babylon discussion, the author argues that Bitcoin is the first tool that allows treasure to be stored "in heaven where moths and vermin do not destroy, and thieves do not break in and steal" (Matthew 6:19–21).[1] He clarifies that he does not believe cyberspace is heaven and recognises the verse concerns aligning hearts and priorities rather than materialism, but maintains the metaphorical connection holds, since nothing physical can qualify.[1]
