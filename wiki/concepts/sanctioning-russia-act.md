# Sanctioning Russia Act

The Sanctioning Russia Act is legislation introduced by Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] of South Carolina, co-sponsored by Senator Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut, that would impose 500 percent tariffs on any country that buys Russian energy.[1] Graham has pledged that the bill would be "bone crushing" for the Russian economy, and the stated goal is to starve Russia's war economy, which continues to earn hundreds of billions of dollars from energy exports.[1] As of June 2025, 82 senators had signed on to the bill, with growing support for a companion measure in the House.[1]

## Criticism and feasibility

Analysts warned that, if enacted, the proposal would effectively cut the United States off from some of the world's largest economies, including European allies.[1] "A 500 percent tariff is essentially a hard decoupling," said Kevin Book, managing director of the energy research firm Clear View Energy Partners.[1] Graham appeared to acknowledge the problem when he proposed a broad carve-out for countries that provide aid to Ukraine, an exemption that would spare the European Union, which still imports almost 20 percent of its gas from Russia.[1]

India and China buy roughly 70 percent of Russian energy exports, but other countries purchasing any oil, gas or uranium from Moscow and not covered by the carve-out would also be exposed.[1] The United States itself, still reliant on imports of enriched uranium from Russia to fuel its nuclear reactors, could run afoul of the bill.[1] Edward Fishman, a senior researcher with the Center on Global Energy Policy at Columbia University, said countries in the bill's crosshairs would struggle to halt Russian energy imports overnight, and that tariffs of 500 percent on Chinese goods would send prices soaring, disrupt supply chains, potentially drive U.S. unemployment to recessionary levels and most likely bring U.S. trade with China to a screeching halt.[1] "It would hurt Americans quite a bit," Fishman said.[1]
