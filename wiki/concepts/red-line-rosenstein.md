# Red Line (Rosenstein)

The "RED LINE" is a term used in Q drops and in Badlands Media commentary for the political boundary that Democrats and the press were said to have drawn around Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]] during the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller special counsel investigation]]: the proposition that firing him would constitute obstruction of justice and trigger impeachment proceedings against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Origin in Q drop 643

Q drop 643, posted January 29, 2018, sets out the formulation directly: "Firing Rod Rosenstein = block Mueller. Firing Rod Rosenstein = set up to firing Mueller. Firing Rod Rosenstein = RED LINE."[1] The same drop asks what Rosenstein's Senate confirmation vote had been and why he had "begged" Speaker [[paul-ryan|Paul Ryan]] to block the FISA memo from congressional review, and complains of outside contractors without clearance being admitted to a [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|SCIF]] in Washington.[1] The drop is headed "Narrative shift."[1]

## The February 2018 memo

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] identifies the trigger as the declassified FISA memo, which disclosed that Rosenstein had personally signed a renewal of the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA warrant]] predicated on the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]].[1] Many called for Trump to fire him. Democrats feared that his removal would lead either to the firing of [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] — whose inquiry Trump was publicly calling a witch hunt — or to his replacement by a loyalist who would sabotage the investigation, and so declared in advance that such a firing would cross a red line.[1]

## Lange's inversion

The article's argument is that the red line was not a constraint on Trump but a product of his own action.[1] Trump had declassified the memo himself; he therefore had no motive to fire Rosenstein, because the disclosure had forced Democrats into the position of publicly defending a man they had installed, and had produced a narrative shift on Trump's terms.[1] Roughly seven months later, further declassified memos — those written by [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] and [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] describing Rosenstein's alleged proposal to wear a wire and to invoke the [[twenty-fifth-amendment|25th Amendment]] — put Rosenstein back in the spotlight and, in Lange's account, forced the same defence a second time.[1]

Lange concludes that Rosenstein was retained rather than removed because he could be leveraged: he had been placed where the enemy wanted him, but the accumulated evidence gave Trump effective authority over both Rosenstein and the special counsel.[1] The claim that Trump orchestrated these events, as opposed to reacting to them, is an interpretive assertion of the Badlands account rather than a documented finding.[1]
