# 2023 Israel–Hamas Hostage Exchange

The 2023 Israel–Hamas hostage exchange was a series of prisoner-for-hostage swaps conducted during a temporary truce in the Gaza war in late November 2023, under which [[hamas|Hamas]] released captives seized during the October 7 attacks in return for the release of Palestinian prisoners held by Israel.[1]

## Second round of releases

After a delay that briefly threatened to collapse the arrangement, the second round of the swap was reported to be back on, with Hamas expected to release 13 Israelis and seven foreign nationals in a single night, while 39 Palestinians were expected to be released in return.[1] Israel had earlier warned that it might resume bombing the Gaza Strip if Hamas failed to fulfil its side of the deal, while Hamas in turn claimed that Israel had failed to meet certain agreed-upon details of the swap.[1]

## IDF statements

[[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] spokesman Rear Admiral [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] told an evening press conference that there was "significant progress" in efforts to release the hostages from Gaza following the Hamas delays, describing the return of the hostages as "our moral and ethical duty" and stating that Israel was "determined to fulfil this in any way."[1] Hagari cautioned that "nothing is final until it actually happens," noting that Israel was negotiating only indirectly and with what he called an "evil terror group," and asked the public for patience while families were informed.[1]

## American captives

Coverage of the exchange noted that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and his White House team had, at that point, failed to secure the release of American citizens among the captives, leaving open the question of whether US nationals would be among the foreign hostages freed.[1]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the deeper significance of the exchanges was that a bipartisan audience had been shown that peace "totally seems to be an option as long as both sides just stop killing each other for a predetermined amount of time."[1] He contended that "Crisis Fatigue" was setting in within the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]], to the displeasure of what he termed the Media Industrial Complex and its [[deep-state|Deep State]] controllers, and framed the dynamic through the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]]: that the managers of conflict require not merely war and strife but public reactions to that strife, and that after the mass-psychological exhaustion of the Ukraine crisis the "Fear Engine" was "running on fumes."[1]
