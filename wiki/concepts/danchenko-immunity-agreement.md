# Danchenko Immunity Agreement

The Danchenko immunity agreement was a letter agreement extended to [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] by the Justice Department during the January 2017 [[fbi|FBI]] interviews at which he was questioned about his role as the primary sub-source of the [[steele-dossier|Steele reporting]].[1] It became a central instrument of the prosecution's case at Danchenko's 2022 trial.[1]

## Terms and signatories

The letter, entered at trial as Government Exhibit 118 and dated 24 January 2017, was signed by [[david-laufman|David Laufman]] of the National Security Division, by Danchenko, and by Danchenko's counsel Mark Schamel.[1] Assistant Special Counsel [[michael-keilty|Michael Keilty]] told the jury that the agreement was presented to Danchenko at the January 2017 meeting and that all he had to do was be truthful and forthcoming about the information he had provided for the reports and about where he had obtained it.[1] Following the three days of interviews and the offer of the agreement, Danchenko was made a Confidential Human Source.[1]

## Use by the prosecution

When the defence argued that admitting Danchenko's emails with [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] and with [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] would let the government "backdoor a concealment theory" in the absence of any duty to disclose, Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] rebutted the point by invoking the agreement: Danchenko had bound himself to make "full and complete disclosure of information" and, the government contended, violated that obligation by withholding what the agents were asking for.[1]

Durham had FBI Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] read the letter aloud after it was displayed for the jury and questioned him at length about its requirements so that the jury would understand what it demanded.[1]
