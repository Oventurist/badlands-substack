# IHRA Definition of Antisemitism

The working definition of antisemitism promulgated by the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]] (IHRA) reads: "Antisemitism is a certain perception of Jews, which may be expressed as hatred toward Jews. Rhetorical and physical manifestations of antisemitism are directed toward Jewish or non-Jewish individuals and/or their property, toward Jewish community institutions and religious facilities."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Role in U.S. policy

The [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]], passed by the U.S. House in May 2024 by 320–91, would codify the IHRA definition into federal civil rights law for use by the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] in campus discrimination investigations.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) The definition also underpins [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]], signed January 29, 2025.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Criticism

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that what does and does not constitute "hatred" is a gray area typically determined by whichever political party is in power, citing the years-long effort to have varied political speech reclassified as [[hate-speech|hate speech]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He describes the definition's accompanying examples as overly broad or vaguely worded, predicting inconsistent enforcement driven by political bias and warning that the definition sweeps in many legitimate criticisms of Israel.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) In his view this could ultimately stoke rather than reduce [[antisemitism]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Adoption by U.S. states and the First Amendment fight

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] has argued since 2023 that state-level adoption of the IHRA definition constitutes a beachhead for a broader assault on the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]], carried out through legislation and executive orders in 38 states by what he calls the [[uniparty|Uniparty]]-Right.[2] Because the IHRA definition equates antisemitism with anti-Zionism, he contends, its codification permits the criminalization of criticism of the Israeli government.[2] His leading example is [[florida-hb-269|Florida HB 269]], sponsored by then-state senator [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] and signed by Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] during a trip to Jerusalem.[2]

He describes the broader dynamic as a Hegelian dialectic designed to discourage and criminalize dissent toward the government of [[israel|Israel]] by equating it with hatred of Jews, and warns of a slippery slope toward criminalizing criticism of the U.S. government on the grounds that it incites violence against politicians.[2]

## Revocation in New York City

Upon taking office on January 1, 2026, New York Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] revoked prior executive orders including [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]]'s endorsement of the IHRA definition, which encompasses some forms of criticism of [[israel|Israel]]; while Mamdani said he would retain the Office to Combat Antisemitism, commentators expected its function to shift.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the move a lesson for Republican governors, noting that state legislatures across the country have officially endorsed the IHRA definition even though the definition has been changed and updated since those laws and executive orders were signed, and asking why governments outsource their guidance to NGOs when that guidance is fluid and subject to change.[3]

## Adoption in U.S. states and Badlands criticism

By October 2024, according to Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], roughly 40 states — mostly Republican-controlled — had installed measures classifying criticism of the Israeli government as hate speech, each citing the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|IHRA]] as the defining authority.[4] Virginia Governor [[glenn-youngkin|Glenn Youngkin]] was described as the first to act, in 2022, and South Dakota Governor [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] titled her edict "Ensuring the Security of God's Chosen People."[4]

The contributor emphasized that the definition equates anti-Zionism with antisemitism and is explicitly described as fluid and subject to future revision, which he treated as the core danger: a foreign-linked body would hold an open-ended power to define what American citizens may say about a foreign government.[4] He argued the [[republican-party|Republican Party]] had thereby become the most dangerous current threat to the Bill of Rights, and that [[project-esther|Project Esther]] proposed making the state-level template federal policy.[4]
