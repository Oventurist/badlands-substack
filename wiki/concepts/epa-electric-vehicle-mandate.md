# EPA Electric Vehicle Mandate

The EPA electric vehicle mandate refers to the tailpipe-emissions standards adopted by the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]] under the Obama and Biden administrations that, in practice, would require automakers to shift at least 54 percent of production to electric vehicles and 16 percent to hybrids in order to meet 2032 requirements — a target critics called unrealistic.[1]

## Criticism

A December 2024 Wall Street Journal opinion piece argued that the incoming [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] could roll back these regulations, urging [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to withdraw from the Obama- and Biden-era emissions standards on day one and to end California's domination of US climate policy.[1] The piece contended that overly stringent emissions targets ignore American consumers' preferences, block economic growth, and are restrictive, costly and unachievable for US manufacturers based on current market trends.[1] It further argued that the United States lacks access to the volume of critical minerals needed to produce the required EV batteries, as well as the electricity generation capacity and charging infrastructure.[1]

## The California waiver

Shortly before the end of the Biden administration, the EPA granted California a waiver permitting the state to set environmental regulations stricter than the federal government's, effectively greenlighting California's aggressive zero-emission vehicle mandate.[1] Critics urged the incoming administration to move immediately to revoke the waiver, an action expected to be challenged in court.[1] The waiver has national consequences because other states are permitted to adopt California's climate policies and impose them on their residents; New York, Maryland and Virginia are among the states that have adopted California's zero-emission mandate.[1] The Journal argued that such policies helped drive voters to re-elect Trump.[1]
