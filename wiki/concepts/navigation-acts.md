# Navigation Acts

The Navigation Acts were a series of trade laws imposed by Great Britain on its American colonies before the US Revolution which, in the account given by the Badlands Media essay "Cracks in the Curtain," "all but crippled the colonies."[1]

The essay presents the Acts as the provocation that led the colonists to create their own form of money, free from the controls of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] — the paper currency known as [[colonial-scrip|Colonial Scrip]].[1] That decision is said to have produced one of the most economically successful periods in American history, arguably better than what the country enjoyed after 1776.[1] Asked how the colonies had achieved such prosperity, [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] is quoted as replying that the colonies issued their own money in proper proportion to the demands of trade and industry, controlling its purchasing power and paying no interest.[1]

The episode is used to argue that the American founders understood deeply what money was and how thoroughly it had been used to control the colonies, and that modern citizens should take the time to understand what they did.[1]
