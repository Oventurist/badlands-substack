# Freedom Convoy

The Freedom Convoy was the 2022 Canadian trucker-led protest against COVID-19 vaccine mandates and related restrictions, which gridlocked the capital of Ottawa for weeks and produced blockades at border crossings before being broken up under the [[emergencies-act|Emergencies Act]].[1]

## Court victory and the turn to lawfare

On January 23, 2024, Federal Court Justice Richard Mosley ruled against the federal government's invocation of the Emergencies Act in response to the protests and blockades.[1] Mosley wrote that the government's use of the act did "not bear the hallmarks of reasonableness—justification, transparency and intelligibility—and was not justified in relation to the relevant factual and legal constraints that were required to be taken into consideration."[1]

Buoyed by the ruling, several convoy participants announced preparations to sue the federal government, the banks that froze accounts, and the police forces that ended the protest.[1] Military veteran and plaintiff Eddie Cornell said, "I think it's the second phase to what took place with the federal court case… We've got a big hill to climb, but it's something that's necessary. It has to be done."[1]

Police veteran and plaintiff Vincent Gircys, whose bank account was frozen for more than a week under the act, said he had been "very disappointed with our justice system—having worked in there for 32 years"—but was pleased that "some level of justice is being restored."[1] Alberta contractor Jeremiah Jost, who drove to Ottawa with his wife as part of the convoy, said he was "incredibly encouraged by Justice Mosley's ruling and his courage to put his neck out," adding that the ruling gave hope to Canadians frustrated with the justice system.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] described the Mosley ruling as the most precedent-setting development for the Canadian public, holding that it was illegal for [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] to invoke the Emergencies Act to "physically, psychologically and financially brutalize citizens for exercising their rights."[1] Esler argued the decision opened pathways for citizen [[lawfare|lawfare]] that many had believed foreclosed after Liberal Justice Paul Rouleau's commission declared Trudeau's invocation entirely justified.[1]

Esler placed the ruling within a broader wave of Canadian legal battles, including the [[coutts-four|Coutts 4]] prosecutions, the ruling that Alberta Health Minister Deena Hinshaw's COVID measures were illegal, the firing of podcaster BertaProudDad, and the fines levied against nurse Kristen Nagle.[1]

## Federal Court ruling of January 2024

On January 23, 2024 the Federal Court ruled that the Liberal government's decision to invoke the [[emergencies-act|Emergencies Act]] in response to the 2022 convoy protests was unreasonable, unjustified and in violation of the Charter.[2] Justice [[richard-mosley|Richard Mosley]] found that although the protests caused harm to Canada's economy, trade and commerce, they did not amount to a threat to national security as defined by the statute, and he sided with the civil liberties groups that argued the government had exceeded its powers.[2] Deputy Prime Minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] said the same day that the government disagreed with the ruling and would appeal.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the convoy's organizers had been "quickly declared enemies of the state," with assets frozen, characters assassinated and livelihoods destroyed for peacefully protesting government policy, and that the ruling confirmed that treatment was unjust — a victory for liberty worth celebrating "even if it's Canadian."[2]
