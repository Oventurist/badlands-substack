# 2023 Trump Classified Documents Indictment

The **2023 Trump classified documents indictment** refers to the federal prosecution of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arising from the investigation into his handling of classified material after leaving office, which reached a decisive stage in early June 2023.[1]

## Target notification

On Wednesday, June 7, 2023, multiple people familiar with the case reported that federal prosecutors had notified Trump that he was a criminal target and was likely to be indicted imminently in the classified-documents probe.[1] Sources told [[just-the-news|Just the News]] that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] declined to delay the planned indictment in order to investigate allegations of witness tampering submitted by the former president's legal team.[1]

## Prosecutorial misconduct allegation

The defense allegation held that a senior prosecutor working on the case had attempted to influence a key witness by discussing a federal judgeship with the witness's lawyer.[1] That allegation remained pending in a secret case before Chief U.S. District Judge [[james-boasberg|James E. Boasberg]], who oversees the federal court in Washington, D.C. and the grand juries convened in that courthouse.[1] Reporting indicated that an historic federal indictment crafted by Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] could be handed up by a federal grand jury against the 45th president as early as that week.[1]

## Declassification letter

The prosecution's premise was contested the same week by the publication of a letter signed by Trump and dated January 19, 2021 — the day before he left office — which was released by [[dc-draino|DC Draino]] and reported by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] as declassifying the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] documents.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the "Trump hoarded classified documents" narrative took a hit as a result, noting that although a signed letter is not legally required for a president to declassify, the supposed absence of such documentation had been central to the claim that Trump acted improperly.[1] The commentary also observed that [[joe-biden|Biden]], [[mike-pence|Pence]], and [[barack-obama|Obama]] were each found to have retained documents while the narrative continued to treat Trump as uniquely unlawful, and framed the letter as new insight into the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]] of August 2022.[1]

## Commentary on political significance

Writing for Badlands Media, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that another Trump indictment would produce continued exposure of establishment conservatism, would codify "Trump v. Establishment" as the dominant narrative heading into 2024, and would provide narrative shielding for future Justice Department indictments of former heads of state while setting legal and psychological templates — an argument credited in part to [[just-human|Just Human]].[1]
