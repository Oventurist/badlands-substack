# Quarterly Earnings Reporting

Quarterly earnings reporting is the requirement that publicly traded U.S. companies disclose financial results every three months, a rule first imposed by the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] in 1970.[1]

## Trump's 2025 proposal

On Monday, 15 September 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said that U.S.-based companies should report every six months instead of every three, arguing the shift would save cash and keep executives focused on their businesses.[1] Writing on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], he said, "This will save money, and allow managers to focus on properly running their companies," and added: "Did you ever hear the statement that, 'China has a 50 to 100 year view on management of a company, whereas we run our companies on a quarterly basis???' Not good!!!"[1]

Despite Trump's invocation of [[china|China]], Chinese companies do file quarterly reports as well as semiannual and annual reports; only companies listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange file solely on a semiannual basis.[1] Any change would require approval from the Securities and Exchange Commission.[1]

## International comparison

Ending mandatory quarterly earnings reports would bring the United States more in line with the United Kingdom and the [[european-union|European Union]], where companies are required to report twice a year but are permitted to submit quarterly reports.[1]
