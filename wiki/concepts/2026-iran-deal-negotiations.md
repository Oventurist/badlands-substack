# 2026 Iran Deal Negotiations

The 2026 Iran deal negotiations refer to high-stakes talks between the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and [[iran|Iran]] that Badlands covered in May 2026 alongside Israeli objections to their likely terms.[1]

## Israeli posture

Israel's national security minister stated that Israel would not let Trump sign a "bad deal" with Iran.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read this as evidence that the Israelis "clearly believe that they are in control and in the driver's seat as far as 'Peace in the Middle East' is concerned," and suggested it explained why [[israel|Israel]] launched yet another major offensive in Lebanon while Trump was ostensibly mid-negotiation.[1]

Unsubstantiated rumors circulated that the deal had already been finalized and merely required signatures.[1]

## Domestic fallout

The commentary predicted that when the deal is formally announced, most of the GOP will "turn on President Trump like a pack of wild rabid dogs," because those congressmen are held to be compromised and to work for the State of Israel rather than the American people.[1] Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] was named as one figure heavily invested in the outcome, citing his attack on Pakistani Defence Minister [[khawaja-asif|Khawaja Asif]].[1]

As a counter-move, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry urged declassifying both the COVID and 2020 election investigations, particularly material implicating the GOP in the coup against Trump, and expressed a wish to see [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] "break all of their toys on her way out" as the best way to hurt the Uniparty by exposing traitors in the GOP.[1]

## June 2026: "Nuclear Honesty" and the Switzerland track

On June 23, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on Truth Social that [[iran|Iran]] had "fully and completely agreed to highest level Nuclear inspections long into the future (Infinity!!!)" to ensure "Nuclear Honesty," calling this a major concession that enabled further negotiations.[2] Iranian statements stressed that missiles and defensive capabilities were not subject to discussion, that there were no plans to permit [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspectors at nuclear sites targeted during the conflict, and that the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] would be administered by Iran under international law, with talks involving [[oman|Oman]].[2] Officials conditioned further progress on full implementation of commitments by all parties and noted that the Strait remained open to shipping.[2]

Kremlin foreign policy aide [[yury-ushakov|Yury Ushakov]] said the developments were positive but that lasting peace would take time, and indicated that [[russia|Russia]] was open to hosting Iran's enriched uranium as a concession to all powers.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that a deal had in fact been struck some time earlier and that the visible negotiations were staging for its eventual public announcement, describing the contradictory messaging from the Switzerland talks as evidence that not every delegate had been read in on the true contours of the arrangement.[2] In this account, "Nuclear Honesty" functions as a translation layer for a private understanding that the old nuclear narrative — long weaponized by the [[globalist-hegemon|Globalist Hegemon]] — is no longer operative, resting on the demonstrated resolve of [[operation-epic-fury|Epic Fury]] and, before it, [[operation-midnight-hammer|Operation Midnight Hammer]].[2] Moscow's uranium-hosting offer is framed as preserving an on-ramp to a civil nuclear energy program consistent with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and multipolar template.[2]

The same negotiating track produced the [[us-iran-lebanon-oversight-mechanism|US-Iran oversight mechanism for Lebanon]], which excluded [[israel|Israel]] and tied Lebanese deconfliction to the broader ceasefire framework.[2]

## "New, more reasonable regime" (March 2026)

In late March 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States was engaged in negotiations with what he described as a "new, more reasonable regime" in [[iran|Iran]], indicating progress toward a potential agreement to end ongoing hostilities.[3] In a public statement he wrote that the United States was "in serious discussions with A NEW, AND MORE REASONABLE, REGIME to end our Military Operations in Iran," warning that if a deal were not shortly reached and if the Hormuz Strait were not immediately "Open for Business," the United States would conclude its "stay" in Iran by "blowing up and completely obliterating" Iran's electric generating plants, oil wells, and Kharg Island, and possibly all desalinization plants — targets he said had purposefully not yet been touched.[3] He framed such action as retribution for American soldiers and others killed by Iran over the old regime's 47-year "Reign of Terror."[3]

The remarks were tied to reopening the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and to broader efforts to end the conflict, with US officials indicating both direct and indirect communication channels with Iranian representatives.[3] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] simultaneously described an "escalate to de-escalate" strategy and characterized US actions as weakening the Iranian regime through sustained economic and military pressure intended to alter Tehran's negotiating position.[3] The statements came amid continued regional military tension, with the United States increasing its presence and retaining the option of further strikes while talks continued.[3] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the sequence as the codification of [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] in the Iranian theater of [[operation-epic-fury|Operation Epic Fury]], predicting "functional continuity under reformed stewardship" rather than genuine regime replacement.[3]

## Geneva round (February 2026)

The second round of high-stakes indirect negotiations between the United States and Iran concluded in Geneva on Tuesday, February 17, 2026, with Iranian officials reporting that the two sides had reached an understanding on core principles aimed at resolving the long-running nuclear dispute.[4] Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said progress had been made and that Tehran would submit detailed proposals within the next two weeks.[4] The Oman-mediated talks featured a U.S. delegation including special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], while Iran made clear it would accept nuclear concessions in exchange for sanctions relief but would not abandon uranium enrichment or negotiate over its ballistic missile program.[4]

Tensions remained high despite the diplomatic movement. Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] warned President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that Iran possesses weapons capable of sending American warships to the bottom of the sea, and as the talks wrapped up Iran, Russia and China launched the [[maritime-security-belt|Maritime Security Belt 2026]] joint naval exercises in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], shortly after separate Iranian Revolutionary Guard drills involving anti-ship missile tests and temporary closures of sections of the chokepoint.[4]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicted that a deal would be deliberately subverted and ultimately rejected, arguing that hardliners in Israel and their American allies — he singled out Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], speaking from Israel about the acceptability of risk to U.S. troops — preferred confrontation to peace, and that Trump was keeping such figures in place long enough to put their positions on the record.[4]

## May 28, 2026 memorandum of understanding

U.S. and Iranian negotiators reached a tentative memorandum of understanding on May 28, 2026 that would extend the existing cease-fire by 60 days and launch a new round of negotiations over [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program, according to U.S. officials, with the proposal subject to final approval by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5] [[reuters|Reuters]] reported that the agreement would begin a formal negotiation period focused on nuclear issues.[5] Reporting by the Associated Press said the draft framework would require Iran to cease collecting tolls in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and to remove mines from the waterway within 30 days, in exchange for the United States beginning to ease its naval blockade of Iranian ports and discussing sanctions relief during the negotiation period.[5]

The future of Iran's highly enriched uranium stockpile remained one of the principal unresolved issues.[5] Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] said negotiators had reached a tentative agreement but were still working through language and nuclear-related provisions, and that it was unclear whether or when Trump would approve the proposal.[5]

## Proposed reconstruction and investment fund

Separate reporting citing the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] said the draft memorandum also discussed a possible international investment or reconstruction fund for Iran in the event a broader final agreement is reached, with details and terms unfinalized.[5] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted the Times had described plans for a $300 billion U.S. investment fund into Iranian reconstruction, and argued that the prospect only appears paradoxical if one accepts the official framing of Iran as America's foremost adversary.[5] In his reading, Trump and the Iranian leadership are jointly squeezing the [[globalist-hegemon|Globalist Hegemon]], with Iran's closest partners — [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]] — forming two of the three pillars of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] alongside the United States, making reconstruction efforts predictable in advance.[5]

Burning Bright tied the episode to his [[iran-war|Iran War]] series and the broader [[multipolar-war|Multipolar War]] thesis, quoting his April analysis that energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz were resuming under new terms with Iran asserting tiered control, that currency rails were decoupling from petrodollar chokeholds, that markets were pricing in reconstruction booms, and that the [[collectorate|European Collectorate]] was left exposed on both power projection and resource security.[5] He described the disentangling of the "Iranian Knot" as a microcosm of a global dialectic between sovereign disentanglement and multipolar reconstruction, arguing Trump strengthens the American people precisely by weakening the U.S. position in the System of Systems.[5]

## Switzerland talks and 60-day roadmap (June 2026)

High-level talks between U.S. and Iranian officials concluded on a Monday in Switzerland after negotiations that began Sunday and continued into the early hours of the next day.[6] Mediators [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[pakistan|Pakistan]] said the sides had agreed to a roadmap toward a final agreement within 60 days and would continue technical discussions during the week.[6] A joint statement released through Qatar's foreign ministry said the parties also agreed to a mechanism intended to end fighting in Lebanon and established a communications channel to help ensure safe passage for commercial vessels through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[6] Reuters reported that the discussions took place under a memorandum of understanding reached the previous week that extended a ceasefire for at least 60 days.[6]

Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araqchi]] said on social media that Iran had obtained waivers for oil and petrochemical exports, the release of some frozen assets and the start of a reconstruction and development plan.[6] The [[white-house|White House]] did not immediately comment on whether the high-level talks had formally wrapped up.[6] Iranian and U.S. sources gave differing accounts of the proceedings: Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency, citing an informed source, said Iranian negotiators stopped returning to the main negotiating room after comments attributed to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], while a U.S. diplomat involved in the talks told Reuters that negotiations continued through the night and covered the Strait of Hormuz, Lebanon and nuclear matters.[6] Badlands Media reported that Trump's posted comments about Iran's proxies during the meeting prompted Araghchi and the Iranian delegation to walk out while Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] was in Switzerland, and speculated that the episode was deliberate rather than an unforced error.[6]

## Unresolved nuclear questions

[[fox-news|Fox News]] reported on June 20, 2026 that the newly signed memorandum of understanding opened a 60-day negotiating period for a potential nuclear agreement while leaving key questions unresolved regarding the disposition of Iran's enriched uranium stockpile, with negotiators expected to address those issues in follow-up talks; planned technical discussions in Switzerland were reportedly postponed.[6] The memorandum reportedly identified [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]]-supervised on-site downblending of enriched uranium as the minimum acceptable method for handling the stockpile, while stating that the process for resolving the issue remained subject to further negotiation, and called for discussions concerning Iran's future nuclear needs and related enrichment issues.[6]

Fox News cited a recent IAEA report stating that the agency has had limited visibility into Iran's declared nuclear program, having not received information from Iran regarding most declared nuclear facilities and not been granted access for broader on-site verification.[6] A senior administration official said the memorandum requires Iran to reaffirm that it will not seek nuclear weapons and is intended to advance negotiations on uranium stockpiles, dismantlement of nuclear facilities, enrichment restrictions and inspection access.[6] Vance said Iran had pledged not to enrich uranium and to permit inspectors to destroy highly enriched stockpiles.[6] Experts interviewed by Fox News argued that effective verification would be essential and that inspectors would need sufficient access to locate, secure and verify nuclear material before any dilution process could be relied upon.[6]

## Draft memorandum and Muslim-leader call (May 2026)

In late May 2026, Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said the United States had "a pretty solid thing on the table" involving opening the straits, while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he had told negotiators not to rush and that the blockade would remain in place until an agreement was reached, certified and signed.[7] Axios reported that a draft U.S.–Iran deal would extend a ceasefire for 60 days, reopen the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and allow [[iran|Iran]] to sell oil freely, with Iran clearing mines from the strait while the United States lifted its blockade and issued some sanctions waivers.[7] The draft memorandum was reported to include Iranian commitments not to pursue nuclear weapons and to negotiate over uranium enrichment and highly enriched uranium stockpiles; Iran's Fars news agency, cited by Arab News and AFP, said oil, gas and petrochemical sanctions would be temporarily lifted during the negotiation period.[7]

Trump posted on Truth Social that "an Agreement has been largely negotiated, subject to finalization between the United States of America, the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the various other Countries, as listed," a message reposted by the [[white-house|White House]] with a graphic listing Middle Eastern Muslim leaders, headed by Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[7] He subsequently posted a screencap quoting Iranian President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]], who said Iran was ready to assure the world that it did not seek nuclear weapons or regional instability.[7]

On the following Sunday, Trump said negotiations were continuing, that he had told U.S. representatives "not to rush into a deal," and that Iran must not develop or obtain a nuclear weapon; in a later post he defended the talks against critics, saying any deal he made would be "good and proper" and that the agreement was "not even fully negotiated yet."[7] Reuters relayed the Axios account of the 60-day ceasefire extension and strait reopening and said the White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment.[7] The Associated Press, citing regional officials and an unnamed U.S. official, reported that the emerging deal would include Iran giving up its stockpile of highly enriched uranium, with details and timelines unresolved, and quoted Rubio saying "significant progress, although not final progress, has been made" and describing the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz as an initial step before further nuclear talks.[7]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the drawn-out "performative dance" of the negotiations served a purpose: it induced subversive actors within the MAGA coalition to reveal themselves.[7] He cited sequential posts by [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] — first asserting that Saudi Arabia was responsible for the September 11 attacks, which the author called a lie told by the [[cia|CIA]], then quoting a Jerusalem Post article about the rumored deal and calling it a "big problem" — as well as Levin's amplification of a podcast clip questioning Gulf Arab "foreign influence" over Trump.[7] [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] and [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] also criticized the reported deal, with Graham focusing on a report that Trump had asked Arab states to join the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] if he succeeded in making peace with Iran — an overture the piece said was met with "crickets."[7]

The commentary invoked former Biden envoy [[robert-malley|Robert Malley]], who argued that even a successful Trump-brokered regional peace would likely not be honored by [[israel|Israel]], which would continue operations in Lebanon and elsewhere while citing newly discovered intelligence.[7] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry concluded that Israel had made itself an international pariah, that it was Israel rather than the Muslim states that needed to relent, and that peace in the Middle East was possible only if Israel were "reigned in and kept under control."[7]
