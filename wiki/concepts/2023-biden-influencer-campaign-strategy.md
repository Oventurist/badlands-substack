# 2023 Biden Influencer Campaign Strategy

The **2023 Biden influencer campaign strategy** was a reported plan by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s political operation to rely on an "army" of online influencers for his expected 2024 reelection bid, first reported by [[axios|Axios]] on April 9, 2023 and carried by [[breitbart|Breitbart]].[1] At the time of the reporting Biden had not yet officially announced that he was running for reelection.[1]

## Reported plan

According to the reporting, influencers recruited to the effort could be given their own "briefing room" at the [[white-house|White House]].[1] The campaign was said to be seeking to "boost Biden's standing among young voters who are crucial to Democrats' success in elections" and to counter former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "massive social media following" should he become the Republican nominee.[1]

The president's digital strategy team hoped to connect with influencers across the country to help Biden tout his record among people who did not follow the social media accounts of Biden, the White House, or the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]].[1] Four Biden digital staffers focused on influencers and independent content creators were reported to be on the White House payroll rather than the campaign's, run by [[rob-flaherty|Rob Flaherty]], who had been named assistant to the president with the same rank as press secretary.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that anyone who thought Biden's "expiration date was up" should guess again, since he would have "the best campaign dark money can buy."[1] He argued that Biden's public-relations strategists were assembling an influential posse of young stars to make voting for him appear a good decision, and dismissed the premise that voters should follow the endorsement of "your favorite Hollywood puppet."[1] He noted the perceived hypocrisy of recruiting young influencers who had criticized the Republican Party as composed of old white men while the Democratic front-runners were themselves two old white men, referencing [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]].[1]

DeLarme also addressed the theory that those "pulling Biden's strings" would eventually stop him and push [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] instead, asking why Biden was trying to stay in the fight if that were so.[1] He suggested it might matter little which "deep-state plant" was installed in the White House, but that with Biden people were at least reaching common ground over his unfitness for the job, and floated the possibility that patriots benefited from Biden and [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] showcasing their ineptitude rather than facing "a more polished actor like Newsom."[1]
