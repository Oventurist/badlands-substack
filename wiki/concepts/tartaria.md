# Tartaria

**Tartaria** is a term used in alternative-history circles for a supposed lost or suppressed civilization. In [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]]'s [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series it plays a double role.[1] The series notes that the [[koryos|Koryos]] cult appears to hail from the same area that many today call "Tartaria," a name the author observes rather resembles *Tartarus*, the region of hell located beneath Hades in Greek mythology.[1]

At the same time, the author argues that there is evidence for the worldwide existence of an advanced and potentially benevolent civilization alongside the Koryos, and suggests this benevolent civilization may be what people actually refer to when they use the word "Tartaria" — while cautioning that more research is required before he is willing to say anything more definitive.[1] In the second installment of the series, "Agents from Russia," he states outright that for purposes of that article the terms "lost, positive civilization" and "Tartaria" mean the same thing, and locates its heart in present-day Russia, Ukraine and Eastern Europe.[2]

## The inversion argument

Asked why a presumed benevolent civilization would be named after hell, the author wagers that this reflects the tendency of dark occultists to invert the meanings of words, summarizing the point with the internet idiom "simple as."[1]

## Popularization and suspected discrediting

"Agents from Russia" observes that Tartarian material "flooded the internet" in recent years, and treats the suddenness of that popularity as itself significant, arguing that an abrupt explosion of interest in a subject often indicates social engineering of some kind.[2] The author declines to endorse or debunk any particular version of Tartarian lore, but notes that channels discussing Tartaria and [[flat-earth-debate|flat Earth]] routinely draw six-figure view counts while other alternative researchers face suppression — remarking that one may "talk about flat Earth and Tartaria, but not Q."[2] He suggests the pairing of Tartaria with flat-Earth claims may be a deliberate discrediting tactic, describing guilt-by-association with "ridiculous" information as one of the oldest disinformation tricks in the book, and states for the record that he does not himself believe the Earth is flat.[2]

## Claims the author accepts

Two elements of Tartarian lore are described as plausible: that a global civilization was written out of the history books, and that the ornate architecture attributed to it genuinely existed and was destroyed in order to blind the public to the past.[2] The strongest evidence offered is the [[worlds-fairs|World's Fairs]] of the nineteenth century, whose buildings the author hypothesizes may have been pre-existing "Tartarian" cities rather than temporary constructions.[2] He notes that searching "Tartarian architecture" alongside the name of almost any major city reveals such buildings, most of which have been demolished, and lists Gilded Age mansions, Beaux-Arts and — to a lesser degree — Art Deco as related styles.[2]

## Etymology argument

The essay draws attention to the shared phoneme in *TartARIA*, *KhazARIA* and *BulgARIA*, asking whether an "Are-an" wolf cult imposed its language on neighboring peoples — a link used to tie Tartaria's territory to that of [[khazaria|Khazaria]] and the Koryos.[2]

## Place in the series' thesis

The series holds that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] likely has a connection to Tartaria, a claim developed in Part 2 and extended in Part 4 by associating him with Druidic royalty through the Kennedy connection.[1] Within the wider argument, Tartaria functions as the positive counterpart to the Koryos: the civilization whose royal heirs the Trump and Kennedy families are said to be, and which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] has allegedly battled for millennia.[1]

"Agents from Russia" develops the connection through [[nikola-tesla|Nikola Tesla]], whose maternal grandfather was an Eastern Orthodox priest in the region at the time it was called Tartaria, and whose father kept a large library the young Tesla read extensively; the author reasons that Tesla may thereby have acquired Tartarian technical knowledge, much of which probably passed to [[john-g-trump|John G. Trump]], Donald Trump's uncle.[2] He notes that [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]]'s family also comes from this general area, that the Trump family favors architecture resembling the Tartarian style, and that Trump owned an Atlantic City hotel and casino named "Trump World's Fair."[2] From this the author asks whether media claims that Trump is a "Russian agent" are in fact coded references to his being an agent of Tartaria, and whether the [[russia-ukraine-war|Russia–Ukraine war]] originates in an ancient conflict between the werewolf cult and Tartaria.[2] Credit for inspiring this section is given to the X account TartariaLives.[2]

## The Tartarus hypothesis

In the third installment, "Hell On," the author states that after publishing the Tartaria material he realised something that had previously escaped his notice: in Greek mythology *Tartarus* is the name of the region of hell lying below Hades, and Tartaria, according to many researchers, was located precisely where the [[koryos|Koryos]] first emerged — an area also once known as [[khazaria|Khazaria]].[3] He treats the closeness of the two names as one piece of evidence that a portal to hell, or something along those lines, may be located in or near the [[black-sea|Black Sea]] region, with the area's status as an origin point of many forms of dark occultism serving as a further piece.[3]

The author does not conclude from this that the hypothesis of a benevolent Tartaria is wrong, but wonders whether the names were altered, whether Tartaria was not the civilization's true name, or whether old-world maps could have been forged — noting how easy it would be to flood the internet with fabricated material in order to obscure the truth about any given matter.[3]
