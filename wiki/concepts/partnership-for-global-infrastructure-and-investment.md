# Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment

The Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment (PGII) is a G7-led development finance programme launched by the White House on 26 June 2022, described in Badlands Media commentary as the newest rebranding of "Build Back Better for the World" (B3W).[1]

## Terms

The programme promised $600 billion over five years to recipient nations in Africa, Southwest Asia, Latin America, East Asia and Eastern Europe, directed at digital infrastructure, telecommunications, green energy and "soft infrastructure" with a focus on gender equity.[1] Its stated purpose was to offer poor nations an alternative to what its sponsors characterized as China's predatory lending ambitions.[1] The source reports that few of the nations offered this "life raft" have shown much interest.[1]

## Lineage of failed counter-BRI programmes

PGII is presented as the last in a chain of western answers to the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], each of which the article says lacked concrete plans or financing mechanisms:[1]

- "[[build-back-better|Build Back Better]]," an ambiguously defined slogan embraced by Joe Biden, Justin Trudeau, Boris Johnson and [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]], later rebranded B3W.[1]
- The "Green Belt Initiative," unveiled by Biden and Johnson in March 2021, which offered no specifics on funding the $3 trillion required for a transition to solar and wind.[1]
- The "[[green-new-deal|Global Green New Deal]]," which former Bank of England Governor [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] heralded as a $130 trillion renaissance into a post-hydrocarbon age.[1]
- The "Global Green Gateway," announced by von der Leyen in September 2021, criticized for ignoring the hundreds of thousands of engineers China has trained in Africa and for projecting Europe's own historic predatory lending practices onto China; von der Leyen stated "We want to create links and not dependencies… It does not make sense for Europe to build roads between a Chinese-owned copper mine and a Chinese-owned harbor."[1] It too faded without a viable lending mechanism or staff.[1]

## Context

The launch came four months into Russia's military intervention in Ukraine, amid what the source calls the erection of a new Iron Curtain cutting Europe off from both Russia and China, and at a moment when global demand for affordable energy and food had risen higher than ever.[1]
