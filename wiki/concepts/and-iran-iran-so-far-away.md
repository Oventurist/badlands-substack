# And Iran, Iran So Far Away

"And Iran, Iran So Far Away," subtitled "The True Implications of the Deep State's Global Power Play," is an essay published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on July 24, 2025 and written by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]. Presented as a direct continuation of the earlier Badlands piece "The Deep Politics of the '12-Day War,'" the article argues that Western hostility toward [[iran]] has little to do with the Iranian nuclear program — dismissed in the text as "Schrödinger's nuke" — and instead reflects a factional struggle within the Western ruling elite over who will shape the future of the Middle East and profit from Iran's reopening.

## Two competing factions

The article describes the contest as one between an "old guard" and an emerging alternative. The old guard is characterised as "the interagency bureaucratic amalgam that has long controlled and spread Western hegemony," described as the Obama-era inheritors of the legacy of Henry Kissinger and Zbigniew Brzezinski. On the author's account this bloc encompasses the giant asset managers [[blackrock|BlackRock Inc.]] and the [[vanguard-group|Vanguard Group]], the think tanks orbiting the [[european-union|EU]], the [[united-nations|UN]] and [[nato]], and institutions such as the [[atlantic-council]], the [[council-on-foreign-relations]] and the [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institute]].

Opposed to it is described as a "loose-knit, big-tent coalition of reluctant forces" gathered behind [[donald-trump]] and comparable populist leaders, and supported "financially and clandestinely" by [[israel]], [[saudi-arabia]] and what the article calls rogue portions of the private sector. The author states that readers may "love or hate these factions," but presents them as the genuine competing powers in the contemporary West, with Iran as one of their principal flashpoints.

## The Iran nuclear deal as a "cookie jar"

At the centre of the analysis is the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Nuclear Deal (JCPOA)]], which the article calls one of the most consequential geopolitical maneuvers in recent history. Iran is likened to a forbidden "cookie jar": whoever opened it would be "dipping into a goldmine." For fifty years, the article notes, Iran was kept under heavy sanctions and remained economically suppressed despite holding the world's second-largest natural gas reserves and third-largest oil reserves.

The prevailing consensus, according to the source, was that a weakened Iran was preferable, because a thriving Iran on the level of Saudi Arabia would dramatically alter Middle Eastern dynamics and potentially precipitate a wider war in which Iranian proxies, suddenly enjoying vastly greater funding, might "wipe Israel off of the map." The article contends that the Saudi–Israeli coalition born in reaction to this Iranian opening — attributed to the Obama [[state-department]] and the NATO foreign policy establishment — was among the major catalysts that allowed Donald Trump to rise within the Republican Party and defeat "the Clinton machine."

## Clinton, Obama and Israeli regime change

The article emphasises the scale of establishment backing enjoyed by [[hillary-clinton]] in 2016: corporations, the billionaire donor class, the media, [[george-soros|Soros]], the [[pritzker-family|Pritzkers]] ("the money behind Obama world"), and figures of high society including Steven Spielberg, Laurene Powell Jobs and "pretty much all of Hollywood." She was, in the author's words, "a shoo-in, the vessel that would continue what Obama had started," yet she lost.

Following the 2015 signing of the JCPOA, tensions between [[barack-obama]] and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu]] were, the article says, well documented. It reports speculation that Obama sought to undermine or oust Netanyahu, effectively pursuing an unspoken policy of regime change in Israel in hopes of replacing his government with the Blue and White party. Simultaneously, Netanyahu is said to have been backing regime change in the United States by supporting Trump.

Cited as evidence of the campaign against Netanyahu are the ongoing corruption probe against him, [[usaid]]-funded protests over judicial reform in 2023, and calls by then Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer]] in March 2024 for new Israeli elections, which the article characterises as effectively declaring Netanyahu illegitimate. These efforts, the source states, "ended abruptly after October 7th."

## Russia, Ukraine and European gas

The article situates the deal in the confrontation with [[russia]]. Russia vocally opposed sanctions relief for Iran, and one motive for the agreement, the source argues, was to allow Western-partnered Iranian gas to supplant Russian gas in Europe at a time when the Obama administration was waging economic warfare by rallying European sanctions against Moscow. The 2014 coup in Ukraine and the contests over Crimea and the Donbass are described as the driving forces behind those sanctions, imposed in response to Russian military support, funding and arming of separatist groups.

The article asserts that "the wizards behind NATO" have been conducting an undeclared second cold war with Russia "all while being in bed with [[china|China]]." When Trump took office he reversed the working consensus from maximum pressure on Russia to maximum pressure on Iran and China — a maneuver the author believes, "perhaps more than anything else, put the crosshairs on Trump's back."

## The foreign policy establishment and the Atlantic Council

Seeking to define "the foreign policy establishment," the article points to the think tanks where the heads of industry, finance, military, intelligence and politics convene, and singles out the Atlantic Council as "the brains behind NATO." It quotes a description of the Council as "an influential extremely militaristic influence network and think-tank, staffed with military, [[cia|CIA]], and their attendant politicians; and CEOs from strategic industries, like oil and media," with strong connections to NATO and forming "a central part of the modern [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial congressional complex]]."

The Council is also described as a "civil society institution," a label the author challenges: civil society is often implied to be a third-party oppositional force to government and big business, but in most cases membership is drawn from precisely those quarters. The article states that the Atlantic Council is funded by the U.S. State Department, the [[pentagon]], USAID, the [[national-endowment-for-democracy]], numerous other U.S. government agencies and several UK government agencies, as well as by nearly every major Western energy firm — [[chevron]], [[exxonmobil|Exxon]], [[bp|British Petroleum]] and [[shell|Royal Dutch Shell]] — all of which are said to have a vested interest in the Iran deal. It further notes that the Council's board includes at least seven former CIA directors.

These energy interests are compared to earlier activity in Ukraine, where Chevron signed $10 billion deals with [[naftogaz]], Ukraine's state-owned oil and gas company. The article contends the firms wanted the same arrangement on a far larger scale in Iran, and as funders of the Atlantic Council could lobby the State Department, CIA and Pentagon for policies that would open the country.

Israel's dissent from the NATO line — and its support for a change of leadership in Washington — is presented as the trigger for the Atlanticists' reciprocal pursuit of regime change in Israel. The author concedes bluntly that "Netanyahu may be an asshole, but to Trump world, he was their asshole."

## Architects of the deal

The article names the individuals it holds responsible for the JCPOA. [[jake-sullivan]] operated at the policy level, and is associated in the text with the 7th Floor Group, the [[carnegie-endowment-for-international-peace|Carnegie Endowment]], the [[trilateral-commission]] and a Rhodes Scholarship. [[william-burns]] served as lead negotiator and is linked to the Council on Foreign Relations, the Carnegie Endowment, the National Endowment for Democracy board, and described as an Epstein associate. Sullivan later became the top cabinet official for [[joe-biden]] and had been a significant behind-the-scenes figure in Hillary Clinton's campaign; Burns became Biden's CIA director. The author calls them "two certified swamp creatures in high places," and quotes a PBS account noting that Burns headed a secret negotiating team that met high-level Iranian representatives first in 2008 under George W. Bush and then in earnest in 2013, when the Obama and Rouhani governments revived the talks. His earlier diplomatic analysis on Russia in 2008 was the subject of the previous Badlands essay [[a-bright-red-line|A Bright Red Line]].

A third figure is [[tom-donilon]], described as a long-active operator who advised the presidential campaigns of Jimmy Carter, Walter Mondale, Joe Biden, Michael Dukakis, Bill Clinton, Barack Obama and Hillary Clinton, designing policy, managing conventions, preparing candidates for debates and overseeing presidential transitions. Donilon has been a fixture at [[bilderberg]] (serving on the steering committee), the CFR, the Trilateral Commission and the [[world-economic-forum]], but the article stresses his role as chairman of the BlackRock Investment Institute, "essentially the 'brain' of BlackRock Inc." Technically Sullivan's superior at the time of the deal, Donilon called Trump's withdrawal "the worst move in the Middle East since the Iraq War." The article notes that Biden originally tapped Donilon for CIA Director before he went to BlackRock instead.

Because BlackRock holds considerable — "if not majority" — equity stakes in Chevron, Exxon, BP and Shell, all of which stood to gain from the deal, the article concludes that when Israel backed Trump it "naturally caused the CIA-controlled [[us-chamber-of-commerce|Chamber of Commerce]] to turn on Israel." The Iran Deal, it argues, split the ruling class factionally more than any other issue of its time.

## China

Trump's withdrawal from the deal was accompanied by the "maximum pressure" campaign, under which he threatened "enormous consequences" for any country evading sanctions on Iran. Biden's policy is presented as the inverse: within months of taking office he allowed China to sign a $400 billion agreement with Iran, reported by The New York Times in March 2021.

The article describes this as an oil and gas deal carrying the added effect of a comprehensive economic and military partnership, with Iran folded into China's [[belt-and-road-initiative]], the centrepiece of Chinese foreign policy. China would pay large sums for oil and gas while also initiating sweeping infrastructure investment in Iran, much as the United States had done in Saudi Arabia. That Biden permitted this "geostrategic nightmare" two months into his presidency might be called incompetence, the author writes, but appears intentional in light of the Biden family's public history of earning money from China — notably the millions the Bidens are said to have made from [[cefc-china-energy]], then one of China's largest energy firms.

Burns and the Carnegie Endowment are likewise accused of profiting from China. The article quotes a Daily Caller report stating that while serving as president of the Carnegie board, Burns welcomed the Chinese businessman [[zhang-yichen]] — linked to the Center for China and Globalization and the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — who donated between $750,000 and $1.5 million to Carnegie. Carnegie is also said to have received millions from the [[china-us-exchange-foundation|China-U.S. Exchange Foundation]] (CUSEF), described as responsible for United Front work in the United States, money used to build the Carnegie–Tsinghua Center in Beijing.

Relations between China and Israel had been relatively good before the $400 billion deal, the article notes, but strained once China committed nearly half a trillion dollars to the state funding proxies on Israel's borders. Iran was essential to the Belt and Road Initiative in a way Israel could never be. With China aligned with the Atlanticists and with Democrats and neoconservatives, Trump world — "populated by notorious China hawks" — was further incentivised to align with the Netanyahu government.

Addressing pundits who call Iran "a paper tiger," the article concedes the point after decades of sanctions but warns that "Iran has powerful friends in low places." Should China choose to protect its investment, it could supply Iran and its proxies with more missiles than the iron dome and David's Sling could intercept in a single barrage, turning Iran into an existential threat to Israel and producing an open-ended proxy war of attrition comparable to Ukraine. The unanswered question posed is whether the United States would respond with hot war against China.

## Pakistan

The article extends the analysis to South Asia, arguing that when the Biden administration and the foreign policy establishment "went all in on Iran," they undertook further moves that altered the geopolitical equation — among them the overthrow of [[imran-khan]] in [[pakistan]]. Khan is described as another notch in the belt of the State Department's regime change machine: a populist figure, former cricket star and founder of the [[pakistan-tehreek-e-insaf]] (PTI) party, initially perceived as tied to the military establishment including the [[inter-services-intelligence]] (ISI), characterised as Pakistan's CIA and kept "on a tight leash" by the United States.

Khan's relationship with the agency deteriorated during his tenure; like Trump, the article suggests, he had his own plans for running the country that ran against his own rogue intelligence services and the foreign policy establishment. After his removal by a parliamentary no-confidence vote in April 2022, which he attributed to military and ISI interference, Khan openly accused the military — including then-ISI chief Nadeem Anjum and former army chief General Qamar Javed Bajwa — of orchestrating his ouster. The article identifies his "aggressively neutral" stance on Russia and his threats to block Pakistani arms shipments to Ukraine as primary reasons a target was placed on his back, concluding that "the establishment dislikes leaders making their own foreign policy decisions."

With Khan removed, the article states, CIA- and deep-state-friendly assets again run Pakistan and the ISI — and by the month before publication Pakistan was threatening to nuke Israel. Referencing the earlier Badlands piece "Operation Cyclone: The Seeds of Terror," it notes that Pakistan remains indispensable to the United States as an ally against Russia, just as in the 1980s when the Reagan government armed and funded the Al Qaeda precursors fighting the Soviets, and that a continuing function of the ISI is to serve as a clearinghouse for U.S. weapons, equipment and funds destined for CIA and Department of Defense conflict zones.

The article further asserts that Biden, having overthrown Khan's democratically elected government, then attempted regime change against [[narendra-modi]] in [[india]] — moves that can be viewed through the lens of a Biden partnership with China. Trump's relationship with Modi is described as the opposite: "fantastic and growing," with Trump seeking to build up India as a wedge against China.

## Conclusion

The author lists the parties with a vested interest in the outcome: U.S. Chamber of Commerce companies, the world's largest energy firms, [[qatar]], Saudi Arabia, Russia, China, Pakistan, India, and the two competing factions of the Western ruling class. Handled differently, the article argues, the Iran question could have rendered Trump a lame-duck president by fracturing his base so badly that Republicans were "absolutely wrecked in the midterms"; yet it could not be ignored either.

The situation is called a geopolitical minefield in which missteps could destabilise the Middle East, draw in nuclear-armed states like Pakistan and India, escalate tensions with China and further erode public trust. Navigating it, the author writes, "requires a finesse that balances populist appeal with strategic pragmatism," and Trump's ability to hold his coalition together while countering the establishment's entrenched power will determine whether he reshapes the global order or falls victim to its traps. Acknowledging the tremendous pressure Trump faces from the Netanyahu government, the piece concedes that "the truth of the situation is no Israel, no Trump 1 or 2." It closes by observing that although the "12-day war" is over, Iran remains a hugely important piece on the board, and that while "the writer's room is currently working on the next story arc of 'War with Iran,' it's evident that the show has been renewed for another season."
