# Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty

The Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) is the international agreement governing the spread of nuclear weapons, under which a limited set of states — including the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]] and [[france|France]] — are recognized as nuclear-armed states.[1]

The treaty regime was invoked in February 2026 when [[russia|Russia]]'s UN ambassador [[vasily-nebenzya|Vasily Nebenzya]] told the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] that Britain and France were considering transferring nuclear weapons capabilities to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and were exploring ways to "circumvent" nonproliferation restrictions in order to arm Kyiv. He offered no publicly verifiable evidence, and neither government had announced such a plan.[1] Ukraine itself relinquished the Soviet nuclear arsenal it inherited during the 1990s under the [[budapest-memorandum|Budapest Memorandum]].[1]

## Israeli non-signatory status (2023 controversy)

Israel's refusal to sign the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty returned to prominence in November 2023 after Israeli Minister of Heritage [[amihai-eliyahu|Amihai Eliyahu]] said that dropping a nuclear bomb on the Gaza Strip was "one of the possibilities."[2] Because Israel has never openly tested its nuclear weapons nor signed the NPT, it is described as the world's only unacknowledged atomic power, and has never been subjected to an inspection by the U.N. nuclear watchdog.[2] Russian foreign ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] used Eliyahu's remark to ask whether it amounted to official acknowledgement of Israel's arsenal, and where the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspectors were.[2] Israel's first deliverable nuclear weapon is thought to have been completed in late 1966 or early 1967, making it the sixth country in the world to develop them.[2]
