# Sabotage of the First Trump Administration

The sabotage of the first Trump administration is a recurring Badlands Media thesis holding that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term was undermined less by his open political opponents than by officials nominally on his own side who occupied positions inside his White House and executive agencies.[1] Erik Carlson's December 27, 2023 article "The Sabotage of Trump's Administration" sets out the argument at length, using [[olivia-troye|Olivia Troye]] as its principal case study.[1]

## The argument

Carlson contends that [[rino|RINOs]] and [[neoconservatives|Neocons]] are a greater threat than the progressive left because they are traitors not merely to the country but to the constituents who believe they are advancing conservative values, selling out to the highest bidder.[1] On this reading, Trump spent his first term fighting not only Democrats but people inside his own administration who were constantly undermining him.[1] He applies the frame directly to the two grievances conservatives most often hold against Trump — the southern border and the COVID-19 vaccines — arguing that Trump neither patrolled the border himself nor developed vaccines in a laboratory, and that his efforts on both fronts were being sabotaged from within and without.[1]

The article suggests the [[deep-state|Deep State]] infiltrated the White House through [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]]'s staff, without resolving whether such figures were known moles from the outset and deliberately fed misinformation, or were only discovered later and jettisoned once they ceased to be useful.[1] Carlson likewise leaves open whether Pence himself was complicit.[1]

## Trust, loyalty and staffing

Carlson argues that roughly 99% of CEOs and politicians are compromised in some way, that the "good guys" cannot imprison everyone without collapsing the economy and triggering civil conflict, and that many figures have quietly cut deals — a framework he uses to reject the habit of dismissing any official over statements made years earlier.[1] In his account, people who once denied that elections were stolen, took COVID vaccines, or believed January 6 was an insurrection are being given a "green light" to support Trump without having to agree with him on everything.[1] He connects the wave of congressional resignations in late 2023 to the same dynamic, floating possibilities ranging from weakening GOP control of the House, to negotiated exits in exchange for non-prosecution, to fear of the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] flight logs — while cautioning that absence from a list is not evidence of innocence.[1]

He also argues that Trump's early personnel record reflects scarcity rather than poor judgement: there may not have been enough unimpeachable figures in Washington to fill the posts that needed filling, leaving loyalty as the operative test.[1] The dismissal of [[rex-tillerson|Rex Tillerson]] over the [[iran-nuclear-agreement|Iran Nuclear Agreement]], the promotion of [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] to the [[state-department|State Department]] and the installation of [[gina-haspel|Gina Haspel]] at the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] are read as steps in a deliberate containment of an agency Trump had come to regard as hostile.[1] Carlson notes that in his final year Trump bypassed the CIA — declining to use it on COVID origins, on Antifa and the BLM riots, or on election fraud — and routed intelligence work through the State Department instead.[1]

## Wider context in the article

The essay opens with unrelated commentary arguing that the 2023 stock market rally was a coordinated scam in which nearly all S&P 500 gains came from seven artificial-intelligence-linked technology stocks while the other 493 companies were flat, and that the NASDAQ's recovery of its 2022 losses made no economic sense against rising homelessness, personal and corporate bankruptcies, record-low savings and record-high credit card debt.[1] Carlson also treats the Republican primary debates as theatre, describing [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] as a RINO/Neocon backed by the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] after [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] faltered, and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] as doing Trump's "dirty work" by making the arguments Trump would be penalised for making himself.[1]

The piece closes by predicting that more figures like Troye will surface before the 2024 election claiming firsthand insider knowledge, and argues that the next administration will differ because Trump — rather than the Republican Party — will hand-pick a staff he can trust, leaving no one inside the White House working against him.[1]
