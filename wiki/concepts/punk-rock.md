# Punk Rock

Punk rock is a musical genre and underground subculture that, in Badlands Media's account, emerged from working-class angst and youth frustration over economic inequality, bourgeois hypocrisy, and government neglect of working people and their struggles.[1] Its core preoccupations were rebellion, anti-authoritarianism, individualism, and free thought, and the scene originally welcomed an array of different ideologies rather than enforcing a single political line.[1]

## Original character

Badlands Media argues that punk's founding ethic was hostility to the [[corporatocracy]] and its legacy media, and that "selling out" to a label or corporation was regarded as one of the greatest sins an aspiring punk or underground hip-hop artist could commit.[1] The article contends that many were drawn to the genre by a desire to belong somewhere in a society they experienced as broken and stale.[1]

## The modern scene

According to the article, the pop-culture "punks" of today have inverted these values, gravitating toward what it calls dissociated fascism, mob conformity, and the suppression of dissent by threat of violence.[1] The original array of ideologies has, in this telling, been reduced to a single outlook after years of indoctrination through college campuses, television, music, and pop culture generally.[1] Badlands Media further argues that people increasingly build a persona around a look or lifestyle without engaging the culture behind it, and that today's self-described punks are more likely to defend large corporations than the sovereignty of their own countries.[1]

## Conservative punk figures

The article's central claim is that several founding punk figures hold politics at odds with the genre's modern progressive branding. [[john-lydon|John Lydon]] of [[sex-pistols|the Sex Pistols]] endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and supported [[brexit]] and [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]]; [[glenn-danzig|Glenn Danzig]] of [[the-misfits|the Misfits]] defended the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s travel ban; and [[johnny-ramone|Johnny Ramone]] of [[the-ramones]] was an outspoken conservative whose debates with his liberal bandmate were, per [[marky-ramone|Marky Ramone]], conducted with mutual respect.[1] Badlands Media uses these examples to argue that [[america-first-movement|America First]] conservatism is the authentic heir to punk's anti-establishment posture.[1]

## Co-optation of counterculture

The article holds that virtually every alternative subculture or counterculture has been infiltrated, absorbed, and commercialized, and that nothing of cultural significance remains organic for long without attracting the attention of the very forces it arose to oppose.[1] That force has gone by many names — the [[deep-state|Deep State]], the Establishment, Corporate America, the financial elite, the 1%, Big Brother, the Man.[1] Badlands Media describes it as a marriage of international corporate conglomerates, the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], the [[military-industrial-complex|Military-Industrial Complex]], [[hollywood]], and Big Tech, which together prop up establishment politicians such as [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], the Clintons, and the Bushes.[1] The article also cautions against assuming that political revolution would loosen elite control, suggesting instead that ruling interests use such moments to enhance control under a rebranded, well-marketed banner.[1]
