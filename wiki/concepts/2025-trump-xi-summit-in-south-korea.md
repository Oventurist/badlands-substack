# 2025 Trump–Xi Summit in South Korea

The 2025 Trump–Xi summit was a bilateral meeting between U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] held in South Korea on October 29–30, 2025, on the margins of Trump's three-country Asia tour and the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation gathering.[1]

## Proceedings

Opening the meeting before cameras, Trump called it "a great honor to be with a friend of mine, really, for a long time now," adding that "we've already agreed to a lot of things, and we'll agree to some more right now," and describing Xi as "a great leader of a great country."[1] Xi replied that it was "a great pleasure" and "very warm seeing you again," noting that since the start of the year the two leaders had held three phone calls and exchanged several letters while staying in close contact.[1] Xi urged that the two "stay the right course" and said that "China's development goes hand in hand with your vision to make America great again."[1]

The remainder of the meeting was closed to the media. Trump had been overheard at a dinner the previous night suggesting the session could run as long as four hours; in the event, the bilateral concluded after roughly one hour and forty minutes.[1] Members of the U.S. delegation were reported smiling as they departed, with Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] offering a thumbs up.[1]

## Outcomes

Agriculture Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke Rollins]] confirmed during the summit that China had resumed purchases of U.S. soybeans, ending a halt that had been a major pain point for American farmers, who had pressed the Trump administration to resolve the issue given that China is the largest market for U.S. soybeans.[1]

Air Force One departed Busan, South Korea, at about 1:15 p.m. local time, ending the Asia tour, with Trump scheduled to arrive back at the White House at 3:05 p.m. Eastern.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentators treated the summit as choreography rather than negotiation. Burning Bright argued that Trump had positioned the meeting — "rather, the STORY of his meeting" — by first rebuffing the European Union, and characterized the emerging arrangement as a "G2" in which global commerce would follow whatever Trump and Xi decided, contending the two leaders were "on the same side."[1] Ashe in America noted that a reporter's expectation of a four-hour meeting was overtaken within 41 minutes by news that China was buying soybeans again, reinforcing the view that "the deals are already done" and that the visible proceedings were pageantry.[1] She read both leaders' statements as consistent with an alliance in which each state remains sovereign and pursues its own national interest.[1]
