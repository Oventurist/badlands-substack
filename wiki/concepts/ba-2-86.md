# BA.2.86

BA.2.86 is a lineage of SARS-CoV-2, the coronavirus that causes COVID-19, that drew attention from public-health agencies in August 2023 because of the unusually large number of mutations distinguishing it from then-circulating variants.[1]

## CDC assessment, August 2023

On August 23, 2023, the U.S. [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] stated that the new BA.2.86 lineage might be more capable than older variants of causing infection in people who had previously had COVID-19 or who had received [[covid-19-vaccines|vaccines]].[1] The agency said it was too soon to know whether the lineage would cause more severe illness than its predecessors, but that the high number of mutations detected raised concerns about its impact on immunity derived from vaccination and prior infection.[1]

Scientists tracked BA.2.86 in particular because it carried 36 mutations distinguishing it from the then-dominant XBB.1.5 variant.[1] The CDC cautioned that virus samples were not yet broadly available for more reliable laboratory testing of antibody responses.[1] The agency had announced earlier in August 2023 that it was tracking the highly mutated lineage, which had by then been detected in the United States, Denmark, and Israel.[1] The CDC also said that the contemporaneous increase in U.S. hospitalizations was not likely being driven by BA.2.86.[1]

## Commentary and reception in alternative media

The [[reuters|Reuters]] report on the CDC statement was aggregated by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] in its [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of August 25, 2023.[1] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the announcement as an admission that those who complied with vaccine mandates for what she characterized as "experimental gene therapy" were more susceptible to new variants, contrasting this with official "safe and effective" messaging.[1]

She argued that the emergence of the variant reflected an attempt to re-run the [[covid-19-narrative|COVID-19 narrative]] on roughly the same timeline as the prior presidential election cycle, pointing to the rewriting of the history of masking, the reinstatement of mask requirements at some businesses and colleges, and [[pfizer|Pfizer]]'s share price — down 28 percent before the bell that Thursday — as evidence that a new booster campaign was commercially convenient.[1] Her recommendation to readers was non-compliance: "Do not play along. Do not comply. Do not be polite."[1]


## Spread and risk assessment (late 2023)

By late November 2023 the BA.2.86 variant, unofficially known as Pirola, was taking hold in the United States, with its prevalence rising from about 1 percent to roughly 9 percent between October 28 and November 25 according to the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]].[2] Compared with the earlier Eris variant, BA.2.86 has significantly lower growth efficiency, meaning it is less capable of replicating in the human body.[2] The [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] designated Pirola a variant of interest on November 21, 2023, while assessing the public health risk it posed as "low at the global level."[2] In an update published November 27, the CDC agreed with that assessment, judging the risk low compared with other circulating variants based on available limited evidence — reporting carried by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]].[2]
