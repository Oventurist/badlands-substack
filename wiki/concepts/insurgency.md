# Insurgency

**Insurgency**, in the doctrinal sense used by the [[counterinsurgency-guide|Counterinsurgency Guide]] and adopted in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary, refers to an organized movement that seeks to gain the support — or at least the acquiescence — of a population in order to displace or subvert an established government.[1]

## Doctrinal description

The guide describes the insurgent political strategy as a combination of three instruments.[1] **Persuasion** typically involves promotion of insurgent ideology, but may also take the form of money, basic social services, control of land or positions of authority, with propaganda serving as its key element at local, national and international levels — deployed to control community action, discredit government action, provoke overreaction by security forces, or exacerbate sectarian tension.[1] **Subversion** is the action used by insurgents to penetrate, manipulate, undermine or disrupt government institutions and organizations.[1] **Coercion** augments or replaces persuasion depending on the government's ability to protect the population, and involves intimidating government supporters or collaborators and forcing community leaders to take sides, often through violence.[1]

## Use in Badlands commentary

In "What's Taking So Long?" (December 28, 2022), [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] wrote that [[qanon|Q]] had told readers the United States is at war with an insurgency that has infiltrated not only every level of government but the whole of society, quoting the guide's definitions from Q post #4700.[1] He argued this was the situation [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] faced on the day he was sworn in and that Trump knew it, becoming a wartime president in an unconventional war whose principal battlefield is the media — because the contest is for the hearts and minds of the people, and the media have been "the enemy's greatest weapon."[1]

Lange identified the insurgency's foot soldiers with [[antifa|Antifa]] and [[black-lives-matter|BLM]], which he said the military had infiltrated, mapped and hollowed out, explaining in his view why no organized resistance formed against the overturning of [[roe-v-wade|Roe v. Wade]] despite the decision being leaked in advance.[1] He also argued that the insurgency had been politically marginalized over the preceding two years, pointed to the decline of the Democratic Party and the retirement or defeat of RINOs as evidence of "political reconciliation," and maintained that the long-term remedy is to awaken the population so that a future insurgency cannot take root.[1]

The counterpart doctrine is described at [[counterinsurgency|Counterinsurgency]].[1]
