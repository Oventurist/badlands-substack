# Biden Reversion to Trump-Era Policies

"Biden reversion to Trump-era policies" describes a pattern, documented across 2023 and early 2024, in which the administration of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] quietly reinstated policies and initiatives it had loudly undone upon taking office, many of them originating with his predecessor [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Documented reversals

Biden entered office promising to undo his predecessor's work, only to revert to Trump-era positions across an array of fronts.[1] In January 2024 he designated Yemen's [[houthis|Houthi]] rebels a "global terrorist group" after months of Red Sea attacks, and cut United States funding to the [[unrwa|United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East]] following allegations that agency staff participated in the 7 October Hamas attacks on Israel.[1] Trump had taken those same steps in 2021 and 2018 respectively, and Biden had reversed both to considerable fanfare shortly after his inauguration.[1] During the intervening rapprochement, Biden steered at least $730 million to UNRWA, which critics said allowed the organization to bolster its longstanding and widely documented ties to Hamas.[1] The Houthis, notable for restoring slavery to Yemen in 2019, have long been committed to the destruction of the United States.[1]

Energy policy followed a similar arc. On his last full day in office Trump imposed tough sanctions on a Russian vessel and its parent company involved in constructing the Nord Stream 2 pipeline, a project that would have doubled Russia's capacity to send natural gas to Europe.[1] Biden waived those sanctions in May 2021, overriding a State Department assessment, then re-imposed them just hours before Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022.[1] The pipeline was ultimately destroyed in September 2022 amid mysterious circumstances.[1] Reporting also grouped border wall construction and domestic oil policy among the reverted initiatives.[1]

## Political significance

GOP consultant Luke Thompson argued that the pattern created an opening for Trump: "Biden ran in 2020 on a whole bunch of hysterical rhetoric about a moral crisis and if he tries to do the same thing in 2024, Trump will have all of these areas to point to and say, 'Well if I'm such a bad guy why is your administration looking more and more like mine in terms of policy every week?'"[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the *Wall Street Journal* had recently published a lengthy piece in which the European Union's bureaucracy expressed alarm that the Biden administration — ostensibly meant to restore the Obama, Bush, and Clinton eras — instead resembled the Trump era in uncanny detail on foreign policy and trade, while the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] made the parallel case on the domestic side.[1] Burning Bright went further, contending that many of these measures *are* Trump policies, either redrafted and passed under new names or literally passed through the same [[congress|Congress]] that blocked them while Trump held public office, and framed the phenomenon as evidence that "winning is all around us, if we cultivate the eyes to see it."[1]
