# Eco-Terrorism

**Eco-terrorism** — political violence and sabotage carried out in the name of environmental protection — is the subject of [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s feature "The Rising Scourge of Eco-Terrorism," published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on 21 October 2025 and highlighted in the 7 November 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] retrospective.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

Ehret opens the essay with the 2022 Hollywood film *How to Blow Up a Pipeline*, in which several young, anxiety-ridden eco-activists conspire together to literally blow up pipelines in Texas in order to "save civilization."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) The film serves as evidence for his argument that sabotage in defense of the climate has acquired cultural legitimacy and is a rising phenomenon rather than a fringe one.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) The theme connects to Ehret's parallel writing on [[ted-kaczynski|Theodore Kaczynski]], whose anti-industrial axioms he traces to the same oligarchical milieu as the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] and the [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] movement.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

## Intellectual scaffolding

Ehret argues that a consistent body of pseudo-intellectual books and peer-reviewed sociological essays is a precondition for the momentum to tear down civilization from either "bottom up" or "top down" directions.[2] The 2022 film [[how-to-blow-up-a-pipeline|*How to Blow Up a Pipeline*]] derived from the 2021 book of the same name by Lund University professor [[andreas-malm|Andreas Malm]], who argues that society's only salvation lies in destroying the energy infrastructure sustaining modern life and who wrote in *The Guardian* in 2021 that "the days of gentle protest may be long over."[2] Malm has been praised by [[naomi-klein|Naomi Klein]] as "one of the most original thinkers" on climate change, and his thesis was repeated by Canadian broadcaster [[david-suzuki|David Suzuki]] at an [[extinction-rebellion|Extinction Rebellion]] event in November 2021.[2] Philosophy professor [[david-skrbina|David Skrbina]], who corresponded with [[ted-kaczynski|Ted Kaczynski]] from 2003 to 2023, extended the same current by arguing that technology advances as an autonomous law of nature that will enslave or eliminate humanity.[2]

## Above grounders and below grounders

The operational template Ehret identifies comes from [[deep-green-resistance|Deep Green Resistance]], founded in 2011 by Kaczynski's pen pal [[derrick-jensen|Derrick Jensen]], whose [[decisive-ecological-warfare|Decisive Ecological Warfare]] doctrine assigns public organizing to "above grounders" and infrastructure sabotage to "under grounders" in order to accelerate collapse deliberately.[2] Its news service maintains an "Underground Action Calendar" documenting over one hundred attacks on rail and energy infrastructure across the Americas and Europe, explicitly intended to "normalize" militant tactics.[2]

Ehret extends the category upward, contending that "above grounders" such as [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] and other Davos figures have damaged humanity's support systems through central banker climate compacts, nuclear moratoria in Europe and fertilizer quotas on farms.[2] He cites California governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s expenditure of over $500 million in taxpayer money to demolish four hydroelectric dams, and notes that 1,951 dams were demolished across the United States in a decade, including 57 in 2021.[2] Canada, meanwhile, unveiled the world's largest cricket farm while threatening a 30% reduction in food production by 2030.[2] Ehret asks whether the wave of explosions at food processing facilities and granaries and the train derailments across North America and Europe over the preceding two years reflect underground eco-terror campaigns.[2]
