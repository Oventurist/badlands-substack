# Digital Wallets

A **digital wallet** is a software application that stores cryptographic keys allowing a holder to receive, hold, and spend digital assets such as cryptocurrencies and non-fungible tokens. In [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary, digital wallets occupy a central place in the argument that the United States and the world are being prepared for a transition away from [[fiat-currency|fiat currency]].[1]

## The normalization thesis

In her December 2022 article on [[trump-digital-trading-cards|Trump Digital Trading Cards]], [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] argued that the principal purpose of the Trump NFT launch was not revenue or vanity but the mass normalization of digital wallets.[1] The purchase instructions on collecttrumpcards.com invited buyers to pay with ETH (WETH) and stated that cards "will be minted and delivered to your digital wallet," which could be accessed simply with an email address — a deliberately low-friction onboarding path for people who had never held crypto.[1] She paired this with [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]]'s "Melania's Vision" NFT of December 16, 2021, and the stated intention of both platforms to release NFTs "regularly," concluding that each release incrementally increases the number of Americans holding a wallet.[1]

Gillespie noted the same idea was raised independently by Dave of the [[x22-report|X22 Report]] in episode 2949a, "The Economic Transition Has Begun, How Do You Introduce Digital Wallets?", in which he said, "I do believe this is the beginning of the transition into a different currency, and he's bringing the people with him."[1]

## Distinction from CBDC

The commentary draws a sharp line between digital wallets as such and [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]], which it treats as a control mechanism capable of switching a person's money off, capping purchases, or geofencing spending.[1] The alternative envisaged is an asset-backed digital currency — gold or silver denominated — held in private digital wallets, on the reasoning that one cannot "break a piece off a gold bar" to buy groceries and so a digital representation of metal is required.[1] Gillespie speculated that a "beta" test of such a currency had already been deployed to a small group worldwide and would be secured by blockchain or quantum technology, though she conceded the underlying mechanism was "way above my pay grade."[1]
