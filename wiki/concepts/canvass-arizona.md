# Arizona Election Canvass

In Arizona election law the "canvass" is the formal process by which a governing body reviews and certifies election returns. It is governed by A.R.S. § 16-642, which provides in subsection (A) that "the governing body holding an election shall meet and canvass the election not less than six days nor more than twenty days following the election." For the November 8, 2022 general election this placed the outer deadline for county canvasses at November 28, 2022.

## The 2022 controversy

The canvass deadline structured the entire dispute described in [[an-sos-from-ground-zero|An SOS From Ground Zero]]. Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] closed her November 19, 2022 letter by noting that the problems she catalogued "relate to Maricopa County's ability to lawfully certify election results" and requesting a response "on or before Maricopa County submits its official canvass to the Secretary of State, which must occur on or before November 28, 2022. See A.R.S. § 16-642(A)." The obligation to canvass fell on the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]] as the governing body, with the certified results transmitted to the [[arizona-secretary-of-state|Secretary of State]].

[[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] emphasizes that the canvass is simply another name for certification, and draws attention to subsection (C) of the statute: "If, at the time of the meeting of the governing body, the returns from any polling place in the election district where the polls were opened and an election held are found to be missing, the canvass shall be postponed from day to day until all the returns are received or until six postponements have been had." Given the reported problems at [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] polling places — printer failures, [[door-3-ballots|Door 3]] commingling, and check-out irregularities — she speculates that the six-postponement limit might be reached, leaving the state in what she calls "uncharted territory," since the statute does not plainly specify what follows.
