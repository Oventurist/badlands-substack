# Haredi Conscription Crisis

The Haredi conscription crisis refers to the political confrontation in Israel over ending the exemption from compulsory military service that Orthodox Jews have enjoyed since the state's founding.[1]

## July 2025 escalation

As Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] concluded a week in Washington in July 2025, generals of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] were reported to be drawing up orders to begin the conscription of Orthodox Jews, who had been exempt from the service required of all other Israeli citizens since Israel's founding.[1]

The Orthodox community — represented in part by hardline figures inside Netanyahu's Security Cabinet — threatened to dissolve his government if he did not block the conscription orders and codify the exemption into law.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] assessed that this domestic pressure, combined with warnings from [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] against any deal that would prevent Israel from continuing its war with [[hamas|Hamas]], helped explain why Netanyahu flew home without an agreement to showcase.[1]


## Lando's 'global struggle' declaration (August 2025)

On Wednesday, August 6, 2025, following the arrest of two Haredi draft evaders, the spiritual leader of the ultra-Orthodox [[degel-hatorah|Degel HaTorah]] party, Rabbi [[dov-lando|Dov Lando]], accused the Israeli government of waging war on the Haredi community.[2] "The State of Israel has declared war on yeshiva students. Haredi Judaism will embark on a global struggle like never before," his spokesman declared.[2] Lando is among the most prominent rabbinic leaders of the "Lithuanian" stream of ultra-Orthodoxy, and Degel HaTorah is one of the two factions — alongside the Hasidic Agudat Yisrael — that make up the [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] party.[2] The statement followed an emergency consultation Lando convened with Haredi activists at his home in Bnei Brak to build a consensus response across Haredi sub-groups.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the confrontation as the opening of a civil conflict within the diaspora, observing that the ultra-Orthodox generally favor continued annexation of neighboring lands while refusing to supply their own children to the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to achieve it.[2]
