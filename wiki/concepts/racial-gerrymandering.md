# Racial Gerrymandering

**Racial gerrymandering** refers to the drawing of electoral district boundaries in which race is the predominant consideration, a practice challenged under the [[fourteenth-amendment|14th]] and [[fifteenth-amendment|15th]] Amendments to the [[us-constitution|United States Constitution]].[1]

## Legal framework after Callais

For decades, states asserted that Section 2 of the [[voting-rights-act-of-1965|Voting Rights Act of 1965]] required the creation of additional majority-minority districts. In [[louisiana-v-callais|Louisiana v. Callais]] (2026), the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] held 6–3 that [[louisiana|Louisiana]]'s second majority-Black district was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander.[1] Justice [[samuel-alito|Samuel Alito]]'s majority opinion held that compliance with Section 2 can justify consideration of race but does not require states to adopt maps that prioritize racial outcomes.[1]

The ruling leaves Section 2 intact but is expected to constrain how it is applied in future [[2026-redistricting-battle|redistricting]] litigation.[1] Justice [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]] would have gone further, excluding districting claims from the statute altogether.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the decision ends race-based manipulation of electoral maps and should produce districts of a more "natural and organic" composition, while leaving purely political gerrymandering untouched.[1]
