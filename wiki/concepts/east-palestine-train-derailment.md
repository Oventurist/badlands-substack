# East Palestine Train Derailment

The East Palestine train derailment occurred on February 3, 2023, when a [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern]] freight train derailed in East Palestine, Ohio, near the Ohio–Pennsylvania border.[1]

## The incident

Thirty-eight cars derailed, twenty of them carrying hazardous materials including vinyl chloride, ethylene glycol, ethylhexyl acrylate, butyl acrylate and isobutylene.[1] According to Congressional records, emergency responders intentionally vented and burned vinyl chloride from some tank cars to reduce the risk of a catastrophic explosion.[1] Residents within roughly a one-mile radius of the derailment were ordered to evacuate because of hazardous material release and fires.[1]

## Cause

The [[national-transportation-safety-board|National Transportation Safety Board]] and the [[federal-railroad-administration|Federal Railroad Administration]] determined that the derailment was caused by a failed and overheated wheel bearing on a railcar.[1]

## Casualties and litigation

No fatalities were reported at the time of the derailment.[1] In February 2025, new lawsuits alleged that at least seven people — including a one-week-old infant — died as a result of exposure to toxic chemicals from the derailment and its aftermath; these deaths are allegations in civil suits and have not affected official casualty figures.[1]

## Federal response under Trump

In February 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that East Palestine would receive $10 million for economic recovery through the [[economic-development-administration|Economic Development Administration]], in addition to his administration's long-term health study for residents.[1] Trump wrote that the town's people "were disgracefully betrayed and forgotten about by Crooked Joe Biden," referring to former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and pledged, "we are with you for the LONG HAUL," saying he wanted the community to "grow, flourish, and be able to take advantage of our now BOOMING Economy."[1]

## Biden's delayed visit

Nearly a month after the derailment, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said on 2 March 2023 that he had "spoken with every official in Ohio" and would "be out there at some point," remarks circulated by RNC Research and widely criticized as evasive.[2] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the residents of East Palestine could "breathe a collective sigh of relief" at the non-commitment, argued that even an immediate visit would be too little too late, and predicted any eventual trip would serve as a campaign photo opportunity once a narrative blaming [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been assembled.[2] He called the administration's disregard for the affected Americans reprehensible, contrasting the delay with Biden's trip to [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2]

## First anniversary and rising derailments

The derailment occurred in February 2023, when a [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern]] freight train carrying hazardous chemicals came off the tracks in East Palestine, Ohio, forcing the evacuation of hundreds of residents and upending life in the town for months.[3] Regulators concluded the cause was an overheated wheel bearing on the 1.75-mile-long train.[3] The rail industry pledged to become safer and members of [[us-congress|Congress]] vowed to pass preventive legislation, but no bill was passed and accidents increased.[3]

Derailments rose at the top five freight railroads in 2023 according to regulatory reports covering the first ten months of the year, with a steep increase in overheated wheel bearing failures specifically; Norfolk Southern was the only one of the five to report a decline.[3] The [[railway-safety-act-of-2023|Railway Safety Act]] introduced in March 2023 stalled amid resistance from rail lobbyists and had not received a full Senate vote.[3]

Marking the first anniversary over the weekend of February 3–4, 2024, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the environmental catastrophe remained unresolved — creeks still polluted, residents still displaced, and no meaningful oversight achieved — and that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] still had not visited the site despite state officials asking him to.[3] She noted that those who questioned the frequency of derailments at the time had been dismissed as conspiracy theorists, only for The New York Times to confirm a year later that derailments were rising, and criticized Transportation Secretary [[pete-buttigieg|Pete Buttigieg]] for inaction.[3]

## Downwind effects in the Mahoning Valley

Following the controlled release of chemicals in East Palestine on Monday, February 13, 2023, residents across the Mahoning Valley — including in Mahoning and Trumbull counties — reported smelling chlorine, while officials said the release was not dangerous.[4] In North Lima, a little more than ten miles from East Palestine, [[amanda-breshears|Amanda Breshears]] said the smell caused her eyes to water and, the following morning, found her five hens and rooster dead in nearly identical positions with no sign that a predator had entered the enclosure.[4]

## Badlands commentary on government trust

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the disaster through a trust theory of government: the people convey their inalienable rights to government as trustees who must defend and foster their fair use, and when trustees fail in that duty the people can and must take those rights back and remove the incompetence.[4] He argued the derailment was one of the more obvious cases of government malfeasance and breach of trust, that criminal charges should follow where open bad faith and malevolence are found, and that in practice bad actors historically escape with fines or payouts treated as the cost of doing business while institutions and courts provide no remedy.[4] He further contended that environmental disasters of this type advance an agenda to push people into mega cities, warning that even if the climate change agenda were stopped, a thoroughly toxified environment would leave people unable to live freely.[4]


## Class action and DNA mutation allegations

A class action lawsuit filed in February 2023 alleged that East Palestine residents "may already be undergoing DNA mutations" as a result of exposure following the derailment.[5] The suit came as [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern Railway]] chief executive [[alan-shaw|Alan Shaw]] published a letter pledging, "We will not walk away, East Palestine," even as company officials skipped a town hall meeting with residents seeking answers.[5] The derailed train comprised 150 cars, twenty of which were carrying hazardous materials, and the subsequent controlled burn of vinyl chloride produced increasing reports of short-term health impacts, with residents as far away as Pittsburgh questioning the potential consequences.[5]

## Truth Community interpretation

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that East Palestine was "an excellent place to learn about how events like this are intentionally curated and used to advance the [[new-world-order|NWO]] agenda."[5] He cited as a first red flag the railroad industry's policy changes that, in his account, effectively guaranteed a number of derailments, and which a vigilant populace should have addressed.[5] He compared the cleanup to the 2010 Deepwater Horizon response, characterising it as "an intentional and planned eco-terrorist event" whose benefits included toxifying the environment so as to force citizens into mega cities in years to come.[5]

Deschamps further pointed to the near-total absence of media coverage and the lack of alarm among professional environmentalists as confirmation that such groups were "merely foot soldiers of the globalist elite," incapable of acting outside the dictates of officialdom.[5] He described the toll on local communities, wildlife and people as unparalleled, singling out the likelihood of DNA mutation and asking what those mutations would do and how they would serve globalist aims.[5] If the event was pre-planned, he concluded, an agenda exploiting the crisis would be rolled out later, and the appropriate response was for the public to learn from it and mount a better defence.[5]

## 2025 settlement contribution trial

The week of March 31, 2025 marked the start of a trial to determine whether two companies would repay [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern]] for the $600 million class-action settlement in the derailment case, which the railroad had already accepted.[6] Norfolk Southern sought contributions from [[gatx-corporation|GATX Corp.]], a railcar lessor, and [[oxy-vinyls|Oxy Vinyls]], a chemical manufacturer.[6] The settlement, together with a 27 percent attorney fee, had been granted the previous year by US District Judge [[benita-pearson|Benita Pearson]], who also supervised the contribution trial.[6] The compensation sum would remain the same if the jury ruled in Norfolk Southern's favor: the two businesses would pay the rail company, but the rail company remained liable for the payout.[6]

## Early citizen-journalist coverage (February 2023)

Within ten days of the February 3, 2023 derailment, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[woke-societies|Woke Societies]] was leading a citizen-journalist effort to publicise the unfolding ecological disaster, publishing a viral Twitter thread on February 13 that described dead animals, residents falling ill, arrested journalists and what he called a mainstream media blackout.[7] He framed the timing pointedly, writing that "while the government is trying to psyop the country with downed UFO stories a domestic Chernobyl is happening in #EastPalestine Ohio since February 3rd."[7] The thread drew roughly 2,760 reposts and 5,040 likes.[7]

Badlands contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] echoed the "Chernobyl-sized disaster" characterisation, arguing that the public was being distracted by objects in the sky, talk of world war and the Super Bowl while no major outlet covered the spill, and that reports of journalists arrested for asking questions suggested "a cover-up of epic proportions."[7] He also criticised Secretary of Transportation [[pete-buttigieg|Pete Buttigieg]] for making no mention of the disaster while commenting publicly on the racial composition of the rail workforce.[7]
