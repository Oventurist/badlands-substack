# Addressing Risks from Chris Krebs and Government Censorship

"Addressing Risks from Chris Krebs and Government Censorship" is a presidential memorandum signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on April 9, 2025, directing the revocation of security clearances held by former [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|CISA]] director [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]] and a review of his conduct in office.[1]

## Text and findings

The memorandum opens by asserting that the federal government has a constitutional duty and moral responsibility to respect and promote the free speech rights of Americans, and charges that in recent years "elitist leaders in Government have unlawfully censored speech and weaponized their undeserved influence to silence perceived political opponents and advance their preferred, and often erroneous, narrative about significant matters of public debate."[1] It names Krebs, the former head of CISA, as "a significant bad-faith actor who weaponized and abused his Government authority."[1] The document states that Krebs, through CISA, promoted the censorship of election information, including known risks associated with certain voting practices, and "falsely and baselessly denied that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen," inappropriately and categorically dismissing widespread election malfeasance and serious vulnerabilities with voting machines.[1]

## Directives

Trump directed the heads of executive departments and agencies to immediately take steps consistent with existing law to revoke any active security clearance held by Krebs, and to suspend any active security clearances held by individuals at entities associated with him, including [[sentinelone|SentinelOne]].[1] The memorandum further ordered a review of Krebs' activities as a government employee, including his leadership of CISA, and recommendations for appropriate remedial or preventative actions.[1]

## Reception in Badlands commentary

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] called it "the most secure memorandum in American history."[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she believed the underlying investigation was already complete, described Krebs — "the unqualified lawyer with his undergrad in environmental science that was inexplicably the nation's top 'cyber expert' in 2020" — as "patient zero," the 2020 narrative peddler whom Trump had just called a fraud and a "bad guy," and argued the move pointed toward "returning the diamonds" and prosecuting the thieves.[1] Her commentary recounted the [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud]] thesis that five states stopped counting on election night because Florida had been called against the regime's expectations, leaving 29 electoral votes to make up.[1] [[canncon|CannCon]] noted that Michigan Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] was unexpectedly present in the Oval Office as Trump signed the order, and [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] said 2020 election fraud exposure was ramping up.[1]
