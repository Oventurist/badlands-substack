# Righteous Russia

"Righteous Russia" is a multi-part essay series written by Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] arguing that the apparent hostility between the United States and Russia is a narrative construction concealing an underlying alignment against the globalist [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1]

## Core argument

The series rests on the observation that, while the "Actuals" of geopolitics are almost impossible to parse, the Narrative matters just as much, because the collective American mind has been programmed with an inherent distrust of "the Russian" for the better part of a century.[1] Burning Bright holds that this engineering can be reversed through what he calls the Reverse Hegelian Dialectic, and cites Christopher Nolan's *Inception* as the premier illustration of the underlying psychological principle: "The greater the trauma, the greater the catharsis."[1]

On that basis he argues that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] would invert the enemy's narrative programming through a trauma–catharsis dialectic, so that escalating rhetoric toward [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] is a cause for excitement rather than alarm.[1]

## Part 4: The Death of War (2022)

Burning Bright quoted his own 2022 instalment to rebut charges of revisionism: "In order to make peace, two leaders must first seem on the verge of war. In order to be seen as unifiers, the prospect of doing so must seem insurmountable."[1] The passage describes a world in a collective emotional state, searching for characters, sides, throughlines and themes — heroes and villains — and predicts that "Trump and Putin will turn this around on them not just because they are better commanders, better strategists and better thinkers than their collective enemy, but because they are better storytellers."[1]

He added, citing [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], that Trump has been calling for an alliance with "the Soviets" since the late 1980s, concluding: "It seems someone was listening."[1] Fellow contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] framed the same dynamic more tersely as "Trump-Putin [[kayfabe|kayfabe]]," expressing the hope that neocons and warmongers would show themselves in full.[1]

## Putin as founder of the Sovereign Alliance

In October 2025 [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] closed an essay on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s dealmaking with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] with the phrase "Righteous Russia indeed," having argued that he had long considered Putin a core member of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and very possibly its unofficial founder.[2] The argument rested on reading Putin's post-2000 de-privatization campaign — the dismantling of [[yukos|Yukos]], [[gazprom|Gazprom]]'s reclamation of gas assets and state takeovers in aerospace and nuclear energy — as the original template that Trump and Xi are now applying with tariffs and supply chain chokepoints.[2]
