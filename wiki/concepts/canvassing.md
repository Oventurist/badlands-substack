# Canvassing

Canvassing is the practice of going door to door to speak with residents for political, electoral, or civic purposes. Defendants in [[naacp-v-useip|*NAACP et al. v. USEIP et al.*]] argued that canvassing is not inherently intimidating and is squarely protected by settled case law.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

All three plaintiff organizations in that case — the [[naacp|NAACP]], the [[league-of-women-voters-colorado|League of Women Voters of Colorado]], and [[mi-familia-vota|Mi Familia Vota]] — conduct political canvassing as a matter of ordinary business, because grassroots organizing requires it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back) The ACLU has published practical guidance advising canvassers of their rights, and the case law on infringement of canvassing rights strongly protects the activity; the defense compiled that history in a trial brief filed with the court before trial.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

The defense summarized its position in three propositions: election denying is not intimidating; canvassing is not intimidating; and [[election-denial|election deniers]] engaging in canvassing is also not intimidating.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back) The problem, defendants argued, arises only when the subject is 2020 and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

## Canvass in election law

In Arizona election law the word "canvass" carries an entirely different meaning: it denotes the official certification of election returns by the governing body that held the election.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Under A.R.S. § 16-642(A), "the governing body holding an election shall meet and canvass the election not less than six days nor more than twenty days following the election," which for the November 8, 2022 general election required [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] to submit its official canvass to the [[arizona-secretary-of-state|Secretary of State]] on or before November 28, 2022.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Subsection (C) of the same statute provides that if returns from any polling place are found to be missing at the time of the governing body's meeting, the canvass shall be postponed from day to day until all returns are received or until six postponements have occurred.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] invoked that deadline in her November 19, 2022 letter to the [[maricopa-county-attorneys-office|Maricopa County Attorney's Office]], writing that because "the canvass is looming" and the outstanding issues bore on the county's ability to lawfully certify results, her unit required a response before the canvass was submitted.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)
