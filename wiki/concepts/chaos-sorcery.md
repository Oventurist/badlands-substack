# Chaos Sorcery

Chaos sorcery is the term used by former satanist priest [[mark-passio|Mark Passio]] for the intentional destabilization of aspects of society — from the economy to religious institutions, from the family unit to the fields of science and politics — as a deliberate instrument of control.[1]

## Definition and scope

In Passio's framing, recounted in the Badlands Media essay "False Flags and the Occult," the [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] is only one of many cultural-warfare weapons developed by what he calls the dark occult groups of the world, a category that includes but is not limited to the so-called Illuminati, globalists, and the many groups working together in some fashion to effect a mutually agreed agenda for total control of the human population.[1]

## Relationship to finance

The article extends the logic to banking, arguing that modern banking relies on instability to maintain dependence on the banking industry and its subsidiary industries of insurance, healthcare, law enforcement, defense manufacturing, human trafficking and the drug trade.[1] The healthier a community and the more law and order it enjoys, the less it must hedge against risk using banker credit; conversely, the more instability, the more money is needed to manage risk — and the international banking cartel is happy to offer solutions to problems it helped create through its partnership with organized crime.[1] The first fractional reserve bank was launched within a decade of the [[dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]], itself associated in the article with the drug trade, weapons trading, early analogues of color revolutions, slavery and high finance.[1]

The money generated by such schemes is presented as a means rather than an end; the real end is argued to be control of the human population for an undisclosed purpose, with money and banking serving as one of the best tools for covert control.[1] The article cites the slogan "You'll own nothing and be happy" as one publicly visible tip of that iceberg.[1]
