# June 2026 Trump–Netanyahu Lebanon Call

The June 2026 Trump–Netanyahu Lebanon call refers to a reported June 1, 2026 telephone exchange in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] delivered an expletive-filled rebuke to Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over escalation in [[lebanon|Lebanon]], and intervened to halt a planned Israeli strike on Beirut.[1]

## The reported exchange

According to [[axios|Axios]], Trump accused Netanyahu of jeopardizing US diplomatic efforts and escalating the conflict in Lebanon, calling him "crazy" and demanding to know "What the f*** are you doing?"[1] Trump reportedly blamed [[israel|Israel]]'s actions for increasing international hostility toward both Netanyahu personally and Israel as a state.[1] Quotations circulated by Badlands included: "You're fucking crazy. You'd be in prison if it weren't for me. I'm saving your ass. Everybody hates you now. Everybody hates Israel because of this."[1]

The confrontation followed [[iran|Iran]]'s threat to abandon negotiations with Washington over Israeli military actions in Lebanon.[1] Axios reported that Trump intervened directly to stop a planned Israeli strike on Beirut, arguing that further escalation would undermine regional diplomacy and isolate Israel internationally.[1] Israeli officials subsequently confirmed that plans for a Beirut operation had been shelved, though Netanyahu publicly maintained that Israel would continue responding to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] attacks and keep military operations underway in southern Lebanon.[1]

Trump later stated on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that he had held a "very productive" call with Netanyahu, that Israeli troops would not enter Beirut, that any forces already en route had been turned back, and that Hezbollah had agreed through intermediaries to halt all shooting as part of a mutual de-escalation arrangement.[1]

## Sourcing and credibility debate

Badlands treated the sourcing question as central. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that dismissing the story as "just Axios" ignored the unique relationship between Trump and correspondent [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]], who along with Mark Caputo routinely conducts direct phone interviews in which Trump reveals exclusive information.[1] He noted that Ravid remained the only journalist to whom Trump had publicly trashed Netanyahu, which he did during a December 2021 call whose audio was recorded and published in Israel but went unreported in American mainstream media — an omission the writer attributed to Zionist control of the press.[1]

During that 2021 call, per the same account, Trump heavily implied that Netanyahu played a role in the 2020 stolen election, with subtext regarding the prerecorded video Netanyahu released congratulating Biden "before the ink was even dry," suggesting premeditation and participation in a conspiracy against Trump.[1]

The described dynamic is transactional: Trump grants exclusive access and scoops, and Ravid accurately relays the information; violating that arrangement would cost Ravid his access.[1] Whether the information Trump conveys is itself accurate is a separate question, and the writer judged it highly likely that Trump has used Ravid to broadcast mis- or disinformation at times.[1]

## Reaction and significance

Reactions from what Badlands termed "Mossad Media" were read as evidence the call was real: [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] called for an FBI investigation to catch the leaker, and [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] denounced disloyalty within Trump's camp.[1] The argument advanced was that if the call had not occurred, those figures would know and would simply dismiss it as fake news; their demand for "heads on pikes" instead suggested fear of an existing audio recording.[1]

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry concluded that whether the anecdote was true was "largely inconsequential" because it had already entered the zeitgeist, and that if Trump fabricated it, Netanyahu and his allies faced an impossible choice between calling the president a liar — thereby isolating themselves and ending American support for the war effort — and absorbing the humiliation.[1] Either way, the episode advanced the [[overton-window|Overton Window]] toward an open Trump–Netanyahu rupture.[1]
