# Emerald Tablet

The Emerald Tablet is the most famous work attributed to [[hermes-trismegistus|Hermes Trismegistus]] and the source of perhaps the best-known Hermetic dictum, "As above, so below."[1] It was seen as the key to astrology, [[alchemy|alchemy]] and other occulted sciences, and has a history as mysterious as its purported author.[1] It was described for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] in his February 2023 survey of [[hermeticism|Hermeticism]].[1]

## Transmission

The Emerald Tablet was highly regarded by Islamic and European alchemists as the foundation of their art.[1] Though attributed to Hermes, the text first appears in a number of early medieval Arabic sources, the oldest dating to the late eighth or early ninth century, and it was translated into Latin several times in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, after which numerous interpretations and commentaries followed.[1]

## Content and significance

History's greatest alchemists believed the artifact harbored the secret of the *prima materia* and the [[philosophers-stone|Philosopher's Stone]], a substance said to transmute base metals such as lead into gold.[1] Its fourteen stanzas are, in DeLarme's account, the very foundation of European alchemy.[1] Unlike most alchemical writings the Tablet was not a large tome of instructive texts but a small batch of concise texts carved into a single emerald slab; although the whole can be read in minutes, many scholars devoted their lives to puzzling over its meaning, and [[isaac-newton|Sir Isaac Newton]] dedicated the later portions of his life to studying it.[1]

The Tablet also underpins one account of Hermes' epithet "Trismegistus": it states that he knows the three parts of the wisdom of the whole universe, namely alchemy, astrology and theurgy.[1] DeLarme directed readers to the Internet Archive to peruse the various translations of the text.[1]
