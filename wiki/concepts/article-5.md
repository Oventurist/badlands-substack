# Article 5 (North Atlantic Treaty)

Article 5 is the collective defence clause of the North Atlantic Treaty and the mechanism by which, in the argument of "A Bright Red Line," Ukrainian accession to [[nato|NATO]] would automatically draw the United States into direct war with Russia.[1]

## Text and effect

The clause provides that an armed attack against one or more members in Europe or North America shall be considered an attack against them all, and that each member, exercising the right of individual or collective self-defence recognized by Article 51 of the United Nations Charter, will assist the attacked party by taking forthwith such action as it deems necessary, including the use of armed force, to restore and maintain the security of the North Atlantic area.[1] Any such attack and all resulting measures must immediately be reported to the UN Security Council, and are to be terminated once the Council has acted to restore international peace and security.[1]

## Precedent

Article 5 was invoked after the attacks of 11 September 2001, after which member nations were obliged to follow the United States into Afghanistan and to offer assistance for Iraq.[1] The article cites this precedent to argue that it "shouldn't take much of an imagination" to foresee that Ukrainian membership would produce a hot war with Russia in Eastern Europe, potentially with U.S. troops on the front lines.[1]
