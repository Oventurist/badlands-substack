# Kiev Power Crisis (2026)

The **Kiev power crisis of 2026** refers to large-scale electricity outages in the Ukrainian capital that Mayor [[vitali-klitschko|Vitali Klitschko]] described as leaving the city "teetering on the brink of catastrophe" in an interview with the *Financial Times* published in mid-February 2026, as reported by RT.[1]

## Klitschko's warning

Klitschko went beyond the immediate outages, saying that "the question of the future of our country – whether we will survive as an independent country or not is still open."[1] He accused President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] of infringing on municipal authority by appointing military-civilian administrations in the capital.[1] RT reported that Zelensky had claimed the previous month that Kiev was lagging behind other Ukrainian cities in its response to power outages; Klitschko dismissed the criticism, arguing that electricity generation falls within the central government's purview rather than the city's.[1]

## Cause and context

According to the report, Russia had in recent weeks intensified drone and missile strikes targeting Ukraine's power grid, with Moscow stating that the strikes are aimed at undercutting weapons production and are retaliation for Ukrainian attacks on Russian civilian infrastructure.[1] Severe cold weather compounded the situation.[1]

## Related corruption scandal

The energy crisis unfolded against the backdrop of a $100 million corruption scheme uncovered in November 2025 by Ukraine's [[national-anti-corruption-bureau-of-ukraine|National Anti-Corruption Bureau]] and the Specialized Anti-Corruption Prosecutor's Office, involving the country's state-owned nuclear energy operator.[1] The scandal led to the resignations of Justice Minister [[german-galushchenko|German Galushchenko]], Energy Minister [[svetlana-grinchuk|Svetlana Grinchuk]] and Zelensky's chief of staff [[andriy-yermak|Andriy Yermak]].[1] Ukrainian media reported in February 2026 that Galushchenko had been detained while attempting to flee to Poland.[1]
