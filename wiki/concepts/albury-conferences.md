# Albury Conferences

The Albury Conferences were a series of conferences on biblical prophecy held between 1826 and 1830 in Surrey, England, and overseen by the financier [[henry-drummond]] at a 70-bedroom estate he purchased for the purpose.[1]

## Participants

The gatherings included leading figures of what the article terms London's gnostic intelligentsia, among them the occultists Robert Haldane and Sir Thomas Carlyle, both of whom became numbered among the twelve "apostles/prophets" of the New Apostolic Catholic Church founded by Drummond and George Irving in 1830.[1] Lord Anthony Ashley Cooper, 7th Earl of Shaftesbury, devoted himself to "Premillennial Dispensationalism" soon after the conferences, and [[john-nelson-darby]] belonged to the same leadership network.[1]

## Jesuit source material

The conferences were sparked by the rediscovery of the writings of the Jesuit [[francisco-ribera]] (1537–1591) of Salamanca, who played a major role in the Council of Trent of 1545 — the council the article says "ensured never-ending wars between Catholics and Protestants" and is sometimes called the counter-reformation.[1] Ribera's texts had reached the English-speaking world through S.R. Maitland, Keeper of Manuscripts for the Archbishop of Canterbury, who found them in the Vatican archives and wrote a series of books on the antichrist and End Times between 1826 and 1830.[1] Through Albury, the article argues, Jesuit futurism became the seedbed of [[dispensationalism]] and [[christian-zionism]].[1]
