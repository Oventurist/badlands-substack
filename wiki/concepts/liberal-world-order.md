# Liberal World Order

The "liberal world order" is the term used by American officials for the US-led system of international institutions, norms, and alliances that has governed global affairs since the mid-twentieth century. In 2023 it became the explicit frame through which Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] described the strategic competition with China.[1]

## Blinken's 2023 formulation

Interviewed by The Atlantic's Jeffrey Goldberg at the Atlantic Festival, Blinken was asked whether Russia or China posed the bigger threat to the United States.[1] He answered that China has a "much greater ability certainly than Russia to try to shape what the international system looks like."[1] Pressed on Beijing's aims, he said, "I think they want a world order, but the world order that they seek is profoundly illiberal in nature; ours is liberal with a small 'L.' And that's the fundamental difference," adding that China "seeks to be the dominant power in the world militarily, economically, diplomatically."[1]

The statement drew attention because Washington had been directing billions of dollars into the Ukraine conflict in the name of defeating and weakening Russia, even as its chief diplomat identified a different state as the primary challenger.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the establishment's turn against "Communist China," after decades of accommodation running from Clinton through [[barack-obama|Obama]], only appears incongruent until it is read through the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] thesis and the emergence of a multipolar order led by China and Russia.[1] Invoking [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]]'s observation that every nation has an "evil twin" — an infiltrated parallel structure controlled to varying degrees by the same subversive network — Burning Bright suggested the powers behind the liberal order had not just discovered that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] worked against their interests, but had overestimated their control inside China much as they were now overestimating it in the West.[1] He concluded that "the turn on 'communism' comes far too late in the game" and that "the mask came off when they forced everyone else to put one on."[1]

## DeLarme's definition

In "The Deep Politics of the '12-Day War'," Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] uses "Liberal World Order" interchangeably with the "International Rules-Based Order" to describe the supranational system of 21st-century deep politics — an international [[deep-state|deep political milieu]] that has long since usurped the United States government from the people and spread its tendrils throughout the world.[2] He characterises it as an amalgam of "open societies" — countries that have opened themselves to [[usaid|USAID]] and the swarm of democracy-promoting NGOs — working in concert, typically more to the benefit of corporations, bankers and international billionaires than of the citizens of each respective nation: "essentially a monolithic transnational kleptocratic system, despite all of the whitewash and disarming window dressing."[2]

Within the one great system, DeLarme writes, sit many subsystems, the relevant one being the [[foreign-policy-establishment|foreign policy establishment]], whose military alliance is [[nato|NATO]].[2] He cites the [[1973-oil-embargo|1973 oil embargo]] as an episode with profound effects on the order, and dates its fracture over [[israel|Israel]] to the 2015 [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]], after which "certain powerful elements of the donor class were no longer aligned with the Liberal World Order" — conditions some believe paved the way for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to emerge.[2]
