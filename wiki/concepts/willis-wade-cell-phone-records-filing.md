# Willis–Wade Cell Phone Records Filing (2024)

In February 2024, attorneys for Donald Trump filed a supplemental brief in the Georgia RICO election case containing cell phone records and geolocation analysis that appeared to contradict the sworn account of Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] and Special Prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] regarding the timing of their romantic relationship.[1] The filing was reported by the legal writer [[techno-fog|TechnoFog]] and covered in the Badlands News Brief.[1]

## The official account

Willis and Wade had maintained, through sworn filings and sworn testimony, that their "personal relationship" began in 2022.[1] In a February 2, 2022 filing, Willis submitted an affidavit from Wade stating: "In 2022, District Attorney Willis and I developed a personal relationship."[1] Both testified that the relationship started sometime in early 2022 — a chronology that mattered because Wade had been appointed Special Prosecutor by Willis on November 1, 2021.[1]

## The records

Trump's attorneys obtained, by subpoena to [[at-and-t|AT&T]], Wade's cell phone records from January 1, 2021 through November 30, 2021.[1] An investigator hired by the attorneys analyzed Wade's location data using an analytical tool that generated geolocation data placing Wade at Willis's South Fulton condominium during that period.[1] The records were said to indicate that the relationship was romantic well before Wade's November 2021 appointment, contradicting the official story.[1]

## Reaction and procedural posture

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the week ahead should be a significant one for "our favorite, flush prosecutor," and that if the process were just, the cell data filed on Friday "would be the final nail in the coffin of Fani's foray into complex RICO litigation."[1] Before the data was filed, mainstream legal analysts had been posturing that Willis would survive the disqualification effort; by Sunday evening that certainty had been replaced by cautious "here's what we know" articles from the [[new-york-times|New York Times]], Newsweek and others.[1]

Outstanding at the time of the brief was whether Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] would allow [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] to return to the stand and what he would be permitted to answer, the judge having previously questioned whether Bradley had been misinterpreting attorney-client privilege throughout.[1] Summations were expected to follow the resolution of Bradley's testimony, with a ruling from the judge at some later point.[1] Badlands noted the ruling could ripple across the wider case, given pending questions in [[harrison-floyd|Harrison Floyd]]'s evidentiary hearing and allegations of misconduct yet to be heard, and anticipated proceedings resuming in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] as early as the following Tuesday.[1]
