# Disbarment of Rudy Giuliani in Washington, D.C.

On September 26, 2024, a three-judge panel of the District of Columbia Court of Appeals ordered the disbarment of [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], the former adviser to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], following a disciplinary board's criticisms of his response to the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## The ruling

The brief order noted, among other things, Giuliani's prior disbarment in the state of New York, where an appeals court had similarly accused him of making "demonstrably false and misleading statements to the courts, lawmakers, and the public at large."[1] The case came before Judges Eric T. Washington, Roy McLeese, and Joshua Deahl.[1]

In June 2023 the District of Columbia Bar's Board on Professional Responsibility had issued a report stating that Giuliani "claimed massive election fraud but had no evidence of it."[1] The board added that while it had considered Giuliani's "conduct following the September 11 attacks as well as his prior service in the Justice Department and as Mayor of New York City," it concluded that "all of that happened long ago."[1] His conduct during the 2020 presidential election, the board said, "transcends all his past accomplishments," and "was unparalleled in its destructive purpose and effect."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Ashe in America argued that Giuliani was being disbarred in Washington even as evidence supporting his claims was surfacing, pointing to [[colorado|Colorado]], where establishment Republicans were, in her telling, pretending to be horrified to learn that voting machines connect to the internet and are enabled for remote access.[1] She noted that she had presented the same technical material to Colorado legislators on April 24, 2021, alongside Colonel Shawn Smith (USAF-retired) and cyber expert Dennis Haugh, in a presentation that included photographs, excerpts from vendor manuals, and cyber threat assessment details.[1]

She described the aftermath for the activists involved: local news outlets winning awards for what she characterized as lying about election activists, activists placed on lists and called terrorists, lost jobs, dozens of threats of personal harm, and two and a half years consumed by what she called frivolous lawfare before a definitive win by directed verdict.[1] Establishment Republicans, she wrote, ignored all of it and stayed silent during the defamation, and had said nothing about that victory — yet a month before the 2024 election were presenting remote access to voting machines as a "breaking" development.[1] She concluded that the episode looked like a divisive narrative deployment, particularly given that Giuliani had just lost his law license for saying the same thing when contesting it could have mattered, while remaining glad that new people were discussing election vulnerabilities and highly skeptical of their motives.[1]
