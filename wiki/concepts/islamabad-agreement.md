# Islamabad Agreement

The **Islamabad agreement** is the name reported for a proposed memorandum of understanding between the United States and [[iran|Iran]], tentatively reached in June 2026 after mediation by [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[pakistan|Pakistan]].[1]

## Background

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appeared to pull back from another round of threatened military escalation against Iran after signalling that negotiations with Tehran might be nearing a breakthrough.[1] Days after warning that the US would "bomb the shit out of them" if Iran failed to reach an agreement, Trump said a major peace deal could be signed soon and indicated that planned strikes had been suspended while talks continued.[1]

The diplomatic shift came despite continued clashes in and around the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], where US forces had carried out strikes on Iranian targets and enforced a naval blockade on Iranian shipping while Iran launched retaliatory missile and drone attacks and threatened broader disruption of regional energy flows.[1] Both sides accused the other of repeatedly violating ceasefire arrangements, and Iran publicly stated that no final agreement had been approved and that key issues, including sanctions relief, remained unresolved.[1] Iranian officials warned that any renewed US attack would trigger an even harsher response than previous exchanges.[1]

## Reported terms

Axios reported on June 12 that the draft agreement would reopen the Strait of Hormuz without tolls and provide sanctions relief tied to Iranian compliance.[1] It would extend a ceasefire for 60 days, including in Lebanon, while the parties negotiate over Iran's nuclear program.[1] Under the framework, Iran would commit not to acquire a nuclear weapon and would work toward resolving disputes over its enriched uranium stockpile; one option under discussion would down-blend highly enriched uranium inside Iran under United Nations supervision, though any nuclear-program measures would require a subsequent and more detailed agreement.[1]

Negotiators reached the tentative deal on a Wednesday night following talks involving Qatari mediator [[ali-al-thawadi|Ali Al-Thawadi]] and Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]], according to diplomats and US officials cited by Axios.[1] Trump said he expected a signing ceremony over the weekend, while an Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson said Tehran had not yet made a final decision.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that anything could still happen, including the deal being blown up by bad-faith actors who had infiltrated the process.[1] He noted that four Air Force C-17s had departed [[israel|Israel]] for Europe in anticipation of a signing ceremony in Geneva reportedly to be attended by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], and said that Vance attending rather than Trump — and Europe hosting — left him worried the accord "isn't the final deal, and may fall apart."[1]

## Collapse of the Islamabad talks (April 2026)

A fragile ceasefire between the United States and [[iran|Iran]] unraveled after negotiations in Islamabad failed, raising the risk of renewed military escalation and disruption to global energy markets.[2] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had announced on April 7, 2026 a two-week suspension of U.S. bombing operations contingent on Tehran reopening the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], under a provisional ceasefire mediated by [[pakistan|Pakistan]] and joined by [[israel|Israel]].[2] Talks aimed at extending the truce collapsed days later over disputes centered on Iran's nuclear program.[2]

In response Trump ordered a naval blockade of Iranian ports and vessels while permitting non-Iranian shipping through the strait, and warned of strikes on Iranian infrastructure; the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] warned that any military vessel approaching the strait would be treated as a ceasefire violation.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the official narrative of failure may be inaccurate, citing a Reuters-sourced report that the United States had agreed to unfreeze $100 billion in Iranian assets held in [[qatar|Qatar]] as a show of good faith — funds tied to sanctions Trump imposed in 2018 upon withdrawing from the nuclear deal — which U.S. officials denied.[2] He also noted reporting that the [[cia|CIA]] had used [[pegasus|Pegasus]] software from [[nso-group|NSO Group]] in a deception operation against IRGC officials during the rescue of a downed U.S. airman, and asked whether the war itself might be a deception operation.[2]
