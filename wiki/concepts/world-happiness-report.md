# World Happiness Report

The World Happiness Report is an annual ranking of national wellbeing produced through a partnership involving [[gallup|Gallup]], the Oxford Wellbeing Research Centre, the [[united-nations|UN]] Sustainable Development Solutions Network, and the report's own editorial board.[1]

## 2024 edition

Findings released on Wednesday, March 20, 2024 showed that the United States had fallen out of the world's twenty happiest countries for the first time ever, sliding from 15th place the previous year to 23rd.[1] The report found happiness decreasing across all American age groups, with a particularly significant decline among young people, who had become the least happy age cohort in the country.[1]

The report stated: "This is a big change from 2006-10, when the young were happier than those in the midlife groups, and about as happy as those aged 60 and over. For the young, the happiness drop was about three-quarters of a point, and greater for females than males."[1] Among Americans below the age of 30 in the 2021–2023 period, the United States ranked 62nd in happiness; among those aged 60 and above, it ranked 10th.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued it was "odd" that the UN would publicize the collapse in young women's happiness "considering their agenda is what made women so unhappy," attributing the decline to generations of conditioning that traditional gender roles are oppressive and that women would be happier competing with rather than complementing men.[1] She identified [[sustainable-development-goals|UN Sustainable Development Goal #5 — Gender Equality]] as "deceptive and destructive" by design, and said the 62nd-place ranking among young people made the effect "super obvious."[1]
