# Health Tech Ecosystem Initiative

The Health Tech Ecosystem Initiative is a joint [[white-house|White House]] and [[centers-for-medicare-and-medicaid-services|Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services]] program announced in late July 2025 to expand the use of digital health tools and interoperable electronic medical records, with a stated focus on consumers.[1]

## Announcement

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] presented the initiative at the White House on a Wednesday afternoon, seated with Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], CMS Administrator [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]], CMS advisor and DOGE administrator [[amy-gleason|Amy Gleason]], and AI and Crypto Czar [[david-sacks|David Sacks]].[1] Trump said the key breakthrough was persuading many of the largest names in health care and technology to agree to common standards for electronic medical records, allowing patients to transmit information between doctors across different networks and record-keeping systems.[1]

Trump said providers would "finally be able to kill the clipboard," replacing repetitive intake paperwork with a system in which patients grant access to their records "at the push of a button," after which all information the physician needs is immediately transmitted.[1] Officials also stated that the system would be "entirely opt in" and that there would be "no centralized government run database."[1]

## Criticism

Badlands commentary framed the initiative as the realization of goals the health technology industry pursued during the COVID-19 pandemic.[1] Contributor Chris Paul described the presentations as dystopian, arguing that the language of patient empowerment and reduced "friction" is a familiar framing for advancing surveillance and control, and questioned who besides the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] and big technology firms had asked for the system.[1] He also questioned the durability of the opt-in guarantee, drawing an analogy to the nominally voluntary health insurance system and asking whether providers would be able to opt out if patients refused.[1]
