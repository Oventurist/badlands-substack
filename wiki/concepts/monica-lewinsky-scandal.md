# Monica Lewinsky Scandal

The Monica Lewinsky scandal is the sexual relationship between President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and White House intern [[monica-lewinsky|Monica Lewinsky]], which [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treats in his October 10, 2024 Badlands Media essay "Three Possible Election Outcomes" as the defining event of Clinton's second term and as a deliberate instrument of national moral decay.[1]

## Carlson's chronology

Carlson dates the affair as beginning in 1995 and lasting until 1997, with public revelation following in 1998.[1] He describes the relationship as one between a 22-year-old intern and a 49-year-old sitting president, and argues that the age gap and the setting were central to its cultural effect.[1]

## The "moral decay" thesis

In Carlson's reading, the scandal "ushered in a moral decay in America" by creating a broad public acceptance of immoral behaviour at the highest level of government.[1] He contends that the majority of Americans were ultimately comfortable with a president conducting an affair in the [[white-house|White House]], and that conduct which would once have forced a president from office in shame instead became routine material for late-night talk show monologues.[1] He proposes a partisan asymmetry test: had [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] conducted an affair with an intern less than half his age while in office, he argues, the same commentators who minimised the Clinton affair "would suddenly turn Puritan before our eyes."[1]

Carlson further argues that whatever moral outrage did exist during Clinton's second term was pacified by the concurrent American technology boom, summarising the mechanism as the observation that "everyone is more forgiving when their 401k is going up."[1]

## Alleged Epstein connection

Carlson raises — as a question rather than an assertion — the possibility that the affair functioned as a compromise operation. He notes that the affair was publicly revealed in 1998 and that [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] purchased his island in the same year, and asks whether the Lewinsky affair was "a giant Epstein honeypot to gain control of Bill Clinton."[1]

## Placement in the Uniparty pattern

The scandal appears in Carlson's wider argument that the second terms of [[deep-state|Deep State]] presidents carry the bulk of the damage they inflict, because a first term too destructive would prevent re-election even with election tampering.[1] Within his [[uniparty|Uniparty]] framework, the Clinton episode exemplifies the claim that moral and cultural decay occurs when Democrats occupy the White House, while wars are initiated under Republican presidents.[1]
