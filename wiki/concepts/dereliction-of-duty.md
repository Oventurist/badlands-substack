# Dereliction of Duty

"Dereliction of duty" is the phrase the article borrows from The Hill's commentary on the [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee]] report to characterize the conduct of senior officials during the [[benghazi-attack|Benghazi attack]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The Hill contrasted the heroism of GRS personnel, Diplomatic Security Agents and Team Tripoli with "the total incompetence and dereliction of duty of so-called 'leaders' in D.C. who chose to talk and spin while actually doing nothing," and criticized after-the-fact excuses for why no attempt was made to rescue more than thirty Americans facing multiple assaults.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Recurring administration justifications centered on the "tyranny of time and distance."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article rejects incompetence as an explanation, arguing instead that a deliberate choice was made not to attempt a rescue of the 37 Americans under attack.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Its test for dereliction is the command sequence: if the Commander in Chief and the Secretary of Defense both issued deployment orders, the failure to carry them out is by definition a dereliction, and the absence of any punishment demands explanation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article's answer is that Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] obstructed the order and faced no consequence because President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] shared the objective.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)
