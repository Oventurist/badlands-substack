# Bank Secrecy Act

The Bank Secrecy Act is the principal U.S. anti-money-laundering statute, imposing reporting and recordkeeping obligations on financial institutions and forming the legal backbone of enforcement work by the [[financial-crimes-enforcement-network|Financial Crimes Enforcement Network]].[1]

## Role in the 2026 whistleblower portal

When the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]] launched a confidential whistleblower tip website on February 13, 2026, Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said the portal would allow individuals to report suspected misconduct involving violations of the Bank Secrecy Act, U.S. sanctions programs and other financial laws.[1] Reports leading to successful enforcement actions may qualify the tipster for monetary awards funded through the enforcement process.[1]

## Bank enforcement wave of 2024

The Bank Secrecy Act framework drove a major enforcement wave in 2024. In October of that year [[toronto-dominion-bank|Toronto-Dominion Bank]] pleaded guilty to Bank Secrecy Act and money laundering conspiracy violations, paying roughly $3 billion in fines and penalties to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and financial regulators for failing to monitor money laundering by fentanyl traffickers and other criminals.[2]

Weeks later, [[bank-of-america|Bank of America]] disclosed in a filing that it was in talks with several federal regulators over "certain aspects of the Corporation's Bank Secrecy Act/anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance programs," including transaction monitoring, training, governance, and customer due diligence, and warned that resolution "may include one or more public orders by the regulators."[2] Former [[state-department|State Department]] investigator [[david-asher|David Asher]], speaking alongside journalist [[sam-cooper|Sam Cooper]], had predicted in September 2024 that the TD case would be followed by action against one of the big four US banks.[2]
