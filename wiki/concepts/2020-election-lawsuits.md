# 2020 Election Lawsuits

The 2020 election lawsuits are the body of litigation filed before, during, and after the 2020 United States presidential election challenging state election procedures, ballot handling, voter rolls, signature verification, and electronic voting equipment.[1] The popular claim that Donald Trump "lost all his election cases" derives from a subset of these suits; a case-by-case review published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], drawing on research by [[rachel-alexander|Rachel Alexander]], the [[election-integrity-info|ElectionIntegrity.Info]] report, and the [[healthy-elections-project|Healthy Elections Project]] tracker, characterized the claim as a fabrication.[1]

## Statistical record

By that tabulation there were 92 lawsuits dealing specifically with the 2020 election. Thirty were decided on the merits and 62 were disposed of because of procedural or other defects.[1] In the 30 merits decisions, Trump and other plaintiffs on the side of election integrity prevailed in 22 — a 73 percent win rate whenever evidence was actually heard.[1]

## Cases decided against the challengers

In October 2020 the Trump campaign challenged a ban on poll observers at early voting locations, asking a court to allow observers where votes were being counted; the court found that the Philadelphia Board of Elections' "satellite offices" were not polling places under the Election Code even if votes were counted there, and denied the petition.[1] The day after the election, the Georgia Republican Party and Donald J. Trump for President, Inc. asked a court to order the Chatham County Board of Elections to sequester and account for absentee ballots received after 7:00 p.m. on election day; the petition was denied on statutory grounds, the court holding the ballots valid and finding no evidence of a violation.[1]

On November 5, 2020 the campaign challenged nearly 600 ballots in Montgomery County, Pennsylvania as ineligible for lack of an address, but the court held the Election Code does not require a voter to provide an address on the declaration envelope.[1] A similar dispute over roughly 2,200 ballots reached the [[pennsylvania-supreme-court|Pennsylvania Supreme Court]], which held that voters should not be disenfranchised based on advisory portions of the Election Code not mandated by statute.[1] Badlands argued this reasoning hollowed out the signature-verification and other statutory controls that had been used to sell [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]] to the public as secure.[1]

In Harris County, Texas, a court found plaintiffs lacked standing to claim harm from 127,000 allegedly illegal ballots — a ruling the article paired with the later restriction on Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]]'s authority to prosecute election cases.[1]

## Cases decided for the challengers

**Changing election laws.** In May 2020 pro-business plaintiffs successfully challenged Colorado Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]]'s exceptions to petition requirements, the state's high court holding the governor was not authorized to create such an exception.[1] In August 2020 the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] and the Trump campaign obtained an injunction against the Iowa Secretary of State's mailing of unrequested, pre-populated absentee ballot request forms, with counties ordered to send blank forms; the court invoked the rule that lower federal courts should ordinarily not alter election rules on the eve of an election.[1] The RNC prevailed on two further similar Iowa challenges.[1] In October 2020 the campaign won an injunction against the Minnesota Secretary of State's extension of the ballot-receipt deadline and secured sequestration of late ballots, the court finding the extension likely violated Article II, Section 1 of the Constitution absent legislative authorization.[1] Trump also won cases on extending polling hours in Nevada and on the unlawful relaxing of election rules in Pennsylvania.[1]

**Election misinformation.** Petitioners Mark Jefferson and the Republican Party of Wisconsin secured a temporary restraining order against a clerk encouraging voters to designate themselves "indefinitely confined" to avoid proof of identification when requesting an absentee ballot; the clerk was ordered to remove and stop posting "legally incorrect" information.[1]

**Voter roll clean-up.** [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] challenged the voter-roll hygiene of Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]], who entered a consent decree providing the group with annual data updates for five years.[1] Comparable agreements were secured against Michigan Secretary of State [[jocelyn-benson|Jocelyn Benson]] and Pennsylvania Secretary [[kathy-boockvar|Kathy Boockvar]].[1] The Pennsylvania Republican Party also won a decision on sequestration of absentee ballots received after 8:00 p.m., later reflected in Supreme Court precedent, and America First plaintiffs prevailed in similar challenges in Minnesota and Virginia.[1]

**Signature verification.** In Nevada the state Republican Central Committee obtained a largely granted order requiring Clark County to produce voter signatures for the general election along with signature images on file, the names, parties, and shifts of counting-board members, and documents on poll-watching, ballot-processing, and security policies and training.[1] In Michigan, plaintiffs challenging [[signature-verification|signature-verification]] standards and seeking an audit prevailed when the court held the standards had been issued in violation of the Administrative Procedures Act.[1]

**Electronic voting equipment.** Michigan plaintiffs were awarded a partial audit after alleging that machines in Antrim County flipped votes, a claim supported in the article by the Allied Special Operations Group forensic report on Antrim County's 2020 election.[1] [[voterga|VoterGA]] won a court order permitting inspection of electronic ballot images in Gwinnett County, Georgia.[1] In the litigation preceding the [[maricopa-county-forensic-audit|2021 Maricopa County audit]] ordered by the [[arizona-state-senate|Arizona Senate]], [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] sued state senators over legislative subpoenas for 2020 election documents and logfiles; the court affirmed the subpoenas were legal and ordered compliance, though the county reportedly did not comply with many of them without consequence.[1]

## Procedural dismissals

The 62 cases never reaching the merits were disposed of on a range of grounds: lack of standing ("merely a generalized grievance and no imminent injury or causation"), lack of jurisdiction, untimeliness, [[laches|laches]] where challenges were brought too late for voters already relying on the process, and even public-health considerations during COVID-19, one court holding that such considerations weighed strongly against an injunction "even if Plaintiffs' claims were meritorious."[1] Attorney [[robert-barnes|Robert Barnes]] summarized the pattern as "Not Ripe in Spring, No Standing by Summer, Laches by Fall, and Moot by Winter."[1]

## Significance

Badlands Media presented the litigation record as evidence that assertions of "no evidence of election fraud" and that 2020 was "the safest and most secure election in history" were narrative rather than fact, and framed the piece as the first in a series debunking election myths.[1]
