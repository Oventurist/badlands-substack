# 2024 EV Emissions Standards Proposal

The 2024 EV emissions standards proposal was a policy advanced by the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in March 2024 that would require 70 percent of all vehicle sales by 2032 to be electric.[1]

## The proposal

The proposed emission standards were announced on Friday, March 22, 2024.[1] They arrived as [[ford-motor-company|Ford]] and other carmakers were cutting back on electric-vehicle programs that had failed to meet expectations, either ending them or significantly scaling them down to save costs.[1]

## Political fallout in Michigan

Revolver News framed the move as a "Motor City Bloodbath," arguing that Biden's campaign had stalled not only in Detroit but across Michigan, with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] holding a commanding lead in the state and the green agenda "dead in the water."[1]

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that Trump was already pulling ahead in nearly all Michigan polls and had seemingly received an unintended boost from the Biden administration.[1] He argued that an electric car future is not feasible at present, citing batteries that cost as much as a used car and tire wear at five times the rate of a gas-driven car, and said that everyone recognizes this except "the screechy Climate-Karens on the left."[1] Noting that Trump led Michigan polls by eight points before the announcement, Fleuret predicted a jump to a 12–15 point lead and concluded: "One could say 'It's gonna be a bloodbath.'"[1]

The framing echoed the contemporaneous [[bloodbath-hoax|bloodbath]] controversy over Trump's remarks about the auto industry.[1]
