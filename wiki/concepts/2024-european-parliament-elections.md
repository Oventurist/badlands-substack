# 2024 European Parliament Elections

The 2024 elections to the European Parliament were held over four days, June 6 to June 9, 2024, across the European continent.[1] Final projections indicated more continuity in the new parliamentary formation than had been expected, but the results produced what Brookings called a political earthquake in France.[1]

## French results

In France the elections delivered a large victory for [[jordan-bardella|Jordan Bardella]] and [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]] — respectively president and parliamentary group leader of the [[national-rally|National Rally]] (RN) — who took roughly 31.4% of the vote share.[1] Adding the 5.5% won by the splinter party Reconquête, the far right accounted for nearly 40% of the French vote, its highest share to date.[1] By contrast, President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s Renaissance party took only 14.6%, despite a platform built around an existential battle between pro- and anti-European forces — a strategy that had worked in the 2019 European elections and the 2022 French presidential election but failed in 2024.[1] Analysts read the results, consistent with pre-election polling, as evidence that support for Le Pen and her protégé Bardella is no longer merely a protest vote and that the far right is now the premier political force in France.[1] In the first European elections of 1979 the RN — then the Front National — had won 1.31%.[1]

The Brookings analysis warned of a high risk that the RN would win the snap elections called in response, which would give France a far-right prime minister for the first time since the end of the Second World War.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the results as proof that MAGA is a global movement, describing the idea that each nation should preserve and protect its people, traditions, culture, and history as the defining cry of the political moment worldwide.[1] She observed that French establishment politicians of both left and right were being roasted on French social media, and referred to French legacy outlets as "oiseaux moqueurs" — French Mockingbirds — who were "wigging."[1] She highlighted Brookings' own observation that the RN "has completely appropriated much of the vocabulary traditionally reserved to the left," a move the think tank compared to the MAGA wing of the US Republican Party, and concluded that deconstructing establishment narratives and forcing a principled stance on rights is working, signing off "Nous accélérons."[1] The same commentary linked the European results to the broader claim that populism is rising because globalists overplayed their hand and abused their power.[1]

## Bloc-wide results

Voters across the European Union's 27 member states mostly backed centrists, but handed important gains to far-right parties, steeling them as a disruptive force and unsettling the bloc's mainstream establishment.[2] Partial results made public late on Sunday, June 9, 2024, showed centrist political groups poised to take some losses while retaining a clear majority of more than 400 seats in the 720-seat assembly, with the conservatives marking a decisive victory.[2] Parliamentary groups holding a nationalist, anti-immigrant agenda were projected to control roughly 130 seats, a better showing than in the 2019 election.[2] Reporting on the balloting concluded that the prevailing winds had grown chill for parts of Europe's political establishment and that the momentum of far-right forces had yet to crest.[2]

Badlands commentary noted that [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] contests normally draw low turnout and enthusiasm because the EU is not itself a sovereign actor, making a parliamentary majority useful mainly for EU policy and intra-member trade questions; the 2024 round broke that pattern with record turnout.[2] [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] of France and [[olaf-scholz|Olaf Scholz]] of Germany were described as the leaders most clearly rattled, both rejected by double-digit deficits.[2] Because the elections do not directly change national leadership, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated them instead as a bellwether showing continental sentiment swinging violently toward strong borders and anti-interventionism.[2] The vote was placed within the wider frame of 2024 as a "Worldwide Election Year," alongside completed contests in Russia and India that returned [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] to additional terms.[2]
