# EU €50 Billion Ukraine Facility

The Ukraine Facility was a €50 billion aid package agreed unanimously by [[european-union|European Union]] leaders on 1 February 2024, funded within the EU budget to provide long-term support to Ukraine.[1]

## Agreement

The deal was reached after some leaders persuaded the sole holdout, Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]], to drop his veto.[1] European Council President [[charles-michel|Charles Michel]] announced on X, minutes after the formal start of the Council meeting, that "all 27 leaders agreed on an additional €50 billion support package for Ukraine within the EU budget," adding that the arrangement "locks in steadfast, long-term, predictable funding for Ukraine."[1]

Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] said he was grateful to Michel and EU leaders for establishing the facility, stressing that "it is very important that the decision was made by all 27 leaders, which once again proves strong EU unity."[1] He argued that continued EU financial support would strengthen long-term economic and financial stability, which was "no less important than military assistance and sanctions pressure on Russia."[1]

## Context

The summit at which the package was approved coincided with major [[2024-european-farmer-protests|farmer protests]] outside the [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] in Brussels, where rubber bullets and water cannons were used against demonstrators objecting to green-agenda regulation.[1] Belgian Prime Minister Alexander De Croo said the farmers' concerns would be added to the summit agenda.[1]
