# Ukrainian Drone Program

The Ukrainian drone program is the largely improvised effort by Ukrainian engineers and volunteer developers to build long-range strike drones capable of hitting targets inside Russian-controlled territory and Russia itself, profiled by [[the-economist|The Economist]] in August 2023 and summarized in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## Crimea operation

Early on the morning of August 25, 2023, a group of drone developers travelled to a launch point in southern Ukraine for one of the most daring aerial missions over Russian-controlled territory to that date: an attack on a military base deep in Crimea.[1] It was described as a test launch, with many of the prototypes in the swarm experimental, but some of them succeeded.[1] Local sources reported explosions inside the base, several dead, and wounded soldiers streaming into the local hospital.[1]

The strike capped a difficult week for the Kremlin, which was already struggling to explain more than a dozen drones striking central Moscow, repeated shutdowns of major airports, and unexplained explosions at arms factories, airfields, fuel depots and railways.[1]

## Morok and development conditions

One of the prototypes used in the Crimean operation was Morok, meaning "dark spirit."[1] A source close to its developers said Ukraine's new aerial strike capacity was the result of "seeds sown many months ago," and described Morok's development as a "miraculous" journey in which the team escaped incoming Russian rockets by minutes after a risky test launch a few kilometres from the Russian border.[1]

Fast and able to carry a heavy payload over several hundred kilometres, Morok ranks among the more promising fixed-wing kamikaze designs under consideration by Ukraine.[1] It progressed largely without government funding, relying on hard work and a few friendly benefactors, and like other developers its team faced difficulty obtaining resources to scale up to serial production.[1]

## Strategic rationale

The program is driven by necessity: Russia, a missile superpower, began the war with clear superiority in long-range strike capacity and later began buying cheap, effective Shahed kamikaze drones from Iran.[1] Ukraine has not been permitted to use Western-donated weapons inside Russia itself, and so has sought other means of striking back.[1]

## Badlands framing

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that waiting out and baiting an adversary's deployments — both actual and narrative — eventually reveals their positions, a tenet he applied to mass psychological warfare as well as kinetic war.[1] He characterized The Economist as a legacy platform whose "mask slips," reading the piece as an admission that the Ukrainian side, which he equated with the globalist West, was conducting a psychological terror campaign against Russian civilians rather than combatants in order to recover ground lost during the thwarted counter-offensive.[1]
