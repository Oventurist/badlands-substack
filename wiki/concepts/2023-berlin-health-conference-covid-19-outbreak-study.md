# 2023 Berlin Health Conference COVID-19 Outbreak Study

The **2023 Berlin health conference COVID-19 outbreak study** was a research paper published on June 13, 2023 in *JAMA Network Open*, a journal of the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]], reporting that every person known to have been infected with COVID-19 after attending a health conference in Germany had been vaccinated.[1]

## Findings

Approximately 4,462 people attended the conference in Berlin in the autumn of 2022, of whom 1,355 completed a survey and only about half of those were tested afterward.[1] Of the respondents who were tested, 109 — about 14 percent — tested positive for COVID-19, and all 109 reported having received at least two doses of a COVID-19 vaccine.[1] Just 19 of the positive cases had evidence of prior infection.[1] Among survey respondents who tested negative, 98 percent were vaccinated and 62.5 percent had documented prior COVID-19 infection.[1] The findings were reported by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]].[1]

## Commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] presented the study as confirmation of the long-standing claim that "the pandemic is of the vaccinated."[1] He argued that mRNA [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] instruct the body's cells to manufacture spike proteins that disperse through the body and cause health problems, and that vaccinated people end up registering more positive signals on COVID tests — while allowing that the reliability of the tests themselves is debatable.[1] He predicted that the disclosure would damage the vaccine industry only marginally, because heavily propagandized populations require multiple disillusioning events to abandon a narrative, and warned that the same figures who anticipated COVID were forecasting a worse pandemic to come.[1]

Deschamps extended the item into a general anthropology of belief, dividing people into the "intrinsically minded," who question and vet claims, break paradigms and are punished by authorities before later being co-opted by them, and the "extrinsically minded," who depend on official narratives and act as the footsoldiers of authoritarianism.[1] He described history as a contest between "shadow men" who capture the extrinsic herd and righteous men who resist them, both small populations competing for the allegiance of the undirected majority — the "unbegun," a term he attributed to former Satanist priest turned freedom activist Mark Passio.[1]
