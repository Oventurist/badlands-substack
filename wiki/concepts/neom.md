# NEOM

NEOM is a large planned development in Saudi Arabia, promoted by Saudi officials as an attempt to build a city that is environmentally friendly and sustainable, offers a very high quality of life, and generates virtually no traffic.[1] Its best-known component is a linear megastructure known as The Line.[1]

## Car-free design

At a January 2023 panel in Switzerland titled "The Evolution of Urban Life," Saudi Minister of State for Foreign Affairs and Climate Envoy [[adel-al-jubeir|Adel bin Ahmed Al-Jubeir]] said The Line "will revolutionize the way people look at cities and urban planning" and declared plainly, "There will be no cars."[1] He said residents would move between places efficiently using transportation modes that are environmentally friendly and based on renewable energy, and described private vehicle ownership as wasteful.[1] The remarks were delivered during the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in Davos.[1]

## Criticism

Critics within the Truth Community read the project as a template for the broader elimination of private property rather than a purely environmental initiative.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] characterised NEOM as a "sustainable smart-city hell-scape," noted reports that it is being constructed with slave labour, and argued that a settlement in one of the most arid and uninhabitable regions on earth, lacking private vehicles or long-distance transport, could become a death trap should anything go wrong.[1] The concept is frequently linked in such commentary to the wider [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]] debate.[1]

## Sindalah Island and the 2024 opening

In October 2024, days before Saudi Arabia opened the eighth edition of its Davos-style [[future-investment-initiative|Future Investment Initiative]], a handpicked group of financiers, entertainers and influencers gathered on Sindalah Island, the first Neom project to open its doors.[2] The resort features ultra-luxury hotels, undeveloped beaches and an 86-berth marina on the Red Sea for visiting yachts.[2] Reporting framed the event as a showcase for [[mohammed-bin-salman|Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman]]'s trillion-dollar bet that the kingdom has a future beyond oil.[2]

The wider Neom area in Saudi Arabia's northwest is expected to cost between $500 billion and $1.5 trillion to build, making it the crown prince's boldest move to date.[2] Karen Young, a senior research scholar at Columbia University's Center on Global Energy Policy, said that "there is still considerable effort to go in partnerships and investment, so timing with FII would make sense," adding that visible infrastructure for power, water and transport would do much to instill investor confidence in the government's commitment.[2] Neom has been variously pitched as a futuristic port, a high-end tourist destination, a clean-energy hub and a venue for the FIFA World Cup and the Asian Winter Games.[2]

## Badlands commentary on the 2024 opening

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] took a markedly warmer view of the Saudi building program in this instance, crediting the crown prince for defying Barack Obama, who reportedly laughed at his 2016 pledge to transform the Middle East into the next great civilization.[2] The commentary cited reports that Saudi construction would absorb 20 percent of the world's steel supply over the following decade, sourced largely from China and India because the kingdom is not a major steel producer, and paired Neom with [[the-mukaab|The Mukaab]] in Riyadh as evidence that the Global South was building while the United States stagnated.[2] It also noted that Saudi Arabia is scheduled to host the World's Fair in 2030 and the FIFA World Cup in 2034, and that the [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]] diversification plan initially drew derision from Western officials.[2]
