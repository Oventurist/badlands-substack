# May 2025 Intelligence on Israeli Strike Preparations Against Iran

In May 2025, CNN reported that the United States had obtained new intelligence suggesting that [[israel|Israel]] was making preparations to strike [[iran|Iranian]] nuclear facilities, even as the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] pursued a diplomatic deal with Tehran.[1]

## The reporting

Multiple U.S. officials familiar with the intelligence told CNN that such a strike would represent a brazen break with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and could risk igniting a broader regional conflict in the Middle East — an outcome Washington had sought to avoid since the war in Gaza inflamed tensions beginning in 2023.[1] Officials cautioned that it was not clear Israeli leaders had made a final decision, and that there was deep disagreement within the U.S. government over the likelihood that Israel would ultimately act, with the outcome expected to depend on Israel's assessment of the U.S.–Iran nuclear negotiations.[1] One person familiar with the intelligence said "the chance of an Israeli strike on an Iranian nuclear facility has gone up significantly in recent months," adding that "the prospect of a Trump-negotiated US-Iran deal that doesn't remove all of Iran's uranium makes the chance of a strike more likely."[1]

## Badlands rejection of the story

The May 21, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] treated the report as fabricated.[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that the story could be identified as false at first glance from the phrase "the US has obtained intelligence" about Israel, arguing that "there is only Regime intelligence and our intelligence" and that Israel is not able to strike Iran on behalf of the Regime.[1] He predicted that if a story of an Israeli strike on Iran appeared, it would be false, and would be pushed only to convince the world that the Iranian "nuclear" threat had been nullified — a threat he characterized as itself nonexistent.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] offered a longer historical frame, tracing the narrative's plausibility to more than forty years of cultural conditioning: the 1979 [[jerusalem-conference-on-terrorism|Jerusalem Conference on Terrorism]], whose essays [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] compiled and released in 1981; the film Delta Force, which he identified as the first mainstream production casting radical Islamic terrorists as villains; and Netanyahu's 1995 book Fighting Terrorism, which warned that Iran was on the cusp of a nuclear weapon and argued for kinetic Western intervention.[1] He concluded that thirty years later Netanyahu was "still singing the same song with the same lyrics," and asked how many people were still falling for the psyop.[1]
