# Badlands Brief: Southern Hospitality Superseded Plus Cross Burning

"Southern Hospitality Superseded Plus Cross Burning" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily news digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on its Substack, dated June 4, 2026. Like other editions, it pairs blockquoted summaries of mainstream and independent news reports with commentary from named Badlands contributors, in this case [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The edition carries the standard disclaimer that the opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## Superseding indictment against the Southern Poverty Law Center

The lead item reports that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] announced on a Tuesday that it had secured a superseding indictment against the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]], adding new allegations concerning the use of the organization's tax-exempt donations to fund informants placed inside extremist groups. According to the article, a grand jury in the Middle District of Alabama alleges that the group used roughly $4.1 million to pay individuals embedded in hate organizations, who prosecutors say then took part in activities including recruiting members and obtaining materials linked to cross burnings and [[ku-klux-klan|Ku Klux Klan]] regalia.

The article notes that the updated indictment reportedly removes language referring to "false or misleading" statements to banks, following the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruling in [[thompson-v-united-states|*Thompson v. United States*]], which clarified that the relevant statute applies only to false statements. The SPLC is described as facing eleven counts of wire fraud, bank fraud, and conspiracy to commit money laundering, with prosecutors alleging that it defrauded donors and banks by routing funds through shell accounts to individuals inside the groups it publicly opposed. The organization has pleaded not guilty and has moved to dismiss the case, characterizing it as a vindictive prosecution.

In commentary, Ashe in America connects the indictment to the 2023 election of [[yemi-mobolade|Yemi Mobolade]], whom she calls "the commie mayor of [[colorado-springs|Colorado Springs]]," elected in "one of the reddest cities in Colorado after a supposed cross burning in front of his campaign sign." She quotes reporting on the resulting prosecution: "Jurors found Bernard, who is also Black, and his wife, Ashley Blackcloud, were guilty of making a threat or conveying false information about a threat. They were also found guilty of conspiring to do that together. Rodriguez said setting the cross on fire and writing a racial slur on one of Mobolade's campaign signs and then spreading word about it harmed Mobolade and his family and affected the city's election."

Ashe writes that "the story was always crazy," that when Mobolade was "let off the hook" observers including herself believed the mayor "was in on it and skating free," and that the stunt did not harm him but instead "ushered him into office." She notes that Mobolade knows the man who did it, while "Mobolade's complicity was off limits," and asks whether the SPLC was involved, arguing that "when supply for racism outweighed demand, racism had to be manufactured." She adds that she and her colleagues are tracking the SPLC connections of the people and entities that filed claims under the [[ku-klux-klan-act-of-1871|Ku Klux Klan Act]] against them in 2022, and introduces the term [[barratry|barratry]], quoting a definition: "Barratry is an old common-law offense that dates back to medieval England. At its core, it means habitually inciting, promoting, or fomenting lawsuits and legal disputes in which the instigator has no legitimate interest." She concludes that the offense is "not really in vogue in 2026" but that "we should make barratry great again, beginning with the SPLC and their collaborators."

## US backing for Hezbollah withdrawal from southern Lebanon

The second item reports that the United States is backing a new [[israel|Israel]]–[[lebanon|Lebanon]] ceasefire framework requiring [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] to halt attacks on Israel and withdraw its operatives from areas south of the [[litani-river|Litani River]], with security zones in southern Lebanon coming under the control of the [[lebanese-armed-forces|Lebanese Armed Forces]]. The agreement was announced jointly by the US, Israel, and Lebanon after negotiations in Washington. The article states that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration views the removal of Hezbollah fighters from southern Lebanon as a central condition for stabilizing the border, and quotes Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] to the effect that the goal is a security arrangement in which armed non-state actors no longer operate in the designated zones.

The piece situates the demand within a long-standing international position that Hezbollah's armed presence south of the Litani violates earlier ceasefire arrangements and should be replaced by Lebanese state authority. It notes significant obstacles: Hezbollah has historically resisted disarmament and insists that any withdrawal or surrender of weapons be accompanied by a complete Israeli withdrawal from Lebanese territory, with recent statements from its leaders rejecting US-backed disarmament plans. The ceasefire is described as fragile, with rocket launches, drone incidents, and Israeli strikes continuing after the announcement, and with enforcement complicated by the fact that Hezbollah was not a direct signatory. The article concludes that the framework moves beyond a simple ceasefire toward replacing Hezbollah's military presence with the authority of the Lebanese state.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry offers an extended forecast: the Lebanese people will plead for Hezbollah to stay because it is the only fighting force defending civilians from the IDF; Hezbollah will nonetheless pull back under pressure and incentive from Trump; Israel will then move quickly to annex the land south of the Litani; and the world will unanimously rebuke Israel, with most countries cutting ties and banning entry of Israelis. He predicts Hezbollah's return "with a vengeance," Trump taking its side, and the intervention of Russia under Putin, [[iran|Iran]], Syria, Egypt, Turkey, and Pakistan, with Saudi Arabia — "always an enemy of Hezbollah" — nonetheless standing with them and the Taliban in Afghanistan already having offered fighters and weaponry. He anticipates escalating EU hostility toward Israel led by France, given its historical connection to Lebanon, followed by Canada; relative Chinese silence combined with alignment with the Global South; and North Korean involvement "for fun." He argues the situation will unite most of the world, which has already been voting together against Israel at the UN while the US uses its Security Council veto, and that with Trump negotiating peace with Iran the US "will have no choice but to abandon Bibi and his merry band of Kahanist psychopaths."

## Trump on Iran, nuclear weapons, and meeting the supreme leader

A third item reports that President Trump said Iran has already agreed in principle not to obtain a nuclear weapon and suggested he would be open to meeting Iran's supreme leader as negotiations continue. In an interview released June 3, Trump said, "They've already agreed they're not gonna have a nuclear weapon," while emphasizing that preventing an Iranian bomb remains his top condition for any final agreement. He indicated that the supreme leader is directly involved in approving the talks and said he would "probably" meet him if it helped secure a deal. The article frames this as a notable shift from months of military escalation toward a diplomatic posture, with the White House signaling confidence in progress, while noting that talks have been complicated by continued Israeli operations in Lebanon and by disputes over sanctions, regional security, and the future of Iran's nuclear program; no finalized deal has been announced by either Washington or Tehran.

Burning Bright's commentary describes "the latest turn of the Iranian Knot," arguing that Trump "already knows how this chapter closes" and has been content to let "the Hegelian machine run its own script long enough to expose its authors before quietly seizing the pen." Amid reports of a heated exchange with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over Israel's planned escalations in Lebanon, Trump offered two lines that Burning Bright says should have collapsed the prevailing narrative: that Iran has already agreed not to pursue nuclear weapons, and that he would like to meet the supreme leader. Crucially, he argues, the figure in question is not "the old Ayatollah whose image had been frozen in amber for decades as the necessary villain of the narrative" but [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]], operating within a reconfigured leadership structure shaped by the strikes that supposedly removed his father and by the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] commanders presented as "the final blocks en route to peace." He notes that the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] had signaled in advance that this figure "looks and sounds nothing like the one the original playbook required," so changed by the strikes Trump ordered at the start of the war.

Burning Bright reads this as "another deliberate inversion" defining Trump's approach: having studied the enemy's preferred ending — "an on-ramp of controlled escalation that would lock the United States back into perpetual Middle Eastern entanglement, justify the continued weaponization of energy corridors and regenerate the military-industrial and narrative mandates the Globalist Hegemon requires to survive" — Trump chose to run their script himself, almost beat for beat, in order to produce widespread "discombobulation." Tension with Netanyahu, headlines of regional friction, and the specter of Hormuz thus serve as "narrative shielding" while he inserts hard pivots toward an off-ramp the script's authors would never have written. He invokes Badlands colleague Chris Paul's concept of "drafting" in the info war: as a driver drafting behind a larger vehicle conserves power before slingshotting past at the moment of maximum vulnerability, Trump has stayed within the slipstream of the protected script "only to edit it from the inside." The result, he writes, is "the methodical humiliation of a machine that now finds its own carefully-constructed story beats weaponized against it."

## Kurt Olsen joins DOJ grand conspiracy probe

The Brief reports that [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]], a White House official who assisted Trump's efforts to examine the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 election]] results, has joined the Justice Department as a senior attorney in the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida. A department spokesperson confirmed he began on Monday. Olsen will report to US Attorney [[jason-reding-quinones|Jason Reding Quinones]], a prosecutor involved in developing a broad "grand conspiracy" inquiry examining whether past investigations and prosecutions involving Trump amounted to criminal conspiracy. That effort, overseen in the Southern District of Florida, is reviewing prior federal probes including the Russian Collusion investigation tied to the 2016 campaign, cases brought by former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], and the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 2022 search of [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] for classified documents. The work is supervised by [[joe-digenova|Joe diGenova]], a Trump ally serving as counselor to acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and leading a civil rights unit in Miami. Olsen is described as having been involved in examining possible foreign interference in US elections and in the collection of voting machines and election materials in Puerto Rico, Georgia, and Arizona.

Ashe in America, referencing her remarks on Badlands Daily, writes that if any attorney in the nation understands election fraud and malfeasance "on every level – fake voters, fake ballots, and fake counts, and their evidentiary application across legal challenges, cyber expert analysis, legislative endeavors, and more," it is Olsen. She notes that Olsen spent the interval between Trump administrations as [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]]'s attorney and "went everywhere the evidence was," and that [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]] is enthusiastic about the appointment, sharing that he and Olsen had agreed in December 2020 that Olsen would take election fraud "to the firing squad." Ashe says she had always assumed that Lindell's "unfortunate photo op" of January 15, 2021 — in which Olsen's name appeared on papers under Lindell's arm — was Olsen's origin story in the election fight, and finds Byrne's earlier date "very interesting." She adds that she does not recall where Lindell got the papers or who put Olsen's name on them, remarking that this "seems important," and closes by congratulating Olsen.

## Trump–Netanyahu call on Lebanon

Another item reports that Trump and Netanyahu both confirmed a tense phone call over Israel's military actions in Lebanon. Trump acknowledged frustration at continued Israeli strikes and confirmed that he sharply criticized Netanyahu, while emphasizing that the two maintain a strong relationship. Trump was reportedly concerned that further escalation could undermine US efforts to stabilize the region and advance diplomatic talks involving Iran, with the call focused on planned Israeli operations in Beirut and the risk of wider conflict. The article frames the dispute as a growing divergence in priorities between Washington and Jerusalem, with Netanyahu focused on pressuring Hezbollah militarily and Trump pushing de-escalation, ceasefires, and a broader regional settlement including an agreement with Iran.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes, "And there you have it," saying he had been unsure whether that point had been reached given prior teases and "rug pulls," but that "the moment has finally come." He expects Trump to continue being kind to Netanyahu — "he will continue to kill Bibi with kindness just like he has done for the past ten years" — but identifies the real tell as Trump's openness to meeting the Ayatollah, the moment "that heads exploded across Tel Aviv," because it made clear that Trump had been truthful in describing the Iran conflict as "The Venezuela Model." As with Venezuela, he suspects Trump will soon call Iran "one of our greatest allies."

## Bonus item: NIH researchers charged in monkeypox smuggling case

The edition closes with a bonus item reporting that two [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]] researchers have been charged in a federal criminal complaint with conspiracy to smuggle monkeypox into the United States and making false statements to federal authorities, according to the US Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Michigan. [[vincent-munster|Vincent Munster]], a Netherlands citizen and chief of the Virus Ecology Section at the Rocky Mountain Laboratory in Montana, and [[claude-kwe|Claude Kwe]], a Cameroon national and research fellow in Munster's section, are accused of attempting to bring biological materials into the country after traveling from the Republic of Congo during a monkeypox outbreak.

Federal authorities say the two arrived at Detroit Metropolitan Airport on January 25, 2026, where [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] officers found a black case the men said contained diagnostic equipment. Investigators allege it instead held 113 vials in Styrofoam coolers; of those tested, 17 contained deactivated monkeypox virus, one contained chickenpox virus, and two contained human DNA. The case is being investigated by the FBI, Customs and Border Protection, and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] Office of Inspector General. The men face up to five years in prison if convicted.
