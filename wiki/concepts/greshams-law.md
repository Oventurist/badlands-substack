# Gresham's Law

Gresham's Law is the monetary principle that "bad money drives out good" — the observation that where two forms of currency circulate side by side, legally overvalued currency will push legally undervalued currency out of circulation, as holders hoard the coinage with the greater intrinsic value and spend the debased coinage.[1]

The principle is named for Sir Thomas Gresham, who lived from 1519 to 1579 and wrote about the value and minting of coins while working as a financier.[1] As Investopedia summarizes it, the law "observes the effects of currency debasement."[1]

## Relevance to the trillion-dollar coin debate

The law entered Badlands Media's coverage of the [[trillion-dollar-coin|trillion-dollar coin]] when the Twitter account BTC Citadel XXl posted it in reply to contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who had asked her followers to "make it make sense" after Nobel laureate [[paul-krugman|Paul Krugman]] revived the coin proposal in May 2023.[1] The argument advanced was that if the trillion-dollar coin held greater intrinsic value than the paper dollar, it would over time displace the dollar in circulation — that it would be "good money that's paying bad debt," albeit "a small band aid on a wound with fatal bleeding."[1]

Ashe in America noted that Gresham's Law was obviously not the "dumb law" that press coverage in 2013 had blamed for the prospect of default — that was the [[debt-ceiling|debt ceiling]] statute — but that the principle was nonetheless directly relevant to the debate.[1] The relevance collapses, however, once one accepts the premise reported by Quartz that the metal content of the coin is irrelevant to its declared face value, because a coin arbitrarily denominated at a trillion dollars has no intrinsic value to drive anything out.[1]

## Debasement in a fiat system

Currency debasement historically meant mixing base metals into coinage struck from precious metals, a practice that hands governments additional money to spend while delivering inflation to citizens.[1] Because modern currency is, in Ashe in America's phrasing, "backed by nothing but hopes and dreams," debasement today refers to the government printing money — increasing the supply of dollars while decreasing the spending power of each one.[1] See [[currency-debasement|currency debasement]].[1]
