# Intelligence Community–Big Tech Revolving Door

The intelligence community–Big Tech revolving door refers to the documented flow of former United States intelligence, law enforcement and homeland security personnel into senior positions at major technology companies, particularly within content moderation units. As [[congress|Congress]] and the courts examined federally sanctioned censorship by Big Tech, the pattern was characterized as a troubling revolving door between the U.S. intelligence community and the technology giants at the center of one of the fiercest free-speech battles in modern American history.[1]

## The Just the News review

A review by [[just-the-news|Just the News]] of the LinkedIn employment histories of senior Big Tech executives found that at least 200 former workers of the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], the [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Homeland Security Department]] had taken Silicon Valley jobs.[1] Many of those hires landed in content moderation units responsible for regulating supposed "disinformation," which the review described as disproportionately throttling news and opinion deviating from approved, left-leaning norms.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] called the arrangement an unholy marriage between Big Tech and the intelligence apparatus, and argued it had been anticipated by [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] as far back as 1971 in *Between Two Ages: America's Role in the Technetronic Era*.[1] He contended that intelligence personnel and the military-industrial complex were arguably involved in the rise and diffusion of digital technology into daily life from the very beginning, and that the connection was no longer difficult to demonstrate given that Just the News had established it simply by scanning public LinkedIn histories.[1] DeLarme expressed hope that the newly formed Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government would take the findings into account in its investigations.[1]
