# Debt Ceiling X-Date

The **X-date** is the day on which the United States government exhausts its cash and the extraordinary accounting measures used to keep paying its bills once the statutory debt ceiling has been reached.[1]

## 2025 "warning track" estimate

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] told lawmakers on a Tuesday in May 2025 that a fresh estimate of when the United States would run out of cash was "forthcoming," adding that the debt ceiling was already "on the warning track."[1] He explained that the Treasury was still counting the flood of annual tax payments that arrived around the April 15 filing deadline, and that those receipts would shape the X-date.[1]

Appearing before a House Appropriations subcommittee, Bessent declined to give a hard figure but assured lawmakers the forecast would land soon, having previously pledged to update [[congress|Congress]] during the first half of May.[1] "Just as an outfielder running for a fly ball, we are on the warning track," he said. "And when you're on the warning track, it means the wall's not far away."[1]

## Legislative stakes

On Capitol Hill the deadline is more than a bookkeeping entry: Republican leaders tied their sweeping bill covering tax cuts, border measures, and energy policy to a debt-limit increase, planning to push it through the House on GOP votes alone before the X-date arrived.[1]
