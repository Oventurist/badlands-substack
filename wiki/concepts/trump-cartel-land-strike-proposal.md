# Trump Cartel Land Strike Proposal

In January 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States may begin conducting land strikes against drug cartels operating in Mexico, expanding on earlier statements about using US military force against transnational criminal organizations.[1] Trump argued that cartels running drug operations across the US–Mexico border pose a direct national security threat, and claimed the cartels were effectively running the Mexican government.[1]

## Regional reaction

The remarks raised concerns in Mexico, where officials warned against any violation of national sovereignty, and in Colombia, where President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] criticized US military actions in the region following Washington's recent operation involving Venezuela.[1] US officials announced no formal military orders and released no timeline for potential strikes.[1] Commentators alleged that Trump's rhetoric had put Mexico on edge diplomatically as regional governments weighed the implications of expanded US military action.[1]

On the Wednesday night following the remarks, Trump posted to Truth Social that he had spoken with Petro by phone and invited him to the [[white-house|White House]] for a face-to-face meeting — a turn Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read as evidence that the preceding confrontation had been kayfabe.[1]

## Badlands analysis of Mexico

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that, unlike the Venezuela and Colombia situations, he believed Trump was telling the truth about Mexico.[1] He described former President Andrés Manuel López Obrador as a hardcore communist who nonetheless respected Trump and got along with him, citing Obrador's deployment of 29,000 Mexican troops to the border.[1] By contrast, current President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] — Obrador's purported protégé — was said to sing a different tune, with Trump visibly frustrated at the failure to finalize a bilateral trade deal and having accused her in May of being afraid of the cartels after she rejected his offer to send US troops.[1]

The commentary advanced the thesis that Mexico is the real Deep State hub of Latin America and that Sheinbaum was an agent dispatched to conduct damage control after Obrador gave Trump everything he asked for.[1] It also noted Sheinbaum's defense of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] and her public request that he receive a fair trial, alongside claims that the US case against Maduro hinges on the testimony of a single Venezuelan general offered as part of a plea deal for clemency — which, if true, could see the prosecution collapse in court and Maduro publicly exonerated.[1]
