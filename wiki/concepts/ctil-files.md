# CTIL Files

The "CTIL Files" are a set of disclosures released in late November 2023 by journalists [[michael-shellenberger|Michael Shellenberger]] and [[matt-taibbi|Matt Taibbi]], published the day before the two independent journalists testified before the House Weaponization Subcommittee.[1] At the time of the Badlands report the material consisted of three threads on [[x-platform|X]] together with supplemental media distributed across multiple alternative outlets.[1]

## Contents

According to Badlands Media's summary, the release comprised strategy documentation, project, change and communications plans, training materials, and video-recorded admissions by the participants themselves.[1] Badlands writers characterized the documents as showing participants admitting to — and in places boasting about — running government-sanctioned information operations directed at the American public.[1]

The stated purpose of the operations, as described in the files, was to prevent a "repeat of 2016," and the Badlands commentary argued that the timeline implicated the leadership and possible direction of the 44th president.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and Paul Fleuret reviewed the files on an extended episode of the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] program Culture of Change, describing the material as stunning and as corroboration of claims [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had made about government-directed information operations.[1] Badlands writers linked the CTIL Files to the concurrent controversies over [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s removal from [[fox-news|Fox News]] and Elon Musk's confrontation with advertisers, presenting the two stories together as a view of the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship-industrial complex]] from strategy through execution.[1]

## Use in Badlands analysis

In December 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands]] founder [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] built on reporting about the CTIL files in his feature "The Illusion of Democracy," using the documents' account of coordination between government agencies and the media industrial complex over 2020 election reporting as a starting point for tracing agency leadership across the Obama-to-Trump and Trump-to-Biden transitions.[2] Badlands commentary presented the files as evidence for what it terms the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s "System of Systems," comprising administrative, clandestine, narrative and legal arms.[2]
