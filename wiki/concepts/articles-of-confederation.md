# Articles of Confederation

The Articles of Confederation were the first governing compact among the American states, adopted two years after the [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]], and are cited in Badlands Media legal commentary as the document that first supplied a name for the alliance of American states.[1]

## Role in the "nation" argument

In the analysis published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] under the byline [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], the Articles matter because of what the Declaration did not do: according to a paper by [[gerald-brown|Dr. Gerald Brown]] titled *Cooperative Federalism*, the Declaration "does not portend to create a single nation," and did not even create a name for the alliance — "that came two years later with the Articles of Confederation."[1] Had a single nation been intended, Brown argues, the proper name would have been singular, such as "The State of New Britain" or "The State of America," rather than plural.[1]

The article uses this sequence to argue that the American states were united in purpose against British rule while remaining free and independent of one another, a condition later carried forward into the [[united-states-constitution|Constitution]]'s language of forming "a more perfect Union" rather than a new nation.[1] The confederal reading is reinforced by [[blacks-law-dictionary|Black's Law Dictionary]]'s distinction between a national government and a federal government "of a community of independent and sovereign states, united by compact."[1]
