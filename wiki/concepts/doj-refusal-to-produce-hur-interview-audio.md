# DOJ Refusal to Produce the Hur Interview Audio

In April 2024 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] declined to produce subpoenaed audio recordings of former Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s interview with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to House Republicans, putting Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] at risk of being held in contempt of Congress.[1]

## Background

[[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] Chair [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.) and [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] Chair [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-Ohio) had threatened to hold Garland in contempt of Congress over the department's failure to produce the records, setting Monday, April 8, 2024 as the compliance deadline.[1] The recordings concerned Hur's interview with Biden about the president's alleged improper retention of classified documents from his time as a senator and vice president — the subject of the [[hur-report|Hur report]].[1]

## The department's response

The Justice Department met the GOP-imposed deadline to respond but notified lawmakers it would not turn over the audio files.[1] Assistant Attorney General Carlos Uriarte wrote to Jordan and Comer that "the Department is concerned that the Committees' particular focus on continuing to demand information that is cumulative of information we already gave you — what the President and Mr. Hur's team said in the interview indicates that the Committees' interests may not be in receiving information in service of legitimate oversight or investigatory functions, but to serve political purposes that should have no role in the treatment of law enforcement files."[1]
