# Find Me the Votes

*Find Me the Votes* is a 2024 book by journalists [[michael-isikoff|Michael Isikoff]] and Daniel Klaidman about the Fulton County, Georgia prosecution of Donald Trump and his allies over efforts to overturn the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## Content

The publisher's description presents the book as the product of two years of immersive reporting and "the most authoritative and dramatic account yet of a defeated president's conspiracy to overturn the 2020 election," including how a local Georgia prosecutor — described as "a daughter of the civil rights movement" — decided to indict Trump and his allies.[1] The description states that from the beginning District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] saw Trump's alleged crimes as a voting rights case and an attempt to deprive Georgia citizens of the franchise, and that the authors take readers inside both the Trump effort and the prosecution team as it built its case.[1]

## Role in the misconduct allegations

The book became evidence in the campaign to disqualify Willis from the case. According to defense attorney [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]], Willis gave the authors unfettered access to the prosecution and provided on-the-record quotes about the pending case.[1] Critics argued that a sitting District Attorney participating in a book premised on the guilt of the accused — published in the middle of the proceedings and before trial — abandoned the presumption of innocence and constituted prosecutorial misconduct.[1]

This was placed alongside Willis's January 14, 2024 "church speech," a roughly 35-minute address in which she publicly framed criticism of her office in racial terms, as part of a pattern of extrajudicial public statements about the accused.[1] Merchant testified that Willis had previously been disqualified from prosecuting cases for similar behavior.[1]
