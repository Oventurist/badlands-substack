# Black Sea

The Black Sea is the inland sea bordered by Ukraine, Russia, Turkey and the Caucasus that Badlands Media's [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series identifies as the possible location of "something akin to a portal to hell."[1]

## Physical anomalies cited

The article assembles several oceanographic features of the sea as circumstantial evidence for its thesis. The Black Sea is almost entirely devoid of oxygen below a thin surface layer, so that for most of its volume it is described as "basically a giant pit of death."[1] It exhibits a phenomenon scientists term "deep red fluorescence" — a red glow attributed to the anaerobic bacteria inhabiting it — and contains high levels of sulphur, an element popularly associated in folklore with the presence of demons.[1] The presence of anaerobic bacteria is noted as correlating with sickness and decay.[1]

It is also the largest meromictic sea on Earth, meaning its layers do not mix; the author reads this as leaving the sea's contents in a state of "suspended animation," directly paralleling the dormant zombie army at the close of the [[illuminatus-trilogy|Illuminatus Trilogy]].[1]

## Historical and occult associations

The region around the Black Sea is where the [[koryos|Koryos]] werewolf cult is said to have emerged from Western Russia and Eastern Europe thousands of years ago, and is also popularly regarded as the heartland of [[tartaria|Tartaria]] — a name the author observes is phonetically close to Tartarus, the region of the Greek underworld lying below Hades.[1] The same territory was at one point known as [[khazaria|Khazaria]].[1] Commenters on earlier installments additionally pointed out that the Ukraine area is where the Prussian army originated, a connection the author had not previously considered.[1]

## Contemporary significance

Because the Black Sea littoral is the site of the war in Ukraine, the article frames the region as the stage of a pivotal battle with dimensions well beyond conventional geopolitics, arguing that the current information war did not arise in a vacuum but follows a long chain of events rooted in that area.[1] Summarising, the author describes the Koryos as one of "hell's proxy armies" and a driving force in world politics from the beginning of recorded history to the present.[1]
