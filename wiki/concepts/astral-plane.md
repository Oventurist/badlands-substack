# Astral Plane

The **astral plane**, or astral realm, is discussed in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — What is Clown World?" as a non-physical domain that the author believes is real and accessible through altered states of consciousness.[1]

## Description

Drawing on his own past use of psychedelics, the author describes the astral plane as colorful and exotic but also strange and demented.[1] He cites [[frederick-dodson|Fred Dodson]], author of *Levels of Heaven and Hell*, for the claim that the dominant hue of the astral plane is the color red, and says this matches his own experience.[1] Red, he notes, was a sacred color to the [[koryos|Koryos]], is one of the most prominent colors associated with the circus, and is also the color of the "permanent revolution" — his term for the communist project of spilling blood wherever it goes.[1]

## Inhabitants

The essay reports that people who take strong psychedelics frequently describe encountering jester-like beings, and that "machine elves" — famously discussed by [[terence-mckenna|Terence McKenna]] — and "trickster jesters" are among the most commonly reported entities.[1] The author argues that consistent reports of the same beings across continents and decades make the simple hallucination hypothesis worth revisiting.[1] While never meeting machine elves himself, he says the substances imparted a carnival-esque flavor to reality, and suggests that the carnival aesthetic common at music festivals reflects "astral influence" intruding into this realm rather than mere human imagination.[1]

He characterizes these pale-faced, red-haired astral jesters as decidedly anti-human beings who delight in fixing human attention, sowing confusion, and pretending it is all a game, and proposes that they resemble the Koryos "like two branches of the same family" — perhaps being one and the same.[1]

## Holding tank hypothesis

Framing the point as speculation, the author hazards that the astral realm is a kind of holding tank for parasitic entities — in his phrase, "the true originators of [[clown-world|Clown World]]."[1] This connects to the essay's treatment of possession: demons, he writes, must act through human beings because they lack physical bodies of their own, which is why the Koryos "channeled" their ancestors in exchange for superhuman strength.[1] He also notes that many people interpret psychedelic entities as emanations of their own psyche, but sees no reason to privilege that reading over the simpler hypothesis that an astral realm exists.[1]
