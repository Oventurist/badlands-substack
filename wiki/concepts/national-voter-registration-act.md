# National Voter Registration Act

The National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) is a federal statute governing voter registration procedures in the United States, including requirements that states maintain and make available accurate voter registration records.[1]

## 2026 Justice Department enforcement

In February 2026 the [[department-of-justice|US Department of Justice]] filed federal lawsuits against Utah, Oklahoma, Kentucky, West Virginia, and New Jersey for failing to provide complete voter registration lists as required under federal voting laws, bringing the running total of targeted jurisdictions to 29 states plus the District of Columbia.[1]

Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and the Department's Civil Rights Division stated that the actions reflected enforcement of the NVRA and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] to ensure accurate and transparent voter rolls.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the suits as part of a broader push for accountability for what she described as stolen elections, arguing that while fake voters are "not the full problem," the machine "doesn't work without fake voters," and asking why states were fighting so hard against transparency.[1]

## Invoked in the 2025 Fulton County inquiry

In November 2025 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s Civil Rights Division stated that the purpose of its records demand to [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia was "to ascertain Georgia's compliance with various provisions of the National Voter Registration Act and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]]," including standards governing election technology and administration.[2]

## 2025 DOJ enforcement posture

In a July 8, 2025 statement of interest filed in [[judicial-watch-v-illinois-state-board-of-elections|Judicial Watch v. Illinois State Board of Elections]], the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] argued that the Act's list maintenance provision requires states to implement effective programs — not symbolic ones — to keep voter registration lists accurate and publicly accessible.[3] Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet K. Dhillon]] said states bear the responsibility to conduct "a robust program of list maintenance" and that the Department would vigorously enforce those requirements.[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary argued that statutory enforcement alone is insufficient and that durable roll integrity requires decentralized voter roll management.[3]
