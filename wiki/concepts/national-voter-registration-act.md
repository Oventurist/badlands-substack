# National Voter Registration Act

The National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) is a United States federal statute governing voter registration procedures. Among its provisions are requirements that states maintain accurate voter registration records and make certain registration list materials available for public inspection, obligations that have become central to modern litigation over the accuracy of state voter rolls.

## Enforcement under the Trump administration

The February 27, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] invoked the NVRA, together with the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]], as the legal basis for a nationwide campaign of lawsuits seeking complete voter registration lists from state election officials. On Thursday, February 26, 2026, the department filed suits against Utah, Oklahoma, Kentucky, West Virginia and New Jersey, bringing the total number of jurisdictions sued to 29 states plus the District of Columbia.

Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and the department's [[civil-rights-division|Civil Rights Division]] framed the filings as ordinary enforcement of statutory requirements intended to ensure accurate and transparent voter rolls. Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the litigation instead as an opening move in a broader accountability effort over election integrity, asking rhetorically why states were "fighting so hard against transparency."
