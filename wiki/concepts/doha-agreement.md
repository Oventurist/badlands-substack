# Doha Agreement (2020)

The Doha Agreement of 2020 was the accord negotiated by the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration with the [[taliban|Taliban]] setting terms for a U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan.[1]

In Badlands analysis it functions as a template rather than a historical footnote. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Trump's patience in dealing with the Taliban as "excellent" and predicted a replay in Lebanon: a historic negotiated deal with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] that simultaneously delivers to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] everything he claimed to want regarding the Arabs.[1] The open question posed is what happens if Netanyahu refuses the peace and demands more war, and at what point the mainstream press concedes that Trump's closest allies are Muslim countries openly angry at [[israel|Israel]].[1]

## Post-withdrawal aid as claimed evidence (2023)

Badlands commentary treated the continuing flow of U.S. money into Afghanistan as circumstantial support for the Doha framework's persistence beyond the Kabul withdrawal. A November 2023 report by [[john-sopko|John Sopko]] of [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|SIGAR]] found that roughly $11 billion in U.S. aid had reached Afghanistan since August 2021, in cash shipments of about $80 million every 10 to 14 days.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] credited [[just-human|Just Human]] with connecting the chaos of the Kabul withdrawal to the Doha Agreement signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which he described as promising cooperation with the [[taliban|Taliban]] to create a de facto Region of Responsibility allowing U.S. troops to leave the "Forever Warzone."[2] He argued that the U.S. military equipment left in Kabul was subsequently used by the Taliban to eradicate most of what remained of ISIS in the country, consistent with the deal's original structure despite its supposed voiding, and read the ongoing funding as both a [[devolution|Devolution]] proof and a "Sovereign Alliance–Regions of Responsibility" proof.[2]


## Later assessments

Badlands commentary in July 2025, written after Russia became the first state to formally recognize the Islamic Emirate, argued that the [[taliban|Taliban]] had proven more honorable than most American politicians by keeping their word to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] under the Doha Agreement.[3] The commentary emphasized that the Taliban had no reason to believe Trump would ever return to office, and that no one in establishment Washington would have faulted them for abandoning the agreement, yet they honored it and hunted down the ISIS-K operatives responsible for killing thirteen U.S. service members at Abbey Gate.[3]

## Continued funding as claimed proof

An August 2023 [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|SIGAR]] report showing that the Biden administration had sent $2.35 billion to [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] under [[taliban|Taliban]] rule was cited in the Badlands corpus as further evidence for the agreement's alleged design.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] recalled the theory — associated with [[just-human|Just Human]]'s 2021 research into the Kabul withdrawal — that the withdrawal represented the consummation of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Doha Agreement, which was intended to install the Taliban in Kabul while funding and arming them sufficiently to act as a long-term bulwark against ISIS extremists.[4] That this outcome unfolded with Trump "supposedly out of office," including the transfer of billions of dollars in US military equipment and a further $2 billion in aid, made the episode in his view a strong [[devolution|Devolution]] proof.[4]

## Counter-ISIS framing (2023)

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the April 2023 killing of the [[isis-k|ISIS-K]] planner of the [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate bombing]] as evidence that the agreement was functioning as [[just-human|Just Human]] had projected in August 2021: that the [[taliban|Taliban]] had been charged by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], under the Doha framework, with helping to eliminate the globalist-funded terrorist threat of [[isis|ISIS]].[5] In his assessment, "they are doing so," and the quiet of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] on Afghanistan reflected outcomes that were "going pretty well."[5]

## Invoked in the June 2024 Taliban outreach to Russia

Badlands commentary on the June 2024 [[taliban|Taliban]] delegation to the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum invoked the Doha Agreement as the explanatory frame, citing [[just-human|Just Human]]'s research that the Taliban had given their word to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to hunt [[isis|ISIS]], [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] and other [[cia|CIA]]-linked mercenaries in exchange for a U.S. withdrawal from [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[6] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the Taliban had kept that word — killing the [[isis-k|ISIS-K]] operatives responsible for the 2021 [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate]] attack and shutting down the CIA's heroin network — while Washington had not, and read Russia's move toward delisting the group and cooperating against CIA-backed terrorists as an extension of the same arrangement.[6]

## Continuing influence on Russian Afghanistan policy (2024)

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued in May 2024 that Russia's rapid rapprochement with the [[taliban|Taliban]] — including proposals to remove the group from Moscow's terrorist list and an invitation to the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum — had the Doha Agreement "written all over it."[7] In this reading, Russia's reliance on the Taliban to contain [[isis-k|ISIS-K]] after the Kabul withdrawal of August 2021 constituted a continuation of [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s post-Doha vision, as did the Taliban's emerging role brokering energy talks between Russia and Pakistan.[7]

## Taliban killing of the Abbey Gate mastermind (April 2023)

In April 2023 the Defense Department confirmed that the [[isis-k|ISIS-K]] leader responsible for planning the 2021 [[abbey-gate-bombing|Kabul airport attack]] had been killed by the [[taliban|Taliban]], an event Badlands commentary read as evidence the Doha Agreement was still operative.[8] [[just-human|Just Human]] quoted [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s remarks of 29 February 2020, on announcing the signing of the agreement: "We'll be very much hoping that they will be doing what they say they're going to be doing. They will be killing terrorists. They will be killing some very bad people. They will keep that fight going."[8] That the Taliban continued to kill ISIS terrorists while Trump was out of office, using equipment seized from the Afghan Armed Forces and without informing the Biden administration, was said to "speak volumes," with the conclusion that "the Doha Agreement is holding and working."[8]
