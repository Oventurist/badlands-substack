# Annexation Petition of 1869

The Annexation Petition of 1869 was the last serious effort by residents of [[british-columbia|British Columbia]] to have their colony absorbed into the United States rather than join Canadian Confederation.[1] It listed the colony's grievances with the Empire and appealed directly to President Grant.[1]

The petition argued that the only remedy for the evils besetting the colony lay "in a close union with the adjoining States and Territories," noting that British Columbians were already bound to them by unity of object and interest, that nearly all commercial relations were with them, that they furnished the chief markets for the products of the colony's mines, lands and waters, supplied most of the necessities of life, and provided the only means of communication with the outer world.[1] It closed: "For these reasons, we earnestly desire the ACQUISITION of this Colony by the United States."[1]

It followed an earlier petition sent to Queen Victoria on 2 July 1867 — one day after the [[british-north-america-act|British North America Act]] took effect — asking either for relief of the colony's debts or for permission to annex to the USA.[1] After the petition failed, Ottawa negotiations beginning 7 June 1870 secured British Columbia's entry into Confederation through debt assumption and a rail promise from [[john-a-macdonald|Sir John A. Macdonald]].[1]
