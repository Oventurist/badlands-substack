# Pelican Island Causeway Barge Collision

The Pelican Island Causeway barge collision occurred on Wednesday, May 15, 2024, when a barge struck the bridge connecting Galveston, Texas, to Pelican Island, spilling oil into the bay and forcing the span's shutdown.[1] The impact unfolded shortly before 10 a.m. CT, according to a statement from the city of Galveston.[1]

## Damage and casualties

Aerial images showed that a section of a rail line alongside the bridge appeared to have collapsed, with slabs of concrete piled on the barge.[1] No injuries were immediately reported.[1] Two operators were aboard the barge at the time of impact, said Spencer Lewis, a spokesperson for the Galveston County Office of Emergency Management; one fell into the water and was immediately rescued, while the other remained aboard and was also eventually rescued, both by the Texas A&M marine unit.[1]

## Campus closure

Texas A&M University at Galveston announced on Wednesday evening that its Pelican Island campus would be closed Thursday through Sunday, citing an "extended outage of the bridge through the weekend."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] read the incident as an instance of the "salami slicing tactic" — using a series of many small actions to produce a larger result that would be difficult or unlawful to accomplish directly — likening it to the Chinese practice of "death by a thousand cuts."[1] He argued that overt coordinated attacks on critical infrastructure are the easier case, while gradual degradation "slice by slice" produces destruction that can no longer be traced to an enemy's efforts.[1] Esler urged cultivating a humble appreciation of the patience and cultural discipline such long-horizon strategy requires, and observed that the same axiom works in reverse: those building a parallel economy and society can apply the same incremental, strategic precision toward a vision of national renewal.[1]
