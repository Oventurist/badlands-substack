# Christian Zionism

Christian Zionism is a theological and political current holding that the Jewish people have a divinely sanctioned right to live in the land of Israel, and that Christians should support the modern Jewish state on that basis.[1] The term became the subject of public controversy in January 2026 after church leaders in Jerusalem denounced it and the US ambassador to Israel publicly defended it.[1]

## The Jerusalem patriarchs' statement

On January 17, 2026, the [[patriarchs-and-heads-of-the-churches-in-jerusalem|Patriarchs and Heads of the Churches in Jerusalem]] issued a public statement describing Christian Zionism as a "damaging" ideology.[1] The statement affirmed that "the flock of Christ in this land is entrusted to the Apostolic Churches which have borne their sacred ministry across centuries with steadfast devotion," and warned that recent activities by local individuals advancing damaging ideologies "mislead the public, sow confusion, and harm the unity of our flock."[1] The patriarchs added that such undertakings "have found favor among certain political actors in Israel and beyond who seek to push a political agenda which may harm the Christian presence in the Holy Land and the wider Middle East."[1]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the letter as a harsh rebuke and read it as condemning political leaders — naming [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] — who use government positions to promote war and undermine peace.[1]

## Huckabee's response

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee replied on X, saying he loved and respected his "brothers and sisters in Christ from traditional, liturgical churches" but did not believe any sect should "claim exclusivity in speaking for Christians worldwide."[1] He argued that "if God can or would break His covenant with the Jews, then what hope would Christians have that He would keep His covenant with us?"[1] Huckabee further contended that the label "Christian Zionism" is "too often used in a pejorative manner to disparage free-church believers, of which there are millions across the planet," and defined a Zionist simply as one who "accepts that the Jewish people have a right to live in their ancient, indigenous, and Biblical homeland."[1] He said it was hard for him to understand why anyone taking the moniker "Christian" would not also be a Zionist.[1]

Badlands commentary described the exchange as an awkward situation for Huckabee, a pastor, to be placed in.[1]

## Holy Week 2026 criticism

After [[israeli-police|Israeli police]] barred Catholic clergy from [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|the Church of the Holy Sepulchre]] on [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] 2026, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that what disgusted him most was that Christian Zionists "will trip all over themselves to make excuses for why this is perfectly acceptable."[2] He argued their priorities were so out of order that they would likely end up betraying Christ and siding with the secular government of [[israel|Israel]], and described the levels of "brainwashing and conditioning" as on par with, or worse than, the most radicalized leftists.[2]

## The Third Temple critique (March 2026)

Following [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s claim that [[chabad-lubavitch|Chabad-Lubavitch]] was a hidden hand behind the war with [[iran|Iran]] and was pushing to rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that a large contingent of Christian Zionists effectively rebuke Jesus and His condemnation of the Pharisees by eagerly awaiting the [[third-temple|Third Temple]]'s reconstruction.[3] He argued that Jesus Himself is the Third Temple and the fulfillment of all prophecy, so no rebuilt structure is needed from a Christian standpoint.[3]

He added that some Christians believe cooperating with the project will prompt Jews in Jerusalem to convert, and cited a sermon by Rabbi [[yosef-mizrachi|Yosef Mizrachi]] mocking those Christians with the epithet "JC Penny" for Jesus.[3] In his reading, the messiah anticipated by such practitioners — a mortal man who will rule the world and fill it with God's knowledge — resembles [[the-antichrist|the Antichrist]] rather than Christ.[3] He closed by noting that a viral clip attributed to [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] in fact showed [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]] of [[lehava|Lehava]], arrested in 2015 for calling for Christians to be expelled from Israel and churches burned, and asked whether Christians understand with whom they are joining forces in Jerusalem.[3]

## Huckabee's February 2026 interview

In his February 20, 2026 interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], U.S. Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] failed to provide what Badlands writers considered a true definition of Christian Zionism, one of several responses cited in criticism of the appearance.[4] In the same interview Huckabee said it "would be fine" if Israel controlled the biblical lands from the Nile to the Euphrates described in Genesis 15:18, a claim Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated as the clearest expression of the maximalist territorial theology bound up with the movement.[4]

## Jesuit and Plymouth Brethren origins

The Badlands series "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler" argues that the dual origins of Christian Zionism and End Times rapture theology lie not in the Bible but in a chain running from the Jesuit [[francisco-ribera]] (1537–1591) through the [[albury-conferences]] of 1826–1830 to [[john-nelson-darby]] and the [[plymouth-brethren]].[6] Ribera's futurist reading of Revelation held that the end times signs would come only when the Temple of Solomon was rebuilt, implying the restoration of the Jews to their homeland.[6] Darby systematized this as [[dispensationalism]] and seeded it into dozens of American movements through six US tours.[6]

The doctrine reached mass American audiences through Cyrus Scofield's 1909 reference Bible, Charles Fox Parham's Pentecostalism, Dwight Lyman Moody's Bible college, and James Hall Brookes' Niagara Bible Conference.[6] The article contends that the entire movement of "war-pushing, faith-healing, rapture-loving preachers from John Hagee to Benny Hinn and Pat Robertson" rests on foundations built by Darby's sect.[6] It further charges that these Christian Zionist heirs, "begging for a first strike onto Iran," now supply popular backing in America for a nuclear-armed End Times project.[6]
