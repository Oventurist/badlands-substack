# Israel–Turkey Deconfliction Mechanism in Syria

The Israel–Turkey deconfliction mechanism is a proposed coordination arrangement discussed by [[israel|Israel]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] in April 2025 to prevent friction between their forces and interests in [[syria|Syria]].[1]

## Talks

A source familiar with the details told The Jerusalem Post on April 9, 2025 that the two countries had recently discussed creating such a mechanism, and that they were looking to "establish a mechanism similar to the one that had existed with Russia" during the rule of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] — an arrangement the source said had "successfully prevented unnecessary friction" between Moscow and Jerusalem.[1] The talks were described as being at a preliminary stage.[1] The report followed public remarks the same day by Turkish Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]], who told CNN's Turkish affiliate CNN Türk that Israel and Turkey had held talks aimed at reducing tensions, saying: "We have no intention of confronting any country within Syria, including Israel."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Israel–Turkey friction in Syria, and not [[iran|Iran]], was the real conflict then unfolding in the Middle East.[1] He tied it to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s alignment with the Kahanists and their [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] ambitions, which in Syria meant annexing Damascus and building [[davids-corridor|David's Corridor]] toward the [[kurds|Kurds]] east of the Euphrates by exploiting divisions among the Kurds, [[druze|Druze]] and [[alawites|Alawites]].[1] Turkey, in this reading, could become a key player in pinching the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] should the corridor be established and used as a route toward Iran, and he speculated about a possible return of Assad — "the Lion of Damascus" — to rally the tribes west of the Euphrates.[1] The same commentary noted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had fully endorsed President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]] and credited Turkey with overthrowing Assad, whose December retreat left Syria to [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Jolani]] and western-backed militants.[1]
