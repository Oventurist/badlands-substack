# 123 Agreement

A "123 Agreement" is a bilateral civil nuclear cooperation agreement concluded by the United States under Section 123 of the [[atomic-energy-act-of-1954|Atomic Energy Act of 1954]]. Such an agreement is the legal precondition for U.S. persons and contractors to transfer nuclear material, equipment, reactor technology, or related services to a foreign partner, and it establishes the nonproliferation terms governing that cooperation.

## Congressional review mechanism

Once the executive branch submits a completed 123 Agreement, Congress has roughly 90 days of continuous session to review it. The agreement takes effect unless Congress affirmatively blocks it, which requires a joint resolution of disapproval — and, because the president may veto that resolution, a two-thirds majority in both chambers to make the block stick. The structure heavily favors enactment, a point emphasized in the July 22, 2026 [[badlands-brief-1ae|Badlands Brief]].

## The Gold Standard and the Additional Protocol

Two optional provisions define how restrictive a given 123 Agreement is. The so-called [[gold-standard|Gold Standard]] commits the partner state to forgo uranium enrichment and reprocessing of spent fuel — the two indigenous routes to weapons-usable material. The Additional Protocol grants the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]] expanded inspection rights, including snap inspections at undeclared sites. The [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] accepted the Gold Standard in its 2009 agreement, which has since served as the benchmark against which later deals are measured.

## The Saudi agreement of 2026

The Brief reports that the Trump administration intended to submit to [[congress|Congress]] a 123 Agreement with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], expected to be signed by Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] and Saudi Energy Minister [[abdulaziz-bin-salman|Abdulaziz bin Salman]], following a preliminary agreement reached in Riyadh the previous year. The pact incorporates neither the Gold Standard nor the Additional Protocol. It creates a legal pathway for cooperation across the nuclear fuel cycle including enrichment, though it does not obligate the United States to transfer any particular technology or capability.

Badlands commentator [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the omission bluntly, noting the agreement "lacks any guardrails to prevent Saudi from developing a nuclear weapon," and recalled Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s statement to Bret Baier that the kingdom would pursue weapons if [[iran|Iran]] did. He also argued that congressional obstruction would simply redirect the work to South Korean or Japanese firms, leaving the kingdom's nuclear program intact and the United States outside it.
