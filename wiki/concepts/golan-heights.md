# Golan Heights

The Golan Heights is a strategic plateau on the Israeli–Syrian frontier that became a focus of international dispute after the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government in Syria in December 2024, when Israeli forces advanced past the demilitarized zone into Syrian territory.[1]

## Israeli advance after Assad's fall

Following the collapse of the Assad government, Israel carried out hundreds of airstrikes against Syrian ports, airfields, and arms depots, and moved its troops beyond the demilitarized zone in the Golan Heights. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] explained that Israel could not allow jihadi groups to fill the vacuum created on the Syrian border and threaten Israeli communities in the Golan. The Israeli government subsequently approved a plan to double the Jewish population of the Golan Heights and strengthen the region, with Netanyahu stating: "We will continue to hold onto it, cause it to blossom, and settle in it."[1]

## Russian position

At his annual end-of-year press conference in December 2024, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said Israel was the main beneficiary of the Syrian crisis. While calling Israel's national security concerns understandable, he stressed that "Russia condemns the seizure of any Syrian territories" and said the Russian position was firm, expressing hope that Israel would eventually withdraw. Putin observed that Israel was instead sending more troops across the border and appeared to be planning to occupy the Golan Heights, noting that the local population had recently submitted a request to be annexed to the Jewish State, and that the question of Syria's disintegration would have to be resolved in accordance with the UN Charter's right to self-determination.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued in [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that Israeli strikes on Syrian ports and airfields implicated the Russian naval facility at Tartus and the Russian airfield at Latakia, which Assad claimed was under drone attack in the moments before his departure. He framed the situation as a geopolitical tête-à-tête forming between Putin and Netanyahu that was also metastasizing between Netanyahu and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], citing Trump's December 2021 interview with Israeli journalist [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] as evidence that Trump and Putin held the same position on Israeli settlements, and asking whether Trump would stand with Putin if Russia acted to halt Israeli expansion.[1]

## Permanent occupation declaration (December 2024)

In December 2024, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] announced that he had ordered the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to begin securing territory in the Golan Heights - a region in the southwest of Syria along the Israeli border occupied by the IDF since the Six Day War in 1967 - and said that this occupation would be permanent.[2] Maps circulated at the time showed the extent of territory already captured beyond the previous lines.[2] Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] added that he intended to establish a "sterile zone" in southern Syria to prevent any "terrorist threat," a claim Badlands commentary mocked given that Al Qaeda-linked fighters had been used to topple [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]; the commentary predicted the zone would eventually reach the Euphrates River.[2]


## Annexation moves after the fall of Assad

After the December 2024 [[fall-of-the-assad-regime|fall of the Assad regime]], Badlands reported that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had publicly boasted of ordering the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to begin annexing land in the Golan Heights, territory that remains legally part of Syria despite having been under Israeli military occupation since the end of the Six Day War in 1967.[3] The commentary described the deployment of a nominal defense force to invade a foreign country and seize land as an Orwellian use of language, and linked the move to Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s October statement that [[damascus|Damascus]] itself was part of Israel on scriptural grounds, presenting both as expressions of the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] project.[3] Netanyahu spoke from Mount Bental on the Syrian border as the Israeli military seized control of the buffer zone between the two countries.[3]

The article also recalled a November 2016 incident in which [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] fighters exchanged fire with the IDF in the Golan Heights, after which former Israeli Defense Minister Moshe Ya'alon said that ISIS had apologized for the clash despite the IDF having allegedly killed four of their fighters.[3]
