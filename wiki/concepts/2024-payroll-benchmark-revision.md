# 2024 Payroll Benchmark Revision

The 2024 payroll benchmark revision was the annual preliminary benchmark adjustment to United States payroll employment data, released by the [[bureau-of-labor-statistics|Bureau of Labor Statistics]] on Wednesday, August 21, 2024, which showed that the American economy had created substantially fewer jobs over the twelve months through March 2024 than had previously been reported.[1]

## Anticipation on Wall Street

Ahead of the release, Wall Street banks braced for a downward revision of as much as one million jobs.[1] Economists at [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] and Wells Fargo predicted the preliminary benchmark revisions would show between 600,000 and one million fewer jobs than reported, according to Bloomberg News, while economists at JPMorgan Chase forecast a smaller adjustment of roughly 360,000.[1] The scale of the expected revision fuelled the perception in financial markets that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] had waited too long to begin cutting interest rates.[1] The revision followed a July jobs report showing a paltry 114,000 jobs added and unemployment rising to 4.3 percent, which had earlier sent the Dow down more than 900 points amid recession fears.[1]

## The reported figure and Badlands commentary

The revision ultimately amounted to 818,000 fewer jobs.[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode fit an established pattern in which "the narrators shout large job numbers from the rooftops before then quietly revising them downward after the initial headlines," describing the gaming of metrics as "a feature of the regime."[1] She contended that the administration's claim of having created fifteen million new jobs was untenable in light of the revision, and that even the revised figure was misleading because many of the positions counted were second and third jobs held by struggling Americans rather than an indicator of underlying economic strength.[1]

She linked the revision to what she characterised as misleading Earned Income Tax Credit messaging and to the administration's claims of corporate price gouging, which she read as narrative cover for inflation, concluding that the positive economic message from [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and the administration was "fabricated, fake, phony."[1]
