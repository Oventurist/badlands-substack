# September 2024 Federal Reserve Rate Cut

On September 18, 2024, the [[federal-open-market-committee|Federal Open Market Committee]] of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] voted to lower its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to a target range of 4.75%–5.0%, the first rate cut in more than four years.[1]

## The decision

Only 9 of 113 economists surveyed had expected a cut of 50 basis points rather than the more modest 25.[1] The committee voted 11 to 1 in favor; Fed governor Miki Bowman dissented in favor of a smaller 25 basis point cut, the first dissent by a governor since 2005.[1]

Alongside the cut, the Fed slashed the rate expectations embedded in its "dot plot" projections, despite leaving growth expectations unadjusted, barely changing its unemployment outlook, and merely hoping for lower inflation.[1] The post-meeting statement added language saying the committee was "strongly committed to supporting maximum employment" in addition to returning inflation to its 2% goal, and said that "in considering additional adjustments" to rates officials would assess incoming data, the evolving outlook and the balance of risks.[1] The Fed also tweaked its language to note that job gains "have slowed" and that inflation "has made further progress toward the committee's 2% objective but remains somewhat elevated."[1]

## Criticism and commentary

Critics noted that the cut was delivered less than two months before the November 2024 presidential election, with equity markets and home prices at record highs—circumstances that ordinarily would not call for what [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized as a "crisis-level" cut.[1]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the move should be read in game-theoretic terms: easing had been universally expected, but only a small minority of economists thought [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] would cut as aggressively as he did, which Burning Bright read as Powell caving to the bully-pulpit pressure of the incumbent administration.[1] Because [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign message was that the entire machine—media, entertainment, intelligence agencies and the central banking apparatus—was arrayed against him, the delivery of a seeming economic win to the administration before November 5 was predicted to backfire on both actual and narrative levels.[1]
