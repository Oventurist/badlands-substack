# 2024 United States General Election

The 2024 United States general election was held on Tuesday, November 5, 2024, and was framed by Badlands Media as the culmination of years of activity by the "truth community" — a "Day of Days" comparable to the D-Day landings at Normandy.[1] Badlands Media itself ran continuous special coverage of the election on Rumble, beginning at 1 p.m. Eastern and running "straight through to the wee hours of the morning," with hosts, guests and audience participation.[1]

## Security posture and unrest fears

Ahead of the vote, [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops were placed on standby in Washington state, Oregon and Nevada as a precaution against potential election-related civil unrest.[1] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] warned that threats to "election infrastructure" remained high.[1] Reporting cited by Badlands noted that Oregon had deployed the Guard together with a unified command for election security in 2020, and that states had increasingly activated the Guard for election cybersecurity duties.[1]

## Media and narrative dimension

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] appeared back-to-back-to-back on the Joe Rogan Experience, the largest podcast in the world, on the eve of the election.[1] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that these appearances were less about turning out the MAGA base than about "prepping the mass psychological terrain" for an expected cascade of information operations that would either defend or reject the popular result, noting Trump's insistence that he needed "a mandate in the vote" for what came after the election rather than for the election itself.[1]

Few operators at the "anon" layers of the information war trusted U.S. election systems, a distrust Badlands treated as healthy on the grounds that Americans needed to reckon with unreliable machinery for recording their sovereign mandate.[1]

## Foreign influence warnings

On the Monday before the vote, the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] and the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] issued a joint statement identifying Russia as the "most active threat" to public confidence in the election, describing manufactured videos and fake articles intended to delegitimize the process, frighten voters and suggest Americans were committing political violence against one another.[1] The statement predicted such activity would intensify through Election Day and focus on swing states.[1]

## Related disputes

Litigation over voter eligibility ran up to the eve of the vote, including the [[iowa-noncitizen-voter-challenge-ruling|Iowa noncitizen voter challenge ruling]].[1] Badlands also argued that the October economic and migration data — in which parole and illegal border entries far exceeded new job creation — was information voters should have had before casting early ballots, and used the episode to argue for a single Election Day so that "every American should be voting with the same available information."[1]

## Election night and results

Voting took place on Tuesday, November 5, 2024. [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appeared to win decisively on election night, though several media organizations declined to call the race for him by sunrise, and Michigan, Wisconsin, Arizona and Nevada remained uncalled while the New York Times rated all four as likely Trump states.[2] Badlands commentary described the outcome as a landslide including a popular-vote win, and argued that the slow-walking of results and localized irregularities may have been intended to minimize Trump's mandate rather than to change the winner.[2] Trump delivered a victory speech at Mar-a-Lago in the early hours.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] streamed more than 15 hours of live special coverage through the night.[2] On that broadcast [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]] described the difference from 2020 in terms of "defenses in depth" — layered protections that made cheating too costly — and called for outright reform of US election systems rather than continued reliance on such defenses.[2]

## AP VoteCast exit polling

The Associated Press VoteCast survey of more than 110,000 voters found that 40 percent named the economy and jobs the most important problem facing the country, roughly 20 percent named immigration, and about 10 percent named abortion.[2] About 80 percent of voters said they wanted at least "substantial change" in how the country is run, and roughly 25 percent wanted a "complete and total upheaval."[2] Just under six in ten respondents said they were at least somewhat concerned a second Trump administration would move the US closer to authoritarianism, with just under half saying the same of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[2] In swing states, top voter issues stayed within five points of the national average and abortion never exceeded 15 percent.[2]

A New York Times/Siena College poll released on October 25 had found 52 percent of likely voters trusted Trump to lead the economy against 45 percent for Harris.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the exit polling as confirmation of an "America First" mandate and noted that pollsters had been badly wrong in three consecutive presidential elections.[2]

## Election Day disruptions

Officials in Maine and Georgia responded to threats against schools and polling places, none of which were found credible.[2] In [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia, five noncredible bomb threats briefly closed two polling locations in Union City, and Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] said the source had been identified as Russia, while the FBI reported similar threats in several states appearing to originate from Russian email domains.[2]

Separately, a [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] investigation reported a potentially illegal [[ballot-harvesting]] operation run by [[ivoteisrael|iVoteIsrael]] in central Israel ahead of the vote.[2]

## Related events

On the same day, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] fired Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], and the US military scheduled a test launch of an unarmed [[minuteman-iii|Minuteman III]] ICBM from [[vandenberg-space-force-base|Vandenberg Space Force Base]] for the hours after polls closed.[2]
