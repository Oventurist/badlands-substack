# MAGAism

**MAGAism** is a term used by Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in the speculative feature "Trump Did It" to describe the political philosophy that, in the article's fictional 2050 retrospective, replaced globalism as the organizing principle of world affairs.[1]

## Definition

As defined in the article, MAGAism is "the idea that every individual has unalienable rights and their government is meant to foster the fair use of those rights, focusing on the family first, then the community, and finally the nation."[1] The ordering — family, community, nation — is presented as an inversion of the globalist hierarchy in which supranational institutions take precedence over local and national bodies.[1]

## Relationship to globalism

The article argues that although [[globalism]] was defeated, the post-globalist world became "more united than ever" as a community of "truly sovereign nation-first countries," with MAGAism sweeping across the globe rather than remaining an American phenomenon.[1] In the narrative's framing, national sovereignty and international cooperation are not opposed; it is the imposition of centralized supranational authority that the article treats as the source of division.[1]

## Founding-era framing

Deschamps writes that "the reformed media" of the fictional future characterized MAGAism as "the great dream of the founding fathers realized," following what the article calls [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "epic 2024 win."[1] The philosophy is presented as continuous with the American founders' understanding of the [[rule-of-law|rule of law]] and of God-given unalienable rights, rather than as a novel ideology.[1]

## Claimed outcomes

In the article's projection, a MAGAist world of 2050 features small government, the abolition of taxes and what the author calls "other inflationary policies that unfairly tax the people," and the elimination of both personal and sovereign debt, with nearly every nation on a path of increasing wealth and prosperity.[1] The bitter political divisions that the article says nearly caused a third world war are retrospectively "exposed as propaganda operations on the part of globalist actors."[1]

## Context and caveats

The author explicitly labels the piece a "fictional projection and tour of the post-globalist world," dramatizing "one likely scenario" in which Trump and allied patriots "had the solution the whole time" but the public was not ready to accept it.[1] MAGAism as described is therefore a speculative construct within a work of advocacy fiction, not a documented movement platform.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] appends its standard note that features represent the opinions of contributing authors.[1]
