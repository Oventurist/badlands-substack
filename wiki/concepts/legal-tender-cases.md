# Legal Tender Cases

The Legal Tender Cases are a set of United States Supreme Court decisions upholding the federal government's power to issue paper money as legal tender.[1] Badlands Media cites them, alongside the [[gold-clause-cases|Gold Clause Cases]], as precedents that entrenched the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s monopoly over money.[1]

## Argument for reversal

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues these rulings were as unconstitutional as *Roe v. Wade* and the gun control laws the modern Court has been reversing, and that a Supreme Court stacked with originalists is deliberately overturning long-standing unconstitutional precedents to set new legal precedent.[1] He anticipates a legal showdown between the states and the federal government over the constitutional definition of money, predicting the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] will rule for the states and return the country to a [[gold-standard|gold standard]].[1]

## Constitutional basis of the challenge

The challenge rests on the constitutional rule that "no state shall … make anything but gold and silver coin a tender in payment of debts," and on the claim that Federal Reserve Notes are "bills of credit" that the Constitution forbids and therefore cannot be legal tender.[1] In this reading fiat money is unconstitutional, as is an all-powerful central bank, and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s prioritization of flipping the federal courts may have had this monetary showdown among its larger motives.[1]
