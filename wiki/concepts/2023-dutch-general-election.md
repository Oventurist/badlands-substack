# 2023 Dutch General Election

The 2023 Dutch general election was a parliamentary election held in the Netherlands in November 2023 in which the populist [[party-for-freedom|PVV (Freedom Party)]], led by [[geert-wilders|Geert Wilders]], finished first, an outcome widely reported as part of a broader European shift toward parties the media labels "far-right."[1]

## Result

The PVV, described in most coverage as "far-right," came out on top in the Wednesday vote.[1] The party, which has promised to ban mosques and campaigns for a far more restrictive immigration policy, was expected to win between 35 and 37 of the 150 seats in the Dutch House of Representatives, up from the 16 seats it previously held.[1]

## European context

The result was presented as one data point in a wider map of gains by parties on the so-called far right of Europe's political spectrum, illustrated by Statista material circulated with the coverage.[1] Commentary carried alongside the story argued that the media had become "so anti-common sense that anyone who dares to take any contrary view is called 'far-right' as if they are Nazis," and cited Armstrong Economics' Martin Armstrong to the effect that "anyone who wants to return just to normal life is now disgustingly far-right."[1] That framing extended to claims that dissent from support for war, high taxes, climate-driven lifestyle sacrifice, and gender ideology in primary schools was being treated as extremism.[1]

## Results and coalition arithmetic

Early results gave [[geert-wilders|Geert Wilders]]' [[party-for-freedom|Party for Freedom]] 37 of the 150 seats in the Dutch parliament, twelve ahead of its closest rival, the Labor-Green Alliance led by EU climate policy veteran Frans Timmermans.[2] Wilders declared victory on the Wednesday evening at a café in The Hague, saying, "The Dutch voter has spoken. We will ensure that the Netherlands will return to the Dutch."[2] Holding fewer than a quarter of the seats, he received the first opportunity to approach other parties about forming a coalition, a task complicated by most parties' pledges not to govern with him because of his views, which include banning mosques and withdrawing the [[netherlands|Netherlands]] from the European Union.[2]
