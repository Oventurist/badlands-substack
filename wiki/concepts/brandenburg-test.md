# Brandenburg Test

The Brandenburg test is the constitutional standard governing when the government may prohibit speech advocating the use of force or crime.[1] Under the two-part test, such speech may be prohibited only if it is both "directed to inciting or producing imminent lawless action" and "likely to incite or produce such action."[1]

## Application in the Colorado ballot case

The test was central to Judge [[sarah-wallace|Sarah Wallace]]'s November 17, 2023 ruling in the Colorado litigation over [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s eligibility for the 2024 primary ballot.[1] Wallace rejected Trump's argument that his speech surrounding January 6, 2021 was protected under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]], and rejected the contention that she was required to weigh only the "objective meaning" of his words, holding that "objectivity" is not a required part of the Brandenburg test.[1]

Wallace found both Brandenburg factors satisfied, concluding that Trump engaged in insurrection through incitement and that his speech was therefore unprotected.[1] She was persuaded by the petitioners' arguments that Trump was aware of his supporters' willingness to engage in political violence and that he intended to incite violence at the Capitol to disrupt certification of the election; she found Professor [[pete-simi|Simi]] credible, and Trump's lawyers did not explicitly argue against the petitioners' claims on that point.[1]
