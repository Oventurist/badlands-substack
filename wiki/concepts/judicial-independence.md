# Judicial Independence

Judicial independence is the principle that courts and judges should decide cases on evidence, precedent and impartial legal reasoning rather than under political pressure. In Badlands Media commentary it is treated as the central structural remedy for what the outlet describes as politically directed prosecution of political opponents.[1]

## Argument in Badlands commentary

In an August 2023 retrospective on the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]], [[alpha-warrior|Alpha Warrior]] argued that in a functioning republic the [[rule-of-law|rule of law]] should be an unwavering beacon of fairness free from political agendas, and that the prospect of imprisoning citizens or presidential candidates on the basis of political bias raises profound concerns about the integrity of the justice system.[1] He identified fortifying the judiciary's independence as the vital first task in any recovery, calling for safeguards that shield judges from political pressure so that verdicts rest on credible evidence, precedent and impartiality rather than partisan agendas.[1]

## Supporting reforms

The same argument pairs judicial independence with a package of complementary measures: independent bodies empowered to investigate allegations of [[politically-motivated-imprisonment|politically motivated imprisonment]] and to make their findings public; reform of law enforcement agencies through enhanced training, stringent oversight and unambiguous protocols so that investigations and arrests are not shaped by political agendas; genuine protection for whistleblowers who expose political manipulation inside government; and an independent, unrestricted press acting as a check on power.[1] Civil society organizations are described as guardians of human rights whose empowerment is critical to keeping institutions transparent and impartial.[1]

Alpha Warrior further called for reviewing the laws governing arrest, detention and prosecution to close avenues for political manipulation, and for aligning domestic practice with international human rights standards through outside oversight.[1] He concluded that none of these measures would suffice without [[electoral-integrity|electoral integrity]], since the foundation of a functioning democratic republic rests on fair and honest elections.[1]
