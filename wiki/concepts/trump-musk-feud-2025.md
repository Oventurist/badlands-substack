# Trump–Musk Feud (2025)

The Trump–Musk feud was the public rupture in June 2025 between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], which erupted into open view after Musk attacked the GOP's sweeping budget bill.[1]

## Trump's threats

On a Saturday in early June 2025, Trump told NBC News in a phone interview that there would be "serious consequences" if Musk funded Democratic candidates to run against Republicans who voted for the budget bill.[1] "If he does, he'll have to pay the consequences for that," Trump said, declining to specify what those consequences would be, adding, "He'll have to pay very serious consequences if he does that."[1]

Asked whether he had any desire to repair the relationship, Trump answered simply, "No," and said he assumed the relationship with the Tesla and SpaceX chief executive was over.[1] He said he had no plans to speak with Musk anytime soon — "I'm too busy doing other things... I have no intention of speaking to him" — and accused Musk of being "disrespectful to the office of the President," saying, "I think it's a very bad thing, because he's very disrespectful. You could not disrespect the office of the President."[1] Trump also asserted that the Republican Party was more unified than ever after the two men fell out in front of the world.[1] The remarks were his most extensive since the pair exchanged threats and attacks on X and Truth Social earlier that week.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the split should be read as a phase shift rather than a genuine rupture, restating his earlier thesis that Trump and Musk were coordinating both convergence and its opposite.[1] Quoting his own follow-up essay, he wrote that "Donald Trump hasn't just set the Battlespace … he IS the Battlespace," and that both men had convinced the vast majority of the American mind that their earlier sit-down was an organic event even though the convergence of these "Mind-Movers in the Moving Mind" had long been projected by those paying attention.[1]

He traced the arc from Trump's long-ago sparring with Musk in the media, through Musk's refusal to engage politically, his evolution into a culture-setter entrenched in the culture war, and Trump's endorsements of Musk's companies and technologies — concluding that the union in the zeitgeist was not merely inevitable but planned.[1] Citing his December 2022 essay on the "Narrative Pincer," he argued that the narrative deployments of Trump and Musk are aimed at the sleeping public rather than the awakened community, that they "are not correlated; they are coordinated," and that "coordination leads to causation," producing predictable reactions from the Media Industrial Complex and effective tension within the reachable middle of the collective mind.[1] See also [[trump-musk-narrative-pincer|Trump–Musk narrative pincer]] and the [[musk-bessent-white-house-altercation|Musk–Bessent White House altercation]].[1]

## Escalation and the "America Party" (July 2025)

By July 1, 2025, the feud had shifted from personal recrimination to institutional threat. As the [[united-states-senate|US Senate]] voted on amendments to the 940-page [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill Act]], Musk again attacked the legislation and its supporters, writing that "every member of Congress who campaigned on reducing government spending and then immediately voted for the biggest debt increase in history should hang their head in shame."[2] He warned that such members "will lose their primary next year if it is the last thing I do on this Earth."[2]

Musk also reiterated his call for the formation of a new "America Party" to serve as "an alternative to the Democrat-Republican uniparty so that the people actually have a VOICE."[2] Trump responded by suggesting that [[doge|DOGE]] — the cost-cutting body Musk had formerly headed — should investigate Musk for allegedly benefiting excessively from government subsidies, and claimed that Musk's real grievance was the planned rollback of electric vehicle subsidies rather than the budget bill itself.[2]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the episode as "pure Trump-a-Mania" and "some of the most obvious kabuki theater since Steve Bannon was expelled from the White House in August 2017."[2] In his reading, the intended net effect was to use Musk's platform to expose the Republican Party as a "band of traitors" on spending while simultaneously challenging the legitimacy of the two-party system.[2]
