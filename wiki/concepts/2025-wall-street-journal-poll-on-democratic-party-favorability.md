# 2025 Wall Street Journal Poll on Democratic Party Favorability

The 2025 [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] poll, released on Saturday, July 26, 2025, found the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]]'s popularity at its lowest point in three decades of WSJ polling.[1]

## Findings

The survey was conducted jointly by Democratic pollster John Anzalone and Republican pollster Tony Fabrizio.[1] It found 63 percent of voters holding an unfavorable view of the Democratic Party, against only 33 percent holding a favorable view and a meager 8 percent holding a "very favorable" opinion — a net negative favorability of 30 percentage points.[1]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s own approval rating remained underwater in the poll, but Democrats fared worse.[1] While voters retained significant concerns over the president's and the Republican Party's handling of the economy, inflation, tariffs and foreign policy, a majority nonetheless said they trusted Republicans more to handle those issues in [[congress|Congress]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that polls are fake in terms of actual voter sentiment but useful as data points for understanding the direction of the narrative, stressing that this survey was completed by a Democrat pollster for the Wall Street Journal.[1] She highlighted the finding that "by 17 points, voters disapproved of Trump's handling of tariffs, while still trusting Republicans more on the issue to Democrats by 7 points."[1]

In her reading, disapproval of Trump on tariffs will wane as revenues come in and the predicted damage fails to materialize, whereas Democrats are not dealing with mere policy disapproval — voters find them deeply unlikable and corrupt, characteristics on which the party keeps doubling down.[1] Noting the poll's own admission that Trump and the Republican Party are also disliked by more Americans than like them, but "by far lower margins than Democrats," she concluded that conditions must be genuinely dire for such results to be published.[1]
