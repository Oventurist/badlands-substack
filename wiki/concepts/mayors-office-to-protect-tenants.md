# Mayor's Office to Protect Tenants

The Mayor's Office to Protect Tenants is a New York City mayoral office that coordinates city agencies to defend tenants' rights, improve housing affordability, address complaints about unsafe or unlawful housing conditions, and hold landlords accountable.[1]

On January 1, 2026, Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] signed an executive order "revitalizing" the office as one of his first acts in office, and named housing organizer [[cea-weaver|Cea Weaver]] — former coordinator of "Housing Justice for All" and a central figure in the passage of New York's 2019 Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act — as its director.[1] The appointment quickly became controversial after clips of Weaver's remarks on shared-equity housing, home ownership, and municipal seizure of poorly maintained buildings circulated online.[1]

The same day, Mamdani issued a separate order revoking all remaining executive orders signed by predecessor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] on or after September 26, 2024 — the date of Adams' indictment — a step expected to affect a range of city policies.[1]
