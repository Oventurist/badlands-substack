# Commonwealth's Attorney

A **Commonwealth's Attorney** (CA) is the elected local prosecutor in Virginia, equivalent to a district attorney elsewhere. Virginia is unusual in that the Virginia Constitution categorizes Commonwealth's Attorneys as **Constitutional Officers** — offices created directly by the constitution rather than positions within the executive branch.

## Structural consequences

Badlands commentary describes the designation as effectively creating a fourth branch of government at the local level. Because a CA is not subordinate to the governor or any state agency, he is accountable to no one except the voters at four-year intervals. There is no recall mechanism, no procedure for removal from office, and no means of removing a CA from an individual case, even where overwhelming evidence of corruption or a blatant conflict of interest exists. In most states, by contrast, a governor may reassign or supersede a prosecutor on a given case — the mechanism invoked in the widely covered Jussie Smollett matter.

## The 2019 constitutional challenge

The Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry recounted suing the Commonwealth in July 2019 to challenge this provision, after a judge rejected a plea deal in the prosecution of the man accused of killing his brother. The deal would have traded a full confession for one year in county jail. The prosecutor — later exposed as a career con artist with a criminal record and no prior prosecutorial experience — told the property owner where the death occurred that the plea was the family's best chance at justice, attributing this to a conversation with the judge. The owner's letter of protest prompted the judge to declare in open court that no such conversation had taken place, that it would have been unlawful had it occurred, to reject the plea and to recuse himself.

The family's subsequent motion to remove the prosecutor cited his statement to them that he cared only about re-election, not their son's death; the writer later attributed this to a need for a fifth year of service to vest a six-figure state pension. A second judge found the argument compelling but denied the motion on the plain language of the constitution, then likewise rejected a plea and recused. The case went to trial in January 2022 — five years, three judges and three prosecutors after the death — and produced a conviction. The original prosecutor lost his re-election bid decisively, lost his law license, and never vested the pension.

The episode is cited as direct precedent for the [[department-of-justice]] probe of Fairfax County Commonwealth's Attorney [[steve-descano]], which tests the same accountability vacuum.
