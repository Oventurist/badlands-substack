# Badlands Brief: Narrative Warfare For Your Safety

"Narrative Warfare For Your Safety" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on March 13, 2026. Like other installments of the daily digest, it pairs excerpted news items with commentary from named Badlands contributors — in this edition [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The edition is dominated by the escalating war between Israel and Iran, the collapse of an Israeli military prosecution over detainee abuse, a White House dispute with ABC News over reported Iranian drone threats, the opening of an FBI office in Ecuador, and the activation of a new National Guard brigade in Washington, DC.

## Trump on the Iran conflict and the World Cup

The Brief opens with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Thursday postings on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], in which he rebuked [[new-york-times|New York Times]] coverage of the Iran conflict. Trump wrote that "We are totally destroying the terrorist regime of Iran, militarily, economically, and otherwise, yet, if you read the Failing New York Times, you would incorrectly think that we are not winning," adding that "Iran's Navy is gone, their Air Force is no longer, missiles, drones and everything else are being decimated, and their leaders have been wiped from the face of the earth." He claimed "unparalleled firepower, unlimited ammunition, and plenty of time," and framed his own role numerologically: the Iranian leadership had "been killing innocent people all over the world for 47 years, and now I, as the 47th President of the United States of America, am killing them. What a great honor it is to do so!"

In a separate post earlier the same day, Trump addressed the [[2026-fifa-world-cup|2026 FIFA World Cup]], stating that "The Iran National Soccer Team is welcome to The World Cup, but I really don't believe it is appropriate that they be there, for their own life and safety." A companion post hyped the tournament, saying ticket sales were "through the roof" and predicting "the Greatest and Safest Sporting Event in American History," with all players, officials, and fans "treated like the 'STARS' that they are."

Ashe in America's commentary notes that Trump was "on a roll" on Truth Social that Thursday, also attacking California governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and Federal Reserve chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], and sharing a photograph of himself with his parents at the military academy. She observes that the 2026 tournament will be jointly hosted by Canada, Mexico, and the United States — the first three-nation co-hosting — running from June through July, and asks whether the Iran conflict will still be underway by then. She flags an apparent tension between the morning post welcoming the Iranian team and the evening post celebrating the killing of Iranian leaders, puzzles over the scare quotes around "STARS," and concludes that the president's feed was "entirely discombobulating," ending with the Badlands refrain "Accelerate."

## The Sde Teiman prosecution and its collapse

The Brief's second item reports that Israeli military prosecutors dropped charges against several soldiers accused of sexually assaulting a male Palestinian detainee at the [[sde-teiman|Sde Teiman]] detention facility during the war in Gaza. An investigation had been opened after video footage of the alleged abuse surfaced, leading to arrests and a national debate inside Israel over detainee treatment; prosecutors ultimately withdrew the charges citing evidentiary problems after months of investigation. The case, the source notes, became a domestic flashpoint accompanied by protests and political disputes.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's commentary is the edition's longest and most polemical. He contrasts public alarm over "the rise of antisemitism" — which he says is blamed on white Christians — with what he characterizes as the conduct of a "self-anointed Jewish Empire," invoking Sodom and Gomorrah. He declines to republish the leaked videos, but summarizes the underlying reporting: a large group of [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] soldiers were recorded dragging Palestinian prisoners into a corner of the detention facility and assaulting them while shielding themselves from security cameras with ballistic shields, with official accounts stating the victim was sodomized with objects including a knife, causing internal injuries requiring surgery. He stresses that the conduct was documented on camera, in IDF military police arrest reports, and in hospital medical records, and that the charges were dropped nonetheless. He states that he deliberately softened the language relative to the original reporting.

He further recounts that when the IDF arrested four of the soldiers, Israeli settlers — led by members of the [[knesset|Knesset]] and by National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] — stormed the military base holding the accused and attempted to free them, with IDF soldiers physically fighting back citizens who tore down barriers. The multi-day demonstrations became known as the "Right to Rape" protests, premised, in his telling, on the view that Palestinians are subhuman. He includes a screenshot of the AI assistant Grok confirming that the riots were in support of the accused.

According to the commentary, Israeli media turned the four soldiers into celebrities and folk heroes. Only one, [[meir-shitrit|Meir Shitrit]], appeared unmasked, on Israel Channel 14; the other three appeared on primetime television with faces and identities concealed to defend their conduct. A Channel 12 panelist is quoted saying, "I told my friends that I don't give a rat's ass what they did to a guy like that." The Knesset then held a publicly broadcast debate on whether it should be lawful for men to sodomize male prisoners with objects, in which MK [[hanoch-milwidsky|Hanoch Milwidsky]] of the [[likud|Likud]] party said, "Shut up! If he is a Nukbha, then everything is legitimate to do!" Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], the commentary adds, rejoiced at the dropping of the charges. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's summarizing argument is that the Israeli debate was never about whether the acts occurred — that was widely accepted — but about whether they should be permissible, and that the answer given was effectively yes.

## Leavitt, ABC News, and the alleged Iranian drone threat

White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] called on [[abc|ABC News]] to retract reporting that the [[fbi|FBI]] had warned of an Iranian drone attack on California in retaliation for US actions; the Brief had carried the underlying story as a "Bonus Item" the previous day. ABC had reported, citing law-enforcement sources, that the FBI assessed Iran had considered or aspired to conduct drone attacks in California, and that investigators were examining intelligence indicating Iran had explored launching drones from ships or other platforms near the US West Coast. Leavitt called the report inaccurate and demanded a correction or retraction, arguing it misrepresented intelligence about potential Iranian retaliation. No attack occurred, and officials said monitoring continues.

Burning Bright's commentary treats the episode as evidence of a trap laid for Trump: an attempt to lure the president "and by extension, the American people" into ownership of "the next generational quagmire," delivering to warmongers, globalist financiers, ideological interventionists, and "performative patriots" the headlines they have wanted since the early 2000s. He argues that Trump and his circle effectively "rug-pulled" a false flag attack that would have been blamed on Iran but planned by parties seeking war with Iran. On this reading, Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s statement that the American pre-emptive strike was meant to pre-empt an Israeli pre-emptive strike makes sense as an effort to head off a "pre-seeded" Iranian 'response' that would have justified a 9/11-style invasion dialectic. He also cites Trump's answer when asked whether Israel had "forced his hand" — "I might have forced theirs." His conclusion is that Trump's response to the trap has been to give the war party the optics it needs "while quietly executing the disentanglement they cannot afford."

## First FBI office in Ecuador

The United States established its first FBI office in [[ecuador|Ecuador]], announced Wednesday, intended to strengthen cooperation with Ecuadorian authorities on drug and weapons trafficking, money laundering, and terrorism financing. Ecuador's interior minister, [[john-reimberg|John Reimberg]], said the key change is a permanent presence of FBI agents working directly with a newly formed unit inside Ecuador's national police. The move comes as President [[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]] continues a military-backed campaign against organized crime, following a recent joint US–Ecuadorian operation against trafficking networks. The US embassy called the opening "another strategic and operational milestone in security," and Acting Chargé d'Affaires [[lawrence-petroni|Lawrence Petroni]] said the agreement and the creation of a trusted FBI unit would expand the ability of both governments to "identify, dismantle, and bring to justice those who traffic drugs, launder money, smuggle weapons, and finance terrorism."

Ashe in America places the office within a lineage beginning with the Special Intelligence Service in 1940, modernized in the 1980s through the Legal Attaché program that stations FBI agents in US embassies, and expanded sharply in the 2010s as part of the bureau's global investigative liaison network. She notes that the FBI coordinates with intelligence services — "so, the [[state-department|State Department]]" — but exercises no law-enforcement powers abroad, working instead through host-nation authorities. She calls this a "reverse-sicario" arrangement and argues it undercuts objections to the claim that there is "no daylight between the FBI and [[cia|CIA]]," since the FBI abroad operates under constraints resembling those nominally placed on the CIA domestically. She closes by saluting the new office as a continuation of an "80+ year tradition of government overreach, both foreign and domestic."

## Netanyahu's pardon, the war, and the October election

Israel's Justice Ministry clemency unit reportedly did not recommend granting a pardon to Netanyahu, meaning any pardon request would face internal opposition. Netanyahu is on trial for bribery, fraud, and breach of trust, which he denies; Israeli law permits presidential pardons, though typically after conviction rather than mid-trial. Separately, Netanyahu said he does not know whether Iranians will overthrow their leadership, and warned Israel could target Iran's Supreme Leader if escalation continues. Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Saar]] said only the Iranian people can bring down their government but that outside assistance may be necessary. The IDF said it struck dozens of targets in Tehran to "deepen the blow" against the Iranian regime, while Iranian official [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] said the war would not end until Iran's enemies were made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry likens the clemency guidance to Groundhog Day: the groundhog saw its shadow, portending four more years of Netanyahu, "which means 4 more years of warmongering and empire-building." He argues the pending charges hang over Netanyahu like the Sword of Damocles, and that with an election scheduled for October and a real prospect of losing office and facing criminal trial, Netanyahu has no choice but to press the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel]] agenda regardless of reputational damage or the antisemitism it may provoke. Expanding Israel's borders or defeating Iran militarily would, in this analysis, secure victory in October.

The complication, he writes, is that Netanyahu is not winning. He cites [[haaretz|Haaretz]] — described as Israel's oldest newspaper and an opponent of Netanyahu — reporting that nearly a dozen smart missiles, each carrying dozens of independently guided submunitions, penetrated the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] and struck central Israel overnight, in one of many salvos. He concludes Israel's defenses have failed and the country is vulnerable. Meanwhile, although the IDF claims strikes across Tehran, he cites reports that [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] approached Iran, presumably on Israel's behalf, to request a ceasefire, and that Iran refused — which he reads as a sign Israel is taking the worse beating. He notes that both Netanyahu and Saar have publicly conceded that the Iranian population has not mobilized against its government and that external force may be required, implying either renewed IDF mobilization of an Israeli public exhausted by two and a half years of war or a demand that American soldiers do the work. Trump, he argues, has put Netanyahu in a position where he can neither de-escalate nor effectively escalate, raising the questions of whether Netanyahu turns to unconventional weapons or to false flags in the United States: "Trump has put Bibi in check, and it appears that we are just a few short moves away from checkmate."

## Bonus item: 260th Special Purpose Brigade

The final item reports that the District of Columbia National Guard activated the 260th Special Purpose Brigade, the Army's only brigade dedicated to coordinating military support to civil authorities and protecting critical infrastructure in the capital. The unit was formed following Executive Order 14339 and a directive from the [[department-of-war|Department of War]] calling for a trained, equipped National Guard unit to support public safety and integrate with federal and local security partners. Brig. Gen. Craig Maceri said the brigade supplies a command structure for a wide range of missions while enabling rapid coordination during major events, emergencies, and security operations. One primary partner is the US Marshals Service. The brigade carries the lineage of the 260th Military Police Command, inactivated in 2011, and its patch draws on the Flag of Washington, DC — three red stars and two red bars from George Washington's coat of arms — with an upright sword symbolizing protection of all who live in, work in, or visit the capital. The DC National Guard reports directly to the President of the United States.
