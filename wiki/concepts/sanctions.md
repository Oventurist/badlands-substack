# Sanctions

Sanctions, in the article's central thesis, are not a punishment imposed on misbehaving states but a scam — a deliberate instrument that creates opportunity for the [[cia|CIA]] and the shadow government while the American public is told a story about accountability.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article's subtitle states the claim flatly: "Sanctions Are a Scam."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## The stated purpose versus the alleged function

The public is told that sanctions punish a country for bad behavior; the article calls this a charade.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Cutting off trade removes a country's access to the world banking system, which is presented as punishment but which, for CIA-controlled countries, becomes a huge advantage.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) A sanctioned state becomes what the article calls a "closed financial system," able to avoid money-laundering laws and accountability entirely.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Sanctions, the author writes, promote corruption: closed systems such as [[iran|Iran]] and [[north-korea|North Korea]] allow taxpayer money to be laundered far more easily because Congress does not track the money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The CIA is said to use sanctions to launder money for shadow government operations beyond congressional oversight.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## The Clinton contradiction

The article builds its case on a timing contradiction. Under the 1994 [[agreed-framework|Agreed Framework]], [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] promised to lift sanctions on North Korea and supply oil in exchange for a halt to its nuclear program.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Five months later, in March 1995, Clinton imposed some of the toughest sanctions ever against Iran under Executive Order 12957, prohibiting United States trade in Iran's oil industry, and in May 1995 issued Executive Order 12959 prohibiting trade with Iran altogether, ending commerce that had been growing since the end of the Iran–Iraq War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Both countries were developing nuclear weapons and sponsoring terrorism, and North Korea's program was arguably more advanced — so the article asks why Clinton treated them so differently, answering: oil and money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The conclusion drawn is that Clinton and the shadow government intended to obtain Iranian oil in order to give it to North Korea.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Sanctions as an arbitrage business

The article describes how sanctions enriched [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]]: they limited his competition and forced desperate dictators into lucrative deals, letting him buy Iranian oil far below market price and sell it far above market price to another sanctioned country, pocketing the difference and becoming a billionaire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Rich bought Iranian oil in 1979 during the hostage-crisis embargo and sold it to apartheid-era [[south-africa|South Africa]], also sanctioned.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) He is described as an "inveterate sanctions-buster" who used offshore front companies and pioneered commodity swaps, including uranium-for-oil deals between South Africa, Iran and Soviet Russia in the 1980s.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article uses this to argue that sanctions did not merely fail to stop nuclear proliferation but supplied its logistics in secret.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Hostages as cover story

Related to the sanctions thesis is the article's claim that hostages have long served as a "cover story" for the CIA and shadow government, because hostages garner sympathy from the American people and thereby hide treasonous operations behind sympathetic negotiations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The 1979 Iranian hostage crisis produced the oil embargo Rich exploited, and the 2009 North Korean journalist hostages produced Bill Clinton's Pyongyang trip.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
