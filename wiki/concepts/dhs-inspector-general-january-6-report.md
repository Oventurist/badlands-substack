# DHS Inspector General January 6 report

The Department of Homeland Security inspector general's after-action report on January 6, 2021 is an April 2024 document whose once-redacted passages, released in August 2024, exposed serious [[us-secret-service|Secret Service]] failures during the Capitol riot.[1]

## Contents of the redacted passages

The redacted sections, obtained by *Just the News*, chronicle how [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]]'s escape vehicle left its post without explicit permission, leaving the vice president stranded at an increasingly violent scene at the Capitol.[1] They also detail how Secret Service agents lost contact with one another on radios and cell phones that dropped signals, and how a required explosives detection team was not deployed when Vice President-elect [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] was mistakenly whisked past a live pipe bomb just before the riot began.[1]

The report further revealed that the Secret Service had received intelligence from the [[fbi|FBI]] and the U.S. Postal Service Inspection Service ahead of January 6 warning of a "high potential for civil unrest" from individuals expected to be "heavily armed."[1] Despite that warning, the agency did not adapt its security plan from the normal five-person detail used inside the Capitol for ordinary congressional visits, leaving agents and officers undermanned as violence surrounded them.[1]

Reporting framed these failures as foreshadowing the Secret Service breakdowns at the Butler, Pennsylvania rally in July 2024; the full report, complete with the most sensitive passages, had been shared with Secret Service management and its Homeland Security overseers in April, weeks before the assassination attempt.[1] The redacted passages were belatedly shared with House and Senate oversight committees only in late August 2024.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] situated the disclosure against the work of the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]], describing it as a prime-time, highly produced, nominally bipartisan body whose report was not merely a media play: it was admitted in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]], the Fourteenth Amendment case in which Colorado litigants sought to remove [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the ballot, and the court relied on it in findings that created the "oath breaking insurrectionists" narrative.[1]

She noted that Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s communications, decision-making and leadership had been off limits to the committee, that many relevant witnesses were never called, that off-narrative testimony and evidence were suppressed, and that the committee destroyed its records shortly after completing its work.[1] The revelation that further material had been withheld until 2024 she called stunning given the impact of the committee and its report in shaping public reality, calling it on its face "gross incompetence, negligence, and high crimes" and suggesting the bureaucratic failure to remediate these shortcomings may have paved the way for the July 13 assassination attempt on Trump.[1] She also raised the possibility that the withholding of critical information from those with constitutional oversight for three years could constitute a [[devolution|devolution]] proof, prompting the question of who is actually running things.[1]
