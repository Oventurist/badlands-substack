# DOJ Lawsuit Against the District of Columbia Over Firearm Laws

In December 2025 the [[department-of-justice|U.S. Department of Justice]], acting through the newly established Second Amendment Section of its Civil Rights Division, filed a federal lawsuit against the District of Columbia's [[metropolitan-police-department-of-the-district-of-columbia|Metropolitan Police Department]] (MPD) and the D.C. government.[1]

## Claims

The suit challenges D.C. government laws and policies, arguing that the District's firearm registration laws and MPD's enforcement practices effectively ban commonly owned semi-automatic rifles and other protected weapons, violating the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] rights of law-abiding citizens.[1] It followed earlier DOJ allegations that D.C. authorities, via the MPD and D.C. Code provisions on firearm registration, were unlawfully imposing a de facto ban on AR-15s and other commonly owned semi-automatic firearms.[1]

## Statement by the Attorney General

Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] said in a statement: "Washington, DC's ban on some of America's most popular firearms is an unconstitutional infringement on the Second Amendment — living in our nation's capital should not preclude law-abiding citizens from exercising their fundamental constitutional right to keep and bear arms."[1]
