# Chemical Weapons Convention

The Chemical Weapons Convention of 1997 is the multilateral treaty that bans chemical weapons and is implemented by the [[organization-for-the-prohibition-of-chemical-weapons|Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons]].[1] Badlands commentary describes it as an augmentation of the Geneva Protocol of 1925, the instrument under which the use of chemical weapons was first outlawed.[1]

## Development and possession

Under this account, while use was prohibited from 1925, the development and possession of such weapons remained legal until 1975, when the [[biological-weapons-convention|Biological Weapons Convention]] explicitly outlawed their "development, production, acquisition, transfer, stockpiling and use," representing the first multilateral treaty to ban an entire category of weapons of mass destruction.[1]

## Application in current disputes

The convention supplies the legal basis for [[russia|Russia]]'s July 2025 request that the OPCW investigate alleged Ukrainian use of toxic munitions, including the reported storage of [[chloropicrin|chloropicrin]], a choking agent banned under the treaty.[1] It also frames the retrospective argument advanced by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] that the 1980 U.S. transfer of chemical weapons to [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] constituted a war crime, and that those same weapons later served as the justification for the 2003 invasion of Iraq.[1] Four nations reportedly remain outside the treaty regime, neither signing it nor acceding to its legitimacy.[1]
