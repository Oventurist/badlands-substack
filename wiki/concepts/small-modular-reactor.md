# Small Modular Reactor

A small modular reactor (SMR) is a compact nuclear fission reactor designed to be factory-built and deployed in series, promoted in the mid-2020s as a source of carbon-free electricity for data centers and other large industrial loads.[1]

## The 2024 tech-sector nuclear pivot

In October 2024, [[amazon|Amazon]] announced investments in small nuclear reactors just two days after a similar announcement by [[google|Google]], as both companies sought new sources of carbon-free electricity to meet surging demand from data centers and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[1] The announcements followed news that the owner of the shuttered [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island nuclear power plant]] planned to restart its reactor so that [[microsoft|Microsoft]] could buy the power for its data centers.[1] All three companies had previously invested in solar and wind generation, and said they now needed to go further to meet both demand and their own emissions commitments.[1]

Nuclear energy is treated as a climate solution because reactors do not emit the greenhouse gases produced by oil, coal and gas plants.[1] Global electricity demand was surging as buildings and vehicles electrified, with record consumption straining grids worldwide.[1] The International Energy Agency forecast that total data-center electricity consumption could exceed 1,000 terawatt hours in 2026, more than doubling from 2022; estimates suggest one terawatt hour can power roughly 70,000 homes for a year.[1]

## Argentine Nuclear Plan (December 2024)

In December 2024, Argentine President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]] made small modular reactors the centerpiece of the [[argentine-nuclear-plan|Argentine Nuclear Plan]], describing SMRs as compact nuclear units designed to supply power to commercial sectors and other large-scale operations.[2] Milei argued that "after years of stagnation, nuclear energy is making a powerful comeback," pointing to [[argentina|Argentina]]'s natural resources, skilled workforce and Patagonia's cold climate as ideal for energy-intensive technologies such as artificial intelligence, and calling nuclear "the only source that is sufficiently efficient, abundant and rapidly scalable to cope with the development of our civilization."[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described SMRs as "the leading tech" for what he called the real Green New Deal that Americans could embrace, in contrast to the wind-and-solar model he blamed for the 2021 Texas grid failure, and framed the technology as part of a broader effort to restore a "Nuclear American Dream" he argued was lost after the Eisenhower administration and the assassination of John F. Kennedy.[2] He noted that President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was filling his cabinet with influential figures from the re-emerging nuclear industry, including [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]].[2]
