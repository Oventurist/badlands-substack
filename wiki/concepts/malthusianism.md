# Malthusianism

Malthusianism is the theory that population growth is potentially exponential while the growth of the food supply is linear, so that humanity is doomed unless its growth is restrained.[1] It derives from a 1798 essay by Thomas Robert Malthus, *An Essay on the Principle of Population*.[1]

Badlands commentary describes the doctrine's restraint mechanisms as war, famine and disease, and characterizes its subscribers as elitist oligarchs who believe enforcing such restraint is their moral obligation.[1] In that framing, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change has since 1988 pursued "subversive psyops" grounded in Malthusian premises.[1] The concept was invoked in coverage of Mexican president-elect [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]], a PhD climate scientist who has contributed to the IPCC.[1]

## The anti-Malthusian counter-tradition

A November 2025 essay by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] traces Malthusianism from the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]] economists [[john-stuart-mill|J. S. Mill]] and [[david-ricardo|David Ricardo]], who elaborated the [[law-of-diminishing-returns|law of diminishing returns]] on Malthus's premises, through Marx and Engels' theory of class struggle, to the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s 1968 neo-Malthusian revival and its computer-modelled [[limits-to-growth|limits to growth]].[2] Those models entered the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] at the 1973 event that produced the [[davos-manifesto|Davos Manifesto]] and [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]]'s [[stakeholder-capitalism|stakeholder capitalism]].[2]

Ehret presents [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]] as the doctrine's most systematic refuter, and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] as carriers of what he calls America's forgotten anti-Malthusian heritage.[2] He argues that whenever people are given proper political liberties and economic opportunities, humanity has increased its carrying capacity in ways no other species can — from one billion people in 1800 to nearly eight billion today, with US life expectancy rising from about 40 to 78 years.[2] In his account, modern [[transhumanism|transhumanism]] and its talk of [[useless-eaters|useless eaters]] is the latest mutation of the same Malthusian and neo-eugenicist premises.[2]

## The neo-Malthusian revival, 1968–1972

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] traces the pressure to accept man-made global warming as undisputed reality to the Malthusian revival of 1968–1972, a short interval in which the vacuum left by the elimination of pro-development leaders such as [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], [[enrico-mattei|Enrico Mattei]], [[charles-de-gaulle|Charles de Gaulle]], [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King]] and [[robert-f-kennedy|Bobby Kennedy]] was filled by establishment figures who ushered in a paradigm shift toward "conservationism" and rejected the industrial growth ethic of western civilization.[3] The revival answered [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]]'s 1946 call for political unification in some sort of world government and for ensuring the eugenic problem be examined so that "much that now is unthinkable may at least become thinkable."[3]

## Malthus' doctrine restated

Members of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]], [[world-wildlife-fund|WWF]] and [[1001-nature-trust|1001 Club]] dubbed themselves "neo-Malthusians," after the ideology popularized by the British Empire's [[thomas-malthus|Thomas Malthus]].[3] Malthus' 1799 *Essay on the Principle of Population* held that human population grows geometrically while food production grows arithmetically, leading invariably to a crisis point of over-population — the mathematical foundation of the concept later dubbed "carrying capacity" by the authors of [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]].[3] Rather than permit populations to develop their minds and make the discoveries that would offset that crisis point, Ehret writes, Malthus and his heirs advocated eliminating the "unfit to make way for the more fit," Malthus going so far as to propose that children born beyond the desired population level "must necessarily perish" and that mortality should be facilitated rather than impeded.[3]

By repackaging these assumptions into a more complex computing system, the neo-Malthusians sought to create a shame-based movement of willful self-annihilation among an entire generation of baby boomers.[3] Ehret observes that if one assumes technological progress has ended, then a closed system of fixed limited resources will appear manageable only by a technocratic elite deciding who receives diminishing returns as the world settles into an imaginary mathematical equilibrium of sustainability.[3]
