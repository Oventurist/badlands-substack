# Individual Sovereignty

Individual Sovereignty is the principle that the individual — described as "the smallest minority" — is the irreducible unit of freedom, and that self-ownership over one's mind, labor, and property is the thing tyranny principally attacks. It was the title of a panel featuring the pseudonymous [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] author PiP at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]'s Great American Restoration Tour 5 in Deadwood, South Dakota, described with the line: "The individual is the smallest minority, and the information war is a battle of the mind. Subjugation or Sovereignty?"[1]

## The sovereignty/subjugation binary

The essay "Revolution Founded at the Crossroads" (October 31, 2024) frames sovereignty as a binary choice made by each person individually, not a condition granted collectively.[1] Because participation in any revolution is voluntary, "everyone must dig deep and decide for themselves which path they will take, when confronted with tyranny."[1] The author argues that tyranny-sponsored conditioning campaigns prevent most people from recognizing "the fullness and precarious nature of their oppression," and that where recognition does occur, conditioned fear deters action — with passivity itself functioning as consent that legitimizes tyranny and further incentivizes abuse.[1]

## Scriptural basis

The author grounds the concept in 1 Samuel 8, where the Israelites reject God's freedom and demand "a king to judge us like all the nations."[1] God's warning through the prophet Samuel is read as a catalogue of sovereignty violations: kings would conscript sons and daughters into military service, fields, factories, and kitchens — meaning "kings would demand sovereignty" — and would seize the best property and redistribute stolen wealth, reaping without sowing.[1] Kings would also demand portions of grain, wine, and sheep, which the author glosses as taxation, concluding with the warning "and you will be his servants," noting that the Hebrew root ʿeḇeḏ also carries the senses of slave, subject, and worshipper.[1] The essay summarizes: God classifies rulers enriching themselves by forcing taxes, confiscation, and labor service as slavery, an application of "Thou shalt not steal" (Exodus 20:15).[1]

Because freedom and love cannot be coerced without becoming tyranny and rape respectively, the author argues God designed humans with free will, knowing many would reject Him — making sovereignty inseparable from voluntary individual choice.[1]

## Sovereignty and money

The essay ties individual sovereignty directly to monetary sovereignty. Discussing [[bitcoin]], the author states: "When I talk about Bitcoin and sovereignty, that is the level of power that I'm projecting," adding "we are removing certain powers from the current power structures. They have abused those TRUSTs."[1] Bitcoin is presented as access to "a unique type of private property, a way to generationally secure our life's energy," and as the first effective mitigation against a hidden enemy that "steals 100% of humanity's life energy over time through explicit and implicit means."[1] Contemporary tyranny is characterized as god-kings demanding "sovereignty, worship, and taxes."[1]

The author also invokes [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2016 claim that Hillary Clinton "meets in secret with international banks to plot the destruction of US sovereignty in order to enrich these global financial powers," casting national and individual sovereignty as parallel targets.[1]
