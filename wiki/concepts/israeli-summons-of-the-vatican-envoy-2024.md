# Israeli Summons of the Vatican Envoy (December 2024)

In December 2024 the [[vatican|Vatican]]'s ambassador to [[israel|Israel]] was summoned to the Israeli Foreign Ministry after [[pope-francis|Pope Francis]] criticized the "cruelty" of airstrikes in Gaza, according to several Israeli media outlets.[1]

## Events

According to the news website Ynet, Archbishop Adolfo Tito Yllana was summoned for a conversation with Foreign Ministry Director General Eyal Bar-Tal on a Tuesday.[1] Bar-Tal condemned the pope's remarks but did not formally reprimand Yllana, the reports said.[1]

The pope had renewed his call for a ceasefire in Gaza ahead of Christmas, highlighting the civilian death toll from Israeli airstrikes: "This is cruelty. This is not war. I wanted to say this because it touches the heart," he said according to Reuters.[1] The previous month, Vatican News cited the pontiff as writing in a forthcoming book that allegations of genocide committed by the Israeli army against the Palestinians "should be carefully investigated."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the summons as an act of hubris by the Israeli government — "summoning" the Vatican "like a dog" to answer for the pope condemning the deaths of innocent people in a war, and doing so at Christmas — noting that such condemnation is standard practice for the papacy in any kinetic conflict.[1] He argued that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had placed himself in an awkward position: if the nuncio obeyed and reversed course, the optics would suggest Israel claims divine authority over the Vatican, a position he suggested some Evangelical Zionists would endorse; if the Vatican refused, it was unclear what Netanyahu was prepared to do to reprimand the pope short of the kind of bombing seen in Gaza, the West Bank, Lebanon and Syria.[1] He also noted a Netanyahu "Christmas message" addressed to Christian friends that ended with a promise of more war, concluding that "the spirit of Christmas is lost on Bibi."[1]
