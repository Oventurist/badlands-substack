# DOGE AI Deregulation Decision Tool

The **DOGE AI Deregulation Decision Tool** is an artificial-intelligence system deployed by the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] to identify and eliminate federal regulations, with the stated goal of cutting Washington's regulatory mandates in half by the first anniversary of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inauguration.[1]

## Design and scope

Detailed in a July 1 PowerPoint obtained by *The Washington Post*, the tool is programmed to review roughly 200,000 federal rules and determine which are obsolete or no longer legally required.[1] Preliminary estimates suggested that approximately 100,000 of those rules could be trimmed — primarily through automation, supplemented by limited staff input.[1] The presentation claimed the tool would save the United States trillions in compliance costs, reduce the federal budget, and attract new outside investment.[1]

## Deployment

The system had already been used to review more than 1,000 regulatory sections at the [[department-of-housing-and-urban-development|Department of Housing and Urban Development]] in under two weeks, and reportedly handled 100% of recent deregulation efforts at the [[consumer-financial-protection-bureau|Consumer Financial Protection Bureau]].[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of July 29, 2025, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] — describing herself as "deregulation's biggest fan" — nonetheless raised concerns about delegating governance decisions to machines, invoking the IBM maxim from a 1970s-era presentation: "A computer can never be held accountable, therefore a computer must never make a management decision."[1] She linked the tool to the broader use of AI in decision-making by institutions such as [[mckinsey|McKinsey]], the "world's strategy firm," and concluded that "reality collapse is accelerating."[1] The commentary also referenced the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] program Badlands Book Club, co-hosted with [[canncon|CannCon]], whose third season, "The Fourth Turning," was launching that evening.[1]
