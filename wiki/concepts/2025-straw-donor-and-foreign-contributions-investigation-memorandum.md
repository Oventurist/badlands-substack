# 2025 Straw Donor and Foreign Contributions Investigation Memorandum

The Investigation into Unlawful "Straw Donor" and Foreign Contributions in American Elections is a presidential memorandum issued by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] in April 2025 directing federal law enforcement to investigate the alleged laundering of political contributions through online fundraising platforms.[1]

## Legal basis

The memorandum grounds itself in federal law at 52 U.S.C. 30121 and 30122, which prohibit making political contributions in the name of another person and prohibit contributions by foreign nationals.[1]

## Findings and allegations

The document states that, notwithstanding those statutes, press reports and investigations by congressional committees had generated "extremely troubling evidence" that online fundraising platforms were willing participants in schemes to launder excessive and prohibited contributions to political candidates and committees.[1] The central concern described is that malign actors evade federal source and amount limits by breaking large contributions from a single source into many smaller contributions nominally attributed to numerous other individuals — potentially without the consent or even the knowledge of those putative contributors.[1]

## Directives

Declaring that such activities undermine the integrity of the electoral process, the memorandum directs the Attorney General, in consultation with the Secretary of the Treasury, to use all lawful authority to investigate allegations of unlawful use of online fundraising platforms to make "straw" or "dummy" contributions or foreign contributions, and to take all appropriate enforcement action.[1] The Attorney General is further directed to report back to the president through the Counsel to the President within 180 days of the memorandum's date.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] called the memorandum "YUGE," describing it as a planned rollout he had previewed at [[gart|GART]] Vegas the previous month.[1] He predicted that many more fundraising platforms would be swept into what he termed the [[actblue|ActBlue]] memorandum — naming [[winred|WinRed]] as an almost certain example — and argued the action spans both sides of the aisle and would benefit grassroots political candidates.[1]
