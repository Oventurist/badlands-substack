# Bill C-16

Bill C-16 is the 2016 Canadian legislative measure, advanced under the government of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]], that amended federal human rights legislation to add gender identity and expression as protected grounds, making [[gender-ideology|gender ideology]] a national legal framework across [[canada|Canada]].[1] A February 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] treats the amendment as the decisive moment at which gender ideology "became law."[1]

## Background

The path to the federal amendment began in the provinces and territories. The Northwest Territories became the first Canadian jurisdiction to specifically include "gender identity" as a forbidden basis of discrimination in its human rights legislation in 2002, which the essay describes as the first departure from the narrower project of shielding same-sex couples from prejudice.[1] Fourteen years of what the author calls well-crafted propaganda followed before the federal change.[1]

The shift is credited in large part to [[ontario|Ontario]], whose policy work through the [[ontario-human-rights-commission|Ontario Human Rights Commission]] supplied the template — including, per the article, treating the use of wrong pronouns as a crime.[1] See [[canadian-human-rights-act|Canadian Human Rights Act]] and [[speech-laws|speech laws]].[1]

## Criticism

Deschamps argues the implications of the change were obscured for Canadians by a psyop conflating gender ideology with gay rights, and that the public's desire to help and do the right thing was weaponized by the government.[1] He characterizes laws requiring citizens to affirm the gender identity of others on pain of hate-crime accusation as extreme concepts most people do not actually hold, and notes that left-wing feminists object because defining gender as a spectrum diminishes the history of women's liberation and women's identity as a class.[1] The essay asks pointedly where left-wing protest was when, in its framing, women were officially declared extinct in Canadian human rights law in 2016.[1]

The article links the legal framework to concrete fiscal consequences, citing Trudeau's authority to have Canadian taxpayers fund roughly $75,000 per person in sex-change benefits for federal employees, and describes the whole arc as decades of [[social-engineering|social engineering]] and [[lawfare|lawfare]].[1]
