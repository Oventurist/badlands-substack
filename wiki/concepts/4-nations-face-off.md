# 2025 4 Nations Face-Off

The 2025 4 Nations Face-Off was a week-long international ice hockey tournament organized by the [[national-hockey-league|National Hockey League]], featuring the United States, Canada, Finland and Sweden.[1] It used a round-robin format in which each team played every other team once, with the two top-placed teams meeting in a final to crown a winner.[1] It was described as the first best-on-best international tournament in over a decade.[1]

## USA–Canada game in Montreal

The United States played Canada on Saturday night at the Bell Centre in Montreal.[1] Badlands contributors noted that while the United States dominates most sports at the Olympic level, hockey is a unique exception, with Canada holding a decisive edge in world championship medal counts.[1]

When the U.S. national anthem began, the Canadian crowd booed and continued booing throughout.[1] The response on the ice was immediate: American player Matthew Tkachuk fought Canada's Brandon Hagel the second the match began; his brother Brady Tkachuk started a second fight two seconds after play resumed; and JT Miller started a third fight six seconds later — three fights within the first nine seconds of official play.[1] Barstool Sports called it "the best 9 seconds in hockey history."[1]

Connor McDavid opened the scoring for Canada with a backhander, but the tone had been set, and the Americans won what Barstool Sports characterized as the best hockey game in thirty years, describing Charlie McAvoy obliterating McDavid along the boards as the moment the U.S. side realized it had nothing to worry about.[1] Brady Tkachuk had called the game the biggest he had ever played in his life.[1] The segment of "Free Bird" by Lynyrd Skynyrd was played in the arena every time the United States scored.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the American victory as retribution against Canada in mock-menacing terms, declaring that the United States would now be taking "the one thing you hold more dear than your nation state … Hockey."[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] pre-empted objections that the event was mere "bread and circus," arguing the tournament's political charge — the booing of the anthem and the underdog upset — made it a genuine expression of national feeling.[1] Coverage of the game was drawn from [[barstool-sports|Barstool Sports]].[1]
