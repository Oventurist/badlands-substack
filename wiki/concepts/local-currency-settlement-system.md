# Local Currency Settlement System

The **Local Currency Settlement (LCS) system** is a bilateral payments arrangement between [[india|India]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] that allows the two countries to conduct trade directly in the Indian rupee and the Emirati dirham rather than converting through United States dollars.[1]

## Establishment

The system was established under a Memorandum of Understanding signed by the two countries in July 2023 and is facilitated by the [[reserve-bank-of-india|Reserve Bank of India]] and the Central Bank of the United Arab Emirates.[1] According to a statement by the Reserve Bank of India, the agreement is intended to facilitate "seamless cross-border transactions and payments, and foster greater economic cooperation."[1] The first test of the LCS involved the sale of 25 kilograms of gold from a UAE gold exporter to a buyer in India for about 128.4 million rupees, roughly $1.54 million.[1]

## First oil settlement

In August 2023 [[indian-oil-corporation|Indian Oil Corp.]], India's top refiner, purchased one million barrels of crude from the [[adnoc|Abu Dhabi National Oil Company]] and paid in rupees, completing the first India–UAE oil transaction settled without converting local currencies into dollars.[1] Indian reporting indicated that the LCS framework would reduce transaction costs and speed up payments between the two countries.[1]

## Significance

Coverage of the transaction, republished by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] from SchiffGold, framed the settlement as "another blow to the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]]" and to dollar dominance in global energy trade, part of the broader [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] trend among large emerging economies.[1]
