# Liberty Vote Acquisition of Dominion Voting Systems

The Liberty Vote acquisition of [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] was announced in October 2025, bringing the election technology vendor under full American ownership as the sole private holding of [[scott-leiendecker|Scott Leiendecker]], founder and chairman of [[liberty-vote|Liberty Vote]].[1] Leiendecker, a former GOP election reform advocate with a long record in election technology, became Dominion's sole owner under the arrangement.[1]

## Terms and stated commitments

The acquisition was reported to have been contingent upon Dominion ending its multiple ongoing lawsuits against prominent conservatives and One America News Network (OANN).[1] Under Leiendecker's leadership, the company said it would relocate all operations to the United States and strengthen election integrity through the adoption of independent third-party auditing standards.[1] "Liberty Vote signals a new chapter for American elections — one where trust is rebuilt from the ground up," Leiendecker said in the announcement.[1]

## Colorado reaction and decommissioning of equipment

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that on the Saturday following the announcement, the El Paso County Clerk in Colorado told constituents at a local church that Liberty Vote was working with the [[election-assistance-commission|EAC]] to decommission all old Dominion Voting System equipment so that it could not be voted on.[1] The clerk said he had learned this on a conference call of large-county clerks with the Liberty Vote CEO, with another call expected involving clerks from all counties.[1]

The clerk also stated that Idaho Labs was being considered as a third-party independent election auditor not tied to any state or federal government, that all manufacturing was returning to the United States, and that all source code was returning to the United States.[1] Ashe in America remarked that it was "crazy that they just admit that US elections were based on foreign source code," while cautioning that elections in Colorado remain centralized corruption.[1]

## The "blindsided" claim

A different county clerk reportedly told Ashe in America that the Colorado "election cabal" — [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]], Andrew Klein, and [[matt-crane|Matt Crane]] — had been blindsided by the sale.[1] She described that as remarkable given that the deal was reportedly inked in September and that Dominion and the Colorado State Department had been so enmeshed as to be indistinguishable, noting that Dominion's US headquarters was in Denver, where she had protested in 2020.[1] If the state officials genuinely did not know, she argued, that implies internal changes had already occurred; the fact that the Liberty Vote CEO was going directly to the clerks while their usual handler was allegedly blindsided was described as new.[1]

Ashe in America framed the episode with the question, "If there was a plan to restore US elections without violence, what would it look like?", adding that smurfing was being investigated in Colorado and that the campaigns reveal much about networks, interests, and money trails.[1] She concluded that the stories of the elections may matter more than the outcomes during "this season of American transformation," while allowing that only "some of it may even be real."[1]
