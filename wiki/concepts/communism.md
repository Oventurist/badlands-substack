# Communism

Communism is the political and economic doctrine under which "the community" — in practice, the government — owns the means of production and private property is abolished.[1] In the July 6, 2026 edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the term was treated as the central organizing label for the ideological threat that [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] and Republicans have described as the greatest danger facing the United States.[1]

## The 2026 political dispute

Trump and Republicans escalated attacks casting progressive Democrats and democratic socialists as communists ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]], following a series of left-wing primary victories.[1] Trump used a July 3 speech at Mount Rushmore to warn of what he called a "communist menace," linking the message to recent progressive wins.[1] Reuters reported that four progressive candidates, including three democratic socialists, won competitive Democratic primaries in New York City and Colorado, with additional progressive victories in Kentucky, New Jersey, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Texas.[1]

The Associated Press reported that experts said Trump's broad claims linking Democrats to communism are inaccurate.[1] Fox News, citing [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] senior fellow Mike Gonzalez, reported that Gonzalez warned socialist electoral gains could lead to "communists in double digits" in the House.[1]

## Badlands framing

Writing in the Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the common deflection that contemporary movements are "not real communism," arguing that communism is only ever judged "real" if it achieves utopia — and because it never has, its historical instances of starvation and mass murder are always disowned after the fact.[1] The commentary conceded that today's variant does not resemble the communist states of the past, describing the modern ideology instead as "a globalist, collectivist novel authoritarianism enabled by technology and fueled by weaponized empathy," while maintaining that "communism" remains the more useful shorthand.[1] The slogan "You will own nothing and be happy" was invoked as the contemporary expression of the abolition of private property.[1]

The piece quoted Winston Churchill's description of socialism as "the philosophy of failure, the creed of ignorance, and the gospel of envy," treating the distinction between socialism and communism as "a distinction without a difference."[1] The ideology was described as embedded in the seats of power, the airwaves, curricula at every level of education, and judicial precedent at both the federal and state levels.[1] The Brief argued that the enemy is becoming less invisible as the ideology and its adherents lose the ability to hide behind institutional legitimacy, framing the question as whether the American people will be brave enough to defeat the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]].[1]

Elsewhere in the same edition, the Brief asserted that Russia is not a communist country while [[israel|Israel]] is, citing [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]]'s Israeli premiership campaign platform of opening the marketplace to reduce government control, and claiming that Soviet oligarchs fled to Israel when the Soviet Union collapsed.[1]
