# Fear as a Control Mechanism

"Fear as a control mechanism" is the argument, set out by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay *Fake News Until Proven Real*, that the operative purpose of mainstream news narratives is not to make audiences believe a particular set of facts but to provoke an initial emotional reaction — chiefly fear and anger — which places the audience in a suggestible, quasi-hypnotic state through which it can be governed.[1]

## The mechanism

Carlson holds that the elicitation of fear is the main component of most lies the [[deep-state|Deep State]] tells the public, and that the fear is what actually performs the work of deception and control.[1] Crucially, he argues, the fear survives the exposure of the lie: even after a claim is debunked, the emotional residue remains and layers upon earlier layers of fear.[1] Where enough layers accumulate, the recipient becomes incapacitated — unable to reason or react — a condition he describes as traumatised.[1] The corollary is that if the hearer never believes the claim, even temporarily, the emotional response never occurs at all.[1]

He summarises the principle as "Fear is the fuel that powers the engine of control."[1]

## Disposability of the narrative

A distinctive feature of the argument is that the narrative itself is treated as expendable. In many cases, Carlson writes, the initial reaction of fear and anger is all the Deep State cabal is seeking; it is indifferent to whether the audience still believes the story a month or a year later, because by the time the lie is uncovered it has already moved to the next one.[1] He characterises this as a perpetual cycle of control, in which discovering that one has been lied to is of no value if one then reacts emotionally to the next story.[1]

Consequently, he argues, the lying media is not defeated when its lies eventually fall apart, but when people stop reacting emotionally at the moment the lies are first told; unearthing old lies only matters if it produces rejection of new ones from the outset.[1]

## Expiring lies

Carlson likens the Deep State to a monkey swinging between cracking branches: when one lie begins to unravel, the standard defence is to distract from it with a fresh lie about something else.[1] He argues that lies which once lasted decades — regarding WWII, the JFK assassination, 9/11, Covid and climate change — now collapse within weeks or days as more people awaken, comparing the shift to products moving from Spam- and Twinkie-like shelf lives to dairy-like ones.[1]

## Counterfactuals

The essay offers several counterfactual illustrations. Had the majority of Americans rejected the [[covid-19-narrative|Covid narrative]] from the moment it was imposed, Carlson writes, it would never have gained traction; because enough people did resist, the narrative lost steam early and the planners had to pivot to race riots, which he describes as a standby tool deployed at least once a decade.[1] He argues that Covid hysteria had been intended to last through the 2020 election.[1] Similarly, had people distrusted the 9/11 story from the moment the planes struck, the anger and fear that enabled the removal of American rights and support for multiple wars would not have been generated.[1] Had enough Americans disbelieved the reporting on the Ukraine conflict, he contends, billions of dollars and thousands of lives might have been spared, and the same reasoning is applied to the initial reports of the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]].[1] He concludes bluntly that Americans' gullibility is responsible for hundreds of thousands of deaths.[1]

Carlson also argues that only fear and anger make otherwise unbelievable stories credible, citing belief that pilots unable to handle Cessnas flew jumbo jets precisely into towers designed to withstand anything — with a third building falling untouched — and belief in a magic bullet that struck multiple people in multiple parts of the body while changing direction repeatedly.[1] He states that he himself "fell hard" for 9/11 but did not believe the Covid, Ukraine or Israel narratives for a moment.[1]
