# State Department Cuba Subversion Report (2026)

The **State Department Cuba Subversion Report** is a document released by the [[state-department|State Department]] in July 2026, titled around the theme of Cuba as "the capital of 21st century communism," alleging a nearly seven-decade Cuban campaign of subversion, espionage, and ideological warfare aimed at undermining the United States from within.[1]

## Contents

The report argues that, far from a simple economic failure, Havana operates as a sophisticated intelligence and influence machine whose founding mission remains the destruction of America through infiltration, proxy support, and turning citizens against their own country.[1] It details [[cuba|Cuba]]'s espionage network, which it says penetrated the State Department, the National Security Council, and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], alongside front groups used to recruit and radicalize Americans.[1] It highlights Havana's history of training guerrillas, backing insurgencies across Latin America, and supporting violent left-wing movements, advanced through conferences and propaganda promoting anti-Western and racial grievances.[1]

The document links Cuban networks to the George Floyd riots, the rise of [[antifa|Antifa]], and surging pro-terror activism on college campuses.[1] It states that Cuba hosts foreign military, intelligence, and terrorist installations from adversaries such as [[russia|Russia]], China, and Iran, serving as a hub connecting these forces with domestic radicals.[1] Meanwhile, the report says, the regime keeps its own population in hunger, darkness, and conditions akin to modern slavery amid a chronically failing economy, and warns that Cuba's power lies in ideological and parasitic subversion rather than material strength — a threat that has outlasted the Soviet Union.[1]

## Badlands critique

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] opened his response by pointing readers to a deep dive on the document by [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], and endorsed Paul's assessment that the report is an attempt to project the State Department's own crimes onto external factions.[1] Whatever the Cuban government's actual conduct, he argued, the narrative that the [[cia|CIA]] was a communist-bashing clan of American patriots is "Grade A fed-slop," and the CIA is better understood as an extension of the global regime and a proxy of British Intelligence.[1]

He cautioned against the reflex within the "Truth Community" of assuming that any government hostile to the United States must itself be a CIA proxy, noting that genuine foreign resistance movements have fought the CIA to retain sovereignty, some earnestly and some opportunistically.[1] Where Fidel Castro and the Cuban government fall within that paradigm, he wrote, is a worthwhile question that should not be settled by assumption, since building on a flawed foundation deepens misunderstanding.[1]

He identified what he saw as internal contradictions: the report casts a small island under US embargo since 1960 as a highly funded and sophisticated opponent capable of infiltrating the US government and dismantling American culture with little resistance, even while the CIA and figures such as Joseph McCarthy were actively hunting communists.[1] He argued the accusations levied at Cuba describe sins the CIA itself committed — Operation Gladio, Operation Condor, and other color revolution operations; the recruitment, financing, training and deployment of foreign terrorist groups including [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] and [[isis|ISIS]] under National Security Advisor Zbigniew Brzezinski among others; and the use of societal institutions, popular culture and education to domesticate American civilization.[1] That the State Department, which he treats as synonymous with the CIA, is now projecting these acts onto Cuba "reeks of desperation."[1] He closed by demanding accountability while resisting GOP attempts to seize control of these narratives and weaponize them against the American people.[1]
