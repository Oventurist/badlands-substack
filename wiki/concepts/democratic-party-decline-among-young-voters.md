# Democratic Party Decline Among Young Voters

The **decline of the Democratic Party's standing among young voters** describes the erosion of support for Democrats among Americans aged 18 to 29 that became a subject of open party concern ahead of the 2024 election cycle.[1]

## Polling and party concern

By August 2023, Democrats were calling on their 2024 candidates to reengage with young voters amid signs that the critical voting bloc might be distancing itself from the party.[1] Recent polling had found the number of young people identifying as Democrats in slight decline, while President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had seen wavering support from a demographic that played a pivotal role in his 2020 victory over former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

Adding to the concern was an analysis by John Della Volpe, polling director at the Harvard Kennedy Institute of Politics, who pointed to the party's likely struggles with voters between 18 and 29.[1] "Nearly every sign that made me confident in historic levels of youth participation in 2018, 2020, and 2022 — is now flashing red," Della Volpe wrote of 2024, adding that "the ground is more fertile for voting when youth believe voting makes a tangible difference."[1]

Cheyenne Hunt, a 25-year-old Democrat and TikTok influencer running to become the first female Gen Z member of Congress, cautioned that Democrats should not take her peers for granted.[1] "There's less of a sense of loyalty to a particular party, I think, and more of a sense of really taking a look at the system and feeling left behind and forgotten — and young people engage with passionate candidates who are going to jump in there and do the dirty work to advocate for our best interests," Hunt told [[the-hill|The Hill]].[1]
