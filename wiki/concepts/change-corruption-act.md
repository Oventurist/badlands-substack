# Change Corruption Act

The Change Corruption Act is legislation introduced in the U.S. Senate in December 2025 by Sens. [[jeff-merkley|Jeff Merkley]] (D-Ore.) and [[catherine-cortez-masto|Catherine Cortez Masto]] (D-Nev.), cosponsored by Sens. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) and Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.).[1] Its operative text states: "No United States currency may feature the likeness of a living or sitting President."[1]

The bill was written to block the U.S. Treasury's plan to issue a commemorative $1 coin bearing the image of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a decision the [[us-mint|U.S. Mint]] was expected to announce as soon as that week to coincide with the nation's 250th birthday.[1] A draft rendering of the coin circulated by the Treasury Department depicts Trump's profile superimposed over the motto "Liberty."[1] The bill's authors emphasized the historical precedent against featuring a living or sitting president on a circulating coin.[1]

Merkley said in a statement that "President Trump's self-celebrating maneuvers are authoritarian actions worthy of dictators like North Korea's Kim Jong Un, not the United States of America," adding that Americans "must reject his efforts to dismantle our 'We, The People' republic and replace it with a strongman state."[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the effort, writing that "legislators ruin everything," that Trump coins "would be awesome," and joking about the legislative "hail Mary" that would follow putting Trump's face on Mount Rushmore.[1]
