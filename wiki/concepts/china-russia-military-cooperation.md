# China–Russia Military Cooperation

China–Russia military cooperation refers to the expanding defense relationship between the People's Republic of [[china|China]] and the Russian Federation, encompassing strategic communication, joint exercises and combined naval patrols.[1]

## 2023 expansion

At the end of March 2023, roughly two weeks after [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] met in Moscow, [[chinese-ministry-of-national-defense|Chinese Defense Ministry]] spokesman Col. [[tan-kefei|Tan Kefei]] said China was ready to increase military cooperation with [[russia|Russia]], describing the aim as fully implementing the "important consensus reached by the two heads of state" and "further strengthening strategic communication and coordination."[1]

Tan said the enhanced cooperation could include additional joint exercises and patrols, citing five days of training conducted by China, Russia and [[iran|Iran]] in the Gulf of Oman during the week of 15 March 2023, which he said would help the three states "jointly carry out diversified maritime military missions."[1]

## Framing as non-alliance

Chinese officials have insisted the relationship differs from Cold War military alliances because it is "based on the principles of non-alliance, non-confrontation, and non-targeting of third parties" — a formulation reporting noted might surprise Ukrainians given the ongoing war.[1]

## Badlands commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] contrasted the deepening China–Russia coordination with what he characterised as the U.S. military's preoccupation under [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] and [[mark-milley|Mark Milley]] with pronouns, diversity, inclusion, equity and [[critical-race-theory|critical race theory]].[1] He argued the coordination between the two adversaries had in fact been developing for years, and noted that many at [[badlands-media|Badlands]] suspect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has worked out some form of arrangement with Putin and Xi, so that only "surface-level conservatives" would read the news as the beginning of the end.[1]
