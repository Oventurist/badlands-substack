# Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF)

A Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility, universally abbreviated SCIF, is an accredited enclosed area within a government or contractor building constructed to permit the handling, discussion, and storage of Sensitive Compartmented Information. SCIFs are physically hardened and shielded against electronic surveillance, access is tightly controlled, and personal electronics are typically prohibited inside.

## Role in the FBI records controversy

The March 24, 2026 Badlands Brief, Alternative Annexations & New(ish) Regime Change, reports that as many as 1.9 million pages of [[fbi|FBI]] records responsive to a [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]] lawsuit were found stored inside a SCIF at Bureau headquarters — described in the coverage as "a secret room." According to a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] filing, multiple safes within the facility hold between roughly 950,000 and 1.9 million pages. The Bureau argued that the nature of the storage means classification review will likely be required in addition to digitization, and that the whole process will take ten to twelve months.

The material reportedly includes legacy records tied largely to two closed investigations along with more recent files connected to active probes and prior Special Counsel matters. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked the premise of the disclosure, asking "How many secret rooms are there at the FBI?" and observing that "apparently there is a SCIF with a bunch of safes that hold all the secrets, but no one thought to check the safes in the secret SCIF before fulfilling FOIAs."
