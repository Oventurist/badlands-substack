# Arizona Proof-of-Citizenship Voting Law

The Arizona proof-of-citizenship voting law is a state statute strengthening documentary proof-of-citizenship requirements for voter registration, which became the subject of emergency litigation before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] during the 2024 election cycle.[1]

## 2024 Supreme Court emergency order

On Thursday, August 22, 2024, the Supreme Court partially agreed to an emergency request from the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] to revive the law.[1] In a 5–4 vote, the justices reinstated the provision requiring Arizona officials to reject state voter registration forms if the applicant did not provide proof of citizenship.[1]

The Court's three liberal justices — [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]], [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]] and [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] — dissented from that portion of the order, joined by conservative Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]].[1] On the RNC's other request, which the Court denied, those same four justices found themselves in the majority, bolstered by support from at least one of Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] or Justice [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]].[1]

RNC Chairman [[michael-whatley|Michael Whatley]] celebrated the outcome on X, writing: "A seismic win in the fight to stop non-citizens from voting — more to come!"[1] The order was reported by The Hill and carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief of August 23, 2024.[1]
