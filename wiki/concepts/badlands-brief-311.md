# Badlands Brief: Massie Faces Massive Loss as Zionists Get Twitchy

"Massie Faces Massive Loss as Zionists Get Twitchy" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack. Dated May 20, 2026, the edition carried 141 likes at the time of capture. Following the Brief's standard format, each segment pairs a summarized news item with signed commentary from a Badlands contributor — in this issue, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. The issue covers the defeat of Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] in his Kentucky primary, the deepening Russia–China relationship and an associated American industrial mobilization, a bipartisan Senate antisemitism bill, the suspension of U.S. participation in a U.S.–Canada defense board, Israeli analysis of Iranian negotiating leverage, and a bonus item on New York vaccine legislation.

## Massie's primary defeat

The Brief reports that Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] lost his Republican primary on Tuesday to Trump-backed challenger [[ed-gallrein|Ed Gallrein]], describing the outcome as another victory for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in ousting Republican critics. Gallrein, a former Navy SEAL, defeated the longtime incumbent in Kentucky's 4th Congressional District after Trump personally endorsed him, campaigned in the state, and repeatedly attacked Massie on social media as "a bad guy" and "an obstructionist and a fool." The race is described as the most expensive U.S. House primary in history.

According to the source, Gallrein campaigned on loyalty to Trump and on his military background, while Massie positioned himself as an independent "[[america-first|America First]]" voice who had opposed some Trump priorities, including certain foreign aid votes and tax legislation. Trump's influence extended beyond the House race: his endorsed candidate, Representative [[andy-barr|Andy Barr]], won the Republican nomination for the open U.S. Senate seat. Gallrein is described as heavily favored in the general election in the deeply Republican district against Democrat Melissa Strange.

In his concession speech, Massie told supporters chanting "no more wars" and "America First" that lawmakers must follow the Constitution over party loyalty, and hinted at a possible 2028 presidential run. Gallrein's victory speech began with thanks to Trump, which the Brief presents as underscoring the president's tightening grip on the Republican Party.

Commenting on the result, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] describes pronounced "libertarian rage on the internet" on Tuesday evening and recounts a local content creator friend saying he was "overdosing on black pills." She writes that "the ground is moving under alt-normie feet," and adds that "on the other side of MassieGate nothing could stop what is coming except Thomas Massie, so thankfully, what is coming has not been stopped." She speculates that the same operation may next center on [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] in her own Colorado district, where the primary is June 30, and asks whether Boebert "can stop what is coming." She also notes as happier news that [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] is out of the gubernatorial race in Georgia, suggesting this frees him to focus on what she calls his "ostensibly upcoming criminal defense," and closes with her recurring refrain that "elections are still fake."

## Sovereign alliance posturing and industrial mobilization

The second segment combines two developments. With [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] traveling to Beijing to meet [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] directly on the heels of Trump doing the same, the [[center-for-european-policy-analysis|Center for European Policy]] argues that the Putin–Xi relationship has evolved beyond a tactical "marriage of convenience" into a mature alliance rooted in a "shared consciousness." Since 2012 the two leaders have met nearly 50 times — more than any other world leader has met Xi — culminating in their February 2022 "no limits" friendship declaration days before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The analysis holds that Beijing has sustained Moscow's war effort through dual-use exports, coordinated information operations, and economic lifelines, while both powers back [[iran|Iran]] and pursue a vision privileging state power over individual liberty and seeking to reorder the international system against the West. CEPA and other think tanks are described as urging the Trump administration to abandon hopes of splitting the pair and instead strengthen democratic alliances against this "meta threat."

Simultaneously, the Brief reports that U.S. manufacturers are answering calls for a World War II–style production surge. [[ford-motor-company|Ford Motor Company]] announced it is exploring how its commercial vehicles — including F-Series and Ranger trucks and Pro Power Onboard technology — could quickly and cost-effectively supply North American and European militaries with mobility, transport, and field-power solutions, explicitly invoking its wartime record of producing hundreds of thousands of aircraft, trucks, and engines. Ford's move is said to align with broader Trump administration talks with automakers including [[general-motors|GM]] and [[oshkosh-corporation|Oshkosh]] to convert idle civilian capacity into defense output, a shift Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] has described as placing the industrial base on a "wartime footing" amid stockpiles depleted by conflicts across Eurasia.

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] interprets the convergence of Russia and China in the central narrative as granting Trump "the mandate he needs for a new era of American Acceleration." He argues that while the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] spins the Trump–Xi Beijing summit and Putin's mirrored visit as either great-power rivalry or a bid to paper over fractures, the pattern he has projected for years is "more translation and actuation than preparation." Under his framework the apparent escalation between Trump, Putin and Xi is "the ultimate cover story — the perfect translation layer" for a shared push into a decentralized, sovereign and abundant future. He describes Ford's WWII-style mobilization talk as the revival of the Detroit once known as the "Arsenal of Democracy," now "not for abstract globalist adventures, but for sovereign power projection along kinetic, technological and energetic axes."

Burning Bright frames the arrangement as the [[multipolar-alliance|Multipolar War]] in its purest form: a war the three leaders are only pretending to wage against each other while actually waging it against what he calls the Hegemon, producing "not merely the tearing down of the old Rules-Based International Order, but the rapid acceleration out of it and into a new paradigm of cooperative competition forged on explicitly nationalist grounds." He writes that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] of Trump, Putin and Xi "has inverted the script," and that dramatic headlines about a "New Axis" or the "[[new-world-order|New World Order]]" framing he says he predicted twelve days before the System applied it generate the public mandate required for the pivot. Protectionism and pragmatism, he argues, are "national-level disguises" over a single current of strategic nationalist competition, in the course of which the "European Collectorate" is "being summarily exposed as feckless and weightless."

## Senate antisemitism legislation

The Brief reports that Senators [[james-lankford|James Lankford]] (R-Okla.) and [[jacky-rosen|Jacky Rosen]] (D-Nev.) unveiled a sweeping bipartisan bill Tuesday aimed at combating rising antisemitism through new federal requirements in education, online platforms, and community security funding. The [[lankford-rosen-antisemitism-bill|legislation]] would require the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] to appoint an antisemitism coordinator and impose new regulations on colleges receiving federal funding, including mandatory non-discrimination policies and grievance processes. It would boost nonprofit security grants to $1 billion for Jewish communities and create a dedicated grant program for places of worship, and it would require online platforms to disclose how they moderate and respond to antisemitic content. The measure was more than a year in development and is backed by groups including the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] and the [[american-jewish-committee|American Jewish Committee]]. A House companion bill was expected soon, with sponsors hoping to advance the package through vehicles such as the annual defense authorization bill. Rosen, who co-chairs the Senate's bipartisan antisemitism task force with Lankford, called the effort a coordinated federal response to protect Jewish communities.

[[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responds that he has predicted this development for three years, calling the bill "the Trojan Horse that will dismantle the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]], which will lead to the destruction of the Bill of Rights, which will formally end the American Experiment launched by the Founding Fathers." He predicts wide bipartisan support across both houses of [[congress|Congress]], possibly the most widely supported bipartisan bill since the Patriot Act, and argues "that is how you know that [[israel|Israel]] controls our Congress." He notes that Representative [[tim-burchett|Tim Burchett]] appeared on the Daily Herold the previous day and told host Jon that he considers claims of disproportionate Israeli influence over Congress exaggerated, even after Jon raised concerns about the bill's First Amendment implications. He concludes that Massie's defeat will further persuade reluctant Republicans to align with what he calls the Israel agenda — "because if Thomas Massie can't beat the [[israel-lobby|Israel lobby]] in a fake election, nobody can" — and calls on readers to "become defiant... become undomesticated... become ungovernable."

## Suspension of the Permanent Joint Board on Defense

According to the source, the United States suspended its participation in the [[permanent-joint-board-on-defense|Permanent Joint Board on Defense]] (PJBD), a bilateral military consultation body with [[canada|Canada]] established in 1940. [[elbridge-colby|Elbridge Colby]], Under Secretary of Defense for Policy, announced via social media that the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] — sometimes referred to in reports as the Department of War — is pausing involvement "to reassess how this forum benefits shared North American defense." The board had served for decades as the primary forum for U.S.–Canada military and security cooperation. Colby cited Canada's failure to make "credible progress on its defense commitments."

The Brief situates the decision amid tensions over Canadian defense spending and remarks by Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]], including his [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] address declaring the U.S.-led global order over. Canada had recently met [[nato|NATO]]'s 2 percent of GDP defense target with $63 billion in investments for ships, F-35s, submarines, and Arctic infrastructure, and pledged to reach 3.5 percent by 2035, but U.S. officials viewed this as insufficient. Analysts described the pause as largely symbolic and political, with core operational cooperation — including [[norad|NORAD]] modernization, intelligence sharing, and border security — expected to continue unaffected.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] quotes reporting puzzling over why Colby made the announcement on a Monday given that Carney's Davos speech was months earlier, and speculates that "months ago, it wasn't time to pause the Permanent Joint Board," or that negotiations had been underway and Canada refused a deal. She endorses the stated posture — "Only by investing in our own defense capabilities will Americans and Canadians be safe, secure, and prosperous" — and writes that "Canada represents the global regime on our northern border," swearing allegiance to the British crown and carrying its king on its money. Until what she calls the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]] is defeated, she argues, a more hostile posture toward Canada "should not only be warranted. It should be welcomed."

## Israeli analysis of Iranian leverage

The Brief summarizes an Israeli think tank analysis arguing that [[iran|Iran]] now has greater incentive to resist U.S. demands and avoid major concessions, even if hostilities resume, following Trump's decision to pause planned renewed airstrikes. [[raz-zimmt|Raz Zimmt]], director of the Iran and Shiite Axis program at Tel Aviv University's [[institute-for-national-security-studies|Institute for National Security Studies]], said Tehran drew two conclusions from the pause, which came at the request of Gulf allies hoping to revive talks. First, the pause proves the United States and its partners fear the economic and political fallout of renewed war more than Iran does. Second, Trump's statements are unreliable, so Iran should not capitulate on core issues such as its nuclear enrichment infrastructure and should instead demand upfront guarantees: a full end to the conflict, sanctions relief or unfrozen assets, and arrangements recognizing Iranian control of the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. The analysis came amid stalled U.S.–Iran negotiations with both sides holding firm.

[[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] takes the important lesson to be that "the Israel lobby/regime is losing patience with President Trump," who he says "has brilliantly locked us into a perpetual holding pattern that allows neither peace nor war to break out." He argues the stasis has frozen the conflict but not public discourse, and that the pro-Israel side is losing ground in the court of public opinion because its go-to tactics — fearmongering, condescension, and claiming eternal victimhood — mirror those used by the Left before, during, and after the first Trump administration. He indicts NeoCons for using radical Islamic terrorism to promote fear of the Muslim world, academia for attempts to shame the public into "contrived philosophies of cultural marxism," and the Woke movement for weaponizing victimhood until "the vast ocean of sympathy they spent generations building is now a dried-out lake bed."

He describes a natural resistance in the zeitgeist provoking an authoritarian institutional response that in turn deepens the resistance, an accelerating "cognitive acceleration of the collective mind" that he says provokes what he calls the Zionist collective into anxious rage, with each intervention only shifting the Overton Window further. As his central example he cites Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]], who released a recorded statement accusing the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] of seeking an arrest warrant against him and other members of the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government, called such acts declarations of war, and explicitly declared war on the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] — effectively, in the author's reading, declaring war on the [[united-nations|UN]] and thus on the entire world. He calls this "the raving of a lunatic... the behavior of a wild animal that has been backed into the corner," and concludes that Smotrich is setting Israel up for continued conflict regardless of U.S. policy, while "it appears that President Trump intends to make peace with Iran."

## Bonus item: New York vaccine legislation

The bonus segment reports that New York Governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]] signed two bills into law on Friday, May 15, aimed at protecting state-level vaccine access and requirements amid federal policy shifts. One measure expands pharmacist authority to administer COVID-19 vaccines to children ages 2 to 18. The second requires health insurers to cover all vaccines recommended by the New York State Commissioner of Health, decoupling state policy from [[advisory-committee-on-immunization-practices|Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]] guidelines, which had recently reduced routine recommendations from 17 diseases to 11. The bills also preserve state authority over school immunization schedules, newborn vaccination protocols, and related requirements. Hochul and state health officials framed the action as safeguarding public health tools against federal changes under the Trump administration, while critics argued the laws represent overreach or resistance to national policy adjustments.
