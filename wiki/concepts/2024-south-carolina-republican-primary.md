# 2024 South Carolina Republican Primary

The 2024 South Carolina Republican presidential primary was held on Saturday, February 24, 2024, and was won decisively by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who defeated former U.N. Ambassador and former South Carolina governor [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] in her own home state.[1] The result further consolidated Trump's path to a third consecutive Republican presidential nomination.[1]

## Result and delegate context

With the South Carolina victory, Trump had swept every contest that awarded Republican delegates in the 2024 cycle, adding the state to earlier wins in Iowa, New Hampshire, Nevada and the U.S. Virgin Islands.[1] Haley, who had served as governor of South Carolina from 2011 to 2017, faced mounting pressure to withdraw from the race but insisted she was "not going anywhere," vowing to remain a candidate at least through the March 5 Super Tuesday primaries.[1] Despite holding far more campaign events in the state than Trump and arguing that the criminal indictments against him would weaken him in a general election against [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], she was unable to dent Trump's momentum.[1] Coverage at the time framed a 2020 rematch between Trump and Biden as increasingly inevitable.[1]

## Race call and AP VoteCast

The [[associated-press|Associated Press]] declared Trump the winner as polls closed statewide at 7 p.m., a call based on analysis of AP VoteCast, a comprehensive survey of South Carolina Republican primary voters.[1] The survey confirmed the findings of pre-election polling showing Trump far outpacing Haley across the state.[1]

## Election integrity commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the race was called immediately upon poll closing and that the day "was not without incident."[1] At the Satchel Ford Elementary precinct in Columbia, ballot counting had to be paused because the internet was down; counting resumed once connectivity was restored.[1] Ashe argued that the episode undercut years of official denials that voting machines were connected to the internet, writing that "no one apologized for lying, for years, about machines and the internet" and that "the goal posts move again."[1]

The brief also reported ubiquitous accounts of Democrats participating in the open Republican primary — so-called crossover voting, a subject [[canncon|CannCon]] had been discussing for some time.[1] In the Badlands framing, Democratic voters were effectively required to prop up Haley's candidacy, and even with that assistance she was being humiliated at the ballot box.[1] Ashe characterized Haley's commitment to remain in the race through Super Tuesday as "so silly, you almost have to wonder if it's real."[1]
