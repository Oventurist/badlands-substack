# The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion

*The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion* is a fabricated text purporting to document a secret Jewish conspiracy to seize control of the world. In "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler — Part 1," [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes it as a blatant forgery that "did more than any other piece of propaganda to inflame the rise of anti-Jewish racism across Europe, Russia and the USA in the decades leading up to World War 2."[1] Ehret argues that the text's renewed popularity among contemporary "conspiracy" researchers, and its use as supposed proof that Jews are the cause of all the world's evils, is what makes a direct debunking necessary today.[1]

## Occult origins and circulation

Ehret traces the Protocols' circulation not to any Jewish source but to a network of European occultists and Theosophists.[1] The Russian mystic [[yuliana-glinka|Yuliana Glinka]] (1844–1918) was a close colleague of the occultist [[gerard-encausse|Gerard Encausse]] (known as Papus), who became a trusted consultant to Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] in 1901 and introduced the superstitious Czar and his wife Alexandra — a granddaughter of Queen Victoria — to the Protocols as "proof" of an evil Jewish conspiracy to take over the world.[1]

The Russian publicist and Theosophist [[mikhail-menshikov|Mikhail Menshikov]], the first to publish the Protocols in 1902, left an account of how Glinka delivered the document to him: she invited him to her elegant apartment, spoke perfect French, informed him that she was in direct contact with the world beyond the grave, inducted him into the mysteries of [[theosophy|Theosophy]], and "finally, she initiated him into the mysteries of the Protocols."[1] An American translation of the Protocols was later sponsored by the industrialist [[henry-ford|Henry Ford]].[1]

## Documented plagiarism

Historian [[david-livingstone|David Livingstone]], in *Ordo Ab Chaos*, identifies the source text that was plagiarized to manufacture the Protocols: *Dialogue in Hell Between Machiavelli and Montesquieu*, an 1864 work by [[maurice-joly|Maurice Joly]], a protégé of [[adolphe-cremieux|Adolphe Crémieux]], head of the [[alliance-israelite-universelle|Alliance Israelite Universelle]].[1] Crémieux was also Grand Master of the Rite of Misraim and Grand Commander of the Supreme Council of France, responsible for the high degrees of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite within the Grand Orient of France; Joly, himself a Jew, was a lifelong Freemason and a member of the Rite of Misraïm. Joly's book was an attack on the political ambitions of Napoleon III, who, represented by Machiavelli, plots to rule the world.[1]

Livingstone next cites *Warrant for Genocide*, which dissected the forgery in detail: over 160 passages in the Protocols, totaling two fifths of the entire text, are clearly based on passages in Joly; in nine chapters the borrowings exceed half the text, in some three quarters, and in Protocol VII almost the entire text. With fewer than a dozen exceptions the order of the borrowed passages matches Joly's, "as though the adaptor had worked through the Dialogue mechanically, page by page," and the twenty-four chapters of the Protocols correspond roughly to the twenty-five of the *Dialogue*. Only near the end, where the prophecy of the Messianic Age predominates, does the adaptor depart from his model. The verdict quoted is unambiguous: "It is in fact as clear a case of plagiarism — and of faking — as one could well desire."[1]

## Modern revival

Ehret argues that many pro-Nazi narratives now dominating parts of the alternative media rely on the Protocols as their evidentiary keystone, citing the ten-part neo-Nazi series [[europa-the-last-battle|*Europa: The Last Battle*]] and the film *Occupied* produced by [[stew-peters|Stew Peters]].[1] He contends that this simplistic equation — that Jews are the source of all evil — logically rehabilitates every historically anti-Jewish movement, from the Crusader Knights to the Nazi movement of the 20th century, and is amplified by heavy propaganda, historical revisionism, and the genuinely destructive conduct of Israel's Likudnik elite pursuing a Greater Israel program.[1] Similar narratives of "Jewish bankers undermining the noble honest royal families of Europe" were advanced, he writes, by occultists such as [[nesta-webster|Nesta Webster]], Papus, [[winston-churchill|Winston Churchill]], [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]], [[alice-bailey|Alice Bailey]], and [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]].[1]

## Circulation in the United States

Part 5 of the series states that [[william-dudley-pelley|William Dudley Pelley]] of the [[silver-shirts|Silver Shirts]] reproduced Protocols of Zion leaflets that were mailed out through Congressional offices under the direction of [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]] during World War II.[2] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] further argues that Viereck's 1932 book The Strangest Friendship in History popularized the forgery within American conspiracy research, shaping the field for the following ninety years while diverting attention from the British imperial agencies and occultists Ehret holds responsible for the American deep state.[2]
