# Assassination of Ismail Haniyeh

The assassination of [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]], the political leader of [[hamas|Hamas]], was carried out by missile strike in late July 2024 and was widely attributed to [[israel|Israel]].[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated the killing as a potential inciting incident — "the striking of the match" — capable of igniting a wider regional war.[1]

## Location

The strike took place in Tehran, the capital of [[iran|Iran]], during the inauguration of Iran's newly elected president.[1] Because the Iranian capital during a presidential inauguration would be expected to be among the most secure locations in the country, the attack was read as an embarrassing security failure for Iran and as a particularly grievous offense likely to trigger a major retaliatory response.[1] Iran's supreme leader, [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]], ordered retaliation directly against Israel, citing the damage the strike did to Iranian national honor.[1]

## Timing

The killing occurred days after CIA director [[william-burns|William Burns]] met Qatari and Egyptian negotiators in Rome, where officials had been hopeful that a ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hamas was near.[1] Within hours of that meeting, Hamas released a statement accusing the Israelis of reneging on their promises, and the talks began to fall apart.[1] Fox News correspondent [[trey-yingst|Trey Yingst]] reported that Haniyeh had been the organization's head negotiator and that his killing indicated ceasefire talks were "basically off the table," with more than 100 hostages still inside Gaza and no diplomatic solution on the table.[1] Yingst quoted the Qatari prime minister asking, "How can mediation succeed when one party assassinates the negotiator on the other side?"[1]

DeLarme argued that assassinating one side of an ongoing negotiation calls into question whether the negotiations were ever seriously intended.[1]

## Pattern of denial

Israel did not publicly claim credit for the strike, though it did not deny it either.[1] DeLarme placed the episode in a longer pattern in which [[mossad|Mossad]], [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]], or the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] carry out operations, deny involvement, and are shielded by Western governments until substantial time has passed — citing the [[lavon-affair|Lavon Affair]] and the [[uss-liberty-incident|USS Liberty incident]] as precedents.[1] In the wake of the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]], Israel had announced it would attempt to assassinate all of Hamas's political and military leadership, so the targeting of Haniyeh itself was not a surprise.[1]

## Escalation context

DeLarme situated the assassination within a sequence of escalations following October 7: Israel's sustained response in Gaza, the deployment of American carrier strike groups into the Eastern Mediterranean to provide tactical support and deter Iran and its proxies, the outbreak of war in the Red Sea with the [[houthis|Houthis]], and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]'s readiness to strike Tel Aviv.[1] He warned that characterizations of Hezbollah as an insignificant force are highly misleading, as the group holds tens of thousands — if not hundreds of thousands — of missiles aimed at Israeli cities.[1]
