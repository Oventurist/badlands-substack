# Armed Conflict Designation Against Drug Cartels

The **armed conflict designation against drug cartels** refers to a determination by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], communicated to [[congress|Congress]] in a confidential notice in early October 2025, that the United States is engaged in a formal "armed conflict" with drug cartels that his administration had labeled terrorist organizations, and that suspected smugglers working for such groups are "unlawful combatants."[1]

## The notice to Congress

The notice was sent to several congressional committees and was obtained by *The New York Times*.[1] It added new detail to what reporting characterized as the administration's thinly articulated legal rationale for why three U.S. military strikes ordered by the president on boats in the Caribbean Sea the previous month — strikes that killed all seventeen people aboard the vessels — should be regarded as lawful acts of war rather than murder.[1]

## Legal consequences

Legal specialists cited in the reporting said that formally deeming the campaign against drug cartels an active armed conflict cemented the president's claim to extraordinary wartime powers.[1] Under international law, a state engaged in an armed conflict may lawfully kill enemy fighters even when those fighters pose no immediate threat, detain them indefinitely without trial, and prosecute them in military courts.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Jon Herold responded to the reporting by noting that he had "long said" the case of [[campbell-v-clinton|Campbell v. Clinton]] would reappear as an argument supporting Trump's wartime authority, quoting the proposition that the court "would not dispute the President on measures necessary to repel foreign aggression" and that "[t]he President alone must determine what degree of force the crisis demands."[1] Herold framed the notice to Congress as a possible vehicle for that doctrine finally being tested.[1]
