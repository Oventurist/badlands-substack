# Badlands Brief: Condemnation, Calibration, & Constitutional Boxes

"Condemnation, Calibration, & Constitutional Boxes" is an installment of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dated June 30, 2026. The edition pairs summarized news items with commentary from the contributors [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. Its main segments cover two [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] rulings — one on late-arriving mail ballots and one on presidential removal power — a disputed announcement of United States–[[iran|Iran]] talks in Doha, escalating militarization in Eastern Europe, and a [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] lawsuit against Colorado's overseas-voting rules. A bonus item reports a federal grand jury investigation into the funding network of Neville Roy Singham.

## Supreme Court upholds Mississippi's late mail ballot law

The Brief reports that on Monday the Supreme Court, rejecting a challenge brought by the Republican National Committee, ruled 5–4 that federal law does not prevent states from counting absentee ballots that are postmarked by Election Day but arrive afterward, thereby upholding Mississippi's mail ballot law. Writing for the majority, Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]] concluded that federal law sets Election Day but does not require every valid absentee ballot to be received by that date. Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] joined Barrett and the Court's three liberal justices. The ruling leaves in place Mississippi's allowance for election officials to count absentee ballots received up to five business days after Election Day, provided they were postmarked on or before Election Day.

Justice [[samuel-alito|Samuel Alito]] dissented, warning that the ruling "leaves open opportunities for voter fraud" and could weaken trust in elections. According to the source, Alito cited a 2005 bipartisan commission led by [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] and [[james-baker|James Baker]] that called absentee voting a major potential fraud risk, as well as a 2008 opinion by Justice [[john-paul-stevens|John Paul Stevens]] noting the real risk of absentee ballot fraud. Alito wrote that the decision "opens Pandora's box," creating uncertainty over whether mailed ballots are final and how states should regulate ballot handling under election-day rules.

In a separate election case, the Court asked the Trump administration to weigh in on whether it should hear a Republican appeal seeking to enforce Pennsylvania's requirement that mail-in ballots include a handwritten date on the outer envelope. That appeal challenges a lower court ruling which blocked enforcement of the requirement on the grounds that it violated constitutional protections for the right to vote.

Ashe in America's commentary argues against despair. "People are dooming out over this decision," she writes, "but if you reframe the goal (correctly) as putting the government back into its constitutional boxes, then the decision is encouraging." In her reading, a court declining to legislate from the bench is a positive development in the larger structural picture, even where the immediate outcome disappoints, particularly if advocates concede the ruling is a sound read of the facts, the evidence and the law. She argues that pressure should instead be applied to [[congress|Congress]] to pass Representative [[clay-higgins|Clay Higgins]]' mail ballot act, and that within the states the people should have the power to force state legislatures to run real elections, concluding: "We are not powerless, they just want us to think we are." The segment headline links the ruling to renewed prospects for the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]] and a federal mail-in ballot act.

## Trump, Iran and the Doha meeting

The Brief reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said on Monday that Iran had requested a meeting with the United States and announced that talks would take place Tuesday in Doha, [[qatar|Qatar]]. The [[white-house|White House]] said Special Envoy Steve Witkoff and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] would travel to Doha for discussions aimed at advancing implementation of the U.S.–Iran memorandum of understanding.

Iran publicly contradicted the announcement. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said no negotiations with the United States were scheduled "at any level" in the coming days, though he confirmed that an Iranian delegation would travel to Doha for technical discussions related to implementing the existing agreement. The differing statements, the source notes, followed renewed military exchanges in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] that challenged the recently established ceasefire, even as both sides continued diplomatic efforts to prevent escalation.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the episode as a return to a game of "Chicken," in which one side acts as though it is not going and then arrives at the last minute — a theory he expects to be tested given that Witkoff and Kushner are ostensibly en route to Qatar. He predicts that Trump's public relationship with Iran will improve dramatically, "even if both sides consistently exchange threats and are told that both sides are bombing one another."

## Supreme Court expands presidential removal power

According to the Brief, the Supreme Court on the same Monday handed Trump a major victory, ruling 6–3 that presidents have broad authority to remove Senate-confirmed leaders of executive branch agencies. The decision overturns a 91-year-old precedent that had allowed Congress to limit a president's firing power to cases involving "inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office." The source cites several Truth Social posts by Trump reacting to the outcome.

In a separate 5–4 decision, however, the Court declined to immediately back Trump's effort to remove [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Governor [[lisa-cook|Lisa Cook]]. The justices sent that case back to a lower court, allowing Cook to remain in her position while the legal challenge continues. The Brief notes that the ruling does not bar Trump from ultimately removing Cook, but it rejects the administration's argument that courts have little authority to review such a decision, temporarily preserving the Federal Reserve's independence as litigation proceeds.

Burning Bright's commentary situates the ruling in what he calls the "Power is Power" refrain, borrowed from *Game of Thrones* and first deployed in his 2022 "Righteous Russia" series as a translation layer for the core dialectic between the theorized [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and the Globalist Hegemon opposed to it. Turning from geopolitics to Trump's domestic mandate, he argues that one of the great paradoxes of the 2026 narrative cycle is that audiences are told to prize the results of the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|midterm elections]] above all else, while Trump himself has been demonstrating that Congress — including what he calls the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] GOP — acts not merely as a retardant to his agenda but is diametrically opposed to its implementation. If the GOP controls Congress and congressional control is paramount to implementing the [[maga|MAGA]] agenda, he asks, why is the President focusing on shoring up presidential authority in direct opposition to that claim? He closes with the questions "Who is the true [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]]?" and "Are they hiding in plain sight?"

## Eastern Europe and Ukraine

The Brief reports that [[germany|Germany]] is preparing legislation that could restore mandatory military service the following year if voluntary recruitment falls short, while Poland has continued strengthening security measures along its border with Germany. These developments come as European governments continue adjusting defense and border policies amid the ongoing war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]]. Meanwhile, [[russia|Russia]] claimed to be expanding military operations in western Ukraine and accused [[nato|NATO]] of supporting Ukrainian strikes on Russian airfields; Russian officials also alleged links between Ukrainian forces and Mexican drug cartels.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls the stories "so goofy" — Ukraine working with the Mexican cartels, Germany bringing back conscription, Poland about to be invaded — arguing that any one of these headlines would be surreal in isolation, but together they "form a constellation of insanity and satire that undermine the verisimilitude of the entire narrative." He concludes that Eastern Europe is making itself into a powder keg because of Ukraine, and that although the countries involved all claim to be on the same team in a race to restore European military power projection, it feels as though they are in an arms race against one another.

## RNC lawsuit over Colorado overseas voting

The Brief reports that the Republican National Committee, joined by two Colorado Republican officials, filed a lawsuit against Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] challenging a policy that allows certain U.S. citizens living overseas to register and vote in Colorado elections. The case, filed in Denver District Court, includes Douglas County Clerk [[sheri-davis|Sheri Davis]] and U.S. Representative Jeff Crank as co-plaintiffs. It targets state guidance interpreting the federal [[uniformed-and-overseas-citizens-absentee-voting-act|Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act]] (UOCAVA), which permits some citizens abroad to register in Colorado even if they have never lived in the state, so long as a parent, guardian, spouse or domestic partner previously resided there. The suit argues that this violates Colorado's constitutional residency requirements — contending that residency cannot be established through family ties and that individuals who have never personally lived in the state do not qualify as residents — and seeks to block the policy and remove affected voters from the rolls.

Ashe in America writes that while she is "always supportive of suing Jena Griswold," she is skeptical of this lawsuit because of who is bringing it. She identifies Sheri Davis as her own county clerk and "a fraud denier," recalling that when she called the clerk's office about the Konnech scandal she was told everything was fine because the vendor said there were no Chinese servers in the elections — a response she says missed the actual allegation, which concerned Chinese access rather than Chinese servers, and which she maintains the evidence supported. She criticizes Crank, the congressman from Colorado's fifth district, for believing himself a champion of election integrity because he voted for the SAVE America Act, describing a "word salad" answer he gave when asked why citizens should pay taxes and noting that his 2026 campaign has centered on expansive spending priorities. Of the RNC she remarks sardonically that it has hardly been a "super huge" advocate for the people and their elections.

She further observes that the plaintiffs brought the suit during the election period on the eve of the Colorado primary "election day," arguing that the timing alone almost ensures the suit will fail. If the suit is political bolstering brought willingly despite knowledge that it will likely fail, she asks, "isn't that an abuse of the legal process? I would say so." She concludes that establishmentarians are easy to spot by their calling cards: "A portfolio of half measures and an overt disdain for the People and their desired outcomes," and wishes Colorado readers a happy Primary Election Day that is "certain to get weird."

## Bonus: federal grand jury probes the Singham network

The bonus item reports that a federal grand jury in Manhattan is investigating China-based tech tycoon Neville Roy Singham for potential financial crimes tied to the funding of socialist, communist and Marxist organizations across the United States over the past decade. The probe was initiated by U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton for the Southern District of New York and authorized by Acting U.S. Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]], focusing on possible wire fraud, bank fraud, money laundering and related violations. Prosecutors have issued subpoenas for bank records and financial documents from organizations in the network. The grand jury action followed a [[fox-news|Fox News]] Digital investigation in mid-March that described how Singham routed hundreds of millions of dollars from his Shanghai base into U.S. nonprofits, media outlets and activist groups promoting identity politics, sectarian division and socialist causes.

According to the source, Singham allegedly moved approximately $278 million from Shanghai through three primary channels: $164,040,000 to Mutod LLC, a now-defunct shell company established in 2017 in Chicago; $110,376,701 to the GS Donor Advised Philanthropy Fund For Wealth Management Inc., Goldman Sachs' philanthropy arm in New York City; and $3,500,000 to Likewise Conceptions LLC, another now-defunct shell set up in 2017 in Crystal Lake, Illinois.

These entities are said to have directed funds into six key nonprofits: $167,540,000 to People's Support Foundation Ltd., a Chicago-based 501(c)(3) with Singham's wife on the board; $68,748,701 to Justice and Education Fund Inc., a New York 501(c)(3) with self-described communists on the board; $22,440,000 to People's Forum Inc, a New York 501(c)(3); $16,760,000 to Tricontinental Ltd, a Massachusetts 501(c)(3) founded by Marxist activist Vijay Prashad; $1,330,000 to CodePink Women For Peace, founded by Singham's wife and ally Medea Benjamin; and $1,098,000 to Breakthrough BT Media Inc., a pro-communist outlet linked to the network of longtime activist Brian Becker. The Brief states that these nonprofits funneled at least $223 million, plus additional support, into a broader global network including the People's Welfare Association, a 501(c)(4) channeling grants worldwide, the ANSWER Coalition, the Party for Socialism and Liberation, and various unidentified groups.

Finally, the source reports that Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] met with Goldman Sachs Chairman and CEO David Solomon earlier in the year in New York, reportedly delivering a direct message urging cooperation with investigators and warning of potential scrutiny over the philanthropy fund's role in moving Singham's money.
