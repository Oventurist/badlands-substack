# Menthol Cigarette Ban

The proposed federal ban on menthol cigarettes was a public-health rule advanced by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration and then shelved in April 2024 after the [[white-house|White House]] weighed its potential health benefits against the political risk of alienating Black voters in an election year.[1]

## The shelving decision

The administration announced on Friday, April 26, 2024 that it was delaying a decision on whether to impose the ban, contending it needed more time to consult outside groups; no timeline was given for revisiting the question, as Biden competed with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for votes in the November election.[1]

## Scale and demographics

Menthol cigarettes account for more than a third of all cigarettes sold in the United States each year and are predominantly used by Black and Hispanic smokers.[1] A Wall Street Journal analysis of National Survey on Drug Use and Health data found that 81% of Black smokers used menthols in 2020, compared with 30% of white smokers and 51% of Hispanic smokers.[1]

## Opposition

Some Black community leaders fought the measure, arguing that a ban would expand the illicit cigarette market and lead police to racially profile Black smokers; the [[american-civil-liberties-union|American Civil Liberties Union]] and some members of the Congressional Black Caucus expressed similar concerns.[1] Health and Human Services Secretary [[xavier-becerra|Xavier Becerra]] said the proposed ban had generated substantial feedback from the civil rights and criminal justice movements, adding that "it's clear that there are still more conversations to have, and that will take significantly more time."[1]
