# Counterinsurgency Guide

The **Counterinsurgency Guide** is a United States government doctrinal publication, hosted on the [[state-department|State Department]]'s archived 2009–2017 website, that sets out the theory and practice of counterinsurgency (COIN).[1] In [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary the document has acquired a second life as an interpretive key to contemporary American politics, because [[qanon|Q]] posted a link to it four times — in posts #4008, #4363, #4364 and #4700.[1]

## Contents as summarised in Badlands commentary

In his December 28, 2022 essay "What's Taking So Long?", [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] drew all of his definitions and block quotations from the guide.[1]

### Insurgency

The guide describes an insurgent political strategy aimed at gaining the support, or at least the acquiescence, of the population through a combination of persuasion, subversion and coercion.[1] **Persuasion** often involves promotion of insurgent ideology but can also include the provision of money, basic social services, control of land or positions of authority, with propaganda as a key element used locally, nationally and internationally to influence potential supporters, opinion leaders and opponents — designed to control community action, discredit government action, provoke overreaction by security forces or exacerbate sectarian tension.[1] **Subversion** is the action used by insurgents to penetrate, manipulate, undermine or disrupt government institutions and organizations.[1] **Coercion** can augment or replace persuasion, with insurgents intimidating government supporters or collaborators and forcing community leaders to take sides, often through violence.[1]

### Counterinsurgency

The guide defines counterinsurgency as "comprehensive civilian and military efforts taken to simultaneously defeat and contain insurgency and address its root causes."[1] Lange placed particular emphasis on the words "comprehensive" and "simultaneously," arguing that failure to grasp the simultaneity of civilian and military effort is why observers misread the pace of events.[1] The guide states that the purpose of COIN is to build popular support for a government while suppressing or co-opting insurgent movements, and that best-practice COIN integrates and synchronizes political, security, economic and informational components that reinforce governmental legitimacy while reducing insurgent influence over the population.[1]

### The four functions

- **Information** is described as the foundation for all other activities, providing the linkages that allow discrete functional elements to cooperate as an integrated whole; the collection, formulation, storage and dissemination of information are crucial in shaping perceptions of the conflict.[1] The guide adds that messages must be synchronized with deeds, cannot be mere spin, must be grounded in truth, and that failure to honor promises is extremely counterproductive — officials should therefore be cautious in making promises, should track them, and should aim at meeting and exceeding popular expectations.[1]
- **Political strategy** is called the key function, because it provides the framework of political reconciliation, reform, popular mobilization and governmental capacity-building around which all other programs are organized; the progress of a COIN campaign is only as good as the political strategy it supports.[1] An effective political strategy strengthens the government's capability and capacity to respond, and to be seen to be responding, to the needs of its people.[1]
- **Security strategy** centres on security sector reform (SSR), developing a whole system including the civil institutions that oversee security forces and intelligence services, the legal framework, and the justice institutions — prosecution services, judiciary and prisons — that implement it, with civil ownership and accountability extending to the local level and all elements of the security apparatus trusted by the population.[1] The guide notes that effective SSR, especially reform of the justice sector, may address many of the grievances that initially fueled the insurgency.[1]
- **Economic strategy** seeks a rapid and obvious improvement in quality of life and economic opportunity in areas where security operations are suppressing insurgent activity, with the aim that the population sees clear benefit in government control.[1]

### The three phases of control

The four functions together enable the affected government to control its environment, containing insurgent activity so that the population will in the long run support the government against insurgents.[1]

1. **Establishment.** The government seeks to establish control over terrain, key infrastructure, economic production assets, population movement, resources and information flow, usually needing to catch up with insurgents who have gained the initiative.[1] Establishing control requires not only military defeat of insurgents but also their political marginalization and the provision of economic and governance benefits.[1]
2. **Consolidation.** Typically the longest phase, lasting years or even decades; control over population groups and population centers matters more than control of territory.[1] Consolidation must be managed so that it progresses neither too slowly — leading to popular frustration and regression to insurgent control — nor too quickly, leading to premature fielding of inadequate local security forces or premature transition.[1]
3. **Transition.** Describes not just the transfer of authority from an intervening nation to the host nation but also the handover from indigenous military forces to local civilian authority, an essential step in normalizing the environment and ending insurgency.[1]

### Nature of COIN

The guide states that COIN integrates the full range of civilian and military agencies and is often more population-centric than enemy-centric.[1] It cautions that this does not make COIN less violent than other conflict: like any other form of warfare it always involves loss of life, is extremely difficult, is often highly controversial politically, involves a series of ambiguous events that are extremely difficult to interpret, and often requires vastly more resources and time than initially anticipated.[1]

## Use in Badlands argument

Lange argued that Q linked the guide deliberately, so that readers would understand both what the military strategy was and why it has taken so long.[1] He used the phase model to place the United States in the late consolidation phase as of December 2022 and to predict a coming transition, and used the passage on ambiguous events to explain episodes such as the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]].[1] See [[counterinsurgency|Counterinsurgency]] for the fuller treatment of that argument.[1]
