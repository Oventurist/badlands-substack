# Fox–Dominion Defamation Settlement

The Fox–Dominion defamation settlement was the April 18, 2023 resolution of the defamation lawsuit brought by [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] against [[fox-news|Fox News]], in which the broadcaster agreed to pay $787.5 million to avert a trial over its coverage of false vote-rigging claims in the 2020 U.S. presidential election.[1] Legal experts described it as the largest settlement ever struck by an American media company.[1]

## Course of the case

Dominion filed suit in 2021 and sought $1.6 billion in damages.[1] The core question was whether Fox was liable for airing false claims that the Denver-based company's ballot-counting machines had been used to manipulate the presidential election in favor of Democrat [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over then-President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The case was tried in Wilmington, Delaware; a jury had already been selected on the morning of April 18 and the trial was poised for opening statements when the two sides and the judge announced the settlement at the eleventh hour.[1]

The settlement spared Fox the prospect of having some of its best-known figures called to the witness stand and subjected to potentially withering questioning, including [[rupert-murdoch|Rupert Murdoch]], the 92-year-old chairman of Fox Corp, and on-air hosts [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] and [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]].[1]

Dominion chief executive [[john-poulos|John Poulos]] called the settlement "historic," saying that "Fox has admitted to telling lies about Dominion that caused enormous damage to my company, our employees and our customers," and adding that "truthful reporting in the media is essential to our democracy."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the *[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]]*, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the settlement as a "black pill" to be resisted rather than accepted, arguing on two grounds. First, that Fox had been the first outlet of what he termed the Media Industrial Complex to call Arizona for Joe Biden on November 3, 2020, "effectively running cover" for the rest of the anti-MAGA media machine to accelerate early and, in his view, questionable reporting of results.[1] Second, that by settling, Fox simultaneously painted [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud]] itself as a conspiracy theory while providing "narrative shielding" against future attempts at exposure.[1] His conclusion: "When someone shows you who they are, believe them."[1]

## Link to Tucker Carlson's removal

On May 9, 2023, it was revealed that the settlement was the reason former host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] had been taken off the air the previous month.[2] Two people briefed on the conversation told [[axios|Axios]] that Carlson had been told by a Fox board member that his abrupt removal was part of the settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], an account relayed by Carlson's legal team.[2] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that Carlson had been removed not for violating policy but for "casting an inquisitive light into the shadowy world of election meddling," and that the disclosure would deepen public disillusionment with claims of a fair and lawful society.[2]

## Narrative shielding interpretation

Writing days after [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s April 2023 departure from [[fox-news|Fox News]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the settlement was itself an act of [[narrative-shielding|narrative shielding]] by the network — protecting the election fraud apparatus by attempting to establish legal and psychological precedent against election reform.[3] He held that this shielding had "just been countered by the Tucker deployment," which carried "the feeling of patriot seeding and Tucker baiting," and that the combined episode exposed FOX to "Normie MAGA" as "the den of wolves it is."[3] Even as the network retained a cable ratings lead after Carlson's exit, he likened its position to "being the prettiest horse at the glue factory."[3]

## Common-ownership critique

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the suit as circular on ownership grounds, noting that [[blackrock|BlackRock]] held roughly $45.7 million in Fox stock and about $59 million in Dominion stock, so that the payout moved money "from the left pocket (Fox-BlackRock)" to "the right pocket (Dominion-BlackRock)" to create the illusion that the injured party had been made whole and to "prove" the 2020 election was fair.[4] He characterized the episode as Kabuki theater that half the country saw through, and pointed to reports that the Murdochs were looking to sell — leaving open why they wanted out and who the buyer would be.[4] The settlement, reached for $787 million as the parties were about to go to trial, preceded by days the market slide at [[fox-corporation|Fox Corporation]] that followed the [[tucker-carlson-departure-from-fox-news|Tucker Carlson departure]].[4]

## Confidentiality dispute and leak demand (May 2023)

Part of the settlement was a confidentiality obligation covering items disclosed during discovery.[5] In a letter dated Friday, May 5, 2023, lawyers for [[fox-news|Fox News]] told [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] that documents Fox had produced in the litigation "were disclosed to media organizations and published for mass consumption," and urged Dominion to "investigate and confirm that you are not the source of these improper disclosures," warning that such leakage would violate "the text and spirit of the parties agreement."[5] The immediate trigger was the publication of leaked, redacted text messages from [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], reported by [[the-hill|The Hill]].[5] Dominion denied involvement, saying "nobody associated with Dominion shared these confidential materials with the press."[5] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the episode as farce, observing that Fox had "folded a straight flush with $787M in the pot" and that Carlson's replacement program had debuted to 1.7 million viewers, barely half his average.[5]
