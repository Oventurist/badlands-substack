# Tucker on Twitter

"Tucker on Twitter" was the short-form video program launched by [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] on Twitter in June 2023 following his departure from [[fox-news|Fox News]].[1] The show became an early demonstration that a major broadcast personality could reach mass audiences on an open platform without a network distribution deal.[1]

## Launch, June 6, 2023

Carlson inaugurated the show on Tuesday, June 6, 2023, posting a roughly ten-and-a-half-minute video captioned "Ep. 1," some six weeks after [[fox-news|Fox News]] abruptly took him off the air following his final "Tucker Carlson Tonight" broadcast on April 21; at the time he was still reportedly negotiating his exit from the network.[3] The first episode opened with the [[kakhovka-dam-destruction|destruction of the Nova Kakhovka dam]] in Ukraine, which Carlson characterized as "an act of terrorism," before he criticized the "American media" who "wasted no time this morning in accusing the Russians of sabotaging their own infrastructure," and took shots at Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and former U.N. Ambassador [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] over their support for Ukraine.[3] He also asserted that aliens are real and faulted the legacy press for ignoring the story.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the debut as a classic Carlson monologue delivered not from a studio but from what appeared to be a wood-panelled clubhouse with pool cues hanging out of focus behind him.[3] She singled out the segment in which Carlson compared his use of the platform to Soviet dissidents' use of shortwave radio to find real information amid state propaganda, and noted his statement that he had been promised there were no censorial gatekeepers on the platform and would leave if that promise proved false — a remark she read as "shots fired" at [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[3] Within one hour the episode had 4.6 million views, more than the Live + SD average of his former Fox program, leading her to judge that Musk was unlikely to allow him to be censored given his value to [[twitter|Twitter]].[3]

## Audience and legal conflict

Carlson's first episode drew more than 115 million views and his second nearly 60 million.[1] Fox News responded by serving Carlson with a cease-and-desist notice over the Twitter show, but he proceeded with a third episode on June 13, 2023, timed to the day of the federal indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Miami.[1] Commentators expected the episode to be the most widely viewed news content of the day despite the wall-to-wall cable coverage on [[cnn|CNN]] and MSNBC.[1] Within a couple of hours the third episode had already accumulated five million views.[1]

## Content of Episode 3

In the third installment Carlson argued that Trump's arrest "was always going to happen" and had been "inevitable since February 16, 2016" — the date he identified as the moment Trump "made a blood enemy of the largest and most powerful organization in human history — the US federal government."[1] He located that moment in the South Carolina Republican candidates' debate, where Trump denied the existence of Iraqi weapons of mass destruction while standing beside a visibly appalled [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]].[1] "By saying that, he sealed his fate," Carlson said. "That was the one thing you weren't allowed to say, because it implicated too many people on both sides which, on this topic is really just one side."[1]

Carlson then catalogued the internal enemies Trump accumulated in office, naming [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] and [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] before delivering a scathing assessment of former Secretary of State and CIA Director [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]], whom he accused of undermining Trump's stated commitment to peace and non-intervention "at every turn."[1] He closed by asserting that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] "is doing what no US President has ever dared to do. He is using law enforcement to lock up his chief political rival," and that Trump is targeted precisely because he "dissents from Washington's longstanding pointless war agenda."[1]

## Significance to independent media

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that "Tucker off the corporate media leash is a refreshing turn of events," noting that he had begun describing permanent Washington as a [[uniparty|uniparty]] and thereby normalizing the idea that the R-versus-D framing is "a high budget psy-op."[1] She observed that the Truth Community had never been Carlson's audience — "Normies are Tucker's audience" — which is what made the reach of the Twitter show consequential, and what she believed motivated Fox's attempt to bar him from posting.[1]

## The Trump interview, August 2023

The platform's reach peaked with Carlson's interview of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], released on X on the evening of August 23, 2023, opposite the [[first-gop-presidential-debate-2023|first Republican presidential primary debate]] in Milwaukee, which Trump skipped.[2] Within hours the interview had garnered more than 150 million views, which [[american-greatness|American Greatness]] described as making it the most-watched television interview in history, breaking the record Carlson had set earlier that summer with [[andrew-tate|Andrew Tate]].[2] By midday the following day the tally had passed 200 million and was still climbing.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the interview surpassed the Tate record by at least 50 million views, and that the margin of that victory alone exceeded the roughly 24 million people who watched the GOP debate — itself reported as an unprecedented audience for a primary debate, against a prior record of 19.7 million.[2] She read the result as proof that "Trump's communication strategy is brilliant" and that the balance of power had shifted: "Mainstream media is dead. New media is now mainstream."[2] American Greatness argued the figures showed Trump's pulse on the cultural trajectory of the country in a way no rival candidate could replicate, coming as his primary polling margins exceeded fifty percentage points despite his latest arraignment.[2]
