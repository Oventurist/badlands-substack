# Russian Recognition of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan

In early July 2025 Russia became the first nation to formally recognize the [[taliban|Taliban]] government of Afghanistan, extending diplomatic recognition to the Islamic Emirate and opening a new embassy in Kabul.[1] It was the first time since the Taliban's return to power in 2021 that a major world player had gone beyond informal contacts to embrace Kabul's de facto rulers as legitimate partners.[1]

## Sequence of events

On July 1, Taliban envoy Gul Hassan presented copies of his credentials to Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Andrey Rudenko.[1] Two days later Moscow made the recognition official — diplomatically, politically, and symbolically.[1] The move followed a crucial domestic decision in April, when Russia removed the Taliban from its national list of terrorist organizations, a designation that had persisted for more than twenty years.[1] The Russian Foreign Ministry framed recognition as a pragmatic step toward security cooperation, economic dialogue, and regional stabilization.[1]

The reaction in Kabul was swift, with the Taliban welcoming the gesture as a possible catalyst for wider international engagement.[1] Even Western governments had kept indirect channels open despite harsh public rhetoric, but none had been willing to take the formal step.[1]

## Badlands commentary

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the framing of the story as Russia having "taken Afghanistan without firing a shot" was misleading, since it implied conquest or control where none existed — the event was a recognition, delivered while America was celebrating Independence Day.[1]

The accompanying commentary advanced a broader revisionist reading of the Afghan war. Drawing on a conversation with Brian Lupo of CannCon's Newsletter, who fought in Afghanistan from 2010 to 2013, it recounted Lupo's assessment that in hindsight he was not certain any of the people he fought were Taliban at all, and that they could as easily have been foot soldiers of drug cartels operating the enormous poppy fields near his forward operating base — poppy fields the commentary alleged were used by the [[cia|CIA]] to fuel the opium epidemic in America.[1]

The piece contended that it was the Taliban who had consistently sought to shut down those cartels, doing so after retaking control in 2021, and that they had driven out radical Islamist organizations including ISIS and al-Qaeda and hunted down the ISIS-K operatives responsible for the Abbey Gate bombing that killed thirteen U.S. service members during the American withdrawal.[1] Against the Republican Party and CIA portrayal of the Taliban as sub-human adversaries who might one day wage war on U.S. soil — a claim the author dismissed by noting Afghanistan's technological primitiveness and lack of an economy capable of funding such a campaign — the commentary held that the Taliban had proven more honorable than most American politicians by keeping their word to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] under the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] despite having no reason to expect his return to office.[1] The author concluded that shared honor and integrity, along with evident self-awareness and self-deprecating humor, provided a basis for earnest diplomacy.[1]
