# Logan Act

The Logan Act is the United States federal statute that prohibits private citizens, without authority of the government, from corresponding or negotiating with foreign governments in relation to disputes with the United States. Although prosecutions under it are effectively unknown, the statute recurs in American political controversy as a rhetorical instrument for accusing out-of-power political figures of unlawful diplomacy.

## Invocation against Donald Trump, August 2024

In August 2024 the Logan Act was invoked against former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after Axios reported that he had telephoned Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] on August 14 to discuss the ceasefire deal the United States was helping to negotiate between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]].[1] Netanyahu's office denied the following day that any call had taken place, and Newsweek reported that it remained unclear whether one had occurred.[1] Critics nonetheless described the alleged conversation as a "massive crime," with the actor Steven Pasquale calling it "pretty balls to the wall treason" and [[the-lincoln-project|The Lincoln Project]] saying there was "absolutely no line he won't cross."[1]

Badlands Media's August 22, 2024 news brief treated the Logan Act framing as the operative point of the story rather than an incidental detail, arguing that the prospect of prosecuting Trump for a Logan Act violation was precisely the benefit accruing to whoever had placed the story — in the author's view Netanyahu — because it promised both legal jeopardy and bad optics for Trump with little more than two months remaining before election day.[1] See [[2024-trump-netanyahu-ceasefire-call-allegation|2024 Trump–Netanyahu ceasefire call allegation]] for the full episode.[1]
