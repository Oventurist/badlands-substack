# Nuclear Energy Pivot

The "Nuclear Energy Pivot" is a framing used by Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] to describe an accelerated American turn toward fission and fusion-adjacent energy technologies, which he has written about since 2024 and which he argues was supercharged by the 2026 Iran conflict.[1]

## Argument

In Burning Bright's account, the central narrative of the Iran war supplied more than political cover for an energy realignment: it generated both the psychological and the political permission structure for the accelerated deployment of advanced reactors, the restart of shuttered facilities, the rebuilding of nuclear supply chains, and a broader renaissance in fission and fusion-adjacent technologies.[1] He argues that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been signaling this direction from the start of his second term and that his 2025 executive orders codified the shift in advance.[1]

## Relationship to the Trump Transition

Burning Bright situates the pivot within what he calls an authentic "Trump Transition" predicated on energy abundance rather than managed scarcity.[1] He identifies two mutually reinforcing fronts: first, that global energy trade has tipped toward the United States and the emerging [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]] sphere being constructed from Venezuela to Argentina; and second, the nuclear pivot itself.[1] Both are presented as downstream of the "Narrative Nuclear Disarmament" campaign that he argues is stripping the hegemon of its final credible pretext for perpetual war.[1]

He further contends that the visible conflict with [[iran|Iran]] functioned as narrative theater, pointing to Trump's claim that hundreds of ships and millions of barrels of oil continued to move through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] throughout the conflict.[1]

## Trump nuclear executive orders (May 2025)

In May 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was reported to be preparing executive orders, expected as soon as that Friday, aimed at jumpstarting the nuclear energy industry by easing the regulatory approval process for new reactors and strengthening fuel supply chains.[2] Facing the first rise in U.S. power demand in two decades, driven by the artificial intelligence boom, Trump had declared an energy emergency on his first day in office.[2] Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] described the race to develop the power sources and data centers needed for AI as "Manhattan Project 2."[2] A draft summary of the orders indicated Trump would invoke the Cold War-era [[defense-production-act|Defense Production Act]] to declare a national emergency over U.S. dependence on Russia and China for enriched uranium, nuclear fuel processing, and advanced reactor inputs.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the orders as one of the key threads of what he called the Timeline War, arguing that the most signal-bearing narratives get lost in the noise of the info war.[2] Citing a May 6 Axios report that the White House sought to hasten nuclear deployment, he described the coverage as "a narrative deployment by the machine to the machine" about the nuclear acceleration push the second administration is being built atop.[2] He contended that the legal and institutional foundations for that acceleration were laid earlier under Joe Biden, wittingly or not, enabling an energy transition that rug-pulls the WEF-style Green New Deal while replacing it with a genuinely green version capable of transforming American energy and American life.[2]

He also pointed to a Financial Times report that Germany had dropped its opposition to nuclear power in a "rapprochement" with France, reading it as the System's own admission that the Great Reset had been derailed — with the Green New Deal's failures, from Spanish blackouts to Texas grid failures, acting as the ultimate reverse indicator that turned the "energy ouroboros" back in on itself and paved the way for common-sense solutions.[2] A third headline, from Nikkei Asia, reported that China was on track to become the top nuclear power generator by 2030, which he cast as narrative help from an unlikely source and evidence of a new arms race waged for energy and abundance rather than weaponry — what he termed the "GOOD Reset."[2]

## 2024 US reactor revivals and the Polish uranium find

By spring 2024 the revival of shuttered American reactors — once considered unthinkable — had become a practical reality. A month after the United States offered $1.5 billion to restart one closed nuclear power plant, industry and government officials expressed a growing sense that it would not be the last, with [[jigar-shah|Jigar Shah]], director of the US Energy Department's Loan Programs Office, saying that "there are a couple of nuclear power plants that we probably should, and can, turn back on."[3] Shah's office had approved a loan in March 2024 to [[holtec-international|Holtec International]] to reopen the [[palisades-nuclear-plant|Palisades nuclear plant]] in Michigan, which the company had bought in 2022 intending to decommission before officials including Governor Gretchen Whitmer pressed for its reopening; Holtec planned for power production to resume in late 2025.[3]

In parallel, the Świętokrzyska Industrial Group Industria (ŚGP Industria) confirmed the presence of uranium in a deposit in the Świętokrzyskie region of southern [[poland|Poland]], with further exploratory work planned to determine the deposit's size and possible extraction methods; the company declined to disclose the exact location.[3] The [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]] estimates Poland holds roughly 100,000 tons of natural uranium, of which only 7,000 tons have so far been confirmed, and ŚGP Industria had received an exploratory concession from the Ministry of Climate and Environment for a polymetallic deposit containing uranium in the region.[3]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the cluster of nuclear headlines through his macroeconomic thesis "The Switch," which holds that although narratives about the energy transition appear catastrophic for the current establishment, the concrete measures being implemented point toward a far brighter future.[3] He argued that restarting closed plants would leave the United States and the world flush with new energy jobs while curtailing the push toward what he called inefficient and damaging solar and wind projects, asserted that the framing of nuclear energy as an existential threat had always been a psychological operation, and concluded that between a nuclear revival and the CHIPS Act the future was bright.[3]
