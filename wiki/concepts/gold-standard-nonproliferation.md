# Gold Standard (Nuclear Nonproliferation)

In U.S. nuclear diplomacy, the "Gold Standard" is the set of commitments in a [[123-agreement|123 Agreement]] under which a partner state renounces uranium enrichment and the reprocessing of spent nuclear fuel — the two pathways to weapons-grade material.[1] It is distinct from the monetary [[gold-standard|gold standard]] in currency policy.

The [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] accepted the Gold Standard when it signed its own 123 Agreement in 2009, establishing the benchmark against which later Gulf agreements are measured.[1]

The civil nuclear pact the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration prepared to send to [[congress|Congress]] in July 2026 for cooperation with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] does not incorporate the Gold Standard, and also omits the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] Additional Protocol permitting snap inspections at undeclared sites.[1] While the agreement creates a legal pathway for cooperation across the fuel cycle including enrichment, it does not require the United States to transfer the related technology or capabilities.[1] Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] has said Riyadh would seek nuclear weapons if [[iran|Iran]] obtained them, which is the central objection raised to the absence of these guardrails.[1]
