# Florida Criminal Probe of the 2016 Russia Assessment

The Florida criminal probe of the 2016 Russia assessment is an investigation being conducted by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] in Florida examining the U.S. intelligence community's response to alleged Russian election interference in 2016.[1]

## New subpoenas

The Justice Department issued additional subpoenas in the probe in February 2026.[1] People familiar with the matter said the latest subpoenas seek documents from the years following the release of the January 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|intelligence community assessment]], which concluded that [[russia|Russia]] interfered in the presidential race in an effort to help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defeat [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[1] The new demands followed an earlier round of subpoenas in November that had focused on the preparation of that assessment.[1] Sources told the Associated Press, on condition of anonymity because the demands are nonpublic, that while the first subpoenas requested documents from the months surrounding the January 2017 publication of the Obama administration assessment, the latest seek any records from the years since.[1]

## Brennan as a target

Former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], who played a central role in producing the report, has been told he is a target of the investigation.[1] His attorneys, in a December letter to the chief judge of the Southern District of Florida, labeled the probe "manufactured" and said it remains unclear what potential crimes prosecutors are investigating.[1] The Justice Department has declined to comment on the case, which is one of several criminal inquiries opened into former officials viewed as Trump adversaries, and it is not yet known whether the investigation will lead to charges.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America welcomed the expansion with the single word "accelerate," arguing that the widened document demands indicate the investigation is not merely revisiting Trump 1.0 activity but examining acts in furtherance of the alleged conspiracy in the years since.[1] She argued that from the moment any official realized the conspiracy was not grounded in fact or law, subsequent acts to further it became criminally liable, and framed the underlying conduct as undermining the President of the United States and intentionally obstructing the democratic will of the people.[1] On that reading the conduct would amount to sedition, or to treason if foreign-incentivized — an intent question that grand juries are now said to be evaluating on the evidence.[1] She noted that Brennan has occupied positions of influence or authority continuously since 9/11.[1]
