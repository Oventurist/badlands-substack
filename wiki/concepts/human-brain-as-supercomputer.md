# Human brain as supercomputer

"The human brain as supercomputer" is the central analogy of Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s April 2023 essay on the information war, in which he argues that every person carries a predictive machine between their ears whose performance has been deliberately degraded.[1]

## The analogy

Carlson holds that all human brains have some capacity for predicting future outcomes based on the information they take in — the same function he attributes to institutional supercomputers such as the one running [[blackrock|BlackRock]]'s [[aladdin|Aladdin]] — but that these "mini-super computers" can get bogged down or hacked and so fail to work to their full capabilities.[1] He notes that the brain is an amazing thing taken for granted, observing that Alexa and Siri have access to all the information on the internet yet give childlike answers to anything harder than the outdoor temperature or a request to play a song.[1]

## Degradation and remedy

Just as a computer performs poorly when it needs updates, has too many windows open, lacks memory, carries a virus or has a slow connection, Carlson argues, human minds are impaired by propaganda, drama and fear, and by bad nutrition, fluoridated water and poor sleep.[1] He asserts that those who control the world intentionally generate massive stress and confusion in order to minimise intellectual capacity.[1]

The remedy he proposes is subtractive: when the garbage is jettisoned, the mind works surprisingly well, and — in what he calls an irony — an emptied mind becomes better at recognising garbage and less likely to store more of it.[1] By contrast he describes a "woke" person as a hoarder whose brain contains so much garbage that logical function is impossible, trapped in a Catch-22 of addiction to the material that makes them mentally unwell.[1]

## Relation to machine AI

The analogy underwrites Carlson's wider claim that the information war is fought over cognition itself: if systems like [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] can be programmed to be "woke" and if advanced bots have been operating on social platforms for decades, then the contest is between institutional predictive machines and the degraded predictive machines of ordinary citizens.[1] See also [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[1]
