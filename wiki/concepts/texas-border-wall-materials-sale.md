# Texas Border Wall Materials Sale

The Texas border wall materials sale refers to the Biden administration's effort in late 2024 to dispose of unused border wall construction components, and the litigation that blocked it. On a Friday in late December 2024, a federal judge in Texas ruled to prohibit the sale of any materials intended for the border wall for the following 30 days, and additionally ordered an investigation into the disposal effort.[1]

## Background

Congress had required the Biden administration to dispose of the stockpiled materials through a measure included in the [[national-defense-authorization-act|National Defense Authorization Act]], which allows the sale of items if they are used to repair existing barriers and not to install new ones.[1] The legal challenge was led in part by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]], who acted amid reports that the administration was selling the parts; the week before the ruling, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked a Texas court to intervene.[1]

## Reaction

Trump celebrated the ruling in a Saturday Truth Social post, calling it a "major, crucial WIN for America" and calling out President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and "his cronies" for seeking to stall construction of the border wall, a central piece of his first administration.[1] He said the ordered investigation into "the illegal selling of the materials" would "expose just how corrupt and anti-American Radical Democrats are," and pledged to rebuild the wall "in order to protect our Country from violent migrant crime, fentanyl smuggling, sex trafficking, terror attacks, and other heinous, Nation ending disasters."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the outcome as Paxton preventing the sale of "the border wall we already paid for," and argued the more consequential development was the judge-ordered investigation.[1] The commentary asked why the outgoing administration would attempt the disposal at all, and why the system's many purported checks and balances failed to stop it without a court order, predicting that this investigation — unlike many others — would reach an actionable conclusion.[1]
