# Trump JFK Transparency Campaign Video (January 2024)

The JFK transparency campaign video was an advertisement released by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in early January 2024 that promised greater transparency on the assassination of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] while featuring footage of former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] meeting with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## Content

The video opens with [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s "thousand years of darkness" speech, in which he called on Americans to tell their elected officials that national policy should be based on a shared sense of morality, and is soundtracked by Aerosmith's "Dream On."[1] It features Trump's promise to obtain transparency surrounding the JFK assassination, showing footage of Kennedy being shot before highlighting the lyric "you got to lose to know how to win."[1] The ad then shows footage of Trump's "arrest" alongside a passage from Sun Tzu's The Art of War: "If you know the enemy and know yourself you need not fear the results of a hundred battles."[1] The chorus drops over images of Trump with his supporters, followed by clips of Clinton meeting with Epstein.[1] The item was reported by [[modernity-news|Modernity News]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that official campaign videos typically appeal to emotion first, and that this one was no different, but argued its outsized impact on Trump's "more anonymous base" came from its allusions to enigmatic and infamous moments in what he described as a long-running patriot plan.[1] He called Trump's promise of declassification on JFK a significant appeal to a bipartisan, multigenerational audience, and singled out the "you got to lose to know how to win" line as his favorite element — readable at face value as a reference to the 2020 election but also, in his view, speaking to deeper layers in the collective mind and recalling both the [[q-operation|Q Op]] and [[devolution|Devolution]].[1] He concluded that "Trump's winks are coming more frequently and overtly of late."[1]
