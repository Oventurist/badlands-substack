# 2024 Clean Vehicle Tax Credit Restrictions

The **2024 clean vehicle tax credit restrictions** were new [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration rules, effective January 1, 2024, that sharply reduced the number of electric vehicle models eligible for a consumer tax credit of as much as $7,500.[1]

## The rule change

Narrower eligibility criteria cut the number of qualifying models to 13 from about two dozen, according to federal data from fueleconomy.gov.[1] The new rules excluded from the credit any vehicle using battery components made by Chinese manufacturers.[1] The preceding month the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] had announced rules targeting battery components made by companies under Chinese jurisdiction or with at least 25 percent Chinese government ownership.[1]

Treasury spokeswoman Ashley Schapitl said the government had been closely coordinating with companies on the new restrictions but that some companies had yet to submit data, which could lead to additions to the list.[1] "Automakers are adjusting their supply chains to ensure buyers continue to be eligible for the new clean vehicle credit, partnering with allies and bringing jobs and investment back to the United States," she said.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] mocked the reversal — "Biden Admin: 'Use electric everything! We will give you tax credits.' Also Biden Admin: 'LoL. Nevermind.'" — noting the drop from 24 eligible models a year earlier to 13.[1] He singled out the Chinese-ownership provision as the story's most interesting facet, invoking [[just-human|Just Human]]'s observation that Biden's [[department-of-justice|DOJ]], Treasury and other agencies had continued to carry out distinctly "Trumpian" moves and mandates.[1] From what he called a 40,000-foot view, Fleuret read the rule as simultaneously stifling electric vehicles and the climate drive while delivering a blow to [[china|China]].[1]
