# Venezuelan Oil Sector Liberalization (2026)

Following the capture of President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] by U.S. forces on January 3, 2026, [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s legislature began debating a major overhaul of the nation's oil sector aimed at ending decades of rigid state control.[1]

## Legislative measures

Lawmakers approved initial measures allowing private and foreign companies greater operational autonomy, lowering royalty and tax requirements, and providing for international arbitration — a significant departure from the policies established under Hugo Chávez.[1] Acting President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] championed opening the oil industry to increased foreign investment following Maduro's ouster.[1]

## Brokerage and financial risk

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called on two of the world's largest commodity trading firms, [[vitol|Vitol]] and [[trafigura|Trafigura]], to begin brokering deals with potential buyers of Venezuelan oil.[1] Both firms had been named as defendants in a lawsuit filed by Maduro in U.S. and Swiss courts in March 2018, and both had executives arrested for corruption.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that once these brokers and their banks begin circulating money for the new deals, regulators could be obliged to file Suspicious Activity Reports if funds move in patterns inconsistent with those described in the 2018 case, with potentially severe consequences for the transnational central banking system.[1]
