# Military Lawyers as Immigration Judges Program

The military lawyers as immigration judges program is a [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] initiative, reported in the autumn of 2025, to assign [[national-guard|National Guard]] and Army Reserve attorneys to serve as temporary immigration judges following the dismissal of dozens of sitting judges.[1]

## Structure

Training for the first group of Army lawyers was scheduled to begin on a Monday in early October 2025, with a second group expected to start in the spring, according to former and current military reserve lawyers.[1] Roughly 100 Army Reserve lawyers were expected to participate, with 50 beginning a nearly six-month assignment immediately after training, according to a September 3 email to an Army Reserve attorney reviewed by the Associated Press.[1] The administration's stated ambition was to bring in as many as 600 military-trained attorneys to help decide which immigrants may remain in the country.[1]

## Context

The move came as the administration ramped up immigration arrests, straining courts that already had years-long waits for hearings; the number of pending cases had more than doubled in four years to 3.4 million.[1]

## Criticism

Advocates were alarmed by the use of military lawyers to staff the backlogged immigration courts.[1] The [[brennan-center-for-justice|Brennan Center for Justice]] argued in a September piece that authorizing 600 military lawyers to act as temporary immigration judges "would deprive immigrants of a fair hearing and further erode the line between the military and civilian government," adding that this held true "regardless of the professionalism of the military lawyers" and noting that immigration judges are specially trained administrative judges within the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] who oversee deportation hearings.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that "all the right people" were upset about the firings and questioned why critics held administrative judges in such high regard, pointing to remarks by Supreme Court Justice [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]] on specially trained administrative judges and arguing that "who the administrative judges are working for determines their definition of justice."[1]

She declined to take a position on whether the fired judges should be replaced by military lawyers, saying her definition of due process for what she called criminal invaders reduced to a single question — "Are you here unlawfully?" — and that existing law already provided processes and time windows for genuine asylum claims.[1] She argued the United States has no obligation to people who break the law to enter, evade detection, commit entitlement fraud, or commit petty and violent crimes, and then claim victimhood when caught, describing the contrary belief as "weaponized empathy" and one of the regime's most powerful tools in the information war.[1] She concluded that she did not care whether military lawyers or jurors selected for a day handled the claims, since the question required no special training, and that if the answer was yes, "the ride out of our nation should be swift."[1]
