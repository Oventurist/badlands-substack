# Philadelphi Corridor

The Philadelphi Corridor, also called the Philadelphi Route, is a nine-mile strip of territory running along the border between the Gaza Strip and [[egypt|Egypt]].[1] Diplomatic accords establish limits on the number of troops that either [[israel|Israel]] or Egypt may position in several delineated zones along and near the border, and do not authorize large numbers of Israeli troops and armored vehicles there.[1]

The corridor became the flashpoint of the February 2024 [[rafah-invasion|Rafah invasion]] crisis. In late December 2023, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said the route "has to be in our hands" if Gaza was to be effectively and permanently demilitarized.[1] In January 2024 an Egyptian official responded that "it must be strictly emphasized that any Israeli move in this direction will lead to a serious threat to Egyptian-Israeli relations."[1] Cairo subsequently warned that an Israeli move into Rafah could trigger suspension of the [[egypt-israel-peace-treaty|1979 peace treaty]].[1]

## Role in the August 2024 negotiations

The Philadelphi Corridor featured among the new conditions that Israeli security officials said would doom the 2024 ceasefire-hostage deal.[2] An updated Israeli proposal reportedly demanded that Israel remain on the Gaza–Egypt border along the corridor during the first phase of the agreement, alongside an inspection mechanism to prevent combatants moving into northern Gaza and a list of all living hostages [[hamas|Hamas]] would release.[2] None of those demands appeared in the Israeli proposal submitted on May 27 and publicly revealed by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]; Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and IDF chief [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]] told [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] the new terms would sabotage the deal.[2]

## Suspension of Egyptian–Israeli security cooperation

Badlands coverage in September 2024 noted that the 1979 Egypt–Israel peace agreement — the first such deal signed by an Arab nation, after three decades of direct warfare — was suspended in May 2024 when the IDF moved to occupy the corridor, the narrow strip of land between [[egypt|Egypt]] and [[israel|Israel]] that had always been controlled by Egypt.[3] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said in the first week of September 2024 that Israel had no intention of relinquishing the corridor back to Egypt, a position the Badlands account described as further exacerbating tensions between the two states.[3]
