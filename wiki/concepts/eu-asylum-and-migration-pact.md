# EU Asylum and Migration Pact

The EU Asylum and Migration Pact is a package of [[european-union|European Union]] legislation governing how member states share responsibility for asylum seekers and irregular migrants arriving in the bloc.[1] Under its central mechanism, member countries are required to accept a designated share of new arrivals or else pay a financial penalty for every migrant they refuse to take.[1]

## Adoption

The [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] approved the pact in April 2024.[1] The package passed largely on the strength of votes from lawmakers affiliated with the [[european-peoples-party|European People's Party]], the Socialists and Democrats (S&D), and [[renew-europe|Renew Europe]], with MEPs urged to set aside their individual criticisms and support the compromise text.[1] Some legislators on both the left and the center-right acknowledged voting for the pact despite what they viewed as significant flaws.[1] [[hilde-vautmans|Hilde Vautmans]], foreign affairs coordinator for Renew Europe, said the legislation "is not perfect but we can only make migration manageable and humane with one European solution."[1]

European Parliament President [[roberta-metsola|Roberta Metsola]] celebrated the vote as "History made," praising what she called a "robust legislative framework on how to deal with migration and asylum" and noting the effort had been "10 years in the making."[1]

## Nationalist opposition

Nationalist politicians across Europe reacted with anger, arguing the pact cedes national sovereignty to an increasingly centralized European Union.[1] [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]] of France's [[national-rally|National Rally]] wrote that "The Migration Pact organizes the tutelage and control of nations, the legal impunity of NGOs complicit with smugglers," and vowed to "put an end to the accelerated pursuit of policies to encourage and organize mass immigration" following the June 9 European elections, at which her party was expected to win the largest share of French seats.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the pact as the product of a decades-old "globalist" project of mass migration, arguing that concepts such as "climate refugees" allow supranational institutions to frame unfettered migration as a moral obligation.[1] The writer summarized the vote's effect as "Accept the newcomers, or you will pay," suggested Europeans should follow the example of [[brexit|Brexit]], and contended that the acceleration of such policies suggests "the globalists know their time is short."[1]
