# Systems Dynamics

**Systems dynamics** is the analytical discipline used throughout the [[structures-of-control-part-1-series|Structures of Control]] series to describe how influence propagates through interconnected structures.[1] It supplies the series' core vocabulary: [[feedback-loop|feedback loops]], [[node-systems-dynamics|nodes]], and the [[holon|holon]].[1]

## Core constructs

In systems dynamics as presented by the article, a holon is an entity that functions simultaneously as an independent system and as an integral component of a larger structure.[1] Nodes are critical points within a system that process inputs and produce outputs, acting as decision-making or transmission hubs that channel energy, information, or resources through a network.[1] Feedback loops arise where node outputs return as inputs, allowing the system to adapt and self-sustain.[1]

## Scaling and network theory

The series argues that isolated 2D loops interconnect to form expansive, adaptive 3D networks spanning political, economic, and cultural domains.[1] Such networks are defined by hubs of influence where inputs converge and outputs ripple outward to shape interconnected subsystems, and they thrive on interdependence in the manner of a symphony in which each node amplifies and harmonizes influence across domains.[1] This interconnection is said to make them resilient, adaptive to disruption, and difficult to dismantle.[1]

Drawing on network theory, the article states that nodes with the highest connectivity — the greatest signal density — typically hold the most influence, serving as pivotal control points.[1] The financial institutions that emerged strengthened from the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]] are offered as the exemplary case, transforming into "supernodes" that orchestrated capital flows and dictated policy.[1]

## Interdisciplinary framing

The analysis is described as bridging systems dynamics with psychology and network theory to uncover the interplay between individual awareness and broader systemic forces.[1] Alongside these, quantum mechanics, holonic structures, and spiral dynamics are named as frameworks that will guide the series in explaining how influence scales across dimensions.[1]
