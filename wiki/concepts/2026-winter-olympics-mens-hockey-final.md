# 2026 Winter Olympics Men's Hockey Final (USA–Canada)

The gold medal game of the men's ice hockey tournament at the 2026 Milan-Cortina Winter Olympics, played in Milan, ended in a 2-1 overtime victory for the United States over Canada.[1] The win gave United States men's hockey its first Olympic gold medal since the 1980 "Miracle on Ice" era, ending a 46-year drought.[1]

## Course of the game

Matt Boldy of the Minnesota Wild opened the scoring for the United States early in the first period, assisted by Auston Matthews of the Toronto Maple Leafs and Quinn Hughes.[1] Canada's Cale Makar of the Colorado Avalanche tied the game late in the second period, with 1:44 remaining, assisted by teammate Devon Toews.[1]

Roughly halfway through the third period, at about the 9:55 mark, Nathan MacKinnon of the Colorado Avalanche received a pass in the slot on a broken play with American goaltender [[connor-hellebuyck|Connor Hellebuyck]] out of position and nearly half the net open, but fired wide — a miss described as shocking for a player leading the NHL in goals that season.[1] The score remained 1-1 through regulation, with Canada outshooting the United States 42-28.[1]

[[jack-hughes|Jack Hughes]] of the New Jersey Devils scored the golden goal 1:41 into overtime, assisted by Zach Werenski of the Columbus Blue Jackets.[1] Hellebuyck finished with 41 saves in the gold medal match.[1]

## Context and commentary

The victory fell on the 46th anniversary of the 1980 "Miracle on Ice" at Lake Placid, New York, where an amateur American squad defeated the Soviet Union.[1] The United States entered the 2026 final as underdogs, as much of the NHL's talent suits up for foreign nations in Olympic play.[1] Earlier in the Games, the U.S. women's team also beat Canada in overtime for gold, capping what Badlands described as a historic weekend and one of the most successful Winter Games in American history.[1]

Badlands writers treated the result as a cultural rather than statistical event. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described bars in Denver erupting in "USA! USA!" chants, noting the awkwardness for Colorado Avalanche fans whose players — MacKinnon, Makar and Toews — played for Canada.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Canada won essentially every statistical category except the score, arguing that the difference was "grit," a quality he framed as distinctively American and as an argument against the idea that newcomers can be made American by importation alone.[1] He also credited "bold leadership," praising a president willing to talk trash before the game and a team able to back it up, tying the win to the coming [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]].[1]

The game carried an emotional dimension in the memory of the late [[johnny-gaudreau|Johnny Gaudreau]], whose family attended the Olympics and was brought onto the ice during the celebration as his jersey was paraded around the rink.[1]

## Prior meeting

The same core rosters had met a year earlier in the championship game of the 4 Nations Face-Off, which Canada won 3-2 after the United States had beaten Canada 3-1 in round-robin play — the game remembered for three fights at the opening puck drop.[1]
