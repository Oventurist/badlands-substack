# Ku Klux Klan Act of 1871

The Ku Klux Klan Act of 1871 is a Reconstruction-era civil-rights statute creating civil liability for conspiracies to deprive persons of protected rights, including the right to vote.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back) One of the three counts in [[naacp-v-useip|*NAACP et al. v. USEIP et al.*]] was brought under the Act.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

Critics of the suit argue the KKK Act count was pleaded chiefly for its reputational effect rather than its legal merit. The 2015 article [[voters-strike-back|"Voters Strike Back"]] — cited by the plaintiffs' expert witness — states plainly that "another reason to bring a KKK Act claim is for the purpose of political messaging," because "liability under the Act carries the additional stigma of conspiracy and its association with the KKK's legacy of politicalized racism," and that the Act "creates the opportunity to brand a particular ballot security group or party committee with these deeply embarrassing associations… the threat of such associations could strengthen the law's deterrent effect and help drive settlement."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

The prevailing pro se defendant wrote that having her name attached to the Klan by the country's best-known civil-rights organizations damaged her reputation, career, family, and sanity, and that the costs awarded by the court did not begin to cover that harm.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)
