# Assange Extradition Case

The **Assange extradition case** is the long-running British legal proceeding over the United States' request to extradite WikiLeaks publisher [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] to face charges under the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]].[1]

## Procedural history

District Judge Vanessa Baraitser ruled in January 2021 to release Assange from Belmarsh Prison and deny the U.S. extradition request, basing her decision on Assange's mental health, his propensity to commit suicide, and the conditions of U.S. prisons.[1] On every point of law, however, Baraitser sided with the United States.[1]

The U.S. appealed, issuing "diplomatic assurances" that Assange would not be mistreated in prison.[1] After a two-day hearing in March 2022, the High Court accepted those assurances and rejected Assange's appeal.[1] His application to the U.K. Supreme Court to hear the case was subsequently denied.[1]

Assange then applied for a fresh appeal of Baraitser's legal rulings and of the home secretary's extradition order.[1] High Court Judge Jonathan Swift rejected that 150-page application in a three-page ruling on June 6, 2023.[1] Assange filed an application to appeal Swift's decision.[1]

## February 2024 hearing

In December 2023 it was confirmed that Assange would face two High Court judges over two days on February 20–21, 2024, in London, at the Royal Courts of Justice — in what was expected to be his last appeal against extradition.[1] The hearing date was confirmed publicly by his wife, [[stella-assange|Stella Assange]].[1] In the appeal Assange sought to challenge both the home secretary's decision to extradite him and, by cross-appeal, Baraitser's adverse legal findings.[1]

## February 2024 High Court hearing

On February 20, 2024, lawyers for [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] opened his final U.K. legal challenge against extradition to the United States on spying charges, asking High Court judges Victoria Sharp and Jeremy Johnson to grant a new appeal hearing.[2] By then Assange had fought extradition for over a decade, including seven years inside the Ecuadorian Embassy in London and five years in a high-security prison.[2] Should the judges refuse, his only remaining recourse would be an application to the European Court of Human Rights, with supporters fearing he could be flown to the United States before any such intervention took effect.[2] A verdict was possible at the close of the two-day hearing but was expected to take several weeks.[2] Reporters Without Borders said the hearing marked "the beginning of the end of the extradition case," since grounds rejected by these judges could not be appealed further within the United Kingdom.[2]
