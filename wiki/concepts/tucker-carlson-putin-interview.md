# Tucker Carlson–Putin Interview

The Tucker Carlson–Putin interview was the February 2024 sit-down between American journalist [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], which dominated global attention and produced immediate political fallout on both sides of the Atlantic.[1]

During the interview Putin confirmed reporting that former British Prime Minister [[boris-johnson|Boris Johnson]] had sabotaged an early Ukrainian peace deal, saying that the Ukrainian side had signed on to some provisions and "the war would have been over long ago. 18 months ago. However, Prime Minister Johnson came, talk to us out of it and we missed that chance."[1] The claim had originally been made public by David Arahamiya, leader of Ukraine's ruling party.[1] Johnson responded with a viral video and a Daily Mail article denouncing "that ludicrous interview" as a "tissue of lies."[1]

In the United States, the interview aired the same week that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] gave a press conference on the [[hur-report|Hur report]] findings — a conference Badlands described as bizarre and as having been held in the middle of the Carlson–Putin broadcast.[1]

## Badlands coverage and reaction

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried live commentary on the interview on the night of February 8, 2024, with [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], and [[bryan-lupo|Bryan Lupo]] hosting a Rumble broadcast for viewers who missed the release.[2]

Writing afterward, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] scored his own pre-interview wish list at four out of five.[2] He had hoped Putin would address the 2014 US-backed coup against a pro-Russian president in Ukraine and the subsequent killing of ethnic Russian civilians in the Donbas by the CIA-trained [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] after two referenda were ignored; [[nato|NATO]]'s repeated violations of its pledge to refrain from eastward expansion since 1989; Russian doubts about the legitimacy of the 2020 US election and the persecution of political rivals such as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; the failure of the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]], Western sanctions, and central bankers; and successful communist subversion in the West.[2] Only the last theme went unaddressed.[2]

Putin instead went deep on the Maidan coup and [[cia|CIA]] backing of it — treating intelligence-agency-led coups as a key vector of asymmetric Western power projection — along with NATO expansionism, the petrodollar as a globalist weapon of war, and the suspect character of US elections in which states decide who appears on the ballot.[2] Burning Bright highlighted as unexpected the lengthy opening history lesson focused on the Russian people rather than the Russian Federation, and Putin's esoteric asides on peace between religions and "inherent laws," which he described as big-picture philosophizing rare among modern leaders.[2] He further argued that Putin hinted at the gravity of [[devolution|Devolution]] and the Sovereign Alliance by suggesting that conditions had changed between 2014 and 2022 in ways that permitted his moves in the Donbas.[2]
