# Maricopa County Forensic Audit

The Maricopa County forensic audit was a review of the 2020 general election in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona, commissioned by the Republican-led Arizona State Senate. Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] described it as the only forensic audit ever performed on an American election and as evidence that Arizona was the intended focal point of a broader effort to expose election fraud.[1]

## Origins

After winning a legal battle over access to tabulation machines and roughly 2.1 million ballots from Maricopa County's 2020 general election, the [[arizona-state-senate|Arizona Senate]] announced that it had hired four out-of-state firms — Wake Technology Services, [[cyber-ninjas|Cyber Ninjas]], CyFIR and Digital Discovery — to conduct a forensic audit and full hand recount, with a report expected in about 60 days.[1] Arizona was, in Lange's account, the only state legislature in the country to take substantive action on alleged fraud in the 2020 election.[1]

## Findings

[[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had carried Arizona by 10,457 votes. Media coverage concentrated on the hand recount of vote totals, which increased Biden's total by 360 votes and was widely presented as confirmation that the certified result was accurate; critics of the audit were labelled "election deniers."[1]

Lange argued that the recount was only a small component of the review, and that examination of the physical ballots produced findings of large-scale irregularity. According to Breitbart's summary of the Arizona Senate draft report, the audit identified 49,718 questionable votes of critical, high or medium severity in Volume III of the report: 34,466 in the voter history phase, 12,660 in the certified results phase and 2,592 in the ballot phase, with no questionable votes identified in the voting machine phase.[1]

The reported breakdown included 23,344 mail-in ballots counted from individuals who no longer lived at the address to which the ballot was sent (critical impact); 9,041 more ballots returned by voters than received (high impact); 5,295 voters who potentially voted in multiple counties (high impact); 3,432 more ballots cast than people shown as having voted (medium impact); 2,592 more duplicates than original ballots (medium impact); 2,382 in-person voters who had moved out of Maricopa County (medium impact); 2,081 voters who moved out of state during the 29 days preceding the election (medium impact); and 1,551 votes counted in excess of voters who voted (medium impact). A further 3,587 votes fell into a low-impact category across fourteen findings.[1] The draft report asked the state attorney general to investigate.[1]

## Aftermath

Lange wrote that the audit "revealed massive cheating" and that evidence from the election servers had still not been made public, and questioned why the state legislature had not decertified the result.[1] His answer was one of timing: he argued that the exposure of the 2020 findings was deliberately held back until after the 2022 midterms so that fraud in that cycle would also be captured, and so that the evidence would ultimately be presented in court and entered into the public record.[1]

## Later commentary

Writing in November 2022, [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] recalled that the country and the world had watched Arizona conduct an extensive audit of the 2020 election through much of 2021, remembering "all those folks in the colored t-shirts sifting through crate after crate of ballots, looking at them under a blacklight."[2] She argued the audit found myriad problems but that the mainstream media, Democrats and some Republicans responded by focusing solely on the hand recount's confirmation of the official tally while ignoring the other findings — and that the public believed them.[2]

Gillespie pointed readers to a September 2021 [[the-gateway-pundit|Gateway Pundit]] article, "BREAKING: THE LIST – Nearly 700,000 Maricopa County Ballots Identified with Issues from the Audit and Separate Canvassing Work to Date," as a recap of both the audit and the separate canvass of the county.[2] Her summary was that Arizona's electoral college votes were awarded to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on a popular-vote margin of 10,547 while the audit and canvass together identified 698,239 ballots with issues.[2]
