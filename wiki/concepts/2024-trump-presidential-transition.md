# 2024 Trump presidential transition

The **2024 Trump presidential transition** is the process by which President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s team prepared to assume control of the federal government from the outgoing [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration following the November 2024 election.[1] It drew attention in late November 2024 for the team's decision to sign only one of the three standard transition agreements.[1]

## Agreements signed and refused

After months of delay, the transition team signed paperwork with the Biden [[white-house|White House]] to begin the official transition, allowing both parties to begin arrangements for the transfer of federal agencies to the incoming administration.[1] It declined, however, to sign an agreement with the [[general-services-administration|General Services Administration]] that would have provided federal funding, office space and cybersecurity support and included an ethics pledge, saying it would instead follow its own ethics plan and raise money for the transition itself.[1] The Washington Post reported that by turning down about $7 million in federal funding, Trump would be able to raise unlimited private donations; incoming chief of staff [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] said the team would refuse foreign donors and disclose its donors publicly.[1]

The team also refused to sign an agreement with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] permitting the [[fbi|FBI]] to conduct security clearances for transition officials, which prevented Biden officials from sharing classified information with many incoming staff.[1] Under federal law, major party nominees are expected to sign both agreements before the election; [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s team did so by the deadlines, while Trump's delay sparked concerns from White House officials and lawmakers.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that MSNBC's account undercut its own thesis by conceding nominees are "expected" — not required — to sign, so Trump was bucking a norm with good cause rather than breaking the law.[1] She wrote that there was panic in Washington, that "the most corrupt people in the Republic" were accusing Trump's transition of raising corruption concerns, and pointed to General Michael Flynn's treatment as reason to doubt the premise that FBI involvement prevents corruption.[1] Citing GSA and FBI interference with the 2016 transfer of power, she framed the refusals as part of Trump's promise to root corruption out of federal institutions, and dismissed Senator [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]]'s objections about norms as entertainment.[1]

## Threats against nominees (November 2024)

During Thanksgiving week 2024, multiple nominees and appointees named to the incoming administration were targeted with bomb threats and [[swatting|swatting]] incidents, with at least four officials reporting bomb threats at their homes.[2] Incoming press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said "several" Cabinet picks were affected and credited law enforcement with acting quickly, stating that "dangerous acts of intimidation and violence will not deter us."[2] The [[fbi|FBI]] confirmed awareness of numerous such incidents and said it was working with law enforcement partners.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the campaign as terror rather than ordinary political violence, and argued that any failure to identify the perpetrators would reflect unwillingness rather than technical inability.[2]

## Interagency coordination and personnel

Incoming national security adviser [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] said on November 24, 2024 that he had met with outgoing national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and that the two administrations were "hand in glove" and "one team" during the handover, warning adversaries against attempting to play one administration off the other.[2] Days later, on November 27, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] named retired lieutenant general [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]] to a newly created post as special envoy for [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Russia.[2] Trump also used the transition period to conduct direct diplomacy, speaking by phone with Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] about migration after threatening sweeping tariffs on Mexico and Canada.[2]

Badlands commentary treated the transition as a period of narrative maneuver as much as governance, with [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] distinguishing "noise" personnel messaging from "signal" appointments such as Kellogg's, and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] summarizing the mood in Washington as "Panic in DC."[2] Democratic megadonor [[reid-hoffman|Reid Hoffman]] was reported during the same period to be weighing a move overseas out of fear of retribution.[2]

## Public approval

A CBS/YouGov poll published on 25 November 2024 found that about three in five American adults — 59% — approved of the way President-elect [[donald-trump|Trump]] was handling his transition into office, and that 55% reported feeling happy or at least satisfied with his election victory.[3] While 95% of Republicans said they felt optimistic or excited about his term, only 15% of Democrats did, and just 44% of Democrats said they were motivated to oppose him, a figure Axios attributed to exhaustion.[3] An overwhelming majority of self-identified Trump voters supported his cabinet picks, including [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and Pete Hegseth.[3] The survey followed a run of contested cabinet selections and promises of far-reaching policy change.[3]

## Standoff over ethics and transparency agreements

The transition's unprecedented delay in signing the standard trio of ethics and transparency agreements with the federal government prevented the incoming administration from having any formal contact with federal agencies, including the dispatch of policy advisers known as "landing teams."[3] It also denied the team access to cybersecurity support and secure email servers for transition work and the ability to request FBI background checks for nominees.[3] Advisers to [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] were rebuffed after repeatedly approaching the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] he had been tapped to lead.[3] Both the transition and the White House confirmed that negotiations were still underway; watchdog groups, ethics experts and former officials warned that coordination normally begins by mid-November and that the delay would leave the new administration unfamiliar with the career workforce and agency budgets, while Biden officials voiced particular concern about national security implications.[3]

## Cabinet selection and the Senate

Trump made his Cabinet announcements at a rapid clip, often over social media and frequently catching Republican senators off guard.[3] The withdrawal of [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] a week after his 13 November nomination affirmed that the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] retained its "advice and consent" power, and Trump moved within hours to name [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], filling out the remaining Cabinet posts over the following weekend.[3] Reporting indicated [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] was likely to be given a senior [[fbi|FBI]] or DOJ role, with Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] also under consideration for FBI director.[3]

## Day-one energy package

The transition team assembled a wide-ranging energy package for rollout within days of inauguration, covering liquefied natural gas export permits, expanded offshore and federal-lands drilling, repeal of electric vehicle tax credits and clean power plant standards, and symbolic approval of the [[keystone-pipeline|Keystone Pipeline]]; transition spokesperson [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the American people could "bank on President Trump using his executive power on day one."[3]

## Trump acting as president before inauguration

In late December 2024, lawmakers in both parties said that President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was seizing the reins of power early and already acting as president, while [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] kept a lower profile during the final days of his presidency.[4] Beyond dictating events on Capitol Hill, Trump made international headlines by threatening to take back control of the Panama Canal and declaring that U.S. "ownership and control of Greenland is an absolute necessity."[4]

Sen. [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] (R-Alaska) said of Trump's impact on the spending debate, with Washington only hours from a potential shutdown, "It's clear he's in charge now," adding that "Nobody is talking about, 'Where's Biden?' in any of this. Trump is in charge now, even without the election certification."[4] Some lawmakers said it at times felt as though Trump, not Biden, was wielding the veto pen as they scrambled to assemble a last-minute deal to avoid a government shutdown.[4] Republican strategist Vin Weber said that "Biden has vacated the field and Trump has filled the vacuum," noting that Trump "got a lot" in the spending fight by getting Republicans to strip more than 1,300 pages from the original deal, and calling it telling that the sitting president had little impact even with one chamber of Congress under his party's control.[4]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the coverage as evidence that the establishment sensed something afoot with what he called #OurBoyBlue, and that post-election reeling was turning to fear at the prospect that the Trump Revenge Tour was real and that they would be at its center.[4] He argued that the fear projected by each figure of the system was proportional to its proximity to the gravitational center of the coming justice campaign, and that as they reverse-engineered Biden's political travels they realized "the great trick they played on the American people was actually played on them."[4] By hook or by crook, he wrote, Biden did more to build the public mandate for what he called the American Restoration in four years than he had spent a lifetime working to prevent.[4]

## Kennedy and Gabbard added to the transition team (August 2024)

On 27 August 2024 the Trump campaign confirmed that former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had tapped [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and former Representative [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] of Hawaii to his transition team, bringing both aboard in formal roles on the heels of their endorsements.[5] Senior campaign adviser Brian Hughes said in a statement that "as President Trump's broad coalition of supporters and endorsers expands across partisan lines, we are proud that Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and Tulsi Gabbard have been added to the Trump/Vance Transition team," adding, "We look forward to having their powerful voices on the team as we work to restore America's greatness."[5] The New York Times first reported the appointments.[5]

Kennedy had all but confirmed the role in a recent interview with former Fox News host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], saying: "I've been asked to go on to the transition team, you know, to help pick the people who will be running the government, and I'm looking forward to that."[5] He had suspended his independent White House bid the previous week and endorsed Trump, a move the campaign argued would boost Trump in battleground states where Kennedy had been drawing support away from him.[5] Gabbard, who left the Democratic Party in 2022 and had since become a regular presence in conservative media, endorsed Trump on 26 August and was also assisting him in preparation for a 10 September debate with Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[5]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] contrasted the appointments with what he called the Uniparty Establishment's display of "faux bipartisanism" in support of the Harris candidacy, arguing that Trump was bringing "true unity to the American electorate" by welcoming two disaffected liberals appealing to common sense as a common goal, and that from a mass-psychological perspective the sequence could hardly have been scripted better.[5]
