# 2022 Real Disposable Income Decline

The 2022 real disposable income decline refers to a fall in inflation-adjusted household income in the United States that, according to the GDP report discussed in early 2023, was the largest measured since 1932 — the peak of the Great Depression.[1] The finding was reported by [[fox-business|Fox Business]] and amplified by [[the-economic-collapse-blog|The Economic Collapse Blog]], which argued that economic conditions were considerably worse than the public was being told.[1]

## Reported conditions

Over the preceding year, prices rose much faster than most incomes, producing a rapid decline in living standards and making it increasingly difficult for U.S. households to get from month to month.[1] The blog reported that more than a third of all U.S. adults were relying on their parents to pay at least some of their bills, but characterized the collapse in real disposable income as the more alarming indicator.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] attributed the situation to the ramifications of massive fiat currency creation during the COVID-19 pandemic, arguing that the consequences were plainly evident in grocery, gas and energy bills.[1] Year-over-year compounding [[inflation|inflation]] had produced large price increases across the board, and the higher cost of living placed significant strain on the average household budget.[1]

Patriots in Progress noted that 57% of Americans would be unable to afford a $1,000 emergency, and that Americans had not been stretched this thin since the Great Depression — an alarming statistic representing only one of many metrics pointing toward rougher waters ahead.[1]
