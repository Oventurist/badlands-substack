# 2025 Attempt to Dismiss Israel's Attorney General

In August 2025 the Israeli government voted unanimously to fire Attorney General [[gali-baharav-miara|Gali Baharav-Miara]], who was at the time prosecuting Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] for corruption.[1] The move was immediately suspended by the [[israeli-high-court-of-justice|Israeli High Court]].[1]

## Government rationale

Netanyahu and his supporters accused Baharav-Miara of exceeding her powers by blocking decisions of the elected government, including a move to dismiss the head of [[shin-bet|Israel's domestic security agency]].[1]

## Response

In a statement after the vote, Baharav-Miara called her dismissal "unlawful" and vowed that she and her office would remain faithful to the law and continue to act professionally and honestly.[1] She described the decision as "a fateful blow to the rule of law, equality before the law, human rights, and the ability of the law enforcement system to deal with government corruption."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized the vote as Netanyahu's "I am the law" moment.[1] He noted that Baharav-Miara had been appointed by Prime Minister Naftali Bennett in 2022, on a nomination submitted in November 2021 by then Justice Minister Gideon Sa'ar, who now serves as Foreign Minister under Netanyahu.[1]

The commentary held that she was being fired because she was investigating Netanyahu's administrations for corruption related to Qatar and the flow of money to Hamas that passed through Netanyahu's office, an inquiry in which several Netanyahu aides had already been arrested and charged.[1] Technically Baharav-Miara remained in post, though all departments had been instructed not to cooperate with her — the same tactic Netanyahu used against former Shin Bet Director [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]], whose firing was likewise halted by the Supreme Court pending adjudication of alleged conflicts of interest, before Bar was leveraged into resigning on June 15.[1]

The piece predicted that Baharav-Miara would be gone soon enough, joining a growing list of disgruntled former Netanyahu-administration officials who possess direct knowledge of the inner workings of his operation, and speculated that one of them would eventually acquire a grievance large enough to break ranks.[1]
