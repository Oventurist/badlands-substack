# 2021 Cuban Protests

The 2021 Cuban protests were a wave of anti-government demonstrations across [[cuba|Cuba]], described at length in the Badlands Media essay "Glimpses into Hell — Part 1."[1] The essay states that the Cuban people mobilized for the first time in decades, the last comparable coordinated demonstrations having occurred nearly thirty years earlier, in 1994.[1]

## Origins

According to Cuban American author [[antonio-garcia-martinez|Antonio Garcia-Martinez]], the movement began when disgruntled citizens started yelling at a Communist Party headquarters, chanting pro-freedom slogans, criticizing the state, and waving American flags.[1] Cuban citizens cited a multitude of grievances, though the essay charges that mainstream media framed the unrest as being solely about access to COVID-19 vaccination — a framing it calls "industrial strength spin."[1] While Cuba's economic situation had been poor for a long time, the essay argues that human-rights violations pushed the public over the edge, including poor access to medical treatment despite the country's often-praised healthcare system.[1]

## State response

When videos of the original protest spread online, others were inspired to act, prompting the state to cut internet access and impose what military jargon calls a communications blackout.[1] Authorities also began arresting protesters and firing them from their jobs.[1] Hundreds of Cubans faced charges of inciting unrest; their families feared trials without due process, and many of the protesters, mostly young people, could not obtain independent defense lawyers.[1] The Communist Party deployed [[rapid-reaction-brigades|Rapid Reaction Brigades]] to suppress dissent, and [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] openly called for confrontation, declaring that "the order to combat has been given. Revolutionaries need to be on the streets."[1] Those who criticize the party risk becoming a *desaparecido*, or [[desaparecidos|disappeared person]].[1]

## Coverage and reaction

The essay says the protests continued after Western outlets stopped reporting on them.[1] [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] issued a statement condemning not the Cuban government but the United States, urging it to lift the [[cuban-embargo|economic embargo]]; the essay calls the statement "exactly backward."[1] It similarly criticizes [[nikole-hannah-jones|Nikole Hannah-Jones]], [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]], and [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]] for defending the Cuban model, and notes that even the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] ran an article on the plight of Afro-Cubans.[1]

## Proposed remedies

[[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], as Governor of Florida, wrote an open letter to the Biden administration urging the federal government to help restore internet access to Cubans, a call echoed by FCC Commissioner [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]], who said the technology exists to break the blockade and only political will is lacking.[1] The essay points to [[loon-llc|Loon LLC]], part of [[alphabet|Alphabet]], which used balloons to bring wifi to Puerto Rico during Hurricane Maria; to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[starlink|Starlink]]; and to [[facebook|Facebook]]'s satellite internet work as possible technical solutions, while noting that Musk subsequently made promises regarding internet access to both Iran and Cuba.[1]
