# Antisemitism Awareness Act

The Antisemitism Awareness Act (AAA) is U.S. legislation that passed the House of Representatives in May 2024 by a vote of 320–91.[1] The act would codify the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]] (IHRA) working definition of antisemitism into federal civil rights law, directing the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] to apply that definition when investigating alleged antisemitic discrimination on college campuses.[1]

## Criticism

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues the IHRA definition's illustrative examples are overly broad and vaguely worded, and that the resulting ambiguity will produce inconsistent enforcement in which political bias determines what qualifies as antisemitic.[1] Because what constitutes "hatred" is a gray area typically decided by whichever party holds power, the act is presented as an instrument of [[government-sponsored-censorship|government-sponsored censorship]] that may itself stoke antisemitic sentiment, undermining the bill's stated intent.[1]

## Sponsors and the interagency blob

DeLarme notes that several of the bill's most prominent supporters emerged from what he calls the interagency blob or [[deep-state|deep state]]: Elissa Slotkin, a CIA analyst turned Democrat politician; Seth Moulton, described as both a CIA product and a Young Global Leader of the World Economic Forum; and Robert Menendez, a regular fixture in Brussels and at the Munich Security Conference.[1] The AAA is paired in the article with [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]] as the two censorship frameworks now threatening to criminalize dissent under vague hate-speech definitions.[1]
