# Antisemitism Awareness Act

The Antisemitism Awareness Act (AAA) is United States legislation that passed the House of Representatives in May 2024 by a vote of 320–91.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) The act aimed to codify the [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance working definition of antisemitism]] into federal civil rights law, directing the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] to apply it when investigating antisemitic discrimination on college campuses.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## House passage, May 1, 2024

The House passed the bipartisan measure 320–91 on Wednesday, May 1, 2024, as pro-Palestinian protests roiled colleges across the United States; twenty-one Republicans and seventy Democrats voted against it.[2] The bill would mandate that the [[education-department|Education Department]] adopt the broad definition of antisemitism used by the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]], an intergovernmental group, when enforcing anti-discrimination laws.[2] That definition describes antisemitism as "a certain perception of Jews, which may be expressed as hatred toward Jews," and lists among its "rhetorical and physical manifestations" calls for the killing or harming of Jews and holding Jews collectively responsible for actions taken by [[israel|Israel]].[2]

The legislation was introduced by Rep. [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] (R-N.Y.) and drew backing from Democratic moderates who support Israel amid its war with [[hamas]].[2] Its prospects in the Senate were unclear; asked whether the chamber would take up the bill, Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] told reporters that "we haven't seen what the House is sending us yet."[2]

## Badlands commentary on federalization

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he had been reporting on the development since September 2023 and discussing it on [[badlands-daily|Badlands Daily]] with CannCon after the October 7 attack, predicting it would reach [[congress|Congress]] and become a national movement to subvert free speech that could later be expanded to cover criticism of the U.S. government as well as Israel.[2] He argued that the IHRA had for years pressed governors and state legislatures to adopt its definition, which focuses almost exclusively on the foreign government of Israel and equates criticism of that government, or of Zionism generally, with hatred of all Jewish people — making criticism of a foreign government "hate speech" in 38 states, including Texas, Arkansas under [[sarah-huckabee-sanders|Sarah Huckabee Sanders]], South Dakota under [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], Virginia, and Florida.[2] He noted that Noem had titled her state legislation the "God's Chosen People Act," and objected that the IHRA definition treats any claim that Jewish leaders played a role in the crucifixion of Jesus as antisemitic despite the biblical account of the Pharisees.[2] He concluded that the episode showed "super based and red-pilled" Republicans weaponizing a woke reading of the Bible against the Bill of Rights, and called for scrutiny of who funds and directs the IHRA.[2]

## Criticism

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that the IHRA definition's examples are overly broad or vaguely worded, and that the resulting ambiguity will lead to inconsistent enforcement in which political biases determine what qualifies as antisemitic.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He warns the act could stoke antisemitic sentiment and thereby undermine its purported intent.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) More broadly he places the AAA within a decade-long effort by the interagency [[deep-state|blob]] to secure [[government-sponsored-censorship|government-sponsored censorship]] under a [[hate-speech|hate speech]] pretext.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme notes that several of the bill's most prominent supporters had intelligence or transnational-institution backgrounds, naming Elissa Slotkin, a former CIA analyst turned Democrat politician; Seth Moulton, whom he describes as a CIA-linked figure and a World Economic Forum Young Global Leader; and Robert Menendez, a frequent participant in Brussels policy circles and the Munich Security Conference.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He pairs the act with [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]] as the twin instruments threatening to criminalize dissent.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)
