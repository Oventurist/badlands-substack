# 2026 US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding

The 2026 US-Iran memorandum of understanding is the fourteen-point framework agreement between the United States and [[iran|Iran]] concluded in June 2026, described in the Badlands Media corpus as the negotiated off-ramp from the year's Iranian conflict.

## Terms

As summarized in the June 19, 2026 Badlands Brief by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], the 14-point memorandum was signed ahead of schedule and codifies an immediate and permanent ceasefire, phased sanctions relief, a $300 billion rehabilitation framework, the urgent reopening of the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], and a 60-day window for final nuclear negotiations. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] subsequently said Iran could retain some ballistic missile capabilities under the arrangement.

## Implementation despite the cancelled ceremony

Formal signing ceremonies scheduled in Switzerland were postponed after [[israel|Israel]] launched overnight strikes against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] targets in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]]; Switzerland confirmed the planned Bürgenstock talks were cancelled, and Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]] delayed or cancelled his travel. The parties nonetheless proceeded with implementation. [[u-s-central-command|CENTCOM]] announced that U.S. forces had lifted the blockade on all maritime traffic entering and exiting Iranian ports "in accordance with the President's direction," while stating that American naval ships would remain in the general area to ensure the agreement's terms were observed. Iran reported moving more than five million barrels of oil since signing the electronic agreement.

## Announcement and the Beirut strike

Reporting in the June 15, 2026 Badlands Brief captured the framework at the moment of announcement, several days before the final signing. Reuters reported that U.S. and Iranian officials had agreed on a framework to end the war, halt the American blockade of Iran, and reopen the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], with the memorandum scheduled for signature that Friday in Switzerland. Trump posted on the Sunday that the deal was "now complete." Pakistani Prime Minister [[shehbaz-sharif|Shehbaz Sharif]] said the pact called for the immediate and permanent termination of military operations on all fronts, including [[lebanon|Lebanon]]. The secretariat of Iran's Supreme National Security Council said military operations would end permanently starting Monday night, and Iran's deputy foreign minister said a broader agreement including sanctions relief would be negotiated during a 60-day ceasefire period.

The announcement was immediately complicated by an Israeli strike on Hezbollah targets in Beirut's southern suburbs, which Israel said answered three projectiles fired toward northern Israel in violation of the ceasefire. Two people were killed and four wounded. [[axios|Axios]] reported that Trump said the agreement remained on track despite the strike, though the incident delayed the planned signing by several hours. Axios reporter [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] quoted Trump venting about Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in a phone call: "Why did Bibi have to do a fucking attack? I was so pissed off... He has no fucking judgement." Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] told Fox News that Iran had planned to retaliate but stood down at American request.

## Israeli and congressional opposition

Israeli reaction to the framework was uniformly hostile. National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] said Israel was not a party to the deal and therefore not bound by it, argued that operations should continue until Hezbollah is dismantled, and opposed withdrawal from Israeli-held territory in Lebanon. Opposition leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] called the deal "terrible for Israel." Former Prime Minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] described an "existential moment" and revived his [[octopus-doctrine|Octopus Doctrine]]. Israeli politicians and military figures characterized the outcome as a "strategic failure," Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] joined the criticism, and violence erupted in the [[west-bank|West Bank]] from settlers reacting to the reports.

Badlands commentary by GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read this as the decisive test: with [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]], [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]], [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] and Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] all mobilizing against the deal, the [[israel-lobby|Israel Lobby]] would use its influence over [[congress|Congress]] to block finalization, and the GOP would turn on Trump. On [[israel|Israel]]'s largest online outlet Ynet, Israeli officials told reporters they had no input or influence in the peace process. The Brief's conclusion was that Trump had averted a wider war by negotiating a peace that explicitly excludes Israel, forcing the "MAGA infiltrators" to reveal themselves — and leaving open the prospect of Israel fighting Iran alone, which the Brief treats as necessary to thwart the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel Project]].

## Contested interpretation

Legacy outlets including [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] argued that Trump emerged weakened, that Iran had weathered the conflict, and that the framework departed from the premises originally used to justify the war. Burning Bright inverts this reading: he argues the same media and Uniparty voices had insisted for months that no diplomatic path existed short of perpetual entanglement or regime change, and that their pivot to calling the off-ramp itself the crisis is a self-parodying shift of "Overton's Goalpost." In his framing the memorandum's second-order effect is the exposure of war hawks and neoconservatives whose real commitment is to the Forever War construct.
