# Humphrey's Executor

**Humphrey's Executor** is the Supreme Court precedent invoked in modern disputes over whether the President may remove officials at independent federal agencies whom [[congress|Congress]] has protected by requiring legal "cause" for termination.[1] The precedent became the central battleground in [[trump-v-wilcox|Trump v. Wilcox]], the 2025 emergency-docket case in which the Court allowed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to proceed with the removal of members of the [[national-labor-relations-board|National Labor Relations Board]] and the [[merit-systems-protection-board|Merit Systems Protection Board]].[1]

Commentators read the Court's action as signaling that a majority accepts broad presidential authority to remove at least some high-level executive officers notwithstanding statutory cause requirements, while still expecting historical and structural carve-outs — most prominently for the leadership of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1] Justice [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]]'s dissent, joined by Justices [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] and [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]], was described in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] as a ten-page defense of the Humphrey's Executor framework and a warning about the consequences of abandoning it.[1]
