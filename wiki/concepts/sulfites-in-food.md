# Sulfites in Food

Sulfites are a class of preservatives — including sodium sulfite (E221) and [[sulfur-dioxide|sulfur dioxide]] (E220) — that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] singled out as among the few additives whose toxicity is essentially undeniable.[1]

## Sodium sulfite

Sodium sulfite is a preservative used in winemaking and other processed foods.[1] According to the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]], approximately one in every 100 people is sensitive to sulfites in food, though the article notes that some observers regard this ratio as optimistic.[1] Some medical experts have suggested a possible link between sulfites and asthma.[1] Sulfite-sensitive individuals may experience headaches, breathing problems, and rashes; in severe cases sulfites can close the airways altogether, causing cardiac arrest and death.[1] Sulfites are also commonly found in some brands of dried fruit.[1]

## Sodium chloride

The article groups common table salt (sodium chloride) alongside sodium sulfite, acknowledging that its inclusion may surprise readers given salt's near-universal presence in diets worldwide.[1] The argument advanced is that most diet-conscious individuals are aware that industrial sodium chloride has almost nothing in common with traditional rock or sea salt, and that even mainstream media outlets concede consumers should avoid it.[1]
