# 2026 Texas Primary Elections

The 2026 Texas primary elections, held on Tuesday, March 3, 2026, produced a series of unexpected incumbent defeats and simultaneously generated claims of "voter disenfranchisement" from candidates in both parties.[1] Coverage of the contests by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] framed the results as an early data point against the prevailing expectation that Republicans would suffer heavy losses in the 2026 midterm cycle.[1]

## Senate contests

State Rep. [[james-talarico|James Talarico]] won the Democratic primary for the United States Senate seat in Texas, defeating Rep. [[jasmine-crockett|Jasmine Crockett]] of the 30th congressional district in a closely watched contest.[1] Talarico advanced to the general election for the seat held by Sen. [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]].[1] On the Republican side, no candidate cleared the threshold for outright nomination, sending incumbent Cornyn into a runoff against Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] scheduled for May 26.[1]

## Disenfranchisement claims

On election night, Crockett told supporters in Dallas that voters had been "disenfranchised," saying her campaign received calls and emails from people who reported being denied entry to polling locations or experiencing confusion about where they were permitted to vote.[1] The confusion stemmed from changes in voting procedures that required some voters to cast ballots at assigned precinct locations rather than at any polling site, a rule change that reportedly created widespread confusion in some counties and resulted in voters being turned away from incorrect polling places.[1]

A Dallas County judge initially ordered polling locations to remain open longer after voters reported problems, but the [[texas-supreme-court|Texas Supreme Court]] later blocked the extension of voting hours.[1] Crockett said the reports led her to believe some people were unable to cast ballots; no formal findings of election fraud were announced.[1] Badlands commentary noted the irony of a Democratic candidate adopting arguments about election administration that had, in other contexts, been labeled [[election-denial|election denialism]].[1]

## House upsets

The primaries produced other major upsets. Four-term Rep. [[dan-crenshaw|Dan Crenshaw]] of the 2nd congressional district lost his primary to state Rep. [[steve-toth|Steve Toth]].[1] Rep. [[al-green|Al Green]] of the 9th congressional district did not lose outright but failed to secure a majority and was forced into a runoff against Christian Menefee in an incumbent-versus-incumbent contest created by redistricting; early reporting that Green had lost outright was premature.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the results as a "war on incumbents" in which "regime darlings" in both parties were ousted, arguing that Democrats were alarmed because the outcome undercut months of messaging that Republicans would be destroyed in the midterms.[1] She also flagged an unresolved story about reported failures to recruit enough election-integrity volunteers in Texas, saying reporting from people on the ground indicated the situation was more complicated than initially discussed on Badlands Daily.[1]
