# World War II

In Badlands Media commentary the Second World War is characterised above all as a war about [[oil|oil]].[1] Joe Lange's November 2022 essay "Money and Oil" uses the conflict to argue that petroleum supply, more than ideology or manpower, determined the outcome of the war and therefore the shape of the post-war power structure.[1]

## Germany

Germany had no oil of its own and needed it to rebuild after [[world-war-i|the First World War]].[1] Lange argues that [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] could have conquered all of Europe but was vulnerable without fuel for his war machine, and that his decision to invade Russia — the "ultimate blunder" of creating a two-front war and dividing his own forces — is explained by his desperate need for the Russian oil fields.[1] Hitler is also said to have abandoned the invasion of Great Britain because of a lack of oil, since a navy, air force and mechanised army are completely dependent on it.[1] Germany ultimately lost, in this account, because it could not sustain oil supply for manufacturing and for its military.[1]

## Britain and American supply

The United States supplied Great Britain with the oil it needed to defend itself, including 100-octane fuel that gave British aircraft superior performance and range and allowed them to control the skies — a factor Lange credits with turning the tide of the war.[1]

## The Pacific war

[[japan|Japan]] was in the same predicament as Germany, possessing no oil of its own and being highly dependent on other nations for both its economy and its military.[1] This produced an expansionist policy directed at the oil of Southeast Asia.[1] Japan's embargo on imports into [[china|China]] prompted American retaliation in the form of an oil embargo against Japan, which Lange identifies as the step that set the stage for war with the United States.[1] Japan lost, in his telling, because it could not supply its navy and air force with fuel; he adds that kamikaze attacks were not merely acts of desperation, since Japanese pilots were deliberately given only enough fuel to fight and not enough to return safely.[1]

## Significance

The war established, in this reading, that the value of oil had become as important as money itself, particularly in wartime — a lesson Lange says the "cabal puppet masters" absorbed and applied to the determination of world power structures thereafter.[1]

## Framing as a Cabal project (Badlands account)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that the desired end result of World War II was a unified Europe controlled by corporations and unelected, semi-secret societies, and that the war was created to achieve it, with the [[british-cabal|Cabal]] controlling and manipulating both sides on the way to a [[one-world-government|one world government]].[2] He puts the worldwide death toll at upwards of 85 million, including 418,000 Americans, and says the [[attack-on-pearl-harbor|attack on Pearl Harbor]] was allowed to proceed to bring the United States into what he calls Britain's war.[2] Following the war, he writes, creating a state of constant conflict in the Middle East became a major goal of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] Cabal.[2]
