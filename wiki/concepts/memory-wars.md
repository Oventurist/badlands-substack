# Memory Wars

The "memory wars" is the term used in the Badlands Media article "Gazing Into the Abyss pt.2: a Therapist's Tale" for the public and professional conflict of the 1990s over the validity of recovered memories of childhood sexual abuse and [[ritual-abuse|ritual abuse]].[1]

## Character of the conflict

The article identifies isolation and peer pressure as a major stressor for therapists working with ritually abused clients, and states that this was particularly bad during the memory wars.[1] The diagnosis of dissociative disorder was not widely accepted at the time, and the [[false-memory-syndrome-foundation|False Memory Syndrome Foundation]], along with other similar groups, took up the task of informing the media that unethical therapists were suggesting to helpless clients that they had been sexually abused, or worse, by family members.[1]

## Consequences for clinicians and publishers

*Treating Abuse Today*, described as a leading journal, was forced out of business by picketers representing supposedly innocent accused parents.[1] Leading therapists in the field of dissociative disorders, such as Bennett Braun and Judith Peterson, were targeted for lawsuits and, in Peterson's case, even criminal charges.[1] Insurance companies chose to settle most of those lawsuits out of court, which created the impression that the therapists were guilty.[1]

The article situates this campaign alongside the direct physical threats described by [[alison-miller|Alison Miller]] as the two principal hazards of practicing in the field, and connects it to the broader Badlands framing of [[false-memory-syndrome|False Memory Syndrome]] as a weaponized narrative.[1]
