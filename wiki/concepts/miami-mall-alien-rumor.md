# Miami Mall Alien Rumor

The Miami mall alien rumor was a January 2024 online phenomenon in which a viral video showing a very large police presence at a Miami shopping mall gave rise to widespread speculation that extraterrestrials had been present at the scene.[1] [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] discussed the episode in his [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "Poisoning the Well" as a test case for the limits of discernment.[1]

## The incident and the rumor

According to Carlson, the official account of the incident was that four teenagers were arrested following a stick fight.[1] The video that circulated widely appeared to him to show between 50 and 100 police cars at the location, a scale of response that struck viewers as disproportionate to the stated cause and led many to conclude that something was being concealed.[1] The alien story followed, and some observers accepted it on the strength of that perceived mismatch.[1]

## Carlson's reading

Carlson offered no confident explanation, stating plainly that he had no idea why so many officers appeared.[1] He raised as a possibility that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had ordered the mass police presence precisely in order to manufacture a distraction — drawing attention away from the ongoing [[epstein-files|Epstein]] disclosures or other material — while simultaneously making truth-seekers who took the alien story seriously appear unbalanced.[1] He offered one supporting observation: no ambulances or fire trucks were visible at the scene, only police, which in his view undercut the plausibility of a genuine emergency.[1]

The episode served in the essay as the illustration of a specific hazard: the reasonable heuristic that where there is smoke there is fire fails when "the Deep State sets up a smoke machine," and Carlson asked whether collective discernment had "turned into overactive suspicion."[1] He listed the incident, alongside the [[brooklyn-synagogue-tunnel|Brooklyn synagogue tunnel]] and the [[billionaire-bunker-narrative|billionaire bunker reports]], among the stories he was holding "in an open hand" pending further information — all three exhibiting inconsistencies between who was supplying the story and whom it appeared to harm.[1] It also fell squarely within his recommended practice of allowing 48 hours before forming an opinion, a component of his broader treatment of [[poisoning-the-well|poisoning the well]] and [[confirmation-bias|confirmation bias]].[1]
