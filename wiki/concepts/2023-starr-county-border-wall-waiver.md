# 2023 Starr County Border Wall Waiver

The 2023 Starr County border wall waiver was a decision by Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]], disclosed in early October 2023, to waive a litany of federal laws and regulations in order to swiftly construct additional border wall in a key Texas hotspot for illegal crossings.[1]

## The notice

A notice posted to the U.S. Federal Register, obtained by Fox News, stated that "The Secretary of Homeland Security has determined, pursuant to law, that it is necessary to waive certain laws, regulations, and other legal requirements in order to ensure the expeditious construction of barriers and roads in the vicinity of the international land border in Starr County, Texas."[1] Mayorkas grounded the action in the [[illegal-immigration-reform-and-immigrant-responsibility-act|Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act]] of 1996, under which Congress granted the Secretary authority to waive all legal requirements he determines in his sole discretion to be necessary for expeditious construction of authorized barriers and roads.[1] In total Mayorkas planned to waive 26 federal laws to expedite construction.[1]

## Justification and context

The notice highlighted the 245,000 migrant encounters recorded over the previous year in the Rio Grande Valley Sector, which includes Starr County.[1] It further noted that since President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took office, law enforcement had encountered more than 7 million people illegally entering the country.[1] In light of the surge in illegal immigration, Mayorkas found that there existed an "acute and immediate need to construct physical barriers and roads in the vicinity of the border of the United States in order to prevent unlawful entries into the United States in the project areas."[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried the item as a bonus entry in its news brief, framing it as Mayorkas "finally" admitting an immediate need to build border wall amid the immigration surge — a reversal of the administration's prior posture and an implicit vindication of the barrier policy associated with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The reporting was originally published by [[just-the-news|Just the News]].[1]
