# Zero Trust

Zero trust is a cybersecurity architecture model built on the premise that no user, device, or network segment should be implicitly trusted, and that every access request must be authenticated and authorized. It has become a central organizing principle for United States federal information security modernization efforts.[1]

## Adoption across government

[[dana-barnes|Dana Barnes]], Vice President of Public Sector at [[forrester|Forrester]], argued in October 2025 that zero trust must be embedded into all government operations rather than treated as a discrete project, extending into software development practices and system consolidation.[1] He stressed that success requires a holistic approach across an entire organization.[1]

## Workforce reduction as accelerant and risk

Barnes characterized the administration's drive to shrink the federal workforce as creating pressure for faster adoption of modern technologies, including artificial intelligence, in order to maintain efficiency with fewer personnel.[1] He simultaneously warned that bureaucratic delays and an insufficiently trained workforce could prevent these technologies from being deployed safely and effectively.[1]

## Commentary

The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] paired coverage of the zero trust discussion with commentary arguing that federal and state institutions alike are failing and that a general loss of faith in government is an intended feature, not a bug, of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s program — with [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] explicitly listing the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the federal court system, the Department of Education, and the Department of Homeland Security as institutions that should not be propped up.[1]
