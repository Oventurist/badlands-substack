# Nationwide Section 3 Ballot Challenges to Trump's 2024 Candidacy

By early December 2023, roughly 22 states had pending challenges to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 candidacy brought under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]], the post-Civil War disqualification clause.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the coordinated effort as election interference by a regime unable to defeat Trump in a fair political contest.[1]

## States that declined to remove Trump

Six states — Florida, Colorado, New Hampshire, Minnesota, Michigan, and Rhode Island — had declined to keep Trump off the ballot as of the article's publication.[1]

In Florida, on August 31, a federal district judge ruled that a lawyer and other voters challenging Trump's candidacy lacked standing.[1] In Minnesota, the state Supreme Court dismissed a challenge on November 8, ruling that no law barred Trump from the primary ballot while leaving challengers free to retry the case for the general election.[1] In Michigan, on November 14, a state judge ruled that Trump had followed state law in qualifying for the primary and deferred eligibility questions about the presidential election to Congress.[1] Rhode Island's claim was summarily dismissed on November 27 because the plaintiff failed to show "injury in fact," a required component of a Section 3 claim.[1]

## Pending challenges

Challenges remained live in Alaska, Arizona, Connecticut, Delaware, Kansas, Massachusetts, Montana, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, South Carolina, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin and Wyoming.[1]

## Colorado as the bellwether

The Colorado case, [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]], advanced furthest: District Judge [[sarah-wallace|Sarah Wallace]] issued what commentators described as a criminal finding in a civil trial, prompting Trump to appeal a ruling that was, on its surface, in his favor.[1] Both the Fourteenth Amendment question and the insurrection question decided in Colorado carried implications for the other 22 challenges, and the case was expected to reach the United States Supreme Court quickly given Colorado's December 29, 2023 ballot-certification deadline.[1]
