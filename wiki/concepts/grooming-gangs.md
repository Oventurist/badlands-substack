# UK Grooming Gangs Scandal

The UK grooming gangs scandal refers to the ongoing revelation of organised sexual exploitation of children in the United Kingdom and the failure — or, in the framing used by critics, the facilitation — of British institutions in responding to it.[1]

## Framing in Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the ongoing revelation of grooming gangs alongside what the author described as "the government-facilitated rape and pillaging of their long and storied history" as evidence that the [[humiliation-ritual|humiliation ritual]] directed at the British people had reached a fever pitch.[1] In this reading the scandal supplies the "dark undercurrent" beneath otherwise lighter news items, including outgoing Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]]'s viral remarks celebrating his government's LGBTQ+ record.[1]

## Relationship to memetic politics

The commentary argued that the contrast between the government's stated social-policy priorities and the grooming gangs revelations is precisely what makes the meme of Britain being intentionally made into a "fake and gay mecca" resonate — an illustration of the [[memetics|memetic]] principle that a core truth need not be burdened by facts.[1]

## Starmer's rejection of a national inquiry

In January 2025 British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] criticised [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and others for jumping on the "bandwagon of the far-right" by raising criticisms over the grooming gang child rape scandal and demanding fresh national investigations.[2] In his first major comments after international scrutiny turned to the failures to protect thousands of mostly young white girls from sexual predators, mostly of Muslim extraction and from Pakistan, Starmer again shot down the idea that a national public inquiry was necessary.[2]

Starmer rejected the charge that he was seeking to obscure his own history from the public, saying that his record "is open" and that "there is nothing secret about being director of public prosecutions," adding that those "spreading lies and misinformation as far and as wide as possible" were "not interested in victims, they are interested in themselves."[2] Calls for a national public inquiry continued to grow, amid earlier revelations that officials had ignored accusations of child sexual abuse committed by Muslim gangs out of concern for appearing racist.[2]

## Migrant crime statistics

The controversy coincided with a first-of-its-kind analysis by the [[centre-for-migration-control|Centre for Migration Control]], using [[home-office|Home Office]] and Office for National Statistics data obtained through Freedom of Information requests, which found that foreigners are more than three times as likely as British citizens to be arrested for sex crimes in the UK.[2] Police in England and Wales arrested more than 9,000 foreign nationals for sexual offences between January and October of the previous year — 26.1% of an estimated 35,000 total arrests — while in the City of London foreign nationals accounted for almost 67% of such arrests.[2] The report appeared amid claims that the government had purposefully suppressed migrant crime data, and against the background of groups of men predominantly of Pakistani origin having reportedly raped and tortured thousands of underage girls in towns across northern England over two decades.[2]

Badlands contributor [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] cautioned against the obvious reading, asking why "our captors" wanted the public to get "big mad about British criminal politicians" and why at that moment, and warning that if readers ended up blaming "Islam" for the grooming gangs, "then the psyop worked perfectly."[2]

## Parliamentary fight over a new national inquiry

On Wednesday, January 8, 2025, Parliament held its first exchange on child sex gangs since the matter regained prominence in the public mind, with Conservative leader [[kemi-badenoch|Kemi Badenoch]] telling the House that the country still does not know how bad the situation truly is because the research simply has not been done.[3] The UK's legacy parties of government, Labour and the Conservatives, continued to bicker bitterly over who bore blame for the decades-long scandal of industrial-scale child rape by migrant-heritage gangs.[3] Responding to Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] — whose position remained that there was a binary choice between acting on recommendations from past, less comprehensive reports and mounting a fresh years-long investigation, and that doing both was impossible — Badenoch said simply: "There is still work to be done."[3]

## The Keighley case

Renewed attention in January 2025 revived the case of [[tauqeer-hussain|Tauqeer "Toki" Hussain]], who was found guilty of two counts of rape of one underage victim and one count of rape of a second underage victim for offences committed in 2012, and given a total sentence of 18 years; Guardian reporting from the 2016 sentencing recorded that he raped both girls in an underground car park, and that twelve men in total were sentenced over the sexual exploitation of a Keighley teenager.[3] A widely shared account held that police caught a member of the gang in the act in 2012, had a brief conversation, and drove away.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the girl was raped in front of disinterested or impotent and unarmed police, and that despite the convictions the gangs continued to operate.[3] She argued that the "great migration" was a resounding success for the regime, which brandished weaponized empathy and the fear of being labelled a racist or bigot as sticks to ensure populations did not protest the relocation of predators into their communities — with the result that countless underage girls were raped over decades.[3] She concluded that the child rapists must be held accountable, and the politicians who enabled them out of cowardice or political expediency "maybe even more so."[3]

## Police implicated in Rotherham

In late January 2025, two former police officers were arrested after being accused of misconduct in public office and of sexually abusing children in an English town where a Muslim child grooming gang had operated for over a decade.[4] The ex-officers, unnamed at the time of publication, were taken into custody as part of an investigation into child sexual exploitation in the rape gang hotspot of Rotherham, England.[4] The arrests followed complaints from four individuals who alleged they were victims of abuse connected to two former South Yorkshire Police constables, with the offenses reportedly occurring during both on-duty and off-duty periods.[4]
