# 2024 Rafah Tent Camp Strike

The 2024 Rafah tent camp strike was an Israeli airstrike in the southern Gaza city of [[rafah|Rafah]] that set fire to a camp housing displaced Palestinians and, according to local officials, killed at least 45 people.[1]

## Netanyahu's response

Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said on Monday, May 27, 2024 that a "tragic mistake" had been made in the strike, without elaborating on the nature of the error.[1] The Israeli military had initially said it carried out a precise airstrike on a [[hamas|Hamas]] compound, killing two senior militants; as details of the strike and the resulting fire emerged, the military said it had opened an investigation into the civilian deaths.[1]

## International reaction

The strike intensified surging international criticism of Israel over its war with Hamas, with even its closest allies expressing outrage at civilian deaths.[1] Israel insisted it adheres to international law even while under scrutiny in the world's top courts, one of which — the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] — had the previous week demanded that it halt the offensive in Rafah.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the timing of the incident "almost farcical" given the ICJ order issued on the Friday before, and highlighted the warning from EU High Representative [[josep-borrell|Josep Borrell]] that "We will have to choose between our support to the international institutions and the rule of law, or our support to Israel."[1] The commentary argued that the real choice was between international institutions and support for the United States, holding that Israel, "despite being fostered and created by the high lords of London," had become an extension of the United States.[1] It noted that France and Germany — described as the two anchor-states of the EU — were expressing disgust at the images coming out of Rafah, and observed that dropping 2,000 pound bombs on tent cities full of refugees "seems like a sure-fire way to kill civilians," suggesting the logic may have been provocation.[1]


## Context: defiance of the ICJ order

The tent-camp strike came at the culmination of a weekend of Israeli airstrikes that began almost immediately after the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] ordered Israel on May 24, 2024 to halt its [[rafah|Rafah]] offensive.[2] Videos posted online on the Sunday evening showed enormous fires raging through refugee tent camps with scenes of scorched bodies and decapitated children.[2] Corporate press reported more than 30 dead while the Palestinian Ministry of Health placed the toll in the hundreds and said over 60 people were burned alive; Badlands commentary conceded it was impossible to know which side was reporting the truth, while noting Israeli leadership had confirmed the strikes and its intent to continue the campaign.[2] Hamas launched a counter-attack of rockets at Tel Aviv late that Sunday with no reported damage or casualties.[2]

Commentators argued that if the allegations of a total siege were accurate — citing defense minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]]'s October 9 statement that "we are imposing a complete siege on the city of Gaza... no electricity, no food, no water, no fuel" — the conduct would meet the textbook definition of war crimes under the Geneva Convention.[2]
