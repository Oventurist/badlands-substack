# Bay of Pigs Invasion (Operation Zapata)

The Bay of Pigs was the [[cia|CIA]]'s abortive 1961 invasion of Cuba by exiled Cuban mercenaries. The Badlands Media account foregrounds a connection it says the mainstream media has never reported: the direct involvement of [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

Three ships owned by Bush's [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Off-Shore Company]] and financed by [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]] — the *Houston*, the *Barbara J* and the *Zapata* — were loaned by his firm to the agency for the invasion.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The CIA code-named the operation OPERATION ZAPATA, which the article treats as more than coincidence and as grounds to ask whether Bush was running it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) Bush consistently denied any CIA membership before becoming director in 1976.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

The operation is also presented as the origin point of the Cuban exile force the CIA recruited and trained in paramilitary work, terrorism, assassination and money laundering — a network the article says Bush led and which recurs later through Cuban-American figures such as [[miguel-recarey|Miguel Recarey]] and [[armando-codina|Armando Codina]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Nixon's blocked file request

When [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]] took office in 1968, one of the first things he wanted to do was review the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s documents on the Bay of Pigs and the assassination of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]].[2] Director [[richard-helms|Richard Helms]] was very reluctant and essentially refused to allow the standing president access, despite Nixon's clear right to it — an episode Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] reads as evidence that the agency understood Nixon was not its friend, and as the motive for the [[watergate|Watergate]] break-in that removed him from power.[2]

## Kennedy's response in Carlson's account (2024)

Erik Carlson's "The CIA is Being Thrown Under the Bus" builds on the famous remark [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] made about the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] after the 1961 Bay of Pigs debacle: "I will splinter the CIA into a thousand pieces and scatter it into the winds."[3]

Carlson reads this as evidence that less than a decade and a half after the agency's creation a sitting president considered it beyond saving — that no part of its foundation or structure could be salvaged and a total teardown was necessary.[3]
