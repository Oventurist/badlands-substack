# January 6 Pardons

The January 6 pardons were a mass clemency action taken by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the evening of January 20, 2025, the first day of his second term. Trump issued "full, complete and unconditional" presidential pardons to roughly 1,500 people involved in the January 6, 2021 attack on Congress, including some who had been convicted of violent acts, making good on a promise to act on such cases on day one.[1]

In addition to the pardons, Trump issued commutations in more than a dozen cases, shortening the sentences of defendants he said required "further research." Among those whose sentences were commuted was [[stewart-rhodes|Stewart Rhodes]], founder of the [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]] militia. Signing the documents in the Oval Office, Trump referred to the convicted defendants as "hostages" and said, "This is a big one. We hope they come out tonight, frankly."[1]

## Reaction and the Jeremy Brown exclusion

Badlands Media contributors welcomed the action while pressing for its scope to be widened. Commentary published by [[canncon|CannCon's Newsletter]] thanked Trump for pardoning J6 defendants and J6-related cases, but noted that the order's language covered those "convicted of offenses related to events" — wording that left out Master Sergeant [[jeremy-brown|Jeremy Brown]].[1]

Brown, a Green Beret with twenty years of service, had by then spent four years in prison on charges arising from a search of his South Florida property. The commentary described the underlying matter as misdemeanor charges, emphasized that Brown was non-violent and never entered the Capitol, alleged that the FBI Joint Terrorism Task Force turned off surveillance cameras Brown had installed on his property during the raid, and stated that Brown had attempted to blow the whistle on FBI and DHS recruiting for an event in January that he believed to be January 6. The piece appealed directly to Trump to extend a pardon to him.[1]

## Context

The pardons came the same day that outgoing President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] issued sweeping [[preemptive-pardons|preemptive pardons]] to members of his family and to figures including [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] and [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] in connection with the [[january-6-committee|House Select Committee on January 6]]. Badlands commentary argued that the Biden pardons, by removing the risk of self-incrimination, obliged their recipients to testify if called — and that the number of people shielded was small relative to the universe of those still exposed.[1]
