# Chinese Spy Balloon Incident (2023)

The Chinese spy balloon incident was the February 2023 transit of a Chinese high-altitude surveillance balloon across United States territory and its subsequent shootdown by the US military, an episode that became a recurring reference point in [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s public remarks about [[china|China]].[1]

## Course of events

According to Biden's own account, the balloon carried "two boxcars full of spy equipment" and was "blown off course up through Alaska and then down through the United States" before being shot down.[1] The United States subsequently briefed dozens of nations on the incident.[1]

The episode became public on Wednesday, February 1, 2023, when residents of the Billings, Montana area reported an unexplained object in the sky and the [[billings-logan-international-airport|Billings Logan International Airport]] was shut down for roughly two hours.[2] Shane Ketterling, the airport's Director of Aviation, said two inbound flights were diverted and one departure delayed while the airspace was closed, and that the shutdown was ordered by the Salt Lake City [[federal-aviation-administration|FAA]] tower, which closed roughly 50 square miles of airspace over the Livingston area, affecting Bozeman, Helena and Billings.[2] On Thursday, February 2, the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] confirmed the object was an intelligence-gathering balloon "most certainly launched by the People's Republic of China."[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] noted that the first question in such sightings is always "ours or theirs" — whether an unidentified object belongs to the US military, a foreign state, or a defense contractor such as Raytheon, Boeing or Lockheed Martin — and argued that China's interest in Montana lay not in farmland but in the state's US intercontinental ballistic missile facilities.[2]

## Political aftermath

Democratic Representative [[mike-quigley|Mike Quigley]] of Illinois said on CNN's "CNN Newsroom" on Friday, February 3, 2023 that while the balloon was a serious issue, "the low orbit Chinese satellites have been there for years, they're there all the time," adding that he did not want Americans to believe the concern was new.[5] Quigley also argued that Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] should not have canceled his trip to China over the balloon, saying grievances should be raised "nose-to-nose" rather than through the press.[5] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] rejected that framing, asking whether US airspace had become meaningless and whether the balloons served purposes satellites could not, and called it a poor look for an American politician to play devil's advocate for the nation's most competent adversary.[5]

## Debris recovery (February 2023)

On Tuesday, February 7, 2023, U.S. Navy divers began recovering the scattered elements of the downed balloon from the ocean, using sophisticated reconnaissance drones dubbed the Kingfish and the Swordfish to hasten the work.[3] The debris lay in waters roughly 50 feet deep but was spread across an area some fifteen football fields long and fifteen football fields across.[3] Recovery proceeded even as a [[china|China]] Foreign Ministry spokesperson protested that "The airship does not belong to the U.S. It belongs to China," and chided Washington for not being "professional" in its handling of the matter.[3]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] called the episode a continuing PSYOP and questioned whether the public deterioration of the administration's relationship with [[china|Beijing]] was genuine or theatre, joking that General [[mark-milley|Milley]] was probably having the debris boxed up to FedEx back to China.[3] He framed the hope in the Badlands community that [[xi-jinping|Xi]] was working with [[donald-trump|Trump]], [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and others to take down a deeply embedded international criminal cabal, while the "normie perspective" saw China entering the last stages as challenger for an Alpha crown with a final battle on the horizon.[3] DeLarme argued the theater of fear was pumping out bad vibes while observing and recording each target group's reactions, and asked readers whose PSYOP it was — the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] at Xi's behest, bad actors within the CCP acting independently, the patriots, or a mysterious fourth party.[3]

At a June 20, 2023 campaign reception in Kentfield, California, Biden used the episode to characterize [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] as a dictator, saying Xi "got very upset" over the shootdown because he had not known the balloon was there and was embarrassed to have been uninformed: "That's what's a great embarrassment for dictators, when they didn't know what happened."[1] Biden added that Xi "denied it was even there."[1]

China's Foreign Ministry, through spokesperson [[mao-ning|Mao Ning]], responded that the remarks were "extremely absurd and irresponsible" and constituted "a blatant political provocation."[1] The exchange landed one day after Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] concluded a Beijing visit intended to stabilize relations, and became the core of the resulting [[biden-dictator-remark-controversy-2023|dictator remark controversy]].[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read Biden's telling of the balloon story as evidence of cognitive decline rather than deliberate insult, arguing that the apparent friction between Washington and Beijing served as cover for a deeper alignment.[1]

## "Decoy" hypothesis and Chinese counter-claims

On February 13, 2023, New York Republican Representative [[claudia-tenney|Claudia Tenney]] publicly questioned whether the balloon shot down off the coast of South Carolina had been intended as a distraction from some other activity by the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]].[4] Speaking on the Just the News, No Noise program, she said the president was "getting inundated with these types of bizarre crossings" and asked whether the objects were surveillance platforms, decoys, or cover for something else that Americans should know about.[4] She noted that China had taken other actions undermining US security.[4] By that date the US military had shot down four suspicious aerial objects during the month, including one over Lake Huron, with officials saying at least one was part of a Chinese surveillance operation.[4]

Beijing simultaneously inverted the accusation. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson [[wang-wenbin|Wang Wenbin]] said on February 13 that US high-altitude balloons had illegally flown over Chinese airspace more than ten times since the previous year without approval from the relevant Chinese authorities, and urged Washington to "change its course and reflect on itself, instead of smearing and accusing China."[4] The ministry also reiterated its criticism that the United States had overreacted in shooting down a Chinese aircraft.[4]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that questioning the official balloon narrative had become "the new normal," with a sitting congresswoman voicing doubts previously confined to those dismissed as conspiracy theorists.[4] He argued that almost everyone agreed the official story was inadequate, and speculated that inducing the public to ask questions might itself be an objective — noting that mass deception normally requires people to stop asking questions, and that [[deep-state|deep state]] globalists seeking blind compliance would not want a questioning populace.[4] He concluded that questioning everything is a pillar of discernment and anathema to established power.[4]

Contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] read the Chinese counter-claim as further saber-rattling, arguing that the deep state wanted a world war involving China, Russia and the United States as a distraction from the record of leftist governments and "the incoming storm they will all be facing."[4]
