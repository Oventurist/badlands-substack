# Greater Idaho Movement

The Greater Idaho movement is a campaign to move the Oregon–Idaho state border westward so that rural, conservative eastern Oregon counties would be governed by Idaho rather than by Oregon's Democratic-controlled state government.[1]

## County votes

On Tuesday, May 21, 2024, Crook County became the 13th county in Oregon to vote to secede from Oregon and join neighboring Idaho, a conservative state that residents believe better represents their values. The measure passed with 53 percent of the vote.[1]

Organizers of Measure 7-86, also known as the "Greater Idaho Measure," attributed the push to Oregon's progressive policies and high crime rates, arguing that rural residents have been alienated within the state and that joining Idaho would bring reduced taxes along with improved governance and representation.[1]

## Proposed border

Under the proposal, the Oregon border would be moved 200 miles to the west, placing 14 counties and several partial counties inside Idaho's state boundaries.[1] The measure's website argues that "the Oregon/Idaho line was established 163 years ago and is now outdated," making no sense in its current location "because it doesn't match the location of the cultural divide in Oregon."[1] Organizers add that they want "an economy that is not held back by Oregon regulations and taxes, including environmental regulations," noting that federal and Idaho regulations would remain and that "Idaho knows how to respect rural counties and their livelihoods."[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the effort as a largely ceremonial endeavor to annex Eastern Oregon into Western Idaho, aimed at getting the attention of the ruling class in Salem after social-justice policy enactments left the rural population disenfranchised.[1] She drew a parallel to Northern Colorado counties that have floated joining Wyoming to escape similar policies, though without comparable organization, and framed the underlying question as whether government will "stop and listen to the people."[1]
