# Aspen Institute Hack-and-Dump Exercise

The Aspen Institute hack-and-dump exercise was a September 2020 tabletop simulation convened by [[aspen-digital|Aspen Digital]], an arm of the [[aspen-institute|Aspen Institute]], two months before the 2020 presidential election.[1] It was surfaced publicly by [[michael-shellenberger|Michael Shellenberger]] in a [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] thread on December 19, 2022.[1]

## Design and participants

According to the Washington Free Beacon account cited by Badlands Media, the exercise brought together social media executives, journalists, and academics to develop a coverage strategy for a hypothetical "hack and dump" of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s emails.[1] Badlands Media likened the format to [[event-201|Event 201]], the pandemic tabletop exercise that game-planned scenarios closely resembling the subsequent COVID-19 response, arguing that in both cases a think tank rehearsed the handling of information shortly before the modeled event occurred.[1]

## Media playbook

One week before [[miranda-devine|Miranda Devine]]'s [[new-york-post|New York Post]] laptop story, Aspen Digital's [[garrett-graff|Garrett Graff]] published a [[wired|Wired]] article laying out how the press should treat hacks and leaks in the final weeks of the race, invoking the media's handling of the John Podesta emails in 2016.[1] Badlands Media read the piece as an operational playbook for suppressing an "October surprise" rather than neutral commentary.[1]

## Significance in the Blinken thread

The exercise is central to Badlands Media's argument that [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] — reported to be the "impetus" behind the [[october-2020-intelligence-officials-letter-on-the-hunter-biden-laptop|October 2020 intelligence officials letter]] and linked to Aspen through the [[aspen-strategy-group|Aspen Strategy Group]] — sat at the intersection of the letter, the institute, and the $10.3 million [[state-department|State Department]] grant awarded to Aspen on September 1, 2021.[1]
