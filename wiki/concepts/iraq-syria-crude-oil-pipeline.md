# Iraq-Syria Crude Oil Pipeline

The Iraq-Syria crude oil pipeline is a long-idled line connecting [[iraq|Iraq]] to [[syria|Syria]]'s Mediterranean coast whose revival the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] is supporting, according to US officials.[1] The project would provide Iraq with an alternative export route to global markets, reducing its dependence on the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Persian Gulf while diversifying regional energy infrastructure.[1]

## Strategic rationale

US officials described the proposal as part of a broader strategy to strengthen regional energy security and limit [[iran|Iran]]'s leverage over global oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.[1] If completed, the pipeline would allow Iraqi crude to reach Mediterranean ports for export without transiting the Gulf, though significant infrastructure repairs, security challenges, and political agreements between Iraq and Syria would still be required.[1]

## Opposition

The proposal drew opposition from Iran-backed Iraqi militias, with one prominent group threatening to disrupt any future pipeline project on the grounds that the United States is "stealing our oil."[1] The development came as Washington continued to pursue regional energy initiatives alongside broader diplomatic efforts aimed at stabilizing the Middle East and reducing vulnerabilities in global energy markets.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the announcement came less than a week after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] revealed his intention to remove Syria from the list of state sponsors of terrorism, opening the country to international investors, and predicted Saudi involvement in the project.[1] He read the initiative as evidence that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] is taking over and stabilizing the Middle East through economic entanglement between countries with a long history of mutual violence.[1] In that framing, the approach runs contrary to plans premised on mass violence and destabilization that would have driven mass migration and emptied lands for [[israeli-settler-movement|Israeli settlers]] pursuing [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]], and heralds a historic transformation of a region long said to be incapable of peace or prosperity.[1]

## Corridor revival (April 2026)

In early April 2026 [[iraq|Iraq]] moved to reopen the land corridor through [[syria|Syria]] for oil exports to Europe following Syria's regime change, providing an alternative to maritime shipments including those transiting the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[2] The plan involves moving Iraqi crude across Syrian territory to Mediterranean ports; the corridor had been largely inactive due to years of conflict and instability in Syria, and the revival came amid heightened regional tensions and energy-supply concerns tied to the [[iran-war|Iran war]].[2] On 2 April Iraq announced it had restarted pipeline shipments to Syria after a decades-long hiatus, citing the unique circumstances created by the Syrian Civil War and the Israeli-Iran war.[2]

## Timeline and geopolitical reading

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] set out a sequence of events: on 19 March [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] issued a statement proposing that all oil pipelines from the Iraqi and Arab side of the Persian Gulf be routed to [[israel|Israel]], prompting accusations that he had started the Iran war to create the need for that solution; on 31 March Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]], visiting Germany, proposed instead that Gulf pipelines run to Syrian ports, some of which already existed but had been abandoned; and on 2 April Iraq restarted its shipments.[2]

He stressed that al-Sharaa was installed by Presidents [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] together with the Iranians via the [[astana-accords|Astana Accords]], with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] playing a pivotal role in normalizing him as a legitimate world leader — Trump having stated on Truth Social that Erdoğan was responsible for toppling Assad and that al-Sharaa was "his guy."[2] Israeli National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] had by contrast called for al-Sharaa's assassination after the Syrian president refused Israeli demands concerning Syrian sovereignty and territorial control.[2]

The commentary linked the corridor to Iraq's stalled government formation, noting that Trump's earlier Truth Social attacks on [[al-malika|Al-Malika]] appeared to have had the opposite of the intended effect, and that Al-Malika — rumored to be running much of the government without formal authority — had a month earlier called al-Sharaa "a brother" and offered to restore ties with Syria.[2] Iraq's sudden cooperation with Syria was therefore read as side-stepping Netanyahu's ploy to make Israel the regional gatekeeper of oil.[2] A footnote in the source reporting that Iraq was also sending oil to Turkey via the [[baku-tbilisi-ceyhan-pipeline|BTC pipeline]] was cited as further evidence of a clandestine [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] plan, implying that Erdoğan and Iraq are working together and that Trump's public aggression toward Iraq is kayfabe.[2]
