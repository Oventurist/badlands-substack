# Natalism

Natalism — also called pronatalism or the pro-birth position — is, per the definition circulated in April 2024 coverage, "an ideology that promotes the reproduction of human life as an important objective of being human and advocates high birthrate," a term dated to 1971.[1]

## The 2024 controversy

In late April 2024, a report published by [[politico|Politico]] framed support for human reproduction as a "far right" position, writing that "the far right is so obsessed with making babies, they just held a whole conference about it" in reference to a natalist convention known as NatalCon.[1] The piece was written by Gaby Del Valle, described as a reporter whose work focuses on immigration, surveillance and the far right.[1]

Critics, including the outlet [[modernity-news|Modernity News]], responded that identity politics had reached the point of coding basic biological continuity as right-wing, observing that "if there is no reproduction of human life, there is no being human to be had."[1] They also argued the Politico piece made light of collapsing global fertility rates while quoting the convention's framing that the threat is "existential, biological, epoch-defining" and that "economies will fail, civilizations will fall, and it will all happen because people aren't having enough babies."[1] Politico's account further characterized attendees as concerned with "a total social overhaul" and "breeding a new majority — one that looks and sounds just like them."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor Simon Esler argued the accusation that pro-natalists merely want to increase the white conservative population is absurd, noting the irony that it is committed left-wing people who are intentionally choosing not to have children.[1] Esler extended the analysis to information warfare, speculating that outrage-generating mainstream articles function as data collection: if AI-driven psychological operations are run on psychographic profiles built from the online behavior of the conservative and free-minded community, then deliberately inflaming that community yields "strategically-beneficial datasets" for learning how to trigger a target population at a chosen time.[1] He warned that while the adversary may be losing, it continues to evolve along the same horizon of warfare, and cautioned against "overly simplistic interpretations of a sophisticated battlefield."[1]
