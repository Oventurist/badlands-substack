# Kazan Declaration

The Kazan Declaration is the outcome document adopted at the October 2024 [[brics|BRICS]] summit hosted in Kazan, Russia, and characterized in commentary as a manifesto for a new world order.[1] Analyst Andrey Kortunov argued that the declaration signaled that BRICS, in its expanded composition, was ready to open a new chapter in its history.[1]

## Scale and structure

The declaration is unusually voluminous by the standards of the group's summitry: it contains 134 paragraphs, compared with 94 in the statement adopted at the Johannesburg summit in August 2023 and 75 in the Beijing document of July 2022.[1] Commentators read this year-on-year growth as evidence of increasing intensity of engagement and a broadening substantive scope of BRICS multilateral cooperation.[1] For the first time, the group set out in detail a unified vision of the current state of the international system.[1]

The text consists of a preamble and four sections addressing (1) strengthening multilateralism, (2) global and regional security, (3) financial and economic cooperation, and (4) humanitarian exchanges.[1]

## Contested passages

Kortunov assessed that a common denominator on the situation in Gaza was difficult to reach given the divergent positions of members such as Iran and the United Arab Emirates toward Israel.[1] A statement on respecting the territorial integrity of Syria could be read as implicit criticism of the Turkish military presence there, which Damascus has not explicitly authorized.[1] Less contentious issues — the nation-building crisis in Haiti, and international terrorism — received comparatively long and detailed treatment, reflecting broader agreement among members from the outset.[1]

## Interpretation

The declaration was expected to attract significant interest in political and academic circles as well as criticism from opponents of BRICS.[1] Kortunov emphasized that BRICS does not aim to "balance" the West and will never become a kind of G7, given the diversity of its members and the absence of a clear hegemonic leader, yet the group is already openly claiming a more prominent role in global governance and in defining the parameters of the emerging order.[1]

Badlands commentary read the declaration as an implicit reference to what its writers call the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], and noted [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s declaration that the BRICS movement represents a "[[new-world-order|New World Order]]" — treated by the Badlands author as a deliberate troll of George H. W. Bush's use of the same phrase in the 1990s to describe globalist hegemony.[1]
