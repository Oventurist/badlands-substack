# Black Support for Trump

**Black support for Trump** describes the measurable rise in support among black American voters for former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] documented in the run-up to the 2024 election.[1]

## Reported trend

Reporting in December 2023 described black Trump supporters who feared backlash and felt they could only whisper "I'm voting for Trump," alongside others becoming louder and prouder in voicing support.[1] Opinion polling showed increasing willingness among black voters to break ranks, and since Trump's 2016 win black support for him had more than tripled, exceeding 20 percent in some surveys.[1]

## Mark Fisher endorsement

[[mark-fisher|Mark Fisher]], co-founder of a [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] group in Rhode Island, drew attention with his endorsement of Trump.[1] "I knew I was going to pay a price for it," Fisher said, "but I felt like the benefit of doing it far outweighed the cost of me playing it safe."[1] He said he felt obligated "to clear a path" for others who thought as he did.[1]

## Context

Pro-Trump black Americans are widely treated as renegades, partly because Trump's opponents have sought to brand him a racist unworthy of black votes, and partly because he is a Republican — black leaders and churches having for generations encouraged support for Democrats, including President Joe Biden.[1]

## April 2024 Wall Street Journal swing-state poll

A Wall Street Journal poll of seven swing states published in April 2024 found that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] faced a dual-natured challenge in shoring up support among Black Americans, one of the Democratic Party's most loyal constituencies.[2] While most Black men said they intended to support Biden, some 30% said they were either definitely or probably going to vote for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; by comparison, AP VoteCast recorded Trump receiving votes from 12% of Black men nationwide in 2020.[2] Among Black women, 11% told the WSJ they were definitely or probably voting for Trump, up from the 6% recorded nationwide by the AP poll in 2020.[2] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] carried the poll as a bonus item indicating an ongoing realignment.[2]
