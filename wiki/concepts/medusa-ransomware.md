# Medusa ransomware

Medusa is a "ransomware-as-a-service variant used to conduct ransomware attacks" that became the subject of a joint United States government cybersecurity advisory published on March 12, 2025.[1] The advisory was issued by the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]], the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], and the Multi-State Information Sharing and Analysis Center, and warned Gmail and Outlook users that the operation had taken hundreds of victims' data hostage for ransom and that others could be next.[1]

## Scale and targeting

As of February 2025, Medusa had claimed more than 300 known victims within "critical infrastructure sectors," according to the advisory.[1] Operating since 2021, the service has relied on common ransomware techniques including phishing and the exploitation of unpatched software vulnerabilities, striking medical, education, and legal organizations among others.[1] The agencies noted that Medusa originally operated as a closed ransomware variant and had changed considerably in the roughly four years since it first appeared, with the techniques required to thwart it evolving alongside it.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the federal warning with skepticism, writing that "the 'Medusa Variant' sounds super scary" and asking, before panicking, whether the warnings were "coming from the cyber experts at CISA or from environmental lawyers cosplaying as cyber experts at CISA."[1] She concluded that the agency had "earned the scrutiny."[1]
