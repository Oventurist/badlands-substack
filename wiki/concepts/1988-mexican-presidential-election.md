# 1988 Mexican presidential election

The 1988 Mexican presidential election brought [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]] of the [[institutional-revolutionary-party|Institutional Revolutionary Party]] to power and is described in the Badlands Media account as one of Mexico's "biggest open secrets" — a rigged vote.[1] The New York Times reported in March 2004 under the headline "Ex-President in Mexico Casts New Light on Rigged 1988 Election" that the 850-page autobiography of outgoing president [[miguel-de-la-madrid|Miguel de la Madrid]] gave the firmest confirmation to date that the election was fraudulent, even if analysts considered the substance already known.[1]

Initial results from areas around the capital showed Salinas losing badly to opposition leader Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas.[1] On election night the secretary of the interior advised de la Madrid that returns were running heavily against the PRI; rather than release them to a public demanding results, the government said the computer system tabulating the votes had crashed, and Salinas was subsequently declared the winner.[1] Three years later, in an alliance between the PRI and the conservative National Action Party, the Mexican Congress ordered the ballots burned, destroying the only hard evidence of the fraud.[1]

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] situates the fraud within a longer pattern of [[cia|CIA]] control over the Mexican government and one-party rule, and links its timing to the American election of the same year that made [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] president.[1] In his reading the installation of Salinas was a precondition for the [[nafta|NAFTA]] arrangement and for the expansion of drug trafficking across the southern border directed through the president's brother [[raul-salinas-de-gortari|Raúl Salinas de Gortari]].[1]
