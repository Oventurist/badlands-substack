# Paris 2024 Olympics Opening Ceremony

The opening ceremony of the 2024 Summer Olympics in Paris, staged on a Friday evening in late July 2024, generated international controversy over a tableau widely read as a parody of the Last Supper. In the Badlands Media corpus the ceremony is treated not primarily as an aesthetic or religious scandal but as a deliberate instrument of distraction and division, an interpretation set out in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s essay Are We Being Distracted?.

## The engineered-outrage reading

Carlson concedes that the ceremony "has offended many, rightfully so," and reports that he was "just as offended as everyone else when I first saw posts about the opening ceremony debauchery." His argument turns on what happened next: by the following day, when he noticed that every other post in his feed concerned the ceremony, he concluded that he was being manipulated. The questions he then poses — "Do you think this wasn't their intention? Do you think anger isn't exactly the reaction they wanted from the world?" — frame the offence as the product rather than the by-product of the staging.

He connects the outrage to a broader theory of control: "Think division. A divided nation is a weak nation. A divided world is a dangerous world. When we are divided and distracted, we are more easily controlled." The essay notes that offence was taken across religious lines — by "Jews, Christians, Muslims, Hindus, Buddhists and agnostics alike" — which Carlson treats as evidence of design rather than of accident.

## The Last Supper tableau

Carlson describes the scene as "the blasphemous Transgender Last Supper" and focuses on the figure seated at the head of the table, characterising her in deliberately provocative terms as "a morbidly obese, lesbian, Jewish woman" and asking, "Can you possibly check off any more boxes? Can you create any more hate and disgust?" The rhetorical point is that the combination of identities was selected to maximise offence across the greatest number of constituencies at once.

## Transgender athletes and the Olympic Committee

The essay pairs the ceremony with what Carlson presents as the [[international-olympic-committee|Olympic Committee]]'s actual policy toward transgender athletes: a rule under which athletes who have not fully transitioned by the age of twelve are barred from competition on the ground that later transition confers an unfair advantage. He treats the contrast as decisive — if the committee genuinely cared about transgender people, it would allow them to compete. Instead, he writes, "they don't care about them. They are being used by the [[cabal|Cabal]] to upset and distract the world," and those used "don't understand they are being used, they believe they are being appreciated."

As a supporting observation, Carlson notes that no biological women appear to seek competition against men: "They claim there is no advantage to being born a male, yet none of the women who transition from female to male seem to ever compete against other males." He adds that if transgender athletes "were finishing in the middle of the pack, no one would care," implying that visible dominance in women's events is itself part of the provocation.

## Function within the July 2024 news cycle

Within the article's larger thesis, the ceremony's role is chronological as much as cultural. While the world's attention was fixed on Paris, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was speaking at the [[bitcoin-2024-conference|Bitcoin Conference]], where he called for the United States government to stockpile [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] as a means of strengthening the dollar. That, Carlson argues, was the story the [[bread-and-circus|bread and circus]] was covering.
