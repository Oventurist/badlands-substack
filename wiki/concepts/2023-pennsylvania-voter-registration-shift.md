# 2023 Pennsylvania Voter Registration Shift

The 2023 Pennsylvania voter registration shift refers to the departure of tens of thousands of registered Democrats from the party in the swing state of Pennsylvania during 2023, reported by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] in January 2024 and carried in the Badlands News Brief of January 12, 2024.[1]

## The numbers

Nearly 59,000 registered Pennsylvania Democrats left the party in 2023 — more people than would be needed to fill Franklin Field football stadium at the University of Pennsylvania.[1] Of those, 36,950 switched to the Republican party and 21,644 switched their affiliation to "other," the category the Pennsylvania Department of State uses to cover parties such as the Green and Libertarian parties.[1]

Conservative political strategist [[charlie-gerow|Charlie Gerow]] attributed the movement to the Democratic Party tilting further to the progressive left, driving historically traditional, working-class families toward the Republican Party in both voting behavior and registration, and described a significant shift underway in the party's base.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that while Republicans were taking a victory lap over the GOP registration gains, the bigger story was the 21,644 voters who moved to "other," which she read as evidence that the American people are largely finished with the two-party system.[1]

She drew a parallel with Colorado, where per the Colorado Secretary of State's website there were 3,810,616 active voters as of January 2, 2024, the vast majority of them unaffiliated, a trend increasing since 2010.[1] Democrats fell from 28.2% of registrations in 2022 to 26.8% in 2024, Republicans from 25.1% to 23.7%, while unaffiliated voters rose from 44.8% to 47.7%; third parties held steady at around 2%.[1] She noted that Colorado's open primaries may drive unaffiliated registration, since unaffiliated voters can vote in either primary and, as seen in 2022, heavily influence Republican candidate selection — a mechanism not yet in place in Pennsylvania, though she warned that activists were pushing to change that.[1]

Her conclusion was that the data show no GOP victory but rather that "the American People are politically nonbinary," a development she framed as good for the awakening even as it makes party narratives hard to stomach.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] added a complementary reading, describing the information war as "bicameral": the actuals, he argued, are moving the world away from terror regimes, globalist proxies and jingoism toward a multi-polar order defined by sovereignty, mutual security and free trade, while the narratives follow the same path in increasingly cartoonish fashion, exposing collectivism and subversive communism in favor of nationalism, traditionalism and stoicism.[1]
