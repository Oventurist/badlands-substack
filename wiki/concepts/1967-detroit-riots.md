# 1967 Detroit Riots

The Detroit riots of July 1967 left 43 people dead, 1,189 injured and 2,000 buildings destroyed.[1] Badlands Media cites the episode chiefly for the response it drew from Senator [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]], who was asked in the midst of the unrest what he would do if he were president.[1]

Kennedy answered that he would force the media to show all of America what life is really like in the ghettos: "Let them show the sound, the feel, the hopelessness, and what it's like to think you'll never get out. Show a black teenager, told by some radio jingle to stay in school, looking at his older brother — who stayed in school who is out of a job. Show the Mafia pushing narcotics; put a candid camera team in a ghetto school and watch what a rotten system of education it really is. Film a mother staying up all night to keep the rats from her baby… Then ask people to watch it… and experience what it was like to live in the most affluent society in history — without hope."[1]

Badlands treats this answer as characteristic of Kennedy's method: rather than reaching for repression, he sought to make the material despair behind the violence visible to the country that had produced it.[1] Later that same summer Kennedy and [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] began the close collaboration that would culminate in King pledging the civil rights movement's full support to Kennedy's 1968 presidential run.[1]
