# Shared Interests

"Shared Interests" is a phrase used by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the 2018 Helsinki press conference with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], and adopted by Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] as the organizing concept of a February 23, 2023 essay arguing that a coalition of world leaders is quietly cooperating to build a replacement for the central-bank-dominated global financial system.[1]

## Origin of the term

At Helsinki, Trump said that if the world's problems were to be solved, nations "are going to have to find ways to cooperate in pursuit of shared interests," pointing to U.S.–Russian cooperation in the Second World War and the maintenance of dialogue during the Cold War.[1] Earlier, on the sidelines of the NATO summit in Brussels, Trump had described Putin not as an enemy but as a "competitor" who might "turn into a friend in due time," adding: "he's representing Russia and I'm representing the United States."[1] Lange argues Trump chose the word deliberately, both to provoke a press corps then focused on Russian collusion allegations and to signal that a coalition was forming, since competition "can spur innovation, efficiency and achievements never thought possible."[1]

## Distinction from common interests

Lange draws a distinction between *common interests* — things nations merely happen to have in common, such as a general desire to be free of what he calls the corrupt global central bank debt system — and *shared interests*, which involve active cooperation in a concrete plan of action.[1] In this framing, the [[brics|BRICS]] nations, together with Trump and prospective member states, are pursuing a shared interest: jointly removing and replacing the existing global debt system.[1]

## Autonomy as the objective

The essay treats "autonomous" as a second key term, glossing it as not controlled by outside forces, independent in judgment, and self-governing.[1] On this reading, the shared interest binding Trump, Putin, [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and the BRICS states is independence from central banks so that each country can be self-governing and manage its economy for the benefit of its own people.[1] Lange concludes that these leaders are "bankrupting the central banks global debt system and building its replacement," and promises a follow-up installment on the digital currencies he believes will emerge from it.[1]
