# Primate Dominance Meta-Analysis

The primate dominance meta-analysis was a five-year study published in July 2025 in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences that challenged the idea of a fixed, sex-based dominance hierarchy — the "alpha male" paradigm — in primates.[1]

## Method and findings

The analysis compiled aggression, threat, and ritualized dominance data from 253 populations representing 121 primate species in order to assess sex-based power dynamics.[1] Researchers found that males dominated females in just 17% of interactions and that females dominated males in 13%, results the authors read as refuting a universal hierarchy.[1] More than half of recorded agonistic encounters involved cross-sex confrontations, indicating widespread inter-sex competition across species.[1]

The study concluded that ecological conditions, reproductive tactics, and group demographics, among other factors, shape primate power relations rather than a fixed sex-based hierarchy.[1] Female primates were found to use reproductive strategies — such as concealed ovulation in bonobos and competition for caregiving males — to tip power in their favor.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Ashe in America responded to the finding with a single dismissive line: "Scientists finally discovered why primates stopped evolving."[1]
