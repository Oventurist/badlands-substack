# US Precision Strike Planning Against Iran (2026)

In January 2026 the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration was reported to be weighing precision strikes against senior Iranian officials and commanders linked to crackdowns on protesters, alongside a potential full blockade of Iranian oil exports intended to collapse the economy and exploit what US intelligence assessed as the government's most vulnerable position since the 1979 revolution.[1]

## Force posture and signaling

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] boasted of deploying a "beautiful armada," including the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group, while stating openness to negotiations and simultaneously warning of military options.[1] [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Iranian Revolutionary Guards]] warned that attacks could disrupt global oil supplies through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], and that neighboring countries permitting the use of their territory would be treated as hostile.[1]

## Iranian response

[[iran|Iran]] implemented emergency measures to ensure government continuity and essential supplies, including the delegation of expanded powers to provincial governors and the designation of successors for key officials — the package described as the [[iran-continuity-of-government-measures-2026|Iran continuity of government measures]].[1] Iranian officials spoke of a "heavy shadow of war" hanging over the region.[1]

## Regional de-escalation

Regional de-escalation efforts continued in parallel, including Saudi–Iranian talks.[1] Iranian President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] telephoned Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] on January 27, 2026, according to the Tasnim News Agency, telling him that "Muslim Unity" was the key to addressing the region's destabilizing events — echoing language MBS had delivered to the 57 nations of the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation|Organization of Islamic Cooperation]] at a Riyadh meeting convened with King Salman in November 2023 following the October 7 attack, the extraordinary session at which [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] normalized relations with Iran and ended a rift dating to the 1979 Iranian Revolution.[1] Immediately after the call, Saudi Arabia announced that its airspace would not be used by any party to conduct operations against Iran.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified two patterns: that Saudi Arabia has repeatedly placed itself between Israel and the United States on one side and Iran on the other — notably in the week before the 2024 US presidential election, when Riyadh announced joint naval exercises with Iran while Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and other Republicans urged strikes on Iran — and that Trump appears to push adversary governments into [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocols.[1] He noted Graham threatening both Iran and Saudi Arabia in a single social media post.[1]
