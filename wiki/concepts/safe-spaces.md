# Safe Spaces

"Safe spaces" is a term describing physical or social environments in which members of a given group can gather without fear of harassment or judgment. The term first gained prominence in the mid-1960s within the LGBTQ+ community and the feminist movement, where it described venues such as gay and lesbian bars, and later expanded into educational institutions and other social settings as a label for spaces where marginalized groups could feel supported.[1]

## Reemergence, 2016–2020

The concept reemerged forcefully between 2016 and 2020, a period marked by the death of George Floyd, pandemic restrictions, and intense partisan polarization ahead of the 2020 election.[1] Libertarians, much of the political right and center, and portions of the anti-establishment left criticized the demand for safe spaces, variously characterizing it as a regression toward segregation or as evidence of arrested development.[1] When conservative speakers such as [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] and [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] appeared on college campuses, students frequently demanded that events be cancelled so the campus could function as a safe space — not for an ethnic or sexual minority, but for anyone who disagreed with the speaker.[1] Shapiro had at least five major speaking engagements cancelled by universities between 2017 and 2019, and Kirk had several as well.[1]

## From ribbing to censorship

What began as partisan mockery — a genre of low-effort memes on the right — turned to outrage as demands for safe spaces escalated into calls for political censorship on campus and online.[1] A survey by the [[foundation-for-individual-rights-and-expression|Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression]] found that 62% of college students believed shouting down speakers was acceptable to some degree, with conservative students reporting higher self-censorship (73% of strong Republicans versus 52% of strong Democrats).[1]

## Inversion after October 7

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that after the [[october-7-attacks|October 7th attacks]], the safe-space logic was inverted and adopted by the right: [[eyal-yakoby|Eyal Yakoby]] told a House press conference that he "did not feel safe" and called for COVID-grade enforcement to protect Jewish students, echoing the very rhetoric conservatives had mocked.[1] DeLarme frames both episodes as vehicles for [[government-sponsored-censorship|government-sponsored censorship]], concluding that "the people who want to erode your freedoms do not care if you acquiesce for a left-wing cause or a right-wing cause, as long as the needle is moved."[1]
