# Iran Hostage Crisis

The Iran Hostage Crisis was the seizure of 52 American diplomats by a group of militant Iranian students in November 1979, following the [[iranian-revolution|Iranian Revolution]] that ousted [[mohammad-reza-pahlavi|the Shah]] and eventually brought the anti-American Ayatollah [[ruhollah-khomeini|Khomeini]] to power.[1] The hostages were released on the day [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] was inaugurated as president, 20 January 1981.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

In a July 2023 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that the crisis was the pivot on which [[iran|Iran]]'s global image turned: it made Iran "the world's new 'bad guy'," a country viewed internationally as violent, savage and terroristic.[1] Carlson drew attention to the fact that the 52 hostages were released rather than executed, taking this as evidence that Iranians "really hated the US for what they had done to them for the past 26 years, but they weren't the savages they were being made out to be."[1] He suggested that after 44 years of boycotts, embargoes and isolation it would be unsurprising if many Iranians genuinely hated the United States, but that the better-informed among them would understand that the US Government and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], rather than the American people, were the proper objects of that hatred.[1]

Carlson treated the absence of an American military response as the crisis's most revealing feature. He asked whether it seemed in character for the CIA to allow a group of protestors to expel it from a resource-rich country it had controlled for 26 years without ever waging war or attempting to take it back, noting that the United States invaded Iraq and Afghanistan "under the guise that Saudis flew planes into American buildings" while the hostage crisis — a far more direct provocation — produced no invasion.[1] His answer was that the agency did not want the country back because it had discovered something more useful: "Superman needed a Lex Luther."[1] He compared belief in a spontaneous, permanent popular overthrow of a CIA-controlled Iran to belief that protestors with sledgehammers ended the Soviet Union at the Berlin Wall, where no snipers fired, in contrast to Ukraine in 2014 where, in his account, snipers picked off protestors because the [[deep-state|Deep State]] needed chaos to break a pro-Putin government.[1]

## Cultural memory

Carlson noted that younger readers are likely to know the hostage crisis primarily through the Ben Affleck film [[argo-film|Argo]], which he treats as an instrument of [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] narrative management in [[hollywood|Hollywood]] released shortly before the agency's public admission of its role in the [[1953-iranian-coup|1953 coup]].[1]
