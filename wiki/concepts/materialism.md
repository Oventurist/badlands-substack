# Materialism

**Materialism** is the philosophical position, treated in Badlands Media's [[the-white-pill-emporium|White Pill Emporium]] series as the defining handicap of contemporary science, that only physically measurable things are real. Part 2 of the series, published on 2 November 2024 by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], argues that the scientific paradigm as it exists today — "to the extent that any coherent paradigm can be found there at all" — does not furnish a complete methodology for arriving at the truth of any matter, precisely because materialism wholesale denies the importance, and often the existence, of those aspects of reality unavailable to physical measurement, including mind, spirit and the metaphysical design aspects of reality.[1]

## The charge of self-contradiction

The essay contends that for many people, particularly trained scientists and academics, whatever cannot be perceived by the physical senses might as well not exist at all, and that the very notion of metaphysics is instinctively repugnant to them.[1] It argues this stance is self-undermining: every time such a thinker uses an abstract tool such as language or mathematics, he temporarily leaves the world of provisional empirical observation and dips his toes into the metaphysics he denies.[1]

## The argument from mathematics

The core argument runs through the example of a falling apple. Observing the apple fall does not, in and of itself, furnish an equation for gravity; only by devising a measuring system and an attendant mathematical model can definitive predictions be made.[1] Since mathematics proceeds purely by necessary operations of logic rather than by observation — no empirical investigation is required to know that two and two make four, because doubling is inherent to the definition of two — none of mathematics was discovered by the empirical method.[1] The author concludes that scientific practice consists of *coupling* something ultimately non-physical, namely logic, to the physical world in order to make sense of the latter, and that the discoverability of physical law by logic and mathematics shows physical reality to be inherently logical.[1]

## Consequences claimed

From this the essay argues that mainstream science and academia have steadfastly ignored a glaring issue: many theories exist for understanding things, but until the [[cognitive-theoretic-model-of-the-universe|Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe]] no one had developed a theory of the way theories relate to their respective domains.[1] The author further holds that the academic world's preference for materialism over metaphysics has cut people off from one of their most potent sources of power, asserting that one will never master the material realm by looking for power only in matter.[1] This claim underwrites the series' wider argument that phenomena such as the [[law-of-attraction|law of attraction]] and synchronicity, and the improbable events surrounding the [[q-operation|Q operation]], are explicable rather than anomalous.[1]
