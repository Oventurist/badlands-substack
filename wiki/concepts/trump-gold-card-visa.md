# Trump Gold Card Visa

The "gold card" is an immigration program rolled out by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in September 2025, offering wealthy foreigners a $1 million fast-track to live and work in the United States.[1] The program had been promised by the administration for months before the formal rollout on a Friday in mid-September 2025.[1]

## Structure

Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] told reporters on a call following the announcement that the gold card would replace the existing EB-1 and EB-2 visa categories for people deemed of "exceptional value" to the United States.[1] The administration expected to make roughly 80,000 gold cards available initially, while continuing to evaluate the model and potentially phasing out other visa categories over time.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] speculated about the consequences of applying the executive order retroactively to companies within what he termed "the Regime construct," asking what such a move would do to the national debt.[1] He characterized the mechanism as "an incredible, beneath-the-surface tool to hold accountable those who have committed crimes against America and crimes against humanity," adding that any real accountability would likely occur "off-screen."[1]

## Announcement (February 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced the program on Tuesday, February 25, 2025, telling reporters in the Oval Office that the $5 million investor visa would be up and running in two weeks and describing it as a "Gold Card" mimicking the green cards held by permanent residents.[2] He said the existing EB-5 investor visa would be scrapped in favor of "selling a Gold Card" to those who want to invest in the United States: "It's going to be a route to citizenship, and wealthy people will be coming into our country by buying this card. They will be wealthy, they will be successful, they will be spending a lot of money and paying a lot of taxes."[2] The announcement came as other immigration routes expanded under the Biden administration were being curtailed, particularly for applicants from Central and South America, while Trump continued to advocate legal, work-based immigration as a source of money and jobs.[2]
