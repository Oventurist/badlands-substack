# 2024 Haiti Gang Crisis

The 2024 Haiti gang crisis was the collapse of state authority in [[haiti|Haiti]] during which armed gangs seized control of much of the capital, Port-au-Prince, culminating in the resignation of Prime Minister [[ariel-henry|Ariel Henry]] on March 12, 2024 and an emergency American military deployment to protect the US embassy.[1]

## Collapse of order in Port-au-Prince

By mid-March 2024 armed gangs had taken over much of Port-au-Prince and a long-simmering security emergency was, in the words of contemporaneous reporting, "boiling over," with violence spreading beyond the capital.[1] Criminal warlords presided over what was described as an utter state of lawlessness.[1] The preceding years had seen a series of kidnappings targeting Americans, including aid workers, as well as other foreign nationals, and the US embassy had for months urged American citizens still in the country to depart the island immediately.[1]

## US military response

On Wednesday, March 13, 2024, [[us-southern-command|US Southern Command]] announced that, at the request of the [[state-department|State Department]], the elite Marine Fleet Anti-terrorism Security Team had been deployed to Haiti to reinforce embassy security.[1] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] confirmed the urgent deployment of the counter-terror task force.[1] SOUTHCOM stated it was "prepared with a wide range of contingency plans to ensure the safety and security of U.S. Citizens in Haiti," and the FAST element was expected both to supplement the existing Marine embassy guard in the event of an attack and to assist in evacuating non-essential American personnel.[1]

The Department of Defense also announced that it had doubled funding for the [[multinational-security-support-mission|Multinational Security Support]] mission that week, and said it was working with Haitian, [[kenya|Kenyan]] and other partners to expedite the mission's deployment in support of the Haitian National Police and the restoration of security.[1]

## Migration dimension

Officials warned of a potential "mass migration" wave out of Haiti that could inundate US coastal communities, particularly in Florida, as a consequence of the collapse.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the episode as a case of "Convergence" — both actual and narrative — arguing that while it was virtually impossible to determine events on the ground through the fog of the information war, the reported sequence of assassinations, resignations and all-out gang war amounted to a transfer of power in Port-au-Prince.[1] He suggested that the dominant public impression was one of chaos that might spill onto American shores, chaos he traced to seeds planted during the Clinton, Bush and especially Obama administrations, and noted that the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] remained firmly entrenched in public memory regarding Haiti.[1]
