# A Conflict of Interest

"A Conflict of Interest" is a Badlands Media article by Joe Lange, published October 3, 2024. The piece advances a conspiratorial framework in which [[q-anon|Q drops]] identify money as the "keystone" of corruption, and argues that successive U.S. administrations—particularly those of [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]], [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], and figures such as [[john-mccain|John McCain]]—have systematically diverted taxpayer funds to hostile actors and terrorist organizations under the cover of military intervention.

The article opens by asserting that the political enemy values "money" above all else, specifically "taxpayer theft," and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] poses a unique threat to this system because he disrupts the flow of public funds to hidden networks. According to the source, [[q-anon|Q drop #2807]] explicitly framed taxpayer money as the common denominator connecting corruption and treason, and the article uses this as a thematic through-line.

## The Iraq War Cash Shipments

A central claim concerns the movement of approximately $40 billion in U.S. cash from the New York Federal Reserve to Iraq between 2003 and 2008. Citing a 2011 CNBC report, the article describes this as "the largest airborne transfer of currency in the history of the world." The cash was palletized at a New York Fed compound in East Rutherford, New Jersey, trucked to Andrews Air Force Base, and flown aboard C-17 transport aircraft to Baghdad International Airport.

The article claims that once the cargo arrived in Baghdad, the chain of custody ended. All flights were met by a single man—a previously unknown [[coalition-provisional-authority|Coalition Provisional Authority]] official, a naturalized American citizen of Lebanese descent named Basel, born in Saudi Arabia and formerly employed as a translator—who was responsible for transporting the pallets to the [[central-bank-of-iraq|Central Bank of Iraq]] along Route Irish, a seven-mile road the U.S. military considered one of the most dangerous in the country. The source notes that a typical pallet held 640 bundles of a thousand bills each and weighed 1,500 pounds, and argues that Basel's singular role, combined with the absence of documentation after the cash left the airport, indicates deliberate design rather than bureaucratic oversight. CNBC reported that Basel was, by 2011, negotiating a contract to move billions of dollars in cash into newly liberated Libya and planning a trip to Sudan.

The article further claims that an investigation during the [[george-w-bush|Bush administration]] uncovered more than $1.5 billion in a bunker in Lebanon, and that Bush personally suppressed the investigation. The money allegedly ended up funding [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]. The source connects Basel's background to [[prince-alwaleed|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]], noting that both were born in Saudi Arabia and of Lebanese descent, and speculates that Basel was affiliated with Alwaleed, who in turn had strong ties to the Bush family and the [[cia|CIA]].

## JFK Jr. and the Pattern of Elimination

The article links the theme of taxpayer theft to the death of [[jfk-jr|John F. Kennedy Jr.]], claiming that the enemy eliminated him because his planned Senate run in New York and higher political aspirations threatened their control. The source asserts that [[jfk-jr|JFK Jr.]] was loved by the American people, was aware of the enemy's identity, and that his plane was sabotaged. It further claims that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was running for the same Senate seat at the time, had no chance of defeating him, and that his death cleared her path.

## The Libya Intervention and John McCain

The article shifts to [[libya|Libya]], arguing that the same "taxpayer theft" playbook was used there, on an even larger scale. It claims that [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] was killed in October 2011, and that five days later a CNBC report revealed Basel was negotiating a new contract to transport billions of dollars into Libya. The source frames this as evidence that the U.S. had been funding its enemies in Iraq and was about to do the same in Libya.

The article focuses heavily on [[john-mccain|John McCain]], claiming he served as the primary political legitimizer for rebel groups in both Libya and Syria. It cites a 2011 Politico report about McCain's April 2011 visit to Benghazi, where he praised the anti-Gaddafi rebels as "heroes" and urged the U.S. and NATO to recognize the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as Libya's legitimate government. The source argues that McCain's high-profile advocacy was designed to funnel taxpayer money to these groups.

According to the article, the rebels McCain supported were members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] (LIFG), an organization the U.S. military, the [[treasury-department|Treasury Department]] and the [[state-department|State Department]] had designated as an [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] affiliate. The source cites the Treasury's October 30, 2008 designation of three LIFG members under Executive Order 13224, quoting OFAC director Adam Szubin, and notes that [[ayman-al-zawahiri|Ayman al-Zawahiri]] announced in November 2007 that LIFG had become an official Al-Qaeda affiliate. The group had been listed as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist on September 23, 2001 and added to the [[united-nations|UN]] 1267 Committee Consolidated List on October 6, 2001. A [[combating-terrorism-center|Combating Terrorism Center]] at West Point study is cited as evidence that the U.S. military knew LIFG fighters were being sent to kill American soldiers in Iraq, with Libya supplying the most foreign fighters relative to population.

The article emphasises McCain's seniority on the [[senate-armed-services-committee|Senate Armed Services Committee]] to argue that he could not have been ignorant of these findings, and treats [[congress|Congress]] as the mechanism through which appropriated funds reached the groups his advocacy legitimised.

The article claims that McCain knowingly met with Al-Qaeda-affiliated terrorists and described them as "brave fighters" and "patriots," calling him "the worst kind of traitor." It further claims that [[benghazi|Benghazi]] became a regional hub for Islamist extremist training after Gaddafi's fall, with Syrian insurgents arriving weekly for training before being flown to Syria through Turkey.

## Mouza Moustafa and the Syrian Rebel Lobby

The article identifies [[mouza-moustafa|Mouza Moustafa]] as a central figure in the effort to channel U.S. taxpayer money to [[isis|ISIS]] through lobbying. Citing an LPR.com profile, the source claims Moustafa was born in Damascus, moved to the U.S. as a teenager, and served as Executive Director of the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] (SETF), Political Director of United For a Free Syria (UFS), and board member of the Coalition for a Democratic Syria (CDS).

According to the article, Moustafa previously served as Executive Director of the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] and worked closely with the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] during the Libya intervention. The source claims that Moustafa was the "go-between" for [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and [[isis|ISIS leader]] [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]], and that McCain traveled to Syria in 2013 to meet with ISIS leaders. The article asserts that [[barack-obama|President Obama]] gave these groups his "full approval and blessing," and that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was similarly complicit.

## Abdelhakim Belhaj

The article profiles [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] as the emir of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] and a key figure in the Libya operation. Citing a Wikipedia entry, the source notes that Belhaj was born May 1, 1966, and became leader of the Islamist al-Watan Party and former head of the Tripoli Military Council after Gaddafi's fall.

According to the article, Belhaj fought in Afghanistan against the Soviets in the 1980s, where he first met [[osama-bin-laden|Osama Bin Laden]]. He was allegedly interrogated by the [[cia|CIA]] in the early 2000s, held in Libya's Abu Salim Prison, and released in 2009 through negotiations involving [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] preacher [[ali-sallabi|Ali Sallabi]] and Gaddafi's son, [[saif-al-islam-gaddafi|Saif al-Islam]]. The source claims the Obama administration pressured Gaddafi to release Belhaj to maintain goodwill with the new president, whom it describes as having "strong ties to the Muslim Brotherhood."

After his release, Belhaj allegedly went to [[qatar|Qatar]] to command the [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]], which the article claims was assigned security at the Benghazi consulate and did nothing to stop the 2012 attack. The source further claims that Belhaj became a billionaire businessman with interests in health care, property, air transport, and media across Libya, Tunisia, Sudan, and Turkey, owning an airline called al-Ajniha that held a monopoly on flights to Tripoli's Maatiga Airport. The article suggests this airline was used to transport [[isis|ISIS]] fighters from Libya to Syria through Turkey.

The article notes that a British parliamentary report pointed to Belhaj's involvement in migrant smuggling and that in January 2019 the Libyan public prosecutor, Siddiq Al Sour, issued a warrant for his arrest on charges of "abuses and war crimes," causing him to flee to Istanbul. It connects this to the June 2017 Qatar diplomatic crisis, after which Belhaj was placed on a terrorist watchlist by Egypt, the UAE, Bahrain, [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and Libya's Tobruk government on suspicion of terrorism-related activity with [[qatar|Qatari]] support — a blockade the article credits to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump's]] approval. Near the end of the Obama administration, the source adds, Belhaj was negotiating with [[united-nations|United Nations]] special representative Martin Kobler over the installation of Fayez El Sarraj's national unity government in Tripoli.

## Conclusion

The article concludes by asserting that the conflict between "insiders" and "outsiders" is fundamentally about money, and that the enemy has already lost control. It frames [[donald-trump|Trump]] as the champion of outsiders who accepted the reality of this war long before his presidential campaign, and claims that "payback is coming." The piece positions itself as part of a larger series on taxpayer theft and deep state corruption.
