# Breslov Hasidism

Breslov Hasidism is the Hasidic sect founded by [[nachman-of-breslov|Rebbe Nachman of Breslov]] (1772–1810), great-grandson of the [[baal-shem-tov|Baal Shem Tov]].[1] It is distinguished by Nachman's parables, its fusion of [[kabbalah|Kabbalistic]] mysticism with Torah study, and the annual Rosh Hashanah pilgrimage to Nachman's gravesite in Uman, Ukraine.[1]

## Pilgrimage to Uman

Nachman taught that the most important act his followers could perform was to spend the Jewish New Year in his physical presence, a teaching now honored by tens of thousands of Israeli citizens who travel each year from Israel to the Uman gravesite.[1] The Badlands News Brief observes that the pilgrimage has generated recurring friction between the Israeli and Ukrainian governments amid Ukraine's war with Russia.[1]

## Contemporary leadership

The sect's contemporary leader is the Israeli rebbe [[eliezer-berland|Eliezer Berland]], who has also claimed to be the Messiah and who has been convicted of rape and fraud and arrested in connection with two murders that Israeli authorities allege were occult ritual killings carried out by his followers.[1] In September 2022, Ukrainian authorities refused Berland entry at the border when he attempted the Uman pilgrimage, causing a diplomatic dispute with Israel.[1]

## Badlands framing

In commentary by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the Brief situates Breslov Hasidism inside a broader argument that the mystical Kabbalistic strain of Judaism — as opposed to Biblical, Torah-based Judaism — underlies what the writer calls an emerging Jewish Supremacy movement among radical Israeli factions, and asks why the United States government and American Christians support it.[1] This discussion appears in the Brief's treatment of Ukraine's law banning religious organizations tied to Russia, which the writer frames as evidence that the Ukraine conflict is in part a religious and spiritual war.[1]
