# Cryptography

**Cryptography** is the study and practice of secret writing and messages.[1] A cryptographic key allows a person either to create (encrypt) or to solve (decrypt) a cryptographic puzzle, functioning much like a Rosetta Stone or translator in a game of hiding and discovering secrets.[1] The contest can be analog, digital, or hybrid, and modern methods also include one-way transformations such as secure hashing algorithms.[1] In the terms cited by Badlands Media, data encryption is a way of translating data from plaintext to ciphertext, with encrypted data accessible only via an encryption key.[1]

## Codes and ciphers

Enciphering, encoding, and encryption are treated as broadly synonymous terms describing different areas of the same discipline, as are their inverse forms.[1] Codes are systems of signals or symbols — letters, numbers, shapes and the like — that carry assigned, abstract, and often secret meanings, and exist for the purpose of communication.[1] Ciphers transform data in order to conceal meaning, cannot be decoded without the key, and may use any combination of symbols, letters, and numbers.[1] Both codes and ciphers are cryptographic puzzles.[1]

Because the human mind thinks abstractly, the possible constructions are effectively endless; people continually assign abstract meaning to symbols and then expend enormous energy either keeping the system secret or persuading others to adopt it.[1]

## Strategic significance

Encryption in many forms has protected information from unauthorized access during storage and transmission throughout recorded history, and the competitive advantage that protected information confers makes it one of the most powerful weapons of [[information-warfare|information warfare]].[1] Sources cited in the article note that codes and ciphers have carried secret messages in times of war and unrest, protecting military and political information should documents fall into enemy hands; that the race to design ever more complicated codes and to break them continues into the era of computer-generated "unbreakable" encryption; and that breaks have come both from captured codebooks or keys and message errors, and from patient study of mathematical frequencies and linguistic patterns.[1]

Codes and ciphers are therefore treated as a factor in a civilization's rise, longevity, and fall, and are heavily researched and used by militaries.[1] A modern example is the Secure Hash Algorithm 2 family, created by the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] in collaboration with the National Institute of Science and Technology; the family consists of six hash functions, one of which — SHA-256 — is the algorithm used in mining [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]].[1]
