# The Temple Trap

"The Temple Trap" is a long-form narrative analysis by Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], described by its author as his most popular feature of 2025.[1]

## Thesis

The piece explores what Burning Bright calls the ongoing attempt by the establishment to saddle [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with the sins of a dark cabal he not only stands opposed to, but that he may have infiltrated some time ago.[1] Framed within Badlands' broader "War of Stories" analysis, it argues that "truth is not merely stranger than fiction; it is based on it," and that the fiction which maps most perfectly onto Trump is not the one surface-level interpreters believe they see — posing the question of whether Trump is an analogue for Bruce Wayne, "or is it the other way around?"[1]

## Application to the Epstein saga

Responding in December 2025 to the release of photographs of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s island by House Democrats and to [[colin-jost|Colin Jost]]'s joke about them on [[saturday-night-live|Saturday Night Live]], Burning Bright laid out three branches: if the Epstein operation was real and as truthers understood it, Trump has nothing to fear; if it was false, Trump has nothing to fear and the machine loses power to inflame the "hivemind"; and if — his heaviest leaning — it was real but not exactly as believed, representing an attempt to gain damaging material on influential leaders, socialites and magnates for a "disembodied cabal" without being the true core of that grouping's designs, then Trump not only has nothing to fear but may enjoy the exposures to come.[1]

In each case, he argued, the establishment is "stampeding under their own momentum straight into the exposure they spent their careers trying to prevent," leaving the collective mind more suspicious of everyone in power — "both in terms of what they say in the light, and more importantly, what they do in the shadows."[1] The trap, he wrote, "was built years ago," with Trump "holding the bicameral door wide open, smiling as they rush through it."[1]
