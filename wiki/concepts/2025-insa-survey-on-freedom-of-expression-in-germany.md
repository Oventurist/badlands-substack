# 2025 INSA Survey on Freedom of Expression in Germany

The 2025 INSA survey on freedom of expression in [[germany|Germany]] was an opinion poll reported in August 2025 that found widespread perception among Germans that people withhold their opinions out of fear of consequences.[1]

## Findings

The survey reported that 84% of German respondents believed people withhold opinions due to fear of consequences, with only 9% disagreeing.[1] A majority — 54% — said they had personally felt unable to express their opinions freely.[1] This perception was rising, showing a six-point increase over the previous survey conducted eight months earlier.[1]

Younger respondents, especially those under 30, reported feeling stifled more than older respondents.[1] Among supporters of political parties, voters for the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD) reported the highest sense of restriction at 76%.[1] Analysts summarizing the poll suggested that free speech could become as politically pivotal an issue in Germany as migration and pandemic policy, and emphasized the need to foster a culture of open dialogue and diverse opinion.[1]

## Commentary

In the Badlands News Brief, [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that the German writer Constantin Von Hoffmeister had expressed the same sentiment during a recent interview, arguing that threats against speech in Germany have proven very effective and that the German people appear unwilling to defy what he termed the Regime.[1]
