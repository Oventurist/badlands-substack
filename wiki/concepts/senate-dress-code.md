# Senate Dress Code

The Senate dress code was an informal, unwritten set of attire requirements observed on the floor of the [[united-states-senate|United States Senate]]. In September 2023 it was quietly abandoned by Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] (D-NY), leaving senators free to wear whatever they wanted on the floor.[1]

## The 2023 change

Several outlets reported on Monday, September 18, 2023, that the Senate's Sergeant at Arms had been instructed to stop enforcing the chamber's unwritten dress requirements for legislators, a change first reported by [[axios|Axios]].[1] The move followed a series of state legislatures that had recently revisited their own dress requirements on the grounds that they were "oppressive" and, per opponents, racist and sexist — with some arguing that unconventional clothing could serve as an effective "statement of resistance."[1]

## Fetterman as beneficiary

Reporting identified Senator [[john-fetterman|John Fetterman]] (D-PA), who favors gym shorts and hoodies over traditional business attire, as a principal beneficiary; the change allowed him to remain on the Senate floor before and after votes rather than avoiding the chamber.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], noted reports that since his election Fetterman had hidden in the cloakroom and entered the chamber only to cast votes, attributing this to the now-defunct dress code's suit requirement.[1] Her commentary mocked the rationale that dress codes are ableist, argued that Schumer had scrapped the rule rather than tolerate that charge of bigotry, and concluded that the episode proved "we are not a serious country."[1]
