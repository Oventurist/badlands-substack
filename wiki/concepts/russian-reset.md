# Russian Reset

The "Russian Reset" was the Obama administration's declared effort to improve relations with Russia, launched in March 2009 when Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] presented Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov with a symbolic "Reset" button. As The Week noted in an account quoted by the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]], the button carried a translation error, using the Russian word for "overcharged" instead of "reset."

Gaffe aside, the same report credits the policy with results: [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and President Dmitri Medvedev negotiated a new arms control treaty and safe passage for Afghanistan-bound American troops through Russia, Medvedev agreed to approve tough U.N. sanctions on [[iran|Iran]], and Obama helped bring Russia into the World Trade Organization.

The Badlands article rejects the conventional reading. Citing Q drop #48 of November 2, 2017 — which quoted Obama in May 2010 saying "Russia should be viewed as a friendly partner under Section 123 the Atomic Energy Act of 1954" and asked "What did the Russia reset really provide? Clearance/pathway to complete the U1 deal?" — the author argues the reset was planned in advance to establish a public narrative that Russia was no longer an adversary, thereby clearing the political path for the sale of [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] to [[rosatom|Rosatom]].

The same Q drop asked "Why is the Canadian PM so important?", which the article connects to the several Canadian figures at the centre of the deal — [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]] and [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]] among them — and to alleged panic on the part of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]. The essay treats the reset as an early illustration of narrative management, contrasting it with Hillary Clinton's later accusations that Vladimir Putin helped [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] win the 2016 election, only six years after she had, in the author's account, allowed Russia to control a majority of the world's uranium.
