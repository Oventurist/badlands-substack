# Born-Alive Abortion Survivors Protection Act

The Born-Alive Abortion Survivors Protection Act is proposed United States legislation that would require health care practitioners to provide the same level of medical attention and treatment to infants born alive during or after an attempted abortion as they would to any other newborn. The bill stated that "any infant born alive after an abortion or within a hospital, clinic, or other facility has the same claim to the protection of the law that would arise for any newborn, or for any person who comes to a hospital, clinic, or other facility for screening and treatment or otherwise becomes a patient within its care."[1]

## January 2025 Senate vote

On Wednesday, January 22, 2025, Senate Democrats blocked the bill. It failed on a 52–47 vote, short of the 60-vote threshold needed to advance in the [[united-states-senate|Senate]]. Proponents said the measure would ensure that newborns surviving abortions received medical care; critics argued it would add legal risks for doctors.[1]

Senate Minority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] characterized the bill as a partisan attack on reproductive rights, medical professionals and American women.[1]
