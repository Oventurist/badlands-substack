# Israeli Economic Autarky

Israeli economic autarky refers to the policy direction articulated by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in September 2025, under which [[israel|Israel]] would reshape its economy to withstand sweeping international sanctions and boycotts arising from the war in [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## Netanyahu's warning

Speaking at the Finance Ministry's annual accountant general conference in Jerusalem, Netanyahu said mounting international pressure over the Gaza war was pushing the country toward economic self-sufficiency.[1] "We will increasingly have to adapt to an economy with autarkic features," he said, adding: "I believe in free markets, I worked to bring Israel a free-market revolution. But we may find ourselves in a situation where our defense industries are blocked."[1] The remark landed against a backdrop of concrete disengagement measures, including Spain's cancellation of roughly €1 billion in Israeli defense contracts.[1]

## Structural critique

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] offered an extended argument that autarky is not available to Israel as a practical matter.[1] He noted that Israel possesses no significant natural resources, sits on arid desert, has no timber base, can barely grow enough food and therefore depends on imports, and lacks mineral deposits.[1] Some oil shale deposits exist, but there are no commercial oil shale operations; a small oil shale–powered plant commissioned in 1978 and completed in 1989 was decommissioned in 2011, with the operating company citing unprofitability and environmental harm, and subsequent testing has not produced extraction on the immediate horizon.[1]

Significant offshore natural gas deposits lie along the coast, but according to this analysis they sit in the territorial waters of Gaza — which he suggested is one reason Netanyahu is so eager to annex the territory.[1] Israel also has almost no manufacturing base and an economy highly dependent on imports; its two most robust industries are software and technology, and diamonds and gems, the latter anchored by the Israeli Diamond Exchange — described as the largest diamond exchange in the world — and itself wholly dependent on external sourcing from the continent of Africa.[1]

The conclusion drawn was that Israel is a country that cannot pursue an isolationist economy, principally for reasons of geography but also of culture: it does not possess the know-how or skill set to become self-reliant quickly, and even with access to that knowledge base the infrastructure and manufacturing buildout would take years.[1] Israel, on this reading, needs friends to survive, and would be wise to make peace with the Arabs, but its hubris betrays it.[1]

## Related developments

The autarky warning coincided with Arab and Islamic leaders' push to have Israel suspended from the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and with the [[spain-israel-arms-deal-cancellations-of-2025|Spanish cancellation of Israeli arms contracts]], both of which pointed toward the isolation Netanyahu was describing.[1]
