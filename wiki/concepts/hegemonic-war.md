# Hegemonic War

A **hegemonic war** is a systemic conflict over which power or bloc will set the terms of the international order.[1] In a November 2024 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], Ryan DeLarme argued that the true threat to the world order was not the regional flashpoints dominating headlines but the larger hegemonic war between [[nato|NATO]] and [[brics|BRICS]] — a contest to that point fought primarily in the economic, technological, and information spaces.[1]

## Fronts

In this framing the Ukraine–Russia proxy war is only one front of the wider hegemonic struggle.[1] Continued military action and NATO-country supply of military aid to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] kept tensions high, including Ukraine's use of U.S.-supplied [[atacms|ATACMS]] missiles to strike targets inside Russian territory.[1] Ongoing Middle Eastern conflicts involving Israel–Palestine, Iran, and Yemen contributed further regional instability, with rhetoric and actions from multiple players escalating.[1]

[[china|China]] — described by Napoleon Bonaparte as a "Sleeping Giant" — was portrayed as stirring militarily, with the [[peoples-liberation-army|PLA]] Southern Theater Command conducting combat readiness patrols over the waters and airspace of Huangyan Dao (Scarborough Shoal) on November 28, 2024, ostensibly to strengthen control and safeguard national sovereignty.[1] China and [[russia|Russia]] had declared a "no limits" partnership, with reports of covert Chinese supply of dual-use technology, increased trade offsetting Western sanctions, joint military exercises, and intelligence sharing.[1] Should tensions culminate in total war, the article argued, China would have its best opportunity to fulfill a self-appointed destiny as the next world superpower by backing Russia completely.[1]

## Escalation and de-escalation

The article catalogued late-2024 escalatory steps attributed to the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, including pressure on Ukraine to lower its minimum conscription age from 25 to 18, and the rapid collapse of the brokered Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire in Lebanon after the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] fired on a vehicle entering an evacuated zone in southern Lebanon.[1] DeLarme concluded that it would ultimately fall to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to decide whether to let neoconservatives drag the United States into total war against Russia, China, and Iran, and speculated that the approach to that precipice was designed to end not in catastrophe but as the final nail in the international [[deep-state|deep state]]'s coffin.[1]
