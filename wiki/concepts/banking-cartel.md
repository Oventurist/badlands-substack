# Banking Cartel

"Banking Cartel" is the term used in Badlands Media commentary for the network of large commercial banks, central bankers, and allied regulators held to act in concert to consolidate and centralise financial power.[1] In a May 31, 2023 essay, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the Banking Cartel "always tends to make moves that further consolidate and centralize their power," and applied the concept to the conduct of major institutions during the [[2023-banking-crisis|2023 banking crisis]].[1]

## Consolidation through crisis

The core claim is that banking crises function as consolidation events. [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] absorbed [[washington-mutual|Washington Mutual]], [[bear-stearns|Bear Stearns]] and other distressed assets during the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]], and acquired [[first-republic-bank|First Republic Bank]] in May 2023, reaching $3.7 trillion in assets under a growth model built, by the firm's own account, on more than 1,200 predecessor institutions.[1] The essay traced the underlying conditions of both crises to the domestic policy of the [[us-congress|United States Congress]] and the monetary policy of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], described as a private central bank.[1]

## Offensive posture toward critics and depositors

The essay also characterised the cartel as taking an offensive posture against those who bet against or criticise banks. [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] urged the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] to pursue [[short-selling|short sellers]] and those spreading "false information" about banks, and within two weeks the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] signalled forthcoming action against short sellers.[1] On the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] Economy Update of May 25, 2023, [[sean-morgan|Sean Morgan]] and [[kirk-elliott|Kirk Elliott]] reported that a London bank had begun requiring customers to justify in writing any withdrawal exceeding £2,000, which Ashe in America presented as a preview of coming restrictions on American depositors' access to their own money.[1] She concluded that for holders of short positions on banks, that moment had already arrived.[1]
