# Deny, Defend, Depose

"Deny," "defend," and "depose" are three words that New York Police Department investigators reported finding engraved on 9mm shell casings recovered at the scene of the fatal shooting of UnitedHealthcare chief executive Brian Thompson in Midtown Manhattan on the morning of Wednesday, December 4, 2024.[1] Officials familiar with the investigation described the inscriptions to the *New York Times*, and noted that ballistic testing on the recovered casings was underway at the time of reporting.[1]

## Context of the shooting

Thompson, aged 50, was shot and killed outside the New York Hilton Midtown hotel just after 6:45 a.m., in what investigators characterized as a targeted attack.[1] The shooter, who remained at large, was described as having waited for Thompson outside the hotel and having fired multiple rounds from a suppressed 9mm handgun before fleeing.[1] The [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]] released images of the suspect wearing a black face mask on Wednesday, and followed on Thursday, December 5, with newly released photographs showing the suspect maskless and, in the assessment of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary, "completely identifiable."[1]

## Interpretation

The three inscribed words are widely read as an allusion to industry-critical shorthand for how health insurers are alleged to handle claims — denying coverage, defending the denial, and forcing claimants into depositions.[1] In this reading the casings function as a message rather than as forensic carelessness, framing the killing as an act aimed at the health insurance business model itself.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the accumulating details with open skepticism, observing that a supposedly "professional assassin" had conducted a public killing on camera, left inscribed shell casings behind, reportedly dropped a cell phone while fleeing, and then appeared on camera smiling and identifiable.[1] The commentary argued that a public assassination captured on camera in Midtown Manhattan an hour before morning rush hour carries its own power: enough to frighten those who believe legacy coverage into changing their behavior, and enough to distract everyone else from other stories.[1] The stated Badlands position is that a narrative deployment can be identified as such without knowing its intended outcome, and that the default posture toward traumatizing legacy media stories should be disbelief.[1]

The inscriptions also became a reference point in the reaction cycle that followed, including social media posts by former *Washington Post* reporter [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]] that appeared to celebrate the killing and to point at other insurance executives.[1]
