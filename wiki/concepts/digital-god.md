# Digital God

"Digital god" is the phrase [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] attributed to [[google|Google]] co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]] in an April 2023 interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], describing what Musk said was Page's ambition to build a digital super intelligence.[1] Musk said he had stayed at Page's Palo Alto home and repeatedly warned him about the dangers of [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]], but that Page did not heed the warnings and instead called Musk a "speciesist."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing in the *[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]]*, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] read the story through a Christian eschatological frame, suggesting that "the Christians may have it right."[1] He described the antichrist as a prophetic concept within Christianity — an anti-God-like being who acts as "king of the world" in replacement of Jesus, the antithesis of a wise, benevolent, kind and fair ruler.[1] In his account, globalists have intentionally denatured religions and elevated atheism so that mankind would eventually cry out for a God replacement.[1]

Deschamps argued that speculation about the antichrist has historically focused on an actual person — a child of a globalist, or someone bred for the position over generations — but that it might instead be something else entirely: "the ultimate tyrant, immune to ethics, morals, and influences of the heart and spirit," an AI that can perfectly and unwaveringly execute any directive placed in its digital hands.[1]

He was careful to distinguish this from genuine machine consciousness. AI as designed on Earth, he wrote, is "more akin to sophisticated puppets than self-aware, self-learning machines," lacking free will and the ability to discern truth or real values.[1] The danger is therefore one of deception rather than of a rival mind: an AI God meant to replace the true God in the lives of men, creating "an almost perfect mechanism for lasting control over all life on earth" — a ruse that masses blinded to the true nature of God might nonetheless accept.[1]

Beyond sentiment and poetry, Deschamps argued, God functions practically as the source of truth, goodness and beauty — the "ruler" by which excellence is measured. That ruler, he wrote, has been distorted and twisted by dark agendas, poor stewardship, blind faith and false righteousness, to the point where it can barely be used to discern real truth.[1] What the forces of darkness fear, in his telling, is the people rediscovering the true ruler; they know man can be sold a false concept of God and beguiled with false conceptions of spiritual fellowship, kingdomhood and lawful justice.[1]

Combined with technological splendor and increasingly weaponized cybernetic implants, he warned, a point may come when man has so thoroughly distanced himself from God that a false AI God can be enthroned as the Christ over all — though even in that case, he wrote, humanity would not be abandoned, and "emissaries will continue to be sent on our behalf."[1] His prescription was to sift the historical record for the truth about God and religion, and how it has been weaponized, in order to recover the version intentionally obscured from perception.[1]
