# Lycanthropy

**Lycanthropy** — werewolfism — is the organizing symbol of the [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series, which reads werewolf motifs in myth, folklore and modern entertainment as traces of the [[koryos|Koryos]] cult.[1]

## Legal and folkloric history

The article notes that centuries ago lycanthropy was declared a crime in France, and that many Christians of the period believed a werewolf could be brought back to their senses by saying their true Christian name three times.[1] The author treats this belief as a folk statement of the series' central thesis: that it is impossible to overestimate the importance of calling things by what they truly are.[1] He connects it to the occult maxim that a spirit must answer when its name is called, while allowing that he does not think this strictly true in all cases — the defensible core being that we can do little about things we cannot describe accurately.[1]

## Myth as encoded record

Roman mythology is read in the same key. The founders of Rome, the twin brothers Remus and Romulus, were suckled by a she-wolf after their mother hid them from their great-uncle King Amulius, who saw them as a threat to the throne.[1] Since the Koryos were, the article says, famous for grooming children into their ranks, the implication of a myth about abandoned twins raised by a wolf is presented as blatant.[1] Many ancient fairy tales are likewise described as veiled warnings about the cult, with the "magic potions" of witches read as the drug cocktails used to intoxicate and seduce recruits.[1]

## Predator framing

The article compares the dark occultists who are said to run the [[deep-state|Deep State]] to a predator class, arguing that surviving in the woods requires not merely knowing predators exist but knowing specifics about them — and that the cult retains its advantage over mankind precisely because so few can describe its beliefs and modes of operation.[1] Evidence of the cult is said to persist in the music industry and Hollywood to this day.[1]
