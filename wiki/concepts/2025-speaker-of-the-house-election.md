# 2025 Speaker of the House Election

The 2025 Speaker of the House election was the vote held on Friday, January 3, 2025, to seat the presiding officer of the 119th Congress. Incumbent Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-La.) sought to retain the gavel against a razor-thin Republican majority and open dissent within his own conference.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried special coverage of the vote beginning at noon Eastern.[1]

## Senate Republican backing

Ahead of the vote, Johnson received public support from Senate Republicans. Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) wrote on X that Johnson had "done an incredible job in the House," adding that he was glad Johnson was "at the helm there as Congress looks forward to growing our economy and safeguarding our communities in the new year."[1] The other member of Louisiana's Senate delegation, Sen. [[bill-cassidy|Bill Cassidy]], also backed him: "I agree with President Trump that [Johnson] is the right man to lead. He's a committed conservative and a man of integrity," referencing President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s recent endorsement.[1]

During the last-minute government-spending fight in December 2024, most Republican senators avoided calling for Johnson's replacement, though Sens. [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] (R-Ky.) and [[mike-lee|Mike Lee]] (R-Utah) suggested someone else would do a better job.[1] Paul told reporters in December that "technically, the rules of the House — I don't think you have to be a member of the House to be speaker," noting that other names had been floated.[1]

## The math

The Democratic whip's office indicated that all Democrats were expected to be present in the Capitol for the vote, a significant fact given the chamber's narrow margins and the threat from some conservative Republicans to oppose Johnson's bid.[1] Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] (R-Ky.) had already vowed to vote for someone other than Johnson on the floor, meaning that one further GOP defection would deny Johnson the simple majority of voting members required — provided every Democrat was present and voting for their preferred candidate, Rep. [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] (D-N.Y.).[1] If even one Democrat were absent, Johnson could afford two GOP defections and still win the gavel.[1]

## Badlands assessment

Writing for the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] predicted the vote would go the way Johnson's previous Speaker election had: election on the first ballot, on a straight party-line vote.[1] She argued that while dissenters such as Massie were vocal, there did not appear to be the sort of coordinated opposition Matt Gaetz (R-Fla.) had assembled against Kevin McCarthy in 2023, and that she did not expect "Massie & Co" to work with Democrats, play the margin, and pull off an upset.[1] Regardless of the final tally, she noted, Johnson was Trump's stated choice and would likely be Speaker of the House in the 119th Congress.[1]
