# Electoral Reform in Canada

Electoral reform in Canada refers to proposed changes to the country's voting system and administration, a subject Badlands Media has framed as central to what it describes as an ongoing [[asymmetric-warfare|asymmetric]] political warfare operation.[1]

## The asymmetry of the existing system

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] noted that in Canada's most recent federal election only 33% of voters put [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] in power, producing what he called the smallest minority government in Canadian history, and that [[olivia-chow|Olivia Chow]] became mayor of Toronto with just 37% of the vote in a municipal microcosm of the same dynamic.[1] He asked whether the Canadian electoral system gives a minority of radicalized left-wing voters the ability to overrule the majority, and whether the system is being deliberately maintained to confer the democratic power of a majority on a radicalized minority.[1]

Deschamps argued the asymmetry has served Trudeau's government well, observing that Trudeau originally campaigned and won on a platform of changing the broken electoral system before revealing he had never intended to do so.[1]

## The Trudeau–Singh reform package

Deschamps reported that Trudeau and New Democratic Party leader [[jagmeet-singh|Jagmeet Singh]], whom he characterized as controlled opposition, had been quietly preparing electoral reform legislation that would sound familiar to Americans critical of the [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election]] between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The reported measures included an expanded three-day voting period during general elections, allowing voters to cast ballots at any polling place within their riding, and improvements to the [[mail-in-voting|mail-in ballot]] process framed around accessibility and integrity.[1]

## Comparison with American election fraud claims

Deschamps argued that the structural difference between the two countries is among the most significant distinctions in comparing alleged election fraud.[1] In the United States, he wrote, it was declared that Joe Biden had received more votes than Barack Obama ever had, because American elections do not divide voter power into small blocs — whereas the Canadian system allows a leader to control the nation with 33% of the vote and, in his characterization, freeze bank accounts at will.[1]

He predicted that if Trudeau were to cheat in the 2025 election he would not need to fabricate a record-vote story; his only task would be to preserve the existing imbalance and worsen it through the pending reforms, since mail-in ballots and a three-day voting period would make another minority victory easy to frame given his control over local media.[1]
