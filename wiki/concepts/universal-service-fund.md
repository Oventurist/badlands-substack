# Universal Service Fund

The Universal Service Fund is an $8 billion federal program that subsidizes the availability and affordability of high-speed internet and telephone service for millions of Americans, including rural libraries and low-income communities.[1]

## 2025 Supreme Court challenge

On March 26, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] heard a conservative-backed challenge to the program that could jeopardize service for millions of beneficiaries.[1] Reporting on the case highlighted the Arkansas River Valley Regional Library System, whose seven libraries serve a rural population with a median family income below $46,000 and whose librarians mounted rooftop antennas during the pandemic to extend internet signal to residents.[1] Regional library director Misty Hawkins said that "lives are being changed through the distribution of a computer, through a connection, or a person being able to have résumé assistance, or being able to get their GED."[1]

## Nondelegation stakes

Beyond the telecommunications subsidies themselves, the case carried broad potential repercussions for how much power [[congress|Congress]] may delegate to federal agencies to make decisions about implementing legislation.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary grouped the case with the congressional fight over public broadcasting funding as fronts in an information war, describing the administrative state as being dismantled narratively before being dismantled in fact.[1]
