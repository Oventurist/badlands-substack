# Elections Are Fake

"Elections are fake" is a formulation advanced by Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] at the close of 2025, holding that the American electoral process does not at any point rest upon the will of the people, and that the consent-of-the-governed premise built atop it is therefore illusory.[1]

## Argument

Writing in response to Politico's retrospective on the mayoralty of [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] — and specifically its lament over the loss of Black political power in New York — Ashe argued that "elections are fake so Politico's assertion is nonsensical," since legitimacy cannot be lost in a process that never conferred it.[1] She traced what she called the "user journey of 'the vote'": the drawing of districts, the registering of voters, the mailing and casting of ballots, their counting, tallying and reporting, risk-limiting audits, the judiciary's treatment of election challenges and even questions, and the silencing of dissent up to and including the removal of rights.[1] At no point in that chain, she contended, does the process involve or rely upon the will of the people.[1]

"That very idea — that things are the way they are because 'the people' 'voted' for them — forms the foundation of our society. And it's fake," she wrote, adding that citizens believe they are providing consent to be governed and can withdraw it "if government gets too uppity," a belief she judged demonstrably unfulfilled.[1] She summarized the position as "Democracy is a rug pull," recounting arguments with two people who privately agreed elections are fake but objected to the word "fake."[1] She urged readers to make a New Year's resolution not to be swept away by "The Midterms" and its "multimedia immersive theatrical experience."[1]

## Polls as beta tests

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] extended the argument to polling, writing that "it's not just that the polls are as fake as the elections" but that "the polls are beta tests for election narratives."[1] On this view, even favorable polling should be read as serving "to prop up a system masquerading as sovereign."[1]
