# Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act

The Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act is a Texas consumer-protection statute prohibiting false, deceptive, and misleading acts and practices in commerce, and it supplies the cause of action the Texas Attorney General has used against corporate defendants accused of misrepresenting their products.[1]

## Use against Pfizer

In November 2023, Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] invoked the Act in his suit against [[pfizer|Pfizer]], accusing the company of "false, deceptive, and misleading acts and practices by making unsupported claims regarding the company's COVID-19 vaccine."[1] The suit alleged five separate violations of the Act, centering on the assertion that Pfizer's vaccine was 95 percent effective and on the impression that the product would end the pandemic.[1] The action is detailed in [[texas-v-pfizer-covid-19-vaccine-lawsuit|Texas v. Pfizer]].[1]

## Use in the 2024 vehicle data investigation

In June 2024 Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] invoked the Act to open an investigation into several car manufacturers reported to be collecting large volumes of driver data directly from vehicles and selling it to third parties, including insurers.[2] The statute authorizes the Office of the Attorney General to investigate false, misleading, or deceptive acts or practices, and on that basis manufacturers and the third-party purchasers were instructed to produce documents about their conduct and about the disclosures they made to customers regarding data collection and sale.[2]
