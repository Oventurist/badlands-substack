# FISAgate

FISAgate is the label used in Badlands Media and Q-adjacent commentary for the alleged abuse of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act by the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration, encompassing both warrantless access to intelligence databases and the obtaining of surveillance warrants on false pretences.[1] In the July 2025 article "Shall We Play a Game?", [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] pairs it with [[obamagate|Obamagate]] as the subject of congressional investigations that moved from the House to the Senate Judiciary Committee under [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] after the [[2018-midterm-elections|2018 midterm elections]].[1]

## The two alleged abuses

Lange describes the Obama administration as having abused the FISA system "two different ways."[1] First, it is said to have illegally accessed the [[national-security-agency|NSA]] database to conduct searches on American citizens without warrants. Second, it is said to have misled the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-court|Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court]] in order to obtain a warrant on [[carter-page|Carter Page]] and thereby surveil the Trump campaign.[1] He argues the common thread identified by former acting Defense Intelligence Agency director David Shedd was that the agencies were "not fully transparent" with the court, a pattern Shedd called an "absence of candor."[1]

## The Collyer opinion

Citing the Daily Signal, the article recounts that a FISA court judge — [[rosemary-collyer|Rosemary M. Collyer]] — signed a 99-page reprimand dated April 26, 2017 finding that the NSA had conducted prohibited database searches between 2011 and 2016 "with much greater frequency than had previously been disclosed to the court," and criticising the NSA's Office of Inspector General and Office of Compliance for Operations for "an institutional lack of candor" raising "very serious Fourth Amendment issues."[1] Lange stresses that this scrutiny began well before the public had heard of Fusion GPS, Christopher Steele, or Carter Page, and long before the Nunes memo, and that the court learned of the abuse from Admiral [[mike-rogers|Mike Rogers]] before the 2016 election.[1]

## Sequencing argument

The article's central inferential claim is one of sequence: because Rogers had cut off the administration's warrantless access to the NSA database, the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA warrant]] became a necessity, and the unverified [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] was used as its predicate.[1] On this reading the Page warrant was not the origin of the scandal but a cover-up of an older and larger surveillance programme said to have run for five years.[1] Lange adds that the Trump [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]] later turned the same instrument around, quoting Q's line "FISA GOES BOTH WAYS," and reports that the Huber/Horowitz and [[john-durham|Durham]] investigations also used FISA warrants to gather evidence.[1]

These claims reflect the Badlands Media interpretation and are contested; the Collyer opinion is a genuine document, but the broader narrative of a coordinated five-year political spying operation has not been established by any court or inspector general finding.[1]

## FISAGATE as cover-up in the boomerang narrative

In "[[the-biggest-boomerang-in-history|The Biggest Boomerang In History]]," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] defines FISAGATE as what [[spygate|SPYGATE]] became after [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] lost the 2016 election: a conspiracy by the outgoing administration to frame and remove a duly elected president, and therefore the biggest cover-up in American history.[2]

Lange writes that [[james-comey|James Comey]]'s [[fbi|FBI]] altered documents to obtain a [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|FISA]] warrant on [[carter-page|Carter Page]] and withheld exculpatory evidence from the FISA court judges, and that the resulting warrant allowed the bureau to spy on the Trump campaign in order to frame [[donald-trump|Trump]].[2] Crucially, in his account the FISA application was itself the cover for surveillance already running through [[five-eyes|FVEY]] partners, not its origin.[2] Q drop 4231 of May 13, 2020, quoted in the article, asks whether FISA unmasking requests preceded the [[sergey-kislyak|Kislyak]] phone call and frames the sequence as FBI entrapment with late FISA justification.[2]

The article closes this thread with the Q formulation "FISA = START," and argues that [[declas|DECLAS]] under Attorney General [[william-barr|William Barr]] turned the cover-up into the evidence of the original crime.[2] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
