# #MeToo Movement

The #MeToo movement is the American cultural and legal reckoning with sexual misconduct by powerful figures that began in 2017 with a flood of allegations against film producer [[harvey-weinstein|Harvey Weinstein]].[1] Weinstein's 2020 New York rape conviction was treated by supporters of the movement as its signature legal achievement and as proof that "no one is above the law."[1]

## The April 2024 setback

On April 25, 2024, the [[new-york-court-of-appeals|New York Court of Appeals]] threw out Weinstein's 2020 conviction, holding that the trial judge had unfairly admitted testimony about allegations that were not part of the case.[1] The ruling shocked and disappointed women who had celebrated the era's historic gains, and left the witnesses who testified bracing for a retrial.[1] Movement advocates stressed that the decision rested on legal technicalities rather than any exoneration of Weinstein's behavior, and maintained that the original trial had irrevocably shifted cultural attitudes toward sexual assault.[1] Weinstein remained incarcerated because of a separate 2022 Los Angeles rape conviction.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media commentary questioned the framing that the ruling "reopens" a painful chapter, asking when that chapter had ever closed and rejecting the notion that Weinstein's arrest and conviction resolved systemic sexual abuse in Hollywood — pointing to the allegations then swirling around [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]] as evidence that the reckoning was "just getting started."[1] The same commentary argued that the movement's supporters now face an awkward position: railing against judicial abuse of discretion in Weinstein's case while defending similar discretionary rulings by New York judges against [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]].[1] It closed on a due-process theme, insisting that even widely hated defendants retain procedural rights and that society should not advocate violating them because of hatred for the accused.[1]

## Invoked in the 2024 Hegseth nomination

Badlands Media framed the November 2024 [[hegseth-sexual-assault-allegation|sexual assault allegation]] against defense secretary nominee [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] as a Me Too deployment, noting that the underlying 2017 incident occurred at the height of the movement, when numerous high-profile men in media faced allegations of assault and harassment.[2] The outlet argued that opponents turning to a Me Too attack so early in the confirmation process indicated they lacked substantive grounds to defeat the nomination.[2]
