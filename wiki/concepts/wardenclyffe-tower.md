# Wardenclyffe Tower

Wardenclyffe Tower was the 186-foot wireless transmission tower built on Long Island by [[nikola-tesla|Nikola Tesla]] and financed by J.P. Morgan, with [[thomas-fortune-ryan|Thomas Fortune Ryan]] also participating in the funding before the outbreak of World War I.[1]

[[william-james-flynn|William James Flynn]] tracked down the Long Island wireless station that had been transmitting signals to German intelligence — including, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, information leading to the sinking of the Lusitania in 1915 — and his interception of messages to the German high command provided the legal justification for the United States government's seizure and destruction of Tesla's Long Island installations, both Wardenclyffe and the Telefunken Wireless Station at Sayville, New York.[1] By July 1917 the Navy, under the leadership of [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] as Assistant Secretary, ordered the demolition of the tower on the basis of naval intelligence reports that "German spies were using the big wireless tower" to communicate with German intelligence.[1]

Ehret raises the speculative question of whether Wardenclyffe was the "mysterious means" of communication referred to in the 1917 Office of Naval Intelligence report on [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]]'s contact with [[rudolf-steiner|Rudolf Steiner]] in Berlin, noting that Crowley's "magickal workings" on Long Island in that period placed him very close to the tower and that he was associated with Tesla's intimate friend [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]].[1]
