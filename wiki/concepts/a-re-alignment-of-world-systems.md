# A Re-Alignment of World Systems

"A Re-Alignment of World Systems," subtitled "On the Signal-Soaked Flushing of Chrystia Freeland," is an essay published by Badlands Media on September 27, 2025 and written by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]. Taking the removal of [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] from Canadian cabinet politics as its point of departure, the article advances a broad interpretive history of what the author calls the post-1971 unipolar project, its collapse, and the emergence of a multipolar world order organized around Russia, China and the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].

## The Freeland announcement

The article opens with a September 16 announcement from the Prime Minister's Office in Ottawa in which [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] thanked Freeland for her service as Finance Minister and Deputy Prime Minister while, in Ehret's reading, signalling that "her political sun was setting." Carney wrote: *"I have asked Chrystia to serve as Canada's new Representative for the Reconstruction of Ukraine."*

Ehret quotes the analyst John Helmer, who covered the ouster and observed that "Freeland's ouster was so rushed, there was no time for her to explain what the hurry was in her departure, nor for Carney to prepare what Freeland would be doing as his special envoy to the Ukraine without any staff or diplomatic rank."

According to the source, the role Freeland was groomed to play — described as a "Rhodes Scholar regime change apostle devoted to unipolarism and eventual war with Russia and China" — has ended because the world is settling into a new alignment of power relations. The article notes that Canada's anti-Russian posture has not ended, citing $22 billion in Canadian taxpayer support for the proxy war since the Special Military Operation began in February 2022, but argues that a new agenda of befriending China is being pursued under Carney, leaving no place for Freeland's anti-China hawkishness. Her belligerent economic position, the article states, already cost Canadian farmers billions when China imposed retaliatory tariffs on Canadian canola in response to Freeland's 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles.

The article warns that China should nonetheless not trust the "new Canada" of Mark Carney, arguing that Canada is a chess piece within a much larger London-directed Great Game, and that the intention to weaken the Russian-Chinese alliance — with resource-rich Canada playing a role in that game — remains. Ehret refers to his own reports of 2012, 2017 and 2020 warning China that "British Canada" functions as a dangerous trap.

## The script up until now

The essay's historical narrative begins with the collapse of the Soviet system in the early 1990s, which it says ushered in a unipolar world order through the European Union and [[nafta|NAFTA]], followed by the World Trade Organization and the 1999 destruction of the Glass Steagall Act. The trans-Atlantic, Ehret writes, "at last was converted into a cage of 'post-sovereign nations' that no longer had actual control of their own powers of credit generation." Under [[nato|NATO]], he argues, even national militaries were subject to technocratic control, producing an arrangement ideal for a governing elite "scientifically managing" from above while ordinary people bickered over diminishing employment and living standards.

The former Soviet bloc, though already in tatters by 1992, had to have its sovereign powers undone by compressing a liberalization process that took thirty years in the West into a single decade. This, the article says, was directed by monetarist "reformers" such as Anatoly Chubais and Yegor Gaidar under Boris Yeltsin. On the Western side, Perestroika — which the article identifies with "Operation 'Hammer'," named for Occidental Petroleum's Armand Hammer, who ran the 1920s New Economic Policy under Trotsky in Russia — involved the IMF's Jeffrey Sachs, Vice President Al Gore, Rhodes Scholar Strobe Talbott, and the Oxford-trained Goldman Sachs investment banker Mark Carney.

Similar privatization and liberalization reforms, the source states, were applied viciously to Ukraine and other Warsaw Pact countries. The resulting oligarchs are named as Oleg Deripaska, Boris Berezovsky, Mikhail Fridman and Roman Abramovich in Russia, alongside Petro Poroshenko, Rinat Akhmetov, Mikhail Khodorkovsky and Viktor Pinchuk in Ukraine. Many, the article notes, purchased homes in upmarket London, giving rise to the nickname "Moscow on Thames."

By the end of the 1990s a new phase of de-nationalization was unleashed with the Blair doctrine, which explicitly called for a "post-Westphalia" world order and, in the article's account, unleashed a wave of regime change wars in the Arab world beginning with 9/11, with a long-term intention to target Libya, Syria, Iran and Lebanon while expanding NATO's hegemony against the potential re-emergence of Russia and China.

## The economic meltdown as intention

The article asserts that "the whole point of the post-1971 world was directed with the intention of destroying the moral-political and economic foundations of Western society," contrasting this with a belief in scientific progress and industrial growth that it credits with all true progress from the fifteenth-century Golden Renaissance to the assassinations of the 1960s. It lists three intended consequences of post-1971 zero-growth policy: the destruction of the productive forces of labor through outsourcing to cheap labour markets driven by shareholder profit; the consolidation of wealth into an ever smaller array of private multi-billionaire owners under a Darwinian logic; and the creation of a vast speculative bubble supported by ever greater rates of unpayable debt, detached from physical production.

Just as in 1929, the source argues, "the plug could be pulled" on the bubble to impose shock therapy on a sleeping population that would beg for fascism if it put bread on the table. That plan failed eighty years ago, the article says, because of the American rejection of fascism under President [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]]; the belief that the formula could succeed in the twenty-first century depended on America being brought firmly under the control of the [[city-of-london|City of London]] and its Wall Street allies.

The renewed attempt, according to Ehret, takes the form of a system of global government managed by draconian regulation under a Green New Deal framework, in which banking regulation, state-directed capital and centralized government — "not evils unto themselves" — are directed only toward green, low energy flux density forms of energy that inherently lower the earth's population. This is contrasted with the protectionism, bank regulation, state credit and central authority exercised by America during the 1930s New Deal or by the Eurasian New Silk Road policy today: one system empowers sovereign nations and raises the productive powers of labor and quality of life, while the "Green" agenda ties monetary incentives to decreasing the earth's carbon footprint. Ehret offers the image of "a drug addict getting paid heroine as an incentive to bleed himself to death."

## The role of Chrystia Freeland

Freeland's role, the article contends, was the classic Rhodes Scholar assignment: to "facilitate the tough transition of the 'pre-collapse' world economy into a new operating system that was meant to be the 'green post-collapse' world economy." This required telling a new pirate class of billionaires that they would have to accept losing much of their wealth and operate under a strict new global operating system of regulation designed to contract society.

The [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholarship]] program, begun in 1902 to advance a re-organized British Empire, had worked alongside the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] for over a century, producing more than 7,000 scholars distributed across media, education, government, military and corporate life. The article quotes [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]]'s 1877 will calling for "a society which should have its members in every part of the British Empire working with one object and one idea," whose members would be tested for endurance and eloquence, "then elected and bound by oath to serve for the rest of his life in his Country," and "sent to that part of the Empire where it was felt he was needed."

After leaving Oxford in 1993, Freeland learned "perception management" at the London *Economist*, *Washington Post*, *Financial Times*, *Globe and Mail* and *Reuters*. After a stint as editor-at-large of Reuters, the article says, she was positioned to "play the role of Valerie Jarrett to the 'Barack Obama' of Canada," Justin Trudeau. As an asset of the global propaganda system she had made high-level contacts with Ukrainian, Russian and Western oligarchs including Pinchuk and Khodorkovsky, and counted Larry Summers, George Soros and Al Gore among her close friends — figures she was happy to bring into Canada during the reorganization of the Liberal Party between 2011 and 2014 under the banner of the [[canada-2020|Canada 2020]] think tank.

The article emphasizes that Freeland came from a family of zealous Ukrainian nationalists under the patriarchy of her grandfather [[michael-chomiak|Michael Chomiak]], described as a Nazi propagandist; this network, it says, was brought to Canada after the Second World War by Anglo-American intelligence and cultivated ever since as a force tied to pro-Nazi Ukrainian counterparts. Her entry into politics was managed by another Rhodes Scholar, [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], interim controller of the Liberal Party during part of the Harper years and a major player in Canada 2020. Rae, NDP Premier of Ontario from 1990 to 1995, abdicated his seat to Freeland, ensuring her entry into Trudeau's inner circle and, in Ehret's phrase, making her "his official handler."

## Freeland and the new global elite

The article examines Freeland's own writing. In her 2011 Atlantic essay *The Rise of the New Global Elite*, she wrote: *"To grasp the difference between today's plutocrats and the hereditary elite, who 'grow rich in their sleep,' one need merely glance at the events that now fill high-end social calendars."* Ehret reads her as dividing the new plutocrats into technocratic-friendly figures ideologically compatible with depopulation — naming Bill Gates, Warren Buffett and Soros — and "bad" plutocrats who do not conform to the British Empire's program of global governance. [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is, in this schema, a terrible plutocrat, while Viktor Yanukovych was a good one until he declined to sacrifice Ukraine to the collapsing European Union and instead threw Ukraine's destiny toward the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]] in October 2013.

Freeland also warned in the same essay that *"if the plutocrats' opposition to increases in their taxes and tighter regulation of their economic activities is understandable, it is also a mistake. The real threat facing the super-elite, at home and abroad, isn't modestly higher taxes, but rather the possibility that inchoate public rage could cohere into a more concrete populist agenda – that, for instance, middle-class Americans could conclude that the world economy isn't working for them and decide that protectionism… is preferable to incremental measures."* She quoted Pimco CEO Mohamed El-Erian that "one of the big surprises of 2010 is that the protectionist dog didn't bark," and closed: *"The lesson of history is that, in the long run, super-elites have two ways to survive: by suppressing dissent or by sharing their wealth… Let us hope the plutocrats aren't already too isolated to recognize this."*

In *Plutocrats vs. Populists* (November 2013), the article says, Freeland let her pro-plutocratic worldview out of the bag, praising a form of plutocratic political power offering "the tantalizing possibility of policy practiced at the highest professional level with none of the messiness and deal making and venality of traditional politics … a technocratic, data-based, objective search for solutions to our problems." She further urged the recruitment of the young: "Smart, publicly minded technocrats go to work for plutocrats whose values they share. The technocrats get to focus full time on the policy issues they love, without the tedium of building, rallying– and serving– a permanent mass membership. They can be pretty well paid to boot."

## The end of a delusion

The concluding section argues that the operating system built by Russia and China around the Belt and Road Initiative has created a force of opposition to what Ehret calls the British-run [[deep-state|Deep State]] design, and that nothing the would-be "gods of Olympus" attempted has succeeded. He points to the Belt and Road Initiative winning over 136 nations, the re-emergence of Pan-African nationalism as an anti-colonialist force, the NATO proxy war strengthening rather than weakening the Russian economy, the solidification of the Iran-Russia-China alliance, Venezuela's failure to fall as other regimes did before 2014, and the serious challenge to NAFTA posed by a restoration of protectionism in the United States. The American rejection of the Paris Accords and the Green New Deal is presented as a shift away from the Malthusian depopulation agenda to which Freeland was devoted.

Freeland's usefulness, Ehret writes, "has withered away as quickly as you can say 'Azov'," and time will tell whether Mark Carney can salvage the decaying British Empire and Cecil Rhodes's dream of recapturing the USA in the twenty-first century. The essay ends: "While the universe may be organized by a principle of reason, no one can say the same applies to the mind of an oligarch."

## Notes

The article appends four notes. The first states that the separation of speculative from commercial banking was the bedrock of financial regulation from 1933 and that its destruction as Clinton's last act in office produced the largest bubble in history, a $700 trillion derivatives time bomb. The second observes that when [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] began exiling unrepentant oligarchs, they made their way to London and became "disposable playthings of the British Empire." The third states that the self-professed "Fabian Society of Canada" was set up at the height of the Depression by five Rhodes Scholars in order to create a Canadian fascist regime in 1932; this organization, the [[league-of-social-reconstruction|League for Social Reconstruction]], founded the Cooperative Commonwealth Federation, which became the [[new-democratic-party|New Democratic Party]] in 1961. The fourth notes that as Premier of Ontario, Bob Rae brought in [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]] as President of Ontario Hydro from 1992 to 1994, a period in which Canada's nuclear sector was nearly shut down and a prototype Green New Deal was applied; it quotes Strong's famous hypothetical novel in which a small group of world leaders conclude that "the only hope for the planet" is that "the industrialized civilizations collapse," asking, "Isn't it our responsibility to bring that about?"
