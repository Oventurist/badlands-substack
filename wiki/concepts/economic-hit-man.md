# Economic Hit Man

"Economic hit man" is a term popularized by author [[john-perkins|John Perkins]] in his book *Confessions of an Economic Hit Man*, describing operatives who induce developing nations to accept unpayable international loans so that their land, resources and sovereignty can be seized as collateral.[1] In an April 27, 2023 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] titled "The Economy: Weaponized," [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that Perkins confirms this is the way conquest or colonialism happens in the modern era, with finance replacing armies as the instrument of empire.[1]

## The seven-stage model

Deschamps set out a seven-stage sequence by which he says bankers convert a sovereign nation into a dependency.[1]

First, a population is deprived of the knowledge and capacity required to maintain a complex monetary system — one involving banks, accounting, transfers, clearing, bonds, hypothecation and surety. Older and simpler systems such as barter or gold and silver coin are less complex, and consequently the capacity of the monetary system to raise productivity across the economic landscape is reduced.[1]

Second, bankers operating through local front organizations and industry leaders push "modernization" onto the population, offering the latest gadgets, technology, methods and access to "first world" products and services — what Deschamps likened to neo-world's-fair tactics. The price of this apparent benevolence is exorbitant loans owed to the [[international-monetary-fund|International Monetary Fund]], structured so as to be nearly impossible to repay. Land and resource rights are demanded as collateral, along with promises of austerity under which the people and their resources are made to service loans that inevitably default.[1]

Third, once default occurs, austerity measures arrive, designed to drain the nation parasitically of its resources while making it impossible for independent businesses to thrive. The country is reoriented toward exporting and ends up paying much more for imports than it receives for exports; the resulting trade deficit becomes leverage that increases profits for the merchants facilitating the transactions and improves the negotiating position of importing nations.[1]

Fourth, restructuring cripples the ability of local business to produce goods and services, as more of the oppressed nation's resources and labor are diverted into whatever industries generate the profits needed to repay IMF loans. Rainforests are burned down, and cartel-like entities — often secretly partnered with globalists — spring up, further breaking down middle-class-owned businesses.[1]

Fifth, whatever self-sufficiency, law and order, and prosperity existed is stripped away, leaving increasing poverty, crime and lawlessness. Black-market enterprises expand to fill the space vacated by legitimate business, and human trafficking, theft and piracy become commonplace while product quality declines sharply.[1]

Sixth, the nation becomes ripe for the removal of any regulation limiting unfair labor practices or resource extraction. Children work in sweatshops, political dissidents are rounded up into labor camps, and international businesses arrive to access cheap labor while paying almost nothing. Corrupt local politicians and industry leaders sell out their countrymen and become, in Deschamps's phrase, the "house slaves" of their own nation.[1]

Seventh, totalitarianism sets in — usually socialism or communism as the governing system for a fully conquered people. Propaganda degrades the remaining values and ideals of a demoralized population until most people forget what life was like before, and the children of the conquered act as cogs in the machinery of continued globalist conquest.[1]

## Historical scope

Deschamps wrote that this timeline describes the fate of dozens of nations over the decades since the neo-globalist method was developed in the late 19th century.[1]
