# Iron Dome

The Iron Dome is Israel's short-range missile defense system, treated in Badlands Media coverage as the emblem of Israeli air-defense capability during the 2026 war with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

In March 2026, [[haaretz|Haaretz]] reported that nearly a dozen smart missiles — each containing dozens of smaller bombs with independent guidance systems — penetrated the Iron Dome and struck central [[israel|Israel]] overnight, as part of one of many Iranian salvos during that night.[1] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry concluded from the reporting that Israel's defense systems had failed and that the country was now vulnerable and "taking a beating."[1]

That assessment underpinned the Brief's broader argument that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was not winning the war, that [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] had reportedly approached Iran seeking a ceasefire only to be refused, and that the Israeli prime minister faced a narrowing set of options ahead of Israel's October election.[1]

## 2021 replenishment vote and the 2023 backlash

A 2021 bill providing $1 billion to replenish Israel's Iron Dome missile defense system, passed by the House after a progressive dispute, became a political marker in the following years.[2] In November 2023, [[democratic-majority-for-israel|Democratic Majority for Israel]] cited Rep. [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]]'s vote against that replenishment bill — alongside her vote against an October 2023 resolution standing with Israel — in a six-figure television advertisement attacking her in her Detroit-area district.[2]

### American "Iron Dome" proposal and expert criticism
In January 2025 President Donald Trump directed the construction of an "Iron Dome" missile defense shield to protect the United States from potential airborne attacks, naming the project after Israel's short-range missile defense system.[3] The White House said the plan would "further the goal of peace through strength," and Trump wrote in the executive order that "over the past 40 years, rather than lessening, the threat from next-generation strategic weapons has become more intense and complex with the development by peer and near-peer adversaries of next-generation delivery systems and their own homeland integrated air and missile defense capabilities."[3] Republican lawmakers subsequently introduced the IRON DOME Act to improve U.S. missile defense capabilities.[3]

Experts quoted by The Independent in February 2025 said the plan defied physics and practicality. The Congressional Research Service noted in a report that "most terrestrial-based radars cannot detect hypersonic weapons until late in the weapon's flight due to line-of-sight limitations of radar detection" — and it is fear of precisely those adversary hypersonic missiles that drives the push for a homeland dome.[3] Badlands commentary suggested a different reading: that with Elon Musk confirming the United States was already using "Starshield" for national defense, the executive order may have functioned as narrative disclosure of a capability already in place, noting that "the DOD's biggest contractor is Elon Musk."[3]
