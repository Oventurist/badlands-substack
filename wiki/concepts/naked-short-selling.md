# Naked Short Selling

Naked short selling is the practice of selling shares short without first borrowing or confirming the availability of the underlying shares, so that non-existent shares are effectively lent and sold into the market.[1] In April 2024 the practice became the subject of a formal congressional referral concerning the stock of [[trump-media-and-technology-group|Trump Media & Technology Group]].[1]

## The DJT episode

[[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]], CEO of Trump Media & Technology Group, wrote to House committee leaders asking them to investigate potential "unlawful manipulation of DJT stock," DJT being the ticker drawn from Donald J. Trump's initials.[1] Nunes pointed out that DJT had appeared every day since April 2, 2024 on Nasdaq's "Reg SHO threshold list," which he characterized as indicative of unlawful trading activity.[1] The Reg SHO threshold list identifies securities with persistent failures to deliver, the customary statistical footprint of naked shorting.[1]

The concern arose shortly after Trump Media & Technology Group, whose flagship product is the social network [[truth-social|Truth Social]], began trading on the Nasdaq stock market in late March 2024.[1]

## Retail investor harm

Nunes wrote that the situation was particularly troubling because naked short selling often entails sophisticated market participants profiting at the expense of retail investors.[1] He noted reports that, as of April 3, 2024, DJT was the single most expensive stock to short in U.S. markets by a significant margin, meaning brokers had a significant financial incentive to lend non-existent shares.[1] The letter was released publicly on Tuesday, April 23, 2024.[1]
