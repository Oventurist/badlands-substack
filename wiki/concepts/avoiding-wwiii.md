# Avoiding WWIII

"Avoiding WWIII: Reviving a Sane Manifest Destiny and Monroe Doctrine" is an essay published by Badlands Media on June 4, 2024 and written by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], editor-in-chief of The Canadian Patriot Review. The article argues that the American doctrines of Manifest Destiny and the Monroe Doctrine were originally republican and anti-imperial in character, that they were later perverted by what the author calls an Anglophile [[deep-state|Deep State]], and that their authentic spirit survives today in the Eurasian development programs of [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]] rather than in the United States. Ehret presents the revival of large-scale infrastructure cooperation — culminating in a Bering Strait tunnel and an Alaska–Canada railway — as the practical alternative to a slide into a third world war.

## Framing and method

The article opens by anticipating the objection that the world has "suffered from enough Monroe Doctrine and Manifest Destiny over the past two centuries," citing wars across the Middle East, genocide against Native Americans, and imperial abuses of Latin American nations. Ehret does not deny these abuses but argues that the origins of the two interconnected philosophies should not be discarded "simply because evil perverts justified evil things using them." He explicitly rejects both the position that American history is entirely evil (attributed to "Wokeist zombies") and the position that it is entirely good (attributed to "simple-minded patriots"), proposing instead a nuanced re-reading of the founding philosophies.

## The republican origins of Manifest Destiny

The earliest expression of America's sense of Manifest Destiny is traced to John Winthrop's "City on a Hill" speech of 1630, in which America's identity was to serve as a beacon and role model of liberty for other nations. The article says the idea was amplified by George Washington and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], who deliberately wanted the first American army and legislature named "Continental" rather than "of the 13 colonies." At the time Washington led the Continental Congress, the Americas were dominated by hereditary French, English, Russian, and Spanish oligarchical empires.

The article stresses that Washington understood that harmony with Natural Law did not make the young republic immune from becoming another evil empire, which is why he warned against the dual evils of foreign entanglements abroad and party politics at home. President John Quincy Adams extended these ideas by drafting the Monroe Doctrine, which he knew could work only if America ventured "not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy."

## How not to do Manifest Destiny

Ehret argues that the forces shaping American expansion, especially during the first 125 years when Manifest Destiny had its greatest influence, "failed this test miserably." In its first twelve decades the United States grew from thirteen backward colonies in 1776 to forty-five industrially advanced states in 1900, but throughout those years the anti-slavery voices of Benjamin Franklin, John Jay, John Quincy Adams, [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], Charles Sumner, William Seward, and William Gilpin were subverted by what the article calls an Anglophile Deep State parasite class running both Wall Street in the north and the southern slave power — a "multiheaded hydra lurking within the heart of the USA."

Where abolitionist-leaning figures such as Franklin and Alexander Hamilton encouraged the sharing of knowledge, technical skills, science and technological progress with both black and Native populations without forcing religious conversion or crushing local traditions, the Deep State in both the northern and southern zones of influence sought only to expand power "through the conquerors whip." The southern perversion of Manifest Destiny, promoted by [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]], Jefferson Davis and Albert Pike, envisioned expanded black slavery and Native Americans cordoned into plantations or reservations under a "superior" white race. Jackson's 1830 Indian Removal Act emptied valuable lands that were quickly handed to southern cotton planters, who expanded the influx of African slaves and heightened the tension between free and slave states leading to the Civil War of 1861–65.

## The slave power spreads

By 1836 the [[second-bank-of-the-united-states|Second National Bank]] had been killed after a mass propaganda campaign convinced "a duped mob" that it was an instrument of tyranny. Over the following six decades, the article notes, the only five presidents who made serious efforts to revive America's nationalist system died in office: Harrison in 1841, Taylor in 1850, Lincoln in 1865, Garfield in 1880, and McKinley in 1901.

Jackson, celebrated for having "killed the bank" and "paid America's debts," is described as "a force of pure destruction": he paid the debt by cutting all infrastructure projects and unleashing mass speculation, producing the devastating 1837 bank panic. The article calls him an unrepentant racist who assisted the slavocracy by emptying southern lands of Cherokee in the genocidal Trail of Tears and handing the land to cotton oligarchs loyal only to profit, their "way of life," and the British Empire. Ehret cites historian Michael Kirsch's 2012 study *How Andrew Jackson Destroyed the United States*.

Between 1801 and 1840 southern cotton exports grew from 100,000 bales per year to 1 million, with 80% going to Britain. The article describes a [[city-of-london|City of London]]–Wall Street–New Orleans triangle dominating the world system, with New Orleans representing over 12% of all U.S. banking capital, and the southern slave states growing into the world's fourth largest economy with British financial and logistical support. Ehret says this degeneration accelerated under Jackson and "his handler" Martin Van Buren, with cotton exports reaching 4 million bales per year by 1860 and the slave power expanding through the Kansas Nebraska Act of 1854. While many radical abolitionists in the United States and British Canada advocated dissolution of the Union as an alternative to civil war, the article praises Frederick Douglass for recognizing "the higher historic fight at hand."

## The transformative power of rail development

Despite the loss of the national bank, the article says a general orientation toward scientific and technological optimism still animated the American character. The first passenger and freight railway, the Baltimore and Ohio, was built in 1827, and rail soon crossed the Appalachians. Over 2,800 miles of rail had been laid by 1840, growing to 9,021 miles in 1850 and 30,000 miles by 1860. A quantum leap followed Lincoln's decision to devote resources at the height of the Civil War to the Trans Continental Railway, built between 1863 and 1869. With the war resolved — "largely through the intervention of the Russians," the article claims — rail growth exploded to over 93,000 miles by 1880 and 163,000 miles by 1890, when William Gilpin published his *Cosmopolitan Railway*.

Gilpin, an Anglophobe suspicious of British imperial intrigue, became a leading figure of anti-slavery America. For decades this grouping rallied around the Whig Party of John Quincy Adams and won two important victories. The 1840 victory brought President William Harrison to office on a platform of reviving Hamilton's national bank, protective tariffs and internal improvements; the 1848 victory brought Zachary Taylor. Ehret asserts both Whig presidents were poisoned: Harrison served only three months, dying under mysterious circumstances before legislation for a Third National Bank — passed by both houses of [[congress|Congress]] — could be signed; Taylor's death was officially attributed to "too many cherries and cold milk."

During the Civil War the British are described as supplying weapons, warships, logistical support, intelligence hubs in Canada and funding to the Confederate rebels, nearly forcing Lincoln to fight on two fronts. The article contrasts the legitimate defenders of Manifest Destiny, who grew territory through diplomacy — the Louisiana Purchase of 1804, the Oregon Territory in 1848, and the Alaska purchase of 1867 — with the "America" of Wall Street and Virginia's slave power, which picked fights with neighbors, as in the Mexican War of 1846–48 and the 1893 overthrow of Hawaii's monarchy. Ehret concludes that these anti-imperial traditions have withered, leaving a hollowed-out industrial base, a broken cultural connection to Christian values, and "an alienated nation of nihilistic consumers without a vision for the future."

## Eco-colonialism in the 20th century

The article argues that the ghettoization of Native peoples in tribal reservations segregated First Nations tribes for generations, locking them into cycles of dependence, poverty, substance abuse, and rates of infant mortality and suicide magnitudes higher than the national average. It further claims these populations have been used by "game masters" to block continental development projects under a policy of "human ecosystems-management." Since the late 1960s, Ehret writes, it has become fashionable to treat native populations as extensions of their local ecosystems, both presumed by computer models to exist in stationary equilibrium, models then used to calculate conservation regions and optimal population growth.

Understanding this, the article says, is vital for anyone trying to grasp why the large-scale growth policies of Franklin Roosevelt and [[john-f-kennedy|JFK]] were derailed in the late 1960s with the onset of the Vietnam War. The vast growth of conservation parks and federal lands kept off limits to infrastructure investment is presented not as the work of "warm-hearted nature lovers" but as a calculated policy by geopolitical gamemasters intent on confining society to a controlled world of "limited resources." Ehret adds that liberal imperialists who wept for abused natives were happy to support the mass sterilization of native women through the 1970s while leaving reservations without clean water, reliable electricity, healthcare, or quality jobs. He quotes Gilpin, Colorado governor during the Civil War and a vocal proponent of a trans-continental railway extending into Eurasia, who described the reservations as "like blocks of stone in the wall of a jail against the frontier line." Money, the article says, was infused into the coffers of corrupt tribal leaders who let oil cartels exploit their resources, a pattern Ehret sees paralleled in neo-colonial policy toward Africa.

## A new Eurasian Manifest Destiny

In Russia, the article says, future orientation has taken the form of a 21st century "Russian Manifest Destiny" aimed at extending civilization into the Siberian Far East and Arctic and beyond Central Asia, Mongolia, Japan and China. Since 2018 Russia's eastern development ambitions have increasingly merged with China's northern extension of the Belt and Road, the Polar Silk Road, amplifying railways, roads, telecommunication hubs, ports, energy projects and sea corridors.

China's own version of Manifest Destiny is identified as the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], unveiled in 2013. The article states that over $3 trillion has been spent on projects involving 140 nations, encompassing high-speed, maglev and conventional rail, development corridors, smart cities, industrial hubs, pipelines, and advanced science initiatives in space exploration, atomic power, fusion research and quantum computing. Corridors run through Russia and the Central Asian "Middle Corridor," while a southern route from China through [[pakistan|Pakistan]], [[iran|Iran]], Iraq, [[syria|Syria]] and Lebanon has taken form, with Syria joining on January 12, 2022. Over 48 of 54 African nations, 18 Ibero-American states and 20 Arab states have also joined.

## Diversity, unity and the Chinese model

Both Russia and China are described as vast nations with undeveloped resources and diverse cultural, religious, linguistic and ethnic groups. The vast majority of Russia's 146 million citizens live in the westernmost fifth of the country, with 80% in or near urban zones from the Baltic to the Caspian, while only 24 million are diffused across northeastern Siberia, a landmass 1.3 times the size of Canada. Nearly 94% of China's population lives east of the Heihe–Tengchong Line, with the inner heartland housing only 6%. Russia hosts 193 ethnic groups comprising nearly 20% of its population; China's Han majority represents 91%, with 56 distinct ethnic groups representing 113 million people scattered across Tibet, Xinjiang and Inner Mongolia. The article poses the question of whether industrial development across multi-ethnic territories must come at the expense of smaller cultures, or whether an organic balance is possible.

Ehret answers that, despite denunciations from the "Five Eyes-managed political class," China's approach to African BRI partners and to its own minorities contrasts sharply with the Western oligarchy's tradition of exploitation and cultural genocide. He points to cultural heritage centers, rising literacy, and government patronage of traditional languages, songs, stories and dances in Tibet and Xinjiang; per capita GDP in Xinjiang increasing a hundredfold since 1979; and average life expectancy rising from 30 years in 1949 to 72 years. Rail projects cited include the 1,776 km Lanzhou–Urumqi high-speed line running parallel to the electrified Lanxin passenger railway completed in 2014 — with Lanxin converted to freight serving the Eurasian Continental Landbridge over 11,000 km from East China to Rotterdam — the Ruoqiang–Hotan railway completing a loop around the Taklamakan desert, and the 600 km Kashgar–Osh railway to Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan. The latter is said to benefit Uzbekistan's Ferghana Valley, described as a landing point for Saudi Wahhabism since the 1990s from which radicalized jihadis were infused into Pakistan, Afghanistan and China. A new highway from Kashgar City to Pakistan is being finalized as a component of the China Pakistan Economic Corridor.

The article states that over 24,400 mosques exist in Xinjiang, alongside 59 Buddhist temples and 253 churches, and that in only eight years Saudi–U.S. funded terrorism in China was dealt with "without a single Arab state bombed back to the stone age." It accuses [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]]-controlled propaganda outlets of obscuring these facts and advises readers to put down the Epoch Times and watch local African news or CGTN's African channels. While conceding that concessions favoring Chinese firms are built into most BRI projects, Ehret contrasts China's approach with [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]]–[[world-bank|World Bank]]–[[usaid|USAID]] programs that he says kept poor nations in usurious debt-trap enslavement, providing fish rather than the capability to fish.

## Russia's monetarist obstacles

In Russia, a privatized central banking system still influenced by IMF monetary protocols has made [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s Far Eastern vision harder to actualize than China's, where a state-owned banking system provides an instrument of long-term growth. Russia's private central bank, established in its current form in 1990, is described as retaining structural ties to the IMF and [[world-trade-organization|WTO]] and as staffed by liberal ideologues who prioritize "balanced budgets" and free markets over the emission of productive credit.

Despite these obstacles, the article reports that Sergei Shoigu's "grand masterplan for Siberia" begins with five new cities housing 500,000 to a million citizens each. Plans also include modernizing and double-tracking the 9,300 km Trans Siberian Railway and the 4,300 km Baikal–Amur Mainline and integrating them with Mongolia, China and Japan, dovetailing with the International North South Transportation Corridor from Moscow to India via Central Asia and Iran. Freight traffic along these lines was projected to rise from 120 million tons per year to 180 million tons in 2024. Russia's Development Plan for the Northern Sea Route, adopted in 2019, seeks 80 million tons of annual shipments by 2024 and includes 40 new vessels, additional nuclear icebreakers, railways and northern seaports, cutting ten days from shipping between China and Europe.

## The Bering Strait tunnel revival

On May 7, 2014, the Chinese government, speaking through the state-owned Beijing Times and China Daily, endorsed the century-old Bering Strait tunnel project. The article notes that the proposal was first advanced by Russia in August 2007 and re-emphasized by the Russian government in 2011, and that both George W. Bush in 2007 and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] later ignored Putin's offers. With Chinese backing, Ehret argues, the 200 km tunnel connecting Russia and Alaska "can no longer be ignored."

On September 26, 2020, Trump announced federal support for a 2,570 km railway connecting Alaska for the first time with Canada and the lower 48 states. The article calls the potential re-emergence of the Alaska–Canada Railway, under a pro-Pacific and pro-Arctic development model, the first genuine display of this paradigm in North America in decades. Its survival depends on Environmental Impact Assessments and the Federal Government of Canada, which Ehret describes as run by "anti-development Rhodes Scholars and technocrats committed to depopulation and world government." If built, the project would open Arctic resources, create tens of thousands of direct and millions of indirect jobs, and vector North America's economy toward Asian markets, breaking the West free of the twin traps of anti-development environmentalism and pro-militarization right-wing views. The Alaska–Alberta Railway Development Corporation, tasked by Trump with building and managing the $17 billion project, features programs to tie North America into the Asian market and to restore a rail system that has fallen derelict since the Second World War and the age of "highways and cars." Whether such dirigiste national planning can now occur, following the jettisoning of [[nafta|NAFTA]] and the TPP, remains to be seen.
