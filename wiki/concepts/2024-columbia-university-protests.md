# 2024 Columbia University Protests

The 2024 Columbia University protests were a wave of pro-Palestinian campus demonstrations at [[columbia-university]] in New York that drew national condemnation and mass arrests in April 2024.[1] Protests had been ongoing since the previous week, when dozens of pro-Palestinian demonstrators set up an encampment of dozens of tents on campus.[1] Columbia University President [[minouche-shafik]] later authorized the [[nypd|New York Police Department]] to help remove the encampment, and more than 100 protesters were taken into custody according to NBC News.[1]

## White House response

The [[white-house]] condemned calls for "violence and physical intimidation targeting Jewish students" as protests continued at colleges across the United States, including the demonstration at Columbia.[1] White House deputy press secretary [[andrew-bates]] said in a statement shared with The Hill that while every American has the right to peaceful protest, "calls for violence and physical intimidation targeting Jewish students and the Jewish community are blatantly Antisemitic, unconscionable, and dangerous — they have absolutely no place on any college campus, or anywhere in the United States of America."[1] Bates added that "echoing the rhetoric of terrorist organizations, especially in the wake of the worst massacre committed against the Jewish people since the Holocaust, is despicable," and that the administration condemned such statements in the strongest terms.[1]

## Campus reaction

The statement came shortly after reports circulated that a rabbi associated with Columbia University had sent a message to 300 Jewish students urging them to leave campus, return home, and remain there ahead of the start of Passover, which began that Monday night.[1] The campus Hillel countered, writing on the social platform X that "We do not believe that Jewish students should leave" Columbia, while affirming that "the University and the City need to do more to ensure the safety of our students."[1] The episode was carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief]].[1]

## Biden's response (May 2024)

After days of demonstrations at Columbia and other Ivy League schools, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] told reporters that he condemned "the anti-Semitic protests" while also condemning "those who don't understand what's going on with the Palestinians."[2] Critics characterised the remark as a "very fine people on both sides" moment applied to the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]] debate, accusing the president of moral relativism in equating keffiyeh-wearing demonstrators on Columbia's "Gaza Quad" who target American Jews with those refusing to accept the charge of genocide in Gaza.[2]

On the same day, in brief remarks at the [[white-house]], Biden affirmed the importance of free speech but delivered what commentators read as primarily a law-and-order message: "We are not an authoritarian nation where we silence people or squash dissent... But neither are we a lawless country. We're a civil society and order must prevail."[2] For weeks the White House had tried to stay out of the campus dispute, treating the protests as a distraction even as demonstrators dubbed the president "Genocide Joe" and conservatives blamed him for campus disorder; his remarks were interpreted as an acceptance of the conservative framing of the protests as fundamentally a problem of discipline, prompted by fear that a public sense of disorder was becoming an electoral liability.[2] Protesters and their defenders argued that the demonstrations had been largely free of real violence until police arrived.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the coverage as evidence of Crisis Convergence and Narrative Whiplash working against the establishment, listing among the messages being programmed into mainstream audiences that Biden was somehow against both sides of the Israel-Gaza inflection point, that chaos historically damages incumbents, and that mutual hatred of Biden had become the only point of agreement across the ideological extremes.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied the campus scenes to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2008 pledge to "fundamentally transform" the United States, arguing the Ivy League was the place to start understanding that transformation because, unlike earlier protest movements, "these people will be working at our top law firms, in media organizations and in the [[state-department|State Department]]."[2]
