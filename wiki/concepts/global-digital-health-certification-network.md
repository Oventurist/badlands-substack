# Global Digital Health Certification Network

The Global Digital Health Certification Network (GDHCN) is a [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] initiative to convert the [[european-union|European Union]]'s digital COVID-19 certification system into a worldwide health credentialing system.[1]

## Launch and purpose

In June 2023 the WHO announced it would take up the EU system of digital COVID-19 certification to establish a global system intended to facilitate global mobility and protect citizens across the world from ongoing and future health threats, including pandemics.[1] This was described as the first building block of the GDHCN, which was to develop a wide range of digital products "to deliver better health for all."[1]

WHO Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]] said the organization aimed to offer all WHO Member States access to an open-source digital health tool built on the EU's "highly successful digital certification network," based on principles of equity, innovation, transparency, and data protection and privacy, with new digital health products intended to help people everywhere receive quality health services quickly and more effectively.[1]

## Criticism

Badlands Media presents the GDHCN as the health-sector arm of a broader [[digital-identification|digital identification]] rollout, arguing that a globally interoperable certificate tied to vaccination status functions as a mobility permission system rather than a neutral health record, and that its adoption completes a "trifecta" of globalist institutions — the EU, the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and the WHO — advancing the same infrastructure.[1]
