# United States Sovereign Wealth Fund Proposal

The idea of a United States sovereign wealth fund — a government-owned investment vehicle financing "great national endeavors" — surfaced as a campaign-season policy dispute in September 2024, when competing versions were floated within two days of one another by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the Biden administration.[1]

## Trump's proposal

Speaking to economic leaders at the Economic Club of New York on Thursday, September 5, 2024, Trump described a sovereign wealth fund as a means of addressing persistent debt issues, to be funded through his plan to impose tariffs on all imports.[1] He said the fund would let the government "invest in state-of-the-art manufacturing hubs, advanced defense capabilities, cutting-edge medical research and help save billions of dollars in preventing disease in the first place," adding that many of those present would help advise and recommend investments.[1]

The initial reaction reported was derision: hard-core Democrats and socialist billionaires balked, mocking the concept as an attempt to imitate Saudi Arabia.[1]

## The Biden administration version

Late on Friday, September 6, Bloomberg reported that aides to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had been crafting a proposal for a sovereign wealth fund that would allow the United States to invest in national security interests including technology, energy, and critical supply-chain links.[1] The work was attributed to National Security Adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and his deputy [[daleep-singh|Daleep Singh]], and Bloomberg acknowledged that it mirrored Trump's proposal at least in spirit.[1]

## Commentary

The Badlands brief presented the sequence as another instance of the [[kamala-harris|Kamala]]/Biden administration appropriating a Trump idea, following the elimination of taxes on tips and measures to stimulate new business creation.[1] It also argued that Bloomberg's insistence that the administration had been working on the concept "for months" was a necessary framing device, since floating the plan one day after Trump would otherwise have looked plainly derivative, and predicted that critics who had mocked the idea would embrace it once attached to Biden.[1]

## Proposed citizen-owned funds in Badlands commentary

"The Harvard Control Grid" proposes sovereign wealth funds "owned by the people" as the replacement for the elite institutional structures it describes.[2] Reading Trump's America First strategy through the asset-recovery lens of [[executive-order-13818|EO 13818]] and [[executive-order-13848|EO 13848]], the article envisions such funds buying up distressed institutional assets, using dividend-producing infrastructure, energy and private equity holdings to fund American citizens directly, and replacing DEI boards with "locally accountable sovereign trustees."[2] It offers as its illustration a [[harvard-management-company|Harvard Management Company]] portfolio "no longer in the hands of Rockefeller proxies, but reclaimed by the very people it exploited."[2]
