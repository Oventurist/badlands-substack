# Frobisher Bay Domed City

The **Frobisher Bay domed city** was a planned Arctic city in what is now Iqaluit, Nunavut, which Prime Minister [[john-diefenbaker|John Diefenbaker]] committed to build as an integral component of his 1958 "Northern Vision" campaign.[1]

The design, produced by Canada's Department of Public Works, announced a domed city encircled by twelve large towers housing 4,500 workers and their families, connected by an underground network of tunnels.[1] To ensure that the settlement was truly a city of the future, recreation facilities, shopping centres and other amenities were included so that "the comfort of Toronto would be something accessible even in the Arctic."[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents Frobisher Bay as the North American forerunner of Russia's nuclear-powered domed city of [[umka-arctic-city|Umka]] on the island of Kotelny and of former defence minister [[sergey-glazyev|Sergey Glazyev]]'s programme to construct five advanced cities in the Russian Arctic.[1] He argues that the Frobisher Bay plan remains the paragon of northern development and complements the [[trans-eurasian-belt-development|Trans-Eurasian Belt Development]] policy.[1]

## Restatement in the Alaska summit essay

Ehret restated the Frobisher Bay case in his August 2025 essay on the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin Alaska summit]], calling the plan the paragon of northern development and a complement to the multi-trillion-dollar [[trans-eurasian-belt-development|Trans-Eurasian Belt Development]] policy.[2] He described it as a "city of the future" model echoed by Russia's [[umka-arctic-city|Umka]] design, whose planners took the International Space Station as their template for housing over 5,000 workers and families in the Arctic climate.[2]
