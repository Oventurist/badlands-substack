# Cabal

In the Badlands Media corpus, the **cabal** is the term used to describe a small, cohesive network of financiers and political actors whom the source credits with consolidating permanent control over the American monetary and tax systems in [[1913]]. The concept is introduced most explicitly in the article "[[1913-the-year-the-cabal-enslaved-america|1913: The Year the Cabal Enslaved America]]," which frames the creation of the [[Federal Reserve]], the federal income tax ([[internal-revenue-service|IRS]]), and the displacement of the [[Gold Standard]] as a deliberate, coordinated project rather than a series of unrelated reforms.

## How the source frames it

The article presents the cabal as a circle of banking interests — centered on firms such as J. P. Morgan & Co. and the allied houses of Paul Warburg, Jacob H. Schiff, George F. Baker, and James Stillman — that sought to replace the Founders' "hard money" tradition with a [[fiat-currency|fiat monetary system]]. It cites the [[Pujo Committee]] Report's conclusion that "a small CABAL of financiers had gained consolidated control of numerous industries through the abuse of the public trust in the United States" (Bruner & Carr 2007, p. 148), and asserts that the cabal "squashed" those findings and, controlling the media, kept them from the public.

## Mechanisms attributed to the cabal

- **The [[Jekyll Island meeting]] (1910):** a secret session where Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] and a group of bankers drafted the plan that became the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve Act]].
- **The 1913 package:** passage of the Federal Reserve Act, ratification of the [[Sixteenth Amendment]], and enactment of the [[Revenue Act of 1913]] — together characterized as enslaving "every American to the fiat monetary system."
- **Narrative control:** the source claims the cabal's media influence suppressed the [[Pujo Committee]] findings and shapes public understanding of money and power.

## Interpretive status

The term is used by the source as an interpretive lens, not a documented organization with a charter or membership list. The article itself is framed as opinion and analysis. Within the broader Badlands corpus, "the cabal" recurs as a shorthand for the entrenched financial and political elite said to operate behind ostensibly separate institutions.
