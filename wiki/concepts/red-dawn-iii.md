# Red Dawn III

**Red Dawn III** is the name of a United States Department of Defense delivery order awarded in July 2021 to the defense contractor [[icr-inc|ICR, INC.]], valued at approximately $2.6 million. The contract came to public attention in October 2023 through reporting by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], which noted that the project's name, location, subject matter and original delivery date appeared to echo the plot and imagery of the 1984 film [[red-dawn-1984-film|Red Dawn]] as well as the [[al-aqsa-flood|Hamas attack of October 7, 2023]].[1]

## Discovery

The author was first sent a link to the delivery order in December 2022 by a researcher identified as 13VanillaBean.[1] At the time she considered it merely irresponsible for the Department of Defense to name a project after such a specific war scenario — particularly a contract awarded to a Colorado company, given that the film is set in [[colorado|Colorado]].[1] The contract was recalled only several days after the initial Hamas assault on [[israel|Israel]], during an appearance on [[badlands-daily|Badlands Daily]], when the author remembered that the project's original delivery date had been October 26, 2023 — the same month paratroopers descended into Israel.[1]

## Contract structure

The award was made by the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]], with the Department of the Air Force as sub-agency and the [[air-force-life-cycle-management-center|Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]] as the awarding office, delivering the work to the [[united-states-air-force|United States Air Force]].[1]

Under the North American Industry Classification System the project is coded 541511, "Custom Computer Programming Services," within 5415 (Computer Systems Design and Related Services) and 54 (Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services).[1] Its Product or Service Code is AC33 — research and development on defense systems for ships, at the advanced development stage.[1]

Red Dawn III is a child contract under a parent program named [[cognition-cognos-support|Cognition Cognos Support]], which carries the same NAICS classification but is coded AC61: basic research on defense system electronics and communication equipment.[1] From this structure the article concluded that Red Dawn III sits inside a larger business-intelligence program in which the DOD uses [[ibm|IBM]] artificial-intelligence tooling, specifically [[ibm-cognos-analytics|Cognos Analytics]], with the child project focused on advanced development of custom programming for shipboard defense systems.[1]

## Sole source award

The delivery order was awarded on a [[sole-source-contracting|sole source]] basis, meaning no competitive bids were considered and the justification asserted that no other vendor could perform the work.[1] The article argued that sole-source contracting can indicate corruption, drawing a comparison to counties that contract ballot printing to [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] on a sole-source basis.[1]

## Schedule changes and payment

By October 2023 the contract's end date had been pushed out roughly six months, from October 2023 to April 2024.[1] According to [[usaspending-gov|USAspending.gov]], ICR, INC. also appeared not to have been paid for the work, leading the author to suspect the project had not yet been delivered — while allowing that the delay could reflect reporting variance or the contemporaneous government shutdown turmoil in the House.[1]

## Claimed coincidences

The article enumerated three parallels it considered noteworthy: the project was awarded in Colorado, where *Red Dawn* is set; its original delivery month, October 2023, coincided with a paratrooper-led attack, as the film opens with a paratrooper assault; and the project concerns programming of shipboard defense systems at a time when the United States moved two carrier strike groups into the Mediterranean in support of the conflict.[1]

## Vendor timing argument

A further section, titled "She was Never Supposed to Lose," argued that ICR, INC.'s contracting history tracked the political calendar: work began in January 2016 and stopped in July 2017, resumed modestly in 2019 after the 2018 midterms that returned [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] to the speakership, and expanded sharply once [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took office.[1] The author asked whether shipboard defense systems genuinely required no custom programming during the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, or whether the program had been paused until a friendly administration was expected to hold the White House.[1] She concluded that she had no answers, only further questions, and that at minimum the naming was irresponsible in a low-trust environment.[1]
