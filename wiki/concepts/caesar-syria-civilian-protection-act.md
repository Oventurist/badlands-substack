# Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act

The Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act — commonly referred to as the "Caesar sanctions" — is a U.S. law that took effect in June 2020. It is named after a Syrian military defector, codenamed "Caesar," who smuggled out photographs of torture victims of the Assad regime.[1]

## Scope

The Act targeted the Assad regime and any party doing business with it, with particular emphasis on the energy, construction, and finance sectors.[1]

## Trump administration position

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] issued temporary waivers of the Caesar sanctions, but only [[congress|Congress]] can vote to cancel them outright.[1] In late October 2025 the administration said it supports Congress permanently removing the sanctions, a step tied to the broader rebuilding of U.S.–Syrian relations ahead of the planned November 10 White House visit by Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]].[1] U.S. envoy to [[syria|Syria]] [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] said al-Sharaa was expected to sign an agreement joining the U.S.-led coalition against ISIS during the visit.[1]

## Survival of the June 2025 executive order

When President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order on June 30, 2025 lifting some financial sanctions on Syria, the Caesar Act's congressionally mandated measures remained in force, along with the U.S. designation of Syria as a state sponsor of terrorism.[2] Those surviving sanctions specifically targeted funds for reconstruction and natural gas development, limiting the practical scope of the [[syria-sanctions-relief|sanctions relief]].[2]
