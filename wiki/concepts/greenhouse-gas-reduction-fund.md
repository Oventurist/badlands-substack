# Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund

The Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund is a $20 billion grant program created under the [[inflation-reduction-act|Inflation Reduction Act]] and administered by the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]] to finance clean-energy and decarbonization projects. Rather than distributing money directly to project developers, the program routed its funds through a small number of intermediary nonprofit organizations that would in turn lend or re-grant the capital — a structure that critics, and later EPA leadership under the second Trump administration, characterized as a system of "cutouts" and "pass-through entities."

## Criminal referrals and program suspension

As reported in the June 3, 2026 [[badlands-media|Badlands]] Brief, EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] made criminal referrals to the EPA inspector general and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] alleging self-dealing in the distribution of Biden-era green energy grants. Zeldin canceled or paused roughly $29 billion in such awards, including the full $20 billion of Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund money, which had been routed through eight nonprofit intermediaries.

Among the specific cases cited was that of former Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund director Jahi Wise, who was described as personally overseeing a $5 billion grant to his own prior employer. A separate $2 billion award went to [[power-forward-communities|Power Forward Communities]], a nonprofit linked to [[stacey-abrams|Stacey Abrams]] that had reported only $100 in revenue the previous year, and $20 million went to the group Young, Gifted & Green, whose chief executive applied for funding while serving on the White House Environmental Justice Advisory Council.

Zeldin described the rushed 2024 distribution as "blatant waste and abuse" that "feels criminal," arguing that the money flowed to entities connected to former Obama–Biden officials, Democratic donors, and politically connected individuals.

## Interpretation in the Badlands corpus

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] situated the fund within what he calls the Scarcity Paradigm — the argument that climate and "energy justice" programs functioned less as environmental policy than as a mechanism for locking in lower energy density while laundering public capital through networks of aligned nonprofits. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the referrals to the accountability work begun by [[doge|DOGE]], arguing that exposure of waste without prosecution would merely embolden the next scheme.
