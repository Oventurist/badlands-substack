# Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund

The Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund is a $20 billion federal grant program created under the [[inflation-reduction-act|Inflation Reduction Act]] and administered by the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]] to finance climate and clean-energy projects.[1]

## Structure and criticism

Rather than disbursing funds directly, the EPA routed the $20 billion through eight nonprofit intermediaries, described by critics as "cutouts."[1] In 2026, EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] canceled or paused roughly $29 billion in Biden-era green energy grants, including the full $20 billion associated with the fund.[1]

## Criminal referrals

Zeldin made criminal referrals to the EPA inspector general and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] alleging self-dealing in the fund's distribution.[1] Among the cases highlighted was former Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund director [[jahi-wise|Jahi Wise]] personally overseeing a $5 billion grant to his prior employer.[1] Other cited examples included a $2 billion award to [[power-forward-communities|Power Forward Communities]], a nonprofit tied to [[stacey-abrams|Stacey Abrams]] that reported only $100 in revenue the previous year, and $20 million granted to [[young-gifted-and-green|Young, Gifted & Green]], whose chief executive applied while serving on the White House Environmental Justice Advisory Council.[1]

Zeldin said the money flowed to "pass-through entities" connected to former Obama–Biden officials, Democratic donors, and politically connected individuals, calling the rushed 2024 distribution "blatant waste and abuse" that "feels criminal."[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the referrals as a necessary accountability sequel to [[doge|DOGE]]'s exposure of waste, fraud, and abuse, arguing that without transparency and prosecution "the next corrupt scheme will be emboldened to go even harder."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the fund within a longer arc in which climate and justice framing served to lock in lower energy densities while laundering political and financial power through the administrative state.[1]
