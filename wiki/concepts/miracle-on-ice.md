# Miracle on Ice (1980 U.S. Olympic Hockey Team)

The "Miracle on Ice" refers to the victory of the 1980 United States Olympic hockey team over the Soviet Union at the Winter Olympics in Lake Placid, New York, an event Badlands Media has described as a sporting moment that lifted America out of a dark period.[1]

## Context

The 1980 Games took place during what the article characterizes as a tenuous period in American history: gas shortages, inflation, rampant unemployment, a Cold War at fever pitch, and Americans held hostage in Iran.[1] American morale was said to be "in the toilet," with the light at the end of the tunnel resembling an oncoming train.[1] The article draws an explicit parallel between that period and present-day conditions in the United States.[1]

## The team and the upset

The American squad was a "rag-tag bunch of college kids" competing in a sport the United States was not known for, and it went on to win a gold medal.[1] Their Soviet opponents had played together for nearly fifteen years, were grown professional men required to serve in the Red Army, and would otherwise have found their way to the NHL in short order.[1] Ten days before the Games, coach [[herb-brooks|Herb Brooks]] scheduled an exhibition against the Soviets at Madison Square Garden in which Team USA was soundly beaten 10–3; the article argues Brooks used that game deliberately to burn off his players' nerves.[1] By the time the two teams met again in the medal round on February 22, 1980, the United States was a completely different team and won 4–3.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands Media frames the victory as a product of teamwork, mutual belief, and belief in the coach, and compares the way the team was regarded to the way its audience regards President Trump.[1] The article contends that the tide in America began changing shortly after the gold-medal run — Reagan was elected, the economy began to rebound, and "America began to find her heartbeat again" — and asserts that the event played some role in that turnaround.[1] It is cited as evidence that sports can serve as a "leaning post" for a demoralized nation.[1]
