# ACE Act

The **ACE Act** is election-integrity legislation advanced by the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] in June 2023 and framed by its sponsors as a package of "common sense election reforms" for the District of Columbia.[1] Testimony submitted to a joint hearing of the House Administration and Oversight committees on June 7, 2023 presented the bill as a response to what its supporters described as a crisis of voter confidence in the United States.[1]

## Background

Supporters of the bill cited polling indicating that 37 percent of Democratic-leaning voters and 71 percent of Republican-leaning voters had expressed concerns about whether the election system accurately reflects the will of the voters.[1] The testimony argued that free, fair, secure, well-run, and transparent elections are "a cornerstone of — and certainly not a 'threat to' — democracy," and explicitly rejected the characterization of election-integrity laws as "Jim Crow 2.0," a phrase associated with [[joe-biden|President Joe Biden]]'s rhetoric on state voting laws.[1]

The testimony further contended that election-integrity measures increase rather than suppress participation, pointing to Georgia and Florida as states that had recently passed comparable reforms and had, in the sponsors' account, seen higher participation and greater confidence in electoral outcomes.[1]

## Provisions

As described in commentary accompanying the hearing, the ACE Act would require voter identification, restrict ballot harvesting, guarantee public observation of the election process, tighten security around mail-in ballots, mandate cleaning of voter rolls, and bar non-U.S. citizens from voting.[1] The bill was drafted to apply specifically to the District of Columbia, resting on [[congress|Congress]]'s constitutional oversight responsibilities for the federal district, while its sponsors maintained that it respected the rights of states to manage their own election processes.[1]

## Reception

[[james-comer|Representative James Comer]], chairman of the Oversight Committee, framed the measure as a template rather than a purely local fix, saying the reforms of the ACE Act would "hopefully create a system that other states will look to as a model for a secure election system."[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] in its news brief treated the bill favorably. The commentator writing as [[thestormhasarrived|TheStormHasArrived]] argued that the measure was "what we need in every state (and more.)"[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a more expansive reading, suggesting that a nationwide overhaul of election systems shortly before the 2024 election would energize turnout and would, in his framing, serve as evidence for the view that the Biden administration was functioning as a disclosure operation.[1]
