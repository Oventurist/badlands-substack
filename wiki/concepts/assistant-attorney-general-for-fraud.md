# Assistant Attorney General for Fraud

The assistant attorney general for fraud is a new federal position announced in January 2026 by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], created to investigate fraud tied to federally backed programs following claims that taxpayers lost billions of dollars in Minnesota.[1]

## Structure and authority

Vance said the new assistant attorney general will coordinate investigations across federal agencies and pursue criminal charges where warranted.[1] Unusually, the role will not be housed within the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]; it will instead operate out of the [[white-house|White House]] and be overseen by [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and the vice president.[1] The officeholder will likely serve through the end of the Trump administration and will have nationwide jurisdiction over fraud investigations, with initial efforts concentrated in Minnesota.[1]

## Confirmation

The position requires Senate confirmation, and Vance said a nominee was expected to be announced in the coming days, adding that Senate Majority Leader John Thune had pledged swift confirmation.[1]

## Related funding actions

The announcement followed the administration's decision to halt more than $10 billion in federal funding for social services programs in Minnesota, New York, California, Illinois, and Colorado, and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]]' freeze of federal child care funding for Minnesota.[1] The initiative is closely tied to the [[minnesota-somali-fraud-hearings|Minnesota Somali fraud hearings]].[1]
