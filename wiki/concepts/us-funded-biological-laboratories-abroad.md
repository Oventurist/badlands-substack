# U.S.-Funded Biological Laboratories Abroad

The network of U.S.-funded biological laboratories abroad comprises more than 120 facilities in over 30 countries, including more than 40 in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], that have received American taxpayer funding for years to study pathogens and improve biosecurity.[1]

## ODNI review

Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] is leading a probe into the laboratories, many of which were funded through the [[cooperative-threat-reduction-program|Pentagon's Cooperative Threat Reduction Program]].[1] The Ukrainian facilities are considered potentially at risk amid the ongoing war.[1] The review follows President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order banning federal support for gain-of-function research in high-risk nations, and seeks to identify exact locations, the pathogens studied, and any dangerous experiments.[1]

## Narrative significance

Badlands commentary treated the review as vindication of long-standing claims that Ukraine hosted illegal and secret bioweapons work denied by [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] and his staff, with contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] arguing the implications would extend to exonerating [[russia|Russia]]'s stated rationale for its military operation if substantiated.[1]

## Russian Ministry of Defence briefings

The [[russian-ministry-of-defence|Ministry of Defence of the Russian Federation]] has maintained a running public analysis of what it calls the military and biological activities of the United States and its allies in Ukraine and elsewhere, arguing that such activity poses a security threat to many nations.[2] In a briefing published on August 16, 2023, Lieutenant General [[igor-kirillov|Igor Kirillov]], chief of the Russian Nuclear, Chemical and Biological Protection Troops, said that while the stated goals of U.S. programmes are disease monitoring and assistance to developing countries, the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] in practice conducts uncontrolled dual-use research in circumvention of its obligations under the Biological and Toxin Weapons Convention.[2]

According to that briefing, American military projects focus primarily on potential biological weapons agents — anthrax, tularemia and coronavirus — alongside pathogens of economically significant infections such as pathogenic avian influenza and African swine fever.[2] Kirillov argued there was a clear trend whereby pathogens within the Pentagon's area of interest, including COVID-19, avian influenza and African swine fever, subsequently become pandemic, with American pharmaceutical companies emerging as the beneficiaries.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] linked the briefing to earlier reporting on U.S.-funded biolabs in Ukraine circulated at the outset of the war, and to the individuals named in that coverage — [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], [[george-soros|George Soros]] and [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] — predicting the subject would become newsworthy again.[2] He tied the absence of accountability for the pandemic to the push for the [[who-pandemic-accord|WHO CA+ pandemic treaty]] and roughly two hundred amendments to the International Health Regulations, which he argued would elevate the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] and its principal funders into a de facto world government in the event of another global health scare.[2]
