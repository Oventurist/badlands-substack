# Defense Production Act

The **Defense Production Act (DPA)** is a United States statute granting the president wartime and emergency authority to direct, prioritize, and finance industrial production deemed essential to national defense. Originally enacted during the Korean War, it allows the executive branch to unlock federal funding, override ordinary procurement sequencing, and compel or subsidize private-sector output in designated sectors.

## Invocation in the 2026 energy push

On April 20, 2026, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] invoked the Act to unlock federal funding for new domestic energy projects, signing five presidential determinations directing the Energy Department to support coal-fired power plants, liquefied natural gas, petroleum refining, and power-grid infrastructure including gas turbines and transformers. The stated purpose was to overcome financing, regulatory, and supply-chain barriers, and to address surging electricity demand from artificial intelligence and heavy industry alongside high oil, gasoline, and power prices that had been aggravated by the conflict with [[iran|Iran]].

## Badlands treatment

Writing in the Badlands Brief of April 22, 2026, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the DPA invocation was the intended endpoint of a deliberate sequence rather than an emergency improvisation. In this reading, the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] blockade and the wider energy squeeze generated the public and global mandate required to activate wartime powers, making the crisis "the perfect narrative shield" for a domestic energy transition and infrastructure buildout planned in advance. He noted having sketched this framework in March 2026 during the initial flare-up in the Iranian theater, and characterized energy as "the backbone of the multipolar reconstruction process." The same essay tied the move to the [[taco-trade|TACO]] critique of Trump, holding that critics misread the objective because they projected a war aim onto him that he never held.
