# Defense Production Act

The Defense Production Act (DPA) is a United States statute granting the president wartime authorities over industrial production and supply chains. In the Badlands corpus it is treated primarily as the legal instrument through which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] financed a domestic energy build-out during the 2026 Iran crisis.[1]

## April 2026 invocation

On April 20, 2026, Trump invoked the Defense Production Act — explicitly described as wartime powers — to unlock federal funding for new domestic energy projects.[1] He signed five presidential determinations directing the Energy Department to support coal-fired power plants, liquefied natural gas, petroleum refining, and power-grid infrastructure such as gas turbines and transformers.[1]

The stated aims were to overcome financing, regulatory, and supply-chain barriers, and to address surging electricity demand from artificial intelligence and heavy industry, along with high oil, gasoline, and power costs that had been aggravated by the Iran conflict.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the invocation was not a reactive emergency measure but the intended end state of a deliberate sequence: a framework he had laid out in March 2026, in which Trump would invoke and then escalate the DPA once the public and global mandate to do so reached crisis levels.[1] In this reading, the act was "supercharged" precisely when the engineered energy squeeze was most visible to the American people, making the global energy crisis a "narrative shield" for a Warp Speed–style domestic energy transition and infrastructure renaissance.[1]

## March 2026 invocation over Iran-driven energy concerns

In March 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] invoked authorities under the Defense Production Act to address energy supply concerns linked to instability involving [[iran|Iran]], directing a US oil company to restart petroleum production operations in California that had previously been halted.[2] The action was taken amid concerns that disruptions tied to the Iran conflict could affect global oil markets and US energy supply.[2]

The [[white-house|White House]] order adjusts delegations of authority under the Act, clarifying which federal officials may exercise certain powers granted by the statute.[2] The Act allows the federal government to direct private industry to prioritize contracts and expand production deemed necessary for national defense; per the order, addressing potential energy supply disruptions linked to Iranian instability — including directing the restart of certain California oil production operations — meets federal delegation authority under the law.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] identified the operator as [[sable-offshore|Sable Offshore]] and the assets as the Santa Ynez Unit and pipeline system, describing a boost of roughly 50,000 barrels per day in in-state production and a corresponding cut to foreign crude imports flowing through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and similar chokepoints.[2] He framed the invocation as "the codification (literally) of sovereign mandate" and as phase one of a broader "Sovereign Disentanglement," in which engineered chaos abroad becomes the permission structure for energy decentralization at home.[2]
