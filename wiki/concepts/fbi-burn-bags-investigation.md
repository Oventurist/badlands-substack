# FBI Burn Bags Investigation

The FBI burn bags investigation is a preliminary inquiry opened by the Federal Bureau of Investigation after classified information tied to the Trump-Russia collusion saga was discovered stashed in "burn bags" at FBI Headquarters.[1]

## Discovery and scope

The [[fbi|FBI]] launched the preliminary investigation in July 2025, according to an opening electronic communication made public in [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filings in federal court on Monday, November 3, 2025, as part of the prosecution of fired bureau director [[james-comey|James Comey]].[1] The bureau said the classified information had been stashed away in a room at FBI Headquarters and was related to [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] as well as other FBI inquiries into President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his allies.[1] The preliminary investigation is authorized to use grand jury subpoenas and polygraph examinations to obtain answers.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the disclosure as an example of the "Boomerang" concept that has become one of the most ubiquitous terms in the information war, arguing that from Russiagate to elections and from borders to law and order, Trump "doesn't toss Boomerangs ... he hurls them," and that many are now coming back around.[1]

## DOJ inquiry into former FBI officials (August 2025)

A source familiar with the matter confirmed to CBS News that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was investigating whether former senior FBI officials mishandled classified documents found in a "burn bag" at the bureau's headquarters.[2] The documents in question related to the FBI's probe into whether Russia influenced the 2016 election in President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s favor — an investigation that has drawn Trump's ire for years, as he has called the allegations a "hoax" and a "witch hunt."[2] In the weeks before the report, some top administration officials claimed that Obama-era intelligence leaders who looked into Russian meddling may have broken the law; nobody had been criminally charged in the probe.[2] The FBI's investigation into Russian election interference, dubbed [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]], was opened during the 2016 campaign and continued into Trump's first term.[2]

The burn bags first entered public discourse in July 2025, when Fox News reported that FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] had discovered thousands of Russia-related documents in bags typically used to hold sensitive materials about to be destroyed.[2] Days later Patel wrote on X: "We just uncovered burn bags/room filled with hidden Russia Gate files."[2]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] cautioned that when one hears a story about damning evidence being found in burn bags, "it's just a cover story to allow for the disclosure of evidence they've had the entire time," noting that he and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] had covered the matter on their show a month earlier.[2]

## July 2025 Fox News report and the Durham annex

Fox News Digital reported on July 30, 2025 that FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] had found a trove of sensitive documents related to the origins of the Trump–Russia probe buried in multiple "burn bags" in a secret room inside the bureau.[3] Sources told Fox News Digital that the "burn bag" system is used to destroy documents designated as classified or higher, and that multiple bags were found filled with thousands of documents.[3]

One of the documents recovered from a burn bag was the classified annex to former special counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s final report, which includes the underlying intelligence he reviewed.[3] Declassification of that annex was being handled in close coordination among [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], Patel, Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and acting [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] Director William Hartman.[3]

Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] used the report as an entry point into the meetings held by Obama White House Counsel [[w-neil-eggleston|W. Neil Eggleston]] with FBI officials including [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] and [[sally-yates|Sally Yates]] in September and October 2016, arguing that each was followed within a day by a significant development in the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] and Carter Page FISA timelines.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] added that while all crimes have actual timelines, the most important implication is the narrative timeline, since "if you're putting together the grandest RICO case of them all, causation needs to be stitched into the sequence."[3] [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] argued that although the Supreme Court's immunity ruling might protect [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] on original charges, he could still be summoned before a grand jury and could not invoke the Fifth Amendment because of that immunity — "if he lies as a private citizen about what he did as president, he'll no longer have that immunity."[3]
