# Silk Road

The Silk Road was a darknet marketplace founded by [[ross-ulbricht|Ross Ulbricht]] that operated over the Tor anonymity network and used Bitcoin for payments, until it was shut down by US authorities following Ulbricht's 2013 arrest.[1]

## Operation and takedown

The site functioned as a general-purpose peer-to-peer marketplace in which vendors and buyers transacted pseudonymously. [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] described it as "eBay with Tor and Bitcoin," noting that while drugs were among the goods sold, so were mundane items such as vintage stamps.[1] Ulbricht was arrested in 2013 and sentenced in 2015 to life without parole for creating and operating the marketplace.[1]

## Interpretations

Critics of the prosecution argue that the government's real objection was not to narcotics trafficking but to the demonstration that individuals could trade freely without state permission, and that the Silk Road case was used to deter the growth of decentralized marketplaces and currencies.[1] Gunnels also floated the possibility that the Silk Road itself was an intelligence operation — a marketplace built and permitted to flourish so that its dramatic destruction would frighten the public away from decentralized commerce.[1]

## Aftermath

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] granted Ulbricht a full and unconditional pardon on January 21, 2025, fulfilling a campaign pledge to the cryptocurrency and libertarian communities.[1]
