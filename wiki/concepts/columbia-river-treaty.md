# Columbia River Treaty

The **Columbia River Treaty** was a Canada–United States hydroelectric agreement that became the central battleground between British Columbia Premier [[wac-bennett|W.A.C. Bennett]] and the federal civil service in Ottawa.[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes a sabotage operation mounted against the treaty by Ottawa's technocrats, and writes that Bennett's struggle to develop [[british-columbia|British Columbia]] was made possible through his association with [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], who sided with Bennett over Ottawa's civil service regarding the treaty.[1] The revenues generated from the treaty allowed Bennett to finance a vast rail expansion through the 1960s, opening development corridors in previously inhospitable regions of the north and enabling permanent cities in the Peace River region.[1] Bennett's clashes over the treaty, followed by the ouster of Prime Minister [[john-diefenbaker|John Diefenbaker]] in 1963, hardened his resolve to nationalize BC Power Corporation and create provincial institutions such as the Bank of British Columbia.[1]

Kennedy]] with siding with Bennett over Ottawa's civil service on the question.[2] Revenues generated from the treaty, he wrote, funded the vast rail expansion of the 1960s that opened development corridors in previously inhospitable regions of the great north and produced permanent cities in the Peace River region.[2]
