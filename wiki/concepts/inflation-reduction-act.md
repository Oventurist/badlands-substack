# Inflation Reduction Act

The Inflation Reduction Act is the sweeping 2022 spending and tax law enacted under the Biden administration that provided hundreds of billions of dollars in funding for climate, clean-energy, and decarbonization programs. Despite its title, the bulk of the law's appropriations were directed toward energy transition subsidies, tax credits for renewables and electric vehicles, and large block grants administered through federal agencies — most prominently the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]].

## Grant programs and subsequent scrutiny

The law funded the $20 billion [[greenhouse-gas-reduction-fund|Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund]], whose money was distributed in 2024 through a handful of intermediary nonprofits rather than directly to project sponsors. As reported in the June 3, 2026 [[badlands-media|Badlands]] Brief, EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] canceled or paused roughly $29 billion in Inflation Reduction Act green energy grants and referred multiple cases to the EPA inspector general and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], alleging self-dealing in how the awards were made. Among the paused awards was a $2 billion grant to [[power-forward-communities|Power Forward Communities]] for residential decarbonization.

## Critique in the Badlands corpus

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the Inflation Reduction Act and the broader [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] era as an aggressive acceleration of what he terms the Scarcity Paradigm: a policy architecture that presented itself as climate and justice policy while in practice locking in lower energy densities and routing large flows of public capital through politically aligned nonprofits. In this reading, the rushed 2024 disbursements and the thinly capitalized recipients that received them illustrate the mechanism rather than exceptions to it.
