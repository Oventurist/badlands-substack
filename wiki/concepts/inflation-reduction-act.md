# Inflation Reduction Act

The Inflation Reduction Act is the Biden-era spending law that funded a large tranche of federal green energy and climate grant programs administered principally by the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]].[1]

## Grant programs and 2026 reversal

Programs financed under the act included the $20 billion [[greenhouse-gas-reduction-fund|Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund]] and a separate $2 billion residential decarbonization award to [[power-forward-communities|Power Forward Communities]].[1] In 2026, EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] canceled or paused roughly $29 billion in grants funded under the law and referred multiple awards to the EPA inspector general and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for criminal review, alleging self-dealing.[1]

Zeldin argued the money had been pushed out through "pass-through entities" tied to former Obama–Biden officials, Democratic donors, and politically connected individuals, describing the rushed 2024 distribution as "blatant waste and abuse."[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the act's grantmaking as a late phase of the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] era, which he characterized as simultaneously constraining domestic energy density and channeling public capital through aligned nonprofit networks.[1] He contrasted this with two years of executive orders under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] aimed at dismantling energy limits and restoring pathways in nuclear power and supersonics.[1]

### Climate grant funds and the 2025 EPA controversy
Under the Inflation Reduction Act, the Environmental Protection Agency received a $20 billion pot of money to dole out to climate groups.[2] In February 2025 the EPA under Administrator Lee Zeldin disclosed that $2 billion of that funding had been awarded to Power Forward Communities, a nonprofit linked to Stacey Abrams, and set aside at Citibank before Joe Biden left office.[2]
