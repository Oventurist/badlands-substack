# Valediction: Three Nights of Desmond

*Valediction: Three Nights of Desmond* is a book published by Trine Day Press and written by the husband-and-wife team of [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]] and [[elizabeth-gould|Elizabeth Gould]].[1] It recounts the authors' first-hand experience as the only two American journalists permitted to enter war-torn Afghanistan in 1981 and again in 1983, and places Afghanistan's story within the context of world history.[1]

## Argument

The book's central discovery, as the authors relate it, is that there was not one United States but two opposing factions of U.S. intelligence at war with each other.[1] The dividing question was whether American foreign policy would presuppose an honest Soviet intention to adhere to détente and the 1972 [[salt-i|SALT]] treaty, or whether U.S. security doctrine would assume the Soviets were liars bent on imposing global world government.[1]

Fitzgerald and Gould document the 1976 formation of the think tank [[team-b|Team B]], which revived the earlier [[committee-on-the-present-danger|Committee on the Present Danger]] led by financier [[paul-nitze|Paul Nitze]], the organisation that in 1950 spearheaded passage of [[nsc-68|NSC-68]].[1] They argue that although history has demonstrated Team B's thesis to be an artificial construction, the propaganda succeeded, and that by 1978 the Trilateral-run coup of U.S. intelligence was nearly complete.[1]

Of the neoconservatives who carried out that transformation, the authors write: "developed by an inbred class of former Trotskyist intellectuals, the Team B approach represented a radical transformation of America's national security bureaucracy into a new kind of elitist cult."[1]

## Afghanistan and the Dubs killing

The narrative opens with Fitzgerald's chance encounter with [[allard-lowenstein|Allard Lowenstein]] before the 1980 elections and Lowenstein's murder two weeks later.[1] It proceeds through the authors' contacts inside the [[state-department|State Department]] and media industrial complex, and culminates in their reconstruction of the February 14, 1979 assassination of U.S. Ambassador [[adolph-dubs|Adolph Dubs]] in Kabul.[1] Their evidence includes the presence of [[cia|CIA]] and [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] agents at the scene, indications Dubs was alive after the famous shootout, the moving of his body, and .22 calibre close-range gunshots most likely fired by Kabul police chief Mohammed Lal, who himself turned up dead months later.[1]

The book connects the murder to [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s design for drawing the Soviets into an Afghan trap, to [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]], and to the subsequent transfer of the world's opium production centre from Southeast Asia to Afghanistan.[1]
