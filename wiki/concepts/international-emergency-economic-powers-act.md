# International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA)

The International Emergency Economic Powers Act is the federal statute the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration used to impose sweeping global tariffs before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] struck that use down.[1]

In its February 20, 2026 decision, the Court held that IEEPA does not authorize broad tariff actions without an explicit delegation from Congress, invalidating the tariff regime built on it.[1] The ruling is discussed in detail at [[supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-2026|the February 2026 tariff ruling]].[1] Following the decision, [[customs-and-border-protection|U.S. Customs and Border Protection]] stopped collecting duties under the IEEPA-based authority, and the administration reissued tariffs of up to 15 percent under the Trade Act of 1974 instead.[1]

The same ruling drove a secondary controversy over whether financial firms — notably [[cantor-fitzgerald|Cantor Fitzgerald]] — had positioned themselves to profit from refunds of IEEPA-based duties.[1]
