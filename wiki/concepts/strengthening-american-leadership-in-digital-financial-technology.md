# Strengthening American Leadership in Digital Financial Technology

"Strengthening American Leadership in Digital Financial Technology" is the executive order on the cryptocurrency industry signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Thursday, January 23, 2025. The long-awaited move appeared to meet several industry demands.[1]

## Background

The crypto industry had lobbied Trump to issue executive orders during his first 100 days in office that would establish a U.S. bitcoin reserve, ensure that the industry could access banking services, and create a crypto advisory council.[1]

## Provisions

The order sets up a crypto working group — a "Presidential Working Group on Digital Asset Markets" — and calls for that group to evaluate "the potential creation and maintenance of a national digital asset stockpile, and propose criteria for establishing such a stockpile."[1] As summarized in the Badlands News Brief, the order directs the creation of a regulatory framework for cryptocurrencies, contemplates a national digital asset stockpile derived from lawfully seized assets, and explicitly bans the development of [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currencies]].[1] The stated effect is to position the United States as a world leader in digital asset innovation while safeguarding against potential abuses of power such as state-controlled digital currencies.[1]

Trump, who has called himself "a crypto president," briefly discussed the industry the same day while addressing world government and business leaders by video at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in Davos, repeating his pledge to make the United States the "world capital" of both crypto and artificial intelligence.[1]

## Reaction and commentary

Badlands contributor Patrick Gunnels wrote that the order was a cause for celebration for all crypto enthusiasts other than [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] maximalists, who he said were furious that Trump would acknowledge any digital money besides Bitcoin.[1] He characterized maximalist doctrine in religious terms — "To maximalists, Bitcoin is the way, the truth and the life" — and argued that the suggestion that other digital assets could play a role in the future of finance was treated as heresy, with the idea of a national stockpile potentially including "shitcoins" sending maximalists into a rage.[1]

Gunnels argued that the reaction revealed a deeper problem: a small clique of influential figures had convinced maximalists that Bitcoin must stand alone to succeed, portraying all other digital assets as threats rather than complements, boxing them into a rigid ideology that denies the broader ecosystem's potential for collaboration and innovation.[1] In his view Bitcoin's decentralization and first-mover advantage ensure its prominence while the ecosystem thrives on diversity; the executive order, he wrote, does not undermine Bitcoin but acknowledges the complexity of digital finance and ensures its responsible growth.[1]
