# Stakeholder Capitalism

Stakeholder Capitalism is the governance doctrine, promoted principally by the World Economic Forum, that corporations should serve a broad set of social "stakeholders" rather than shareholders alone.[1] Badlands Media treats it as the organizing framework behind the Sustainable Development Goals; Environmental, Social & Governance (ESG) scoring; Diversity, Equity & Inclusion (DEI) programs; and public-private partnerships.[1]

## Polling as a rejection signal

An August 2024 Gallup–Bentley University study of 5,835 U.S. adults, fielded from late April to early May, found that just 38 percent of Americans believe businesses in general should take a public stance on current events and public policy — a decline of ten percentage points from 2022.[1] The steepest drops occurred among groups previously most receptive to corporate activism, including Democrats, Americans under 45, Black Americans and Asian Americans, while Republicans were among the only cohorts where support rose.[1] Cynthia Clark, a professor of management at Bentley University and one of the survey's creators, suggested the shift over three years may reflect "none other than fatigue."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing in the *Badlands News Brief*, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the study as "a direct rejection of Stakeholder Capitalism," arguing that 62 percent of Americans now reject the agenda.[1] She described the program as a "globalist change agenda of weaponized empathy" driven through businesses global and local with the complicity of NGOs and third-party associations, and without opposition from officials sworn to protect rights.[1] In her account this is why "rainbows and fists show up in your consumer product packaging, your children's public school curriculum, and your workplace culture" — coordinated social change on behalf of the [[new-world-order|New World Order]].[1] She framed the unwinding of that influence as a long game won "one conversation at a time," concluding: "Never stop fighting. We're winning."[1]
