# Nature of the Control System

"Nature of the Control System" is the title of a Badlands Media essay series begun by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] with the February 10, 2023 feature "Cracks in the Curtain," which is billed as the first installment in a series discussing the nature of the control system.[1]

## Premise

The series opens with the question of how easy it is to control a civilization.[1] Deschamps observes that commonly cited mechanisms — money printing, media manipulation of public opinion (propaganda), and food control — are all genuine factors, but that such answers are too vague, and that what is needed is detailed, granular information.[1] Invoking the saying that the devil is in the details, he argues that citizens should know at least as much as "the enemies of freedom" in order to counter the machinations directed at them.[1]

The first installment is deliberately presented in an "easy and carefree way," surveying key concepts that later installments are to examine as "essential nodes of understanding."[1]

## Pillars of control

The essay's central contention is that because money is essential to advanced societies, insidious groups learned long ago that they could effectively control an entire civilization through the money system.[1] Doing so, however, also required gaining control of other pillars: the law, banking and accounting, religion, and the media.[1] With such a control system in place, those groups could enact laws granting unfair advantages to one sector of the economy while forcing another to do more than its fair share of work.[1]

Deschamps frames investigating money — how it is defined within an economy, the laws and regulations managing it, how the general population uses it, and how the elites who control it use it — as pulling back the curtain on the Wizard of Oz and exposing one of the pillars of the engines of control.[1] He describes this as the "study everything" approach.[1]

The essay also argues that consolidation of power by what it calls the cabal has gone on almost completely unchecked over the past 400 years, largely because citizens rarely have the time, energy, and cohesive support among themselves to rein in government after emergency powers are claimed.[1]

## Parasitism and philanthropy

A closing theme is that the people with the most influence over society have become parasites, when the reverse should be true: those with the most influence should be the most altruistic, protecting the whole of society for everyone's benefit.[1] The essay identifies charities as one instrument of this inversion, arguing that through the guise of philanthropy and well-crafted media campaigns the "parasitic elites" advance their agenda, with only a small portion of the population wise enough to see through the ruse.[1]

Future installments are promised on the secrets of money, how these relate to the control system, and what would be required to "flip the script" and build a system immune to corruption.[1]
