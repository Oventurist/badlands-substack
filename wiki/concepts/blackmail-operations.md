# Blackmail Operations

Blackmail operations are the systematic use of manufactured compromising situations to obtain leverage over powerful individuals — a practice Badlands commentary describes as central to the relationship between intelligence services and organized crime.[1]

## Mechanics

The method is described as simple and ancient: bait sexually temptable powerful people into compromising situations, document an act that can be used to pressure them, then deploy that leverage when compliance is required.[1] Where bribes, ideological appeals, or other positive reinforcements fail or lose their sway, "the blackmail card almost always delivers."[1] Criminal networks that traffic in this form of leverage — the sort with which intelligence agencies have long partnered — are said to know how to use it to maximum effect.[1]

## The Epstein case as exemplar

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] called [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] "the blackmail mogul of the late 20th century," working an old and effective game as old as prostitution itself.[1] He described [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] — Epstein's handler or boss, depending on who is asked — as "going down with the ship," blaming everything on Epstein, which Deschamps argued is likely exactly what she was trained to do and what Epstein himself willingly embraced before his death.[1]

In this reading, such operatives know how to get the goods on anyone and how to absorb blame in order to protect their dynastic benefactors.[1] The reported presence of [[william-burns|William Burns]], [[cia|CIA]] director under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], on Epstein's private calendar was taken as evidence that the network's reach extends "as high as they get."[1] Deschamps added that the CIA "was born out of the shadowy underworld of blackmail and organized crime to begin with," and dismissed the idea that the practice ended with the 20th century.[1]
