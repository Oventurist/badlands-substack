# Badlands Brief: Tuckabee Unites The Arab World As Mexico Courts Discombobulation

"Tuckabee Unites The Arab World As Mexico Courts Discombobulation" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published February 24, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The Brief is described by its publishers as a daily digest of Badlands commentary on "the narratives dominating the info war," and this edition carries contributions from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. Its lead items cover the 2026 State of the Union address, the killing of cartel leader El Mencho in Jalisco, an armed breach at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]], and regional condemnation of remarks by US Ambassador Mike Huckabee, followed by bonus items on [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and on mortgage rates.

## The 2026 State of the Union

The Brief reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] was scheduled to deliver the 2026 State of the Union address to a joint session of [[congress|Congress]] at 9 PM ET, amid what the source calls ongoing political and policy challenges including tariff policy disputes and international tensions. Multiple House and Senate Democrats planned to skip the address in favor of alternate events or protests; those named as not attending include Ed Markey, [[chris-van-hollen|Chris Van Hollen]], [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]], Tina Smith, and [[jeff-merkley|Jeff Merkley]]. More than a dozen Democratic congresswomen were reported to be planning to wear white during the address as a form of protest.

The US women's Olympic ice hockey team, fresh off a gold-medal win in Milan, declined an invitation to attend. In a statement released Monday the team said it was "sincerely grateful for the invitation extended to our gold medal–winning U.S. Women's Hockey Team" and "deeply appreciate[d] the recognition of their extraordinary achievement," but that "due to the timing and previously scheduled academic and professional commitments following the Games, the athletes are unable to participate." Trump also invited the US men's Olympic hockey team after their gold-medal win; a viral video appeared to show the team accepting, though no official confirmation was reported. Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] told reporters that Capitol leadership was working to accommodate a hockey team given the size of the group and the limited space in the chamber: "There's no way to have special guests on the floor because it's a literal session of Congress. But we're going to work and do what we can to accommodate."

Ashe in America notes that in December Trump honored the 1980 Team USA men's hockey squad, and that he was expected to honor "their descendants" — gold medalists exactly 46 years later, in overtime. Her commentary is an extended argument for the civic value of hockey, which she credits with instilling "honor, humility, patriotism," discipline, sportsmanship, and merit-based rather than equity-based competition, noting that in youth hockey boys and girls play together. She calls the small number of Americans in the NHL "our great national shame" and frames the evening as "the first SOTU of the Golden Age," on the grounds that media outlets had insisted the prior year's joint address was not technically a State of the Union.

## The Jalisco raid and the killing of El Mencho

The Brief reports that Mexican military forces killed Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes, known as "El Mencho," the alleged leader of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel (CJNG), during a weekend operation in Jalisco state, and that a reported 25 members of the Mexican national guard were killed in the operation. Violence subsequently erupted across parts of western [[mexico|Mexico]], including vehicle burnings and road blockades attributed to cartel retaliation. Trump responded on social media: "Mexico must step up their effort on Cartels and Drugs!" Separate reporting by the New York Post said the administration had quietly secured the transfer of roughly 100 cartel leaders from Mexico into US custody before the operation, without further detail on the scope of US coordination. El Mencho had long been among Mexico's most wanted traffickers, with multimillion-dollar rewards previously offered for information leading to his capture. The Mexican government had not released a full after-action report, and security forces reportedly remained deployed in Jalisco amid fears of further retaliation.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that Badlands had speculated for months that Mexico would be "the real target for a military operation," citing Trump's earlier statements that the cartels control Mexico and his acknowledgment that he had offered to send US troops to fight the cartels but was turned down by President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]]. He notes as counterpoint that a Mexican Senate commission had approved a proposal for SEAL Team 2 to train Mexican special forces, and that Sheinbaum had formally invited nearly twenty US Navy SEALs in a training capacity. Observing that the reported handover of more than 100 cartel bosses — including El Mencho's brother — preceded the raid, he suggests "it could be kayfabe to keep the cartels guessing" and urges readers to "withhold belief" until the data supports an informed opinion. He nonetheless states his personal view that the war against the cartels is genuine, crediting damage to cartel infrastructure to Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]], Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], and Nayib Bukele of El Salvador, and noting Petro's call for Trump to lead a "crusade against the bankers and financiers that support the cartels." He closes with a single word: "Accelerate."

## The Mar-a-Lago perimeter breach

On Sunday, February 22, 2026, an armed man breached the secure perimeter of Trump's Mar-a-Lago resort in Palm Beach, Florida. The suspect, identified as 21-year-old Austin Tucker Martin of North Carolina, was shot and killed by [[united-states-secret-service|US Secret Service]] agents and a Palm Beach County sheriff's deputy at approximately 1:38 AM ET near the property's north gate; he had been reported missing by his family days earlier. Martin was reportedly carrying a shotgun and a fuel canister; ordered to drop them, he dropped the fuel can but raised the shotgun into a shooting position, at which point officers fired. The President and First Lady were at the White House at the time. Martin had reportedly fixated on [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] stories according to text messages obtained by news outlets, and the [[fbi|FBI]] and local authorities were investigating; motive remained undetermined. Addressing the incident Monday, Trump said, "I don't know how long I'll be around, got a lot of people gunning for me, don't I, hon?"

Ashe in America connects the remark to the film *Melania*, which she says makes clear that First Lady [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] holds serious safety concerns about her husband and family, devoting significant time to her anxiety about the 2025 Inauguration Day and her statement that Barron would not leave the car if the parade were held outdoors — the parade was ultimately held indoors. She uses this to argue against "full control" theories, which would require treating the First Family's own film as a fabrication. Asking whether the Golden Age can simultaneously be a time of accountability, justice, truth and reconciliation and a time of "illegal psychological warfare operations on the American people," she says she is skeptical, adding that such destabilization "seems more like what the three letter agencies do to destabilize a foreign nation." She closes by asking readers to pray for the President's protection.

## Huckabee's remarks and regional condemnation

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee triggered regional backlash after telling [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] in a televised interview that [[israel|Israel]] has a biblical right to a large swath of the Middle East — referencing land "from the Nile to the Euphrates" — and adding that "it would be fine if they took it all," while also saying Israel is not currently seeking to expand its borders. Arab and Muslim governments and regional bodies condemned the remarks over the weekend, including [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Egypt, Jordan, the [[arab-league|League of Arab States]], and the Organization of Islamic Cooperation. Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry "categorically" denounced the comments and called for clarification, describing them as contrary to international law, the UN Charter, and diplomatic norms. A US Embassy spokesperson said the comments did not reflect any change in US policy and that Huckabee's full remarks made clear Israel had no desire to change its current boundaries. The controversy unfolded as Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] told Kan public radio that he expected [[hamas|Hamas]] to be given an ultimatum to disarm "in the coming days" and that "in the end" Israel will occupy [[gaza|Gaza]] and reestablish Jewish settlements there.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry reproduces a lengthy formal statement from the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, noting that it was co-signed by countries from every continent and by members of NATO, [[brics|BRICS]], the EU, the Arab League, the 57-nation Organization of Islamic Cooperation, and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization. He concludes that Huckabee "has succeeded not only in uniting the Muslim World, but what seems like the entire free world, against Israel," and dismisses eschatological readings of the moment — "OH WOW JUST LIKE IN THE BOOK OF REVELATION!" — in favor of the interpretation that "the arrogance and hubris of Israel's ruling elite has proven itself to be so wicked that the whole world is collectively retching in disgust."

He then compiles what he presents as a pattern of Israeli disrespect toward Trump: Smotrich's public statement on occupying Gaza; Ze'ev Elkin of the Finance Ministry asserting that Israel will not fund Gaza reconstruction under Trump's [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]]; and the formal rejection of the Board of Peace the previous month by top officials in [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s government, including Smotrich. He also recounts Trump's first Board of Peace meeting in Washington, at which the President indirectly called out [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] and [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] for their attacks on Emir Tamim of [[qatar|Qatar]]; Loomer responded by posting the video and doubling down, saying she "hates Qatar." Levin is accused of "acting like a mental patient," posting "Get Khamenei!" multiple times a day in what the author reads as a call to assassinate the leader of [[iran|Iran]] — [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] — while Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] is quoted comparing Trump to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and calling him a "wuss" should he decline to attack Iran.

The author argues that these figures "are not earnestly seeking peace" but are "bloodthirsty sociopaths fawning for war," likening the spectacle to "some satanic ritual." He states his belief that the moment for war with Iran has passed, on the grounds that Trump gave his word to "his Muslim friends" that he would bring peace and stability to the region, and that the greatest possible demonstration of strength to Arab partners would be to stand up to the Israel Lobby and the neoconservatives and halt their warmongering. Turning to Israeli domestic politics, he cites opposition leader Yair Lapid declaring that he would stand with Netanyahu in a war against Iran and expressing sympathy in Hebrew for the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel]] concept Huckabee described, and [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] — the other favorite in the October prime ministerial election — saying "the world will never love Israel, so it should learn to fear it." He concludes that supporters of war are themselves "radicalized religious extremists."

## Bonus items

The Brief reports that California Governor Gavin Newsom faced criticism after a video from a book tour stop in Atlanta showed him telling Mayor Andre Dickens and a reportedly majority-black audience, "I'm like you … I'm no better than you. I'm a 960 SAT guy." Newsom referenced his low SAT score and struggles with dyslexia, claiming "you'll never see me read a speech, because I can't read a speech"; critics circulated past video of him boasting about his reading ability. Newsom and his office called the criticism manufactured outrage, noting he has discussed his dyslexia and test scores publicly for years. On Monday he responded to [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] on X: "You didn't give a shit about the President of the United States of America posting an ape video of President Obama or calling African nations shitholes — but you're going to call me racist for talking about my lifelong struggle with dyslexia? … Spare me your fake fucking outrage, Sean." Republican pundits used the episode to highlight what they called Democratic double standards on race.

A second bonus item reports that the average US 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to 5.99% — the first time below 6% since 2022 — according to Barron's citing Freddie Mac data, prompting renewed refinancing interest and expanding refinancing eligibility for borrowers who took loans at higher rates over the previous two years. Relatedly, the US-based lender RateFi announced a mortgage product allowing borrowers to count cryptocurrency holdings such as [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and Ethereum toward qualification in underwriting without liquidating those assets.
