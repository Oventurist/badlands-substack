# 2024 Trump–Netanyahu Ceasefire Call Allegation

The 2024 Trump–Netanyahu ceasefire call allegation was a mid-August 2024 media controversy in which former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was accused of violating federal law by allegedly telephoning Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to discuss the Gaza ceasefire deal that the United States was helping to negotiate between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]].[1] The episode was covered in Badlands Media's news brief of August 22, 2024, which treated it as an information-warfare operation rather than a genuine scandal.[1]

## The reported call

According to Axios, which cited two unnamed "U.S. sources who were briefed on the call," Trump made the call on August 14, 2024.[1] The following day, however, Netanyahu's office denied that any such conversation had occurred; in a statement quoted by The Times of Israel, a spokesperson said that "contrary to media reports, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu did not speak yesterday with former President Donald Trump."[1] Newsweek, reporting the story, noted that it remained unclear whether a call had taken place and said it had contacted representatives of the former president for comment.[1]

## Reaction

Critics framed the alleged call as a violation of the [[logan-act|Logan Act]], the federal statute barring private citizens from negotiating with foreign governments in disputes with the United States.[1] The actor Steven Pasquale called it "pretty balls to the wall treason," and [[the-lincoln-project|The Lincoln Project]] — a group describing its mission as to "stop Trump, break MAGA and save America" — wrote that "there's absolutely no line he won't cross."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] disputed Newsweek's reliance on the Lincoln Project as an authority and argued that the story was a deliberate "psyop" whose most likely originator was Netanyahu himself.[1] His argument rested on motive: Netanyahu, he wrote, was the actor who gained relief from pressure to conclude a ceasefire deal, who acquired a scapegoat in Trump, and who stood to benefit if a Logan Act prosecution damaged Trump roughly two months before election day.[1]

The piece placed the allegation in a longer history of friction between the two men. It recalled that Trump publicly turned on Netanyahu in December 2021 in remarks to the Israeli journalist [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]], saying he did not trust Netanyahu and did not believe he had ever wanted peace with the Palestinians, and that he had shut down Netanyahu's campaign to expand settlements in the West Bank.[1] The author traced this to Trump's first presidential meeting with Netanyahu in February 2017, at which Trump told him to restrain settlement construction and Netanyahu scoffed, and to Trump's May 2017 visit to Israel.[1]

Ravid's subsequent television interview was cited for two further episodes: Trump's statement that he "was not happy with Israel" over the January 2020 [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]] strike — "Israel did not do the right thing. People will be hearing about that, at the right time" — and Ravid's account that Netanyahu had backed out of the operation the day before it was scheduled, with Trump reporting that Netanyahu was prepared to "fight Iran to the last American soldier."[1] Ravid also described how, on August 12, 2020, twenty-four hours before the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] were to be finalised, Israeli ambassador [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] telephoned the White House to say Netanyahu did not want the deal, and concluded that Abu Dhabi crown prince [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohammed bin Zayed]] was "the unsung hero of the Abraham Accords."[1]

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry rejected Ravid's framing that Trump had been deceived by his own staff about Israeli and Palestinian intentions, arguing instead that Trump had understood Netanyahu's character before taking office and that his deliberate disrespect toward Netanyahu in 2017 was a provocation that contributed to efforts to remove him from power in 2020.[1] He predicted that leaks might emerge from Tel Aviv to bolster the allegations, but concluded that "Donald Trump is a chess master."[1]
