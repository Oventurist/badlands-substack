# The Grand Chessboard

*The Grand Chessboard* is a 1998 book by [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] setting out a strategy for American primacy in Eurasia.[1] In Badlands commentary, the work is cited for Brzezinski's warning that the only thing capable of potentially threatening the emerging New World Order would be a triumvirate alliance of Russia, China, and Iran.[1]

That framing underpins the movement's interest in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]], whose territory sits inside the triangle formed by the capitals of those three powers, and in the Eurasian rail corridors associated with the [[kazakhstan-united-states-locomotive-deal|$4 billion US–Kazakh locomotive deal]] announced in September 2025.[1] Commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry used the Brzezinski thesis to argue that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s railroad master plan — linking Azerbaijan, India, Pakistan, Iran, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, China, and Russia — represents a counter-move against Western hegemony precisely along the axis Brzezinski feared.[1]

## Publication and influence

Badlands contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes *The Grand Chessboard*, written in 1997, as a poisonous book that became the guiding light for the neoconservative Project for a New American Century, led by the same neoconservatives who had emerged into power under Brzezinski's sponsorship in the 1970s — [[donald-rumsfeld|Donald Rumsfeld]], [[richard-perle|Richard Perle]], [[paul-wolfowitz|Paul Wolfowitz]], [[richard-helms|Richard Helms]] and [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]].[2]

In it Brzezinski wrote that "for the United States, Eurasian geostrategy involves the purposeful management of geo-strategically dynamic states and the careful handling of geopolitically catalytic states, in keeping with the twin interests of America in the short-term preservation of its unique global power and in the long-run transformation of it into increasingly institutionalized global cooperation."[2] He added, in a terminology hearkening back to the more brutal age of ancient empires, that "the three grand imperatives of imperial geostrategy are to prevent collusion and maintain security dependence among the vassals, to keep tributaries pliant and protected, and to keep the barbarians from coming together."[2]

The book identifies the coalition Brzezinski most feared: "Potentially, the most dangerous scenario would be a grand coalition of China, Russia, and perhaps Iran, an 'anti-hegemonic' coalition united not by ideology but by complementary grievances."[2] He further wrote that "how the United States both manipulates and accommodates the principal geostrategic players on the Eurasian chessboard and how it manages Eurasia's key geopolitical pivots will be critical to the longevity and stability of America's global primacy."[2] Ehret argues that by 2025 exactly such a multipolar alliance, led by Russia and China and joined by a growing array of nations, had created a coherent alternative security, cultural and financial architecture capable of challenging the unipolar hegemon Brzezinski believed should govern the New World Order.[2]
