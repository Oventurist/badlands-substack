# Biden Burisma Bribery Allegation

The Biden Burisma bribery allegation refers to a claim, contained in an FBI informant document provided to [[us-house-of-representatives|Congress]] in June 2023, that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] accepted a $5 million bribe from a Ukrainian oligarch.[1]

## Disclosure and timing

The [[fbi|FBI]] turned the informant document over to Congress on Thursday, June 8, 2023 — the same day the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] announced its indictment of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The coincidence of timing became central to how the story was received in independent media, which treated the simultaneous announcements as a deliberate burial of the bribery claim.[1]

## Coverage disparity

A [[media-research-center|Media Research Center]] tally found that [[abc-news|ABC]], [[cbs-news|CBS]] and [[nbc-news|NBC]] gave the allegation zero seconds of airtime while devoting 291 minutes to the Trump indictment.[1] Despite the absence of network coverage, polling by the Trafalgar Group found that 52 percent of voters believed the Biden family had received payments from foreign nationals to influence policy, against 31 percent who did not and 15 percent unsure.[1]

## Commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], argued that Biden's alleged crimes are "easily proven and obvious to even the most law-illiterate individual," which in his view explains why the facts were being withheld by the legacy press while attention was concentrated on Trump.[1] He proposed that the [[deep-state|deep state]] had already extracted what it needed from the indictment regardless of its legal outcome: a story large enough to keep the bribery allegation out of the consciousness of left-of-center audiences, and a durable talking point for the 2024 presidential race.[1] He added that the tactic does not work universally, and that each repetition causes more people to "see the system of systems for what it is."[1]
