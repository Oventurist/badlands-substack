# Harper v. Hall

Harper v. Hall is the North Carolina partisan gerrymandering case in which the state supreme court determined that state Republicans had drawn legislative and congressional maps to produce a partisan advantage in North Carolina's congressional delegation and in the state House.[1]

## Rehearing

The ruling was handed down in late 2022 by an outgoing Democratic majority on the [[north-carolina-supreme-court|North Carolina Supreme Court]].[1] Following the November 2022 elections, which produced a 5–2 Republican majority on the court, the new majority voted on February 3, 2023 to rehear Harper v. Hall along with a companion case on a state [[north-carolina-voter-id-law|voter ID law]], suggesting it was open to overturning both decisions altogether.[1] Rehearing is highly unusual in North Carolina, where a petition for rehearing had been granted only twice in the previous thirty years.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] disputed the underlying findings, asserting that neither the gerrymandering holding nor the voter ID holding was accurate, and presented the anticipated reversal as evidence that electoral participation can change judicial outcomes.[1]
