# Texas Senate Bill 4 (Immigration Enforcement Law)

Texas Senate Bill 4 is a state immigration law, signed by Governor Greg Abbott, that empowers Texas police to arrest migrants who cross the border illegally — a function traditionally reserved to federal authorities. The law was challenged in court by the Biden administration on the grounds that immigration enforcement falls within exclusive federal jurisdiction.[1]

## Litigation and Supreme Court holds

The law was originally scheduled to take effect on March 10, 2024, but [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] Justice [[samuel-alito|Samuel Alito]] repeatedly intervened to keep a lower court decision on hold pending the Court's review.[1] On Monday, March 18, 2024, Alito extended the temporary block once more, this time stating that the law would remain on hold "pending further order" of the Court — effectively blocking the statute indefinitely and giving all nine justices additional time to determine next steps.[1] It was the third time Alito had stepped in to preserve the freeze, and his most recent order would otherwise have expired that evening, allowing the law to take effect without further action.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] framed the litigation as a question of precedent rather than of sympathy for Texas, arguing that the border in question is also a federal border over which the federal government holds jurisdiction and a right of protection.[1] He contended that the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration was being "boxed in" by the dispute — appearing hypocritical both for inaction at the border and for fighting Texas's own defensive measures — and suggested this was precisely why Alito continued extending the hold.[1] Fleuret further argued that the same precedent would benefit a future [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, allowing federal immigration measures such as a border wall to stand firm against obstruction by individual states.[1]
