# Ritz-Carlton Purge

The Ritz-Carlton Purge was the mass detention of Saudi royals and officials that accompanied [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s formal coronation as Crown Prince on November 4, 2017.[1]

## Events

Upon being coronated, MBS — who had served as Saudi Defense Minister and thus commanded the military for the previous five years — rounded up "40" of his relatives and imprisoned them in the Ritz-Carlton hotel, accusing them of corruption and treason.[1] News reports at the time also indicated that martial law had been declared in the kingdom, a development the anonymous poster known as [[qanon|Q]] highlighted the same evening while asking why it was relevant.[1]

The author notes that "40" functions in Arabia as a colloquial term equivalent to the Western "dozens," pointing to forty years in the desert, forty days and forty nights, and the fable of "Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves."[1]

## Related moves

In parallel, MBS and his father [[king-salman|King Salman]] deposed the would-be Crown Prince, [[muhammad-bin-nayef|Nayef]], and the prince and minister then in charge of the Saudi military — both cousins of MBS — who were arrested and stripped of all powers.[1] Nayef was charged with treason and later accused of plotting to murder the King.[1] Among those the New York Times sought to distinguish from the royal family in its coverage was [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1]

The purge was preceded in the same year by the [[sword-dance|Sword Dance]] in Riyadh, and the article treats the sequence as evidence of a genuine anti-corruption campaign rather than a simple power grab directed by Washington.[1]
