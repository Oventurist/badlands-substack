# 2025 Resumption of US Nuclear Weapons Testing

In late 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ordered the resumption of United States nuclear weapons testing, ending a moratorium in place since 1992.[1]

## Trump's order

Trump asserted the move was necessary to keep pace with alleged nuclear activities by [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]], claiming the United States must maintain parity in both capability and credibility.[1] Writing on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] shortly before his highly anticipated meeting with Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] in South Korea, he said: "Because of other countries testing programs, I have instructed the Department of War to start testing our Nuclear Weapons on an equal basis. That process will begin immediately."[1] The directive placed execution with the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], referred to by the administration as the Department of War.[1]

## Russian response

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] said on Tuesday, November 11, 2025 that if the United States tests nuclear weapons, "Russia will respond in kind."[1] He cited a position laid out by President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in 2023, telling reporters via the state news agency TASS: "He stated that if any of the nuclear powers conducts a nuclear weapons test – not a carrier test or a subcritical experiment, but an actual nuclear weapons test – then Russia will respond in kind."[1]

Simultaneously, Russia offered to open talks with the United States over Washington's allegations that Moscow had carried out secret underground nuclear tests, in a bid to ease tensions between the world's two largest nuclear powers.[1] Russia has tested nuclear-powered and nuclear-capable delivery systems in recent weeks but rejects the accusation that it secretly detonated a nuclear device; both Moscow and Beijing rejected Trump's characterization of their drills.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that the alternation between escalatory and de-escalatory headlines is itself a signal, describing a transition from de-nuclearization to "re-nuclearization" running beneath prevailing disarmament narratives.[1] In this reading Trump, Putin and Xi are engaged in a nominal new global nuclear arms race that functions as a "translation layer" for an underlying process of transformation and peace, with Trump's "bad cop routine" aimed at audiences conditioned to fear nuclear-armed adversaries.[1] The analysis treats Trump as both lead character and lead author of the narrative — a "Bicameral" duality — and holds that enemy narratives become inversely correlated reverse indicators useful for macro pattern recognition.[1]
