# Peace of Westphalia

The 1648 Peace of Westphalia was the settlement that emerged in the wake of the Thirty Years' War.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Significance

Badlands Media commentary describes Westphalia as having established the framework of the modern sovereign nation state system at the heart of international law.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) It is cited as the first in a sequence of peace conferences — followed by the [[congress-of-vienna|Congress of Vienna]], the League of Nations and the [[united-nations|United Nations]] — that were convened only after years of devastating war and bloodshed had become insufferable.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Contemporary relevance

The author uses this history to argue that in the nuclear age humanity can no longer afford to wait for such conferences to follow catastrophe, and that a summit must occur before rather than after the next war.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## The Principle of the Benefit of the Other

Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies Westphalia as the origin of what he calls the Principle of the Benefit of the Other, created in 1648 as the basis of international relations and of the modern sovereign nation state, premised on the Christian concept that respect for one's neighbour is linked to one's own self-interest.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) With the treaty that ended the Thirty Years' War bloodbath that had reduced Europe to ashes, a new Christian concept was enshrined in international law asserting that each nation's self-interest would be wholly premised on the benefit of its neighbour's welfare and on the forgiveness of past transgressions.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

Ehret argues that this mode of thinking is emblematically expressed in the [[penn-treaty-of-1682|Penn Treaty of 1682]], concluded by [[william-penn|William Penn]] with the Delaware natives just forty years after Westphalia, whose articles bind both parties to mutual hospitality, to disbelieving slanders about one another until they have inquired directly, and to burying satisfied grievances "as in a bottomless pit."2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
