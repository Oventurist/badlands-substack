# Mid-Decade Redistricting

Mid-decade redistricting is the practice of redrawing congressional district maps between the decennial censuses, outside the customary once-per-decade cycle. By 2026 it had become a nationwide partisan battleground ahead of the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|midterm elections]].

The [[badlands-brief-323|Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026]], citing [[politico]], reports that congressional Democrats escalated their rhetoric over the redistricting fight after Republican-led map changes in states such as [[texas]] threatened the party's [[house-of-representatives|House]] prospects. Some lawmakers and strategists described the fight as requiring a "war posture," and Democratic operatives argued the party must abandon "unilateral disarmament" and aggressively counter GOP efforts using every available legal and political tool. One strategist said the goal was to politically "crush" the "souls" of the [[maga|MAGA]] movement, language the report treats as reflecting the increasingly existential tone surrounding control of the chamber.

The Brief frames the resulting environment as a growing nationwide push for mid-decade map battles, with both parties preparing for what is expected to be one of the most aggressively litigated and politically charged election cycles in modern US history.
