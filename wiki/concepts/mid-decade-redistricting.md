# Mid-Decade Redistricting

Mid-decade redistricting is the practice of redrawing U.S. House district boundaries outside the traditional post-census cycle.[1]

## Virginia referendum (2026)

The [[virginia-supreme-court|Virginia Supreme Court]] ruled on February 13, 2026 that an April 21, 2026 referendum on a Democratic-backed congressional redistricting amendment could proceed, allowing voters to decide the plan even as legal challenges continued.[1] The measure, passed by the Democratic majority in the General Assembly, would amend the state constitution to permit mid-decade redistricting of U.S. House districts, a departure from the traditional post-census process.[1]

If approved, the redrawn map could allow Democrats to expand their share of Virginia's 11-member U.S. House delegation from six seats to potentially ten, making the referendum a national focal point in the broader battle over control of Congress ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[1] A lower court had previously blocked the referendum on procedural grounds, but the Supreme Court reversed that decision and permitted the special election to proceed while litigation continues.[1]

## Texas, 2025

In July 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters he wanted Texas lawmakers to redraw the state's congressional district map to give Republicans five additional House seats.[2] "There could be some other states we're going to get another three, or four or five in addition. Texas would be the biggest one," he said. "Just a simple redrawing we pick up five seats."[2] The White House and [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] pushed for the redistricting, and Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] asked state leaders to take it up during a summer special session.[2] Democrats at the time controlled 12 of Texas's 38 congressional districts, and the move was seen as an opportunity for Republicans to forestall a Democratic House takeover in 2026 — though some regarded it as a dangerous risk.[2] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded dryly that "Texas totally has the best fake elections."[2]
