# Digital Vaccine Passport

The digital vaccine passport is a proposed global system of interoperable digital health credentials, developed jointly by the [[european-union|European Union]] and the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]], intended to verify vaccination status and facilitate cross-border mobility ahead of what the two bodies describe as "future pandemics."[1]

## Origins and agreements

The European Union originated the concept through its [[eu-digital-covid-certificate|EU Digital COVID-19 Certificate]].[1] The WHO and the EU signed an agreement on November 30, 2022, to ensure transparency and coordination on future health issues, and the two met in Geneva in June 2023 to discuss pandemic preparedness plans.[1]

The WHO stated that it was establishing a global digital health certification network built on the EU DCC's foundations, principles, and open technologies, that it would handle standard-setting and the validation of digital signatures to prevent fraud, and that it would not have access to underlying personal data, which would remain the exclusive domain of governments.[1] Thierry Breton, EU Commissioner for Internal Market, welcomed the WHO's adoption of the EU model.[1]

## WHO framing

WHO Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]] justified the effort by warning that "the threat of another variant emerging that causes new surges of disease and death remains," along with the threat of a deadlier pathogen, and that "when the next pandemic comes knocking — and it will — we must be ready to answer decisively, collectively, and equitably."[1]

## Criticism

Armstrong Economics reported that governments and global organizations still sought to control populations through forced vaccination despite what it characterized as the failure of the mRNA vaccine to prevent transmission or infection.[1]

Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the effort as the work of an "international criminal syndicate," arguing that the confidence with which the WHO and EU speak of a coming pandemic is itself notable.[1] He contended that language about helping "facilitate global mobility" in practice means limiting freedom of movement, that a primary objective is to normalize linking biometric data to digital identification, and that "public health" is being used as a conduit for superseding national and personal sovereignty.[1] DeLarme also noted that the WHO press release stated the program would particularly target low- and middle-income countries, arguing that poorer nations would once again serve as test subjects for the ruling class.[1] He observed that many Americans still dismiss the digital passport push as conspiracy theory even though the WHO has been explicit about its intentions.[1]
