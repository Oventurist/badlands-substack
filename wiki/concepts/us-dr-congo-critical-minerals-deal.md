# US–DR Congo Critical Minerals Deal

The US–DR Congo critical minerals deal is a strategic agreement under negotiation in April 2025 between the United States and the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]], intended to reshape the global critical minerals landscape and expand American influence in central Africa.[1]

## Terms under discussion

Reported by the Financial Times and relayed by the Sri Lanka Guardian, the arrangement — still in early stages — would facilitate greater American private-sector investment in the DRC's mineral reserves, particularly lithium, copper and cobalt, in exchange for increased U.S. backing for the embattled government of President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]].[1]

[[massad-boulos|Massad Boulos]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s newly appointed Africa adviser, confirmed that a "path forward" had been agreed with Tshisekedi during recent talks.[1] In a video statement released by the Congolese government, Boulos emphasized U.S. intent to drive mining-sector investment, positioning the United States as a key player in a region long dominated by Chinese mining interests.[1] According to Financial Times sources, the [[development-finance-corporation|U.S. International Development Finance Corporation]] is expected to underwrite portions of the planned investments, with American firms taking a larger role in exploiting Congo's mineral deposits.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the WEF regime had used proxy terrorist groups to destabilize the DR Congo and reclaim control of rare earth minerals lost through [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], the December 2017 order that targeted Israeli oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]].[1] In this reading, those destabilization efforts backfired, and Trump would end up claiming at least some of the minerals — with DRC deposits estimated at $24 trillion — for the United States.[1]
