# In Defense of Q (series)

**In Defense of Q** is a multi-part essay series written by the [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] author for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], conceived in approximately June 2024 and launched with Part One, "In Defense of Q - We Were Never Told to Sit Still," published July 16, 2024 under the subtitle "True Hope is Not a Pacifier; It's an Activator."[1] The series follows the author's earlier piece "Q is Real Because Math Says So," which argued mathematically that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[q-operation|Q]] are connected.[1]

## Scope and framing

The author stated that the series' aim is to supplement existing introductory material on the Q drops with practical guidance on how to be as effective a [[digital-soldiers|Digital Soldier]] as possible, and that it would be written in tandem with his ongoing Werewolf Series.[1] He justified the interruption of that other project by citing an "absolutely URGENT" need to clarify the psychological dynamics at work in what he termed the 5G War.[1]

Parts One and Two were written before the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|July 2024 attempt on Trump's life]] and were released as originally drafted; the author wrote that the shooting shifted reality so completely that earlier pieces "almost feel like a postcard from a past life," and announced that Part Three would incorporate lessons drawn from the assassination attempt.[1] He added that in a rapidly shifting landscape any piece may be outdated within a week or two, and encouraged readers to generalize the underlying principles and apply them to changing circumstances — a capacity he described as innate, healing for the mind, and deliberately targeted for destruction by the school system.[1]

## Argument of Part One

Part One responds to the claim, encountered increasingly often by the author, that Q was a psyop intended to "pacify" patriots.[1] He treated the claim as one of many measures being taken against Q, while allowing that most people repeating it are not operatives, since only a few operatives are needed to seed a narrative.[1] Offering what he called a "Hypnotist's Perspective," he set out to show that Q sought to *activate* patriots rather than pacify them, and that Q is a benevolent entity seeking not uncritical support but the maturation of citizens capable of discerning and communicating truth.[1]

### The puzzle argument

The essay's central argument is that Q gave followers puzzles rather than answers, and that this alone suffices to refute the pacification thesis.[1] The author illustrated the point with a childhood memory of playing puzzle-based video games — the *Myst* series in particular — with an older cousin who refused to supply solutions, offering only hints after long struggle, on the reasoning that the author would never become good at such games without working things out himself.[1] Had the cousin wished to pacify him, the author argued, he would simply have given the answers; it was precisely the refusal that produced competence.[1] Likewise, he wrote, "Q never held anyone's hand."[1]

### On hope versus fear

The essay conceded that some individuals did become passive on the reasoning that "Q's got it covered," but argued that far more people have become complacent for the opposite reason, and that over a long enough timeline hope is a far better motivator than fear because "fear burns people out—hope gives people their energy back."[1] Against the charge of false hope, the author asked how anyone can know a hope is false if they cannot accurately describe the objective reason for it.[1] In his experience many who trust Q are extremely active — engaging the legal system and political process, producing content, and working twelve or more hours a day for little money and at personal risk.[1]

### On misattribution

The author argued that dozens of ideas and narratives attributed to Q have nothing to do with Q, and that much professed distrust of Q is really distrust of movements and influencers claiming affiliation with it.[1] His minimal definition: "Q is an insider who made thousands of posts on [[4chan|4chan]]. That's it."[1] Because the drops are freely available, he held there is no excuse for making claims about the material without reading it, and suggested that a well-funded figure making easily disproven claims about Q may be paid controlled opposition — or may simply have an outsized ego.[1] He treated Q's insider status as mathematically guaranteed while cautioning that insider status alone implies neither truthfulness nor good intentions, adding that anyone denying Q's connection to Trump reveals a lack of elementary understanding.[1] Q's material, he noted, holds that the whole picture is known only to ten people, though he argued this does not prevent accurate assessment of the visible portions.[1]

### Operation Trust comparison

A substantial section rebuts the claim that Q is a rehash of [[operation-trust|Operation Trust]].[1] The author invited critics to present a substantive argument against Q not resting on superficial appearances, stating that none had yet been offered and that Q's critics routinely make factual errors that render their arguments moot — while conceding that many self-described Q supporters likewise fail to do their homework.[1]

## Related recommendations

The author recommended ['Q for Dummies'](https://badlands.substack.com/p/q-for-dummies-chapter-1) by [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] as a grounding in the drops themselves, and noted that the [[joe-biden|Biden]]–Trump debate had created what he considered exceptional opportunities for Digital Soldiers to demonstrate Q's value to others.[1] The piece carries the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributor opinions do not necessarily represent the views of the network, and directs readers to the author's independent Substack.[1]

## Succession by The White Pill Emporium

In Part 1 of [[the-white-pill-emporium|The White Pill Emporium]], published 22 October 2024, the author reported that much of the material for the In Defense of Q series had undergone dramatic change in recent months owing to the mind-boggling pace of the news, and that he tries not to waste readers' time by publishing anything that is not timely and practical.[2] He presented the Emporium as "something new and improved," a successor series intended primarily for anons familiar with [[q-operation|Q]] and [[devolution|Devolution]] but containing something for anyone willing to read and listen in good faith.[2] He noted that several themes from the earlier work would be deliberately reiterated, on the reasoning that repeated exposure to certain ideas helps train the subconscious mind to recognise patterns.[2]
