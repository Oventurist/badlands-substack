# Interim National Defense Strategic Guidance

The **Interim National Defense Strategic Guidance** is an internal [[pentagon|Pentagon]] document setting priorities for the U.S. armed forces, reported in late March 2025 to direct the Department of Defense to focus above all on preparing for a potential conflict with [[china|China]].[1]

## Contents as reported

The nine-page paper reportedly narrowed U.S. defense strategy in the Indo-Pacific region to a single scenario: preventing Beijing from establishing full control over [[taiwan|Taiwan]] by military means.[1] According to *The Washington Post*, which obtained the document, it states that "China is the Department's sole pacing threat, and denial of a Chinese fait accompli seizure of Taiwan — while simultaneously defending the U.S. homeland is the Department's sole pacing scenario."[1]

Beijing was named as the only potential adversary in a "major power war" for which Washington needs to plan contingencies.[1] Other potential threats — including Russia and [[iran|Iran]] — were effectively delegated to America's allies.[1] Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] had previously described China as the main U.S. defense priority.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the memo as evidence that "NeoCons who have infiltrated the Trump administration" would need to be addressed, arguing the sequence had run "first Ukraine, then Israel, now Taiwan."[1] He asserted that there was "nothing of significance on the island of Taiwan that is worth American lives and American treasure," that the manufacturing base exported there decades earlier ought to be repatriated, and that the political conflict between the island and Beijing could not be resolved by overwhelming military force.[1] He added that he did not believe President [[donald-trump|Trump]] planned kinetic involvement, but urged readers to condition themselves to exercise influence over government through public mandate.[1]
