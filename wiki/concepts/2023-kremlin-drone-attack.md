# 2023 Kremlin Drone Attack

The 2023 Kremlin drone attack was an overnight drone incident over the Kremlin in Moscow, reported in early May 2023, which Russian officials characterized as an assassination attempt targeting President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1] The episode became an immediate flashpoint in the information war surrounding the Russia–Ukraine conflict, with each side accusing the other of authorship and Western officials publicly declining to endorse Moscow's account.[1]

## Russian response

Former Russian president and deputy chairman of the security council [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]] responded on social media by declaring that the attack had left Moscow with "no options" but to "eliminate" Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and "his clique" — in effect a call for a decapitation strike against the government in Kiev.[1]

## Ukrainian denial and US reaction

Zelensky vehemently denied that his government was behind the attack.[1] US Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], speaking at a Washington Post event previewing the 2023 World Press Freedom Index, cast doubt on the Russian narrative, saying "I can't in any way validate them," adding "we simply don't know" and that he would "take anything coming out of the Kremlin with a very large shaker of salt."[1]

## Truth-community interpretation

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the incident through the lens of [[devolution]], arguing that the [[state-department|State Department]] functions as an arm of the deep state.[1] The commentary assembled a sequence of data points: Ukraine accused of drone strikes aimed at Putin; Ukraine's denial; months of State Department commentary on supplying Ukraine with missiles; the [[cia|CIA]]'s admitted training of the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]], Russia's sworn enemy; and allegations that the CIA used Ukrainian proxies to sabotage Nord Stream 2.[1] Burning Bright framed the resulting picture as "narrative convergence," linking the possibility of a false flag "on the collective mind" to the CIA being "hung out to dry" and to the timing of JFK files and Epstein-related news.[1]
