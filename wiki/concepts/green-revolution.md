# Green Revolution

The **Green Revolution** refers to the transfer of industrial agricultural technology — high-yield seed varieties, synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, and mechanized irrigation — to the developing world beginning in the 1940s.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

In his essay on Trump's 2025 agenda, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described the Green Revolution as a continuation of the same program he attributes to the industrial revolutions of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries: the removal of rural populations from self-sustaining, church-centered communities and their relocation into cities as low-wage labor.[1] By his account the effort succeeded on its own terms, and the bulk of the low-cost labor pool that later underpinned free-trade [[globalism|globalism]] originated in populations displaced by Green Revolution agricultural restructuring.[1]

Deschamps connected the program to the lending practices described by John Perkins in *Confessions of an Economic Hit Man*, arguing that large International Monetary Fund loans were pushed onto poor countries to accelerate the movement of rural families out of traditional communities and into work for international corporations.[1] The sequence — technological modernization, debt, urban migration — is presented in the article as a repeatable template rather than a series of unrelated development episodes.[1]
