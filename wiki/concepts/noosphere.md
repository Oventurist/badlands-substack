# Noosphere

The Noosphere is the term used by the Jesuit [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] for the third phase of his four-stage cosmology — the emergence of cognition, or homogenesis — preceding the final spiritual convergence of humanity at the [[omega-point|Omega Point]].[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] stresses that Chardin's Noosphere was a very different beast from the Noosphere developed by the Russian academician [[vladimir-vernadsky|Vladimir Vernadsky]] (1863–1945), who was locked into his own parallel fight against the mechanists attempting to crush the soul of science in Russia.[1]

In Chardin's usage the concept dovetailed with the rise of [[cybernetics|cybernetics]] after 1945, and he explicitly framed radio, television and "astonishing electronic computers" as the material scaffolding of a collective consciousness — "a kind of super-Brain, capable of attaining mastery over some super-sphere in the universe."[1]

## Vernadsky's noosphere and climate

In his June 5, 2025 essay on climate science, Ehret developed Vernadsky's version of the concept at length, describing the noosphere as the phenomenon that began expressing itself when humanity appeared, as opposed to the lithosphere and biosphere.[2] Vernadsky understood this new geological force to be driven by human creative reason and taught that the law of humanity must accord with the law of nature, writing that in the noosphere "for the first time, man becomes a large-scale geological force" who "can, and must, rebuild the province of his life by his work and thought."[2]

Ehret emphasizes that in Vernadsky's mind neither the noosphere nor the biosphere obeyed a law of mathematical equilibrium or stasis, but was governed by an asymmetrical harmony and progress from lower to higher states of organization — a framework under which mankind becomes fit to improve upon nature by turning deserts green, harnessing the atom, and applying scientific progress to health and agriculture.[2] He contrasts this open-system, anti-entropic outlook with the closed-system assumptions underlying [[carbon-dioxide|carbon dioxide]] climate modelling and the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] program of [[mass-decarbonization|mass decarbonization]].[2]
