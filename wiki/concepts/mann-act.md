# Mann Act

The Mann Act is a United States federal statute criminalising the transportation of individuals across state lines for purposes of prostitution or other illicit sexual activity.[1]

## Invocation against Hunter Biden (2023)

In July 2023, [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said the criminal referrals he intended to file against [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] would include a Mann Act violation for allegedly transporting a woman across state lines for prostitution.[1] Comer said Rep. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] had presented the supporting evidence in committee -- a sexually explicit image printed from the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]], plane tickets, and records indicating the woman had been paid with company funds -- stating: "She showed the plane tickets, she showed the pictures, she showed the evidence. You know, there's no question he violated it."[1] The Mann Act allegation was listed alongside claimed money laundering, racketeering, wire fraud and [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]] violations.[1]

## Early enforcement and the Bureau of Investigation

Badlands Media commentary describes the Mann Act, otherwise known as the "white slave traffic act," as the statute whose anticipated enforcement gave the newly formed [[fbi|Bureau of Investigation]] its first assignment: surveilling houses of prostitution.[2] The article contrasts a Bureau of 34 employees singularly focused on ending sex slavery with a modern FBI of 35,000 that, in the author's view, is not.[2] It also notes the act's continued use, citing the arrest of a Hollywood producer accused of running a prostitution ring for elites — with accusers claiming they were drugged, held prisoner and forced into prostitution — whom the FBI pursued on Mann Act charges.[2]
