# Eric Swalwell Sexual Misconduct Scandal (2026)

The Eric Swalwell sexual misconduct scandal is the April 2026 cascade of allegations that led Democratic Representative [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] of California to resign from [[congress|Congress]] effective immediately, one day after suspending his campaign for California governor.[1]

## Timeline

On April 10 the San Francisco Chronicle published an initial report in which a former staffer alleged inappropriate sexual encounters and two instances of sexual assault, in 2019 and in New York in 2024; hours later CNN corroborated details from the same woman and added three other women alleging unsolicited explicit photos and texts, unwanted advances, and other misconduct.[1] Swalwell denied all claims.[1] On April 11 campaign staffers began resigning, prominent Democrats called for him to leave the governor's race, and the [[manhattan-district-attorneys-office|Manhattan District Attorney's office]] opened a criminal investigation into the 2024 New York allegation.[1] On April 12 he suspended his gubernatorial campaign, acknowledging "mistakes in judgment" while strongly denying non-consensual acts.[1] On April 13 the [[house-ethics-committee|House Ethics Committee]] opened a formal investigation into sexual misconduct including toward a supervised employee, and Swalwell announced his resignation.[1] On April 14 a fifth woman, former model [[lonna-drewes|Lonna Drewes]], held a press conference alleging he drugged, raped, and choked her unconscious in a 2018 hotel room.[1]

## The Drewes allegation

Drewes filed a fresh rape claim with the [[los-angeles-county-sheriffs-department|Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department]], alleging that in July 2018 Swalwell spiked her wine at the Montrose Hotel in West Hollywood after meeting her three times that year while offering political connections for her business.[1] Corroborating details reported include contemporaneous journal entries, preserved texts and photos, accounts shared with friends at the time, and a formal complaint filed the day the story broke; FEC records show "Swalwell for Congress" spent $361 at that hotel on July 18, 2018, and the Sheriff's Department confirmed the location.[1]

## Scope and related material

The claims bring the total public accusations to at least five, with some reporting saying six, including rape by a former staffer while she was too intoxicated to consent.[1] Additional scrutiny involves a hotel-room video circulated on X and verified as authentic by forensic analysis held by Representative [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]], showing Swalwell kissing an alleged sex worker; Luna stated the person who recorded it was reportedly an underage female and that more damaging material would be released within 24 hours.[1] Luna also publicly accused Hill insiders and reporters of having known about the stories for years without reporting them.[1] Swalwell maintains the accusations are false, and they remain unproven in court.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "it was an open secret, they say," and judged the rollout comprehensive and well done — planned and timed to reignite outrage with a new victim statement, video clip, or witness precisely as the last one began to fizzle.[1] One leading belief she reported is that the Democrats no longer needed Swalwell, so they distanced themselves and left him outside the regime's protection, allowing everyone to claim they did not know and act surprised.[1] She also noted that Representative [[tony-gonzales|Tony Gonzales]] resigned the same Tuesday, and that Senator [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]] faced calls to resign after failing to convince the press he was not the other man in a video circulating alongside the Swalwell material.[1]
