# 18 U.S.C. § 2383

**18 U.S.C. § 2383**, the federal "rebellion or insurrection" statute, is the provision of the U.S. Criminal Code that covers anybody who incites or engages in any insurrection or rebellion against the authority of the United States.[1] It occupies a central place in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] criticism of the [[colorado-ballot-disqualification-case|Colorado ballot disqualification case]] because it is the charge that was available to prosecutors and was never brought.[1]

## Role in the Trump disqualification debate

Writing on January 1, 2024, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that if [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had actually been convicted under § 2383 and found to have engaged in or incited insurrection, one could at least make a more plausible argument that he was ineligible to run under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].[1] But Trump was never charged with inciting an insurrection, notwithstanding indictments in four separate jurisdictions brought by DOJ special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], a Manhattan prosecutor, and the Democratic prosecutor in Atlanta.[1]

DeLarme noted that the federal January 6 indictment reported by the Associated Press charged conspiring to defraud the United States, conspiring to obstruct an official proceeding, obstructing an official proceeding, and violating a post-Civil War Reconstruction-era civil rights statute criminalizing conspiracy to violate constitutionally guaranteed rights — in that case the right to vote — but contained no insurrection count.[1]

Because no § 2383 charge was filed, he argued, Trump was never afforded the constitutional safeguards that attach to a criminal defendant: a jury of his peers, the right of cross-examination, [[due-process|due process]], and the right to counsel.[1] Four state judges nonetheless ruled him guilty of conduct constituting a crime for which he was never tried, a result DeLarme presented as the case's most egregious defect and as a precedent enabling the government to declare anyone culpable of anything without trial.[1]
