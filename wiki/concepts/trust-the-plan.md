# Trust the plan

"Trust the plan" is a slogan of the Anon community which Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] defends and redefines in his April 2023 essay on predictive supercomputing.[1]

## Carlson's definition

Carlson insists the phrase "doesn't mean inaction"; it simply means there is a good game plan in place — one that still needs to be implemented to the end and tweaked along the way.[1] He describes his own posture as choosing to trust the plan while still preparing and taking action where he can.[1]

## Simulation as the basis of trust

The essay grounds the slogan in the claim that the [[white-hats|white hats]] have access to the same predictive supercomputers as the [[black-hats|black hats]] and have used them to choose timing.[1] On this account they predicted that allowing the 2020 election to be stolen produced a better long-term outcome than a [[donald-trump|Trump]] victory, that dropping the full truth at once would be more damaging than dripping it out, and that mass arrests in 2017 would have gone badly — and they can probably predict the exact moment the truth should be revealed and arrests made.[1] Carlson adds that the white hats are likely suppressing some Anons who get too close to the target, because the enemy must not learn the playbook.[1]

## Answering the suffering objection

Carlson addresses the common challenge that people continue to be hurt if the Patriots are in control. His answer is a sports analogy: a team running out the clock still concedes yards and points.[1] The [[deep-state|Deep State]], he writes, has lost the chess game and cannot avoid losing, but it retains a few moves — comparing the situation to Hitler, who knew the war was over long before it ended yet prolonged it to hurt as many people as possible on the way down.[1] Asked how a school shooting could occur under Patriot control, he replies simply that they do not control every aspect of this war or this world.[1]

He closes with a religious framing, citing Jesus's observation that there will always be hungry and poor as a description of a sinful and fallen world rather than an excuse, and comparing the country's coming freedom to Jesus walking out of the tomb after three days.[1]

## "There IS a plan"

[[pepe-lives-matter|Pepe Lives Matter]]'s December 7, 2022 essay "Thrive in the Transition" restated the slogan as a matter of faith rather than of inside knowledge, asserting flatly that "there IS a plan" while conceding that the intervening period feels like a fog in which doubt has overcome many.[2] On that reading the blatancy of visible corruption is not evidence that the plan has failed but a necessary unveiling — a prerequisite to the plan being truly implemented.[2]

The essay used [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 announcement speech, which it characterised as saying plainly that the precipice had not yet been reached and that unity and breakthrough would follow a broader awakening, to explain the apparent delay: not enough people were yet awake.[2] It reconciled Trump's simultaneous "best is yet to come" and imminent-World-War-III messaging by arguing that if the best were not coming the fight would be futile, and that the catastrophist messaging is therefore aimed at unconvinced spectators rather than at the [[anons|anons]].[2] Its closing move relocated the plan inward, suggesting that God expanding and preparing the individual during the barren season "maybe... is the plan."[2] See [[thriving-in-the-transition|Thriving in the Transition]].[2]

## "TRUST [was] the plan"

A July 11, 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]], itself titled "TRUST: The Plan," proposed a deliberately inverted reading of the slogan.[3] Noting that Q repeats "TRUST the plan" in 42 drops, the author argued the phrase also communicates "TRUST [was] the plan" — that dishonest actors slowly domesticated and enslaved the population through what the essay calls the incremental, widespread, intentional, endless exploitation of TRUST at all levels, and that the word [intentional] in that description should not be missed.[3]

The essay built the case by unpacking the dictionary senses of the word trust: firm belief or faith; acceptance of truth without evidence or investigation ("TRUST, don't verify"); responsibility for a person or duty; property held by a trustee for a beneficiary; a monopoly, as in the antitrust laws enforced by the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]]; credit; and hope or expectation.[3] Because trust is simultaneously an asset, a contract, and a belief system, the author reasoned, its deception sits at the "tippy top" of any dishonest actor's priority list, citing Sun Tzu's maxim that all warfare is based on deception.[3] The Soviet counterintelligence effort [[operation-trust|Operation Trust]] and the collapse of the [[knickerbocker-trust-company|Knickerbocker Trust Company]] in the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]] were offered as historical illustrations.[3]

Surveying the 182 of 4,966 drops in which Q mentions trust, the essay summarised Q as instructing anons to trust God, the mission and plan, POTUS, Adm. Rogers and Dan, and named officials such as Sessions, Wray, Kansas, Horowitz, Huber and Grassley — while warning them not to trust the media or what they read.[3] It highlighted drop #3931 on legitimacy and deceit, in which Q describes evil flourishing because it was protected, safeguarded and camouflaged to appear as trusted, and drop #1328, in which Q+ draws focus to the definition of traitor as "a person who betrays any TRUST."[3] The essay's proposed remedy was not merely electoral: quoting Q's line that "this is not another [4] year election" and 1 Corinthians 16:13, it argued the fix is to eliminate misplaced and exploited trust altogether by moving from trust-based systems to trust-less ones — [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and the [[proof-of-work|proof-of-work]] power projection thesis of [[softwar|Softwar]].[3] It also contrasted Reagan's "[[trust-but-verify|trust, but verify]]" with the cybersecurity posture of "never trust, always verify."[3] See [[trust-law|trust law]] and the [[cantillon-effect|Cantillon effect]].[3]

## "We are the plan"

A further inversion of the slogan appeared in [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]]'s December 9, 2022 Badlands Media essay "We Are 'The Plan'," written in response to what she described as an undercurrent of weary, frustrated and impatient comments on Telegram, Truth Social and Substack.[4] Gillespie conceded that "trusting the plan" is maddening but argued that for her own sanity she must believe a plan exists and is being executed by people in the know to root out corruption on innumerable levels, adding that it cannot happen all at once because, as Q says, "the truth would put 99% of the people in the hospital."[4] Because millions have not yet opened their eyes, she concluded, the responsibility for moving disclosure forward rests with the audience itself: "WE ARE THE PLAN."[4]

The essay contrasted this posture with that of [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]], quoting a Telegram post by Politick_Rick observing that Bannon "never says 'trust the plan'" but instead tells listeners to take back U.S. politics at the local level, and criticising the wider pundit class — including [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] — for describing problems without offering solutions, which the post argued breeds inaction and serves globalist interests.[4] Gillespie also invoked [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s "An Introduction to Devolution," which frames [[devolution|devolution]] as a two-part operation in which the military and Trump have already secured election-theft evidence and are waiting on the second part — We the People — and asserts that "Trump implementing devolution means nothing without We the People."[4]

## Invoked in the 2024 cabinet debate

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] closed his December 3, 2024 essay on the [[2024-trump-cabinet-selection-controversy|controversy over Trump's cabinet picks]] with a direct appeal to the slogan, writing "Trust the plan" and arguing that if supporters fully understood the plan, so would the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[5] He framed patience as the only available option — either [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s plan works or "we're doomed" — while explicitly denying that this amounted to handing Trump a blank check or abandoning accountability.[5] He compared premature disclosure to telling an NFL defense which play you intend to run next.[5]
