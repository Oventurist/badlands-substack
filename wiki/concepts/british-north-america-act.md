# British North America Act

The British North America Act was enacted on 1 July 1867, consolidating Britain's North American colonies into a new federation under a constitution that dedicated the federation's existence to being conducive "to the Welfare of the Provinces and promote the interests of the British Empire."[1]

Matthew Ehret characterizes the Act as Britain's "other" anti-American confederacy operation — the one that succeeded, in contrast to the Southern Confederacy that failed.[1] The project began during the American [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] at the 1864 [[charlottetown-conference|Charlottetown convention]], described in the article as a week-long "booze-soaked orgy," which hammered out the resolutions later put into law in 1867.[1]

Ehret notes that days before the enactment, would-be Confederate President [[jefferson-davis|Jefferson Davis]] addressed cheering crowds in Lennoxville, Quebec, urging them to "hold fast to [your] British principles" and cultivate close and affectionate connections with the mother country.[1] Fathers of Confederation such as [[georges-etienne-cartier|Sir Georges-Etienne Cartier]], [[john-a-macdonald|Sir John A. Macdonald]] and [[alexander-galt|Sir Alexander Galt]] articulated the monarchical and anti-annexationist purposes of the new state.[1] In Ehret's reading, the resulting federation functioned as a British-controlled northern blockade against the sovereign nation-state intention of the United States and against the emerging rail link between America and Russia.[1]
