# Self-Suggestion

**Self-suggestion** is the idea, advanced by the Badlands Media contributor [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], that the things a person repeatedly says to himself function as hypnotic commands, so that everyone is in effect his own hypnotist whether or not he realises it.[1] In "Mental Fortitude for 5G Warriors" (December 13, 2023) the author states that hypnosis is a constant factor in cognition, mood and outlook, and that the only difference between a professional hypnotist and an ordinary person is awareness of the process.[1] Subliminal influence, he writes, is so pervasive that noticing it is akin to a fish noticing the water in which it swims.[1]

## Neurological framing

The author frames the practical objective as shifting control of behaviour away from the amygdala and toward the neocortex and pre-frontal lobes — less reactivity, more intentionality.[1] He argues that much of a person's unconscious attitudes and beliefs were formed beneath the threshold of conscious awareness, before the pre-frontal lobes matured, which is why he treats the ability to "talk yourself down" during an amygdala response as a learnable skill rather than a disposition.[1]

## Prescribed exercises

The core exercise is to spend five to ten minutes each day, aloud or silently, speaking to oneself the way someone who unconditionally loves you and wants the best for you would speak — with the same care shown to a frustrated six- or seven-year-old — and to refrain entirely from self-insult during that window.[1] For men the author adds a variant: imagine being one's own coach, one who believes in you completely.[1] He compares the practice to watering a plant, arguing that a few minutes a day will yield results and that humans wither without love as plants wither without water, and instructs readers who find the practice awkward or silly to do it anyway.[1]

For those who cannot tolerate saying kind things about themselves, he offers a graded alternative, likening it to not beginning gym training with the heaviest weights: spend five to ten minutes a day for a week or two writing in detail about the qualities admired in another person, then ask what it is about oneself that makes perceiving those qualities possible.[1] The implied conclusion is that one could not recognise admirable traits without reflecting something admirable oneself, and that a decent person who means well therefore has every right to compliment himself regularly.[1]

## Role in the 5G war

Self-suggestion is presented as the first of three pillars of preparation for the [[5g-war|5G war]], preceding intellectual rigor and social effectiveness, on the reasoning that emotional health makes high-level analysis of events easier.[1] The author addresses the material specifically to [[digital-soldiers|Digital Soldiers]], describing it as a distillation of the most powerful self-transformation ideas he had found, adjusted for their use.[1]
