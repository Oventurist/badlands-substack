# ReArm Europe

ReArm Europe was the rearmament program announced in March 2025 by [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]], President of the [[european-commission|European Commission]], as the [[european-union|European Union]]'s answer to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threats to curtail American support for [[nato|NATO]] and to pause funding for Ukraine.[1] The package included roughly €150 billion in EU loans for rearmament and was framed by von der Leyen as the opening of an "era of rearmament."[1]

## Rebranding and resistance

Two of the bloc's largest members, Italy and Spain, judged the branding too aggressive, and the initiative was renamed "Readiness 2030."[1] The renaming accompanied wider difficulty in assembling money and political will: the Dutch and others opposed raising collective debt for defense, and Hungary proved difficult to keep on board.[1]

Parallel efforts by [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]], the EU's foreign and security policy chief, also stalled. Her proposal to raise up to €40 billion (more than $43 billion) for Ukraine via a small fixed levy on each member state's national income went nowhere, and a fallback plan for an additional €5 billion toward supplying Ukraine two million artillery shells during 2025 was rejected by Italy, Slovakia, and France.[1] Member states insisted that contributions to Ukraine remain voluntary and bilateral rather than mandated from Brussels.[1]

## Assessment

Badlands contributor Ryan DeLarme read the program's trajectory as evidence that European rearmament rhetoric outstrips European capacity, arguing that the campaign "appears poised to fall flat" and that the bloc is far less cohesive than in prior decades amid a continent-wide contest between populism and globalism.[1]
