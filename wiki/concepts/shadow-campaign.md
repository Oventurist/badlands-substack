# Shadow Campaign

"Shadow campaign" is the label applied by Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and numerous analysts to California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s national political activity during 2023 — an unannounced bid for the Democratic presidential nomination conducted without ever declaring candidacy.[1]

## Mechanics

The campaign's visible components were advertising and money moving outside California's borders.[1] Newsom repeatedly ran ads in other states, including Florida and Texas, and contributed millions of dollars to a Super PAC broadcasting advertisements beyond California that portrayed him as the last resort capable of challenging President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] observed that how Newsom, in his capacity as a state governor, could assume such a role outside his own state remained uncertain.[1]

## Contingency on Biden

The audacity of the bid — which would normally draw the ire of party loyalists — rested on the possibility that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would eventually fall ill or withdraw from the campaign, leaving an opening at the top of the ticket.[1]

## Denials

Newsom consistently refuted the allegation of a covert campaign throughout his [[great-red-vs-blue-state-debate|debate]] with DeSantis, claiming he was present to defend the nation against the far right and telling DeSantis, "You and President Trump are trying to light democracy on fire."[1] DeLarme read the denials as exposing a presidential aspiration Newsom had voiced openly since his second year as a San Francisco supervisor, when he told reporters in 1998: "If you're in politics and you want to make an impact, you should be as successful as possible, and the most influential position is President."[1]
