# Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021

The Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021 is Scottish legislation that came into force on April 1, 2024, creating a new criminal offence of "stirring up hatred" in relation to age, disability, religion, sexual orientation, transgender identity or being intersex.[1]

## Provisions

Under the Act a person may be imprisoned for up to seven years for engaging in "insulting" behaviour toward a protected group.[1] Critically, the prosecution is required to prove only that the stirring up of hatred was "likely," rather than that it was intended.[1]

## Enforcement and controversy

Senior police officers expected a deluge of complaints over online posts once the law commenced.[1] During the training program on enforcing the new law, officers were taught that even the content of plays and comedy gigs should be considered as potential hate crimes.[1]

[[siobhian-brown|Siobhian Brown]], the [[scottish-national-party|Scottish National Party]]'s community safety minister, initially stated that calling a "trans woman" "he" would not be a crime; after the law came into force she revised that position, saying, "It could be reported and it could be investigated. Whether or not the police would think it was criminal is up to [[police-scotland|Police Scotland]] for that."[1] Author [[jk-rowling|J.K. Rowling]] was identified as a figure who could be prosecuted under the Act for "misgendering" transgender people, and vowed to continue calling biological males men.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] linked the Act to [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]'s Online Harms Bill in [[canada|Canada]], arguing that the synchronized rollout revealed "an apolitical, globalist enemy attacking the very concept of the sovereign nation state."[1] Esler contended that such laws are dangerous chiefly in precedent, being vague and largely unenforceable — pointing to Ontario provisions in force since around 2017 that classify misgendering as a hate crime yet produced no wave of arrests.[1] In his analysis, "woke philosophy is inherently cannibalistic," its illogical nature causing it to consume itself, which is one reason the resulting laws are hard to enforce.[1] He suggested the creation of "maddening, unenforceable laws" is intended more for psychological impact than as functional lawfare, since the enforcing side lacks "the ready, willing and able army of foot soldiers" needed to confront what he called the anti-woke global majority, forcing reliance on cognitive warfare.[1] His conclusion was that belief in the false authority of officials, and a lack of citizens prepared to stand up for themselves, are what make such laws dangerous — "not the laws themselves."[1]
