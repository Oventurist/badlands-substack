# Geostationary Satellite Data Leak Research

In October 2025, researchers at the University of California San Diego (UCSD) and the University of Maryland (UMD) disclosed the results of a three-year study in which they intercepted unencrypted geostationary satellite signals using a satellite dish costing about $185, capturing large volumes of sensitive personal, corporate, military, and infrastructure data transmitted in the clear.[1]

## Findings

The researchers accessed T-Mobile users' calls, texts, and phone numbers, exposing more than 2,700 users in a nine-hour window.[1] UCSD professor Aaron Schulman said of the results, "It just completely shocked us."[1]

In-flight Wi-Fi data carried over Intelsat and Panasonic systems on ten airlines revealed unencrypted browsing metadata and audio broadcasts.[1] The team also captured corporate emails from [[walmart|Walmart's]] Mexican subsidiary and ATM communications from Santander Mexico, Banjercito, and Banorte, all transmitted without encryption.[1]

Critical infrastructure was likewise exposed, including traffic from Mexico's Comisión Federal de Electricidad, which revealed work orders containing customer names and addresses along with equipment failure details.[1] U.S. and Mexican military communications disclosed personnel, equipment, and facility locations, including Mexican military asset tracking for Mil Mi-17 and UH-60 Black Hawk helicopters.[1]
