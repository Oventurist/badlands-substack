# Trump v. Woodward Lawsuit

The Trump v. Woodward lawsuit was a civil action filed at the end of January 2023 by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against journalist [[bob-woodward|Bob Woodward]], publisher [[simon-and-schuster|Simon & Schuster]] and its parent company [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] over the commercial release of audio recordings of Woodward's interviews with Trump.[1]

## Claims and damages

Trump's complaint asserted that he had never agreed to allow the audio of Woodward's interview tapes to be sold to the public, distinguishing consent to be interviewed for a book from consent to have the raw recordings marketed as a product.[1] The suit sought at least $49,980,000, a figure derived from an estimate that the audiobook "The Trump Tapes" had sold more than two million copies at $24.99 per copy.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that Woodward and his publishing partners had "changed [Trump's] words and the context" of them, and that the recordings were "doctored and sold" after being gathered for the ostensible purpose of writing a book.[1] Paul questioned Woodward's reputation as a preeminent journalist, suggesting that the standing was conferred chiefly by peers engaged in efforts against elected presidents.[1]

In Paul's assessment, the most consequential element of the filing was not what the released audio contained but what it omitted: a conversation he described as a total refutation of the basis for the Ukraine impeachment case against Trump.[1] Paul expanded on this argument in a public thread on Twitter accompanying the brief.[1]

The case was presented in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as part of a wider pattern in which legacy media figures faced legal and reputational exposure for their handling of the Trump presidency, alongside the [[columbia-journalism-review|Columbia Journalism Review]]'s critique of Russiagate reporting.[1]
