# Disinformation Expert

"Disinformation expert" is the job title given to a class of researchers, officials and consultants who claim the competence to adjudicate what online speech is true, false or permissible. Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes it as a "freshly invented expertise" with no industry standard, no academic discipline and no structured pathway by which one ascends to the designation.[1] The article's central objection is that while societies have always recognised experts and authorities on particular subjects, never before has anyone been deemed an expert on all subjects.[1]

## Origins and institutional ties

The profession is presented as an outgrowth of the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]], a network of organisations that emerged over the previous decade to implement an institutionalised form of online censorship in concert with the federal government, suppressing dissent deemed dangerous to the objectives of the U.S. security state.[1] Its practitioners, the article argues, frequently emerge from the U.S. government itself and typically from [[department-of-homeland-security|Homeland Security]].[1] The short-lived [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]] under [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]] and the CISA-affiliated academic [[kate-starbird|Kate Starbird]] are offered as the two paradigm cases.[1]

## The free-speech argument

The article grounds its critique in an epistemological defence of open debate: free speech allows citizens to hear multiple interpretations and decide for themselves, and being wrong is described as a blessing because error sharpens one's epistemological faculties.[1] Disinformation experts, by contrast, are said to seek the state-given capacity to enforce their decrees about veracity so that disfavoured viewpoints can be surgically eliminated from public discourse.[1] The article concedes that a genuine disinformation problem exists in both mainstream and independent media, with demonstrably false claims routinely peddled as fact, but maintains that the danger of censorship will always outweigh the danger of free speech, and that bad information should be broken down and debated in public.[1]

## Public support for censorship

An August 2021 [[pew-research-center|Pew Research]] survey is cited as evidence that a sizable share of Americans actively favour systemic censorship.[1] Asked whether the government should control and limit the flow of information online in the name of protecting the public from disinformation, even at the cost of limiting free speech, 65 percent of Democrats and 28 percent of Republicans said yes.[1] Asked whether social media platforms should censor political views or expression to prevent disinformation, 76 percent of Democrats and 37 percent of Republicans agreed.[1] The article treats these figures as raising the question of who could possibly be competent to identify disinformation not merely to dissuade belief but to prevent expression.[1]

## International legal frameworks

Although the "Scary Poppins" public-relations disaster shut down the Disinformation Governance Board, the article argues that such efforts have only temporarily receded, with Western governments still working with organisations funded by neoliberal billionaires toward an official ministry-of-truth body.[1] New legal frameworks facilitating censorship had already been adopted in Canada via the [[online-streaming-act|Online Streaming Act]], in the United Kingdom via the [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]], and across the European Union via the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[1] The article closes by hoping the job title can soon be consigned to the trash heap of history.[1]
