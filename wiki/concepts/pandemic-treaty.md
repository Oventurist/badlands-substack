# Pandemic Treaty

The "Pandemic Treaty" is the informal name given to a proposed international accord negotiated under the auspices of the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] that would establish binding commitments among member states for the prevention of, preparedness for, and response to future pandemics. In commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], the instrument was characterized as a mechanism that "would put the World Health Organization in the role of a temporary world governing body" during a declared pandemic emergency.[1]

## Reception and opposition

According to Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], the treaty proposal "received serious blowback from the more discerning portion of the human population," generating sustained public resistance in multiple countries from critics who viewed it as a transfer of sovereign public-health authority to an unelected international body.[1]

## Relationship to the International Health Regulations

Badlands commentary argued that while public attention was concentrated on the treaty itself, a parallel and less-scrutinized track was advancing: a series of proposed amendments to the [[international-health-regulations|International Health Regulations]] (IHR) that would place the World Health Organization "in a position to supersede the governments of the world in the event of another pandemic." DeLarme wrote that these proposed amendments were also "starting to be exposed for what they are."[1]

## Place in a broader pattern

The treaty was presented by Badlands as the first of at least three fronts on which what DeLarme called "the predatory class" was pursuing emergency governing authority, the others being the IHR amendments and the [[united-nations|United Nations]] "emergency platform" proposal contained in the [[pact-for-the-future|Pact for the Future]].[1] In that framing, each initiative represented a potential "stepping stone toward establishing a permanent world-governing body."[1]
