# 2023 Russian-Chinese Naval Patrol off Alaska

The **2023 Russian-Chinese naval patrol off Alaska** was a joint maritime deployment by [[russia|Russian]] and [[china|Chinese]] naval forces that operated off the Alaskan coast in the first week of August 2023, prompting a United States military monitoring response.[1] Defense officials and lawmakers described it over that weekend as a show of force that nevertheless did not pose a threat to the United States or Canada, according to a [[united-states-northern-command|US Northern Command]] spokesperson who spoke to [[cnn|CNN]].[1]

## Military response

NORTHCOM and [[norad|the North American Aerospace Defense Command]] deployed planes and ships to monitor the patrol, which remained in international waters throughout.[1] Alaska's Republican Senators [[dan-sullivan|Dan Sullivan]] and [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] said in a Saturday statement that a total of eleven Russian and Chinese vessels had been operating near the [[aleutian-islands|Aleutian Islands]] and had been met in response by four [[us-navy|US Navy]] destroyers.[1] Murkowski said the senators had been in close contact with Alaska Command leadership for several days and had received detailed classified briefings on the transiting vessels.[1]

Sullivan noted that Chinese and Russian vessels had approached Alaska in similar fashion the previous summer and had been encountered by a [[us-coast-guard|US Coast Guard]] ship on a routine patrol — a response the senator called "tepid" and which prompted him to press senior military leaders to prepare a more robust posture for future incidents.[1]

## Chinese explanation

Chinese Embassy spokesperson Liu Pengyu told CNN that under the annual cooperation plan between the Chinese and Russian militaries, naval vessels of the two countries had recently conducted joint maritime patrols in the western and northern Pacific Ocean, adding that the action "is not targeted at any third party and has nothing to do with the current international and regional situation."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the patrol was at least the third of its kind in about eighteen months and that the accompanying messaging — that it was routine and unrelated to geopolitical tensions — matched the language used when a comparable episode occurred in September 2022.[1] She argued that the most striking element was the source of the American response: it came from the two Alaskan senators rather than from the executive branch, the commander in chief, or the national security establishment.[1]

CNN also quoted a research fellow based in Australia, but more than twelve hours after the incident was reported there was still no official [[white-house|White House]] statement — a silence Ashe compared to the executive's reticence during the Chinese surveillance balloon incident earlier that year.[1] She concluded that silence appeared to be the administration's policy toward Chinese encroachments near the US mainland, and noted the absence of the customary invocations of Russian threat as a missed rhetorical opportunity.[1]
