# Auktionshaus Felzmann Concentration Camp Artefact Auction

The Auktionshaus Felzmann concentration camp artefact auction was a planned November 2025 sale in Neuss, Germany, of artefacts belonging to prisoners of Nazi concentration camps, cancelled after a public outcry.[1]

## The planned sale

The German auction house Felzmann had reportedly scheduled the sale for Monday, November 17, 2025.[1] Among more than 600 items offered were a letter from an Auschwitz prisoner and a medical diagnosis concerning the forced sterilisation of a prisoner from the Dachau concentration camp, according to German media reports.[1] A Holocaust survivors' group and politicians called for the sale to be cancelled, and the auction listing on the Auktionshaus Felzmann website had been removed by mid-afternoon on Sunday.[1]

## Cancellation and reaction

Poland's Deputy Prime Minister [[radoslaw-sikorski|Radosław Sikorski]] announced the cancellation, thanking his German counterpart Johann Wadephul for agreeing that "such a scandal must be prevented."[1] Sikorski wrote on X that "respect for victims requires the dignity of silence, not the din of commerce."[1] German State Minister for Culture Wolfram Weimer told the German news agency DPA that "documents or expert reports by Nazi perpetrators that were offered at the auction are not for private collections."[1]

The story ran as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], where [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited it as part of a cluster of Nazi-themed news items that weekend — alongside the [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] DNA documentary — which she read as narrative groundwork.[1]
