# Gaza Humanitarian Pier

The Gaza humanitarian pier was a $320 million floating causeway built and erected off the coast of Gaza by the United States military under the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration to deliver humanitarian aid by sea into the Gaza Strip during the war between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]].[1]

## Structural failure

On Saturday, May 25, 2024, a section of the pier broke off and floated onto an Israeli beach, the latest in a series of setbacks for the project.[1] Two days earlier, three U.S. troops had been reported injured aboard the pier, one of them critically.[1] Times of Israel military correspondent [[emanuel-fabian|Emanuel Fabian]] reported that an American vessel used to unload humanitarian aid from ships into Gaza via the pier had disconnected from a small tug boat during stormy seas and become stuck on the coast at Ashdod, and that a second ship sent to extract it also became beached.[1] A further U.S. Army vessel ran aground in shallow water during the rescue attempt.[1] The vessels had been moving two pieces of the floating pier overnight to the Port of Ashdod in southern Israel when the section detached and drifted away, and footage showed American troops standing helplessly on the beach.[1]

[[us-central-command|U.S. Central Command]] confirmed the incident in a press release, stating that four U.S. Army vessels supporting the maritime humanitarian aid mission in Gaza had been affected by heavy sea states, that the vessels broke free from their moorings, that two were anchored on the beach near the pier, and that the third and fourth were beached on the coast of Israel near Ashkelon, with recovery efforts under way with assistance from the Israeli Navy.[1]

## Earlier disruption

The pier operation had already been paused for two days the previous week after desperate Palestinians mobbed and ransacked the first trucks carrying aid unloaded from the pier before they could reach a distribution warehouse managed by the World Food Programme.[1]
