# Grok 4

Grok 4 is the artificial intelligence chatbot released by [[xai|xAI]] and demonstrated by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] in July 2025.[1]

## Launch claims

During the demonstration Musk argued that Grok 4 was the smartest AI in the world, better than almost all graduate students across all disciplines simultaneously.[1] At the same event he said the notion that humans once managed an economy would seem quaint in the future, comparable in retrospect to "cavemen throwing sticks into a fire."[1]

The remarks were framed by Fortune as the latest instance of a Silicon Valley figure declining to affirm the value of human continuity: two weeks earlier [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] had struggled to answer whether he would prefer the human race to endure, and it was now Musk's turn to opine on whether technology might remove the need for mankind's existence.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded dryly that [[artificial-intelligence|AI]] probably won't destroy humanity, "but if it does, at least we'll get to witness it."[1]

## Launch and reception

[[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s artificial intelligence startup [[xai|xAI]] rolled out Grok 4 in July 2025, only months after the previous iteration, underscoring the frenetic pace of AI development.[2] Flanked by members of the xAI team and clad in a leather jacket, Musk demoed the bot in a video livestream late on Wednesday night, July 9, 2025.[2] Available immediately, Grok 4 was described by Musk as "smarter than almost all graduate students, in all disciplines, simultaneously," with improved voice conversations and company-touted benchmarks scoring higher than OpenAI and others.[2] Musk conceded that "at times it may lack common sense, and it has not yet invented new technologies or discovered new physics, but that is just a matter of time."[2]

The release came a day after xAI was forced to remove inappropriate Grok posts from [[x-corp|X]] that included antisemitic comments and replies to users, an episode known as the [[grok-mechahitler-incident|MechaHitler incident]]; the company said it had taken action to ban hate speech before Grok posts on X.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary tied the controversy to the same-week resignation of X chief executive [[linda-yaccarino|Linda Yaccarino]], while contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] connected the broader antisemitism-speech fight to [[glenn-youngkin|Glenn Youngkin]]'s signing of what he described as the nation's first antisemitism hate speech law conflating the definition with anti-Zionism.[2]
