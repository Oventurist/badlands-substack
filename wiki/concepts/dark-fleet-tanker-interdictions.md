# Dark Fleet Tanker Interdictions

The "dark fleet" tanker interdictions are the U.S. maritime seizure campaign launched in the Caribbean following the January 3, 2026 special forces raid that captured Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1]

## Seizures and warrants

The United States seized multiple oil tankers transporting Venezuelan oil and pursued court warrants for dozens more vessels suspected of carrying unauthorized Venezuelan, Iranian, or Russian oil.[1] [[pentagon|Pentagon]] officials vowed to interdict all such "dark fleet" ships.[1] During the Maduro raid itself, most Cuban members of his security team were reportedly killed.[1]

## Cuba pressure

[[donald-trump|President Trump]] suggested that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] could lead a post-regime [[cuba|Cuba]], urged Havana to "make a deal" amid accusations of its reliance on Venezuelan oil, and reinstated Cuba on the state sponsors of terrorism list.[1]

## Privateering framing

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the campaign as Trump "cosplaying as Pirate King of the Caribbean" — seizing vessels, plundering their cargo, and promising more captures — an inversion of the British Empire's Age of Sail practice of issuing letters of marque to naval captains who raided Spanish treasure ships returning from Mexico and Panama.[1] Those captains, known as privateers, held a license to loot without being hanged; many drifted into outright piracy, and most notorious European pirates began in the navy.[1] Captain Henry Morgan, the commentary noted, accumulated enough political clout to be appointed Governor of Jamaica by the British Crown, using the office to launch formal military operations against the populations of modern-day Colombia and [[venezuela|Venezuela]] — in one famous battle, indigenous defenders corralled over 2,000 bulls into the jungle and triggered a stampede that trampled thousands of invading pirates to death.[1] The argument is that Trump is reviving this system of direct power projection for political advantage, establishing precedents amid an overwhelming sense of chaos and the abandonment of international law.[1]
