# Russiagate Hoax

The "Russiagate Hoax" is the term used across Badlands Media coverage for the theory that the investigation into alleged Russian collusion with the 2016 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] campaign was not a legitimate counterintelligence matter but a manufactured political operation. In the weekly recap presented in [[the-blitz|The Blitz]] Episode 14, the continued "unraveling" of Russiagate was identified as one of the two dominant stories of the week.[1]

## Coverage in Blitz Episode 14

Hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the Russiagate unraveling as a direct extension of the indictment of the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]], describing the two developments as dovetailing into a single accountability narrative.[1] The episode's framing — subtitled "The Dawning Age of American Accountability" — presented the disintegration of the Russiagate storyline as a "white pill," a term used in Badlands commentary for genuinely encouraging news.[1]

## Narrative role

Within Badlands coverage, Russiagate serves as the archetypal case of institutional narrative failure: a story sustained by intelligence-community sourcing and legacy media amplification, whose collapse validates the independent-media analysis that ran counter to it.[1] Its recurring appearance in Blitz recaps reflects the show's role as a dissection of the week's Badlands Brief reporting rather than a source of standalone investigation.[1]

## April 2026: records fight and prosecutions

In April 2026 Just the News reported that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] had requested transcripts and records from two Senate committees involving former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], with a February 23 deadline that the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] had not met, delaying part of an investigation based in Fort Pierce, Florida.[2] Aides to Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] said he was seeking unanimous consent to release the records, and prosecutors had been allowed to review one Senate Intelligence Committee report in a secure room; former U.S. Attorney [[joseph-digenova|Joseph diGenova]] was set to begin as a lead prosecutor on Russia-collusion matters.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied the developments to the Grand RICO case, arguing that accountability and Truth and Reconciliation are required to end institutional deception, and cited Rasmussen polling showing 64% of voters believe the [[fbi|FBI]] was weaponized and 82% consider government weaponization a threat to the Republic.[2] [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] bet Roger Stone a steak dinner that indictments would come by June 20.[2]

## Trump's Newsweek op-ed, August 2023

Writing in Newsweek in early August 2023, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] argued that "the Russia Hoax was the most atrocious weaponization of our government in American history," calling it "a crime like no other" and saying the [[durham-report|Durham report]] made this clear.[3] He wrote that the hoax "stoked mass hysteria" that drove Russia "straight into the arms of China" and produced "a proxy war with Russia" that could lead to a third world war, concluding: "Either the Deep State destroys America, or we destroy the Deep State."[3]

Trump framed his 2016 candidacy as an existential threat to a Washington establishment that "got rich and powerful bleeding America dry," citing his vows to stop mass illegal immigration, terminate globalist trade deals, end the sellout of the country to Communist China, stand up to the permanent bureaucracy and the corporate media, and break the neocon addiction to endless foreign wars.[3] To stop that agenda, he wrote, "an unelected cabal in the senior ranks of our government," acting in concert with [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and the fake news media, launched "the de-facto coup attempt known as the Russia Hoax" to prevent his election or sabotage his presidency.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that since the first impeachment, where she said Democrat impeachment managers fabricated a crime and lied with impunity, the perpetrators had been caught but accountability for the American people had not been realized.[3] She quoted Trump's inaugural address on a small group in the nation's capital reaping the rewards of government while the people bore the cost, and argued that the weaponization of government had been normalized to the point of open display at all levels.[3]

## Durham hearing and the narrative war (June 2023)

The [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|June 21, 2023 House Judiciary hearing]] on the [[durham-report|Durham Report]] became a focal point of the dispute over the Russia collusion narrative.[4] [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and other Democrats defended the [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] report's legitimacy and argued that Durham's small number of prosecutions showed there had been no underlying crime; [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators countered that the report destroyed the credibility of the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] and that far greater crimes remained unexposed.[4] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the hearing functioned as a deliberate punching bag on which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] and its media allies expended their delegitimization campaign before more damning material surfaced.[4]

## Durham's 2023 testimony

Testifying before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on June 21, 2023, Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] said the [[cia|CIA]] knew that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] had approved a plan to tie then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Russia in order to divert attention from her classified email scandal.[4] He testified that the [[fbi|FBI]] had failed to "sufficiently scrutinize information it received" and did not "apply the same standards to allegations it received about the Clinton and Trump campaigns," adding that the bureau "was too willing to accept and use politically funded and uncorroborated opposition research, such as the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]]."[4]

Badlands commentators divided over the significance of the hearing. [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] outlined three competing readings — that Durham was compromised "fruit from the [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]] tree," that he had tried and found the system too corrupt, or that the entire sequence was theater intended to build public consent for military intervention — while holding open the possibility that the report would prove instrumental later.[4] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the hearing functioned as a deliberate lightning rod, letting the [[deep-state|Deep State]] and its media allies expend their delegitimization campaign on a comparatively thin first impression while more damning material remained undisclosed.[4]

## Continuing cover-up thesis (2023)

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued in August 2023 that RussiaGate consumed two years and millions of taxpayer dollars, obstructed government, interfered with the peaceful transition of power, and remained an active cover-up.[5] She singled out MSNBC's [[rachel-maddow|Rachel Maddow]] as "one of the main peddlers" of the hoax and noted the irony of Maddow questioning [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] about election denial.[5]

## Allied intelligence origins (2024 reporting)

In February 2024, Public and Racket reported that multiple credible sources described the U.S. Intelligence Community, including the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], illegally mobilizing foreign intelligence agencies to target [[donald-trump|Trump]] advisors long before the summer of 2016.[6] The reporting was framed as filling gaps in the public understanding of the Russia collusion hoax and as consistent with testimony already in the public record.[6] It followed [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s conclusion that the [[fbi|FBI]] should never have opened its collusion investigation in late July 2016.[6]

Until that point the official account had been that the FBI's investigation began after Australian intelligence officials told U.S. officials that a Trump aide had boasted to an Australian diplomat that Russia held damaging material on [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[6] According to sources close to a [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]] investigation, the U.S. IC instead asked the [[five-eyes|Five Eyes]] alliance — the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand — to surveil Trump's associates and share the resulting intelligence with American agencies.[6]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted the strangeness of watching a claim the Truth Community had discussed in detail for more than a decade arrive as "breaking news," but argued that the hoax re-entering the narrative war during the 2024 election cycle, complete with seeding about Trump declassifications, was more tantalizing than annoying, and predicted that further evidentiary disclosures would draw new minds toward the community.[6]

## Schiff's 2023 Senate bid and the Durham spin cycle

The Russiagate narrative remained politically live in January 2023, when [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] - described by Badlands contributor Justin Deschamps as one of the chief congressmen pushing the hoax from his perch on the House Intelligence Committee - launched a campaign for a California seat in the U.S. Senate.[7] Deschamps argued that Schiff had violated the public trust by using his committee position to push "blatant falsehoods and lies" about Donald Trump, and that those same lies served as a pretext to deprive January 6 defendants of due process.[7]

In the same period The New York Times published a retrospective on how Attorney General [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]]'s effort to find flaws in the Russia inquiry had "unraveled," which Just Human read as an anonymously sourced attempt to re-establish that the Trump-Russia investigations were legitimate while [[john-durham|Durham]]'s counter-investigation was not.[7]

## Twitter Files installment #21

In late April 2023 The Gateway Pundit presented the twenty-first release of the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] as fresh documentary support for the claim that the narrative of Russian accounts materially impacting U.S. elections had been a lie from the outset.[8] The outlet argued that before the 2016 election the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[barack-obama|Obama]] camps, fearing that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would investigate their conduct if elected, invented the story tying him to Russians — even as the administration had approved the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] sale of U.S. uranium to Russian interests.[8]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the "Russian bot" and "Russian troll" epithets were deployed against ordinary dissenters so indiscriminately that [[twitter|Twitter]] even labelled Jill Stein as Russian, and argued that the platform functioned as a state actor: censoring at government direction, granting the government access to user account data, and permitting manipulation of propaganda on the platform.[8] He characterised the resulting apparatus as a bot army mobilised to shield Obama and Biden while attacking Trump and his supporters, concluding that "the Obama coup continues to this day."[8]

## 2025 declassifications, post-mortem evidence and grand jury referral

Newly declassified evidence reported in August 2025 showed that the FBI had been alerted to intelligence in 2016 indicating that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] planned to smear then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] by linking him to Russian leader [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], and that bureau leaders then played into the campaign's strategy by conducting a sweeping investigation into false claims of Trump-Russia collusion.[9] Clinton's own words in a 2016 election post-mortem were expected to be central to investigators.[9] Public records show that Clinton, in coordination with campaign general counsel [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], campaign manager Robby Mook, campaign chairman [[john-podesta|John Podesta]], communications director [[jennifer-palmieri|Jennifer Palmieri]], policy adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and others, launched an effort to tie Trump to Putin as the 2016 race for the White House proceeded.[9]

Badlands contributor Jon Herold laid out a compressed timeline: on 26 July 2016 Clinton approved the plan to vilify Trump; on 27 July 2016 [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] told the Democratic National Convention that Trump "cozies up to Putin," with his return to the White House absent from his daily schedule; and on 28 July 2016 [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] briefed Obama on the Clinton plan and the Fusion Cell was created.[9] Herold asked why Obama would raise the Putin allegation a day before being briefed on it by Brennan.[9]

On 4 August 2025 Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] signed an order directing a federal prosecutor to present evidence to a grand jury to weigh charges related to the hoax, following criminal referrals to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] from Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], who described the politicization of intelligence as a "treasonous conspiracy."[9] A Justice Department strike force established by Bondi was left to determine whether the affair was a political dirty trick or rose to the level of criminal conspiracy.[9]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America argued that accountability must proceed "by the book" with defendants' rights preserved, holding that the measure of justice is accountability rather than transparency, and that for "the biggest and most treasonous conspiracy in the history of America" accountability means setting an example that still makes people think twice a century later.[9] Burning Bright added that the public dismantling of Russiagate does not merely exonerate Trump but seeds a "Russian redemption arc" in the Western mind, recasting Russia as a fellow victim of globalist subversion rather than the perennial boogeyman of Hollywood and Cold War political narrative.[9]

## Ratcliffe's July 2025 prosecution comments

On July 28, 2025, [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] told Fox News host Maria Bartiromo that former Obama-era officials had manufactured "Russiagate" to undermine [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2016, and that figures including [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] could face indictment or prosecution.[10] "This was a Hillary Clinton campaign scheme," he said, alleging that Clinton conspired to "falsely accuse" Trump of colluding with Russia through what became the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]], and that Clinton, [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] lied under oath about their roles.[10] Asked about accountability, Ratcliffe answered: "Well, that's why I've made the referrals that I have."[10]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the moment as the transformation of "Grand Conspiracy" from a hashtag into a legally codified label for Disclosure, noting that DNI [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] had codified the framing days earlier and that the parallel Epstein and Russiagate disclosure tracks gave Trump bipartisan "narrative shielding."[10]

## July 2025 declassification

On Friday, July 18, 2025, Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] declassified documents that Fox News Digital reported as "overwhelming evidence" that the Obama administration had "manufactured and politicized intelligence" to build the narrative of Russian interference in the 2016 election, despite intelligence community assessments to the contrary.[11] The material showed that in the months before the November 2016 election the intelligence community consistently judged that Russia was "probably not trying…to influence the election by using cyber means," and that after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s victory over [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and his national security team laid the groundwork for the collusion probe.[11]

Gabbard's team released over 100 pages of memos and emails, which she described as evidence of a "treasonous conspiracy" and a "years-long coup against President Trump," calling for former officials to be prosecuted and saying there was "no rational or logical explanation" for why the investigations run by [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] and [[john-durham|John Durham]] failed to surface the same evidence.[11] Badlands commentary pointed to remarks by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on January 5, 2017, the day before the Intelligence Community Assessment was released, as evidence that senior officials pushed a narrative they knew to be false.[11]


## Nunes on fatalities and the Section 242 charging theory (August 2025)

In August 2025 [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]] told Real America's Voice that the conspiracy had killed people: "People have died. People have lost their livelihoods. People have lost their careers. People have not been promoted. Families have been destroyed. Finances have been wiped out. Millions of Americans were kicked off social media. News networks were not allowed onto the legacy media platforms."[12] Around the same time, [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] said the overriding charge in the coming cases would be [[deprivation-of-rights-under-color-of-law|deprivation of rights under color of law]] under Title 18, U.S. Code, Section 242.[12] Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] noted the statute's penalty provision: where death results from acts committed in violation of the section, the offender may be fined, imprisoned for any term of years or for life, or sentenced to death.[12] The distillation of RussiaGate revelations coincided with fresh federal espionage prosecutions of alleged Russian assets, including the arrest of soldier [[taylor-adam-lee|Taylor Adam Lee]].[12]

## Calls for prosecutions and agency overhaul (August 2025)

In August 2025, an opinion piece in The Hill argued that newly released documents and damning evidence "conclusively substantiate" that Russiagate was a conspiracy hatched, implemented, and relentlessly promoted by top officials in the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], [[fbi|FBI]], and across what it called the Obama–Biden–Clinton political machine to rig a presidential election and undermine a duly elected president, corrupting institutions essential to protecting American liberty.[13] Despite the accumulated evidence and exhaustive investigations, the piece noted, those responsible remained unpunished.[13]

The article charged that former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] and former Director of National Intelligence [[james-clapper|James Clapper]], among other intelligence officials, had lied to Congress and the public about their reliance on the discredited [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] — a report paid for by the Clinton campaign and the DNC — while engineering different versions of critical intelligence assessments to cover their tracks.[13] Although the intelligence community publicly maintained that the dossier played no role in the official assessment on "Russian Activities and Intentions in Recent U.S. Elections," newly declassified oversight reviews were said to flatly contradict those claims.[13]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] described the effort as "a lifelong trade for them – to subvert the United States of America," predicting that once fully developed the record would make it easy to reassess events of the previous ten to twenty years involving "these same characters."[13] The renewed scrutiny coincided with Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s revocation of security clearances from 37 current and former officials, including some who worked on the 2016 Russian interference assessment.[13]

## Columbia Journalism Review critique (2023)

At the end of January 2023, the [[columbia-journalism-review|Columbia Journalism Review]] issued a scathing indictment of the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] for yellow journalism during the Trump-Russia saga, concluding in effect that the paper of record had been operating in bad faith.[14] Coverage of the report by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] noted that the findings applied not only to the Times but reflected what objective observers had long concluded about the press as a whole.[14]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that legacy outlets had failed in discernment and objectivity when sourcing and confirming erroneous claims generated — and sometimes paid for — by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s political opposition, and that the spectacle of media watchdogs turning on the most prestigious organizations in their world indicated a mass shift taking place behind the scenes.[14] In this reading, vectors within the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] were attempting to get ahead of forthcoming revelations about Russiagate just as they were about COVID-era government propaganda, and no amount of spin or circular firing squad would avert accountability.[14]

## Exposure as a turning point (May 2023)

When [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s final report was released in May 2023, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said through a spokesman that it "proves" the federal government had coordinated to interfere in the 2016 election, writing on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that "the American Public was scammed" and describing the affair as "the Democrat Hoax that was perpetrated upon me and the American people."[15] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the significance of the moment was chiefly temporal: the Russia collusion hoax had marked the beginning of the Deep State's narrative offensive against Trump and the MAGA movement, and its formal exposure — reported even by outlets such as [[cnn|CNN]] — might mark the beginning of the reverse process.[15] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] added that although the report accomplished less than some hoped, legacy media attempts to keep the Russiagate narrative alive were exhausting what public credibility those outlets retained, while the America First movement focused on issues voters cared about.[15]
