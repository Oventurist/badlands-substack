# Belgium Narco-State Controversy

The **Belgium narco-state controversy** refers to a November 2025 diplomatic exchange in which Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] argued that the United States would be better served targeting drug networks in Belgium than bombing small vessels off the coast of [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[1]

## Origin of the term

Lavrov's remark drew on a statement by a Belgian judge who wrote to the country's parliament that "mafia-like structures have taken root" and asked whether the country was "evolving into a narco-state."[1]

## Lavrov's criticism of US maritime strikes

Speaking at a press conference on Tuesday, November 11, 2025, Lavrov denounced the maritime airstrikes authorized by the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which Washington says target drug smugglers operating in the Caribbean.[1] He called the attacks "acts taken without due process" that show the United States "places itself above the law."[1] He added: "Rather than targeting Nigeria and Venezuela with anti-drug operations and [potential] seizures of oil fields, the US should probably focus on eradicating this social ill in Belgium. The US already has troops there and will not have to chase after small boats with crews of three people each."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the remark as another front in what he described as a coordinated pincer movement by Trump and Russia against the EU and [[nato|NATO]] "Old Guard," distinct from their joint pressure on the [[bbc|BBC]] over the January 6 footage scandal.[1] He argued that the Ukraine war had functioned to lock the EU and NATO into an existential kinetic conflict, and that Trump had converted European hubris into a mechanism forcing Europe to purchase American weapons rather than relying on open-ended U.S. taxpayer funding.[1] He praised Lavrov for "accurately annotating the globalist deep state," contending that the [[deep-state|deep state]] is simultaneously a cartel and a narco-state because it created the Central and South American cartels whose product was then unleashed on the American home front.[1]
