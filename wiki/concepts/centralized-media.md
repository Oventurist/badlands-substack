# Centralized Media

"Centralized media" is the Badlands Media term for the mainstream media (MSM) as a consolidated ownership structure rather than a set of independent outlets. It denotes the small group of conglomerates that control broadcast, print, internet, radio and film output, together with the institutional investors who own them in common.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## The Big Six

As of 2017 there were six major conglomerates that "lorded over all mainstream media," with very little existing outside their umbrellas: Time Warner, Comcast, [[disney|Disney]], Sony, National Amusements — now [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] — and Rupert Murdoch's NewsCorp.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Common ownership

Although these firms appeared to compete, the article argues they were mutually owned by the world's largest institutional investors — asset managers including [[blackrock|BlackRock Inc.]], [[vanguard-group|the Vanguard Group]], [[state-street|State Street]] and [[berkshire-hathaway|Berkshire Hathaway]] — whose representatives are described as regular fixtures at elite policy bodies such as the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) On this reading the "big six" are "just the propaganda arm of the big 1—the Western establishment, the system of systems."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Function

Historically the primary mandate of a free press was to act as a counterbalance keeping establishment power in check; Badlands argues that today the media not only fails at that role but that "the media IS the establishment."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Rather than a check, the press is characterized as the propaganda arm of the state, working directly in tandem with the [[us-security-state|United States intelligence apparatus]] and the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The article invokes political scientist [[bernard-c-cohen|Bernard C. Cohen]]'s agenda-setting formulation to describe the mechanism: the press may not tell people what to think, but it is stunningly successful in telling readers what to think about.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

Its opposite is the [[decentralized-media|decentralized media]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)
