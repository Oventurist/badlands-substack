# Maersk Container Ship Attack

The Maersk container ship attack was an assault by Iran-backed [[houthis|Houthi]] forces on a Maersk vessel in the Red Sea on Sunday, December 31, 2023, and the U.S. military response that followed.[1]

## The attack and U.S. response

Following the strike on the container ship, U.S. forces responded with attack helicopters that eliminated three small boats and ten rebels.[1] Maersk halted Red Sea transit after the container ship was hit by a missile.[1]

## Houthi reaction

After the skirmish a spokesperson for the Yemeni militia group warned of "consequences and repercussions" for what it called U.S. aggression.[1] Houthi military spokesman [[yahya-sarea|Yahya Sarea]] confirmed on the Yemeni television channel Al-Masirah that U.S. forces had killed ten of its fighters, saying: "US enemy forces attacked three boats belonging to the Yemeni Naval Forces, which led to the martyrdom and the loss of ten people from the Naval Forces."[1] Sarea said the fighters were "performing their humanitarian and moral duty" to deter Israel-related commercial vessels from transiting the Red Sea "in solidarity and support for the Palestinian people."[1]

## Regional escalation

Regional instability risks mounted in the wake of the attack, with [[iran|Iran]] dispatching a warship to the Red Sea in what Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read as "a major marker of attempted intimidation."[1] He noted a contradictory pattern in the theater: escalation in the Suez approaches through increasingly frequent Houthi strikes, alongside [[israel|Israel]] signalling a drawdown in [[gaza|Gaza]] while maintaining a "war throughout 2024 and beyond" public posture.[1] He also flagged the under-reported return of the [[uss-gerald-r-ford|USS Ford]] carrier to the United States after an extended deployment monitoring the Israel crisis, replaced by another carrier reportedly carrying 2,000 marines, and cautioned that "any mass change in operations in this theater should be watched closely."[1]
