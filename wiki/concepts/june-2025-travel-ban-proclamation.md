# June 2025 Travel Ban Proclamation

The June 2025 travel ban proclamation, formally titled "Restricting the Entry of Foreign Nationals to Protect the United States From Foreign Terrorists and Other National Security and Public Safety Threats," was a presidential action issued by [[donald-trump|President Donald J. Trump]] in June 2025 that fully or partially suspended the entry of nationals from nineteen countries.[1]

## Background

In the text of the proclamation Trump recalled that during his first administration he had restricted the entry of foreign nationals into the United States, an action he said successfully prevented national security threats from reaching American borders and which was upheld by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1] He cited Executive Order 14161 of January 20, 2025 — "Protecting the United States From Foreign Terrorists and Other National Security and Public Safety Threats" — in which he stated that it is the policy of the United States to protect its citizens from aliens who intend to commit terrorist attacks, threaten national security, espouse hateful ideology, or otherwise exploit the immigration laws for malevolent purposes.[1]

Under that earlier order the Secretary of State was directed, in coordination with the Attorney General, the Secretary of Homeland Security and the Director of National Intelligence, to identify countries worldwide whose vetting and screening information was so deficient as to warrant a full or partial suspension of the admission of their nationals.[1]

## Countries designated

After reviewing the resulting report and accounting for the foreign policy, national security and counterterrorism objectives of the United States, Trump determined to fully restrict and limit the entry of nationals of twelve countries: Afghanistan, Burma, Chad, the Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Haiti, Iran, Libya, Somalia, Sudan and Yemen.[1] He further determined to partially restrict and limit the entry of nationals of seven additional countries: Burundi, Cuba, Laos, Sierra Leone, Togo, Turkmenistan and Venezuela.[1] In both tiers the restrictions distinguished between, but applied to both, the entry of immigrants and nonimmigrants.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] noted approvingly that the proclamation used the name "Burma" rather than "Myanmar," calling the choice "based," and asked rhetorically whether "the Regime" was fleeing the listed countries.[1]
