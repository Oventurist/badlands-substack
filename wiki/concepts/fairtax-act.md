# FairTax Act

The FairTax Act, introduced in the U.S. House of Representatives as H.R. 25, is a bill that would replace the existing federal tax code with a national consumption tax, eliminating both the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] and Tax Day.[1]

## Tax Day alternative resolution (2024)

On April 16, 2024, Republican Georgia Representatives [[buddy-carter|Buddy Carter]] and [[andrew-clyde|Andrew Clyde]] introduced a resolution that would designate April 16 — the day after Tax Day — as "FairTax Day."[1] The resolution was framed as part of H.R. 25 and was intended, in the lawmakers' description, to demonstrate readiness to begin a process giving the people power over their paychecks.[1]

Carter argued that "Americans spend over six billion hours per year filing their taxes," and that "instead of putting your hard-earned dollars straight into Uncle Sam's pocket, the FairTax allows you to keep 100% of your paycheck and choose how much you pay in taxes every year."[1] He described the measure as "a simple, fair, and preferred alternative to the current system, which punishes success and puts unelected bureaucrats in charge of your paycheck," and called for making April 15, 2024, "our last Tax Day ever."[1] The proposal is described as a Georgia-grown initiative.[1]
