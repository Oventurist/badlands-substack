# Wolf Act

The Wolf Act is the 2011 U.S. legislative provision that banned scientific collaboration between the United States and China on space science and technology. It was passed by [[congress|Congress]] under the administration of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], on the stated grounds that "China's cyber-espionage was a threat to American democracy" and that collaboration carried high espionage risks.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the ban had the opposite effect to that intended by its authors. Barred from participating in space science with [[nasa|NASA]], China was forced to rely on its own creative powers and subsequently leapt ahead in the space race: landing the first robot on the Moon in forty years in 2013, becoming the first nation to land on the far side of the Moon with the Chang'e-4 / "Jade Rabbit" mission on January 3, 2019, and building its own orbital outpost, the [[tiangong-space-station|Tiangong]] or "Heavenly Kingdom" station.[1]

Ehret notes that the Wolf Act was passed several weeks before the July 2011 end of America's manned shuttle program, and that over the following thirteen years Chinese offers to build an advanced joint program with the United States "were met with insults," resulting in China going it alone and ultimately outpacing the most advanced Western technological breakthroughs.[1]
