# Forever Wars

"Forever wars" — also rendered as "endless wars" — is the term used in populist and [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] commentary for the open-ended American military engagements and foreign funding commitments that critics argue serve financial rather than strategic ends.

In the [[2023-trump-cpac-speech|2023 Trump CPAC speech]], [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told the [[conservative-political-action-conference|CPAC]] audience: "We are never going back to a party that wants to give unlimited money to fight foreign wars, that are endless wars, that are stupid wars!"[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] treated the line as one of the most significant of the address, writing that "war is possibly the biggest operation currently set in place to wash and re-distribute American taxpayer money," and that publicly declaring an intention to end it "tells the [[deep-state|Deep State]] that you know what it really is."[1]

## Q drops on war financing

Getz connected the theme to [[qanon|Q]] posts from January 2018. Drop 119 asked, "Who AUDITS the billions paid for war?", while post 489 stated that "WAR = scam (trillions)" and asked, "Who audits where the money actually goes? Who receives it?"[1] Getz argued that these questions "made far less sense in January of 2018 than they do now, over $100 Billion to Ukraine later," presenting the scale of Ukraine funding as retrospective vindication of the posts.[1]

## Partisan history

Getz advanced a historical claim that for a century the Democrats had been "the purveyors of foreign War," listing Woodrow Wilson and World War I, Franklin D. Roosevelt and World War II, Harry Truman and Korea, and Lyndon Johnson and Vietnam.[1] On this account it was only when [[neoconservatives|neoconservatism]] "blurred party lines," allowing [[rino|RINOs]] to emerge within the [[republican-party|Republican Party]], that the right adopted the same posture — a development he attributed to the "Neo-con Bushes" and their [[council-on-foreign-relations|CFR]] associates, traceable back to [[prescott-bush|Prescott Bush]].[1] Getz concluded that Trump "was right about the Forever Wars, and he was right about the Republican Party," and that the recognition was now general: "Everybody knows now, and we are never going back."[1]
