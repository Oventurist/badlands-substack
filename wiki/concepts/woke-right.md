# Woke Right

"Woke right" is a contested polemical term applied to a segment of the political right accused of mirroring the analytical structure of progressive identity politics with the roles reversed.[1]

## The argument

Proponents of the label argue that a set of claims which in 2021 would have marked a speaker as a progressive follower of activist-scholars such as Robin DiAngelo and Ibram X. Kendi — that certain demographic groups are oppressed by race, gender and sexuality; that society is suffused with subtle "hegemonic narratives" justifying dominant-group power; that most people are blind to these forces; and that only radical social transformation, not reform, can remedy them — are now advanced by a small but vocal online right, including some Christians.[1] In the inverted version, straight white men are the oppressed class, society is said to be ruled by women and structured by hegemonic narratives such as "the longhouse" and "the postwar consensus," "normies" are the blind, and the proposed solution is a "Protestant Franco or a Christian prince."[1]

## Dispute over the label

The term is itself disputed. Badlands' [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] reacted sharply to its adoption by figures he characterized as establishment neoconservatives, singling out [[seth-dillon|Seth Dillon]] of [[babylon-bee|The Babylon Bee]] for what Paul described as joining a chorus "deciding that 'conspiracy theorists' are 'woke.'"[1] Paul argued the usage validated warnings he had issued in 2022, when, he said, the same commentators now deploying the term had dismissed his analysis as "kayfabe" and called for "unity."[1]
