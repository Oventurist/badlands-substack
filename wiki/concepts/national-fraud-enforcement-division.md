# National Fraud Enforcement Division

The National Fraud Enforcement Division is a new division of the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] created by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to investigate and prosecute fraud against taxpayers and federal programs.[1] On January 28, 2026, Trump nominated federal prosecutor [[colin-mcdonald|Colin McDonald]] as its inaugural Assistant Attorney General.[1]

The division's stated mandate covers large-scale misuse of federal funds and related financial crimes.[1] Trump justified its creation by pointing to fraud schemes uncovered in states including Minnesota and California, asserting that "thieves have stolen Hundreds of Billions of Taxpayer Dollars" from federal programs.[1] Primary reporting did not describe the division as limited to election fraud, although election-related financial crimes could fall within its remit.[1]

## Interpretation within Badlands coverage

Badlands writers treated the division as a structural, enduring counterpart to the discrete [[fulton-county-election-raid|Fulton County raid]] — the raid being an event, the division being a permanent enforcement apparatus.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the two developments, together with declassifications concerning the 2016 [[russiagate-hoax|Russia collusion]] narrative, represented "chapters from the same playbook," in which intelligence apparatus both domestic and foreign were used to stage-manage transitions of power.[1] The [[minnesota-color-revolution|Minnesota]] fraud exposures were described as the epicenter that helped ignite the division's creation.[1]

## Launch and staffing (April 2026)

Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] formally announced the launch of the division on April 7, 2026, stating that it would add 93 dedicated prosecutors deployed across every federal judicial district nationwide, with their work focused exclusively on fraud cases.[2] Blanche noted that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] was already managing more than 8,000 ongoing fraud matters, which he described as only part of a much broader problem of abuse.[2] The initiative was said to coordinate closely with the [[task-force-to-eliminate-fraud|Task Force to Eliminate Fraud]] led by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and was framed as a "supercharging" of enforcement efforts against those exploiting federal programs and taxpayer funds.[2] Blanche issued a warning that "fraudsters, scammers, tax cheats, or anyone who lies to get rich off the generosity of the American people should be on notice."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that almost 100 new federal prosecutors were being added while 8,000 cases represented "only a portion" of the needed work, remarking that "that's a lot of fraud."[2] She called the move encouraging, arguing that the disclosures of the [[doge|DOGE]] efforts "require justice not just air time" and that hiring full-time prosecutors implies justice is part of the plan.[2]
