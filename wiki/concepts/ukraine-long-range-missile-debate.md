# Ukraine Long-Range Missile Debate

The Ukraine long-range missile debate concerns whether the United States should permit the Ukrainian military to use U.S.-provided long-range missiles, including the Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS), to strike targets deep inside Russia.[1]

## Background

By September 2024 Ukrainian wartime leaders were again preoccupied with a decision in Washington that they believed would shape their fate as much as events on the front lines.[1] The spring and summer fighting seasons had produced successes—an incursion into Russia and technological advances in drone and robotic warfare that blunted Russian advantages in manpower and weapons—but at enormous human cost, with pictures of the dead hanging across Kyiv.[1] Russia was intensifying bombing of the capital and of energy infrastructure, and there were growing worries that the coming winter would be the hardest yet.[1]

## The decision before Biden

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] faced a choice over whether to expand the approved target list beyond the immediate Russian border area.[1] Ukrainian officials argued the request was fair, since Russia was waging war from its own territory using bombers and missiles, and Kyiv wanted to defend itself with the weapons it already had.[1] Politico argued the step was no more escalatory than earlier decisions to supply Abrams tanks, F-16s or ATACMS, each of which Biden approved after protracted deliberation and none of which produced the consequences forecast.[1]

[[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] warned the week before that Ukrainian ATACMS strikes on Russia "will mean that [[nato|NATO]] countries — the United States and European countries — are at war with Russia."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the Western leadership pushing for deeper strikes as "ostensibly suicidal—psychotic, even—in their desire to fight Russia to the bitter end," and at minimum as having lost touch with reality.[1] He cited remarks by [[boris-johnson|Boris Johnson]] at the [[yalta-european-strategy-conference|Yalta European Strategy Conference]] as evidence, pointed to [[jeffrey-sachs|Jeffrey Sachs]]'s rebuttal in an interview with [[andrew-napolitano|Judge Andrew Napolitano]], and predicted that "real nuclear scare events" would begin materializing soon as leaders proved willing to risk world war.[1]
