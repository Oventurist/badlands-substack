# Badlands Brief: As Expected Energy Wars, "Life Insurance", & Crazies Keeping Their Guns (Maybe)

"As Expected Energy Wars, 'Life Insurance', & Crazies Keeping Their Guns (Maybe)" is an installment of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated March 3, 2026. Like other editions, it pairs wire-service style news summaries with signed commentary from Badlands contributors. This issue covers the expansion of United States airstrikes on [[iran|Iran]], the release of congressional testimony from [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] in the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein]] inquiries, shipping disruptions in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], scrutiny of federal immigration enforcement, remarks by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] on the origins of the Iran strikes, and a bonus item on a [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] argument over the federal gun ban for drug users.

## US airstrikes on Iran expand

The Brief reports that the United States and [[israel|Israel]] launched coordinated air and missile strikes against Iran on Friday, targeting military leadership, missile systems, and nuclear-related infrastructure, as confirmed by US officials. Supreme Leader Ayatollah [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] was killed in the attacks, and US officials said more than 1,000 targets had been struck since the campaign began.

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the operation aimed to halt Iran's nuclear and long-range missile capabilities and could last several weeks, while indicating he remained open to talks with Iranian officials even as military pressure continued. Secretary of War [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] said the United States did not intend to deploy ground troops but did not rule it out if necessary, describing the campaign as focused on military objectives rather than governance change.

Former national security adviser [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] warned that the strategy could lead to "turmoil and bloodshed" and urged clearer post-strike planning. Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said attempts at regime change through external force would be "mission impossible," adding that Iran was interested in de-escalation but that there was no direct communication with Washington. Iran launched retaliatory missile and drone attacks against US and allied positions in the region. The Brief notes as a related item that [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] was already "eyeing" Cuba as a next theater.

In commentary, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argues that "Donald Trump's [[kobayashi-maru|Kobayashi Maru]] isn't breaking him. It's breaking the Forever War narrative." He writes that [[operation-epic-fury|Operation Epic Fury]] launched "with the full cinematic weight the Collective Mind has been primed to expect: precision strikes, retaliatory missiles, Trump addressing the Iranian people directly, all befitting the surface-level optics of a fresh Regime Change Op" that, according to "every script the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]] has run for decades," should spiral into another Middle East quagmire. Instead, he contends, "the pivot is already beginning."

Burning Bright frames the campaign as an application of the [[venezuela-model|Venezuela Model]], citing an exclusive interview in which Trump told The New York Times repeatedly that he was following the template he used in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] — not the version "the Globalists hoped for with [[maria-corina-machado|Maria Corina Machado]] and her NGO-backed regime change fantasy," but one in which the visible figurehead is removed while "the majority of the governing structure remains intact, stabilized, pragmatic and suddenly open to the deals that sovereignty and [[america-first|America First]] demand." In Venezuela, he writes, the operation removed [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] as "the visible lightning rod," cut off cartel pipelines feeding "the actual Globalist proxies," installed continuity with "a sovereign-aligned hand at the wheel," and moved into energy cooperation and stability narratives — "No endless occupation. No boots on the ground. Just [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] (a term I invented on the back of it,) wearing the mask of the very thing the enemy had spent years demanding."

Applying this to Iran, he argues the surface story hands the NeoCon establishment the optics of a new Forever War, yet John Bolton — "the walking, talking mascot of the entire NeoCon Collective" — is "sounding the alarm instead of sounding the victory horn." Bolton, in this reading, is not upset that Trump supposedly started a war but that Trump "doesn't seem like he's actually following through with the sort of full-scale regime change the NeoCons had in mind," faulting the lack of consultation with opposition on the ground and the absence of strategy for "cutting off the head of the Iranian snake." Burning Bright concludes that Trump "invited them onto a complex, some might say impossible Iranian rug ... and that it's already halfway out from under them."

## Strait of Hormuz disruptions and energy supply

The Brief reports that escalating hostilities involving Iran disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a key global oil transit route connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea, through which approximately 20 percent of the world's oil supply passes. Recent attacks and electronic interference led shipping companies to suspend or reduce transits. Saudi oil facilities were pulled into the broader regional conflict following traditional and drone attacks on oil infrastructure in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]. Oil prices and tanker costs rose in response, with energy analysts warning that prolonged disruption could significantly affect global energy markets. US naval forces were operating in the region to safeguard maritime traffic and global commerce.

[[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] notes that [[saudi-aramco|Saudi Aramco]] refineries were attacked, including the largest oil refinery in the world, and yet Iran's deputy foreign minister appeared on [[cnn|CNN]] to state that Iran did not conduct the attacks and that Saudi Arabia was well aware of this. He adds that [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] claimed both [[qatar|Qatar]] and Saudi Arabia had arrested [[mossad|Mossad]] agents planning false-flag bombings in those countries to be blamed on Iran. He cautions that "to what extent these claims are true remains to be seen," since "the Gulf State monarchies are notoriously private and would not necessarily announce something like this to the public," but reasons that it "wouldn't make much sense" for Carlson to fabricate something damaging to the states he is defending. What is clear to him is "a concerted effort right now to destroy the security architecture in the Middle East that Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salmon]] has spent years establishing," such that "in a very short time we have gone from the Middle East being a harbinger of the Golden Age to the potential spoiler of it."

## Immigration enforcement under scrutiny

A Minnesota prosecutor launched an investigation into the conduct of federal immigration agents, including a top [[united-states-border-patrol|US Border Patrol]] official, tied to enforcement actions earlier in the year. [[hennepin-county-attorneys-office|Hennepin County]] Attorney [[mary-moriarty|Mary Moriarty]] said her office had created a portal for the public to submit evidence and would probe the "potentially unlawful behavior" of Border Patrol agents, including [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]], and pursue charges where appropriate. The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] responded that enforcement is a federal responsibility and that state prosecutors lack authority to charge federal officers.

In a separate incident, a nearly blind refugee from [[myanmar|Myanmar]], released by US Border Patrol after a misdemeanor plea deal, was found dead in Buffalo, New York, five days after his release. Border Patrol said agents dropped the man, Nurul Amin Shah Alam, at a coffee shop near his last known address after determining he could not be deported; the circumstances of his death were under investigation. A [[reuters|Reuters]]/Ipsos poll found a majority of Americans support deporting unauthorized immigrants but disapprove of aggressive enforcement tactics.

## Rubio on the origins of the strikes

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Monday that the United States decided to launch strikes against Iran after determining that Israel was planning its own attack on Iranian targets that would trigger Iranian retaliation against American forces. "We knew that there was going to be an Israeli action, we knew that that would precipitate an attack against American forces, and we knew that if we didn't preemptively go after them before they launched those attacks, we would suffer higher casualties," Rubio said. He made the comments before a closed-door briefing to [[congress|Congress]] on the joint operation.

House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] and other Republican lawmakers said in congressional briefings that Israel was prepared to attack Iran "with or without American support," and that US involvement reflected planning to protect US forces from anticipated Iranian retaliation. Rubio also discussed US military objectives, including degrading Iran's ballistic missile and naval capabilities, while saying the United States was not currently postured for ground forces in Iran.

## Bonus item: Supreme Court and the drug-user gun ban

The Supreme Court heard arguments Monday in a case challenging the federal law barring unlawful users of controlled substances from possessing firearms, with several justices expressing skepticism about the statute's breadth. The challenge arose after lower courts questioned whether the statute is consistent with the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] under the framework established in [[new-york-state-rifle-and-pistol-association-v-bruen|New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen]], which requires firearm regulations to align with the nation's historical tradition of gun regulation. During oral argument, multiple justices asked whether historical analogues exist that would justify broadly disarming individuals based solely on current drug use; the federal government defended the statute, arguing that individuals actively using illegal drugs may pose heightened safety risks comparable to historically disarmed groups.
