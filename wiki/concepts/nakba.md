# Nakba

The **Nakba** — Arabic for "catastrophe" — refers to the mass displacement of Palestinian Arabs during the 1947–48 civil war and the founding of the State of Israel.[1] In Badlands Media's telling, the Nakba was not an incidental byproduct of war but a deliberate terror campaign conducted against Palestinian civilians in order to depopulate territory claimed for the new Jewish state.[1]

## Perpetrators

The campaign is attributed principally to four Zionist paramilitary organizations — the [[irgun|Irgun]], the [[lehi|Lehi]], the [[haganah|Haganah]] and the [[palmach|Palmach]] — which merged in 1948 to form the [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli Defense Force]].[1] Badlands characterizes the operational hallmark of the campaign as the targeting of "soft targets" such as bus stops and cafés, where grenades were thrown or automatic weapons fired into crowds with the intent of killing or maiming as many people as possible, so as to maximize terror and instill enough existential dread in the Arab population that they would abandon the area.[1]

## Deir Yassin

The culmination of the campaign, in the Badlands account, was the [[deir-yassin-massacre|Deir Yassin massacre]] of April 1948, after which large numbers of Arabs fled.[1] One month later the four militias formally constituted the IDF.[1]

## Historical framing

Badlands situates the Nakba within a broader claim that terrorism was "imported to the Middle East by the Bolsheviks," asserting that the first Middle East terrorists were Jewish Bolsheviks in the 1930s and that the earliest Arab terrorists of the late 1930s were on British payrolls, with the Grand Mufti of Jerusalem described as an asset of British intelligence — a "controlled opposition" dynamic.[1] The Nakba is presented as the first of a recurring pattern of "temporary" evacuations of Palestinian civilians, later echoed by the 1967 [[naksa|Naksa]] and by twenty-first-century operations in Gaza and the West Bank.[1]
