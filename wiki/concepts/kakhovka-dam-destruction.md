# Kakhovka Dam Destruction

The Kakhovka Dam, a Soviet-era structure holding back the Dnipro/Dnieper — one of Europe's major rivers — was bombed in early June 2023, unleashing floodwaters on settlements downstream.[1] Given the colossal volume of water released, fatalities were comparatively limited: the Ukrainian government spoke of 17 killed as a consequence of the attack, with a further 31 missing.[1]

## Competing claims

Both [[russia|Russia]] and [[ukraine|Ukraine]] accused each other of destroying the dam.[1] On 19 June 2023, Ukraine published new evidence it said favored the Russian hypothesis: images purporting to show a car packed with explosives parked atop the dam before the blast.[1] Ukrainian officials, speaking through the [[associated-press|Associated Press]], emphasized that the dam and surrounding area were occupied by Russian forces at the time of the explosion, and said Russian troops were stationed in a crucial area inside the dam where the Ukrainians locate the center of the blast.[1] The AP reported that images taken from above the dam and shared with the wire service "appear to show an explosive-laden car atop the structure."[1]

## Skeptical analysis

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] found the Ukrainian evidence highly suspicious.[1] He noted that the purported device — a car apparently containing two oil drums and a land mine, visible from the air through a cut-open roof — was discovered by the Ukrainians only after the tragedy.[1] Deschamps argued that while the dam's destruction might marginally serve Russian objectives, it carried enormous collateral damage that would be difficult to manage after the war, whereas it clearly favored Ukraine's public relations campaign for sympathy and foreign aid.[1]

He further argued that an improvised device of oil drums and an old land mine seemed out of place given Russia's access to far more sophisticated weaponry, drawing a parallel to the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream]] case in asking what would motivate Russia to act against its own interest.[1] He observed that media coverage was being used to fan the narrative that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] is a "mad dog" who must be stopped by any means necessary.[1]

## Initial reporting and evacuations (June 6, 2023)

Images and videos posted on social media on the morning of Tuesday, June 6, 2023 showed the dam and its associated hydroelectric power plant, located in the Russian-occupied part of Kherson region in southern Ukraine, destroyed.[2] Water rushed through the breach into the Dnieper River, which separates Ukrainian and Russian forces, putting tens of thousands of homes at risk and raising concerns for the safety of Europe's largest nuclear power plant at [[zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporizhzhia]]; thousands of residents were evacuated.[2] It was not immediately clear who was responsible for blowing up the Nova Kakhovka dam.[2]

Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] posted a drone video showing the damaged dam as water gushed downriver and blamed "Russian terrorists."[2] Russia denied the attack: the Russian-installed head of the Kherson administration, [[vladimir-saldo|Vladimir Saldo]], said the destruction had released "a large, but not critical amount of water" down the Dnieper and would "not prevent our military from defending the left bank," accusing Kyiv of striking the dam to "divert attention" from failed counteroffensives.[2] The Russian-installed mayor of Nova Kakhovka, Vladimir Leontiev, said "night attacks" on the dam had "led to the destruction of the valves" and that water from the Kakhovka reservoir "began to uncontrollably be discharged downstream."[2] The [[washington-post|Washington Post]] published satellite imagery of the damage the same day.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the dam story broke on the same day as reporting that the United States had advance intelligence of a Ukrainian plan to attack the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream]] pipeline, and presented the juxtaposition of the two headlines as itself an information-war datapoint.[2] [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] devoted the opening of the first episode of [[tucker-on-twitter|"Tucker on Twitter"]] that evening to the dam's destruction, calling it "an act of terrorism" and criticizing American media for immediately accusing the Russians of sabotaging their own infrastructure.[2]

## Information-war framing

Deschamps situated the dam story within a broader reading of the [[russia-ukraine-war|war]] as dominated by public relations objectives — "a massive prop in a psyop operation, originated by the [[deep-state|Deep State]]," targeting middle-tier bureaucrats, media figures and a general population dependent on unverified expert opinion.[1] He held that war is well suited to grand-scale fear operations because the fog of war obscures verification and because the threat of imminent peril pushes people toward trusting experts.[1]

He predicted the photograph and claim would be debunked in short order and, failing that, would add little fuel to the "hate Putin and let's start WWIII" narrative.[1] In his view, the deeper effect of the dam story and of the war generally may be to disillusion the middle-manager class of world governments — the pawns of the globalists — quietly red-pilling them about the insidiousness of the neoconservative and neoliberal [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]] working hand in glove with big media and social media, which he framed as itself part of the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] operation.[1]
