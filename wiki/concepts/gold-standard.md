# Gold Standard

The gold standard is the monetary system in which a country's currency is backed by gold. According to the article, the United States operated under a gold standard from 1873 to 1933, beginning with the Coinage Act of 1873. The source claims that prior to 1913, the value of money was "astronomically more" than it is today, and few citizens had to worry about loans or debt.

The article asserts that the gold standard "forced the bankers into a siloed depression while citizens flourished." It claims the deflation caused by the gold standard increased the buying power of America's currency, giving more power to the people and increasing productivity. The source cites [[David Ames Wells]]'s observation that the economic changes during the period were "more important and more varied than during any period of the world's history."

According to the article, the Founding Fathers established an economy of "hard money"—a system of tradable coins—that was "the perfect economic system for the American people." This system, the source claims, allowed most Americans to embody the entrepreneurial spirit and own their money, homes, land, and freedom.

The article claims that bankers hated the gold standard so intensely that they began calling for a permanent fiat currency issued by a central bank as early as 1873. The source asserts that abandoning the gold standard was a priority for bankers because it eliminated citizens' ability to demand gold redemption, thereby giving banks "the ability to manipulate society in any way they want to."

The article identifies [[Andrew Jackson]] as a champion of hard money and claims [[Donald Trump]] was trying to bring back the gold standard in a posture reminiscent of Jackson. Trump's nomination of [[Judy Shelton]] to the Federal Reserve is said to reflect this advocacy, as Shelton was "an advocate of bringing back the gold standard."
