# Gold Standard

The gold standard is the monetary regime the article dates as spanning from 1873 to 1933 and holds up as superior to the [[fiat-currency|fiat]] system that replaced it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The [[coinage-act-of-1873|Coinage Act of 1873]] ended the coinage of silver and put the American economy on the gold standard for the first time, causing deflation, which increased the buying power of America's currency.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Under this regime, the article argues, citizens could demand their gold back from bankers — the one mechanism capable of stopping [[fractional-reserve-lending|fractional reserve lending]] — and so getting off the gold standard became a priority of the bankers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Bankers are said to have hated the gold standard so much that from 1873 they began calling for the government to permanently adopt a fiat currency issued by a central bank, because the gold standard forced them into a siloed depression while citizens flourished.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) That period was labelled the [[long-depression|Long Depression]] by bankers, though [[murray-rothbard|Murray Rothbard]] and [[david-ames-wells|David Ames Wells]] are cited to argue it was a period of rising real wages and productivity.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

In the modern era, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] nominated [[judy-shelton|Judy Shelton]] to the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] because she advocated bringing back the gold standard.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The article argues that in light of the reckless money printing prompted by the Covid-19 crisis, every currency in the world is becoming less valuable and there must be a reversal mirroring what happened in 1873: "We need a new deflationary period."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

A later Badlands Media essay places the gold standard among four financial initiatives it attributes to Trump, alongside a digital dollar, a Postal Banking [[public-banking-option|public option]] and the creation of [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) In that account, Americans holding accounts denominated in "pure sovereign money" issued by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] rather than fiat Federal Reserve Notes would no longer require [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance]], and the country is described as going back to a gold standard that is redeemable.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Gold-backed digital money

In "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", Joe Lange extends the gold-standard argument to digital currency, writing that his previous article documented not only the United States but the entire world returning to gold, with central banks buying record amounts of it.[3] He argues that a [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDC]] is merely a digital [[fiat-currency|fiat]] currency unless it is backed by gold, which he reads the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] as requiring.[3]

Because states are making gold and silver coins legal tender and money will be "redeemable," he rejects the claim that cash will be abolished.[3] He notes that the majority of countries have repatriated their gold and are buying more to back their own currencies, that the [[brics|BRICS]] bloc plans a new currency backed by gold and other commodities, and that China's [[shanghai-petroleum-and-natural-gas-exchange|Shanghai Petroleum and Natural Gas Exchange]] already allows oil sold in yuan to be converted into gold.[3] Restoring the standard, in his account, requires the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to revisit the Legal Tender and Gold Clause decisions.[3]

## Criticism: "the gold standard is garbage"

A dissenting Badlands Media treatment appears in [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]]' February 2023 essay "Cracks in the Curtain," the first installment of the [[nature-of-the-control-system|Nature of the Control System]] series, which argues that a return to the gold standard is only one of many solutions and can accomplish little unless combined with other efforts — namely, citizens taking back their financial power from the international bankers.[4] The essay concedes that it is smart to hold gold in a financial portfolio because, even if devalued for a time, it tends to hold value in the long run, and notes an expert claim that the true inflation rate for 2022 was 32%, some 22 points above the official 9.1% figure.[4]

The central objection is that the government can simply stop citizens from using gold as money.[4] Several times in history, monetary authorities — banks, a reigning king, or a commander-in-chief — have decreed that gold cannot be used as money, after which financial chaos usually follows, hitting the less well-off hardest, while elites are tipped off in advance and profit from the resulting transfer of wealth.[4] [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]]'s [[executive-order-6102|Executive Order 6102]] of April 5, 1933 is offered as the paradigm case, issued under the [[trading-with-the-enemy-act-of-1917|Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917]] and the [[emergency-banking-act-of-1933|Emergency Banking Act]].[4] The essay also frames the run-up to Central Bank Digital Currencies as the next iteration of this pattern, predicting that the cabal will at some point trigger an event causing a shift toward CBDCs.[4]

Deschamps cites market analyst and forecaster [[martin-armstrong|Martin Armstrong]] — developer of the market prediction software known as Socrates — who regards the gold standard as "garbage," and who discussed the money cycle and the failure of the gold standard on the Palisades Gold Radio show in December 2020.[4] Against the observation that the founding fathers encoded the standard into the US Constitution on the basis of the prevailing theories of bimetallism and its predecessor mercantilism, the essay replies that the answer lies in how [[money|money]] holds value in general.[4]

A further objection is historical manipulation: a de facto gold standard operated in Great Britain during the 18th century before being overturned by a bimetal standard of silver and gold, and when silver was later fully demonetized an official gold standard was implemented — coinciding with one of the worst depressions in US history, the [[long-depression|Long Depression]] beginning in 1873.[4] The essay's conclusion is that whether a money system is gold-backed, silver-backed, crypto-based, or made of leather skins, it is only as good as the people who use it; in an author's note Deschamps clarifies that gold-backed money has real benefits and that a return to some form of gold standard is worth considering, but is not a silver bullet.[4]

## Abandonment and the petrodollar (November 2022)

Joe Lange's essay "Money and Oil" attributes the United States' departure from the gold standard to the same central banks he says control the world's money supply, describing gold as "the only real money in the world for thousands of years" whose removal as backing enabled the creation of the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]].[5] With the dollar as the world's reserve currency, those banks could then require all payments for [[oil|oil]] to be made in dollars, producing a fiat and energy feedback loop.[5]

Lange presents a return to the gold standard as the intended endpoint of [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s strategy against the central banks, citing a Q post that he reads as revealing a plan to destroy the global debt system and restore gold backing.[5] He also notes that [[russia|Russia]] responded to Western sanctions by demanding payment for its oil and gas in rubles or gold, which strengthened the ruble.[5]

## The case for restoration (May 2023)

In "The Midas Touch," Joe Lange argues that returning to the gold standard would restore value in the currency, prevent inflation, encourage savings, restore trust in banking, prevent deficit spending and decentralize the global financial system.[6] Gold, he writes, is the key to decentralizing the entire global debt system.[6] He cites [[alan-greenspan|Alan Greenspan]]'s observation that under the gold standard a free banking system protects an economy's stability and balanced growth, and that in its absence there is no way to protect savings from confiscation through inflation.[6]

Lange presents [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as a long-standing gold advocate, noting that in September 2011 Trump accepted gold rather than dollars as a rent deposit from a new tenant, saying it was "a sad day when a large property owner starts accepting gold instead of the dollar" and blaming the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s money printing and the Obama administration's fiscal policies.[6] Trump's transition-era consultations with [[judy-shelton|Judy Shelton]] and [[john-allison|John Allison]] of the [[cato-institute|Cato Institute]] are read as further evidence of intent.[6]

## Signals of a coming restoration

Lange assembles several developments as convergent signals: Treasury Secretary [[steven-mnuchin|Steven Mnuchin]]'s 2017 visit to [[fort-knox|Fort Knox]]; widespread [[gold-repatriation|gold repatriation]] by foreign central banks; record [[central-bank-gold-buying|central bank gold buying]]; and the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]] reclassification of gold as a tier 1 asset in 2019.[6]

## The state-level movement

Within the United States, 43 states had ended sales taxes on gold and silver by 2023, a campaign backed by the [[sound-money-defense-league|Sound Money Defense League]]; the seven holdouts were New Mexico, Hawaii, Wisconsin, Kentucky, Maine, New Jersey and Vermont.[6] Arkansas became the fourth state — after Utah, Wyoming and Oklahoma — to make gold and silver legal tender through the [[arkansas-legal-tender-act|Arkansas Legal Tender Act]], while the [[tenth-amendment-center|Tenth Amendment Center]] reported 23 states developing similar rules.[6] Texas built the [[texas-bullion-depository|Texas Bullion Depository]], opened in 2018, and Tennessee moved to follow with its own depository and a law authorizing state gold and silver reserves signed by Governor [[bill-lee|Bill Lee]].[6]

Lange grounds the movement in constitutional text — "no state shall … make anything but gold and silver coin a tender in payment of debts" — and in Dean Clancy's reading that Federal Reserve Notes are forbidden "bills of credit."[6] He anticipates a [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] showdown overturning the [[legal-tender-cases|Legal Tender Cases]] and [[gold-clause-cases|Gold Clause Cases]] and returning the country to gold.[6]
