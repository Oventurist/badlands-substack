# Gold Standard

The gold standard is the monetary regime the article dates as spanning from 1873 to 1933 and holds up as superior to the [[fiat-currency|fiat]] system that replaced it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The [[coinage-act-of-1873|Coinage Act of 1873]] ended the coinage of silver and put the American economy on the gold standard for the first time, causing deflation, which increased the buying power of America's currency.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Under this regime, the article argues, citizens could demand their gold back from bankers — the one mechanism capable of stopping [[fractional-reserve-lending|fractional reserve lending]] — and so getting off the gold standard became a priority of the bankers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Bankers are said to have hated the gold standard so much that from 1873 they began calling for the government to permanently adopt a fiat currency issued by a central bank, because the gold standard forced them into a siloed depression while citizens flourished.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) That period was labelled the [[long-depression|Long Depression]] by bankers, though [[murray-rothbard|Murray Rothbard]] and [[david-ames-wells|David Ames Wells]] are cited to argue it was a period of rising real wages and productivity.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

In the modern era, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] nominated [[judy-shelton|Judy Shelton]] to the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] because she advocated bringing back the gold standard.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The article argues that in light of the reckless money printing prompted by the Covid-19 crisis, every currency in the world is becoming less valuable and there must be a reversal mirroring what happened in 1873: "We need a new deflationary period."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

A later Badlands Media essay places the gold standard among four financial initiatives it attributes to Trump, alongside a digital dollar, a Postal Banking [[public-banking-option|public option]] and the creation of [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) In that account, Americans holding accounts denominated in "pure sovereign money" issued by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] rather than fiat Federal Reserve Notes would no longer require [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance]], and the country is described as going back to a gold standard that is redeemable.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Gold-backed digital money

In "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", Joe Lange extends the gold-standard argument to digital currency, writing that his previous article documented not only the United States but the entire world returning to gold, with central banks buying record amounts of it.[3] He argues that a [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDC]] is merely a digital [[fiat-currency|fiat]] currency unless it is backed by gold, which he reads the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] as requiring.[3]

Because states are making gold and silver coins legal tender and money will be "redeemable," he rejects the claim that cash will be abolished.[3] He notes that the majority of countries have repatriated their gold and are buying more to back their own currencies, that the [[brics|BRICS]] bloc plans a new currency backed by gold and other commodities, and that China's [[shanghai-petroleum-and-natural-gas-exchange|Shanghai Petroleum and Natural Gas Exchange]] already allows oil sold in yuan to be converted into gold.[3] Restoring the standard, in his account, requires the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to revisit the Legal Tender and Gold Clause decisions.[3]
