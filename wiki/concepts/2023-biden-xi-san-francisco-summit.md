# 2023 Biden–Xi San Francisco Summit

The 2023 Biden–Xi summit was a bilateral meeting between U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] held on Wednesday, November 15, 2023, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in San Francisco.[1] Anticipation of the meeting dominated the news cycle in the preceding days, with two principal deliverables reported in advance: the restoration of military-to-military communication channels, and a joint pledge restricting the use of [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] in weapons systems.[1]

## Military-to-military contacts

Speaking on Sunday, November 12, White House national security advisor [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] said Biden would ask Xi at the summit to restore military-to-military contacts that Beijing had severed the previous year.[1] China formally cut off regular armed forces contacts in August 2022 after then-House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] visited Taiwan, a trip Beijing regarded as conferring official sanction on the existence of self-governing Taiwan, which China claims as part of its territory.[1]

An earlier attempt to revive the channels had failed: in June 2023, China rebuffed Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]]'s request to resume contacts, though Blinken said he had repeatedly asked Beijing to do so.[1] Sullivan characterized obtaining Chinese agreement as "a priority for President Biden," who "believes that having military-to-military communication is necessary to manage competition responsibly, and to ensure that competition does not turn into conflict."[1] Sullivan added that regular contacts can avoid "mistakes or miscalculations or miscommunication."[1]

## Artificial intelligence pledge

Reporting attributed to two sources familiar with the matter indicated that Biden and Xi were poised to announce a pledge banning the use of artificial intelligence in autonomous weaponry, such as drones, and in the control and deployment of nuclear warheads.[1] The potential dangers of AI were expected to be a major focus of the discussions, with both Washington and Beijing having expressed concern over unregulated use of the technology to fuel conflicts.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated both tracks of the summit skeptically. On the military-contacts item, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the news as a springboard to discuss the "China Joe" moniker applied to Biden, arguing that while the nickname was fitting, the Biden administration was "completely hemmed in on the game board" by the [[devolution|Devolution]] operation, and positing a [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] including nationalist interests in China headed by Xi working to destabilize the globalist [[deep-state|Deep State]] from within.[1]

On the AI pledge, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned the premise itself — "Wait, was that a serious consideration at some point? Who was advocating for AI to control nuclear launch codes?" — and predicted that Xi would find a way to weaponize the agreement against the Biden administration.[1] The commentary noted the dissonance between a year of reporting that Xi and Biden were at odds amid crisis-level superpower tension and the sudden announcement of a landmark accord, and closed with the observation that the writer was "damn near more comfortable with AI controlling the launch codes than with Biden having them," asking who actually holds the nuclear football.[1] The item also referenced the designation of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] as the administration's AI czar as evidence of how seriously the U.S. claimed to be taking the AI threat.[1]
