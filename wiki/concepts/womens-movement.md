# Women's Movement

The Women's Movement of the 1960s and early 1970s is treated in a December 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] as one of the principal social forces that produced the [[latchkey-generation|Latchkey Generation]].[1] Carlson describes it as "a movement led by CIA assets like [[gloria-steinem|Gloria Steinem]]" that encouraged women to leave their husbands and enter the workforce.[1]

According to the essay, the movement devalued the traditional family structure: the desire to attain a traditional family ceased to be a goal for a growing number of Americans, and stay-at-home mothers came to be viewed as second-class citizens by many within the movement.[1] Carlson links this shift to rising divorce rates — from 2.5 per 1,000 Americans in the 1950s to 3.2 by the end of the 1960s, with roughly half of marriages begun in the 1970s ending in divorce.[1]

## Economic critique

Carlson's core economic claim is that the mass influx of women into the workforce in the late 1960s and early 1970s drove up the cost of living, especially housing, to the point that families who preferred a single-earner model were forced into two incomes simply to make ends meet.[1] He argues the Women's Movement benefited women least, just as the [[civil-rights-movement|Civil Rights Movement]] benefited Black Americans least, and that the real beneficiaries were the banking, real estate, retail, and service industries, for whom second incomes justified higher prices.[1]

He asserts that both movements were heavily orchestrated by the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[cia|CIA]] and that "there was nothing organic about either of them," while clarifying that he does not dispute that every human being deserves equal treatment — only that these particular movements were, in his framing, [[deep-state|Cabal]]-run psyops.[1]
