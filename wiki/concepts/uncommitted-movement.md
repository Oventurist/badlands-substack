# Uncommitted Movement

The "uncommitted" movement was a protest-vote campaign that emerged during the 2024 Democratic presidential primary, urging voters to select "uncommitted" rather than endorse the Biden–Harris ticket over the administration's handling of the Israel–Hamas war.[1]

## Origins and composition

The movement began in Michigan, where more than 100,000 voters cast uncommitted ballots in protest of the Biden–Harris administration.[1] Its base was composed of Arab-Americans, Muslims, young voters, and far-left progressives, concentrated in Wayne County communities such as [[hamtramck|Hamtramck]] and Dearborn.[1] Badlands Media identified Congresswoman [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]], a founding member of "The Squad," as the figure who popularized the movement.[1]

## Drift toward Trump in late 2024

By September 2024 the movement appeared, at least anecdotally, to be leaning toward [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], with Hamtramck Mayor [[amer-ghalib|Amer Ghalib]] holding a "very productive" meeting with Trump in Flint and weighing an endorsement.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because ending the war in Gaza was the movement's paramount issue — and because that war commenced and escalated under [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s watch — uncommitted Free Palestine activists voting in their own self-interest would plausibly break for Trump, even without public endorsements.[1]

## Origins in the February 2024 Michigan primary

The movement's decisive moment came ahead of Michigan's February 27, 2024 presidential primary, when Rep. [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] urged residents to vote "uncommitted" in protest of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s handling of the Israel–Hamas war.[2] In a video posted by the group [[listen-to-michigan|Listen to Michigan]] — a coalition of more than 30 Michigan officials pledging uncommitted ballots to demand a ceasefire — Tlaib said residents felt "completely neglected and just unseen by our government."[2] Democrats feared the protest could damage Biden in a battleground state with one of the country's largest Arab American and Muslim populations, which was crucial to his 2020 win.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the tactic as designed to prove that the Palestinian-aligned contingent could be outcome-determinative in November, and read it as part of a broader unravelling of the Democratic coalition alongside union and climate-activist defections.[2]
