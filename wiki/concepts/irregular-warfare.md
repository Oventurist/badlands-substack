# Irregular Warfare

**Irregular warfare** is defined in United States joint doctrine as "a violent struggle among state and non-state actors for legitimacy and influence over the relevant populations."[1] In the Badlands Media article of the same name, author Joe Lange uses the doctrinal term as the organizing framework for his claim that the United States has been engaged in a real, ongoing war since January 2017, fought not against an army on a battlefield but against an insurgency embedded inside the federal government and other institutions.[1]

## The framing

Lange argues that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has been a "wartime president" from the moment he was first sworn into office in 2017, waging that war with the assistance of military intelligence against an embedded insurgency directed by foreign patrons.[1] He names [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] as the principal "puppet master" who, in his account, controlled the American insurgency — including the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] — at the time Trump took office, promising a separate article laying out that evidence.[1] Because the enemy is described as infiltrating rather than invading, the conflict is characterized as "infiltration, not invasion," and therefore as irregular rather than conventional warfare.[1]

## The narrative battlefield

Within this framework the most important front is the "narrative war" for the hearts and minds of the population, in which the media is treated as one of the enemy's most important weapons.[1] The article cites [[qanon|Q]] drop 3369 (July 8, 2019), which asserts that the job of a media 90 percent owned by six corporations is not to inform the public with facts but to paint a narrative designed to "keep the sheep in-line."[1] Trump's early and sustained attacks on the press as "Fake News" and the "enemy of the people" are presented not as temperament but as a pre-planned strategy to contest that battlefield, one credited with driving public trust in the media to record lows.[1] Q drop 3662 (December 7, 2019) — "Knowledge is power. Think for yourself... THE GREAT AWAKENING" — is offered as evidence that the Q operation itself was a narrative-warfare instrument.[1]

Lange also credits selective disclosure through trusted independent outlets, singling out journalist [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] for exposing corruption inside the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration.[1]

## Deception and "slow walking"

A recurring premise is Sun Tzu's maxim that "all warfare is based on deception," which Lange uses to argue that appearances inside the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and [[fbi|FBI]] have been deliberately misleading.[1] He points to Q drop 1711 (July 26, 2018), which asks whether Trump would allow a takeover of "his DOJ" if nothing were being done behind the scenes, and Q drop 1318 (May 8, 2018), which asks why [[jeff-sessions|Sessions]] and [[christopher-wray|Wray]] would be cleaning house if they were themselves "DC SWAMP/DEEP STATE," and why documents were being "slow walked."[1] The answer proposed is that withheld material — including the 37 pages of FBI memos on [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] identified in Solomon's 2018 reporting for The Hill — is evidence in an ongoing grand jury and RICO process and therefore cannot lawfully be released.[1]

## Military intelligence jurisdiction

The article argues that because the insurgency had captured the civilian intelligence agencies, Trump used his constitutional authority to give military intelligence jurisdiction over the resulting criminal cases, invoking Q drop 2937 (March 3, 2019) on "plausible deniability," cleaning house first, and "umbrella surveillance."[1] [[michael-flynn|General Michael Flynn]] and [[mike-rogers|Admiral Mike Rogers]] are described as assisting prosecutor [[john-huber|John Huber]] and Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]] in the RICO investigations Sessions launched in 2017.[1] Q drop 17 (October 31, 2017) is quoted for the proposition that military intelligence can be applied to prosecute bad actors while avoiding corrupt agencies and judges.[1]

## The Marines as the "trump card"

A substantial section develops the claim that the [[united-states-marine-corps|Marine Corps]] is uniquely available to the president. The article quotes the U.S. Naval Institute noting that Congress in 1798 provided the Corps should be prepared "for any other duty on shore that the President may direct," language echoed in the National Security Act of 1947.[1] Combined with the [[war-powers-act|War Powers Act]] of 1973 and declared national emergencies, Lange concludes Trump can deploy Marines domestically or abroad without congressional approval or a governor's permission, and suggests this authority explains both border-wall construction by the military and the decline of Antifa and BLM street violence.[1] Q drop 14 (October 31, 2017), which asks what authority the president has over the Marines if Soros-funded operations turn violent, is cited in support.[1]

The Marine connection is then applied to [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]], a Marine officer who served in Vietnam: because Article 2 of the Uniform Code of Military Justice retains jurisdiction over retired officers drawing retirement pay, Lange speculates Mueller faced the prospect of court martial and therefore sought a deal.[1]

## Reception and status

The article is explicitly interpretive, built on Q drops, quotations from The Hill, the Los Angeles Times, the Rush Limbaugh Show, and the U.S. Naval Institute, and Badlands Media notes that contributor opinions do not necessarily represent the outlet's views.[1] Its central claims — an ongoing eight-year RICO investigation, flipped cooperating witnesses at the top of the DOJ, and military-intelligence jurisdiction over domestic corruption cases — are not established in mainstream reporting and should be treated as the author's thesis rather than documented fact.
