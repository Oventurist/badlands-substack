# Situmorang v. Google

Situmorang v. Google is a proposed Canadian class action alleging that [[google]] collected facial biometric data without consent through Google Photos. In January 2024 the British Columbia Court of Appeal revived the case, ruling that plaintiff Yeremia Situmorang could return to the B.C. Supreme Court to seek certification of his class action against the tech giant.[1]

## Allegations

Situmorang filed a civil claim alleging that pictures of his children taken on his Android phone were automatically uploaded to Google Photos without his consent.[1] A court document records his argument that "facial biometric data is intrinsically sensitive personal information, akin to an individual's DNA or fingerprints," and that collecting such data without consent constitutes a violation of privacy.[1] The action is brought on behalf of anyone whose facial biometric identifiers were extracted and collected via Google Photos, including both users and non-users of the cloud-based image-storage platform.[1] The case was reported by [[activist-post|The Activist Post]].[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[simon-esler]] argued that, given the history of tech giants such as Facebook being tied to DARPA and of government agencies seeking new ways to weaponise personal data, the suit carried deep implications.[1] He noted that the case was ignited by a father who realised his children's faces were being collected and analysed without his consent, and that it aligned with a similar suit filed by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton]] making essentially the same accusation.[1] Esler observed a double edge to the underlying technology: facial-recognition advances had enabled deep-fake tools used in meme warfare against corrupt officials, while law-enforcement operations had also caught offenders harvesting images of children to generate child sexual abuse material for a growing deep-fake pornography market.[1] He concluded that the public would need to be deeply involved in the lawfare unfolding around these technologies and their novel ethical dilemmas.[1]
