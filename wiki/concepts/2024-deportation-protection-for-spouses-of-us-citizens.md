# 2024 Deportation Protection for Spouses of US Citizens

On June 18, 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] announced that his administration would not deport many illegal immigrants who are married to U.S. citizens, or whose parent is married to a U.S. citizen.[1]

## Scope of the program

The [[white-house|White House]] said approximately 500,000 spouses of U.S. citizens, along with about 50,000 children, would be shielded from deportation and given the ability to gain legal status without leaving the United States. The process required illegal immigrant adults to have lived in the United States for at least ten years as of June 17, 2024, and to be married to a U.S. citizen. The [[department-of-homeland-security|U.S. Department of Homeland Security]] was designated to manage the program.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the announcement as a fabricated policy win, arguing that spouses of U.S. citizens and their children are not a major concern of immigration critics and that such spouses were widely assumed already to be immune from deportation.[1] She raised the question of scope creep in the term "children" — noting that if a 70-year-old immigrant marries a 70-year-old U.S. citizen, the protection could extend to adult offspring in their forties and fifties.[1]

The broader critique held that promising not to deport roughly 450,000 U.S. citizens' spouses and stepchildren was a winning political position for a problem that did not exist, since those people were not going to be deported anyway, while potentially incentivizing marriage among illegal immigrants in vulnerable situations. The commentary closed by asking what was being done about the millions of military-aged males who had entered the country, and whose vote the president was courting with the measure.[1]
