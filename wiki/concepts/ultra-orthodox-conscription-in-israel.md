# Ultra-Orthodox Conscription in Israel

Ultra-Orthodox conscription refers to the long-running conflict in Israel over whether Haredi yeshiva students must serve in the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] on the same terms as other Israeli citizens.[1] The dispute escalated sharply in mid-2024 when the state moved to begin issuing recruitment orders to ultra-Orthodox men.[1]

## Sephardic rabbinical ban, July 2024

Senior Sephardic rabbis in Israel instructed yeshiva students to ignore any IDF recruitment orders received in the following weeks and banned them from reporting to recruitment offices at all — even for the purpose of seeking an exemption, according to Hebrew media reports.[1] The rabbinical leadership's decision stemmed from anger over the plan to begin issuing draft orders to ultra-Orthodox men the following month, and over proposed legislation before the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee that would introduce binding recruitment targets.[1] In their letter the rabbis claimed the targets would bring about "the destruction of the Torah" and the ruin of "the souls of a holy people."[1]

## Commentary and historical framing

Badlands Media commentary situated the standoff within a longer history of Jewish communal self-governance, arguing that parallel rabbinical courts instructing communities to disregard secular law had historically generated friction with host states in Europe and contributed to expulsions.[1] The writer connected this to Catherine the Great's creation of the [[pale-of-settlement|Pale of Settlement]] in Russia, where rabbinic courts became powerful and, in the writer's view, corrupt authorities — a corruption that in turn spurred the rise of Orthodox Judaism in the late 1700s as a parallel community within the wider Jewish community.[1]

In this reading, the 2024 confrontation replays that dynamic inside the Jewish state itself, with Orthodox rabbinic authorities directing their followers to defy state authorities who are themselves Jewish, after those authorities ruled the Haredi exemption from IDF service invalid.[1] The commentary further noted the historic rivalry between the Orthodox and the Revisionist current that became the Zionist movement.[1]

## Related military strains

The commentary paired the conscription revolt with reports of potential mutinies within the IDF, describing a senior officer corps that believed [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had placed it in an impossible position to declare military victory and that favoured a truce even if it left Hamas in power.[1] Former IDF leaders were also reported to be speaking out publicly against Netanyahu, claiming he had been baited by [[hamas|Hamas]] into invading Gaza.[1]
