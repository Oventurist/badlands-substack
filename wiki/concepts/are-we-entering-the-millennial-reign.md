# Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?

"Are We Entering the Millennial Reign? — Eschaton or Coincidence?" is a [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] feature published on July 24, 2023 by the pseudonymous contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] ("PiP"), whose byline elsewhere carries the sign-off "Shadilay, Pepes!" The piece advances an extended speculative hypothesis that the events described in the [[book-of-revelation|book of Revelation]] are unfolding in the present day, and that [[bitcoin]] — rather than being merely "magic internet money" — may be a material manifestation of biblical prophecy. It closes with a customary editorial disclaimer that Badlands Media articles represent the opinions of their contributing authors.

## Framing and disclaimers

The author opens by acknowledging that Revelation is among the most debated books of the Bible, owing to its symbolism, metaphor, apocalyptic language, prophecy, the historical distance between the first-century writing and the future it describes, cultural difference, "the inadequacy of human language," and millennia of accumulated interpretation. He adds that infiltration, division, misleading and pacification of the church have also intervened. Summing up, he writes that if Revelation had a social media profile, "it's relationship status with humanity would be set to, 'it's complicated,'" and states his single confident conclusion in capitals: "WE ARE ALL WRONG!"

The article repeatedly disclaims prophetic authority. The author states he is not claiming to be right, not claiming anything will happen, and not claiming anything in the piece is a requirement for salvation, which he identifies with a personal relationship with Jesus Christ per Romans 10:9. The exercise is framed as an invitation to think outside the box and think critically about matters he calls inconsequential to salvation. Whether the world is "marching through 'the Eschaton'" or merely passing through another cycle of history, he writes, it is hard for him to conclude the present times are anything other than "Biblical."

## Background on Revelation

For readers unfamiliar with the text, the article notes that Revelation was written by [[john-the-apostle|the apostle John]], author also of the Gospel of John and 1–3 John, who had been exiled to the island of Patmos as a death sentence for his faith — a light sentence, the author observes, compared with other apostles such as Peter, who was crucified upside down. He argues that biblical prophecy has historically unfolded in a manner similar to the [[q|Q drops]] — "future proves past" — with some prophecies still awaiting fulfillment, and that believers should continually assess the complete picture of world events against biblical prophecy and warnings.

## The Bitcoin thesis

The author states that he is "deep down a variety of rabbit holes (Bible, Q, Bitcoin, Pepe, Trump, etc.)" and is applying a Systems Engineering approach to see how they fit together. The hypothesis, he explains, grew out of a season of seeking God while trying to reconcile the scale of evil in the world — which he calls demonstrably as bad as biblical descriptions of Sodom and Gomorrah — with commonly taught doctrines such as the pre-Tribulation [[rapture]] and the [[great-tribulation|Tribulation]]. The governing question is why, if the judgments of Revelation are being poured out, the church has not been raptured. He is careful to state that he neither believes Bitcoin is God nor worships Bitcoin.

The Bitcoin background offered describes the October 31, 2008 release of the Bitcoin White Paper by the pseudonymous [[satoshi-nakamoto|Satoshi Nakamoto]] — a phenomenon known as the "Immaculate Conception" — and the mining of the Genesis block on January 3, 2009. Satoshi mined blocks for six days before releasing the software to the world, helped the network for a few years, then disappeared: an arc the author reads as a metaphor for the six days of creation. He notes that the underlying technologies (TCP/IP, TOR, SHA-256, GPS) were built by the military for nuclear-resistant, wartime application, and cites [[united-states-space-force|United States Space Force]] Major [[jason-lowery|Jason Lowery]]'s thesis [[softwar|Softwar]] in asking whether Bitcoin was released as a military weapon and digital counterinsurgency operation aimed at the root cause of humanity's cycle of destruction and bloodshed: the centralized control of money.

## Reinterpreting the rapture

The article notes that the term "rapture" appears nowhere in the New Testament, and that the concept derives from Paul's use of "be caught up" (Greek *harpazō*, Latin *rapio*) in 1 Thessalonians 4:17, a term meaning "to seize, carry off by force," "to seize on, claim for one's self eagerly," or "to snatch out or away." Other cited occurrences include Matthew 11:12 and 13:19, John 6:15, 10:12 and 10:28–29, Acts 8:39 and 23:10, 2 Corinthians 12:2 and 12:4, Jude 1:23, and Revelation 12:5, with supporting verses at 1 Corinthians 15:51–52, Matthew 24:40–41 and Luke 17:34–36.

While accepting that [[apostle-paul|Paul]] described a real coming event, the author doubts it is synonymous with the church disappearing, since believers appear throughout the eschatological narrative receiving warnings and encouragement. He points to a verse roughly "7/11ths of the way through Revelation" — after the seal and trumpet judgments and just before the Beast and the Mark of the Beast — reading "blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on," and suggests the present may sit near that point on the timeline: "almost over, a lot of pain behind us, a bit more pain ahead, but God wins."

Turning to Matthew 24:36–44, he observes that the passage does not specify whether believers or non-believers are the ones "taken," while insisting the end times will resemble the days of Noah, when the evil was removed by the flood. He therefore proposes that the rapture may be the process or event by which believers endure the Tribulation, resist the Mark of the Beast and enter the [[millennial-reign|Millennial Reign]] — restated bluntly as: "the rapture will remove the evil."

## Eyes like a flame of fire

Describing Christ's appearance at the Second Coming, the author highlights the phrase "eyes like a flame of fire," used also by [[prophet-daniel|Daniel]], and notes that both writers reached for a simile despite knowing exactly what fire looked like. Fire in scripture, he writes, represents purification and judgment — and Bitcoin's decentralized ledger "purifies and judges the fiat slave system's centralized ledger," something previous technology could not do. He then connects the simile to the global adoption of "laser eyes" profile pictures by Bitcoiners, reasoning that if Daniel wrote in roughly 600 BC and John in roughly 80 AD, and lasers were not discovered until 1960, a vision of Christ with laser eyes could only have been rendered as eyes *like* fire.

## The seven churches and the modern church

Of the seven churches addressed in Christ's letters, five (about 71%) are rebuked — for turning from Christ, tolerating false teachings and prophets, immorality, spiritual deadness, lukewarmness, and placing faith in worldly riches — and called to repentance and renewed faithfulness. The author believes today's church exhibits roughly the same breakdown. His leading example is the church's response to the [[covid-19-pandemic|Covid]] mandates: citing an October 2020 report seven months into the pandemic, he notes that "39 percent of Christians now say they would want their congregation to abide by a government shutdown order," even as "an increasing number of Christians say churches should defy restrictions." He observes that while "god-kings" imposed harsh restrictions on churches — even attempting to restrict singing during worship — liquor stores, pot shops and big-brand retailers such as Walmart, Amazon and Home Depot faced fewer restrictions.

## The Tribulation and the death tolls

The article summarizes the debate over whether the Tribulation is a literal seven-year period (from the seventieth week of Daniel 9) or symbolic, noting Revelation's three references to three-and-a-half-year periods (Revelation 11:2–3, 12:6, 13:5), and counts 7 seals, 7 trumpets and 7 bowls — "21 judgements. Kek." The seals are said to symbolize conquest, conflict, bloodshed, warfare, violence, scarcity, economic hardship, famine, poverty, death, pestilence, beasts, suffering, persecution, martyrdom and cosmic upheaval; the trumpets, devastation of natural resources and beasts, contaminated and bitter water, reduced light, increased darkness, spiritual torment, war and death; the bowls, the culmination of God's wrath in more intense and specific final judgments.

For the present era to qualify as the Tribulation, the author says he would need credible evidence of consequences on the scale of the fourth seal and sixth trumpet — the death of one quarter and one third of humanity respectively. The text, he notes, is unclear whether these are the same event within a range or sequential events totalling one half, and whether the deaths are immediate or spread over time. Examining the root words for the weapons given to the rider, the four angels and their army — sword, hunger, death, beasts, fire, smoke, brimstone — he concludes they encompass all types of destruction, warfare, famine, beasts and death. The closest historical comparison offered is the [[black-death|Black Death]], culminating around 1351, which records suggest killed between one tenth and one quarter of the global population.

Against a world population of roughly 8 billion, he asks whether the count should include everyone "who would be here but aren't" as a result of abortion, cancer, sterilization, infertility, destruction of the nuclear family, starvation, thirst, overdose, suicide, murder, war, psyops, genocide, eugenics, slavery and trafficking. Noting that approximately 70% of all people received at least a first dose of the [[covid-19-vaccines|Covid vaccine]], and writing as "a safety professional" horrified by subsequent death and injury data, he asks how many will prove unable to reproduce or will develop lethal conditions over time. He cites [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s warnings about population collapse as a corroborating data point.

## Kim Clement's prophecies

Among the "unique" data points the author adduces are the prophecies of [[kim-clement|Kim Clement]], which he calls remarkably accurate, noting that Clement made prophecies about [[donald-trump|Trump]] becoming president over the course of a decade, likely without ever meeting him; a possible meeting in June 2015 is mentioned but unconfirmed. Clement died on November 23, 2016. The author highlights the prophecy that "Trump shall become a trumpet," asking whether this referenced one of the seven trumpets of Revelation, and finds Clement's mention of a "brilliant plan" that could only be "given by God" of interest.

## The Beast, the Antichrist and the Mark

Discussing Revelation 13, the article distinguishes [[the-beast|the Beast]] from [[antichrist|the Antichrist]], noting the two are often used interchangeably and that being "anti-Christ" (1 and 2 John) is a distinct idea. Paul's "son of lawlessness" in 2 Thessalonians opposes God, exalts himself and performs deceitful signs and wonders, but holds only temporary power. In the Old Testament the concept is not explicit, but Daniel 7:19–27, 9:24–27 and 11:31–45 describe a beast and oppressive ruler who defiles the sanctuary, replaces sacrifices with abomination, corrupts many by flattery, speaks blasphemies and prospers until the appointed time of wrath, before the Ancient of Days removes his dominion and grants the everlasting kingdom to the saints. Ezekiel 28:1–19 addresses the prince of Tyre, who exalted himself as a god despite being a mere man and was cast down for pride, wealth, violence, sin and corruption. In both testaments, the author stresses, believers are present in the world as events unfold.

He concludes that the Beast symbolizes a system of power and control aligned with Satan that positions itself as God and demands worship — "the Beast thinks it's large and in charge" — and declines to focus on identifying a single human face, since the personifications likely describe the spiritual nature of Satan. As for a present-day candidate, he points to "globalist, communist, technocratic, warmongering, evil, perverted, lying god-kings" who have declared themselves rulers of the world and demanded complete compliance.

Regarding [[mark-of-the-beast|the Mark of the Beast]], the author finds the physical aspect the least interesting, since tattoos and similar technologies existed when the prophecy was written. What makes it unique, he argues, is that the Beast requires it of everyone and uses it as a discriminator on participation in the economy — the ability to buy or sell — enforced through fear and coercion. By that test, the vaccine campaign was a near miss: global, forced, fear-driven and tied to vaccine passports as an economic discriminator, but it failed to reach universal coverage. "I'd say it was a close shave!"

The stronger candidate, he argues, is [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]]. Quoting a cited source, he notes that "all 'global citizens' are required to possess proper 'digital identification' or else they will not be permitted to access the new global digital financial system," and that using CBDCs will require proving identity through a new global digital ID system. He restates this as actions underway to implement total control and enslavement, and notes that General [[michael-flynn|Flynn]] has highlighted CBDC data and referred to them as the Mark of the Beast. "Ready or not, they are here!"

## Babylon and the fiat system

The fall of [[babylon]] is read as a more detailed look at the Beast system during the Tribulation. The announcing angel attributes the fall to Babylon's rejection of God and its becoming a dwelling place for demons, a prison for foul spirits and a cage for birds — meaning Babylon falls from within due to unchecked, festering evil, while serving as the vehicle through which the whole world is corrupted. The author sees an uncanny resemblance to today's [[fiat-currency|fiat slave system]], with the kings of the earth who "sleep with the Beast" enriching themselves and then lamenting as their earthly treasures are rendered worthless. He notes that so far everything goes to zero against Bitcoin over time, and finds it unique that Bitcoin is the first tool allowing treasures to be stored "in heaven where moths and vermin do not destroy, and thieves do not break in and steal" (Matthew 6:19–21) — while conceding that cyberspace is not heaven and that the verse concerns the alignment of hearts and priorities. The explicit mention of "1 hour" in the Babylon passage prompts him to ask whether this is where the concept of a "god-candle" comes into play. He closes the section on Babylon's description as a global marketplace for every kind of food, drink, trinket, commodity and service — "even bodies and souls."

## The Millennial Reign

The concept of the thousand-year reign, the article notes, derives from the last section of Revelation together with Isaiah 2:1–4 and 11:1–10, Zechariah 14:9, Matthew 25:31–46 and Luke 19:11–27. Examining Christ's returning name, the author observes that He has "a name written that no one knows except Himself" — so that "in the end, Christ comes back as Anonymous. Wait, Christ is an Anon? Anon like Satoshi? Anon like Q? Anon like Pepe? Kek." The known name is "The Word of God," the Logos, the Truth — and Bitcoin, he argues, purifies and judges the Beast system with a language and an immutable truth, which is perhaps why Bitcoiners are said to sound like they are speaking in tongues.

In the establishing of the Millennial Reign, an angel descends with a key and a chain and physically restrains Satan for 1,000 years, after which Satan cannot deceive the nations because a foundation of truth has been established. The author finds it strange that Bitcoin physically restrains the god-kings — again citing Lowery's *[[softwar|Softwar]]* — using public and private keys and a decentralized ledger known as the timechain. He asks whether CBDCs are the Mark of the Beast and whether the world is marching into the battle of Armageddon, and notes that scripture reserves the Millennial Reign for those who do not worship the Beast, his image, or receive the Mark.

## Conclusion

The article closes by hoping readers will ask questions they would otherwise never have asked, dig where they would never have dug, and love where they would never have loved, while reiterating that these matters are inconsequential to salvation. In a joking aside, the author notes he could have written more but suspects [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] would fire him "or at the very least suggest an untimely and happenstance meeting with his wolf," to which an editor's note replies: "this is correct. PiP has been warned." It ends: "Let's bring Christ and Bitcoin to the world! Hodl the line, relinquish nothing, advance when possible! God Bless, Patriots!"
