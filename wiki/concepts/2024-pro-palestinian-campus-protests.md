# 2024 Pro-Palestinian Campus Protests

The 2024 pro-Palestinian campus protests were a wave of encampments and demonstrations that swept prestigious United States universities in April 2024 following mass arrests at [[columbia-university|Columbia University]], driven by opposition to Israel's war with [[hamas|Hamas]] in [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## Course of events

More than 100 demonstrators camped on Columbia's green were arrested in mid-April 2024, after which similar encampments sprouted at universities around the country.[1] On Monday, April 22, Columbia canceled in-person classes, dozens of protesters were arrested at [[new-york-university|New York University]] and [[yale-university|Yale University]], and [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] closed the gates to Harvard Yard to the public.[1]

At NYU the encampment swelled to hundreds of protesters through the day; the school warned the crowd to leave and then called police after the scene became disorderly and it received reports of "intimidating chants and several antisemitic incidents," with arrests beginning shortly after 8:30 p.m.[1] NYU law student Byul Yoon called the university's decision to allow police arrests on campus "a really outrageous crackdown."[1]

Universities were described as struggling with where to draw the line between allowing free expression and maintaining safe and inclusive campuses.[1]

## UCLA "liberated zone" and escalation to violence

By May 1, 2024, far-left activists at [[ucla|UCLA]] had declared the campus a "liberated zone," erecting barriers, posting guards, and creating makeshift checkpoints that required a special wristband for passage, thereby refusing entry to many students not affiliated with the pro-Gaza protests.[2] Observers compared the arrangement to the Seattle CHAZ encampment of 2020 and noted that selectively barring students is not a protected form of protest or free speech, which is why many campuses had police remove demonstrators — whereas UCLA appeared to support the activists and allowed them to take control.[2] The blockade triggered an all-out brawl in which weapons were used and firecrackers were fired at activist tents, with no substantial police presence visible; it was unclear whether the fighting was between pro-Gaza and pro-Israel groups or simply reflected students enraged by the blockade.[2]

The same day, the House of Representatives passed the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] 320–91 as the protests roiled colleges nationwide, tying the campus wave directly to federal legislation.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the protests as an "Ouroboros pattern" — a movement devouring itself — spinning out of what Badlands calls the Crisis Cascade, and threatening the culture-setting paradigm of the ideology said to control the nation.[1] In this reading, protests framed as pro-Palestinian shifted subtly into anti-Israel demonstrations, and establishment narrative-setters found themselves unable to corral "the sociopolitical beast they cultivated, pampered and unleashed during the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] Administration."[1] With Trump out of formal power yet still prosecuted, the activists had no sanctioned target for the tension instilled in them and began turning on their masters' own stories.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked the campus unrest to the radicalizing effect of the Gaza bombardment, arguing that the sentiment generated by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s campaign would spread beyond Palestine and into the region — "just look at what is happening right now on American campuses."[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked the proliferation of autonomous zones — "So many Chazzes!" — and described the movement as "Gaza Floyd," a state-sanctioned rebellion season and a more inclusive reboot of the 2020 Summer of Love in which [[antifa]] traded black bloc attire for keffiyeh and makeshift thobes worn as face coverings while destroying property and harassing passersby.[2] She noted students nationwide participating in the Muslim call to prayer and privileged demonstrators demanding humanitarian aid while occupying Ivy League campus buildings, and asked pointedly what would happen when the activists left campus.[2]

## Late-April escalation and the Harvard encampment (2024)

By the week of April 22, 2024, colleges and universities across the country were struggling to keep pro-Palestinian protesters off school grounds as students, and by some accounts a mix of professional demonstrators funded by radical groups and foreign money, stormed campuses and set up encampments.[3] Coverage described the wave as likely a coordinated nationwide effort, noting that pro-Palestinian demonstrators had also been shutting down critical infrastructure including airport terminals, bridges and other chokepoints capable of disrupting the economy.[3]

Protesters entered [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] and established an encampment in Harvard Yard, days after the university restricted access to the Yard to Harvard ID holders, demanding that the school divest from Israel's war in Gaza.[3] On the Monday night preceding, police arrested protesters at New York University, ending a multiday standoff.[3]

## Faculty reaction and the coddling thesis

Badlands commentary connected the unrest to the arguments of the documentary *The Coddling of the American Mind*, screened by the Toronto chapter of [[heterodox-academy|Heterodox Academy]], which follows college students who became ideologically captured and later recovered.[3] Professors attending the screening described watching students at their institutions descend into what they viewed as ideological madness, citing as a shared example a sudden spike in students presenting accommodation letters demanding extra time on assignments.[3]

The writer attributed this to the implantation of what the filmmakers call the Three Great Untruths: that what does not kill you makes you weaker, that one should always trust one's feelings, and that life is a battle between good people and evil people.[3] The conclusion drawn was that while youth were being targeted by sophisticated fifth-generation psychological operations, the countervailing effect was an awakening among academics, and that organizations defending viewpoint diversity should expect an influx of members as the scale of student ideological capture became apparent.[3]
