# New England Secessionist Plot

The New England secessionist plot was an early nineteenth-century design, described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] for Badlands Media, to break the United States into two confederacies — a northern Anglo-Saxon federation merged with British Canada and a southern slave confederacy — both bound by a special relationship with England and dominated by the [[city-of-london|City of London]]'s economic web of finance.[1]

## Origins

Although slavery had been nearly extinguished by 1792, forces loyal to the British Empire within the eastern establishment, led by [[timothy-pickering|Timothy Pickering]], [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]], Col. [[james-wilkinson|James Wilkinson]], [[george-cabot|George Cabot]] and Albert Gallatin, worked to advance a break-up of the republic under the guise that slave states and free states could not co-exist.[1] Rather than continue the struggle to abolish slavery by imposing the authority of the Constitution, the plotters argued that the nation and constitution should be dissolved completely.[1] By 1784 six states had voted to abolish slavery outright — Rhode Island in 1774, Vermont in 1777, Pennsylvania in 1780, Massachusetts in 1781, New Hampshire in 1783 and Connecticut in 1784 — the importation of new slaves was banned by every other state by 1793, and the Northwest Ordinances of the 1787 Continental Congress barred slavery from the Northwest Territories.[1]

## The 1803 plan

After Burr's defeat by [[thomas-jefferson|Thomas Jefferson]] in 1800 left him a lame-duck vice president without federal leverage, a new plot took shape in 1803 that required Burr's control of New York State as the economic powerhouse needed to fuse the free states into British Canada.[1] Pickering's December 24, 1803 letter to Senator Richard Peters anticipated "a new confederacy, exempt from the corrupt and corrupting influence and oppression of the aristocratic Democrats of the South," in which "the white and black population will mark the boundary" and the British Provinces would join the Northern confederacy with the assent of Britain.[1] Senator [[william-plumer|William Plumer]] recorded a meeting at his house in the winter of 1803–04 with Senator James Hillhouse, Burr, and Senator Uriah Tracy at which Burr indicated a separation was both coming and necessary.[1]

## Collapse

The plan hinged on Burr's 1804 victory as Governor of New York, and [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] again devoted all of his energy to defeating him, producing 28,000 votes for Burr against 35,000 for Morgan Lewis.[1] Without New York, northern secession could not succeed, and the plotters were forced to concoct a new strategy — leading in turn to the western scheme of the [[burr-conspiracy|Burr conspiracy]].[1] The enraged Burr, in the wake of his gubernatorial defeat, organized the duel that killed Hamilton on July 12, 1804.[1]
