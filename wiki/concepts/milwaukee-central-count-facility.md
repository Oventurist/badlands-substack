# Milwaukee Central Count Facility

The Central Count facility is the centralized site at which the city of Milwaukee, Wisconsin processes absentee ballots on election night. It became a focal point of disputes over the 2020 presidential election in Wisconsin.[1]

## 2020 election night

Because Wisconsin law requires absentee ballots to be processed on election day, Milwaukee tabulated its absentee vote centrally rather than at individual polling places, producing a large, late-reported bloc of results.[1] Commentary in the Badlands Brief characterized the resulting late-night reporting as the delivery of "just the margin needed at 3am" by then-[[milwaukee-election-commission|Milwaukee Election Commission]] director [[claire-woodall-vogg|Claire Woodall-Vogg]], and referred to an alleged 4:30 a.m. infusion of roughly 138,000 votes.[1]

Two documentary threads recur in accounts of the facility. The first is a flash drive containing absentee ballot tabulation data that Woodall-Vogg said had been left at Central Count before being retrieved and transported by police.[1] The second is a sworn affidavit attributed to Milwaukee County Election Commission employee [[david-j-bolter|David J. Bolter]] describing a November 4, 2020 announcement about the arrival of additional ballots.[1]

## 2026 federal inquiry

In May 2026 it was reported that the [[fbi|FBI]] was preparing to interview Milwaukee police officers — as many as 30 — about activities at Central Count during election-night ballot processing, with the officers described as potential witnesses rather than investigative targets.[1] Investigators were said to be examining whether police assigned to the facility had been instructed to avoid certain areas while election operations were underway.[1] Milwaukee County Executive [[david-crowley|David Crowley]] and Mayor [[cavalier-johnson|Cavalier Johnson]] called the inquiry "an act of intimidation."[1]

## Significance

Within the framework of [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud claims]], Central Count is treated as one of a set of urban tabulation centers — alongside sites in Georgia, Michigan and Pennsylvania — where late reporting, restricted observation and consultant involvement are alleged to have converged.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] connected the Milwaukee events to [[the-elections-group|The Elections Group]] through Ryan Chew and [[ryan-macias|Ryan Macias]], arguing that the same "sources and methods and actors" appeared across states.[1]
