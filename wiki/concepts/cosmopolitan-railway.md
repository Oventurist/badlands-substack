# Cosmopolitan Railway

The *Cosmopolitan Railway* was the 1890 work of Governor [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]], [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]]'s former bodyguard and a global promoter of rail development.[1] In it Gilpin outlined the extension of rail lines through [[british-columbia|British Columbia]], Alaska, the Bering Strait and across Eurasia — a physical realization of the plan to join the American and Russian continental systems.[1]

The proposal followed directly from the 1863 Russian and American legislation to build telegraph lines connecting both continents through the Bering Strait, and from Lincoln's December 1864 message to Congress describing the "world-encircling communication" that the overland telegraph by way of Bering's Straits and Asiatic Russia would create.[1] Matthew Ehret presents Gilpin's design as the nineteenth-century ancestor of today's [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] rail corridor proposal.[1]

## Gilpin's 1890 vision

In 1890 [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]], friend of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and first governor of Colorado, published *The Cosmopolitan Railway*, outlining his dream of a world of rail networks, industrial progress and cooperation.[2] Gilpin wrote that "the cosmopolitan railway will make the whole world one community" and would "reduce the separate nations to families of our great nation," with extended intercommunication giving rise to "logical and philosophical reciprocities, which will become the germs for innumerable new developments."[2] The article likens this win-win philosophy to Xi Jinping's New Silk Road.[2]

Gilpin observed the common cultural and geographic properties of American and Russian societies, with Russia's Manifest Destiny calling forth a pioneering spirit to "go east" and America's a westward expansion, and argued that both nations could "add a hundredfold to their wealth and power and influence" by spending two or three hundred millions apiece on a highway of the nations through their waste places.[2]
