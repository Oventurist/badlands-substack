# Accelerationism

Accelerationism, in the usage relevant to the Badlands Media corpus, refers to the deliberate hastening of a system's collapse so that a replacement can emerge sooner. The term functions in two registers in the corpus: as a recurring rhetorical sign-off by Badlands commentators ("Accelerate."), and, as of May 2026, as a term reportedly used inside the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration to describe its policy toward [[cuba|Cuba]].

## Use as administration strategy

The Badlands Brief of May 29, 2026 reports that the administration is applying escalating economic pressure to Cuba's government through expanded sanctions, a strategy that a senior administration official described to Axios as "accelerationism," glossed in the reporting as "the philosophy of hastening societal collapse." The stated aim is not immediate regime elimination but the methodical constriction of the Cuban economy in stages, creating conditions for a peaceful transition. Elements of the approach include secondary sanctions on companies doing business with [[gaesa|GAESA]], the cutoff of Venezuelan oil shipments, an indictment of [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]] over the [[brothers-to-the-rescue|Brothers to the Rescue]] shootdowns, more aggressive enforcement of existing sanctions, multiagency planning for potential unrest, and limited humanitarian assistance routed through non-government channels such as the [[catholic-church|Catholic Church]] to demonstrate alternatives to the existing system.

## Use in Badlands commentary

Badlands writers had used "Accelerate" as a slogan well before the Axios report, applying it to the collapse of legacy media credibility, failing lawfare against the administration, and the unravelling of establishment narratives. [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded to the Cuba reporting by joking, "Am I tripping on acid, or has the Trump administration been reading the Badlands News Brief?" In the same issue, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] closed her segment on the failure of Democratic election lawsuits with the same one-word exhortation, illustrating the term's dual function as both analysis and rallying cry.
