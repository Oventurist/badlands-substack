# Financial Services Modernization Act

The Financial Services Modernization Act was the 1999 law that repealed the Depression-era separation of commercial from investment banking established by the [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall Act]].[1] It was authored by Senator [[phil-gramm|Phil Gramm]] of Texas, a Republican celebrated as one of the biggest conservatives in Congress, and signed into law by President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]].[1]

Joe Lange describes the act as rendering useless the safeguard that had protected Americans' savings from Wall Street speculation, allowing investment banks to sell risky securities to the public through commercial banks.[1] The repeal permitted the blockbuster merger of [[citibank|Citibank]] and Travelers, owner of the investment banks Salomon Brothers and Smith Barney, creating [[citigroup|Citigroup]] — a merger the article ties directly to the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]] and to Citigroup's record bailout.[1]
