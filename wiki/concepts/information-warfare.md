# Information Warfare

**Information warfare** is conflict waged over the control, protection, and dissemination of information rather than solely over physical terrain.[1] Because protected information confers competitive and strategic advantage, encryption has functioned as one of its most powerful weapons throughout history.[1] The Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers" traces the discipline from an entirely analog battlefield, invoking [[sun-tzu|Sun Tzu]], through to the digital present.[1]

## Historical case studies

The article surveys five chronological case studies in which codes and ciphers shaped the outcome of contests over power. The parables of [[jesus|Jesus]] are read as cryptographic puzzles decodable only by those in relationship with Him, and as memetic warfare that grew and protected a network simultaneously.[1] The ciphers of [[mary-queen-of-scots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] illustrate the trust problem of centralized power and the vulnerability of intercepted correspondence.[1] The [[culper-spy-ring|Culper Spy Ring]] and its numerical code book protected the identities and communications of insurgents operating inside British colonies under [[george-washington|George Washington]].[1] The [[enigma-machine|Enigma machine]] and the codebreaking work associated with [[alan-turing|Alan Turing]] mark the birth of the computing age.[1] Finally, the [[navajo-code-talkers|Navajo Code Talker]] program of the [[united-states-marine-corps|United States Marine Corps]] demonstrates how an entire language can be weaponized to win a contest of power projection.[1]

## The present conflict

The article situates the current moment as an irregular and asymmetrical [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation war]], in which the sources and methods of information control and dissemination are understandably irregular and widely misunderstood.[1] Within that frame, the use of ciphers and codes by [[qanon|Q]] and by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — notably [[covfefe|Covfefe]] — is presented as entirely expected rather than aberrant.[1] Q drop #1127 is cited for the proposition that communications methods are intentionally different for national security purposes, and drop #1002 for the statements that "symbolism will be their downfall" and that the [[national-security-agency|NSA]] is "the BITE that has no CURE."[1]

Humanity is also described, following Lt. General [[steven-kwast|Steven Kwast]], as transitioning from the Industrial Age to the Network Age, with [[jason-lowery|Jason Lowery]]'s [[softwar|Softwar]] thesis supplying the argument that [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] carries national strategic significance in a new war-fighting domain.[1] The article closes on the maxim that "all warfare is based on deception."[1]

## Treatment in The Werewolf Game

The opening installment of the Badlands Media series [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]], published March 18, 2024, is subtitled "The Hidden Dimensions of Information Warfare" and argues that the fundamental dynamics of information warfare are not yet widely understood, even by people who claim to know how propaganda works.[2] Its central proposition is that an informed minority almost always defeats an uninformed majority in any information-based conflict.[2]

The essay contrasts this with physical warfare, in which the quantity of soldiers has historically mattered most, and notes that it is therefore counterintuitive to many people that other forms of war can easily be won by small numbers against large numbers.[2] Battling an opponent effectively in an information war is said to require a set of perspectives and skills most people never consider, beginning with becoming cognizant of one's own nescience regarding information one does not yet possess.[2]

Among the practical skills the article prescribes are learning to profile psychopaths and psychopathic behaviours, reading micro-expressions that betray lying, and productively analysing seemingly unrelated signs or "comms."[2] The Werewolf Game parlor game itself — said to have been invented by a Russian sociology student precisely to demonstrate how information warfare works — is presented as a teaching tool for these faculties, on the argument that any tactic which helps villagers win the game transfers to real life.[2] The essay further contends that in an information war it is often the most innocent-seeming comms, including fairy tales and mythology, that carry the most information.[2]
