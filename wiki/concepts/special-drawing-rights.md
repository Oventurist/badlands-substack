# Special Drawing Rights

Special Drawing Rights (SDR) are an international monetary reserve instrument created by the [[international-monetary-fund|International Monetary Fund]] in 1969 to operate as a supplement to the existing money reserves of member countries.[1]

## The dollar's weight in the basket

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] lists the SDR basket first among the measures of the U.S. dollar's global reserve currency dominance, noting that the dollar carries the highest weight in the basket and is therefore the most influential component.[1] The other dimensions of dollar dominance he enumerates include its status as the dominant currency for international trade, the leading currency in global financial infrastructure, the pricing currency for major commodities such as oil and gold, the main currency for development financing, bank deposits and corporate borrowing, the primary currency for investing in global equity markets, and the principal safe-haven currency in times of crisis.[1]

## BRICS critique

Because SDR weighting institutionalizes the dollar's primacy, the arrangement features in the [[brics|BRICS]] case for [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] and for greater representation of developing countries within global financial institutions.[1] Russian President Dmitri Medvedev argued at the first BRIC summit that "there can be no successful currency system, and particularly a global system, if the financial instruments that are used are denominated in only one currency."[1]
