# Turkish Fighter Jet Procurement Push

In October 2025 [[turkey|Turkey]] pressed European partners and the United States for ways to obtain advanced fighter jets quickly, seeking to close the gap with regional rivals such as [[israel|Israel]].[1]

## Objectives

A NATO member with the alliance's second-largest military, Turkey aimed to leverage its best relations with the West in years to add 40 Eurofighter Typhoons to its ageing fleet — for which it had signed a preliminary agreement in July 2025 — and later also US-made [[f-35|F-35]] jets, despite Washington sanctions that currently block any such deal.[1]

## Drivers

Israeli strikes on Turkey's neighbours Iran and Syria, as well as on Lebanon and Qatar, unnerved Ankara over the preceding year.[1] Israel, described as the Middle East's most advanced military with hundreds of US-supplied F-15, F-16 and F-35 fighters, laid bare key Turkish vulnerabilities, prompting a push for rapid air power reinforcement so as not to be left exposed to potential threats.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the optics suggested an intended narrative in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had developed a strategic partnership with President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]], signified by the nuclear energy deal the [[white-house|White House]] announced the day before Benjamin Netanyahu addressed the UN General Assembly.[1] That partnership, in his reading, has a security component not fully resolved or disclosed but known to include advanced fighter jets and other equipment, and the resulting agreements appear to concern a balance of power in Syria with Israel on one side and Turkey on the other.[1] He also noted that since the overthrow of Assad in December, Trump has given Erdogan the narrative credit for "conquering" Syria.[1]
