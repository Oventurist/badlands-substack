# Badlands Brief: Clinton Clashes, Royal Trafficking, & Fond WEF Farewells

"Clinton Clashes, Royal Trafficking, & Fond WEF Farewells" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on February 27, 2026. Following the standard format of the series, it pairs block-quoted summaries of mainstream news reporting with commentary from Badlands contributors — in this edition [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. The edition covers the state of US–Iran nuclear negotiations alongside American military deployments in the Middle East, federal grant money awarded to Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]], a dispute between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Representatives [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] and [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]], closed-door congressional testimony by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] regarding [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], the resignation of [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] chief executive [[borge-brende|Børge Brende]], several legal and oversight developments including the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] investigation, and a bonus item on a British review of whether Epstein used [[royal-air-force|Royal Air Force]] bases. The post recorded 151 likes.

## Iran negotiations and regional deployments

The Brief reports that the United States and [[iran|Iran]] concluded a round of indirect nuclear negotiations in Geneva on Thursday without reaching an agreement, with both delegations planning further technical talks in Vienna the following week. Iran's Foreign Ministry delegation and US negotiators met under Omani mediation to discuss Iran's nuclear program and sanctions issues; Oman's foreign minister said the talks showed progress but that no breakthrough deal had been achieved. Citing Iran International, the Brief states that Iran rejected key US demands relating to its nuclear program and continued enrichment, while Iranian officials described the round as unusually long and intense.

In a related item, the Brief notes that the United States has deployed significant military assets to the Middle East during the diplomatic effort, including two aircraft carrier strike groups and more than 100 fighter jets. Separate reporting indicated that US combat aircraft, including eleven F-22 stealth fighters, had been observed at a base in [[israel|Israel]], with the movements documented by satellite imagery. The [[state-department|US State Department]] ordered the departure of nonessential diplomatic staff from Lebanon, citing security concerns tied to broader Iran-related tensions.

In Washington, House Democratic leaders announced plans to force a vote on a war powers resolution intended to limit presidential authority to initiate military action against Iran without congressional approval. Meanwhile, Reuters cited anonymous US intelligence sources familiar with internal assessments to dispute Trump's claim that Iran was close to developing missiles capable of reaching the United States.

Jon Herold's commentary rejects the emerging framing. He writes that "Trump is the President of peace," noting that Trump began no new wars in his first term, has "ended 8 wars so far this term," and campaigned on avoiding foreign wars, a promise Herold says he has kept "outside of some discombobulating." He argues that a coordinated effort is under way to strip Trump of that record and repaint him as a warhawk, pointing to "fake news and anonymously sourced stories," "'Former' CIA agents with 'insider intel' saying we're going to strike Iran any day now," and [[congress|Congress]] "lining up votes on war powers resolution to 'end hostilities' that haven't happened yet." He characterizes these as psychological operations designed to delegitimize Trump and urges readers not to fall for them. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry adds his expectation that "this whole 'Iran War' psyop ends with Trump announcing a major economic trade deal, and that deal becomes the symbolic hallmark of world peace."

## Børge Brende's resignation from the World Economic Forum

The Brief reports that Børge Brende, president and chief executive of the World Economic Forum, stepped down on Thursday after scrutiny of his past interactions with Epstein. Documents and releases tied to the Epstein files showed that Brende had dined with Epstein multiple times and exchanged communications in 2018–2019, though Brende said he had been unaware of Epstein's criminal history. An internal review found no further concerns beyond what had already been disclosed, but Brende said he resigned so that the Forum could continue its work "without distractions." [[alois-zwinggi|Alois Zwinggi]] was named interim chief executive while a permanent successor is sought.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry observes that the sequence began three weeks earlier when Brende requested an internal investigation into himself following the release of the Epstein files, and that he is now resigning outright. He writes that it is "hard to make sense of these developments across Europe, except to note that they continue to unfold and certainly imply that these elites were guilty of something," adding that whatever it was appears "significant enough that they are quitting their jobs, getting arrested, and attempting suicide."

## Arctic Frost, voter rolls, collective bargaining, and the White House ballroom

The Brief reports that the [[fbi|FBI]] subpoenaed the phone records of Kash Patel and [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] — now FBI Director and White House Chief of Staff respectively — as part of the Arctic Frost investigation, at a time when both were private citizens. Those records were found in files labeled "Prohibited," according to Reuters citing statements from Director Patel on Wednesday. Congressional oversight disclosures indicate that hundreds of Trump-aligned individuals and groups, including sitting members of Congress, were targeted with subpoenas and data requests under Arctic Frost, drawing scrutiny over the investigation's scope and methods.

In the courts, the Justice Department filed federal lawsuits on Thursday against Utah, Oklahoma, Kentucky, West Virginia and New Jersey for failing to provide complete voter registration lists as required under federal voting law, bringing the running total to 29 states plus the District of Columbia. Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and the DOJ's [[civil-rights-division|Civil Rights Division]] stated that the actions reflect enforcement of the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] (NVRA) and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] (HAVA) to ensure accurate and transparent voter rolls.

A three-judge panel of the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit]] vacated a lower court's preliminary injunction that had blocked implementation of [[executive-order-14251|Executive Order 14251]], which excludes certain federal agencies from collective bargaining requirements. The order directs that agencies whose primary functions relate to intelligence, counterintelligence, investigative or national security work may be excluded from collective bargaining obligations under the Federal Service Labor-Management Relations Statute. The panel held that the unions challenging the order were unlikely to succeed on the merits of their claim that it constituted retaliation violating the First Amendment, and that the injunction had therefore been wrongly imposed.

Separately, a federal judge declined to issue a preliminary injunction halting construction of Trump's privately funded ballroom at the White House, allowing work to continue while litigation proceeds. The suit had been brought by preservationists and other opponents arguing that the construction violated historic preservation standards. Trump posted on Truth Social that the judge "has just thrown out, and completely erased, the effort to stop its construction," emphasizing that "not one dollar of Taxpayer money is being spent" and that "all money necessary to build this magnificent building is being put up by Patriot Donors and Contributors." He added that the ballroom, anticipated to host future inaugurations and large state visits, was "ahead of schedule, and under budget" and would "stand long into the future as a symbol to the Greatness of America!"

Ashe in America writes that it annoys her that "politicians and bureaucrats are only authentically angry when it's about themselves," but that Arctic Frost "is looking more and more like a reckoning." The FBI secretly pulling phone records for Patel, Wiles and hundreds of other Trump allies is, she argues, "hard to spin as anything other than political targeting – weaponization of government" and "abuse of power." She predicts the media will frame accountability for that abuse as itself an outrageous abuse of power, but insists accountability "is necessary if our nation is to heal," noting that "the hunted have become the hunters." Placing stolen elections at the top of the accountability list, she frames the 29-state voter roll litigation as part of the remedy: "Fake voters are not the full problem, and remedying them is not the full solution, but the machine doesn't work without fake voters. Why do you think states are fighting so hard against transparency?" She closes by noting two other attacks on the president's agenda were thwarted that week — "Collective bargaining in national security agencies is dead, and the big beautiful ballroom is very much alive."
