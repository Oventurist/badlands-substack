# US–Ukraine Bilateral Security Agreement (2024)

The United States and Ukraine were expected to sign a bilateral security pact on the sidelines of the [[2024-g7-summit-in-italy|G7 summit in Italy]] on Thursday, June 13, 2024, laying out a path for a long-term US security relationship with Kyiv.[1]

## Contents

The agreement followed months of negotiations and was expected to commit the United States for ten years to continued training of Ukraine's armed forces, expanded cooperation in the production of weapons and military equipment, continued military assistance, and greater intelligence sharing.[1] It contained no specific monetary pledge; an annex was to describe how the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration planned to work with [[congress|Congress]] on implementing the commitments, particularly the long-term funding required.[1]

## Executive agreement status

Crucially, the pledge took the form of an "executive agreement" rather than a treaty, making it less formal and not necessarily binding on future presidents.[1] [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the presumptive Republican nominee, had not said whether he would continue support for Ukraine if elected, offering only that he would negotiate a quick end to the Russia–Ukraine war without explaining how; he had also pressed European states to spend more on their own defense and said he would "encourage" Russia "to do whatever the hell they want" if they did not.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the announcement should be read on two levels.[1] He invoked an earlier observation by [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] on the Power Hour that exhaustive media coverage of a purported Biden executive order on the border omitted the fact that no executive order existed — the administration had merely made a public declaration on border enforcement, which he equated to doing nothing.[1] By the same logic, he wrote, the ten-year deal to arm and train Ukrainian forces is narratively catastrophic for the regime among normies who oppose funding foreign wars, while in administrative substance an executive agreement carries no permanent enforceability and can be undone immediately by a future administration.[1]
