# United States v. Hasbajrami

*United States v. Hasbajrami* is the federal criminal case in which the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York ruled, in a decision reported in January 2025, that warrantless searches conducted under [[section-702|Section 702]] of the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act]] violate the Fourth Amendment. The ruling was described as a landmark and as the first time a court had made such a determination regarding Section 702, a provision long criticized for enabling warrantless surveillance of Americans, including protesters, journalists, and members of Congress.[1]

## Background

The case centers on queries conducted by the [[fbi|FBI]] during an investigation of the defendant, Agron Hasbajrami. Initially, the government concealed its use of Section 702 surveillance in Hasbajrami's case and in others, reversing course only after revelations about the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s practice of hiding such surveillance in criminal cases.[1]

The district court ruling built on a pivotal 2019 decision by the Second Circuit Court of Appeals, which found that Section 702 queries involving individuals in the United States constitute searches requiring Fourth Amendment scrutiny.[1]

Badlands Media commentator Chris Paul urged pushing the reasoning further, writing "Take it all the way for the 14th Amendment challenge!" and asserting that the Fourteenth Amendment "is illegitimate and was never ratified," such that "the entire body of law built on top of it is null and void."[1]
