# 2024 Putin Visit to North Korea and Vietnam

In June 2024, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] conducted a two-stop tour of East and Southeast Asia, holding a summit with North Korean leader [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] in Pyongyang before traveling on to Hanoi for meetings with Vietnamese leaders.[1] The trip drew intense attention in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] corpus, which treated it as a landmark event in the reordering of global alignments rather than as routine diplomacy.[1]

## North Korea leg and the sanctions challenge

Following his negotiations with Kim, Putin declared that the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] sanctions regime against [[north-korea|North Korea]] — which he described as inspired by the United States and its allies — "must be revised," calling the indefinite restrictive regime unjustifiable.[1] He argued that the propaganda cliches reproduced over and over by Western countries were no longer able to disguise their aggressive geopolitical plans, including in the North-East Asia region, and pledged that Moscow and Pyongyang would continue to oppose "the practice of sanctions strangulation as a tool that the West is accustomed to using to maintain its hegemony in politics, economy and other spheres."[1]

At an accompanying press conference, Putin explicitly compared the sanctions to the Nazi [[siege-of-leningrad|siege of Leningrad]], a blockade that killed over 1.5 million Russians, including his own older brother.[1] Kim also presented Putin with a gift of dogs, an exchange that circulated widely and was read by some Badlands commentators as "dog comms."[1]

## Vietnam leg

In Hanoi, Putin met Vietnamese National Assembly Chairman [[tran-thanh-man|Tran Thanh Man]], praising parliamentary cooperation as creating "a solid and reliable basis" for security, economic and humanitarian interaction, and describing the Vietnamese direction in Russian foreign policy as "one of the unconditional priorities."[1] He reported productive talks with Vietnam's president, prime minister and the secretary general of the Communist Party of Vietnam Central Committee.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the tour deliberately mirrored the 2017 foreign trip of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]: a historic visit to a country long branded as a backward nation of radicalized zealots, a conquering-hero reception with the host city decked out in the visiting nation's flag, spectacle and pomp, the reintroduction of the host leader to the public as a benevolent figure, and the signing of historic bilateral agreements — before the visiting president departed for the next destination.[1] He placed Putin's Pyongyang visit "in the same tier as Trump's first visit to Riyadh," a symbolic moment expected to grow more legendary with age.[1]

On this reading, the practical function of the trip was a PR campaign to reintroduce Kim Jong Un to the Western public so that North Korea could ultimately be recognized as part of an emerging free world.[1] Citing remarks by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] on the Devolution Power Hour, the commentary described Kim as a young leader likely groomed to be a CIA puppet whose strings had since been cut by Trump, leaving him in need of genuine mentorship from the captain of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and neighboring head of state.[1] Putin's challenge to the UN was expected not to go unanswered, potentially making Russia North Korea's champion and liberator in what Badlands writers call the War of Sovereignty.[1]

Both destinations were also framed as former proxy theaters of the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[cia|CIA]] — North Korea as an installed puppet boogeyman and [[vietnam|Vietnam]] as a narcotics theater opened by the [[gulf-of-tonkin-incident|Gulf of Tonkin]] false flag — making Putin's arrival in each a symbolic reversal.[1]
