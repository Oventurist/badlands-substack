# Childhood Vaccine Exemptions

**Childhood vaccine exemptions** are legal exemptions — medical or nonmedical (religious or philosophical) — that allow parents to enroll children in school without the full schedule of state-mandated immunizations. In November 2023 the practice drew national attention when a report from the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] published on November 10 found that the United States faced its highest-ever childhood vaccine exemption rate in history.[1]

## The 2023 CDC findings

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, the United States had maintained nearly 95 percent nationwide vaccination coverage for ten years.[1] Between 2020 and 2021, vaccine coverage among kindergarten-aged children fell to 94 percent; between 2021 and 2022 it dropped again, to 93 percent.[1] The report's authors declined to attribute the decline to a single cause, writing that "it is not clear whether this reflects a true increase in opposition to vaccination, or if parents are opting for nonmedical [vaccine] exemptions because of barriers to vaccination or out of convenience."[1] They concluded that "whether because of an increase in hesitancy or barriers to vaccination, the COVID-19 pandemic affected childhood routine vaccination."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], framed the rising exemption rate as a downstream effect of a broader collapse of institutional trust rather than a narrow medical dispute.[1] She recounted that a close friend with seven children had three sons vaccine-injured around the turn of the century, an experience that shaped her own views, and said she stopped vaccinating her own children entirely during what she called "the hysterical HPV push" as her sons entered their teens.[1] She argued that "the awakening to corruption, and its necessary narrative cover, are not exclusive from the truth about the eugenicists' vaccines," and that this truth "has been crying out for decades, but people didn't have ears to hear as long as they still believed in the benevolence of government."[1] With, in her estimation, more than two thirds of Americans now awake to widespread government corruption, they were necessarily examining vaccines more critically.[1] She concluded that "this all comes down to trust," that the people no longer trust institutions "which have repeatedly proven themselves untrustworthy," and that "these childhood vaccine exemptions save lives."[1]
