# Strait of Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical oil transit route and the central chokepoint of the 2026 conflict between the United States and [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## The 48-hour ultimatum

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened on March 22, 2026 to strike Iran's power plants unless Tehran fully reopened the strait within 48 hours, escalating a conflict that began on February 28 and had entered its fourth week by Monday, March 23.[1] Iran's [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Revolutionary Guards]] responded that the strait would be "completely" closed if the United States followed through, and Iranian officials warned that energy infrastructure in countries hosting U.S. military bases would become legitimate targets if Iranian power plants were attacked.[1]

## Market and allied effects

Brent crude rose to $112.19 a barrel on March 22, reflecting market concern over the strait.[1] Concerns over oil supply disruptions deepened as fighting continued across the region.[1] Securing the route also drove U.S. pressure on allies: [[japan|Japan]]'s March 2026 White House summit with Trump turned in part on energy security tied to the strait, and Washington pressed Tokyo to take a more active role in protecting Middle Eastern shipping despite Japan's pacifist constitution.[1]

## Strategic framing

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s [[iranian-knot|Iranian Knot]] analysis argues that [[russia|Russia]] continues to facilitate critical energy routes through "the very chokepoint under siege," while China-linked shipping maintains safe passage even as legacy media focus on missile exchanges.[1]
