# Badlands Brief: Elections are Fake; as is Trump's Friendship with Bibi

"Elections are Fake; as is Trump's Friendship with Bibi" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on June 9, 2026. The Brief describes itself as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war." Commentary in this edition is contributed by [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The issue covers four principal subjects — a reported American intervention to stop an Israeli strike on Iran, the Los Angeles mayoral primary and the lineage of American voting-machine software, a federal denaturalization campaign, and a reported American interest in purchasing the Chagos Islands — plus a bonus item on Chinese maritime activity east of Taiwan.

## Netanyahu calls off a major Iran strike

The lead item summarizes reporting that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] intervened to prevent a broader renewal of fighting between [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]], warning Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that Israel could find itself "on your own very soon" if it expanded military operations against Iran. The warning was described in comments Trump gave to [[axios|Axios]] and was later confirmed by [[reuters|Reuters]], which reported that Trump said he had cautioned Netanyahu against resuming a wider war.

According to the Axios account cited by the Brief, Netanyahu canceled what would have been Israel's largest strike on Iran since the April ceasefire following discussions with Trump. The exchange occurred amid renewed hostilities that began with Iranian missile launches against Israel and subsequent Israeli strikes on Iranian targets; the Israeli attacks were described as the first reported Israeli strikes on Iran since the ceasefire took effect in April. On June 8, Trump publicly called on both countries to "immediately stop shooting," spoke again with Netanyahu, and asked him to halt the planned strikes. Trump also said Iran had informed the United States that it did not intend to conduct further attacks and had requested that Washington relay that message to Israel. Netanyahu subsequently said Israel would "hold fire" against Iran while indicating that Israel reserved the right to respond if attacked in future. The Brief notes that the developments temporarily reduced fears that the exchange of missile and air strikes would escalate into a broader regional conflict.

It's the last thing anybody would expect."

He contends that observers remain reluctant to accept the interpretation even when presented with evidence, assuming instead either that Trump and Netanyahu are jointly running an operation, or that Trump earnestly seeks peace but will ultimately cave to Netanyahu and to the pressure of the Israel lobby. In his reading, the only way Trump could credibly sell the idea that he was an ardent Zionist was for his own support base to believe it; this required Trump to intentionally damage his own reputation in order to sell the ruse "to the public, to the press, and most importantly, to the zealots running the Isreali government and the Deep State insurgency occupying DC."

As supporting evidence, the commentary recalls an exchange in which a reporter asked, "Mr. President, did Israel force your hand to launch these strikes against Iran?" and Trump replied, "No, I might have forced [Israel's] hand." The author notes that Trump added, "We were having negotiations with these lunatics, and it was my opinion that they (Israel) were going to attack first…", and asks whether the "lunatics" referenced were in fact Netanyahu's cabinet.

He further cites [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] as corroboration, arguing that Levin "is now beginning to turn on President Trump" — first accusing the president of being "desperate for a deal" and mocking his negotiating ability, then defending Netanyahu while disparaging Trump: "I don't know why he keeps bashing the Prime Minister of Israel! …Why the constant beating up of Netanyahu?!" The author reads Levin's shift as proof of "trouble in paradise" and as an indication that Levin understands the United States has already decided to abandon the Israeli military campaign in the Middle East. He closes by urging readers to "maintain an open and skeptical mind toward all things being reported."

## The Los Angeles mayoral race and the lineage of voting-machine software

The Brief's second item reports that [[los-angeles|Los Angeles]] City Council member [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]] advanced to the November runoff for mayor and will face incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]], after finishing ahead of former reality television personality [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] in the city's 2026 primary. The race drew national attention because of Pratt's celebrity profile and his criticism of city leadership following the [[palisades-fire|Palisades Fire]]. Raman entered the race shortly before the filing deadline and campaigned on housing affordability, homelessness, and city services; pre-primary public polling showed Bass, Raman, and Pratt in a closely contested race. Pratt's campaign emphasized homelessness, crime, and public dissatisfaction with city government, but vote counting left him outside the top two. Voters chose from fourteen candidates in the nonpartisan primary.

### Ashe in America on Smartmatic's code lineage

Ashe in America notes, citing [[canncon|CannCon]], that Los Angeles "is the only jurisdiction in the US that still uses [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]]," and argues that Smartmatic nonetheless "has a founding role in the code bases of the other US election systems in use around the country." She presents a summary based on CannCon's research, describing a joint project the two had worked on some years earlier that never came to fruition.

The narrative begins in April 2000 — seven months before the disputed Florida presidential recount in Broward and Palm Beach counties — when Alfredo Jose Anzola incorporated Smartmatic Corp. in Delaware and headquartered it in Boca Raton, part of Palm Beach County. In 2004 Smartmatic won its first contract with [[venezuela|Venezuela]] ahead of the recall election of [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]], months after receiving investment from the Venezuelan government. Ashe quotes The New York Times: "Seven months before that voting contract was awarded, a Venezuelan government financing agency invested more than $200,000 into a smaller technology company, owned by some of the same people as Smartmatic, that joined with Smartmatic as a minor partner in the bid. In return, the government agency was given a 28 percent stake in the smaller company and a seat on its board, which was occupied by a senior government official who had previously advised Mr. Chávez on elections technology." Venezuelan officials later insisted the money was a small-business loan repaid before the referendum. That smaller company, [[bitza|Bitza]], had 28 percent of its shares held by the Venezuelan government while simultaneously writing the software used in Smartmatic voting machines.

In 2005 Smartmatic purchased [[sequoia-voting-systems|Sequoia Voting Systems]] from the British firm [[de-la-rue|De La Rue PLC]] for $16 million — a $19 million loss for De La Rue against its $35 million investment four years earlier. Two years later Smartmatic announced it would divest Sequoia; a Delaware court filing of April 2008 revealed that Smartmatic retained a $2 million unsecured promissory note from the divestiture and, more significantly, that Sequoia was still using Smartmatic's intellectual property.

The account then turns to 2010, when the Obama-era [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] under Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] forced [[election-systems-and-software|Election Systems & Software]] (ES&S) to divest the former Diebold subsidiary [[premier-election-solutions|Premier Election Solutions]], which ES&S had purchased six months earlier. The DOJ required ES&S to divest "all of the intellectual property associated with all versions — past, present, and in development — of the Premier voting equipment systems to another company," to "grant a fully paid-up, irrevocable, perpetual license to use the AutoMARK, ES&S's ballot marking device," and to ensure the "right to modify and improve both Premier products and the AutoMARK." The department further specified that "ES&S must sell the divestiture assets to a buyer approved by the department" and required ES&S to grant the divestiture buyer an opportunity to compete for services to Premier customers then under contract. The approved buyer was [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], which a few months after acquiring Premier also purchased Sequoia.

Ashe quotes [[john-c-bonifaz|John C. Bonifaz]] — whom she describes parenthetically as "one of the masterminds in the lawfare against me and my co-defendants" — from a letter to the [[national-institute-of-standards-and-technology|National Institute of Standards and Technology]]: "Dominion, Now the Largest or Second Largest Voting System Company, Is Foreign Controlled and Depends Upon Secret Source Code Created and Owned by Smartmatic, a Foreign Controlled Company With Ties to The Venezuelan Government Led by Hugo Chavez… Dominion is reported by Dun & Bradstreet to be a Toronto-based company with one listed key official, board members and employee, John Poulos, and under $18 million in sales for which the company obtains no income." She also quotes Dominion's own press release: "Dominion Voting Systems Corporation today announced that it has acquired the assets of Sequoia Voting Systems, a major U.S. provider of voting solutions serving nearly 300 jurisdictions in 16 states. As part of the transaction, Dominion has acquired Sequoia's inventory and all intellectual property, including software, firmware and hardware, for Sequoia's precinct and central count optical scan and DRE voting solutions."

Summarizing the chain — "Smartmatic buys Sequoia. ES&S divests of Premier to Dominion. Smartmatic Sells Sequoia. Smartmatic maintains an interest while Sequoia utilizes Smartmatic IP. Dominion buys Sequoia" — she concludes that Smartmatic intellectual property is "ostensibly present throughout the system, despite its hot potato M&A activity," that Americans with oversight authority know this, and that they nevertheless "pretend that American elections are real and impervious to manipulation" while being "direct beneficiaries of the system." She ends by suggesting that "Spencer Pratt and Smartmatic in LA in 2026 might be about a lot more than who is going to run the City of Angels."

## The denaturalization campaign

The third item reports that the Trump administration announced on June 8 that it is seeking to revoke the citizenship of seventeen naturalized U.S. citizens accused of immigration fraud or other crimes, describing the effort as the largest [[denaturalization|denaturalization]] campaign ever undertaken by the federal government, with cases filed in federal courts across the country. Federal law allows the government to pursue denaturalization where officials allege citizenship was obtained through fraud, concealment of material facts, or other disqualifying conduct; according to CBS News, the Justice Department expanded its denaturalization priorities in 2025 as part of a broader immigration enforcement initiative.

Court filings cited in the reporting allege that some targeted individuals concealed criminal activity during naturalization, used false identities, or otherwise failed to satisfy statutory requirements including the demonstration of good moral character. The group includes individuals accused or convicted of offenses ranging from child sexual abuse and fraud-related crimes to visa fraud and money laundering, with allegations remaining subject to judicial review. The Justice Department said the proceedings are intended to address alleged abuse of the naturalization process, and those named may challenge the government's claims in court. If a court orders denaturalization, the person generally reverts to a prior immigration status and loses the rights and protections of citizenship, including protection from deportation.

Ashe in America comments that immigration fraud "is one of the only ways that naturalized citizens can lose their citizenship," noting that the Brief has previously discussed the subject in the context of Minnesota congresswoman [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]], "who allegedly married her brother to help him obtain lawful status." Observing that Omar is not among the seventeen, she writes that "obviously there's more to do," that it is good that those naturalized through fraud are being removed, and that "it must be way more than 17. The job isn't over until every last fake American is stripped of their status and returned to their homeland."

## A proposed American purchase of the Chagos Islands

The fourth item reports, citing a Telegraph story relayed by [[rt|RT]], that U.S. officials are discussing a proposal that could lead to the United States purchasing the [[chagos-islands|Chagos Islands]] from [[mauritius|Mauritius]] after the territory is transferred from British control. The islands include [[diego-garcia|Diego Garcia]], a joint U.S.–U.K. military base described by RT as one of Washington's most significant overseas installations. The proposal arises against the backdrop of a planned transfer of sovereignty from the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]] to Mauritius; Britain previously approved an arrangement to hand over the islands while retaining access to Diego Garcia through a 99-year lease. The sovereignty dispute has been shaped by a 2019 advisory opinion of the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] finding that the U.K.'s separation of the islands from Mauritius in 1965 was unlawful. Trump had previously criticized the U.K.–Mauritius agreement as "an act of great stupidity" posing national security concerns. Mauritius has not confirmed receiving any formal U.S. offer; RT, citing Reuters, reported that the Mauritian government said it had received no official proposal and reiterated that its sovereignty over the Chagos Archipelago is "non-negotiable."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls the report "an interesting wrinkle in the effort to bring our military home and stop acting like the World Police." He writes that he favors shuttering "the overwhelming majority of the 800+ military installations we have built around the world" while recognizing the strategic value of maintaining a few bases abroad where it makes sense — and that Diego Garcia is one such case. He describes the Chagos Archipelago as a group of seven atolls comprising more than sixty islands in the Indian Ocean just south of the Maldives, with Diego Garcia the largest and most notorious, housing a British airbase operated jointly with the United States and often described as "an unsinkable aircraft carrier." He notes that London approved the handover plan in May 2025 alongside the 99-year lease, and that Trump now seeks to bypass the British and deal directly with Mauritius, which "would leave the British sidelined and at the mercy of the United States." He concludes that "under President Trump there appears to be a pattern of dismantling the remnants of the old British Empire."

## Bonus item: China's "special maritime operation" off Taiwan

The bonus item reports that [[china|China]] launched what it called a "special maritime law enforcement operation" east of [[taiwan|Taiwan]] on a Saturday, according to China's Ministry of Transport and the state news agency Xinhua. Chinese authorities said the operation was intended to exercise maritime administrative law-enforcement jurisdiction and protect national rights and interests.

The action followed a May 28 summit in Tokyo at which Japanese Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]] and Philippine President [[ferdinand-marcos-jr|Ferdinand Marcos Jr.]] announced plans to begin maritime delimitation talks concerning their exclusive economic zones and continental shelves. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[mao-ning|Mao Ning]] said the waters under discussion lie east of Taiwan and that any delimitation negotiations there must involve China. Taiwan also objected, saying the area overlaps waters in which it claims rights and interests and urging [[japan|Japan]] and the [[philippines|Philippines]] to consult Taipei. Japanese Chief Cabinet Secretary [[minoru-kihara|Minoru Kihara]] said any Tokyo–Manila agreement would not be legally binding on third parties. Taiwan's coast guard said it deployed at least five vessels in response and monitored four Chinese government vessels departing from Xiamen.
