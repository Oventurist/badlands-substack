# NIH Transgender Youth Hormone Study

The NIH transgender youth hormone study, titled "Psychosocial Functioning in Transgender Youth after 2 Years of Hormones," was a two-year study funded by the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]] that examined 315 individuals between the ages of 12 and 20 who identify as transgender and were given cross-sex hormones.[1]

## Deaths and congressional response

The portion of the study involving 240 children was funded by NIH, which admits that "two young people tragically died by suicide."[1] Despite the deaths, the NIH carried the study on to its conclusion.[1]

In May 2023, Republican lawmakers in both the House and Senate demanded answers from the NIH about the study.[1] More than a dozen lawmakers, including [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]], [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]], penned a letter to NIH director Dr. [[lawrence-tabak|Lawrence Tabak]].[1] The episode was reported by [[summit-news|Summit News]], drawing on Fox News reporting.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that "there is no 'good' outcome for a patient receiving 'gender affirming care,'" contending that at best such patients avoid cancer and other major health problems while living with what he described as hormonally altered bodies.[1] He wrote that suicide "might be preferable to some of the more horrific complications that result from top and bottom surgeries," asserted that lies pushed by the medical system are endemic throughout modern medical institutions, and suggested that publicizing the harmful details helps warn those who might otherwise be drawn into the practice.[1] The study features in wider debate over [[pediatric-gender-transition-treatment|pediatric gender transition treatment]].[1]
