# Phoenix Program

The Phoenix Program was a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] operation conducted during the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam War]] which Badlands Media describes as "a CIA death squad program targeting civilians."[1]

## Badlands framing

The program is invoked in the August 2023 Badlands article "Clowns in America" as one of the covert CIA operations under way in Southeast Asia at the moment [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] travelled to Vietnam in late 1967 with his [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]] business partner [[thomas-j-devine|Thomas J. Devine]], a CIA officer operating under commercial cover.[1] Writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] paired it with the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret CIA war in Laos]] to protect the opium trade and argued that the timing of the Bush–Devine trip was not coincidental.[1]

The program is presented as part of a pattern of agency conduct — assassination plots, secret wars, antidemocratic coups and mind-control experiments — that the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] investigations of the 1970s exposed but never stopped.[1]



## Aquino and the occult aftermath

Matthew Ehret writes that in 1968 [[michael-aquino|Michael Aquino]] was employed in Operation Phoenix directly under the command of psychological warfare specialist [[edward-lansdale|Edward Lansdale]], helping oversee the assassination of more than 40,000 civilians whose existence supposedly aided the Vietcong.[2] The program specialized in psychological warfare, torture, terror and mass murder, innovating the philosophy of "burning the village to save the village."[2] Researcher Anthony Forwood argues that the psyop created "legions of cold-blooded psychopathic killers" who returned to the United States transformed, many entering Satanism under Aquino's influence, a development Forwood links to a rise in satanically tinged serial murders in southern California.[2]

## Methods and export to Latin America

Joe Lange's follow-up article describes Phoenix as a CIA assassination and torture program directed at civilians in violation of the Geneva Conventions, later expanded by the agency to other parts of the world and leaving many countries trapped in perpetual poverty and chaos — countries that were, he notes, strategically important to the CIA's global drug trafficking operations.[3] Phoenix became the primary counterinsurgency operation against the Viet Cong; although ostensibly under military control through CORDS and MACV, the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] often directed operations on the ground, provided much of the training for the Provincial Reconnaissance Units, and oversaw the interrogation centers.[3]

The program became notorious for widespread torture, summary executions and indiscriminate killings, with reporting suggesting that at times gathering information was secondary to inflicting terror on the Viet Cong and the local populace.[3] Documented torture tactics included rape, gang rape, rape using eels, snakes or hard objects, and rape followed by murder, along with waterboarding, beatings with rubber hoses and whips, the use of dogs to maul prisoners, and electric shock applied by attaching wires to the genitals, tongue or other sensitive parts of the body.[3] Another common practice, "the airplane," involved tying a prisoner's arms behind the back, looping the rope over a ceiling hook to suspend the prisoner in midair, and then beating them.[3]

Lange stresses the observation that Phoenix produced "the type of violence that would typify CIA operations and interrogation in Latin America and elsewhere in subsequent years."[3] The link was institutional: the counterinsurgency manuals taught at the [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]] were produced during the Army's Project X of 1965–66, which drew directly on knowledge generated by Phoenix, and were reintroduced into the SOA curriculum under the [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] administration in 1982 after having been suspended under [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]].[3] In Lange's reading this is the mechanism by which the CIA installed brutal dictators in strategic countries and trained their militaries and police to brutalize their own populations, with El Salvador cited as one of the worst examples of the Phoenix model applied in Central America.[3]
