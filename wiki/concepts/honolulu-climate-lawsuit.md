# Honolulu Climate Lawsuit

The Honolulu climate lawsuit is a case brought in 2020 by the city of [[honolulu]] against several major fossil fuel companies, including [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] and [[chevron|Chevron]], alleging that the companies' products cause greenhouse gas emissions and global warming and that the companies sold them without warning consumers about the risks.[1]

## Claims and remedy sought

Honolulu pleaded a series of state-law theories — principally public nuisance and trespass — and asked that the companies pay billions of dollars to the state to abate the effects of climate change, including severe weather events, sea level rise, heat waves, flooding and warming generally.[1] The [[hawaii-supreme-court|Hawaii Supreme Court]] ruled that the case could proceed to trial, and by April 2024 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] was considering requests from many states to intervene.[1]

## Stakes

Observers warned that a Honolulu victory would set a precedent for Democrat-run states nationwide, letting them overwhelm energy companies with lawsuits until the companies accepted [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]]-like parameters and applied them everywhere — nullifying the policy choices of conservative states.[1] The projected cost to the public was described as devastating: energy expenses high enough to make domestic manufacturing impossible and to degrade quality of life for decades.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] called the case one that "could reshape the corporate and legal landscapes for generations," noting that energy utilities are so heavily regulated that public and private roles blur, with NGOs adding plausible deniability when injury occurs.[1] She raised two objections to the failure-to-warn theory: first, that essentially no one is unaware of the claimed link between fossil fuels and global warming after twenty-plus years of official messaging; and second, that if labeling were genuinely desired, [[congress|Congress]] and the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] had ample authority to require it.[1] In her view Honolulu's real aim was to advance the unpopular Green New Deal agenda through the courts, in a manner analogous to litigation campaigns against firearm manufacturers.[1]
