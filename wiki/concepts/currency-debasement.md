# Currency Debasement

Currency debasement is the lowering of the value of a currency.[1] Historically the term referred to the practice of mixing base metals into coinage that was supposed to be struck from precious metals, allowing the issuing government to produce more nominal money from the same quantity of gold or silver.[1] The arrangement gives governments additional money to spend while handing citizens inflation.[1]

Debasement is the phenomenon whose effects [[greshams-law|Gresham's Law]] describes, since holders of the older, fuller-weight coinage withdraw it from circulation and pass along the adulterated coin.[1]

## Debasement under fiat money

Because modern currency is, as Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] put it, "backed by nothing but hopes and dreams," debasement no longer requires physically adulterating metal.[1] In a fiat system it refers instead to the government printing money — increasing the supply of dollars while decreasing the spending power of each dollar.[1]

## The trillion-dollar coin as debasement

The debate over the [[trillion-dollar-coin|trillion-dollar coin]], revived in May 2023 by economist [[paul-krugman|Paul Krugman]], is a case in point.[1] Because there is not remotely enough platinum or palladium on Earth to give such a coin genuine intrinsic value — a trillion dollars of platinum would require roughly 183 times annual global supply — the plan reduces to striking an ordinary coin and declaring it worth a trillion dollars, which a TD Securities analyst in 2013 described as "akin to debt monetization."[1] Krugman nonetheless claimed the maneuver would not be inflationary, a position Ashe in America treated as indefensible given that it depends entirely on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] declaring value out of thin air.[1]
