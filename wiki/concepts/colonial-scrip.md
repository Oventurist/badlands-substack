# Colonial Scrip

Colonial Scrip is the name given to the paper currency issued in the American colonies before the Revolutionary War. In Badlands Media commentary, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described it as a "debt-free and interest-free fiat currency" under which the American colonists "enjoyed incredible economic property prior to the Revolutionary War."[1]

Deschamps argued that colonial-era Americans "were far more adept and knowledgeable about how to manage a healthy monetary system," but that within roughly a hundred years that competence and the confidence flowing from it were "almost totally destroyed by subversion, manipulation, and de-education schemes."[1] He connected this history to the founding of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in 1913 and to modern central-bank hostility toward cryptocurrency voiced by [[agustin-carstens|Agustin Carstens]] of the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]], presenting cryptocurrency as a modern attempt to restore citizen-produced money.[1]

## Origins in the Navigation Acts

In his February 2023 essay "Cracks in the Curtain," Deschamps traced the currency's creation to the [[navigation-acts|Navigation Acts]] imposed by Great Britain before the Revolution, which he says all but crippled the colonies and prompted the colonists to create their own form of money free from the controls of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]].[2] The result, he argues, was one of the most economically successful periods in American history, arguably better than what the country enjoyed after 1776.[2]

Asked how the colonies had achieved such prosperity, [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] is quoted as answering: "That is simple. In the Colonies we issue our own money. It is called Colonial Scrip. We issue it in proper proportion to the demands of trade and industry to make the products pass easily from the producers to the consumers. In this manner creating for ourselves our own paper money, we control its purchasing power, and we have no interest to pay."[2] Deschamps concludes that the founders understood deeply what [[money|money]] was and how much it had been used to control the colonies, and that modern citizens should take the time to understand what they did.[2]
