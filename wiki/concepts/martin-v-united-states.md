# Martin v. United States

Martin v. United States is the case brought by a Georgia family whose home was mistakenly raided by [[fbi|FBI]] agents in 2017 and who asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] to let them sue, circumventing the immunity protections typically afforded to federal employees.[1]

## The raid

Curtrinia "Trina" Martin, her seven-year-old son Gabe, and her then-fiancé Hillard "Toi" Cliatt were asleep in their home just before dawn on October 18, 2017 when masked FBI agents battered down the front door and set off flash-bang grenades.[1] Martin, naked from the waist down, was held at gunpoint by agents in her walk-in closet and kept away from Gabe, who cowered under his bedsheets, "terrified."[1] Cliatt was dragged across the floor and handcuffed while several agents circled him with firearms.[1] It was not until an agent asked Cliatt to repeat the address of the house that it became clear the raid had targeted the wrong home.[1]

## Significance

The petition asks the Supreme Court to permit a damages suit despite the immunity ordinarily shielding federal officers, making the case a test of whether victims of wrong-house federal raids have any practical remedy.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried the story as a bonus item in its April 30, 2025 news brief, alongside the suspension of Wisconsin judge [[hannah-dugan|Hannah Dugan]], as part of a broader running theme about the conduct and accountability of the Bureau.[1]
