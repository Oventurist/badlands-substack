# Fusarium graminearum Smuggling Case

The Fusarium graminearum smuggling case is a June 2025 federal criminal matter in which two Chinese nationals were charged with bringing a dangerous biological pathogen into the United States for work at a University of Michigan laboratory.[1]

## Charges

Yunqing Jian, 33, and Zunyong Liu, 34, both citizens of the People's Republic of China, were charged in a criminal complaint with conspiracy, smuggling goods into the United States, false statements, and visa fraud.[1] The charges were announced by United States Attorney Jerome F. Gorgon, Jr., joined by Special Agent in Charge Cheyvoryea Gibson of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] Detroit Division and Marty C. Raybon, Director of Field Operations for U.S. Customs and Border Protection.[1] The FBI arrested Jian in connection with the allegations.[1]

## The pathogen

Fusarium graminearum is a fungus that scientific literature classifies as a potential agroterrorism weapon.[1] It causes "head blight," a disease of wheat, barley, maize, and rice that is responsible for billions of dollars in economic losses worldwide each year, and its toxins cause vomiting, liver damage, and reproductive defects in humans and livestock.[1]
