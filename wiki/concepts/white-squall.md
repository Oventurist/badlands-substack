# White Squall (Q Comm)

*White Squall* is a film referenced within [[qanon|Q]] circles and interpreted by Badlands Media contributors as a narrative metaphor — a "comm" — for the journey of Trump, Q, and Anons since Q first appeared in October 2017.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## The metaphor

The film follows a group of boys taken to sea by a captain known as Skipper, who guide their ship through a series of storms culminating in a giant tidal wave.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The author argues the correspondences between the film's finale and current events are too numerous to be coincidence.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

Most significant is the closing sequence: Skipper is placed on trial for doing the best he could with his ship and crew while facing enormous, destructive natural forces — a structure the author maps directly onto [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s prosecutions by [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] and [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Redemption by the crew

At the end of the film, Skipper is redeemed by his surviving crew, who stand by him during the trial.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The article reads this as prefiguring the role of Anons as the "New News" — the Q slogan "you are the news now" — shaping the informational landscape so that Trump receives a fair hearing.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The author notes that in highly politicized proceedings, what happens in a courtroom is often downstream of the court of public opinion.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The piece closes with the Q rallying cry "WWG1WGA."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## The phrase in the Q drops

The phrase "white squall" appears in Q drops #1602 and #2543, where, on the reading advanced in the April 25, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Significance of White Squall" by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], it confirms not only the existence of [[the-storm|the storm]] but also its fundamental characteristics and features.[2] The essay treats the phrase as multi-layered communication, since it denotes both a specific type of storm formation and a film of the same name, and argues that it behooves readers to unpack as many layers as possible.[2]

## Meteorological and symbolic reading

A squall is a sudden, violent windstorm at sea, normally accompanied by black clouds except in the rare white variety, which carries tremendous destructive capacity.[2] The essay links this to the wider use of tidal waves in popular culture as symbols for the destruction of "life as we know it," and to the frequency with which people report recurring tidal-wave dreams, which it takes as evidence that wave symbolism reaches deep into the collective psyche.[2] The consistent interpretation offered is that tidal waves and storms represent fundamental change — a full-spectrum transformation of life and reality itself.[2]

The author asks why Q specified a *white* squall rather than a hurricane or ordinary tidal wave, and answers that because a white squall involves sudden winds, Q is signalling the element of air.[2] Invoking the [[cabal|cabal]]'s occult practices and Tarot divination, in which air corresponds to the suit of swords and to intellect and the processes by which truth is determined, the essay proposes that a sudden burst of wind denotes a rapid distribution of truthful information — possibly [[declas|DECLAS]].[2]

## A storm of truth

Because the burst of wind leads to the tidal wave, the symbolism implies that massive waves rise as a consequence of the distribution of truth, which the essay calls the most destructive possible event for an empire built on lies.[2] It argues that the deepest dividing line in the present world runs between those who subscribe to that empire of lies and those who know they are being lied to, and that this line cuts through families and all parts of society.[2] The author cites Matthew 10:34–36, in which Jesus says he came not to bring peace but a sword and to set family members against one another, reading it symbolically rather than literally, with Jesus equated to Truth and the sword to the air element that divides people according to their disposition toward it.[2]

## The film as narrative for anons

The essay reads the film's plot through the [[heros-journey|Hero's Journey]] as described by [[joseph-campbell|Joseph Campbell]] in [[the-hero-with-a-thousand-faces|The Hero with a Thousand Faces]], arguing that a hero must leave familiar territory for a region rendered supernatural by its unfamiliarity, in answer to a call to adventure.[2] In the film a rag-tag group of boys goes to sea under [[skipper-sheldon|Skipper Sheldon]] and can only get home by coordinating well enough to run the ship, each overcoming his own internal challenges to become part of the team.[2]

The author maps this onto [[anons|anons]], who existed within a [[truth-movement|truth movement]] already plagued by competing factions and egos, and who are high in disagreeability — a double-edged trait that makes them excellent independent researchers but poor team members.[2] What they needed was a strong leader to rally around, just as the boys needed Skipper; the essay notes that the [[4chan|4chan]] board where Q posted was a markedly masculine space and holds that the archetypes of [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Skipper closely resemble each other.[2]

Unusually for the form, the essay argues, the film makes heroes of the entire crew together rather than one individual, and the boon they bring back is a capacity for self-improvement and teamwork.[2] Since the cabal's principal weapon is narratives that divide people so they cannot work together, the only counter is a united opposition of independent researchers and citizen journalists; on this reading Trump and Q have been teaching anons to coordinate across a vast, unpredictable sea of information.[2] Because neither Trump nor the Q team could openly announce a military operation, comms were coordinated to give self-selected anons the means to navigate the [[5g-war|5G war]].[2]

## Skipper on trial

At the film's end Skipper is put on trial and is ultimately rescued by his crew, who stand by him although polite society wishes to penalise him for taking the boys on the voyage.[2] The essay reads this as symbolising both that society is not yet ready for what anons have to offer and as a blatant prediction of Trump's own legal situation.[2] Rather than standing beside him in a courtroom, anons stand by Trump by assembling [[q-proofs|Q proofs]], researching his presidency and publishing their findings; their unity in that mission redeems both Skipper and the voyage itself.[2] The author concludes that White Squall is not about a lone hero but about the formation of a heroic brotherhood requiring all hands on deck.[2]

## Origin of "Where we go one, we go all"

A February 14, 2024 essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] identifies *White Squall* — a 1996 film directed by Ridley Scott and starring Jeff Bridges — as the source of the phrase [[wwg1wga|"Where we go one, we go all"]], which the Q team used often and which became a rallying cry in the truth community and MAGA.[3] Carlson summarises the plot as a group of young men sailing a ship captained by Bridges's character who run into a massive storm that comes out of nowhere.[3] Before the storm the crew was disjointed, selfish, undisciplined and unwilling to work together; the sudden storm forced them to set aside petty disagreements and function as a cohesive team to keep the ship from going under.[3]

Carlson places [[ridley-scott|Ridley Scott]] among filmmakers — with his brother Tony Scott, Mel Gibson, Christopher Nolan and Stanley Kubrick — whom he believes have used their films to transmit coded messaging about conspiracies to the public.[3]
