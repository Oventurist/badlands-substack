# Mandatory Repatriation Tax

The **Mandatory Repatriation Tax (MRT)**, also known as the Section 965 transition tax, is a provision of the [[tax-cuts-and-jobs-act|Tax Cuts and Jobs Act]] approved by the Republican-controlled [[congress|Congress]] in 2017 and signed into law by then-President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Constitutional challenge

The MRT became the subject of [[moore-v-united-states|Moore v. United States]], which the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] took up on December 5, 2023 as a case about "unrealized" income and the constitutional limitations on federal taxing power.[1] Conservative constitutionalists argued that a ruling finding the MRT in violation of the [[sixteenth-amendment|16th Amendment]] would establish a precedent barring Congress from enacting a wealth tax.[1] Liberal groups, including the [[institute-on-taxation-and-economic-policy|Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy]], warned that invalidating the tax could destabilize the tax system and enrich profitable corporations.[1]
