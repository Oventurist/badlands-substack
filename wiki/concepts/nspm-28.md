# NSPM 28: The National Operations Security Program

NSPM 28, the National Security Presidential Memorandum establishing the National Operations Security Program, was signed on January 13, 2021 — one week before the end of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term.[1] It occupies a central place in the "devolution" research thread advanced by Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], who treats it as documentary evidence that formal secrecy discipline was extended across the civilian executive branch immediately before the transfer of power.[1]

## Content and significance

According to Herold's reading of the memorandum, NSPM 28 "established" and "implemented" [[operations-security|OPSEC]] at the Executive Branch of the U.S. government and "beyond to trusted partners."[1] Because operations security is a discipline typically associated with military operations, Herold argued that Trump's decision to impose it on the [[white-house|White House]] and an undefined circle of trusted partners on his way out the door was anomalous and deserving of scrutiny.[1]

## Context within the final-days memoranda

Herold had previously published a thread documenting what he described as an unprecedented nine NSPMs issued by Trump during his last eight days in office, arguing that such activity was "not something a lame duck President would be doing."[1] NSPM 28 was the first of those dated January 13, 2021.[1]

## Interpretive use

Herold uses NSPM 28 to answer a recurring objection to his research — that named figures have denied any knowledge of a covert operation. His position is that the defining characteristic of OPSEC is that "anybody involved in ops will NOT openly discuss their involvement and maybe even deny the op altogether," so denials cannot be treated as disproof.[1] In his framing, the memorandum supplies a formal, documented mechanism explaining why such denials would be expected.[1]
