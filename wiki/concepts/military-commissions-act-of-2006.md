# Military Commissions Act of 2006

The Military Commissions Act of 2006 (S. 3930, 109th Congress) is a United States statute enacted during the war on terror that established the framework for trying detainees by military commission and defined the term "unlawful enemy combatant."[1]

## Definition of unlawful enemy combatant

The act's central definitional provision is the one most frequently cited in contemporary debate: it defines who may be treated as an unlawful enemy combatant — colloquially, a terrorist — and extends that exposure beyond direct participants.[1] As summarized in Badlands Media commentary, the practical effect is that anyone who provides material support to an unlawful enemy combatant is at risk of being designated an unlawful enemy combatant themselves.[1]

## Relevance to the cartel terrorist designation

The statute resurfaced in January 2025 after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order designating Mexican cartels and other criminal organizations as foreign terrorist organizations.[1] The [[new-york-times|New York Times]] reported that the designation could force American companies to forgo business in Mexico rather than risk U.S. sanctions, warning of major consequences given the two countries' deep economic interdependence and Mexico's status as the United States' largest trading partner in goods.[1] The order was described as intended to apply maximum pressure on Mexico over the drug trade while also giving the administration expanded power to impose economic penalties, travel restrictions, and potentially even to take military action abroad.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked the Military Commissions Act to argue that the material-support exposure was the point rather than an unintended side effect, and dismissed the Times' emphasis on avocado farming and tourism as narrative cover, noting congressional investigations from the 1970s that documented [[cia|CIA]] entanglement with drug cartels in Central and South America.[1]
