# 2023 NPR Layoffs

The 2023 NPR layoffs were a round of staff reductions carried out by [[npr|National Public Radio]] in March 2023 in response to a projected sponsorship shortfall.[1]

## Scope

NPR laid off 84 people and halted production on four seasonal podcasts, among them *Invisibilia*, *Louder Than a Riot* and *Rough Translation*.[1] The organisation had warned in February that cuts were coming after projecting a $30 million sponsorship shortfall for the year.[1]

Thirteen additional roles on NPR's digital team were slated for elimination, but that group had unionised through a separate union representing broadcast employees and technicians and had not yet agreed a contract with NPR despite voluntary recognition; as a result those employees could not be laid off until a contract or a separate lay-off agreement was reached.[1]

## Internal fallout

The layoffs spilled into a series of tense all-hands meetings in which affected employees questioned executives about their decisions.[1] All laid-off employees were given 30 days, or as much time as needed, to remain on staff and transition their work, but although technically still employees they were barred from entering NPR offices, so the all-hands meetings were held over Zoom.[1] According to attendees, some executives called in from home and addressed the group in scripted remarks or in vague terms.[1]

## Badlands commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] used the layoffs to mock NPR's cultural positioning, describing its correspondents as sounding "freakishly unamused" regardless of subject and calling the network "basically a cultural weapon at this point," while welcoming its loss of staff.[1] The commentary situates NPR within the broader [[media-industrial-complex|media industrial complex]] critique advanced by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[1]
