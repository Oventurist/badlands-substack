# Judicial Watch v. Illinois State Board of Elections

Judicial Watch v. Illinois State Board of Elections is a federal lawsuit brought by the watchdog organization [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] against the [[illinois-state-board-of-elections|Illinois State Board of Elections]] concerning the state's duty to maintain accurate voter registration lists under the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]].[1]

## DOJ statement of interest

On Tuesday, July 8, 2025, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed a statement of interest in the case, reinforcing its position that states must take "reasonable efforts" under the NVRA to remove ineligible voters and maintain transparent voter rolls.[1] According to the filing, the statute's list maintenance provision requires states to implement effective programs — not symbolic ones — to keep registration lists accurate and publicly accessible.[1]

Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet K. Dhillon]], who heads the Civil Rights Division, said in an accompanying statement that removing ineligible voters is "critical" so that "elections are conducted fairly, accurately, and without fraud," and pledged vigorous federal enforcement of the NVRA's list maintenance obligations.[1]

## Reception

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] covered the filing as evidence of movement on election integrity under the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] urged that any resulting agreement include continuing court jurisdiction for enforcement, pointing to the Judicial Watch v. Griswold NVRA settlement in Colorado, and argued that durable roll integrity ultimately requires decentralized voter roll management.[1]
