# Men, Where Have You Gone? Please Come Back.

"Men, Where Have You Gone? Please Come Back." is a Modern Love essay published by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] on 20 June 2025, in which a 54-year-old woman describes noticing the absence of men from the dating scene in Wicker Park, Chicago.[1]

## Content

The author sets the scene on a warm Saturday night in May on a vibrant block crowded with seven restaurants, describing low lights, shared plates and angled shoulders, and then the crowd moving past: women walking in pairs or alone and dressed with care, while table after table showed a noticeable absence of men who appeared to be on dates.[1] She recounts having dated since the mid-1980s, having been married, been a mother, divorced, and had many relationships long and short, and remembers a period when part of heterosexual male culture involved showing up with a woman to signal status, success and desirability.[1] Women, she writes, were once signifiers of value even to other men — a dynamic she calls not always healthy but one that required men to show up and put in effort — and she argues that dynamic "has quietly collapsed," with many men no longer seeking women to impress other men or to connect across difference, performing instead alone and having "filtered us out."[1]

## Badlands response

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] rejected the framing, arguing that if the dynamic had collapsed the collapse would not have been quiet but the result of a sustained campaign against "the patriarchy" spearheaded in part by The New York Times itself.[1] She maintained the dynamic has not collapsed at all: outside what she called the Marxist bubble the author occupies, women remain a status symbol for high-value men, who signal status through high-value women.[1] Her definition of a high-value woman is one who works in partnership with her man, provides wise counsel but submits to his authority, wants to raise his children and to provide a sanctuary in his home, invoking the line "I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine."[1] She contrasted this with what she described as an insufferable nag demanding that her man castrate himself, and argued that years of attacking masculinity, casting every man as a secret predator, and mocking male culture and men's spaces could not occur without repercussions.[1] She closed by dismissing the essay's premise with "thoughts and prayers for your ongoing misery."[1] The item supplied the "Mutiny of The Men" element of the brief's title.[1]
