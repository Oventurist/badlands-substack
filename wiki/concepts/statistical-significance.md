# Statistical Significance

Statistical significance — the convention by which researchers decide whether an observed result is too improbable to be attributed to chance — appears in the Badlands Media corpus chiefly as an argumentative device in defenses of the [[q-operation|Q operation]]. In the June 27, 2024 essay "Q is Real, Because Math Says So," published by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], the standard threshold of mainstream science is turned back against skeptics of Q.[1]

## The 5% convention

The essay states that researchers commonly use 5% as the threshold for rejecting or accepting a hypothesis, meaning that a scenario with less than a 5% likelihood of occurring is by standard practice rejected as a causal explanation.[1] It concedes that there is no hard and fast rule requiring 5% specifically, but presents it as the working standard used by many researchers in mainstream science, citing the popular statistics book *Naked Statistics* for readers who wish to see how academia handles the question.[1]

## Application to the Q question

From this premise the author constructs an inversion argument: if the probability that Q is a [[larp|LARP]] is vanishingly small — he offers 0.000000000001% as a deliberately arbitrary stand-in figure — then rejecting the hypothesis that Q is a legitimate insider would require rejecting almost the entirety of mainstream science's own inferential standard.[1] He notes for the record that because the factors involved number in the thousands, the figure is a large round number chosen to make a point rather than a computed estimate, and argues that a precise calculation is unnecessary given that the dice analogy already showed how few factors are required to produce highly divergent probabilities.[1] He adds that he is himself no admirer of mainstream science or academia, and that his point is only that critics cannot label Q a LARP without simultaneously disavowing their own methodology.[1]

## Rationality as proportion

The essay extends the statistical framing into a general account of rationality, observing that the word *rational* contains *ratio* and arguing that the essence of rationality is putting things in proper proportion, while irrationality disregards proportion and careens toward extremes.[1] On this reading, betting on the 0.00000000001% outcome rather than the 99.99999999999% one is the genuinely extreme position, and the author remarks on the irony that Q believers rather than Q skeptics are the ones labeled extremists, invoking what he calls the law of psychological projection.[1] He also rejects "it sounds too crazy to be true" as a logical argument, glossing it as the claim that "the limits of my imagination are synonymous with the limits of reality itself."[1]
