# Ritual Abuse

Ritual abuse is the organized, ceremonial abuse of victims — frequently children — by cults and, in the framing of Badlands Media, by military- and intelligence-affiliated groups that have "perfected the art of mind control."[1] The Badlands article "Gazing Into the Abyss pt.2: a Therapist's Tale" builds on the earlier piece "The Deep State's Sinister Core" by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], which set out the general definition, and focuses instead on clinical case material.[1]

## The credibility problem

The article argues that brushing up against the reality of ritual abuse is jarring for the average person, and that the knee-jerk reaction is to conclude a victim's story is fabricated — a reaction the perpetrator groups are said to count on.[1] It asserts the phenomenon is very real and always has been.[1]

To rebut the idea that such crimes belong only to distant places, the article catalogues international cases: the Belgian town of Sars-la-Buissière, where Marc Dutroux tortured and killed children; the French town of Angers, where 45 children were raped and sold into prostitution by their own parents; the forests around Milan, where children were said to have been ritually sacrificed; London, where a child's torso found in the Thames prompted a Scotland Yard investigation; and Portugal, where more than 600 cases of sexual violence against children occurred at the state-run Casa Pia institution for poor children and under-age orphans.[1] The article's conclusion is that the same activity occurs in the reader's own backyard, or at minimum in the nearest metropolitan area.[1]

## Clinical burden

For psychotherapists, victims of ritual abuse are described as by far the most challenging patients they will encounter, because such patients have not only endured the worst abuses imaginable but in many cases have been forced to participate in the torture, rape and murder of others as part of their own abuse.[1] Clinical psychologist [[alison-miller|Alison Miller]] characterizes the start of this work as a "hit the ground running" period demanding new skills and knowledge under the pressure of horrific disclosures.[1] Dr. James Randall Noblitt's initiation into the same field was outlined in the preceding Badlands article.[1]

## Alters, parts and programming

In the ritual-abuse and mind-control context, the terms "parts" or "alters" denote fractured, compartmentalized aspects of the primary or "host" identity that have taken on their own life and memory, generally forming when trauma is so harsh that the host identity could not continue functioning while retaining awareness of it.[1] Perpetrator groups exploit this structure through "programs" — conditioned responses trained into child parts that can be triggered to make a client fall asleep in session, report the contents of therapy back to the group, lose the ability to understand English, self-harm, or attack the therapist.[1] Post-hypnotic triggering of the kind described in G. H. Estabrooks' *Hypnotism* is cited as the mechanism behind a cult "pick-up system," in which hospitalized members felt an urge to smoke at a precise time each night and were met outside by handlers.[1]

## Risk to clinicians

Therapists in this field face direct danger and professional isolation.[1] The first generation of such clinicians were told by clients that one massive cult was everywhere, knew everything, had access to state-of-the-art technology, and would kill clients and therapists alike to suppress disclosure.[1] Miller's own case notes record death threats, surveillance of her office building, and an incident in which clients were driven to her home, abused by a woman impersonating her, and instructed to burn her house down with her family inside.[1]

## Recovery

The article stresses that outcomes are not uniformly fatal: two of Miller's "first four" clients reintegrated their alters, recovered, and now live productive lives far from the cult's grasp.[1] It argues this shows humans can heal from virtually any psychological wound given determination and compassion, and that recovered survivors often become effective advocates and helpers for those still suffering.[1] Related material cited by the article includes a [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] special on ritual abuse, a Mary Knight interview, a 2009 presentation featuring Randy Noblitt, Miller's 2017 Survivorship Conference talk, and a Ted Gunderson presentation on FBI files concerning CIA involvement in satanic ritual abuse.[1]
