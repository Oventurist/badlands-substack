# 2024 Super Tuesday

**Super Tuesday 2024**, held on March 5, 2024, was the largest single day of nominating contests in the 2024 United States presidential primary calendar. Both President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] dominated the day's contests, piling up wins in states including Texas — the second-largest delegate prize of the night — and moving effectively unopposed toward their parties' nominations and a November rematch for the White House.[1]

## Republican results

Trump's only remaining primary rival, former South Carolina governor [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]], secured Vermont according to the Associated Press, depriving Trump of a clean sweep of the night.[1] Even with that lone defeat, Trump won eleven other states by 11 p.m. Eastern time, when polls closed in California, the state holding the biggest delegate trove of the evening.[1] Speaking at Mar-a-Lago, his Palm Beach, Florida home, Trump made no mention of Haley, instead calling for "unity," declaring: "We want to have unity, and we're going to have unity, and it's going to happen very quickly."[1]

Badlands commentary treated the Vermont result as trivially small and heavily amplified by the press. Writing for Badlands Media, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the running tally left Haley with only the District of Columbia and Vermont while Trump took the rest of the nation, and that the media's focus on the Green Mountain State reflected Trump living "rent free" in their heads.[1] She emphasized that Vermont operates an open primary in which independents and Democrats shape the outcome, and that Haley's projected margin was roughly 3,000 votes — hardly a comeback.[1]

## Democratic results and the uncommitted protest vote

Biden won overwhelmingly in every state called by the Associated Press on his way to an expected sweep. His single stumble came in American Samoa, a small U.S. territory in the Pacific, where a little-known Democratic businessman, Jason Palmer, defeated the sitting president.[1] Biden used the night to pivot toward the general election, stating: "My message to the country is this: Every generation of Americans will face a moment when it has to defend democracy... This is our moment. This is our fight. Together, we will win."[1]

A significant feature of the Democratic contests was the performance of "uncommitted" ballots. In Minnesota, Massachusetts, and North Carolina, uncommitted totals reached ten percent or more.[1] The [[uncommitted-movement|vote uncommitted movement]], a protest effort associated with Representative [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] and the progressive "Squad," was intended as a show of force pressuring Biden over Palestine.[1] Ashe in America argued that some of those voters may simply have been dissatisfied with Biden personally, and that in either case the uncommitted coalition's numbers were problematic for the president heading into November, especially given the support [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] was expected to erode from his coalition.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the outcome itself was never in doubt and that the significance of the night lay elsewhere: not in codifying Trump's renewed ascendancy but in "demonstrating that he never left," and that the movement behind him never left either.[1] In this reading, Super Tuesday codified the existential threat the movement posed to what Burning Bright calls the "System of Systems," and preceded an anticipated cascade of narrative crisis and institutional panic.[1] Trump, in this framing, functions less as a conventional leader than as a symbol of what has been taken from the public and of what would be reclaimed — politically, administratively, and culturally.[1] Burning Bright described the moment as the "narrowing phase."[1]
