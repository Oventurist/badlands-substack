# DOT Automatic Airline Refund Rule (2024)

The DOT automatic airline refund rule is a set of final regulations issued by the Biden administration on Wednesday, April 24, 2024, requiring airlines to automatically issue cash refunds for cancellations and significant delays and to better disclose fees for baggage and reservation cancellations.[1]

## Provisions

The [[us-department-of-transportation|Transportation Department]] said airlines would be required to provide automatic cash refunds within a few days for canceled flights and for "significant" delays.[1] Under the regulations in force before the rule, airlines themselves decided how long a delay had to last before a refund obligation was triggered; the administration removed that discretion by defining a significant delay as at least three hours for domestic flights and six hours for international ones.[1]

Airlines remained free to offer a passenger an alternative flight or a travel credit in place of cash, but consumers were given the right to reject such an offer and take the refund.[1] The rule also tightened disclosure requirements around ancillary fees, an area of longstanding consumer complaint.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary paired the refund rule with the [[noncompete-agreement-ban|FTC noncompete ban]] issued the previous day, describing them together as rare wins for the Biden administration in the public mind and as genuine push-back against industries that had gone too far in stripping customers and employees of leverage.[1] The stated reservation was structural: that both announcements carried an implicit endorsement of the administrative authority of federal agencies over private enterprise, and that in a genuinely free market divorced from administrative oversight such problems would correct themselves.[1]
