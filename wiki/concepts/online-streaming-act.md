# Online Streaming Act (Bill C-11)

The Online Streaming Act, known as Bill C-11, is Canadian legislation identified in Badlands Media coverage as one of the principal instruments in the multinational regulatory attack on online speech.[1]

## Placement among censorship instruments

Ahead of the 2024 U.S. election, Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] published a piece for Badlands Media outlining a multinational offensive against free speech and cataloguing the tools available to censors.[1] That inventory comprised Canada's C-11, the United Kingdom's [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]], and the European Union's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[1]

## Relative weight

In the April 2025 assessment, the article concluded that of these instruments the Digital Services Act was the one with "the most teeth," and the vehicle in which recently dismissed disinformation practitioners had placed their hope — attributing this to Europe having been the true progenitor of the censorship apparatus in the first place.[1] C-11 is accordingly presented as part of a coordinated Western regulatory pattern rather than an isolated national measure.[1]
