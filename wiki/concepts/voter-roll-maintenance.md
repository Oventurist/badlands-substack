# Voter Roll Maintenance

Voter roll maintenance, sometimes called voter roll hygiene or list maintenance, is the statutory obligation of state election officials to keep registration lists accurate by removing voters who have died, moved away, or otherwise become ineligible.[1] Election-integrity advocates treat it as a precondition for secure elections, arguing that inflated rolls create the conditions for excess ballots to circulate during an election period.[1]

## Litigation over list maintenance

List-maintenance suits produced several of the merits victories catalogued in reviews of the [[2020-election-lawsuits|2020 election lawsuits]].[1] [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] challenged the voter-roll hygiene of Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]], who entered a consent decree agreeing to provide the group with annual data updates for five years; the settlement's terms barred Judicial Watch from characterizing it as an admission of fault.[1] Comparable cleanup agreements were secured against Michigan Secretary of State [[jocelyn-benson|Jocelyn Benson]] and against Pennsylvania Secretary [[kathy-boockvar|Kathy Boockvar]], the latter through the [[public-interest-legal-foundation|Public Interest Legal Foundation]].[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that if the Colorado, Michigan, and Pennsylvania rolls were as clean as officials claimed, the litigation would have offered an opportunity to prove it rather than to settle.[1] The publication linked list maintenance to broader claims that inflated rolls are used to "make it rain ballots" during the election period, and that statutory controls promised to prevent disenfranchisement do not function as advertised.[1]
