# Syntactic Metaverse

The **syntactic metaverse** is a feature of [[chris-langan|Chris Langan]]'s [[cognitive-theoretic-model-of-the-universe|Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe]] (CTMU) as summarised by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] in Part 1 of [[the-white-pill-emporium|The White Pill Emporium]] (22 October 2024).[1] The author glosses it, for brevity, as "all parts and expressions of reality considered simultaneously."[1]

## Description

Because materialism holds that only material things exist, the syntactic metaverse is presented as a necessary object that materialism cannot accommodate: an information structure containing all possibilities and metaphysically woven into everything, not in a material sense but as a *ruleset* that governs matter.[1] The same structure is said to double as a "field of potential" holding future possible states and coordinates for material things.[1]

## Relation to time and intuition

The author introduces the concept alongside the question of where the past and future are located, noting that neither can be measured or physically pointed to, and yet few argue that they do not exist; physicalism, on his account, is confined to the physical-sensory world of the present moment, and he directs sceptics to David Hume's problem of induction.[1] He offers anecdotal evidence for a "grand, interconnected awareness" — reporting that of hundreds of people asked over some fifteen years whether they had thought of a long-unseen person shortly before hearing from them, every single one said yes.[1]

The syntactic metaverse is described as one feature of what is ultimately a cognition-based theory of reality, hence the "Cognitive Theoretic" element of the CTMU's name, with the implication that each person is "quite literally the universe itself observing itself."[1] From this the author derives the doctrine of observer participancy — see [[observer-effect|observer effect]] — and speculates that [[q-operation|Q]] used anons as a kind of signal repeater to seed ideas in the collective psyche, and that technologies such as [[project-looking-glass|Project Looking Glass]] might permit inspection of timelines and collective thought-forms.[1]
