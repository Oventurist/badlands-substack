# New Economic Policy

The New Economic Policy (NEP) was the program of economic reform introduced by [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]] and [[vladimir-lenin|Vladimir Lenin]] upon taking charge of the Russian economy, characterized in Badlands Media coverage as a destructive wave of liberalization rather than a pragmatic retreat.[1]

The policy saw vast liberalization of the entire state, with Western corporate powers sweeping in to buy up former national utilities for pennies on the dollar.[1] The most powerful Western magnate granted full access under the policy was [[armand-hammer|Armand Hammer]] of [[occidental-petroleum|Occidental Petroleum]], who became the principal moderator of nearly every business deal struck between the Soviet government and Western corporations during the 1920s.[1] Hammer recalled Lenin telling him: "We do not need doctors, we need businessmen… communism is not working, and we must change to a New Economic Policy."[1]

The article argues that under the NEP Russia sank into brutal economic enslavement to foreign powers at a pace which would not be seen again for over sixty years, and that the liberalization of the "dark 1920s" closely paralleled the free-trade and privatization program of [[perestroika|Perestroika]] in the 1990s.[1] Hammer was forced out of Russia the moment Trotsky was expelled, returning to dominance only after [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]]'s death in 1953.[1]
