# Masterpiece Cakeshop Litigation

The Masterpiece Cakeshop litigation is a series of legal actions spanning more than eleven years against Colorado baker [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]], owner of [[masterpiece-cakeshop|Masterpiece Cakeshop]], arising from his refusal on religious grounds to create certain custom cakes.[1]

## First case

Phillips declined to create a custom cake for a same-sex wedding, prompting a 2012 lawsuit that ultimately reached the US Supreme Court.[1] In 2018 the nine justices ruled that the state of Colorado had been hostile to Phillips's religious beliefs and that government cannot compel anyone to create custom works of art communicating a message.[1]

## Second case

Also in 2018, Phillips faced a second lawsuit, this time from a transgender lawyer who requested a cake celebrating a gender transition.[1] When Phillips declined, he was sued again on grounds that he had discriminated against the requester.[1] The [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] later agreed to hear the case, which Phillips called "the light at the end of the tunnel" after more than eleven years in court.[1] "The state court turned us down in our first case, but I think they realize now that I serve everybody," he told Fox News Digital.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Phillips as one of the most persecuted Christians in Colorado, arguing that his religious convictions had made him a repeated target of state officials and that the successive suits demonstrate the litigants were not pursuing tolerance or equality but compelled denunciation of Biblical teaching.[1] In that reading, "it's not about cake" — the courts were effectively being asked to rule on the book of Leviticus, and the willingness of courts to hear such cases at all indicated that the religious protections of the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] were in danger.[1] The commentary situated the dispute within a domestic culture war continuing even as attention was fixed on escalation abroad.[1]
