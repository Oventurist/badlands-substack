# Umbrella Movement

The Umbrella Movement, also referred to as the Hong Kong Democracy Protests of 2014, was a mass civil disobedience campaign in [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]] triggered by Beijing's refusal to deliver promised democratization.[1]

## Cause

Within the 50-year autonomy deal it had been promised that steps would be taken to ensure universal voting rights for Hong Kong citizens by 2010.[1] There was unease within the [[hong-kong-pro-democracy-movement|pro-democracy movement]] as to whether even the status quo would remain intact, let alone whether [[china|China]] would allow citizens to elect their own Chief Executive and Legislative Council.[1] Instead, by 2010 China had reversed its stand and decided not to go through with the promised democratization.[1]

## The protests

In the fall of 2014, hundreds of thousands of Hong Kongers took to the streets, occupying the financial district for 79 days and venting their rage at what they said was a broken promise.[1] These protests were the largest act of civil disobedience in Hong Kong's history up to that point.[1] China responded by explaining that, to it, the [[sino-british-joint-declaration|Joint Declaration]] was no more than a historic document and was worth nothing.[1]

## Aftermath

Many participants were prosecuted for the peaceful protest and were sentenced in 2018; among them was the father of activist [[samuel-chu|Samuel Chu]], who received two years.[1] By the time of those sentencings, China had proposed a new extradition law allowing the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] to send Hong Kong citizens to the mainland for trial, setting off the [[2019-hong-kong-anti-extradition-protests|2019 protests]].[1]
