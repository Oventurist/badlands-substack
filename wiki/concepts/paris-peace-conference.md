# Paris Peace Conference

The Paris Peace Conference of 1919 settled the terms ending the First World War and produced the Treaty of Versailles.[1]

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 1" recounts that by late 1918 the stalemate on the Western Front, combined with the entry of America into the war, forced Germany and the Central Powers to accept peace terms, paving the way for the conference.[1] Attending were President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] and his closest advisors, including Colonel [[edward-mandell-house|Edward Mandell House]], international bankers [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]] and [[bernard-baruch|Bernard Baruch]], and almost two dozen members of [[the-inquiry|the Inquiry]].[1]

The attendees embraced Wilson's plan for peace, including the formation of a [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]]; but under American law the covenant required ratification by the U.S. Senate, which declined, many senators distrusting a supranational organization.[1]

## The Majestic Hotel meeting

Despite that setback, House met with British and American delegates at Paris's Majestic Hotel on May 30 and resolved to form an "Institute of International Affairs" with branches in both America and England — the article's framing being that if denied their League publicly, they would carry on their goals behind the scenes.[1] The English branch became the Royal Institute for International Affairs, today [[chatham-house|Chatham House]], and the U.S. branch was incorporated on July 21 as the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]].[1]
