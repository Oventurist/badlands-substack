# Due Process

**Due process** is the constitutional guarantee that no person may be deprived of life, liberty or property without the procedural protections afforded by law, and it is the doctrinal core of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]'s objection to the [[colorado-ballot-disqualification-case|Colorado ballot disqualification case]].[1]

## Application in the Colorado disqualification dispute

In a January 1, 2024 essay, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that without the due process required to find someone guilty of a crime, four Colorado state judges ruled that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was guilty of an offense — insurrection — with which he had never been charged and for which he had therefore never been given the opportunity to assert the constitutional protections available to criminal defendants.[1] Those protections, he enumerated, include a jury of one's peers, the right of cross-examination, the right to counsel, and due process itself.[1]

DeLarme framed the absence of these safeguards as a question about the health of the system: whether a body that assigns culpability to an uncharged individual in order to disqualify him from running for president can be described as a functioning democracy, or whether it is setting an extremely dangerous precedent.[1] Accepting the proposition that "we all know it was an insurrection, and he did it, so we don't need a trial," he wrote, is a slippery slope toward being able to charge anyone at any time with a crime regardless of whether they actually did anything.[1]

He connected the argument to the unavailability of any conviction under [[18-usc-2383|18 U.S.C. § 2383]] and to the free-speech protections of [[brandenburg-v-ohio|Brandenburg v. Ohio]] and [[naacp-v-claiborne-hardware|NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware]], arguing that the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] had substituted a civil finding of fact for the criminal process the Constitution requires before a disqualifying determination under the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] can attach.[1]
