# Proposed Multinational Peacekeeping Force in Gaza

In late 2023, U.S. officials and partner governments began early, closed-door discussions about establishing a multinational peacekeeping force in Gaza for the period after Israel dislodged [[hamas|Hamas]] from power.[1]

## Senate confirmation of talks

Senators [[chris-van-hollen|Chris Van Hollen]] of Maryland and [[richard-blumenthal|Richard Blumenthal]] of Connecticut confirmed on Wednesday, November 1, 2023 that such talks were underway — the clearest indication to that point that the United States and its partners were seriously weighing the deployment of foreign troops to the enclave.[1] The force was not expected to include American troops.[1] "There are ongoing conversations regarding the possible composition of an international force," Van Hollen said, declining to elaborate and describing the discussions as "very preliminary and fragile."[1] He added that he thought "it'd be important to have some kind of multinational force in Gaza as a transition to whatever comes next."[1]

## Background

Hamas, which killed 1,400 people in Israel on October 7, 2023, had governed Gaza for more than fifteen years.[1] Israel responded with a military operation aimed at ending Hamas rule, comprising a large bombing campaign, ground invasion and siege that had killed more than 8,000 people by the time of the senators' remarks.[1]
