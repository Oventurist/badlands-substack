# Papal Conclave

The **papal conclave** is the closed election by which the College of Cardinals chooses a new pope. It was placed at the center of the Badlands News Brief for April 21, 2025 by the death of [[pope-francis|Pope Francis]], whose passing at age 88 triggered the succession machinery at the [[vatican|Vatican]].[1]

## Process

When a pope dies, the Vatican begins its traditional nine days of mourning, known as the *novendiales*.[1] The election of a successor begins between 15 and 20 days after the death.[1] The camerlengo, a cardinal of the Catholic Church, is charged with organizing the election, the process known as the conclave.[1] During the intervening period the Church is in the state of interregnum, or *sede vacante* — the "vacant seat" — in which there is no pope in power.[1]

## Cultural depiction

The mechanics of the conclave were recently dramatized in the Oscar-winning film *Conclave*, which the Badlands commentary discussed directly.[1] Badlands writer Ashe in America described watching the film the day Francis died as a strange coincidence, saying the political posturing, secrets and sabotage depicted were simply human nature, but calling the ending — which she declined to spoil beyond a reference to Katy Perry's "divine feminine" — the genuinely odd element, and wondering aloud how realistic the portrayed process actually is.[1] Other Badlands commentary in the same brief raised the "strange voting process to determine a new pope and how the numbers shake out."[1]

## May 2025 conclave

The conclave convened to elect a successor to Pope Francis opened on Wednesday, May 7, 2025, and ended its first day without electing a pope, an outcome few had expected to differ.[2] Kurt Martens, a professor of canon law at The Catholic University, told CNN that he had anticipated a late first ballot both because Cardinal Cantalamessa "has a tendency to preach long" and because the taking of the oath as the cardinals entered the conclave "took forever" — much longer than the previous time.[2] Martens also noted that each of the 133 cardinals must walk individually to the altar to cast a ballot, and that revisers must be chosen to confirm the scrutineers did their job, making the process inherently lengthy: "So be patient. And extend your airtime a little longer."[2]

With the cardinals sequestered and their devices confiscated, coverage during the waiting period ranged from historical listicles to explanations of vestments to political speculation.[2] A viral moment came when CBS Mornings co-anchor [[tony-dokoupil|Tony Dokoupil]] suggested on the network's live broadcast that the cardinals were "raw-dogging" the social-media-free election, a remark that generated backlash over the use of modern slang in conclave coverage.[2] Badlands writer Ashe in America argued that no one actually disputed Dokoupil's point — that the cardinals had entered sequestration without cell phones — only his phrasing, calling offense at words "a very first-world problem," and asking whether societal degradation had reached a point where "Holy Men" could not go a few days without Instagram.[2]
