# 2025 Los Angeles Immigration Raids and National Guard Deployment

In June 2025, federal immigration raids in Los Angeles triggered mass protests, a federal deployment of California National Guard troops and Marines ordered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and a fast-moving legal battle over whether the president could keep those troops federalized.[1]

## Litigation over control of the Guard

U.S. District Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] of San Francisco ruled on June 12, 2025 that Trump had broken the law when he mobilized thousands of Guard members amid the protests, and ordered the troops returned to the control of Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] by noon Friday.[1] In a 36-page decision Breyer wrote that Trump's actions "were illegal — both exceeding the scope of his statutory authority and violating the 10th Amendment to the United States Constitution," and said he was "troubled by the implication" in the administration's argument that protest against the federal government — "a core civil liberty protected by the First Amendment" — could justify a finding of rebellion.[1]

In a late-night order the same Thursday, the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|U.S. 9th Circuit Court of Appeals]] paused Breyer's order.[1] A three-judge panel, including two Trump appointees and one Biden appointee, set a hearing for the following Tuesday, leaving the [[national-guard|National Guard]] federalized through the weekend.[1]

## The Padilla incident

On the same day, Democratic Senator [[alex-padilla|Alex Padilla]] was shoved, forced to the ground and handcuffed by security agents after attempting to question Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] at a Los Angeles press conference about the raids.[1]

## Economic backlash and Trump's concession

Trump publicly acknowledged that his "very aggressive" immigration policies were removing long-time workers from the farming and hospitality industries, writing on Truth Social that "Our great Farmers and people in the Hotel and Leisure business have been stating that our very aggressive policy on immigration is taking very good, long time workers away from them, with those jobs being almost impossible to replace," and promising that changes were coming.[1] The concession followed reports of federal immigration raids on farms that stoked fears among agricultural workers during the summer growing season.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that California and Newsom had less than a day to celebrate their district-court win, and that with the Guard still under Trump's control heading into the "No Kings Summer of Love Kick Off," she expected protest-rioters to try to provoke a photographic response fitting the regime's desired optics of Trump as George III.[1] On the Padilla arrest she argued that "no one is above the law," saying Padilla was on camera interfering with ICE agents and resisting arrest.[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded to the farm-labor story by suggesting the affected companies "should have no problem purchasing Trump Cards for these very important (otherwise) illegal aliens."[1]
