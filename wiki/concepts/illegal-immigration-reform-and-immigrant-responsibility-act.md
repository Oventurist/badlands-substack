# Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act of 1996

The Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act is a 1996 federal statute governing immigration enforcement that, among other provisions, grants the Secretary of Homeland Security sweeping authority to set aside other legal requirements in order to build border infrastructure.[1]

## Waiver authority

Under the act, as invoked by Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] in October 2023, "Congress granted to the Secretary of Homeland Security the authority to waive all legal requirements that I, in my sole discretion, determine necessary to ensure the expeditious construction of barriers and roads authorized by [the act]."[1] The provision therefore allows a single cabinet officer to suspend environmental, procurement, and other statutory constraints without further congressional action.[1]

## 2023 invocation

Mayorkas relied on the statute when he waived 26 federal laws and regulations to accelerate construction of additional border wall in Starr County, Texas, a key hotspot for illegal crossings.[1] The waiver notice, posted to the U.S. Federal Register, stated that the Secretary had determined it was necessary to waive certain laws, regulations, and other legal requirements to ensure the expeditious construction of barriers and roads near the international land border in Starr County.[1] The notice cited 245,000 migrant encounters over the preceding year in the Rio Grande Valley Sector, which includes Starr County, and Mayorkas found an "acute and immediate need" for physical barriers and roads to prevent unlawful entries in the project areas.[1] Reporting noted that law enforcement had encountered more than 7 million people entering the country illegally since President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took office.[1]
