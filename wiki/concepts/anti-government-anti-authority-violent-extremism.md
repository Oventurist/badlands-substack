# Anti-Government or Anti-Authority Violent Extremism

Anti-Government or Anti-Authority Violent Extremism (AGAAVE) is a threat category created by the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] to evaluate domestic threats. According to a Newsweek report of October 2023 summarized in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], the category encompasses individuals the Bureau deems a threat but who do not fall under anarchist, militia, or sovereign citizen groupings.[1]

## The Newsweek report and FBI denial

Newsweek, citing more than a dozen current and former anonymous government officials, reported that the AGAAVE program often investigates supporters of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ahead of the 2024 presidential election.[1] The FBI denied the report the following day. "Any allegation that the FBI targets individuals solely for their political beliefs is categorically false," an FBI spokesperson said. "The FBI investigates those who commit acts of violence or threaten violence, and we do not take action based on political belief or any First Amendment protected activity."[1]

The spokesperson added that the "threat posed by domestic violent extremists is persistent, evolving, and deadly," and that the Bureau's goal is "to detect and stop terrorist attacks," with focus on "potential criminal violations, violence, and threats of violence."[1] The statement described AGAAVE as "one category of domestic terrorism, as well as one of the FBI's top threat priorities," including "anarchist violent extremists, militia violent extremists, sovereign citizen violent extremists, and other violent extremists—some of whom are motivated by a desire to harm those with a real or perceived association with a political party or faction."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that "words matter" and that the FBI's use of "solely based on their political beliefs" amounts to an admission that political ideology is a contributing factor in the Bureau's targeting formula — a denial that does not even rule out ideology being the primary or lead factor, since "solely" merely means it is not the only one.[1] Rather than calming tensions and assuring the public that government is not weaponized against Trump supporters, she wrote, the statement "practically confirms it."[1]

She further objected that motive is something that ought to be determined by a court, and that the word "violent" was deployed without specificity by an administration that "views words as violence."[1] She noted that the majority of January 6 defendants — including many detained without charges — have no history of violence, concluding: "Minority Report is here, and it's woker than I remember."[1]
