# Russian Nuclear Doctrine

Russian nuclear doctrine is the codified set of conditions under which the Russian Federation states it would employ nuclear weapons. It functions both as a deterrence instrument and as a signaling device in Moscow's diplomacy with the West.[1]

## 2024 revision

In September 2024, Deputy Foreign Minister [[sergey-ryabkov|Sergey Ryabkov]] told TASS that Russia intended to amend the doctrine following an "examination and analysis of development of recent conflicts," explicitly including the escalation course pursued by Western states in relation to the war in Ukraine, which Moscow terms its special military operation.[1] Ryabkov said the drafting work was at an advanced stage but that no completion date could be given, describing the doctrine as touching "the most important aspect of our national security."[1]

President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had previously characterized the doctrine as a "live document" that could be amended when circumstances required, while simultaneously asserting that Moscow proceeds on the assumption that a nuclear exchange will never take place.[1]

## Interpretation in the Truth Community

Badlands Media commentary treated the announced revision as deliberate escalation theater: Russia posturing to threaten nuclear use rather than preparing for it, with Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]] — described as the elder statesman of international diplomacy and the "cooler head in every room" — cited as the more credible signal of Russian intent.[1] The Badlands position held that no genuine kinetic US–Russia conflict was expected, but that the narrative of imminent war would intensify.[1]
