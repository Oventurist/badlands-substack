# Bitcoin 2025 Conference

The Bitcoin 2025 conference, held in Las Vegas in late May 2025, was described in press coverage as "ground zero for cryptocurrency's political coronation."[1] Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] delivered what was characterized as unprecedented federal backing for the asset class at the event, while prediction markets heated up around Bitcoin's march toward $150,000 by year-end.[1]

## Price forecasts and prediction markets

[[kalshi|Kalshi]], the federally regulated prediction market, allowed users to trade on Bitcoin price outcomes through year-end; its contract "Will Bitcoin hit $150,000 by December 31, 2025?" was trading at 35 cents, implying roughly a 35% probability.[1] Several industry watchers who spoke to CNBC forecast [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] would reach $200,000 in 2025.[1] Sina Golara, co-founder and COO of 21st Capital, used a quantile regression model projecting a year-end range of $136,000 to $285,000.[1] Analysts from Bitwise, Standard Chartered, and VanEck placed Bitcoin in the $180,000–$200,000 range, with consensus pointing to continued institutional adoption and regulatory clarity as the primary drivers.[1]

## Badlands coverage and the media contrast

Badlands Media sent contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] to the conference, broadcasting live from the Rumble Studio in Las Vegas.[1] Among the segments highlighted was a conversation with Ryan Matta about using Bitcoin to fund offensive operations against child sex traffickers.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that legacy media coverage of the conference was strikingly sparse on the substance and ideas presented, and that Bitcoin headlines were instead almost exclusively focused on a cryptocurrency torture case in New York.[1] She wrote that she had expected reporting on new applications and use cases for building "some (somewhat rational and sane) version of Gtopia," and concluded: "Fear sells, but this is more than that. It's almost like they don't want people to imagine the future and consider the art of the possible. They just want people afraid."[1]

That competing storyline concerned an investigation by the [[nypd|New York Police Department]] into two detectives who had provided security at a Manhattan townhouse where two cryptocurrency investors were accused of torturing a man for three weeks.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the conference in strategic terms, arguing that with [[donald-trump|Trump]] signaling a massive shift into the future of not just finance but asymmetric and fifth-generation warfighting, the crypto space was "WELL worth engaging with" as a new battlespace.[1]
