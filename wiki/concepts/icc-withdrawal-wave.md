# ICC Withdrawal Wave (2025–2026)

The ICC withdrawal wave refers to the sequence of states announcing their exit from the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] beginning in 2025, driven by accusations that the Hague-based body is ineffective and geographically biased.[1]

## Participating states

Niger, Mali, and [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] all began withdrawing in 2025. [[venezuela|Venezuela]] announced its formal withdrawal in late July 2026, accusing the court of "geographical bias" that disproportionately targets countries in Africa and Latin America. [[chad|Chad]] followed days later, submitting a formal letter to the United Nations and accusing the court of ineffectiveness and bias against African countries. Under the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]], withdrawal takes at least one year to complete.[1]

## US involvement

US Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said in July 2026 that the United States was launching a campaign to dismantle the ICC's threat to national sovereignty. A February 2025 executive order signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposed sanctions on the court over its investigations of [[israel|Israel]]. The [[state-department|State Department]]'s Bureau of African Affairs publicly welcomed Chad's withdrawal on X and called on more of the court's 125 member states to withdraw and "reclaim their sovereignty from this flawed institution."[1]
