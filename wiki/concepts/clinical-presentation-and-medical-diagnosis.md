# Clinical Presentation and Medical Diagnosis

Clinical presentation and medical diagnosis are the subject of an extended [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], prompted by September 2023 reporting that physicians were struggling to distinguish COVID-19 from allergies and the common cold.[1]

## Occasion: shifting COVID-19 symptoms

Medical professionals reported growing difficulty telling COVID-19 apart from allergies and the common cold, with the earlier hallmark signs — loss of taste or smell and a dry cough — no longer characteristic.[1] Dr. Erick Eiting, vice chair of operations for emergency medicine at Mount Sinai Downtown in New York City, said the symptoms had shifted to "a lot of congestion, sometimes sneezing, usually a mild sore throat," with sore throat typically appearing first and nasal congestion following.[1] Eiting acknowledged that identification depended entirely on testing: "The only way that we knew that it was Covid was because we happened to be testing them."[1]

## Clinical versus medical diagnosis

Deschamps drew a distinction he argued is routinely and incorrectly blurred: a clinical diagnosis is made from a patient's medical history, physical examination, and symptoms, while a medical diagnosis combines clinical findings with laboratory findings.[1] "Presentation" refers to how a disease-afflicted person appears to a doctor or clinician, and he located much of the system's vulnerability there — the place where, in his account, a "medical mafia" applies pressure to get professionals and patients to accept treatments that make matters worse.[1]

## The AIDS illustration

To illustrate the ambiguity of presentation, Deschamps listed conditions whose clinical picture resembles AIDS, including early-stage HIV infection, primary HIV infection (acute retroviral syndrome), other retroviral infections such as HTLV types 1 and 2, viral infections such as Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus and hepatitis B, bacterial infections such as tuberculosis and syphilis, parasitic infections such as toxoplasmosis and cryptococcosis, fungal infections such as candidiasis and histoplasmosis, malignancies such as Kaposi's sarcoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, drug-induced immunodeficiency, autoimmune disorders such as systemic lupus erythematosus and rheumatoid arthritis, primary immunodeficiency disorders such as common variable immunodeficiency and Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome, and nutritional deficiencies such as vitamin A deficiency and protein-energy malnutrition.[1] He then contrasted the side-effect profile of the former HIV drug AZT — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, fatigue, muscle pain, anemia, and neutropenia — with the symptoms attributed to HIV-induced AIDS, including weight loss, fatigue, fever, night sweats, swollen lymph nodes, diarrhea, cough, shortness of breath, skin rash, mouth sores, headaches, confusion, depression, and anxiety.[1] His conclusion was that a physician could not reliably distinguish the two without a test.[1]

## Dependence on medical authority

From this Deschamps argued that portions of the medical system are effectively blind to certain diseases outside of laboratory testing, and are therefore wholly dependent on medical authorities — leaving doctors unable to use their own judgment to override bad medical or public-health policy.[1] He asserted that most physicians are trained to accept medical dogma rather than question and verify it, and that only a small share actively test the validity of diagnoses.[1] A doctor, he wrote, cannot oppose an authoritarian public-health policy and remain licensed.[1]

## Policy harms alleged

He offered three examples of harms he attributed to authoritative public-health policy rather than to individual physicians: gender-affirming care, which he characterized as causing sterilization and castration; COVID-19 treatment protocols, which he said caused deaths through ventilators, Remdesivir, or the withholding of early treatment; and en masse cesarean section, which he argued deprives newborns of gut-microbiome transfer from the birth canal and thereby contributes to transgenerational disease and autoimmune problems.[1] He attributed such policies to figures like [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]], describing them as bureaucrats wielding unchecked power on behalf of globalist population reduction, and expressed hope that awareness gained through the COVID saga might end adherence to medical authoritarianism.[1]
