# UK Military Stockpile Depletion

In early January 2024, *The Times of London* reported that the United Kingdom had "nothing" left in its own military stockpiles after nearly two years as one of [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s biggest weapons suppliers.[1] The story was amplified by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and carried in the Badlands News Brief as part of what it described as a continuing trend of major Western publications belatedly admitting that all was not well with Ukraine policy and the state of the war.[1]

## Reporting

British defense officials and European leaders were said to be "cranking through the gears" to ramp up weapons production, according to an unnamed staffer from Prime Minister [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]]'s office.[1] The report also relied on an unnamed Ukrainian source, and appeared at a time when "Britain's military intelligence chiefs believe Ukraine cannot win the war against Russia in 2024 because it does not have the manpower or the weapons for a big battlefield breakthrough."[1]

An internal UK government debate had emerged over the future course of London's policy, with some officials arguing that Western allies simply needed more time to ramp up arms production.[1] A major uncertainty amid broad Western "war fatigue" was the question of who would occupy the White House in 2025; European officials were reported to be nervous that a [[donald-trump|Trump]] victory would end efforts to fuel the proxy war against [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1] A British source told *The Times* that if the United States were forced to take a backseat because of domestic politics — with Republicans holding up further billions in defense aid for Kyiv — Europe would have to step up and keep aid flowing.[1]

## Zelensky's standing

The report also described [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] as becoming increasingly unpopular internationally.[1] A former Ukrainian official attributed the decline to the immense death toll with no end-game or negotiation plans in sight, describing growing anger at the spectacle of men and women being "sent to the front line to die."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] connected the reporting to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s earlier description of the Western armament campaign as a "recycling program," originally read as a dig at the shipment of WWII-era equipment to arm a proxy just enough to escalate the conflict without altering its overall prospects.[1] He proposed a "Bicameral" reading of the proxy war: either the Globalist West was desperate and gave Ukraine everything it had, which "turned out not to be all that much," or observers had been witnessing "a de facto disarmament campaign meant to strip said Globalists of their kinetic power projection capabilities."[1] He closed by asking whether readers were starting to see how important the U.S. military had been to the Globalist Agenda.[1]
