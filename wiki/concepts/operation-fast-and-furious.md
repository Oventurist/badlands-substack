# Operation Fast and Furious

**Operation Fast and Furious** was a Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) gunwalking operation in which the agency allowed 1,961 firearms to "walk" out of the United States, ostensibly in an effort to identify the high-profile cartel leaders who received them.[1]

## Consequences

According to [[abc-news|ABC News]], the agency eventually lost track of the weapons, which often ended up in the hands of Mexican hit men — including those who ordered and carried out the attacks at Salvarcar and at El Aliviane, a rehabilitation center in Ciudad Juárez where 18 young men were killed on September 2, 2009.[1] The timing of the operation coincided with an upsurge of violence among Mexico's strongest cartels: in 2009 the northern Mexican states served as a battlefield between the Sinaloa and Juárez trafficking organizations and as expansion territory for the increasingly powerful Zetas.[1] Documents obtained by Univision News indicated that from October 2009 to the end of 2010, nearly 175 weapons from the operation inadvertently armed the various warring factions across northern Mexico.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

The article states that Fast and Furious began under [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and carried over into the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration.[1] It rejects the stated rationale as a lie, arguing that no cartel leader was ever endangered by the operation and that both presidents were in fact arming [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-controlled cartels seeking to maintain or expand their turf.[1] It further asserts that the [[los-zetas|Zetas]] cartel was founded by Mexican special forces trained by the CIA, and that drug trafficking constitutes a valuable money flow to the CIA, [[hezbollah]] and [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1] The operation is presented as one of the streams targeted by [[money-flow-disruption|money flow disruption]].[1]

## Assessment

The operational facts are drawn from quoted reporting; the CIA-control and financing allegations are the author's interpretation. Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors.[1]

## Chapter 18 reading

Chapter 18 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series describes Fast and Furious as an operation run by the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] under [[barack-obama|Obama]] and Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]], in which assault weapons were sold to Mexican drug cartels ostensibly to entrap them and bring them to justice.[2] The author rejects that stated purpose, arguing the real aim was to flood American streets with guns so that crime would spike and gun-control measures could follow, stripping citizens of the means of self-defense — while those running it collected billions on the back end.[2]
