# Badlands Brief: Spain Wins The [American] World Cup

"Spain Wins The [American] World Cup" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary newsletter published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dated July 20, 2026. Following the Brief's standard format, each item pairs a summarized news report with commentary from a named Badlands contributor — in this edition [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and [[canncon|CannCon]]. The issue recorded 126 likes at the time of capture and closes with the standard disclaimer that the opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## Spain defeats Argentina in the 2026 World Cup final

The lead item covers the [[2026-fifa-world-cup|2026 FIFA World Cup]] final, in which [[spain|Spain]] defeated [[argentina|Argentina]] 1–0 after extra time on Sunday, capturing the country's second men's world championship. Substitute [[ferran-torres|Ferran Torres]] scored the match's only goal in the 106th minute at [[metlife-stadium|MetLife Stadium]]. The summary notes that Spain controlled much of the match through possession and shot volume before breaking through in extra time, and that Argentina finished with ten players after [[enzo-fernandez|Enzo Fernández]] was sent off. The *Daily Mirror* reported a post-whistle confrontation involving Argentina midfielder [[leandro-paredes|Leandro Paredes]] and several Spain players.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's commentary frames the final as memorable for reasons beyond the scoreline. He writes that the pre-match narrative had "everyone and their mother" picking Argentina, largely because of [[lionel-messi|Lionel Messi]], and that many fans believe referees have aided Argentina in its attempt to go back-to-back after winning in Qatar in 2022. He cites fan-compiled video from the England–Argentina semi-final in which Argentine players allegedly escaped sanction while England received yellow cards.

The commentary includes an explanation of soccer's foul conventions for an American audience: contact with the ball during a contested moment generally excuses incidental contact with an opponent, while contact with the opponent and not the ball stops play; violent or dangerous contact may draw a yellow card, and malicious or excessively dangerous contact a red card, forcing the offending team to finish a man down. The author argues that a pattern of leniency has followed Argentina for years, and that fans allege [[fifa|FIFA]] rigs matches in Argentina's favor to boost Messi's scoring and win record for marketing and advertising reasons. In support of the suspicion he notes that the [[fbi|FBI]] announced the previous week that it is investigating the Argentine football association for money laundering in the United States, and recalls the [[2015-fifa-corruption-case|2015 FIFA corruption case]] in which several FIFA officials were arrested — while clarifying that the 2015 scandal concerned marketing and advertising money rather than play on the field.

The piece then offers a geopolitical reading of fan alignment: because pundits, celebrities, political leaders and what the author calls "the entire Zionist Diaspora" — naming [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] — backed Argentina, much of the internet cheered for Spain. The stated reason is two-fold: Argentine President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]] has been a close friend of [[israel|Israel]], while Spain has led a trend of European countries breaking with unconditional support for Israel.

Describing the match itself, the author points to uncalled fouls, including a viral incident in which an Argentine player jumped on a Spanish player, grabbed his collar and dug cleat spikes into his calf without a card being shown, with announcers calling it "an interesting technique." He states he would not object to soccer permitting rougher play — arguing it would toughen players and appeal to Americans — but objects that only Argentina appears to enjoy impunity under the current rules.

The dismissal of Enzo Fernández is described as "poetic justice": with fewer than seven minutes left in regulation and the match scoreless, Fernández attempted to draw a foul by flopping and instead received a yellow card for complaining. In extra time he received a second yellow for a challenge in which, the author argues, he put his feet together to form "a wall, of sorts, with his shins" to trip the Spanish player; the author concedes the contact was incidental enough not to merit a straight red. The two cautions produced the ejection.

The commentary reproduces match statistics screenshots showing Argentina without a single shot on goal just after halftime and still without one at the time of Fernández's first yellow card, characterizing the performance as "absolutely abysmal" for the team the experts had favored. It also notes that Spain had two additional goals disallowed — one for offside after the winning goal, and one earlier, while the game was scoreless, after an Argentine player flopped and a foul was called. The author dismisses the announcers' judgment that the call was "absolutely the right call," noting the same announcers had predicted an Argentine victory.

After the final whistle, the author writes, Argentine defender [[nahuel-molina|Nahuel Molina]] punched the Spanish captain as the two passed one another, triggering a brawl — "the cherry on top of an ostensibly dirty match." The section closes by thanking President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for bringing the tournament to the United States under a contract secured during his first term, arguing it enriched the country's 250th anniversary celebration.

## Canadian wildfire smoke and proposed sanctions

The second item reports that Republican Senator [[bernie-moreno|Bernie Moreno]] plans to introduce legislation imposing sanctions on [[canada|Canada]] and certain Canadian government officials in response to wildfire smoke affecting United States air quality. In a [[fox-news|Fox News]] interview Moreno said the proposal would include sanctions, asset freezes, visa restrictions and tariffs. Citing the [[washington-post|Washington Post]]'s Capital Weather team, the summary describes unhealthy air quality across parts of the Great Lakes, Ohio Valley, Mid-Atlantic and Northeast, and notes that amusement parks including [[six-flags|Six Flags Great America]] and [[cedar-point|Cedar Point]] modified operations. Four Republican House members sent a letter criticizing Canadian forest management policy. The summary flags that the attribution of smoke impacts primarily to management failures is the source article's own analysis, and that speculation about arson or "ecoterrorism" is presented without verifying evidence.

Ashe in America's commentary notes that [[colorado|Colorado]] has been burning for over a month and jokes that Americans can "set aside our differences and blame Canada." She states she fully supports sanctions and quotes Trump: "We are holding Canada responsible…the United States is being unnecessarily invaded by filthy, polluted, and unhealthy air, the quality of which is dangerous, and totally unacceptable!" Her central claim is that the real story is the president "taking on the climate cult." She argues that adherents of the [[climate-change-narrative|climate change narrative]] attribute the fires to warming and drying while simultaneously opposing forest management, concluding that "the climate cult predicted fires, so they need fires." She frames Canada as the battleground for defeating that movement and adds the aside, "Remember, voter intent is real. In elections, only voter intent is real."

## Trump on broadcast licenses

The third item reports that Trump said television networks which declined to air his primetime [[white-house|White House]] address on election security should have their broadcast licenses revoked. He criticized [[abc|ABC]] and [[nbc|NBC]] by name and alleged that outlets refusing to carry the address were part of a "plot." Reuters reported the speech renewed claims about election security and alleged Chinese interference that U.S. intelligence has not substantiated. ABC and NBC streamed the address on digital news platforms without interrupting their broadcast schedules; [[cnn|CNN]] declined to air it on its main channel, while [[cbs|CBS]] and Fox News carried it live. The next day, [[federal-communications-commission|FCC]] Commissioner [[anna-gomez|Anna Gomez]] called the proposal unconstitutional and said broadcasters face no FCC penalty for declining to air a presidential speech; legal experts cited by Reuters noted [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] discretion over programming and that revoking a license requires a formal administrative process.

Burning Bright's commentary treats the episode as confirmation of a longstanding thesis about the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]]. He recalls Trump's 2015-era designation of the "Fake News Media" as the "enemy of the people," which he says he originally read as "true-ISH, but largely hyperbolic," and which he now regards as literally a declaration of war. He writes that Badlands Media was founded expressly to combat engineered media noise, and that while the means of delivering large-scale justice are mostly out of the audience's hands if it wishes to remain peaceful — "and we DO want to remain peaceful" — the narrative battlespace is the mass psychological battlespace and therefore the decisive front. He argues Trump targets the press not out of annoyance but because media are "the key power projection arm of the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]]," and predicts that as the disclosure phase accelerates the media machine becomes more exposed and legally compromised. He closes: "It's time for the vapid vultures and chyronic cretins to make a choice between truth and lies, loyalty and treason, good and evil."

## Saudi uranium enrichment agreement

The fourth item reports, per CNN, that the Trump administration has tentatively agreed to a draft civilian nuclear cooperation agreement permitting [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to enrich uranium. The agreement remains unsigned by Trump despite negotiations concluding in October 2025 and has not been submitted to [[congress|Congress]] for the review required after presidential signature. CNN reported the draft would not require Saudi Arabia to adopt the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]]'s [[additional-protocol|Additional Protocol]]. Unnamed sources attributed the delay to the ongoing war with [[iran|Iran]] and to possible bipartisan congressional opposition; those explanations are unconfirmed by the White House. Democratic lawmakers and nonproliferation advocates have criticized the framework for omitting both the Additional Protocol and the U.S. "gold standard" under which partners forgo enrichment and reprocessing. The administration says the deal strengthens strategic and economic ties while complying with legal safety requirements.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls this "perhaps the least surprising headline of the past 8 months," saying the story has been tracked for years. He accepts that Saudi Arabia will obtain nuclear power plants and will need enrichment to fuel them, and states he is untroubled because he understands what drives Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], adding that he is not "brainwashed by identity politics to believe that a Muslim nation can't responsibly handle weapons of mass destruction." He recalls that MBS told [[bret-baier|Bret Baier]] during the Biden administration that he wanted nuclear weapons as well as nuclear power; that Trump told MBS in Riyadh the previous year that he would help Saudi Arabia become a nuclear power; and that during MBS's White House visit the preceding September the crown prince signed the documents and treaties required to legally possess nuclear technology, with Trump promising at a high-profile dinner to help fulfill his nuclear ambitions.

In a postscript, the author identifies a figure photographed in Trump's box at the World Cup final as [[khalid-bin-salman|Khalid bin Salman]], MBS's younger brother and Saudi defense minister.

## Araghchi, Mojtaba Khamenei, and regional realignment

The fifth item reports that Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said Iran's [[supreme-national-security-council|Supreme National Security Council]] is responsible for assessing war or ceasefire decisions, with the final decision resting with Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]]. Araghchi said he has faced criticism from rival factions after the temporary understanding with the United States, and that Iran agreed to resume negotiations after Washington again demanded zero enrichment and threatened military action — a decision he framed as demonstrating that Iran had exhausted diplomatic options. In the same interview with documentary filmmaker Javad Mogouee, Araghchi said he has never met [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] in person and that only a small number of people have met him.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that the timing of "these narrative deployments is not lost on me." He notes that less than a week after Trump met Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Al-Sharaa]] on the sidelines of the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Ankara — where he declared his intention to remove [[syria|Syria]] from the state sponsor of terrorism list — Trump met newly elected Iraqi Prime Minister [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali Al-Zaidi]] at the White House, professed affection for the young leader, and announced U.S. support for an [[iraq|Iraq]]–Syria oil pipeline bypassing the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Red Sea to reach the Mediterranean. He observes that Iran and Iraq, "two bitter enemies who fought the bloodiest war of the 20th century in the 1980's," are now cooperating on regional de-escalation according to Iranian state media. Noting also that Kremlin spokesman [[dmitri-peskov|Dmitri Peskov]] said [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] has no current plans to speak with the new supreme leader by phone, the author asks twice whether Mojtaba Khamenei "is even real," and speculates that the public may be "fed a story that will help unwind the narrative of the theocratic system notoriously associated with Iran."

## Traoré's chemical poisoning allegation

The sixth item reports that [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]]'s military leader, Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], said in a speech in the Yaadga region that operatives from an unnamed Western intelligence service once offered his government four chemical products that could be mixed into food and consumer goods destined for territory controlled by armed groups. Traoré said his government rejected the proposal and that the visitors claimed the substances had been used previously in Africa and Asia; he did not name the country, provide evidence, or date the meeting. In the same address he urged citizens to buy locally produced goods, alleged that foreign actors exploit ethnic and religious divisions to destabilize African states, and said he received a 2023 letter from a French national he associated with [[france|France]]'s external intelligence agency. The article situates the remarks against strained relations with Western countries following the expulsion of French troops and closer security cooperation with Russia, [[mali|Mali]] and [[niger|Niger]].

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls this "quite the sinister development," noting it coincides with Burkina Faso declaring two [[european-union|EU]] diplomats persona non grata with three days to leave. He writes that, as the newsletter has tracked, Western intelligence agencies funded the same [[isis|ISIS]]-aligned groups in the Sahel they are now offering to poison. Citing Traoré's account that the chemicals target neurological and physical functions and had already been deployed elsewhere, and asserting that Traoré "has a proven record of telling the truth in his public statements," he concludes that Western agencies have constructed a [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] to poison African populations, since no dissemination method could confine the chemicals to ISIS fighters. He ends with a call for accountability: "Enough is enough."

## Israeli crocodile proposal

The final news item — presented under a duplicated headline in the original — reports that Israel has taken a legal step that could allow Nile crocodiles to be used for security purposes at prison facilities after Environmental Protection Minister [[idit-silman|Idit Silman]] reclassified the species into a category permitting security agencies to keep the animals under specified conditions. Israeli media reported the change was intended to facilitate a proposal backed by National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]], who has advocated surrounding prisons holding Palestinian security detainees with crocodiles as an escape deterrent, saying the concept was inspired by Florida's [[alligator-alcatraz|"Alligator Alcatraz"]]. The [[israel-prison-service|Israel Prison Service]] had previously sent officials to a crocodile farm. The reclassification proceeded over objections from the [[israel-nature-and-parks-authority|Israel Nature and Parks Authority]] and the Environmental Protection Ministry's legal adviser, who argued the proposal lacked legal and professional justification; the change enables consideration of a pilot program but does not mean crocodiles have been deployed.

CannCon's entire commentary on the item reads: "Proof Ghost is Mossad." An editor's note explains that he is referring to a border wall design GhostofBasedPatrickHenry sent to the White House in 2017, which [[rise-attire|Rise Attire]] has printed on apparel. A postscript notes that the author produced a follow-up design in 2017 "just for fun," adding that "like most sequels," it was "not as good as the original."
