# 2024 Republican National Convention

The 2024 Republican National Convention was the nominating convention at which [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] formally accepted the Republican Party's presidential nomination, concluding on the evening of Thursday, 18 July 2024.[1] The convention took place five days after the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] in Butler, Pennsylvania, an event that reshaped both the tone of the gathering and the content of Trump's acceptance address.[1]

## Trump's acceptance speech

Trump delivered his highly anticipated speech on the convention's final evening after deciding earlier in the week to abandon his original 20-page draft in light of the shooting.[1] Announcing that he would recount the events only once — "you'll never hear it from me a second time, because it's too painful to tell" — he described turning to his right to view an immigration chart when he heard "a loud whizzing sound" and felt something strike his right ear.[1] He recalled bringing his hand down covered in blood, realizing the crowd was under attack, and dropping to the ground in a single movement.[1]

Trump attributed his survival to divine protection, saying that "there was blood pouring everywhere, and yet, in a certain way I felt very safe, because I had God on my side," and that had he not moved his head at the last instant the gunman [[thomas-matthew-crooks|Thomas Matthew Crooks]]'s bullet "would have perfectly hit its mark."[1] He praised the crowd for not stampeding, saying tens of thousands "stood by and didn't move an inch," many rising to point toward the sniper's position, which he said saved lives.[1] When Trump said "I am not supposed to be here tonight," the crowd shouted back, "Yes you are"; he replied that he stood before them "only by the grace of almighty God" and noted that many observers had called the moment providential.[1]

## Political significance

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that public mandate is codified through [[narrative-warfare|Narrative Warfare]] and that Trump now wielded both asymmetric and disproportionate power projection in that domain, free to adopt any register from humbled statesman to righteous fury.[1] He observed that Trump's rhetoric had shifted toward a "grand unification theme" even by mainstream-media accounts, at a moment when Americans were seeking an alternative to "decades of engineered chaos and the false binary the [[deep-state|Deep State]] has relegated the national experience to."[1] Days after escaping death by a quarter inch, Burning Bright wrote, Trump was greeted "like a rock star" at the convention, "with vanquished political foes bending the knee before him."[1]

Democrats reportedly expected post-convention polling to show a possible blowout capable of dragging down congressional candidates as well.[1]

## Wrestling symbolism on the final night

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the speaker lineup for the convention's final day, held the Thursday before [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s withdrawal, was a deliberate communications exercise.[2] Most of that evening's speakers had connections to combat sports, but two in particular seemed to carry specific messages: [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]], former president and CEO of [[world-wrestling-entertainment|WWE]], who described [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] joining one of the promotion's "most compelling and highest-rated storylines" and revealed that she had run the Small Business Administration in his cabinet; and [[hulk-hogan|Hulk Hogan]], who spoke within hours of her and demonstrated [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] by moving between his wrestling persona and his legal identity as Terry Bollea, coining "[[trumpamania|Trumpmania]]" in the process.[2]

Accepting the Republican nomination on Thursday night, Trump boasted that "I will end every single international crisis that the current administration has created, including the horrible war with Russia and Ukraine, which would have never happened if I was president," though the war in Ukraine was not otherwise a major topic at the Milwaukee convention.[2]

## Day 3: Vance's introduction

The convention's third night was devoted both to welcoming [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s running mate and to blasting President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s leadership on the world stage.[3] Vance, the 39-year-old Ohio senator, offered his life story as a son of Appalachia to reaffirm Trump's connections to Americans who feel alienated socially, economically, and politically.[3]

Reporting on the evening described the GOP on display as more populist, protectionist, and isolationist: Vance blasted Wall Street and "multinational corporations," hailed Trump as a man who "will stand up for American companies" and "union and nonunion" workers, and spoke of raising wages, battling China in the global marketplace, and making other nations foot the military bill for international security and stability.[3] His nomination effectively coronated him as the heir apparent to Trump's movement, and the night's mix of national security talk, attacks on Biden, and the unveiling of a potential MAGA heir was said to encapsulate what Trump's "America First" agenda means for the party and for the US role as a global superpower.[3] Other takeaways from Day 3 included a January 6 curtain call and continued secrecy about Trump's health following the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|assassination attempt]] days earlier.[3]

On the convention's first night, July 16, [[cnn|CNN]]'s [[john-king|John King]] used the network's electoral map to argue Trump was on course for a landslide of up to 330 electoral votes, calling Biden's position a "dire strait" sixteen weeks from Election Day.[3]
