# Fake Iran War

**Fake Iran War** is the term used in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary for the ongoing conflict narrative surrounding [[iran|Iran]], which Badlands hosts treat as a staged or largely theatrical confrontation whose continuation serves purposes other than the ones publicly stated.[1]

## Treatment in Badlands commentary

The twenty-first episode of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]], published 13 June 2026 under the title "Exploring Trump's Trap ... And Who's Fallen Into It," devoted a portion of its runtime to what the hosts called "the real reason for the perpetuation of the Fake Iran War."[1] The framing assumes the war's outward form is not the substance of the event: the question posed is not whether the conflict is real but why it is being sustained.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the episode's co-hosts, placed the Fake Iran War alongside two other threads — the amplified effects of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "glorious discombobulator" and the construction of the [[multipolar-world|Multipolar World]] — suggesting that the persistence of the Iran narrative is one of the instruments through which the emerging distribution of global power is being arranged.[1]

## Earlier framing (March 2026)

The narrative predates the June 2026 treatment. Episode 11 of [[badlands-blitz|The Blitz]], "Iran, NATO and the Invisible Enemy," published 21 March 2026, described the Iran War as a "spiraling narrative" and used it as the map through which [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] traced a strategic pincer said to be in the offing by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] against the true [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]].[2] In that treatment the conflict functions less as an end in itself than as the visible surface of a deeper contest, with [[nato|NATO]] and the alliance structure implicated in the same manoeuvre.[2]

## The perpetual holding pattern (2026)

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in July 2026 that after tens of thousands of words analyzing the Fake Iran War, the war that is not a war — and that the establishment alternately decries while questioning its reality — had entered a perpetual holding pattern he believes is central to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s plan against the central planners.[3] Alongside a series of what he calls Actual Disentanglement operations, including the on-shoring of U.S. energy production and the slow dissolution of the controlled-opposition dynamic between the mirrored regimes of [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]], he argues the greater effect of Trump's "perpetual engine" is mass psychological impact in the Mind War.[3]

## The dueling Politico headlines

His evidence was a pair of [[politico|Politico]] headlines published in the same week: one reporting that Democrats were desperate to halt Trump's continuation of the Iran campaign, the other that the GOP was becoming almost hysterical with anxiety over the same continuation.[3] Operating from the premise that the Democratic Party does not care about the human cost in the Middle East and never has, Burning Bright identified a contradiction — if perpetuating the Iran War is bad for Trump's mandate and both sides agree it is bad, why is the left also trying to stop it, and why is the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] contingent on the right doing the same.[3]

His conclusion is that one can sometimes glimpse the real war operating beneath the fake ones, and that the media protectorate occasionally crosses its propagandist wires in ways invisible even in hindsight.[3] Whenever the so-called Left and so-called Right agree on something anathema to Trump's moves, he writes, they are not being principled — they are reminding the public that the real enemy has been inside the gates all along, and that the only parties they treat as threats are the American people and the man acting as their tribune.[3]

## Easter 2026 evidence

The Easter weekend of April 2026 supplied Badlands with fresh material for the thesis. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[donald-trump|Trump]] was "praising Allah and dropping F bombs on Easter, while promoting his peace ultimatum deadline like a pay-per-view fight night," and concluded that people still believe "this war is real as presented to us."[4] He called the weekend goofy: a number of U.S. military aircraft were reported shot down in or around Iranian airspace within hours of each other, followed by a large rescue operation for the pilots of an F-15 — an episode he described as feeling like "one of the fakest" narratives available.[4]

What made it feel especially staged, in his account, was Trump pairing cartoonish "TrumpaMania" rhetoric with an immediate statement to Fox News that [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and the Iranians were close to a deal that could be in place as soon as Monday.[4] The concurrent decision by [[planet-labs|Planet Labs]] to withhold satellite imagery of [[iran|Iran]] indefinitely was read as removing the open-source evidence that would show Iran had not taken the damage claimed.[4] A variant of the thesis was also floated: that [[israel|Israel]] and Iran are in a genuine conflict while Trump merely exaggerates U.S. involvement, possibly with the [[4th-psychological-operations-group|4th Psyop Group]] running an information operation against [[mossad|Mossad]] and the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] themselves.[4]

## April 2026: the Touska escalation

The April 19, 2026 [[mv-touska-seizure|M/V Touska seizure]] arrived immediately after [[donald-trump|Trump]] had attempted to mandate calm, and was treated by Badlands as a further instance of the engineered fog around a war designed never to arrive.[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that his original Iran War thesis required no edits, since the key is not parsing what is occurring in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] but identifying which actors the escalatory narratives emanate from and which they conspicuously do not.[5]

He posed two diagnostic questions for readers: whether Trump's saber-rattling provokes a deployment or counters one, and whether he is searching for an on-ramp or an off-ramp — resolving, in his view, to whether one believes Trump and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] want a legacy defined by peace.[5] The episode was discussed on [[badlands-blitz|Badlands Blitz]] episode 13, framed as the "on-again, off-again, real, but also fake war between the US & Iran."[5]

## Pegasus deception operation (April 2026)

Reporting that the [[cia|CIA]] had used [[nso-group|NSO Group]]'s [[pegasus|Pegasus]] software to run a deception operation against [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] officials during the rescue of a downed U.S. airman was cited by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] as open confirmation that the U.S. military is deploying deception psyops as part of its Iran strategy.[6] He asked whether such operations might be far greater in scale than a single rescue — whether the pilot's shootdown, and indeed the entire war with [[iran|Iran]], could themselves be deception operations.[6]

He argued that Iran's practice of responding to U.S. missile attacks with AI-generated videos rendered in a LEGO aesthetic is not a believable war strategy for a radical Islamic regime, reinforcing the fake-war reading.[6] The same commentary noted reports that the United States had agreed to unfreeze $100 billion in Iranian assets held in [[qatar|Qatar]], suggesting the "failed" [[islamabad-agreement|Islamabad]] negotiations may not have failed as presented.[6]
