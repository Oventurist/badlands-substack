# The Vampire Metaphor for the Cabal

The vampire metaphor for the [[cabal|Cabal]] is the organising analogy of [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s January 24, 2023 Badlands Media essay "The World is a Vampire," which takes its title from the opening line of the 1995 Smashing Pumpkins song "Bullet with Butterfly Wings," written by frontman Billy Corgan.[1] Carlson quotes the song's first four lines and chorus — "The world is a vampire / Sent to drain / Secret destroyer / Hold you up to the flame / Despite all my rage I'm still just a rat in the cage" — and remarks that Corgan appears to have been awake far longer than he has, dating his own awakening to late 2017.[1]

## The analogy

Carlson argues that the story of Dracula and of vampires generally was created and is used to describe how the Cabal operates, both literally and figuratively.[1] He enumerates the attributes of the vampire of folklore and maps each onto the elite:

- Vampires consume the blood of others.[1]
- Vampires live forever.[1]
- Vampires are the chosen few, with ordinary people acting as sheep for them to feed upon.[1]
- Vampires live in the dark and operate in the shadows.[1]
- Vampires cannot be exposed to light without dying a painful death; Carlson reads "light" as a metaphor for truth and exposure.[1]
- Vampires cannot enter a home unless invited in.[1]

The invitation rule is the load-bearing element of the analogy. Carlson contends it explains why so much effort is expended, through entertainment media, on telling the public what the Cabal intends to do and how it will be done: the elite require tacit approval, or "buy-in," which they believe shields them from the full force of [[karmic-retribution|karmic retribution]].[1] The vampires enter homes, in his account, through televisions, computers and phones — via movies, television, books, music, sports, newspapers and magazines.[1]

## Implications drawn

Viewed through this lens, Carlson writes, the Cabal's behaviour becomes coherent: they view the public as food and as an energy source; they view themselves as elites descended from illustrious bloodlines, superior and Godlike; they exist in the dark, in secret and in the shadows; and they control the population by getting it to invite them into its homes and lives.[1] The corresponding remedy he proposes is to destroy their power by forcing them into the light through truth and exposure.[1]
