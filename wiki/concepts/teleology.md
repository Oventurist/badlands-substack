# Teleology

**Teleology** — from the Greek *telos*, meaning purpose — is the doctrine that things are made with intrinsic purpose, and is a central organizing idea of Part 8 of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[1]

## Teleology versus secular materialism

The essay argues that the idea of an intelligent universe governed by a Creator who endows things with intrinsic purpose is the ultimate heresy in the eyes of secular materialism, and that mainstream academia and science deny metaphysics and teleology altogether.[1] Against this the author contends that there is no sufficient reason why souls cannot exist or be created purposefully, and that materialism offers no genuine solutions to the paradoxes of science and philosophy it claims to resolve.[1]

## Uniqueness of souls

The argument proceeds by asking why God would create replica souls, and what a replaceable duplicate would do to the value of a soul.[1] What makes a soul special is said to be its irreplaceable uniqueness: the more a person is in touch with their own soul, the more their insights, talents and aptitudes can be rendered into works no one else could create.[1]

## Anti-telic systems

Modern society is described as treating human beings as replaceable parts of a machine because it prejudicially denies teleology and metaphysics, and the essay holds it highly significant that psychopaths likewise regard human beings as mere objects — accessories or obstacles to their own purposes.[1] The resulting system is characterized as psychopathic and built on an anti-teleological foundation.[1] A telic universe is described as alive and full of spirit, whereas a universe devoid of *telos* is a dead machine auto-generating content without intrinsic purpose — "one giant [[npc-non-player-character|NPC]] unto itself."[1]

The *telic*/*anti-telic* distinction supplies the definition of [[cyber-necromancy|cyber-necromancy]] as the thwarting of all that is telic by any anti-telic means.[1] The essay concludes that if there is no intrinsic purpose to life then there is no reason not to pursue convenience and pleasure above all else — precisely the mindset the author says dark occultists wish people to adopt — whereas if existence has purpose, allowing one's natural gifts to atrophy through outsourcing thought to third parties makes no sense.[1]

## Teleology and the rule of law

In "The Zombie Apocalypse vs. The Great Awakening" (June 21, 2023), [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] argues that one of the goals of socially engineering society into a secular worldview is to eliminate teleology, and that this was necessary in order to spiritually disarm the masses.[2] He notes that mainstream academia is for the most part opposed to the very concept, with scientists trained to scoff at intrinsic purpose — a stance he calls self-contradictory, since the pursuit of science makes no sense if the universe is not inherently lawful and ordered.[2] He adds that declaring something "unknowable" ironically presupposes some direct knowledge of the thing said to be unknowable.[2]

The essay connects the denial of teleology to the collapse of the [[rule-of-law|rule of law]]: without God, what mankind calls law devolves into mere preference, and atrocities such as theft, slavery and murder carry no significance beyond their effect on victims absent a valid telos imparting transcendent meaning.[2] If it is God's Will that mankind be free, thwarting human freedom works against what the author calls the self-selected telos of God, which is why injustice matters to God and creation at large and not only to those directly harmed.[2] The resulting arrangement is described as a [[slavery-matrix|slavery matrix]] operating in direct conflict with any reasonable conception of God's Will, and the secularist-materialist denial of transcendent significance is presented as the reason people can be persuaded to give up their souls.[2]
