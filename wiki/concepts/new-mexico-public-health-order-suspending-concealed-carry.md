# New Mexico Public Health Order Suspending Concealed Carry

In September 2023, New Mexico Governor [[michelle-lujan-grisham|Michelle Lujan Grisham]] issued an emergency public health order that had the effect of banning individuals from carrying firearms in Albuquerque and Bernalillo County for at least 30 days.[1] Lujan Grisham, a Democrat, said she issued the order in response to recent shootings, including the death of an 11-year-old boy.[1]

## Legal challenge and refusal to enforce

The order drew an immediate lawsuit challenging its constitutionality, and became a nationally covered dispute over whether emergency health powers can be used to suspend a constitutional right.[1] Bernalillo County District Attorney [[sam-bregman|Sam Bregman]] said on the Saturday after the order that he would not enforce it, joining Albuquerque Mayor Tim Keller and Police Chief Harold Medina in declining to do so.[1] "As an officer of the court, I cannot and will not enforce something that is clearly unconstitutional," Bregman said, adding that his office "will continue to focus on criminals of any age that use guns in the commission of a crime."[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], described the governor's measure as obviously unconstitutional Second Amendment restrictions imposed by a "tyrant governor," and said that seeing a district attorney stand up for the Bill of Rights prompted a double take.[1] She called for the same posture to be demanded in every local jurisdiction in the state, argued that officials had grown too comfortable claiming emergency powers to infringe rights, welcomed the litigation, and closed with the phrase "shall not be infringed."[1]

## Impeachment calls and constitutional argument

AP News reporting cited in the September 11, 2023 Badlands News Brief added that the governor specified state police would enforce what amounted to civil violations, and that Bernalillo County Sheriff [[john-allen-sheriff|John Allen]] said he was uneasy about the order because it raised too many questions about constitutional rights — joining Albuquerque police chief [[harold-medina|Harold Medina]] in effectively declining enforcement.[2] New Mexico lawmakers called for the governor's impeachment over the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] overreach.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the legal question through [[marbury-v-madison|Marbury v. Madison]] (1803): all laws repugnant to the Constitution are null and void, so a governor cannot suspend the Second Amendment.[2] She noted that [[ted-lieu|Ted Lieu]] and [[david-hogg|David Hogg]] reached the same conclusion, but attributed their reasoning to a desire for federal-level restrictions rather than any commitment to states' rights, comparing it to establishment demands for federal abortion legislation after [[roe-v-wade|Roe v. Wade]] was returned to the states.[2]

## Federal court blocks the order

On Wednesday, September 13, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[david-urias|David Urias]] blocked part of the public health order, granting a temporary restraining order that halted the suspension of the right to openly carry or conceal guns in public until a further hearing in early October.[3] The order had been imposed the previous Friday and applied a statistical threshold for violent crime that covered only Albuquerque and the surrounding area; the second-term governor cited recent shootings around the state that left children dead, saying something needed to be done.[3] Urias, who was appointed to the federal bench by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], agreed with plaintiffs who accused [[michelle-lujan-grisham|Lujan Grisham]] of trampling on constitutional rights, while saying he did not blame her for trying to act "in the face of these terrible deaths" and that his duty was to decide a much narrower question about the rights afforded under the U.S. Constitution.[3] The ruling was described as a setback for the governor amid mounting criticism and widening political divides; she said in a statement the same day that she would not be resigned to the status quo.[3]
