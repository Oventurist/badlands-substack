# Gender Ideology

"Gender ideology" is the term used by critics for the body of belief holding that biological sex cannot define who is male and who is female, and that each person's internally felt gender identity is the operative social and legal category.[1] In a February 2024 Badlands Media essay, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] — assisted by [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] — argued that in [[canada|Canada]] this ideology has ceased to be merely a cultural current and has been written into law, becoming in practice "the supreme law" of the country.[1]

## The conflation thesis

The essay's central claim is that public acceptance of gender ideology in Canada was manufactured by deliberately confusing it with support for gay and lesbian rights.[1] Canadians who rightly condemned historic abuses — such as the police raids on gay bathhouses in Toronto — were, the article argues, unable to distinguish between that legitimate civil-rights struggle and the adoption of gender ideology as settled fact.[1] The narrative shifted from "Gay Pride" to "2SLGBTQ+ Pride," and then to "2SLGBTQIA+," each expansion of the acronym mutating the original same-sex-rights issue into what the author calls an ever-growing quagmire.[1]

Deschamps contends the two frameworks are not merely different but incompatible: gender theory requires accepting that biology cannot define males and females, which he argues dissolves the very basis of being gay, lesbian, or bisexual, since same-sex attraction presupposes a sex binary.[1] On this reading gender ideology is inherently homophobic, and the fact that the gay-rights movement was used to normalize it in Canadian society is treated as evidence that sustained cognitive dissonance was required of the public.[1] Many gay and lesbian organizations, and left-wing feminists who argue that a gender spectrum erases the history of women's liberation, are cited as dissenting from the fusion.[1]

## Institutional and commercial scale

The article describes Gay Pride as having become a massive industry operating with governments and some of the world's largest institutions "to the tune of billions," accumulating cultural influence that now surpasses the legal frameworks originally built to protect same-sex couples and prohibit discrimination.[1]

## Legal entrenchment in Canada

The Northwest Territories became the first Canadian jurisdiction to name "gender identity" as a prohibited ground of discrimination in its human rights legislation in 2002 — described in the essay as the first move away from simply shielding same-sex couples from prejudice.[1] Fourteen years later, in 2016, the government of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] amended federal human rights legislation, making gender identity a national legal framework, a shift the article credits substantially to the province of [[ontario|Ontario]] and the [[ontario-human-rights-commission|Ontario Human Rights Commission]].[1] See [[bill-c-16|Bill C-16]] and the [[canadian-human-rights-act|Canadian Human Rights Act]].[1]

The article frames the resulting regime as one in which laws effectively require citizens to identify others by their claimed gender identity or risk accusation of a hate crime, and points to the extension of federal employee benefits covering roughly $75,000 per person for sex-change procedures as a downstream consequence of that legal architecture.[1]

## Mechanism: dialectic and incrementalism

Deschamps attributes the outcome to a Marxist tactic he names Hegelian Dialectic Social Engineering — see [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian dialectic]], [[social-engineering|social engineering]], and [[neo-marxism|Neo-Marxism]] — in which dissent against each proposed change is reframed as rising hate against "this community," after which the change is pushed through.[1] Because all three major federal parties contributed to gender-identity lawmaking, the essay treats apparent partisan conflict on the subject as theatre and describes the situation as a [[uniparty|Uniparty]] problem.[1]

## Medical dimension

Gender ideology in law is paired in the essay with [[gender-affirming-care|gender affirmative care]] in medicine, promoted internationally by [[wpath|WPATH]], which the author characterizes as an activist group masquerading as a scientific institution.[1] Canada and the United States are described as having adopted the model wholesale, while the UK, Sweden, Norway, and Finland have pushed back.[1]

## The "What Is a Woman?" debate (2022-2023)

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]] documentary [[what-is-a-woman|"What Is a Woman?"]] as a case study in the logical structure of gender theory, observing that the advocates interviewed by [[matt-walsh|Matt Walsh]] repeatedly became trapped in logical fallacies and threatened to end their interviews, accusing him of hatred and bigotry for asking simple questions.[2] The emblematic moment came when a self-described "social scientist" was asked whether he could define "woman" without using the word "woman" and stared slack-jawed into the distance.[2]

She identified specific claims of gender theory as myths: that pronoun changes and cross-dressing carry no lasting impact on a child's mental health and development, and that pausing puberty with Lupron - a drug known for its use in the chemical castration of sex offenders - is completely reversible.[2] She also noted that advocates in the film denied any male athletic advantage on the grounds that transgender athletes have also lost contests, without presenting evidence, and wrote sardonically that "this is what passes for 'science' these days."[2]

Ashe argued that the phenomenon is not an organic social contagion but "a change program, being commissioned and propagated by the most powerful entities on earth," pointing to the United Nations' [[sustainable-development-goals|Sustainable Development Goal #5]] as explicitly enshrining gender theory as a global imperative, and to the appearance of the UN SDGs in American classrooms.[2] She described the "rainbow terrorist coalition" as having successfully reframed any pushback as the claim that transgender people have no right to exist, a framing she said had become the debate itself and had killed nuance.[2] Her concluding position was that "biology isn't just reality, it's actuality," and that truth remains true even if the entire global establishment disputes it.[2]
