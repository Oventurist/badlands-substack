# Dark Night of the Soul

The **Dark Night of the Soul**, as used in the March 29, 2025 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] feature "Walking the Path: From Awakening to Integration" by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]], names the disorienting middle stretch of an [[spiritual-awakening|awakening]] in which old frameworks dissolve before new ones have formed.[1] The essay calls it "a time of questioning, turbulence, and deconstruction" and insists it is necessary — "a dissolution where old frameworks crumble, making space for what is next."[1]

## Grieving the old self

The article holds that one of the hardest parts of awakening is grieving who one used to be: the self comforted by particular beliefs, relationships and routines no longer fits, producing waves of sadness, nostalgia and even guilt, "as if you are betraying a past version of you."[1] The turbulence cycles between extremes — deep peace one moment, overwhelming grief the next — and reaches a point that "feels unbearable," with the person suspended between realities, "one foot in the past, the other reaching ahead."[1]

[[gabor-mate|Gabor Maté]], author of *When the Body Says No*, is quoted that "What we don't process owns us," a line the author applies to awakening as mourning the familiar while embracing the unknown.[1] Unprocessed emotions stored in the body from years of suppression are said to rise during this stage; left unchecked they keep a person vigilant and resistant to expansion, but the essay frames the episode as a transition and a shedding rather than an ending: "The path narrows, but it does not break."[1]

## The fog and the pull of the old

The stage is rendered in two second-person vignettes characteristic of the author's style. In "The Fog on the Path," dusk fog curls around the walker's ankles: yesterday's certainties give way to an unanswered call from a friend, a job outgrown and a world that feels foreign, until the walker sits on a cold stone and whispers, "This too is the path."[1]

In "The Pull of the Old," the fog lifts but the phone buzzes and old habits call — scroll, react, numb out — a "siren from the shore you have left."[1] [[psychological-integration|Integration]] falters here because the surrounding world has not shifted with the person and its noise tugs at the edges.[1] The essay concedes this phase is harder than awakening itself, because "seeing truth is sudden; living it is slow, a dance between surrender and struggle."[1]

## Function

The article's stated takeaway is that "disorientation clears the path," and that the fog should be embraced for its reshaping effect rather than resisted.[1] The stage is followed in the essay's structure by the renegotiation of relationships, the confrontation with fear of the unknown, and finally integration proper, in which the swings between euphoria and despair are regulated into "a steady stride."[1]
