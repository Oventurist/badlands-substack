# February 2024 US–UK Airstrikes on Houthi Targets in Yemen

On Saturday, 3 February 2024, the United States and the United Kingdom launched joint airstrikes against Iran-backed [[houthis|Houthi]] positions in [[yemen|Yemen]] in response to the group's continuing attacks on shipping in the Red Sea, a coalition operation confirmed in a joint statement by the two governments.[1]

## Conduct of the strikes

The coalition struck 36 Houthi targets across 13 locations in Yemen using missiles launched from the aircraft carrier [[uss-dwight-d-eisenhower-carrier-strike-group|USS Dwight D. Eisenhower]], operating in the Red Sea.[1] A United States official said more than two dozen aircraft were also launched from the ship, some carrying 2,000-pound bombs, Sidewinder air-to-air missiles, and other precision-guided munitions.[1] It was not immediately clear whether anyone had been killed or wounded.[1]

According to the joint statement, the strikes targeted sites associated with the Houthis' deeply buried weapons storage facilities, missile systems and launchers, air defense systems, and radars.[1] The governments described the operation as consisting of "precision strikes" intended to "disrupt and degrade the capabilities" the Houthis had used to attack vessels in the Red Sea, a campaign they said threatened global trade and the lives of the sailors crewing those ships.[1]

## Stated justification

The statement framed the operation as a response to a series of what it called illegal, dangerous, and destabilizing Houthi actions taken since earlier coalition strikes on 11 and 22 January 2024, singling out the 27 January attack that struck and set ablaze the Marshall Islands-flagged oil tanker M/V Marlin Luanda.[1]

## Wider context

The strikes came weeks after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] re-designated the Houthis a "global terrorist group" in January 2024 following months of Red Sea attacks — a reversal of his earlier removal of the designation and a return to a step first taken by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2021.[1] Commentary in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]'s news brief placed the Yemen escalation within a broader pattern of the Biden administration quietly reverting to Trump-era policy across foreign and domestic fronts.[1]
