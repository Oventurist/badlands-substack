# Hurricane Melissa

Hurricane Melissa was the most powerful storm of the 2025 Atlantic hurricane season, striking Jamaica, Haiti, and Cuba in late October 2025.[1]

## Impact

Melissa made landfall in Jamaica on Tuesday, October 28, 2025, as a Category 5 hurricane with 185 mph winds — the strongest storm in the island's recorded history — leaving a trail of devastation and flooding.[1] Jamaica's education minister, [[dana-morris-dixon|Dana Morris Dixon]], said that 77 percent of the island remained without power on Wednesday.[1] Prime Minister [[andrew-holness|Andrew Holness]] declared Jamaica a disaster area, and St. Elizabeth Parish was reported to be "underwater."[1]

At least 40 people were reported dead in Haiti.[1] Seven additional deaths had been confirmed across the Caribbean during storm preparations — three in Jamaica, three in Haiti, and one in the Dominican Republic.[1]

The storm subsequently weakened to a Category 3 hurricane and struck Cuba near Chivirico in Santiago de Cuba Province with sustained winds of 120 mph, described as "extremely dangerous."[1] Melissa continued moving north, triggering widespread flooding and emergency declarations across the Caribbean.[1]


## Aftermath in Haiti, Jamaica and Cuba

By Thursday, October 30, 2025, communities across the northern Caribbean were digging out from Melissa's destruction amid the rumble of large machinery, the whine of chain saws and the chopping of machetes.[2] In Jamaica, government workers and residents began clearing roads in a push to reach dozens of isolated communities in the island's southeast that had sustained a direct hit from one of the most powerful Atlantic hurricanes on record, while stunned residents stared at roofless homes and waterlogged belongings.[2]

Sylvester Guthrie, a resident of Lacovia in the southern parish of St. Elizabeth, said "I don't have a house now," holding onto his bicycle as the only possession of value left after the storm.[2] Emergency relief flights landed at Jamaica's main international airport as crews distributed water, medicine and other basic supplies, and helicopters dropped food over communities where the storm had flattened homes, wiped out roads and destroyed bridges, cutting them off from assistance.[2] Education Minister Dana Morris Dixon said "the entire Jamaica is really broken because of what has happened."[2]


## Category 4 Intensification (October 2025)

In late October 2025 the U.S. National Hurricane Center reported that Hurricane Melissa had strengthened into a powerful Category 4 storm and could reach Category 5 intensity by that Sunday night.[3] The storm was unleashing torrential rain and "life-threatening," catastrophic flooding across the northern Caribbean, including Haiti and Jamaica, and was forecast to reach Jamaica's southern coast by Tuesday morning.[3] Melissa was the thirteenth named storm of an already active Atlantic hurricane season running from June through November, and posed a severe danger to millions across Jamaica, Haiti and the Dominican Republic.[3] Officials warned that some areas could see up to 35 inches of rain, capable of triggering life-threatening floods and landslides, particularly in regions still recovering from prior storms.[3]
