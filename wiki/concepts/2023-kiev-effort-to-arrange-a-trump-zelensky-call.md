# 2023 Kiev Effort to Arrange a Trump–Zelensky Call

In November 2023, the Ukrainian news outlet Strana reported, citing an inside source in Kiev, that Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]] was eager to speak directly with former U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the event the Republican frontrunner retook the White House in 2024.[1]

## Reported rationale

An individual close to Zelensky's office reasoned that Trump, as the de facto 2024 presidential nominee of the Republican Party, effectively controlled the flow of foreign aid to Kiev, because Republicans held the majority in the House of Representatives — the chamber that had been blocking White House efforts to continue funding the Ukrainian conflict.[1] The Ukrainian leader's cabinet "understands this, and therefore is trying to organize a direct conversation between Trump and Zelensky," the source told Strana.[1] The report was carried in English by RT and picked up in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief of November 14, 2023.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the item as a case study in reading hostile media. Adapting a line from the dinner scene between Jack Nicholson's Frank Costello and Leonardo DiCaprio's Billy Costigan in *The Departed* — "you can learn a lot, watching things eat" — he offered his own [[information-war|Information War]] variant: "you can learn a lot, watching things lie."[1] He applied the principle to the [[deep-state|Deep State]] generally and specifically to what he called the clowns within the Media Industrial Complex.[1]

In his account, mainstream outlets had coalesced around a cynical and derisive reading of the story, suggesting Zelensky was mocking Trump and that any prospect of the two brokering a peace deal — something their globalist "puppeteers" would do anything to stop — was a pipe dream.[1] Burning Bright asked how fitting it would be for that consensus to be proven bitterly wrong on the world stage, closing with the Badlands refrain that "we're watching a movie, and we're going to like how it ends."[1]
