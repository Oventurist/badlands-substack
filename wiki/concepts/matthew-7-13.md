# Matthew 7:13

**Matthew 7:13** is a verse from the Sermon on the Mount, quoted in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as the scriptural frame for the claim that ease is a trap: "Enter through the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many enter through it."[1]

## Use in Badlands commentary

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] built his February 28, 2023 essay "The Road Without Struggle" around the verse.[1] He read it as teaching that the road to salvation is narrow, difficult and specific, and that it is the only road — that there are not multiple routes of varying steepness, and that anyone not traveling the one specific road will never arrive.[1]

Carlson then extended the verse beyond soteriology to what he considered all areas of life.[1] His illustrations were deliberately mundane: it is easier to use a fast-food drive-through after work than to buy and cook healthier ingredients, but the easy path repeated erodes health; it is easier to collapse on the couch than to talk with one's children or spouse or to exercise, but the harder choice strengthens relationships and the body.[1]

## Political extension

The essay's larger argument was that the paved, flat, obstacle-free path is not accidental but engineered — that systems have been deliberately built to exhaust people into desiring ease, and that the resulting compliance is the mechanism of control.[1] Carlson listed the political forms of the broad road: accepting what the media says, assuming government has the public's best interests at heart, affirming what he characterized as glorified mental disorders to avoid pushback, and parroting what children's teachers teach.[1] "It's less stressful if we all agree," he wrote, framing consensus itself as the destination of the wide gate.[1]
