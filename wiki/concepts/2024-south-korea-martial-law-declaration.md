# 2024 South Korea Martial Law Declaration

The 2024 South Korea martial law declaration was a short-lived imposition of military rule by South Korean President [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]] on the night of Tuesday, December 3, 2024, which collapsed within roughly six hours after the country's legislature voted to reject it.[1] The episode blindsided the South Korean public, Yoon's own [[people-power-party|People Power Party]], and global allies including the United States, and immediately triggered impeachment proceedings against the president.[1]

## Declaration and reversal

Yoon imposed martial law during what was described as a tense night of political drama in which troops surrounded parliament while lawmakers voted to reject military rule.[1] Yoon said his government withdrew military personnel following the bipartisan parliamentary vote rejecting martial law, and the measure was formally lifted at approximately 4:30 a.m. Wednesday during a Cabinet meeting.[1] In total, martial law was in effect for about six hours.[1]

The declaration was characterized as a surprise gamble by Yoon to assert direct political control in response to continued deadlock in parliament, prompting lawmakers to race to the legislature to reverse the order.[1]

## Political fallout

[[south-korea|South Korea's]] main opposition party urged Yoon to resign immediately or face impeachment; Yoon did not publicly respond to the demand, though his office said senior presidential advisers and secretaries offered to resign collectively, and the president postponed his official Wednesday morning schedule.[1] The [[democratic-party-of-korea|Democratic Party]] announced it would pursue charges of treason and impeachment against Yoon, as well as against South Korea's defense minister and safety minister, alleging that the declaration of martial law was illegal.[1]

Although Yoon rescinded the decree only hours after issuing it, the move triggered an outpouring of anger from the public and from his own party, leaving him facing widespread calls to resign.[1]

## Market and analyst reaction

The chaotic developments whipsawed investors and prompted reassurances from monetary authorities that they would provide help in markets if needed.[1] Rory Green, an economist at the research firm TS Lombard, said Yoon "is now doomed to face impeachment," predicting a presidential election probably early in the second quarter of 2025 in which the opposition Democratic Party would be "strong favorites" to win.[1]

## Scenes at the National Assembly

Opposition figures documented their own responses on social media. Opposition leader [[lee-jae-myung|Lee Jae-myung]] posted a video filmed from the backseat of his car declaring that Yoon Suk Yeol was no longer president, and later livestreamed himself scaling the walls of the [[national-assembly-of-south-korea|National Assembly]].[1] Similar videos showed other opposition members of parliament climbing the walls of the legislature to gain access while decrying Yoon's action as a coup and calling for his removal from office.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the episode as a "parallel inversion" of the concurrent story in Syria, where proxy forces were attempting to violently overthrow a government, noting that in South Korea there appeared to be no evidence of unrest — which he argued explained why parliament voted to reject the declaration.[1] He joked that the troops "obviously didn't get the memo in time," wondering why they did not simply use the door, and observed that even the official [[brics|BRICS]] account on X was posting about the affair.[1] He further asserted that [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] was "some kind of a hostage being forced to play the role of dictator," with the strings pulling him apparently cut during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump's]] first term, and welcomed the treason impeachment push with the single word "Accelerate."[1]

## Alleged provocation scheme

By mid-December 2024, mounting suspicions held that former Defense Minister [[kim-yong-hyun|Kim Yong-hyun]] had intended to justify the declaration of martial law — or of a "state of united defense" — by instigating a military conflict with [[north-korea|North Korea]], ordering strikes on the sites launching trash-filled balloons and flying unmanned drones over Pyongyang.[2] Democratic Party lawmaker Park Beom-kye said a military whistleblower told him the drones North Korea claimed Seoul had sent to Pyongyang in October were indeed dispatched on the orders of the South Korean military, specifically the former defense minister.[2]

Park stated that the [[defense-counterintelligence-command|Defense Counterintelligence Command]], then led by Yeo In-hyeong — another high school classmate of Kim's — planned the specifics of the operation, making it "clear that this plan was conceived to offer a pretext for the invocation of martial law."[2] A November reference document issued under Yeo's orders and released by the Democratic Party showed the Command had reviewed declaring martial law and united defense simultaneously to execute military responses and control public order in the event of armed conflict with the North, permissible in the case of "enemy infiltration, provocation and domestic circumstances."[2]

## North Korean reaction

On Wednesday, December 11, 2024, North Korean state media reported for the first time on President [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]]'s failed December 3 martial law attempt.[2] The Korean Central News Agency said "the shocking incident of the puppet Yoon Suk Yeol regime...suddenly declaring a martial law decree and unhesitatingly wielding the guns and knives of its fascist dictatorship wrought havoc across South Korea," a report also carried by the Rodong Sinmun daily alongside a photo of a rally outside the National Assembly in Seoul.[2] The report said "several helicopters and fully armed martial law forces, including the gangster organization the Army Special Warfare Command, were deployed to seal off the National Assembly," described the six-hour decree and the failure of the impeachment motion, and quoted demonstrators calling Yoon a "disaster" and demanding his "immediate impeachment" and "punishment."[2] It added that "the international community is sternly watching," assessing that the incident exposed vulnerabilities in South Korean society and that Yoon's political life could face an early end.[2] Pyongyang had maintained silence since the crisis began.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the development fascinating because it rested on testimony by military leaders to Parliament that directly contradicted the account of the now-disgraced president.[2] He cited a widely shared X thread claiming the plan went further: that the HID, a South Korean special forces unit established to assassinate North Korean leaders in wartime, would don North Korean uniforms and assassinate opposition party leaders, with a backup plan in which the South Korean military would engage the HID troops believing them to be infiltrating North Korean assassins and kill both them and the opposition leaders in an elaborate false flag.[2] He noted this specific scheme is hard to verify, while the widely reported core — that the defense minister and president sought to provoke North Korea into striking first — was not in dispute.[2]

The alleged plan reportedly went back nearly a year, aiming to provoke an attack by sending drones and balloons into the airspace over Pyongyang to disperse thousands of propaganda flyers.[2] [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]]'s response was to send trash-filled "poop balloons" back to the South in an act of counter-provocation — which GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued both defused the situation and destroyed the South Korean president attempting to escalate it.[2] Yoon ordered his military to strike the balloons' launch sites and send more drones to Pyongyang; Kim answered with still more balloons rather than munitions.[2] The military leaders did not regard the balloons as a grave threat and refused the strike order, leaving Yoon exposed after declaring martial law.[2] The commentary celebrated Kim as having "out-foxed the CIA and beat them at game theory," and noted the irony of North Korea's official statement calling Yoon a "puppet" president.[2]

## Russian reaction

In a surprise televised address on Tuesday, December 3, 2024, President [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]] imposed emergency martial law, claiming that the opposition — which he accused of sympathy toward the North — had been preparing a "rebellion."[3] Within hours, 190 members of parliament who managed to reach the National Assembly despite military cordons voted unanimously to lift the decree, and the head of state soon reversed his decision.[3]

On Wednesday, December 4, Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] said the episode explained why [[north-korea|North Korea]] is "so concerned over its security," observing that in a couple of hours the South "can morph from a professed democracy into absolute chaos, with tanks on the streets, a storming of parliament, popular confrontation and some brute-force tactics."[3] She argued that with such an "unpredictable" neighbour, Pyongyang's security focus was entirely justified.[3]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the moment as "narrative inversion," arguing that [[south-korea|South Korea]] had become the place of political instability, tyranny and military coups long attributed to the North, and was visibly a puppet state of Western intelligence agencies.[3] He drew a parallel to Israel's conduct toward its neighbours as an inversion of accusations long directed at Iran, and closed by urging readers to pray for [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]], predicting attempts on his life as Western powers grew more desperate to maintain power projection.[3]
