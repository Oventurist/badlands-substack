# 2023 Israel Aid Bill Standoff

The 2023 Israel aid bill standoff was a legislative impasse in early November 2023 in which Senate Democrats blocked a Republican-led measure providing standalone military aid to Israel because it omitted funding for Ukraine.[1]

## The bill

The House of Representatives had approved, in a bipartisan vote, legislation giving Israel $14 billion in the wake of the invasion by the [[hamas|Hamas]] terrorist group; the House version also cut Internal Revenue Service funding to offset the cost.[1] Senator [[roger-marshall|Roger Marshall]] and other Republican senators called for quick passage, with Marshall telling the Senate floor that "a timely military aid package with a unified voice from Congress showing support for Israel will not only add to Israel's stability, it will slow down and hopefully stop the evil plots of Hamas, Iran, and its proxies."[1] He noted that the funding level matched President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s October request, which had also included additional money for Ukraine.[1]

## The block

Senate Democrats blocked the standalone bill on November 7, 2023.[1] Senate Appropriations Committee chairwoman [[patty-murray|Patty Murray]] said on the floor that "our allies in Ukraine can no more afford a delay than our allies in Israel," adding that "there is strong support here in Congress to address these urgent priorities in one package—and that is exactly what I am working with colleagues on both sides of the aisle to do now."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright recalled the "gnashing of teeth" when newly elected Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] promised to send U.S. aid to Israel, and argued that America First narrative shielding could continue unabated if Johnson's bifurcation of the Ukraine and Israel packages — both described as establishment proxy wars — led the establishment to block aid altogether.[1] The result was framed as "Congressional Paralysis": no fresh aid deployed to either Israel or Ukraine after Johnson's election, with narrative whiplash falling on the establishment on two levels — war hawks in both parties furious over the lack of Israel aid and hungry for a new Middle East forever war, and the American people watching the establishment block foreign aid on the grounds that not enough tax dollars were flowing to foreign wars.[1] The commentary concluded, "Wins all around."[1]
