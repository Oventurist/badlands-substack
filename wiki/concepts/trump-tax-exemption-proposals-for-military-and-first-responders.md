# Trump Tax Exemption Proposals for Military and First Responders

In October 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would consider exempting police officers, firefighters, active duty military personnel and veterans from paying federal income taxes, the latest in a series of campaign proposals directed at key groups of supporters.[1]

Asked about excluding first responders and service members from tax bills on the online show *Maintaining with Tyrus*, which aired Friday, October 18, 2024, Trump replied, "It's something I would think about," and added, "You're like my tax person there, but yeah. I mean something has to be done. It's almost an incentive to where you can get people interested."[1] He offered no further detail on how a service member tax exclusion would work.[1]

The proposal joined a long list of tax pledges Trump made while campaigning against [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], including eliminating taxes on tipped wages, overtime pay and Social Security benefits.[1] All of those ideas would require congressional approval to become law.[1]

[[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] reported that the military and first responder exemption could rank among the largest new tax cuts Trump had discussed, potentially exempting more than 20 million people from federal tax payments — roughly 18 million living veterans, about 1.3 million active duty troops, approximately 1 million police officers and more than 300,000 professional firefighters, according to several estimates.[1] The United States already offers some broad tax exemption to military members, but it is largely limited to those serving in active combat zones.[1]
