# Four Modernizations

The Four Modernizations was a long-term Chinese development strategy shaped by [[zhou-enlai|Zhou Enlai]] and carried out by [[deng-xiaoping|Deng Xiaoping]], launched after the death of [[mao-zedong|Mao Zedong]] and the imprisonment of the Gang of Four which had run the Cultural Revolution.[1]

In [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret's]] account, Mao and the Gang of Four had appeared highly compatible with [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger's]] static model of world order — a design admitting no room for population growth or technological progress and organized around a "master-slave" dependency between developed and undeveloped sectors.[1] The Four Modernizations broke with that arrangement and proved "far more foresighted than Kissinger realized," setting China on a trajectory of industrial and scientific development rather than managed stagnation.[1]

The programme is presented as the origin of the long-horizon investment posture visible today in projects such as the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and in China's national orientation toward increasing the free energy available to grow its economy — a direction contrasted with the closed-system decay attributed to the western world.[1]
