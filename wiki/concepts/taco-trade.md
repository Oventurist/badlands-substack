# TACO Trade

**TACO** is an acronym standing for "Trump Always Chickens Out," coined in financial commentary to describe an investment pattern in which markets sell off on announcements of aggressive tariff or trade measures by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and then rally when those measures are delayed, softened, or withdrawn. Traders who buy into the initial decline on the expectation of a reversal are said to be doing the "TACO trade," and the term has been extended by critics into a general characterization of Trump as a leader whose threats are not credible.

## Badlands treatment

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] rejects the characterization. Badlands Blitz Episode 14, published April 25, 2026, devotes a segment to "what the 'TACO Tuesday' crowd gets wrong about Donald Trump," with hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] arguing that observers who read Trump's reversals as capitulation misunderstand a deliberate negotiating method in which escalation and withdrawal are both instruments. The announcement does not spell out the argument in detail, presenting it instead as one of the week's items in a broader accountability and [[information-war|information-war]] framing.

## "TACO Tuesday" and the Iran ceasefire

The April 22, 2026 Badlands Brief extended the term from trade to war policy. After [[donald-trump|Trump]] unilaterally extended a fragile U.S.–[[iran|Iran]] ceasefire rather than resuming strikes, critics again invoked "Trump Always Chickens Out." [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] answered that the label is "completely, spectacularly wrong" because critics project onto Trump an end goal — war with Iran — that he never held. The real objective, in his account, is the rewiring of global energy routes through controlled pressure and calibrated signaling, culminating in Trump's invocation of the [[defense-production-act|Defense Production Act]] to fund domestic coal, LNG, refining, and grid infrastructure. On that reading the engineered energy squeeze served as narrative cover for a planned industrial buildout, and "TACO Tuesday strikes again — and that's a good thing."
