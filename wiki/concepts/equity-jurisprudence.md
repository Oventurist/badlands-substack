# Equity Jurisprudence

Equity jurisprudence is the body of law administered historically by the English courts of chancery, and in Badlands Media's account a British legal inheritance that survived American independence and helped make the creation of a private central bank possible.[1]

## English origins

A February 16, 2023 Badlands Media essay states that the [[inns-of-court|inns of court]] in England — of which the [[middle-temple|Middle Temple]] was one of four — were responsible for creating much of what is recognized today as equity jurisprudence, including the formation of chancery courts.[1] The inns are described as institutions functioning simultaneously as law schools and as sources of original law under the [[british-crown|Crown]].[1]

## Survival in the United States

The article's principal American example is the [[delaware-court-of-chancery|Delaware Court of Chancery]], whose general equity jurisdiction is measured against that of the High Court of Chancery of Great Britain as it existed prior to the separation of the American colonies — meaning, the essay concludes, that eighteenth-century British equity law was imported into Delaware and operates there to this day.[1]

The essay places this continuity within its wider claim that a "bastard system" of law was assembled in the United States, in which legal structures aimed at undermining state and individual sovereignty were steadily implemented before the capture of the American credit system by a private entity could be accomplished.[1] See [[bastard-system-of-laws|bastard system of laws]].
