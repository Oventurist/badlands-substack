## Texas primaries: ousters and "election deniers"

The Brief reports that Texas primary elections held on Tuesday produced claims of "voter disenfranchisement" alongside major political upsets. State Representative [[james-talarico|James Talarico]] won the Democratic primary for United States Senate in Texas, defeating Representative [[jasmine-crockett|Jasmine Crockett]] of Texas's 30th congressional district in a closely watched contest. Talarico advances to the general election for the seat held by Senator [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]].

On election night, Crockett told supporters in Dallas that voters had been "disenfranchised," saying her campaign received calls and emails from people who reported being denied entry to polling locations or experiencing confusion about where they were allowed to vote. According to the source, the confusion stemmed from changes in voting procedures requiring some voters to cast ballots at assigned precinct locations rather than at any polling site — a rule change that reportedly created widespread confusion in some counties and resulted in voters being turned away from incorrect polling locations. A Dallas County judge initially ordered polling locations to remain open longer after voters reported problems, but the [[texas-supreme-court|Texas Supreme Court]] later blocked the extension of voting hours. Crockett said the confusion and voter reports led her to believe some people were unable to cast ballots; the Brief notes that no formal findings of election fraud have been announced.

A Republican Senate runoff for the same seat is scheduled for May 26 between the incumbent Cornyn and Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]]. Other upsets included the defeat of four-term Representative [[dan-crenshaw|Dan Crenshaw]] of Texas's 2nd district by state Representative [[steve-toth|Steve Toth]]. Representative [[al-green|Al Green]] of Texas's 9th district did not lose outright but failed to secure a majority and was headed to a runoff against [[christian-menefee|Christian Menefee]] in an incumbent-versus-incumbent, redistricting-driven challenge; the Brief notes that early reporting that Green had lost outright was premature.

Ashe in America comments that the Texas results have Democrats "freaking out because of the impact on the narrative," arguing that the party's power projection for months had been that "Republicans are going to be destroyed in the midterms," while the first electoral test produced the opposite. She characterizes the outcome as a "war on incumbents" in which "regime darlings are being ousted — in both parties," and says it remains to be seen whether the trend extends to other states. She adds a note that she and [[canncon|CannCon]] discussed the primary fallout on Badlands Daily, including the reported "failure" to recruit enough election volunteers, but that she has since learned from people on the ground that there is more to that story, which she intends to report before Friday's *Why We Vote*. She concludes: "I don't know if we're getting real elections, but we're definitely get closer to real elections being possible."

## The world weighs in on the new world war

The Brief reports that the United States joined Israel in military strikes against Iran on Friday, and that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he ordered US forces to join the attack because he believed Iran was preparing to strike first. The President's remarks followed comments by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] that the United States acted because it expected Iran would retaliate against Israel's planned action and potentially target US forces.

"Russian hardliners" — a characterization the Brief attributes to [[reuters|Reuters]] and the wires — reacted strongly to the US attack, with some who had previously viewed Trump favorably now reportedly describing him as a threat. Some Russian hawks called on the [[kremlin|Kremlin]] to abandon US-mediated Ukraine peace talks and escalate Russia's war effort. The Brief also reports that Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] asked the [[white-house|White House]] for clarification after learning that Trump administration officials might be communicating with the Iranian government, according to two sources familiar with the matter, and that the Israeli government was reportedly concerned the United States might pursue a ceasefire or diplomatic arrangement with Iran before Israel achieves its war objectives.

## State-level clemency discussions for Tina Peters

The Brief reports that on Monday President Trump posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] to "Free Tina Peters," and that on Tuesday Colorado Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] signaled on X that he is open to commuting Peters's sentence. Polis's statement, quoted in full by the Brief, noted that former state Senator [[sonya-jaquez-lewis|Sonya Jaquez Lewis]] had been sentenced the previous week to probation and community service after being convicted of four felonies including attempt to influence a public official. Polis wrote that Jaquez Lewis "made a horrible mistake, and she was wrong," that he had known her as a friend for many years, and that "on a personal level I was glad to hear she isn't going to prison which is a hard place for anyone, no less a retired 68-year old pharmacist." He continued: "But it is not lost on me that she was convicted of the exact same felony charge as Tina Peters — attempting to influence a public official — and yet Tina Peters, as a non-violent first time offender got a nine year sentence. Justice in Colorado and America needs to be applied evenly, you never know when you might need to depend on the rule of law." Polis said this context informs his consideration of cases with sentencing disparities, which is why he extended the deadline for clemency applications until April 3, and that he would make decisions on such cases throughout the remainder of his governorship.

On Wednesday, Peters's attorney [[john-case|John Case]] shared a statement with Badlands Media's Ashe Epp responding to the governor: "Tina Peters is grateful to Governor Polis for considering her request for clemency... While Tina suffers in prison, another public official convicted of the same offense pays a fine and performs 150 hours of public service - less than one month of work. Tina's political enemies (Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]], Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]], and [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] shill [[matt-crane|Matt Crane]]) today released statements demanding that Governor Polis show no mercy. It will take great courage for Governor Polis to grant Tina clemency, and Tina thanks him." When pressed on whether a clemency application had actually been submitted, a member of the team responded only: "There is no missing paperwork."

Ashe in America writes that the story is "consuming my week," being fast-moving with more breaking news expected. Recapping her Sunday piece "Same Statute, Different Outcomes," she contrasts the prosecutorial theories used in the two cases: "In Jaquez-Lewis's case, prosecutors used a proceeding-based theory for the influence charge and minimized multiplicity by treating the committee as a single official. In Peters' case, prosecutors used a recipient-based theory and maximized multiplicity by treating each communication as a separate statutory violation. Both approaches are legally permissible under existing Colorado doctrine — but it's the intent of the discretion that is curious." She notes that the influence counts were stacked per recipient in one case and not the other, producing almost a decade of prison in one and probation in the other, and rejects the explanation that the difference reduces to the defendants' level of repentance: "Jaquez-Lewis is a democrat whose alleged illegal conduct was in service of keeping her democrat seat of power in a system controlled at each level by democrats. Tina Peters is a republican whose alleged illegal conduct was in service of investigating legitimate concerns about how powerful people attain their seats of power in the first place."

Recalling her earlier prediction that "Polis was going to disappoint his party this year," she says that on Tuesday "Polis dropped his nuke, and his allies in the state freaked all the way out." Quoting her follow-up piece, she writes that "the far left in the Centennial State lost their minds, especially the radicals running the disaster that is Colorado's 'Justice' system. Tina Peters is their most valuable political scalp, and she is the absolute last gasp of their carefully created narrative about '[[election-denial|election denialism]]' being dangerous to 'democracy.' ### Supreme Court asylum ruling

The Brief reports that the Supreme Court ruled unanimously on Wednesday that federal appeals courts must defer to immigration agencies' factual findings when deciding whether an asylum seeker's accepted facts qualify as persecution, a decision characterized as a legal win for the Trump administration. Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]], writing for the Court, said the [[immigration-and-nationality-act|Immigration and Nationality Act]] requires this deferential review, meaning appeals courts can overturn an agency's decision only if it is clearly unsupported by the evidence. The Court affirmed the lower court's ruling with no dissents. The case involved Douglas Humberto Urias-Orellana, a Salvadoran national who entered the United States without authorization in 2021 with his wife and child and testified that a hitman had targeted him in El Salvador; an immigration judge found his testimony credible but determined the events did not meet the legal threshold for asylum, a conclusion the Supreme Court left in place.

### Bondi subpoenaed in congressional Epstein probe

The Brief reports that the [[house-committee-on-oversight-and-accountability|US House Oversight Committee]] issued subpoenas in the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] case that week, one of them targeting Attorney General Pam Bondi. Lawmakers are seeking documents and communications related to Epstein investigations in Florida during Bondi's tenure as state attorney general. The subpoenas follow allegations that key records in the investigation had not been fully released.
