# Jewish Terrorism

"Jewish terrorism" is the term used in Israeli security discourse — and adopted in Badlands commentary — for politically or religiously motivated violence committed by Israeli Jewish civilians, particularly settlers in the [[west-bank|West Bank]], against Palestinians, Christians, and at times Israeli institutions and citizens. The May 1, 2026 [[badlands-brief-579|Badlands Brief]] treats the phenomenon as a rising internal threat that Israel's own security establishment has begun to acknowledge publicly.

## IDF warning

A senior [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] general warned, in reporting cited by the Brief from the Times of Israel, that rising extremist violence by Israeli civilians in the West Bank could trigger a broader Palestinian uprising. The warning followed a string of internal security incidents including assaults and trespassing cases. Israeli authorities have made arrests in several cases, but officials acknowledge gaps in enforcement capacity — notably in the Negev, where a state comptroller report attributed rampant crime to police manpower shortages. The concern expressed is that internal instability, rather than external threats alone, could drive the next phase of conflict.

## Incidents cited

The Brief highlights several episodes. Israeli settlers stormed the home of the Chief of Military Prisons, angered that the IDF had been arresting Ultra-Orthodox students who refuse to comply with conscription orders; the rioters were charged with assault and trespassing. Commentary from [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] states that the murder rate in the Negev has tripled in recent months. The most prominent incident described is an apparently random attack on a French nun at a Jerusalem biblical site by a 36-year-old Israeli man; the nun was reportedly hospitalized with head injuries, and Israeli police confirmed both the authenticity of video of the assault and the suspect's arrest.

## Conscription and entitlement argument

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry links the wave of violence to the conscription dispute. He argues that Ultra-Orthodox refusal to serve arises not from opposition to the IDF's military campaigns but from "a sense of entitlement," rooted in nearly 80 years of exemption from required military service on the premise of rabbinical study. That exemption was ultimately brought before Israel's Supreme Court, which determined it had never been codified into law and was therefore not legitimate in practice.

## Political and diplomatic dimension

The Brief notes that Jewish terrorism had become a talking point in the upcoming Israeli elections, citing prime ministerial candidate [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] acknowledging a descent into lawlessness and vowing to restore law and order as knives came out for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry sharply criticizes US Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], who he says has been repeatedly deployed to deny that attacks on Christians are occurring at all, calling him "a tremendous coward who is devoid of any principles." He concludes that global opinion has turned against Israel not because of biblical prophecy or antisemitism but because ordinary people exercise discernment, and challenges [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionists]] over how long they will "forsake the teachings and spirit of Christ to advance the political interests of a foreign government."
