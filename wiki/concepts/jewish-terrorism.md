# Jewish Terrorism (Israeli Settler Violence)

"Jewish terrorism" is the term used by Israeli security officials and in Badlands coverage for extremist violence committed by Israeli civilians, particularly settlers, inside Israel and the West Bank. In late April 2026, a senior [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] general warned that rising extremist violence by Israeli civilians in the West Bank could trigger a broader Palestinian uprising.[1]

## Incidents and enforcement

The warning came amid a string of internal security incidents, including assaults, trespassing cases, and a widely reported attack on a French nun at a Jerusalem biblical site.[1] Israeli authorities made arrests in several recent cases, but officials acknowledged gaps in enforcement capacity — particularly in regions such as the Negev, where police manpower shortages were tied to rising crime in a state comptroller report.[1] The Badlands account reported that the murder rate in the Negev had tripled in recent months.[1]

Writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that this violence was sweeping through the holy land, targeting not only Christians and Muslims in Palestine but Jews in Israel as well.[1] He cited settlers storming the home of the Chief of Military Prisons, angered that the IDF had been arresting Ultra-Orthodox students who refused conscription orders.[1]

## The conscription dispute

According to the account, the Ultra-Orthodox refusal to serve stemmed not from disagreement with the IDF's campaigns but from a sense of entitlement built on nearly 80 years of exemption from required military service, premised on rabbinical study.[1] That exemption was brought before the Supreme Court, which determined it had never been codified into law and was therefore not legitimate in practice.[1]

## The French nun attack

The most recent episode described was a seemingly random attack on a French nun in Jerusalem by a 36-year-old Israeli man.[1] The nun was reportedly hospitalized with head injuries, and Israeli police issued a statement affirming that video of the incident was genuine and that the man had been arrested.[1]

## Political fallout

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry criticized US Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], who he said had for months been "trotted out by the Israelis" to deny that attacks against Christians were occurring, predicting he would continue to ignore mounting evidence.[1] Jewish terrorism had by then become a talking point in the upcoming Israeli elections, with prime ministerial candidate [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] acknowledging a descent into lawlessness, the rise of terrorism among settlers, and vowing to restore law and order as knives came out for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

The article argued that the world's turn against Israel owed not to biblical prophecy or antisemitism but to ordinary discernment of settler violence and the perceived lack of candor from responsible authorities, and questioned how long self-described Christian Zionists would make excuses for it.[1]

## April 2026 escalation

In April 2026 Badlands reported that Jewish terrorism was "exploding" around the Middle East, anchored by the [[debel-statue-desecration|desecration of a statue of Jesus]] by an Israeli soldier with a sledgehammer in the Christian village of Debl in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[2] The [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] verified the image and said the matter was being handled through the chain of command, while Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] called the act "grave and disgraceful."[2] Badlands noted that the Israeli government issues such statements whenever acts of terrorism are committed by the IDF or by the [[israeli-settler-movement|Israeli Settler Movement]], and that nothing ever happens to those responsible.[2]

[[pinchas-wallerstein|Pinchas Wallerstein]], a founder of the modern settler movement, told [[haaretz|Haaretz]] he was horrified by the rise of Jewish terrorism and labelled it "treason" against the State of Israel.[2] Other incidents cited include the destruction of the Tomb of Saint Peter, described as accidental, and warnings from [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s government that Lebanese Christians should not return to their villages despite the ceasefire.[2] Journalist [[shaiel-ben-ephraim|Shaiel Ben-Ephraim]] compiled allegations of torture and rape of Palestinian detainees in Israeli custody, and Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] renewed calls to reoccupy and settle Gaza.[2]
