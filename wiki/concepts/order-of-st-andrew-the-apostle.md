# Order of St. Andrew the Apostle

The Order of St. Andrew the Apostle is Russia's highest civilian honour and the highest order of chivalry in the country.[1] It was established in 1698 by Tsar Peter the Great and holds deep historical and cultural significance as a symbol of honour and achievement.[1]

## Insignia

The badge of the order is a golden cross with blue enamel featuring a crucified Saint Andrew the Apostle, adorned with a double-headed eagle and a ribbon inscribed with "FOR FAITH AND LOYALTY."[1]

## Criteria and custom

The order is bestowed upon distinguished leaders in government, military, science, culture and the economy for exceptional contributions to Russia's prosperity, security and sovereignty, and may also be awarded to foreign heads of state for "outstanding service" to Russia.[1] After the order is conferred, the office of the Russian president arranges for a portrait of the recipient to be created; these portraits are displayed at the State Central Museum of Contemporary History of Russia.[1]

## 2024 award to Narendra Modi

In July 2024, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] officially bestowed the order on Indian Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]], with the ceremony recognising Modi's "exceptional" contributions to advancing the strategic partnership between [[russia|Russia]] and [[india|India]].[1] Modi responded, "Honoured to receive the Order of St. Andrew the Apostle. I dedicate it to the people of India."[1] Badlands commentary noted the cross-confessional symbolism of a Christian leader bestowing a Christian accolade on a Hindu leader, drawing a parallel to the [[order-of-abdulaziz-al-saud|Order of Abdulaziz Al Saud]] awarded to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2017.[1]
