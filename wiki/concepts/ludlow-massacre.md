# Ludlow Massacre

The **Ludlow Massacre** of 1914 was an episode of lethal violence against striking miners in Colorado that marked a turning point in the public posture of the Rockefeller family toward organized labor.[1]

## The event

Colorado militia members fired on strikers at the Rockefeller-owned Colorado Fuel and Iron Company, killing forty people.[1]

## Aftermath

[[john-d-rockefeller-jr|John D. Rockefeller Jr.]], who had inherited and largely continued his father's business practices — including firm opposition to unions — softened that stance, at least publicly, following the massacre.[1] Badlands Media presents the episode as one of the moments in which the family's philanthropic public image was consciously constructed in response to hostile publicity.[1]
