# Commutation of George Santos's Sentence

The **commutation of George Santos's sentence** was President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s October 2025 grant of clemency to former New York Representative [[george-santos|George Santos]], who was serving a seven-year federal sentence for wire fraud and identity theft.[1]

## The clemency grant

Santos had served less than three months of the seven-year sentence when Trump announced in a social media post that he would commute the remainder.[1] Santos told CNN host [[dana-bash|Dana Bash]] that other inmates saw the announcement and called him over to see it: "I was not in the room at the time, and then I made my way to see it. I called my family and they told me it had happened, but I was not aware. It was a surprise."[1]

## Speaker Johnson's defense

House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] defended the decision on ABC's "This Week" with Jonathan Karl.[1] "The president has the right in the Constitution for pardon and commutation, of course," the Louisiana Republican said. "We believe in redemption. This is a personal belief of mine. And I hope Mr. Santos makes the most of his second chance."[1] Johnson added that Trump did not grant clemency to Santos because of his political affiliation, and argued that the president was being "transparent."[1]

The item was carried as a bonus segment in the October 20, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], sourced to [[politico|Politico]], without accompanying Badlands commentary.[1]
