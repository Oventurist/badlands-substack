# 2025 Trump Middle East Tour

The 2025 Trump Middle East tour was the first foreign trip of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term, taking him through Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates in May 2025.[1] The centerpiece of the swing was an investment forum held in Riyadh on May 13, 2025, where Trump addressed Gulf business and political leaders alongside Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1]

## Riyadh agenda

At the Riyadh forum Trump announced a sweeping package of agreements with the Kingdom, including a $142 billion defense agreement and a $600 billion Saudi investment package aimed at artificial intelligence, energy and infrastructure projects in the United States.[1] He publicly praised the crown prince as "an incredible man" and a "great guy," telling the audience "I like him a lot. I like him too much," and made no reference to the human rights disputes — including the murder of journalist [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]] — that had defined the relationship under President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

Trump used the same platform to express what he called his "fervent hope" that Saudi Arabia would join the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], the normalization agreements between Israel and Arab states concluded during his first term, while conceding the Kingdom would move "in your own time."[1] He framed the prospective accession in ceremonial terms: "It will be a special day in the Middle East, with the whole world watching, when Saudi Arabia joins us."[1]

## Syria announcement

The most consequential policy break of the trip was Trump's announcement that the United States would lift all sanctions on Syria and normalize relations with its new government, a move he attributed to lobbying by Mohammed bin Salman and Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]].[1] The decision drew a standing ovation in Riyadh, where Trump quipped, "Oh, what I do for the crown prince."[1] He was scheduled to meet interim Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] in Riyadh the following day.[1] See [[syria-sanctions-relief|Syria sanctions relief]].

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributors read the trip as a symbolic milestone rather than a routine state visit. [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described it as "the coming out party of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]," arguing that Trump "doesn't roll up to the World Economic Forum. He rolls up to the Kingdom, where he's greeted as one of their own," and that he arrived "with a royal retinue of oligarchs he brought to heel."[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the week "a camelback caravan of confirmation" for hosts who had long predicted Saudi accession to the Accords, while [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dubbed the emerging arrangement the "JACOB Accords" — "Just A Couple Of Bros" — and predicted that "the Old Guard is done."[1] [[gmoney|GMONEY]] summarized the financial implications in four words: "The [[petrodollar|Petrodollar]] is TOAST!"[1]

## Pre-trip reporting

Ahead of the visit, Arabic media reported that Trump's meeting with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] in Riyadh on Tuesday, May 13, 2025, was also set to include Palestinian Authority President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]], and Syria's de facto leader [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]].[2] Citing an unnamed informed source, the Palestinian newspaper Al-Quds said the Saudi prince "looks forward to Trump's agreement to the Saudi condition of establishing a Palestinian state," a condition Trump had claimed in February 2025 that Saudi Arabia was no longer demanding as a precondition for normalizing ties with Israel — a statement that drew an urgent Saudi denial at the time.[2] According to the same source, Trump had acceded to bin Salman's request to include the other Arab leaders at the start of the trip, which spanned Saudi Arabia, [[qatar|Qatar]], and the United Arab Emirates between May 13 and 16, and he would not visit Israel, whose government accuses both Abbas and Sharaa of supporting terrorism.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked Trump's teased "major announcement on a certain subject that some people have waited many years" for to a promise made to Abbas during his White House visit on May 3, 2017, seventeen days before the Riyadh sword dance.[2] He read Abbas's presence in Riyadh as "a rare moment of defiance where the 'radical' position is to make real lasting peace," and argued that Trump had "rug-pulled everybody" by moving toward formal recognition of Palestinian statehood despite the expected anger of peers in Washington, New York City, and South Florida.[2] Bringing in Sharaa from Syria and Aoun from Lebanon, he wrote, assembled all of Israel's bordering neighbors against whom Israel was then engaged in offensive operations, sending a clear signal to Tel Aviv — and to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] — that Abbas was Trump's man.[2]

## Exclusion of Israel from the itinerary

Asked on Tuesday, May 6, 2025 whether he would visit [[israel|Israel]] during the trip, President [[donald-trump|Trump]] said he would not: "We're not planning on stopping in Israel. We will be doing it at some point, but not [on] this trip."[3] He made the remark to reporters in the Oval Office during the ceremonial swearing-in of [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] as his special envoy to the Mideast.[3] Trump was scheduled to depart for Saudi Arabia on Monday before stops in Qatar and the United Arab Emirates, returning on Friday, May 16.[3] Earlier that Tuesday, Axios reported that Israeli Ambassador to the US Yechiel Leiter had recently proposed to the White House that Trump add a short stop in Israel to the itinerary.[3]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the omission alongside the surprise [[us-houthi-ceasefire-announcement-2025|US–Houthi truce]] as evidence that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] was being repeatedly sidelined, and suggested a comparable dynamic could emerge between Trump and the leadership of [[iran|Iran]].[3]
