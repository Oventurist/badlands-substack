# NSC-68

NSC-68 was the 1950 United States national security directive whose passage was spearheaded by financier [[paul-nitze|Paul Nitze]] through the [[committee-on-the-present-danger|Committee on the Present Danger]].[1] It first justified the notion that the United States should maximise its build-up of nuclear warheads on the supposition that the country stood in a moral equivalent of war with Russia.[1]

Throughout the 1960s, opposing forces pushed back against the doctrine NSC-68 embodied, producing the nuclear test ban treaty, the Open Skies Treaty, the Space Treaty and other trust-building measures; the 1972 [[salt-i|SALT]] agreement was an extension of those mechanisms.[1] The founding of [[team-b|Team B]] in 1976 revived the NSC-68 lineage of thinking within the U.S. national security bureaucracy.[1]
