# Live Not by Lies

**"Live Not by Lies"** is an essay by the Russian writer [[aleksandr-solzhenitsyn|Alexander Solzhenitsyn]] urging individual refusal to participate in official falsehood as the accessible form of resistance to authoritarian rule.[1] Badlands writers have invoked it as a guide for navigating the American political environment ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.[1]

## Argument

The passage quoted in the Badlands Brief asks whether there is any way out other than waiting passively, and answers that nothing will change so long as people daily acknowledge, extol and strengthen the most perceptible aspect of their encirclement: lies.[1] Solzhenitsyn writes that when violence intrudes into peaceful life its face glows with self-confidence, but that violence quickly grows old, loses confidence in itself, and must summon falsehood as its ally — since violence can conceal itself with nothing except lies, and lies can be maintained only by violence.[1]

Because violence does not lay its paw on every shoulder every day, the essay argues, what it demands from the ordinary person is only obedience to lies and daily participation in them; all loyalty consists in that.[1] From this Solzhenitsyn derives what he calls the simplest and most accessible key to self-neglected liberation: personal non-participation in lies.[1] Though lies conceal and embrace everything, he concludes, the individual can be obstinate in this smallest of matters — let them embrace everything, but not with any help from me.[1]

## Use in Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] quoted the essay at length in June 2026 as direction for readers facing the midterm cycle.[1] Responding to a reader afterward, she argued that the biggest lie of all is that elections are real and that Americans consented to be governed by their rulers through any lawful process, calling this the foundation of the other lies and the most uncomfortable to confront.[1] She affirmed nevertheless that voter intent is real and genuine elections are possible, but only once there is honesty and accountability about how fake they have been — and treated the deportation of noncitizens who vote illegally as a step in that direction.[1]
