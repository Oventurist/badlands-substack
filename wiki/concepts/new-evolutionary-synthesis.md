# New Evolutionary Synthesis

The New Evolutionary Synthesis was the system under which [[julian-huxley|Sir Julian Huxley]] worked for decades to salvage and repackage the Darwinism of his grandfather Thomas Huxley's circle, in collaboration with [[h-g-wells|H.G. Wells]] and J.B.S. Haldane.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the synthesis was outlined in Huxley's *The Uniqueness of Man*, and that it was essentially identical to the thesis of [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] — except devoid of any pretense to harmonize with Biblical scripture.[1] Chardin himself cited Huxley's essay collection in 1941, writing that Huxley had presented ideas "so parallel to my own ideas (even though without integrating God as the term of the series) that I feel greatly cheered."[1]

Ehret notes the reciprocity of the relationship: Chardin wrote his 1949 *Man's Place in Nature* as an homage to Thomas Huxley's 1904 *Man's Place in Nature and Other Anthropological Essays*, while Julian Huxley in turn wrote the introduction to Chardin's famous treatise *The Phenomenon of Man*.[1] The synthesis thus served as the scientific counterpart to Chardin's religious project, feeding directly into [[transhumanism|transhumanism]] and the revival of [[eugenics|eugenics]].[1]
