# 2026 Colombian Presidential Election

The 2026 Colombian presidential election culminated in a June 21 runoff won narrowly by conservative lawyer [[abelardo-de-la-espriella|Abelardo de la Espriella]] over progressive Senator [[ivan-cepeda|Iván Cepeda]], and was followed by a legitimacy dispute over the winner's United States citizenship and alleged intelligence ties.[1]

## Result

De la Espriella, a 47-year-old conservative lawyer, won with 49.6% of the vote to Cepeda's 48.7%, with inauguration scheduled for August 7.[1] He gained U.S. citizenship as an adult while running a Florida law firm and received an endorsement from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during the campaign.[1] Cepeda, also 47 and a leader of the Historical Pact party, initially delayed conceding until electoral authorities certified the results on June 24.[1]

## Cepeda's conditions and threatened civil disobedience

On June 30, 2026, Cepeda announced that he would not recognize de la Espriella as incoming president unless he renounced his U.S. citizenship and met other conditions.[1] He demanded that de la Espriella clarify whether he is acting as a U.S. "agent," citing his past legal representation of a former paramilitary leader who was a [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] informant, and called on him to halt any effort to extradite outgoing President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]], who faces U.S. federal charges in Brooklyn related to alleged ties to drug traffickers.[1]

If the demands went unmet, Cepeda said he would lead "peaceful civil disobedience," stating that "as the leader of the opposition, I will embark on the path of peaceful civil disobedience that implies not recognizing the authority of someone who will not defend our national sovereignty."[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the situation as difficult to parse.[1] On one side, de la Espriella served as lawyer for [[alex-saab|Alex Saab]] beginning in 2014, making him an ally of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], with speculation that he facilitated Saab's deal with the DEA and FBI.[1] On the other, Cepeda is the chosen successor of Petro, who restored relations between [[colombia|Colombia]] and [[venezuela|Venezuela]] after taking power in 2022.[1]

De la Espriella claims he will aggressively combat the cartels and round up street gangs, but Petro had already waged war on the cartels in collaboration with the US military since at least 2023, and destroyed tens of thousands of acres of coca by incentivizing farmers to uproot the plants and replace them with cocoa and coffee crops.[1] Petro and Cepeda are now raising questions about the integrity of the election while accusing de la Espriella of being a [[cia|CIA]] stooge, with Cepeda calling for mass civil disobedience unless the president-elect renounces his American citizenship and discloses his ties to the intelligence community.[1]
