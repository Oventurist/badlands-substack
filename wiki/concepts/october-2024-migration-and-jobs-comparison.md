# October 2024 Migration and Jobs Comparison

The October 2024 migration and jobs comparison refers to reporting, published on the eve of the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 general election]], that the Biden administration admitted roughly four times more migrants during October than the number of additional jobs created in the U.S. economy.[1]

## The figures

According to data obtained by NewsNation, business groups created 12,000 additional jobs in September amid an economic slowdown and the Boeing strike — one-ninth of the 112,500 new jobs that had been expected.[1] Over the same period, President Joe Biden's border deputies invited 49,840 additional "parole migrants" to fly or bus into the United States for jobs, admitted through the Office of Field Operations at ports of entry along the border.[1] A further 56,580 migrants, including many women and children, crossed the border illegally and were registered by the Border Patrol, some of whom were expected to be deported.[1] The administration also welcomed at least 150,000 legal immigrants, foreign temporary workers and refugees.[1]

## Reaction

Representative [[tom-tiffany|Tom Tiffany]] (R-WI) said that "more illegal aliens crossed our border last week than jobs added for all of October," calling it "Kamalonomics" and blaming [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], who he said had dodged oversight of migration beginning in March 2021.[1]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that "Harris delivered more invaders than incomes," described the release as the worst jobs report in modern history, and contended that the news cycle had been diverted by "animal-induced emotional blackmail and vapid celebrity endorsements" over the preceding weekend.[1] She added that many of the "new" jobs counted were second and third jobs Americans needed to survive inflation — lower-skilled positions most exposed to competition from new arrivals — and that bringing in more people than jobs hurt struggling Americans most.[1] Because early voters had already cast ballots before the data appeared, she treated the episode as an argument for a single Election Day on which "every American should be voting with the same available information."[1]
