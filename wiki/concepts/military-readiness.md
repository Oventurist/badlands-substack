# Military Readiness

Military readiness is conventionally defined as the military's capacity to engage in combat and to fulfill assigned missions and tasks.[1] It is a multifaceted concept: among other requirements, personnel serving in the armed forces must be physically, mentally, and emotionally fit for duty, and historically any compromise to physical, emotional, or mental health was treated as a factor that could hinder readiness and reduce the military's capacity to perform its primary function.[1]

## The 2023 transgender policy debate

Readiness became the central axis of criticism after The Dossier published a 34-page [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] memo in July 2023 describing benefits extended to service members who identify as transgender, including deployment waivers and indefinite waivers of physical fitness standards.[1] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that gender-affirming care compromises physical, emotional, and mental health and therefore renders recipients unfit for duty by the standard the military had applied for more than a century, concluding that the policy amounted to a deliberate decision to compromise readiness.[1]

## Comparison with the vaccine mandate

Deschamps drew a direct contrast with the COVID-19 vaccine mandate, which the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] justified on readiness grounds: personnel were required to be vaccinated to be considered fit for the battlefield, yet transgender-identifying personnel could in effect obtain waivers from comparable requirements.[1] He argued that the two standards could not be reconciled unless the underlying agenda had nothing to do with readiness and everything to do with undermining the military itself.[1]

## Recruiting environment

The debate unfolded while the United States military faced its worst recruiting environment since 1973, the year conscription ended and the current all-volunteer force was established.[1] The Dossier concluded that the military in 2023 represented "more of a social welfare and social justice program than an entity purposed with defeating an aggressing army."[1]

## Further commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the armed forces had gone "from 'don't ask, don't tell,' to 'don't expect mission readiness,'" describing the outcome as a permanent class of non-deployable, taxpayer-funded personnel and asking whether a 300-day deployability waiver combined with lifelong hormone therapy rendered such service members permanently unable to deploy.[1] She characterized the intentional undermining of the capability and mission readiness of the United States Armed Forces as a national security threat that "cannot be overstated."[1]
