# Babylon system

"Babylon system" is a term used in Badlands Media commentary to describe the whole of the political, financial and media order that the outlet's contributors regard as illegitimate — a system of managed captivity rather than a set of discrete institutions.[1] The phrase draws on the biblical image of Babylon as an imperial power holding a people in bondage, and it functions in this commentary as a summary label for what is elsewhere called the [[cabal|cabal]].[1]

## Usage

In an October 21, 2022 essay titled "Power to the People," Badlands Media contributor [[pepe-lives-matter|Pepe Lives Matter]] wrote that the ruling faction had "usurped the will of the people for decades," and that once the public learned the truth for itself that will would change.[1] Observing that "everyone who is paying attention is now appalled at the system in which we find ourselves," he identified that system by name: "This is the Babylon system."[1] Within that frame he argued that individual prosecutions mattered less than exposure, since "sometimes you sacrifice a pawn to go after the King and Queen," and that winning the wider contest was more important than charging every participant.[1]

## Relationship to the Exodus framing

The Babylon label sits inside a broader biblical analogy that the same commentary applies to contemporary politics, in which the population plays the part of the Israelites and the ruling faction the part of Pharaoh.[1] On this reading the obstacle to liberation is not the strength of the oppressor but the doubt of the oppressed: the Israelites' decades of wandering are attributed to a failure of faith rather than to Pharaoh's power, and readers are urged to "skip past the doubt cycle entirely" instead of clinging to what the essay calls comfortable slavery.[1] Victories such as the fall of the [[georgia-guidestones|Georgia Guidestones]] and the overturning of [[roe-v-wade|Roe v. Wade]] are offered as evidence the system is failing, while setbacks such as the acquittal of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] in Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s prosecution are dismissed as immaterial to the larger case, which the writer defines as crimes against children and treason.[1] The essay predicts the system's collapse and the eventual arrival of justice, holding that the movement's aim is to replace a failed political establishment so that power returns to the people.[1]
