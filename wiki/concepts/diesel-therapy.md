# Diesel Therapy

"Diesel therapy" is the practice of continually transferring a detainee from one jail or prison to another so quickly that the prisoner never spends enough time at any single location to establish commissary access, receive mail, or gain access to legal discovery.[1] The term is used by prisoners' advocates as a description of punitive transport used as an informal punishment or as a means of degrading a defendant's ability to prepare a defense.[1]

## Application to January 6 defendants

Investigative journalist [[mel-hawley|Mel Hawley]] reported that several January 6, 2021 defendants had been put through diesel therapy.[1] Proud Boys leader Zach Rehl described the experience in his Jailed 4 J6 Journal, writing that he was "getting shuffled all over really fast, meeting different officers and personnel and not getting told squat," and that when he demanded to know what he was charged with he was told only "conspiracy," with the officer shrugging when asked conspiracy of what.[1]

The practice was raised in rebuttal to Congressman [[ken-buck|Ken Buck]]'s September 2023 assertion that January 6 defendants had not been denied contact with counsel and that courts had made accommodations, including continuances, to ensure adequate attorney access.[1] Critics pointed out that [[brandon-fellows|Brandon Fellows]] went to trial after having access to discovery for only nine hours before representing himself pro se, and that the FBI was caught during the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] leaders' trial reading and sharing attorney-client privileged communications.[1]
