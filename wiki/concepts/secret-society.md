# Secret Society

"Secret society" is the organizing concept of [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s Badlands Media series "The Men Behind the Curtain," which argues that clandestine fraternities have shaped world events across centuries and continue to do so in modern institutional form.[1]

## Historical warnings

The article opens by noting that the idea of powerful men conspiring behind the scenes is "as old as time" and that far more than fringe theorists have warned of clandestine power.[1] [[george-washington|George Washington]], shortly before his death, read [[john-robison|John Robison]]'s [[proofs-of-a-conspiracy|Proofs of a Conspiracy]] and wrote that he did not doubt "that the doctrines of the Illuminati and principles of Jacobinism had not spread in the United States," adding that "no one is more truly satisfied of this fact than I am."[1] Robison had concluded that the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]], a hidden group of Freemasons, had infiltrated the new world.[1]

British Prime Minister [[benjamin-disraeli|Benjamin Disraeli]] told the House of Commons in 1856 that much of Europe was "covered with a network of these secret societies" whose objects included abolishing constitutional government, changing land tenure and ending ecclesiastical establishments.[1] President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] wrote of "a power somewhere so organized, so subtle, so watchful, so interlocked, so complete, so pervasive" that leading men dared not speak above their breath in condemning it.[1] Former New York mayor [[john-f-hylan|John F. Hylan]] in 1922 described "the invisible government, which like a giant octopus sprawls its slimy length over our city, state, and nation," headed by Rockefeller–Standard Oil interests and international bankers.[1] [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]] is quoted saying that in politics "nothing happens by accident."[1]

## Modern forms

DeLarme argues that while the term evokes apron-wearing [[freemasonry|Freemasons]] or cloaked figures practicing ceremonial magick, today's clandestine fraternities take a modern appearance, exemplified by the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], the elusive [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg Group]] and Klaus Schwab's very public [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[1]

He applies a formal test to the CFR: Article II of its bylaws states that anyone revealing details of Council meetings can be dropped from membership, which he argues qualifies the organization as a secret society and explains the volume of conspiracy theories surrounding it.[1] The essay closes by asking whether such prestigious members are merely part of a fancy club or the archetypal "men behind the curtain," concluding that enduring uncertainty seems to be the only constant.[1]
