# 2025 Supreme Court Ruling on Venezuelan Temporary Protected Status

In early October 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] allowed the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] to end temporary legal protections for more than 300,000 Venezuelan citizens living in the United States.[1]

## The order

On Friday, October 3, 2025, the Court granted an emergency appeal from the administration seeking to curtail the [[temporary-protected-status|Temporary Protected Status]] program, under which the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] may allow undocumented immigrants from countries facing humanitarian crises to remain in the country and obtain work permits, temporarily avoiding deportation.[1] The majority wrote in an unsigned order that "although the posture of the case has changed, the legal arguments and relative harms generally have not," and that for that reason "the same result that we reached in May is appropriate here."[1]

## Background

The Biden administration had twice used TPS to offer protection to an estimated 600,000 Venezuelan migrants, and the White House of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] revoked protections from most of them swiftly after his return to office.[1] Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] ended a 2023 designation that had made 350,000 [[venezuela|Venezuelans]] eligible for TPS, and attempted to revoke two extensions announced by the Biden administration that would have allowed all qualifying Venezuelans to keep the status through October 2026.[1] Although the Supreme Court initially approved this action temporarily in the spring, a federal district judge in San Francisco blocked Noem's attempt before the Supreme Court lifted that ruling.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] greeted the decision as the opening of "SCOTUS season," noting that the October 2025 session was underway with three days of oral arguments that week, including an elections case scheduled for Wednesday.[1] She called ending the policy "obviously the right move," characterizing the 2021 Venezuelan TPS declaration as a [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] policy she had championed as a 2019 presidential candidate and that [[joe-biden|Biden]] adopted as part of his platform and enacted almost immediately upon taking office — speed she took as a sign it may have been a condition of Harris joining the ticket.[1] Arguing that Biden and Harris were illegitimate and that their policy had flooded American communities with military-aged male migrants, she asked in what universe such policies had to remain in place months after they left office.[1]
