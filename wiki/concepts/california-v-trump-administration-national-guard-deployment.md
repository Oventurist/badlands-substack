# California v. Trump Administration (National Guard Deployment)

California sued the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] in June 2025 after the state's [[national-guard|National Guard]] was federalized and deployed to respond to protesters in Los Angeles, calling the move an "unprecedented power grab."[1]

## The lawsuit

In the suit, filed in federal court, attorneys for the state argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "unlawfully bypassed" Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] by placing National Guard troops under federal control without the governor's permission.[1] The troops had been deployed to protect federal property and personnel under a statute permitting the president to call the Guard into federal service during a "rebellion," but California argued the law does not apply to the protests Los Angeles faced over [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] operations.[1]

"At no point in the past three days has there been a rebellion or an insurrection. Nor have these protests risen to the level of protests or riots that Los Angeles and other major cities have seen at points in the past, including in recent years," the lawsuit read.[1] The complaint further argued that the cited authority — Section 12406 — explicitly requires orders calling up the National Guard to pass through governors, who normally control their own Guard forces.[1] The suit contended that the deployment stoked "fear and terror" and "heightened tensions with protesters and residents," and that local police had handled any violence at the protests effectively.[1]

## Context and commentary

The deployment came as Trump ordered 2,000 California National Guard troops into the city against Newsom's wishes, a contrast to 1992, when President [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] deployed the Guard to Los Angeles at the request of California officials during the [[la-riots-1992|Los Angeles riots]] that followed the acquittal of the officers who beat [[rodney-king|Rodney King]].[1] Separately, Trump publicly endorsed the idea of arresting Newsom, telling reporters "I would do it if I were Tom" when asked by Fox News correspondent [[peter-doocy|Peter Doocy]] whether border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] should act on Newsom's dare.[1]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asked what "illegal order" the state was referring to and predicted "a fun lawsuit."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a Kayfabian reading, suggesting Newsom's escalation — including calls for a federal tax revolt amid a California fiscal decline — served Trump's interests, and speculated that the concept of paid insurrectionists and political operatives seeded during the 2020 riots had been revived in 2025 as "another in a long line of Trump-Musk pincers."[1] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued the confrontation could clear out California's government via insurrection charges rather than through the election system.[1]
