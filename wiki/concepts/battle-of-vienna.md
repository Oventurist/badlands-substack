# Battle of Vienna

The Battle of Vienna of 1683 was the engagement in which the [[ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]] besieged the Austrian capital with a host of 150,000 invaders and was defeated by a relieving force whose decisive element was a cavalry charge 18,000 strong — described as the largest in recorded history — led by Polish King John III and his 3,000 [[winged-hussars|Winged Hussars]].[1]

## Commemoration

The anniversary, September 12, produces an annual flood of commemorative posts on social media feeds, which prompted the Badlands history segment written by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]].[1]

## Composition of the relieving force

While many present the battle as a purely "Christianity vs Islam" conflict, part of the cavalry force consisted of the [[lipka-tatars|Lipka Tartars]], the all-Muslim unit maintained by Poland whose lineage traced to the Mongolian Golden Horde and who had taught the Poles their mastery of horses after being exiled from Hungary in the 1500s.[1]

## Consequences and cultural legacy

The Ottoman defeat at Vienna is considered the beginning of the decline of that empire, which was dissolved at the end of World War I, with the core Ottoman homeland becoming modern-day Turkey.[1] Badlands argues the charge most likely inspired J.R.R. Tolkien's "Ride of the Rohirrim" at the end of the *Lord of the Rings* trilogy, and connects it forward to the American Revolution: roughly 90 years later, [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] recruited Hussar leader Count [[casimir-pulaski|Casimir Pulaski]] to fight the British Empire in America.[1]
