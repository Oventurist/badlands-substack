# Second GOP Presidential Debate (2023)

The second Republican presidential primary debate of the 2024 cycle was held at the Ronald Reagan Presidential Foundation and Institute in Simi Valley, California, on September 27, 2023. [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the polling frontrunner, did not attend, instead spending the debate night in battleground Michigan delivering a speech to striking auto workers in which he argued that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s electric-vehicle mandates would send their jobs to China.[1]

## Polling aftermath

A [[morning-consult|Morning Consult]] survey released September 29 and conducted on September 28 among 1,183 potential Republican primary voters found Trump's support rising to 63 percent from 58 percent in the firm's pre-debate survey.[1] Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], seen as Trump's biggest rival, fell from 15 to 12 percent while holding second place. Entrepreneur [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] and former South Carolina Governor [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] each lost two points, to 9 and 5 percent; former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] lost one point to 5 percent; and Senator Tim Scott of South Carolina was unchanged at 2 percent.[1]

Morning Consult concluded that the data reinforced its view that Trump was in the driver's seat of the primary and that Trump-less debates were not materially helping the other candidates — and might in fact be helping the former president.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that he and [[just-human|Just Human]] had discussed the debates on a Sunday night broadcast and had genuinely forgotten the second debate had occurred — an admission he treated as both humorous and illustrative.[1] In his reading, whether the remaining GOP candidates were participating under the direct orders of Trump and patriots or were working on behalf of [[deep-state|Deep State]] forces seeking to destabilize and distort the [[america-first-movement|America First]] movement, the "charlatans within the GOP" were being forced to go through the motions without Trump present to anchor them.[1]

Trump's withdrawal from the debate stage, the commentary argued, had both rendered the debates irrelevant as a subversion vehicle and cast the remaining candidates in his narrative shadow: by staying in the race, everyone from DeSantis to "RamaSwampy" was forced to simultaneously reinforce Trump's paradigm while attempting to differentiate from it — a posture the author summarized with Tony Stark's line, "that's ... not a great plan."[1]

## Trump's counterprogramming

Ahead of the debate, reporting indicated that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] planned to travel to Detroit on debate day to deliver a prime-time speech to more than 500 current and former union members, inserting himself into the dispute between striking autoworkers and the major auto manufacturers.[2] It was the second consecutive primary debate he skipped in favor of counterprogramming, following his online interview with Tucker Carlson during the first debate in August.[2] His campaign planned to fill the room with plumbers, pipe-fitters and electricians alongside autoworkers, and briefly considered a picket-line appearance that advisers called unlikely because of the logistical burden of his security protections.[2] Trump had not directly addressed the striking workers' wage demands and had attacked [[united-auto-workers|UAW]] leadership while casting himself broadly as their ally.[2] The venue for the debate he skipped was the Ronald Reagan Presidential Library and Museum, scheduled for September 27 in California.[2]

## Conduct of the debate

Contemporaneous accounts described the Simi Valley event as a messy affair in which [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], needing a strong night to revive a stalled campaign and close on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], sounded more poised and natural than in the first debate but was ultimately compromised along with the other six candidates by the format, by fussy moderators and by the field's own propensity to bicker and interrupt.[3] Although the setting was the Ronald Reagan Presidential Library & Museum, Reagan's "11th commandment" — "Thou shalt not speak ill of any fellow Republican" — was in short supply, a symptom of the palpable desperation of a field that had failed to dent Trump's large national and early-state polling leads.[3] The scramble to emerge as the clear alternative to Trump produced frequent cross-talk that made the exchange hard for television viewers to follow, along with constant interruptions and pointed insults, some aimed at the absent frontrunner.[3]

## Badlands assessment

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] found the evening uniformly cringeworthy, singling out [[chris-christie|Chris Christie]]'s "Donald Duck" jab and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]]'s description of the panel as all "good people," and concluded the debate did nothing but make the GOP look like "the party full of do-nothings that it really is."[3] He argued that Trump had again proved that not participating was the only way to win, contrasting the candidates' pontification with the former president's speech on inflation, immigration and what Fleuret called the ineptitude of "Resident Biden."[3] The debate, in his reading, chiefly demonstrated that the party's old guard was "in its death rattle" and that [[america-first-movement|America First]] was there to stay.[3]
