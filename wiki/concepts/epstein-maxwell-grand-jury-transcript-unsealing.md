# Epstein and Maxwell Grand Jury Transcript Unsealing

In July 2025, the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] moved to have federal courts release testimony heard by the grand juries that indicted the late financier [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and British socialite [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] on sex trafficking charges, as the president sought to calm an uproar over his administration's handling of the matter.[1]

## Court filings

The [[justice-department|Justice Department]] first sought court permission on July 18, 2025 to make public transcripts of confidential witness testimony given years earlier in the two cases.[1] Manhattan-based US District Judges Richard Berman and Paul Engelmayer asked the government to flesh out the legal bases for the requests.[1] In a pair of court filings submitted just before midnight on Tuesday, July 29, prosecutors argued that unsealing the materials would be appropriate given the "abundant public interest" in the Epstein case and persistent scrutiny of how it was handled by federal law enforcement.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the story to revisit claims about podcasters credited with helping Trump win the election.[1] CannCon's Newsletter argued that rather than worrying about what Maxwell might testify to, Senate Democrats should sign an amicus brief supporting the DOJ's attempt to unseal the grand jury transcripts under the "historical public interest" exemption from Rule 6(e), an exemption upheld by the Second Circuit Court of Appeals in the *Rosenberg* case.[1]
