# Court Shopping

Court shopping — also known as forum shopping or judge shopping — is the litigation tactic of filing a lawsuit in the jurisdiction whose bench is most likely to rule favorably.[1] It became a prominent feature of legal opposition to the second administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Use against the Trump administration

Opponents of the Trump administration used court shopping frequently to obtain favorable rulings and rapid blocks of key parts of the president's agenda, contributing to a series of early legal setbacks in his second term.[1] Because the federal government has jurisdiction over the entire country, groups suing the administration can file in a far wider array of federal judicial districts than is possible in typical litigation.[1] In practice, most challenges to and blocks of Trump initiatives originated in liberal judicial districts.[1]

## Supreme Court reprieve

A 2025 decision by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] was reported as offering the administration some relief from the tactic.[1] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the litigation wave as advantageous to Trump, quoting the sentiment, "I love it when they sue, because when it goes to the Supreme Court and we get a decision, it clarifies authorities, sets precedent and puts appropriate guardrails on government," and arguing that Trump calls himself a counter-puncher not because he reacts but because he controls the fight.[1]
