# Post-Industrial Monetarism

"Post-industrial monetarism" is the name [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] gives to the economic model adopted to replace the industrial economic model that had proven itself in the years preceding and following World War II.[1]

## Cultural preconditions

Ehret argues that the death of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] ushered in an age of pessimism and cultural irrationalism from which society never recovered.[1] The destruction of long-term vision — exemplified by the space program, the St. Lawrence Seaway and the New Deal projects — led populations to treat present pleasures as the only reality and future goods as merely the mystical sum of present pleasures.[1] In this setting money was permitted to act as a power unto itself for short-term gains rather than serving investment in the real productive wealth of society.[1]

## Institutional shift

The system was ushered in by [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]]'s 1971 announcement destroying the fixed-exchange-rate [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]] and replacing it with a floating rate system.[1] In the same year the Rothschild [[inter-alpha-group|Inter-Alpha Group]] of banks was formed under the umbrella of the Royal Bank of Scotland, which Ehret says today controls upwards of 70% of the global financial system.[1] In a 1983 speech, [[jacob-rothschild|Lord Jacob Rothschild]] stated that "two broad types of giant institutions, the worldwide financial service company and the international commercial bank with a global trading competence, may converge to form the ultimate, all-powerful, many-headed financial conglomerate."[1]

## Geopolitical dimension

Ehret writes that this policy demanded the destruction of the sovereign nation-state system and the imposition of a new feudal structure of world governance through control of the money system and the manipulation of populations who believed hedonistic market forces govern the world.[1] Geopolitical structures foreign to US constitutional traditions were imposed by nests of Oxford-trained [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholars]] and [[fabian-society|Fabians]] who converted America into a global "dumb giant" enforcing a neocolonial program under an "Anglo-US Special Relationship."[1] Ehret names the Dulles brothers, Rhodes Scholar George McGhee, McGeorge Bundy, [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] and Bush as advancing this British-directed plan through the 20th century.[1]
