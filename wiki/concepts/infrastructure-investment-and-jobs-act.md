# Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act

The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (commonly the "Infrastructure Act") is a 2021 United States law enacted under the Biden administration covering a broad range of physical and digital infrastructure spending.[1]

## Digital equity provision and FCC implementation

In November 2023 [[federal-communications-commission|FCC]] Commissioner [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] warned that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was pressuring the commission to approve rules derived from "a one-page section" of the Act, a provision Carr said contained "new rules of breathtaking scope, all in the name of 'digital equity'."[1] Carr characterized the resulting authority as allowing the federal government to "micromanage nearly every aspect of how the internet functions."[1]

Carr tied the dispute to broader broadband policy failures, blaming the administration's economic policies for the skyrocketing costs of building internet infrastructure in the United States and arguing that regulatory red tape had led the FCC to needlessly block and delay new broadband builds even while pursuing expansion of 5G services.[1]

## Commentary

The warning was reported by [[vigilant-news|Vigilant News]] and carried in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of November 10, 2023, where [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] framed the proposed rulemaking as "another shameless power grab by the Biden administration" that most of the public would have remained unaware of without Carr's intervention.[1]
