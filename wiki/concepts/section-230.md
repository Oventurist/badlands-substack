# Section 230

Section 230 is the federal liability shield contained in the Communications Decency Act, a 1996 law that gives internet websites broad liability protections for most third-party content posted on their sites.[1] It protects internet companies from many legal claims arising from content posted by their users.[1]

## Supreme Court rulings of May 2023

On Thursday, May 18, 2023, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] passed up an opportunity to clarify the scope of Section 230 in a pair of rulings that many observers believed could have changed social media and the internet permanently.[1] The justices shut down lawsuits seeking to hold giants such as [[google|Google]] and [[twitter|Twitter]] liable for terrorism-promoting content on their platforms, but nixed the suits without issuing any sweeping pronouncements on the immunity provision, which has come under increasing fire from both Republicans and Democrats.[1]

As [[politico|Politico]] reported, the cases marked the first time the high court had dealt with Section 230, and the outcome was a major win for technology companies, which had argued that narrowing their decades-old liability shield could be disastrous for the internet and lead to a greater influx of hate speech and extremist content online.[1]

## Criticism

In October 2020, Justice [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]] took issue with Section 230 in a dissent in the case Malwarebytes Inc. v. Enigma Software Group USA.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] argued that Section 230 has been "used as a weaponized tool against anyone not conforming to narratives set on many social internet websites," an abuse long called out by Justice Thomas and several members of Congress.[1] In this view, the provision functions as an excuse that private social media companies use to regulate and censor free speech, leaving corruptible media platforms as the arbiters of truth rather than allowing truth to be discovered.[1] Johnny Q called on the Supreme Court to stop side-stepping the issue, contending that the silencing of dissenting voices erodes society, explains collapsing trust in the mainstream media, and does a disservice to all sides of the political spectrum.[1]
