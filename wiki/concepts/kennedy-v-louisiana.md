# Kennedy v. Louisiana

Kennedy v. Louisiana is a 2008 decision of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|United States Supreme Court]] that blocked the imposition of the death penalty for the rape of a child where the crime did not result in the victim's death.[1]

## Contemporary challenges

The precedent became a live issue again in 2023–2024 as state legislatures moved to enact capital child-rape statutes in apparent conflict with it. Florida passed such a law in 2023, and in February 2024 Idaho's House approved [[idaho-house-bill-515|House Bill 515]], which would permit the death penalty for lewd conduct with children under 12 in aggravated cases.[1]

Rep. [[bruce-skaug|Bruce Skaug]], an attorney and co-sponsor of the Idaho measure, acknowledged the tension with Kennedy but argued the sitting Court would likely reach a different result, telling House lawmakers, "Well there's constitutional and there's constitutional. Depends on the court of the day."[1]
