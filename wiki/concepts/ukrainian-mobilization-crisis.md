# Ukrainian Mobilization Crisis

The **Ukrainian mobilization crisis** refers to the deepening manpower shortage in [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s armed forces during the [[russia-ukraine-war|Russia–Ukraine war]] and the increasingly expansive conscription measures Kyiv considered in response.[1]

## Manpower shortfall

As Ukraine's manpower crisis deepened in 2025, Russian state outlet RT reported that Kyiv was resorting to increasingly desperate measures to fill the thinning ranks of its army, with conscription drives failing and volunteer numbers dwindling.[1] Despite what the report characterized as brutal mobilization efforts, Ukraine's Armed Forces remained critically understaffed, and even aggressive recruitment campaigns and tightening draft laws had failed to produce the needed surge in enlistments.[1]

## Proposed expansion of the draft

According to the report, the government was moving toward slashing the minimum conscription age from 25 to 18, which would send teenagers into the conflict, while serious discussions were said to be underway about mobilizing women en masse — a step that would mark a historic escalation in Kyiv's attempts to prolong the war.[1] Because the account originates with RT, a Russian state broadcaster, its claims should be treated as contested.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that "the Deep State is going to get progressively more desperate, as it clings to maintaining control over the crime scene that is The Ukraine."[1] He argued that "Putin is coming, and he is bringing [[north-korea|North Korea]] with him," and said he did not expect a peace to be brokered, predicting that what he called "the false Kievan regime will fight to the last Ukrainian woman and child."[1]

## November 2025: desertions and the Jolie roadblock incident

By November 2025 the manpower shortfall had become the subject of open complaint from serving Ukrainian commanders. [[sergey-filimonov|Sergey Filimonov]], commander of the elite 108th Separate Assault Battalion known as the "Da Vinci Wolves," wrote on X that a newly formed brigade drawn from the ranks of the 150th already had roughly 3,000 personnel absent without leave while still in formation.[2] Telegraph contributor Owen Matthews reported that perhaps as many as 20,000 Ukrainian service members desert or go absent every four weeks, that Ukrainian authorities had opened some 290,000 criminal cases for desertion since February 2022, and that Kiev's military might be 200,000 soldiers short of the minimum needed to blunt Russian advances.[2] Current and former officers described frontline units operating at half or even a third of required strength, and nearly 100,000 young men were reported to have left the country after the government permitted men aged 18 to 22 to cross the border in August 2025.[2]

The coercive side of mobilization drew attention the same week when draft enforcement officers stopped the convoy of actress [[angelina-jolie|Angelina Jolie]] at a military roadblock near Yuzhnoukrainsk in the Nikolaev Region and conscripted a member of her entourage, described variously as her driver or bodyguard.[2] Jolie reportedly went to the draft center in person and, according to TASS sources, attempted to contact the office of [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]] to secure his release.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the paired stories to argue that Ukrainian men are dragged from their homes and cars to die in a pointless war, and joked that every member of [[congress|Congress]] who voted to send Ukraine money and weapons should be conscripted into its army.[2]
