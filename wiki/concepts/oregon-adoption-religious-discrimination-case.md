# Oregon Adoption Religious Discrimination Case

The Oregon adoption religious discrimination case refers to an April 2023 dispute in which the State of Oregon denied a woman's application to adopt because of her Christian faith and her refusal to agree to support gender transition should the adopted child identify as transgender.[1]

## Litigation

[[alliance-defending-freedom|Alliance Defending Freedom]] filed suit on the applicant's behalf, arguing that Oregon officials had placed politics above children's welfare.[1] Commentary accompanying the reporting characterised the state's position as a backdoor mechanism for openly discriminating against people of faith, motivated by ideological aims rather than child welfare.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] placed the case in the longer history of American religious liberty, noting that Rhode Island became the first colony with no established church and the first to grant religious freedom to all, including Quakers and Jews; that Thomas Jefferson drafted a bill as Virginia's governor in 1779 guaranteeing religious freedom for Virginians of all faiths and of none; and that James Madison later drafted the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] as part of the Bill of Rights, protecting freedom of religion, speech, the press, assembly and petition.[1]

He further observed that the Constitution prohibits religious tests as qualifications for public office, breaking with European tradition, and argued that Oregon's refusal inverted that principle more than 240 years later.[1] DeLarme added that the principle cuts both ways — that coercing faith upon those who have not chosen it is "the folly of man," and that denying a rite afforded to others on the basis of religion is reprehensible, especially when done by a government charged with upholding religious freedom.[1]
