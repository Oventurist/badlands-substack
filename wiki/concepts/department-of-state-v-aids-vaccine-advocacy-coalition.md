# Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition

*Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition* is a 2025 emergency-docket case at the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] arising from the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's effort to freeze roughly $2 billion in foreign-aid funds administered through the [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]].[1]

## The ruling

On March 5, 2025, in a 5–4 split, the Supreme Court denied the administration's application for a stay of a district court's temporary restraining order against the funding freeze.[1] The unsigned order left in place the TRO issued by U.S. District Judge [[amir-ali|Amir Ali]], which compelled payment of the frozen money.[1]

Although the order was unsigned, legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] observed that Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] and Associate Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]] had joined the Court's three liberal justices; the alignment was inferable from the dissent authored by Associate Justice [[samuel-alito|Samuel Alito]], joined by Justices [[clarence-thomas|Thomas]], [[neil-gorsuch|Gorsuch]] and [[brett-kavanaugh|Kavanaugh]].[1] Turley characterized the administration as "down by one vote but hardly out" in its broader fight with the lower courts over control of the funding.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that headlines framed the decision as an emphatic win for the administrative state and a staggering loss for the implementation of Trump's DOGE agenda, but argued that a closer reading showed the order merely setting the stage for continued appeals in either direction.[1] He suggested the episode formed part of "an era of narrowing" of authority — from the presidency down to the courts — in which the limits of current authority must first be stretched before a mandate for its curtailment can be drawn.[1]
