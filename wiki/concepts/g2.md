# G2

The "G2" is shorthand for the proposition that the world's most consequential economic and geopolitical decisions are increasingly shaped not by traditional multilateral institutions but by the bilateral relationship between Washington and Beijing — the United States and [[china|China]] as the world's two largest powers exercising outsized influence over global outcomes. The term supplies the title of the Badlands Brief of June 16, 2026, "The G7 Laments the G2."

## Emergence at the 2026 G7 summit

The Brief reports, citing the Associated Press and Politico, that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arrived on Monday in Évian-les-Bains, France, for the [[group-of-seven|G7]] summit, having just announced a framework agreement aimed at ending the recent conflict with [[iran|Iran]] and thereby entering the meetings with a major foreign-policy development in hand. The published agenda covered Iran, [[ukraine|Ukraine]], trade disputes, energy security, artificial intelligence, and China's economic influence.

Against that agenda, the Brief records that European officials and analysts were increasingly concerned that the decisive forum was no longer the G7 at all. Several reports cited note that China's economic power and the state of US–China relations continue to shape most of the issues under discussion, even as leaders sought coordinated approaches to trade, critical minerals, AI, and the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East. Trump was expected to press for progress on Ukraine following his Iran effort, while European leaders sought continued American engagement on security and economic matters.

## The Badlands reading

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treats the G2 framing as something more than diplomatic commentary. In his account, Trump's earlier invocation of a "G2" with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] "was not just a narrative deployment, but a public acknowledgment of the pincer already closing on the post-war order." He argues that the American and Chinese economies, when they choose to circumvent what he calls the "Global Collectorate that refers to itself as the 'G7'" rather than compete inside its rigged architecture, "possess the gravitational mass to reroute trade flows, reset financial rails and redirect energy corridors on a scale the old order cannot absorb."

The essay extends the same logic to the kinetic plane between the United States and Russia, holding that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s assertion that Russia stands alone against the "Collective West" is true "only insofar as the optics the Globalist media still controls." In the "Realm of the Real," the argument runs, Trump and Xi are already in Putin's corner economically, energetically and strategically. What the "Globalist Beast" fears in this trinity is said to be not perpetual war but "cooperative competition among sovereign poles capable of enforcing a Multipolar World without needing the Hegemon's permission or its institutions ... or its Forever Wars."

Burning Bright further argues that the true target of Trump's global campaign "has never been the Proxies that have been made of Iran, Venezuela, Ukraine, Cuba, Taiwan or any of the rest," which he casts as symptoms, but "the scourge of globalism itself and the Prussian rails on which its actuals and narratives have run for decades." As the hegemon loses the ability to keep those rails greased and its stories coherent, he writes, the proxies "begin to slip their handlers, falling into the waiting arms of Trump and his ever-present and omnipresent 'deals.'" These are interpretive claims advanced by the commentator. See also [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]].
