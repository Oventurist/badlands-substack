# German Knife Amnesty Proposal

The German knife amnesty proposal was a 2024 policy suggestion by [[jochen-kopelke|Jochen Kopelke]], chairman of the German police union [[gewerkschaft-der-polizei|Gewerkschaft der Polizei]] (GdP), that criminals and youths who surrender knives to authorities receive substantial material rewards — including, in his most widely quoted formulation, a year's subscription to the Netflix streaming service.[1]

## Background and proposal

Kopelke raised the idea in response to what he described as a rising knife-crime epidemic enveloping German cities, calling on the federal left-wing administration to "think outside the box" to tackle the issue effectively.[1] He proposed a general amnesty for anyone in possession of a knife who was willing to hand the weapon in, but argued that an amnesty alone would not work without incentives: "For this measure to be effective, the federal government must create serious incentives for those who donate," he said, adding that "specifically, that could mean a year of Netflix for the delivery of a banned butterfly knife."[1]

German law prohibits possession of knives with a blade length of 12 centimetres or greater, but butterfly knives have nonetheless become a go-to weapon and knife crime has spiralled in recent years.[1] The proposal was reported by ReMix News and excerpted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the proposal as an unexpected 2024 development and as a window into how far civilian disarmament proceeds once, in her framing, authoritarians gain a foothold — predicting that hammers and garden tools would be targeted next.[1] She argued that the underlying principle at stake is not arms as such but the capacity of a people to defend themselves, which she identified as the basis of the Second Amendment to the US Constitution, and contrasted Germany and Britain unfavourably with the United States on that score.[1] She closed by mocking the exchange of weapons for state-subsidised streaming access with the refrain "Not one inch, America. Shall not be infringed."[1]
