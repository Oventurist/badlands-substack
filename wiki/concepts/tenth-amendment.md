# Tenth Amendment

The Tenth Amendment to the United States Constitution provides that "the powers not delegated to the United States by the Constitution, nor prohibited by it to the States, are reserved to the States respectively, or to the people."[1] It is the textual anchor of American federalism and of arguments for state resistance to federal directives.

## Invocation in the Utah sovereignty debate

The amendment featured prominently in Badlands Media commentary on the [[utah-constitutional-sovereignty-act|Utah Constitutional Sovereignty Act]], signed by Governor [[spencer-cox|Spencer Cox]] in January 2024.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the legal analysts cited in mainstream coverage of the law discussed its constitutionality — chiefly against the Supremacy Clause — without mentioning the Tenth Amendment at all.[1] In her reading, when the federal government overreaches its constitutional authority, the states possess both the right and the duty to assert their reserved sovereignty, something she argued has happened repeatedly throughout American history and is occurring in multiple forms in the present.[1]

She further argued that the Tenth Amendment should already cover the authority Utah sought to codify, with individual conflicts litigated on their merits, but that the scale of federal institutional transformation under the Biden administration might warrant states doing more to reclaim sovereignty.[1] Related contemporaneous disputes cited in the same commentary included the Texas border standoff and Colorado's Fourteenth Amendment ballot challenge.[1]
