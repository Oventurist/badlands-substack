# Rainbow Color Revolution

"Rainbow Color Revolution" is the term coined by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in an essay published June 23, 2023, for what he describes as an ongoing, domestically directed [[color-revolution|color revolution]] against the United States, conducted by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and allied elites using the same techniques the agency had refined in foreign coups.[1] The essay's subtitle, "Nothing New Under the Sun," signals its central claim: that the American situation is a repetition of a long-established regime-change template rather than a novel political crisis.[1]

## Naming and framing

Carlson built the name by analogy. Observing that the 1917 Russian upheaval has been called the "Red Revolution," and that the "color" in any color revolution is simply a distinguishing label attached to an episode of government overthrow, he assigned the rainbow to the American case.[1] The choice reflects his argument that the operation's public face is the language of "inclusivity" and "tolerance" — commitments he holds the elites pushing them do not actually share, using them instead to usher in control.[1]

## Definition of a color revolution

Carlson surveyed existing definitions before offering his own. He quoted Urban Dictionary's formulation — a revolution or coup in a country negatively perceived by the US Government that aligns it with a pro-US domestic government, "often with covert or open American support" — and Wikipedia's account of a term used since about 2004 for anti-regime protest movements and changes of government in post-Soviet Eurasia and the former Yugoslavia, later applied more widely to the Middle East, the Asian Pacific region and South America from the late 1980s to the 2020s.[1]

His own definition is non-traditional warfare: the covert overthrow of a government without an overt declaration of war, in many cases led by the CIA, using propaganda, orchestrated protests and street violence to create unrest, after which a US-friendly and US-controlled government is typically installed.[1] He argued that color revolutions long predate the late 1980s, counting the [[french-revolution|French Revolution]] and "certainly" the [[bolshevik-revolution|Russian Revolution]] among them, and that the method has evolved toward heavier propaganda and manipulation and away from conventional force.[1]

## Historical precedents cited

### The Russian Revolution

Carlson recounted that unrest grew in the Russian military and population in early 1917 as World War I went badly, eroding confidence that Tzar Nicholas II could run the empire in wartime and leading him to abdicate to a government led by the Russian Parliament.[1] In October 1917 the Bolsheviks, a group of far-left radicals led by [[vladimir-lenin|Vladimir Lenin]], overthrew both the monarch and the new government — an operation involving only a small share of the population but manufacturing a false perception of broad support through staged protests, strikes and street violence.[1] Civil war between the "Reds" and the "Whites" followed, ending in the creation of the Soviet Union; the Tzar and his family were murdered in 1918 by the same people.[1] Carlson used this sequence as the direct analogue for present-day America, where he saw far-left elitists propagandizing the country and creating division and unrest to gain and maintain power.[1]

### Iran, 1953

He then turned to Iran, where Prime Minister [[mohammad-mosaddegh|Mohammad Mosaddegh]], democratically elected in 1953, audited British control of Iranian oil production and moved to nationalize it, expelling foreign companies and triggering a British-led worldwide boycott of Iranian oil.[1] As a favor to Britain, the CIA mounted a [[1953-iranian-coup|coup]] using propaganda, election rigging and paid, bused-in thugs, with pro-Shah riots causing 200 to 300 deaths; Mosaddegh was replaced by [[mohammad-reza-pahlavi|the Shah]] and convicted of treason.[1] Carlson considered the parallel to the American case self-evident, noting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — whom he said the CIA helped remove from office through propaganda, election rigging and paid rioting — had just been indicted under the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]].[1]

### The post-Soviet wave

With the fall of the Soviet Union in the late 1980s and early 1990s, Carlson wrote, the CIA "found their groove," intervening where newly independent Eastern Bloc leaders appeared anti-American or pro-Russian.[1] He listed Georgia's 2003 Rose Revolution, Ukraine's 2004 [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]], Kyrgyzstan's 2005 Tulip Revolution and Egypt's 2011 [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]], all marketed as "forwarding democracy," exporting "American values" and supporting humanitarian rights, but in his reading covert coups for control.[1] He asserted that in the preceding thirty years, 90% of government overthrows involved non-violent revolutions.[1]

### Ukraine, 2014

Carlson identified the 2014 [[euromaidan|Ukrainian upheaval]] as a CIA-fomented color revolution to remove a pro-Russia, pro-Putin president in favor of a pro-America, pro-CIA leader, alleging paid violent protesters and CIA snipers shooting from tall buildings, and the creation of the [[azov-battalion|Azov]] units through agency training and funding.[1] Once Ukraine became what he called a CIA puppet, he argued, US-run bioweapon labs appeared alongside trafficking in women, children, drugs and body parts, with [[barack-obama|Obama]] and the Biden family deeply involved — in his view a major reason for the current war and for continued US funding of Ukraine.[1]

## The alleged CIA template

Carlson set out a reusable operational sequence: identify the target; launch psychological war; find or create political activists and supply them with information and funding; organize them into political protests supported by NGOs and the media; and provide financial support to the country's opposition parties.[1] The outcome, he argued, is not democracy or prosperity but chaos and instability, with many participants convinced they are doing the right thing while being manipulated and used.[1]

## Application to the United States

On Carlson's account the agency has turned this apparatus inward: the media is controlled, NGOs and radical groups have been created, organized and funded to protest violently, and he named [[cnn|CNN]], [[msnbc|MSNBC]], [[fox-news|FOX]], [[npr|NPR]], the [[washington-post|Washington Post]], the [[new-york-times|New York Times]], every [[george-soros|Soros]]-run NGO, [[antifa|Antifa]] and [[black-lives-matter|BLM]].[1] The inversion he stresses is that instead of infusing "American values," the operation removes American values, morals and God from society — no different, in his framing, from previous Communist takeovers.[1] Where the foreign revolutions were disguised as bringing democracy and fighting Communism while delivering totalitarianism, the domestic version disguises Communism and totalitarianism as socialism and progressive policy.[1]

He pointed to [[esg|ESG]] and [[social-credit-score|social credit scores]] as the enforcement mechanism, warning that those who stand up to the elites will eventually have an "off switch" flipped on their lives through frozen bank accounts, job loss, removal of their children, or travel restrictions.[1]

## Origins and the Cabal thesis

Referring to his earlier post on early Communist-fighting patriots and their ties to Donald Trump, Carlson noted that the CIA was formed in 1947 following World War II but had already existed as the [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]], which ran covert operations during the war.[1] By 1953 it was overthrowing the Iranian government at the same time Joseph McCarthy was pursuing Communists in the United States — to him, evidence that the agency had been strategically planning to overthrow America since at least the early 1950s and to replace the US government with a Communist-like totalitarian one, prompting the question of whether that was the real reason the CIA was created.[1] He linked this to the claim that Wall Street bankers funded both the Bolsheviks in Russia and the Nazis in Germany, reasoning that financiers who bankrolled totalitarian governments abroad would also fund one at home, giving the Cabal complete power and control.[1]

## Outlook

Carlson closed on an optimistic note, arguing that the operation's very blatancy is "boomeranging" and inspiring people to say "enough is enough."[1] He described the elites as desperate — "like the guy at a bar who hears it's 'last call'" — mortally wounded but still dangerous, and their plan, once cloaked in secrecy, as now being exposed to the masses through the sacrifices of Patriots willing to risk everything.[1] He introduced the idea of a personal "saturation point," the moment a person can absorb no more lies, urging those who have reached theirs to be patient with those who have not.[1] While calling the takeover Communist, he clarified that Communism is only a tool used by the Cabal, and that what is really underway is a battle between good and evil — "a war for our souls. This is a war we can't afford to lose."[1]
