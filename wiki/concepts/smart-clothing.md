# Smart Clothing

Smart clothing, or electronic textiles, refers to garments embedded with sensors and networking capability able to monitor the wearer's body and environment. Badlands Media treats it as one of the frontier technologies of the emerging [[technocracy|technocracy]] — an extension of surveillance from social media platforms, online shopping and facial-recognition systems into the house a person lives in and the clothes on their back.[1]

## Marketing and the health frame

Like other technocratic products, smart clothes are marketed as healthy and helpful, promising to monitor sleep patterns, heartbeat, nutrition and physical activity. Badlands describes this as bait designed to lure in a first cohort of subjects and normalize the technology.[1]

A January article published in PubMed hyped electronic textiles as a "new age of wearable technology for healthcare and fitness solutions," touting applications in products as varied as diapers, masks and bedding, for uses including monitoring health conditions, treating chronic diseases, rehabilitation and improving health and social lifestyles.[1]

## Health objections

The article raises the long-term dangers of electromagnetic radiation, noting that most people already carry phones in their pockets all day in close proximity to sensitive anatomy and wear Bluetooth earbuds for significant portions of the day — and questions the wisdom of going a step further and draping oneself in "electro-smog emitting apparel."[1]

## Surveillance objections

Beyond health, the article warns that smart clothing involves "certain three-letter agencies." [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]], an adviser to [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], is credited with popularizing the idea of taking "surveillance under the skin." Badlands argues this is not an idle notion: there has been extensive discussion at WEF gatherings in Davos about implementing technology that harvests biometric data, and smart clothing is a stepping stone — a bridge between the smartphone and an eventual push for a digital ID chip literally under the skin.[1]

The concrete manifestation of this is [[smart-epants|SMART ePANTS]], the intelligence community's computerized clothing program run by [[iarpa|IARPA]] under the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]].[1]
