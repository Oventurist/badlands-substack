# Pentagon Press Credentialing Policy (2025)

The Pentagon press credentialing policy of October 2025 was a [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] media restriction that conditioned regular press credentials at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] on journalists agreeing not to seek out information that had not been approved by Pentagon officials — including unclassified material.[1]

## Walkout of October 15, 2025

Media organizations had until Tuesday evening, October 14, to decide whether to sign. Reporters from all major news outlets — some of whom had covered the Defense Department for decades — instead cleared out of the building on Wednesday, October 15, turning in their press badges and removing belongings from their workspaces. Many waited until the 4 p.m. departure deadline before leaving. The episode was described in press coverage as "a dark day for press freedom."[1]

[[one-america-news-network|One America News Network]] was the only outlet to sign on to the policy. Fox News, the major broadcast networks, PBS, the [[associated-press|Associated Press]], and The Wall Street Journal all refused.[1]

## Defense of the policy

Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] defended the restrictions, saying the Pentagon "now has same rules as every U.S military installation." Reporting noted that he had also falsely claimed reporters were previously allowed to roam freely around the complex without badges.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Chris Paul framed the standoff sardonically, asking why the media is "so pro-espionage" — casting the outlets' refusal as a defense of unrestricted collection inside a defense installation rather than a defense of ordinary newsgathering.[1]
