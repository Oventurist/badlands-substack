# Section 301

Section 301 is the enforcement provision of the [[trade-act-of-1974]] that authorizes the [[office-of-the-united-states-trade-representative|United States Trade Representative]] to investigate foreign acts, policies, and practices that are unjustifiable, unreasonable, or discriminatory and that burden or restrict United States commerce, and to impose responsive measures — most commonly tariffs — at the President's direction.

The statutory process runs through defined stages: initiation of an investigation, consultations with the foreign government, a determination as to whether the challenged practices are actionable, a public comment period and hearing on any proposed remedy, and finally the imposition of duties or other action. Because Section 301 vests the decision in the executive branch rather than requiring new legislation from [[congress]], it has become the central legal instrument of tariff policy under [[donald-trump]].

## Brazil, 2026

The Badlands Brief of July 17, 2026 describes a textbook application of the statute. After a yearlong Section 301 investigation into [[brazil]], USTR determined in June 2026 that certain Brazilian practices were unreasonable and restricted US commerce. The agency reviewed more than 360 public comments and held a July hearing with 77 witnesses before the administration imposed a 25% tariff on certain Brazilian goods. US Trade Representative [[jamieson-greer]] said the action was taken at the President's direction and formed part of the [[america-first]] trade agenda.
