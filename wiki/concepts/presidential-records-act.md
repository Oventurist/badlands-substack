# Presidential Records Act

The Presidential Records Act (PRA) is the U.S. statute governing the ownership and custody of the records of presidents and vice presidents, requiring that presidential records be turned over to the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] rather than retained as personal property.[1]

## 2023 classified documents sweep

In January 2023 the National Archives formally asked former presidents and vice presidents to re-check their personal records for any classified documents or other presidential records, following the discovery over the preceding year of classified documents at the homes of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

The Archives sent a letter on Thursday, January 26, 2023 to representatives of former presidents and vice presidents from the last six presidential administrations covered by the Presidential Records Act — from Ronald Reagan's White House to the present.[1] The letter requested that they check their files to ensure that material thought to be personal does not "inadvertently" contain presidential records that are required by law to be turned over to the Archives.[1]

Representatives for four former presidents had previously stated that they held no classified records: the representatives for Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, Barack Obama and the late George H. W. Bush all said that all classified records had been turned over to the National Archives upon leaving office.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that because NARA had written to every president and vice president from Reagan through Biden, all of them were now "on the record denying having any classified records," and predicted that such claims would prove "erroneous at best."[1] He characterized the control of classified documents as "a long time method of hiding abuses and crimes by those in high office."[1] The statute is a companion to the [[federal-records-act|Federal Records Act]].

## Invoked in the Trump documents case (2023)

After [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s June 2023 arraignment in the [[trump-classified-documents-case|classified documents case]], Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the Presidential Records Act supplies special privileges and protections to those who have served as president, and that it supersedes the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]] under which Trump was charged.[2] On that reading, the volume of material a former president takes with him on leaving the [[white-house|White House]] is legally immaterial — "whether it was a single postcard or a million documents ... he is allowed to do so."[2]

DeLarme contrasted that protection with the position of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], who as vice president would enjoy no comparable shield and who stood accused of pay-to-play and bribery schemes as well as of using the [[fbi|FBI]] to cover them up.[2]
