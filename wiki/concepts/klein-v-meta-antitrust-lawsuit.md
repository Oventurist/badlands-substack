# Klein v. Meta antitrust lawsuit

The Klein v. Meta antitrust lawsuit is a class action filed by two US citizens, Maximilian Klein and Sarah Grabert, against [[meta-platforms|Meta]], the parent company of Facebook.[1] Court documents originally filed in April 2023 were unsealed on March 23, 2024, and appeared to expose an intricate commercial relationship between two of Silicon Valley's largest players.[1]

## Allegations regarding Netflix and user messages

The unsealed filings allege that Facebook permitted [[netflix]] to view user direct messages "for nearly a decade" so the streaming company could better tailor content for its own subscribers.[1] The complaint contends that the two firms "enjoyed a special relationship," with the social platform granting the streaming service "bespoke access" to user data, alongside "custom partnerships and integrations that helped supercharge Facebook's ad targeting and ranking models" from at least 2011.[1]

Lawyers attributed the arrangement in part to the personal relationship between Netflix co-founder [[reed-hastings|Reed Hastings]] and Facebook founder [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], alleging that within a month of Hastings joining Facebook's board of directors the companies signed an "Inbox API" agreement that "allowed Netflix programmatic access to Facebook's user's private message inboxes."[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], placed the case in a sequence of data-privacy failures previously covered in the brief — [[murthy-v-missouri|Missouri v. Biden]] and Twitter's broken privacy assurances, the Google "incognito mode" settlement, and now Facebook selling direct-message access to the highest bidder.[1] The argument advanced was that such breaches will continue as long as technology companies own the rights to user data, since "our digital presence is an extension of our actual lives" and privacy remains a "pinky pie crust promise until data ownership becomes a reality."[1]
