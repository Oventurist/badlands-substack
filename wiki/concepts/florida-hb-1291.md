# Florida HB 1291

Florida HB 1291 is a 2024 state law banning what its sponsors termed "indoctrination" in teacher-training programs. It was signed by Republican Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] on May 2, 2024.[1]

## Provisions

The measure prohibits programs, courses, and curricula used in teacher preparation from "distorting" historical events or promoting political ideologies concerning race, sex, and gender.[1] Teacher preparation, training, and certification programs are barred from teaching "identity politics" or from asserting that systemic racism, sexism, oppression, or white privilege are inherent in America's culture.[1]

The law preserves a pathway for accredited postsecondary institutions to seek approval from the Florida Department of Education to create institutes that train teachers on improving classroom instruction and on meeting certification or recertification requirements.[1] Institutions may also seek approval to instruct existing and prospective substitute teachers in classroom duties, and to help holders of baccalaureate degrees become certified.[1]

## Related executive action

Announcing the signing, DeSantis's office said the governor was working with Attorney General [[ashley-moody|Ashley Moody]]'s office to shield Florida's workforce from ideological discrimination and harassment.[1] DeSantis said he does not believe companies have the right to compel employees to attend training designed to convince them that they are "inherently racist or sexist," or to diminish their sense of self-worth on account of their religious or social upbringing.[1] The governor's office framed the package as strengthening Florida's protections against "the agenda of the global elite."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] credited the sponsors with noble intent but questioned whether ending ideological formation of teachers is an achievable goal for government.[1] The commentary asked whether Florida would also ban collegiate coursework in social justice, doubted that legislation could displace multiple generations of what it called Marxist educators, and warned that the attempt would simply drive their methods to evolve.[1] It raised a series of enforcement questions — whether transplant teachers from other states would be re-educated, whether ideological tests would precede certification, and what the statute's enforcement mechanisms actually comprise — and cautioned that government powers created by one party do not remain with that party, asking how such authority might be wielded should Democrats regain power in Florida.[1]
