# Baal

**Baal** (also rendered Ba'al, and at [[carthage|Carthage]] as Ba'al Hammon) is an ancient Near Eastern deity described in Badlands Media coverage as a god of [[child-sacrifice|child sacrifice]].[1] Alongside [[molech|Moloch]], Baal functions in this literature as a marker used to identify occult symbolism in elite culture.[1]

## Carthaginian worship

Baal's importance to modern arguments about elite fixation on child sacrifice rests, according to [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], on history itself.[1] Excavation of the Precinct of Tanit at Carthage produced urns of cremated children with stone markers dedicating them by a special symbol to Tanit, the preeminent Carthaginian goddess, and to Ba'al Hammon, of whom Tanit was the consort.[1] The findings, reported by Dr. Lawrence Stager and [[david-soren|Dr. David Soren]] and published in a 1987 New York Times article, indicated that the practice intensified over time rather than fading into animal substitution.[1]

## "Baalenciaga"

The name resurfaced in modern controversy when researchers examining the fashion house [[balenciaga|Balenciaga]]'s advertising history discovered a campaign in which the brand created a custom caution-tape-style prop altering the spelling of its own name to "Baalenciaga."[1] Esler treats this as evidence that the modern echo of Carthaginian practice is specific rather than generic, since the reference is to Ba'al by name.[1]
