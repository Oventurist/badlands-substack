# Congressional Paralysis

"Congressional Paralysis" is an analytical frame used by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators to describe the state of the U.S. House of Representatives during the leaderless period that followed the [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy|ouster of Kevin McCarthy]], and its significance for American war-making capacity during the outbreak of the Israel–Hamas war in October 2023.[1]

## Origin of the frame

With no Speaker seated, one chamber of [[us-congress|Congress]] was effectively unable to conduct business at a moment of simultaneous crises at home and abroad.[1] The withdrawal of [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] from the speakership race on October 12, 2023 — after he was nominated 113–99 but could not approach the 217 votes required on the floor — extended that paralysis indefinitely.[1] Commentators [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], [[just-human|Just Human]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] developed the paralysis thesis over the preceding days as the situation persisted.[1]

## The argument

The frame holds that the timing was not merely unfortunate but consequential. Because Congress is the branch constitutionally empowered to declare war, a rudderless House deprived what Badlands writers call the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] war hawks of the ability to "strike while the iron's hot" and move the United States into a new kinetic conflict — following the loss of the Afghanistan war through the consummation of the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] and the exhaustion of the Ukraine effort.[1]

Burning Bright argued that the establishment war caucus was more exposed by the Israel conflict than at any time since 9/11, before a [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]] already tired of its domestic conduct.[1] In this reading, [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] "didn't just remove a Speaker, he removed the Uniparty's Narrative Shielding," leaving the world to watch congressional vultures circle the carcass of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s [[system-of-systems|System of Systems]].[1] The situation could be read either as coincidence or as a further instance of narrative exposure of Deep State factions without actual damage to the United States.[1]

Burning Bright further argued that the [[devolution|Devolution]]/[[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] operation attributed to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would negate any actual U.S. war declaration regardless, leaving the narrative benefit of visible congressional dysfunction as the principal effect.[1] The frame concludes that "an inactive Congress isn't a threat to the American people. It's a promise."[1]
