# Financial Innovation and Technology for the 21st Century Act

The Financial Innovation and Technology for the 21st Century Act (H.R. 4763), commonly known as FIT21, is a crypto market structure bill considered by the U.S. House of Representatives in May 2024.[1]

## White House position

On Wednesday, May 22, 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s White House published a statement of administration policy opposing passage of the bill, citing concerns over a lack of investor protections. Critically for the digital-asset industry, the president did not threaten to veto the measure, which was read as a positive sign.[1]

"The Administration is eager to work with Congress to ensure a comprehensive and balanced regulatory framework for digital assets, building on existing authorities, which will promote the responsible development of digital assets and payment innovation and help reinforce United States leadership in the global financial system," the statement said, adding that "H.R. 4763 in its current form lacks sufficient protections for consumers and investors who engage in certain digital asset transactions."[1]

The suggestion that the White House would work with Congress on future digital-asset legislation stood in contrast to previous statements from [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] Chair [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]], who has repeatedly said he does not believe the industry needs additional crypto-specific legislation.[1]

## Political context

The softened stance came days after the [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign announced it would begin accepting cryptocurrency donations through Coinbase Commerce and vowed to build a "crypto army."[1] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the pairing of headlines as an example of Trump puppeteering not only the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] but the Biden administration itself, arguing that after Trump came out in favor of broader crypto acceptance the administration and its media allies were forced to capitulate just days after threatening to bring the hammer down on the industry.[1]
