# PROMIS software

PROMIS is a case-management and database program that, in the account advanced by Badlands writers, was covertly modified with a hidden backdoor and distributed internationally as an intelligence-collection instrument.[1]

## Distribution through Robert Maxwell

Citing research by the author Gordon Thomas, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]] — father of [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] — was dispatched by [[mossad|Mossad]] to sell PROMIS to entities across the world, including the Swiss bank [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]].[1]

## Claimed intelligence value

Because the distributed copies were equipped with a secret backdoor, PROMIS is said to have allowed Mossad to monitor the activities of all governments running it.[1] Beyond governmental surveillance, the same access allegedly permitted tracking of money-laundering practices by organized-crime syndicates moving funds through the banking system, as well as arms deals conducted through weapons manufacturers.[1]

It was through this surveillance, the account continues, that Mossad learned the [[cia|CIA]] had been using Credit Suisse to launder revenue from off-the-book endeavors — knowledge that Robert Maxwell is said to have exploited to become the most prolific money launderer of the late 1980s.[1]

## Origins and the trapdoor scheme

PROMIS - an acronym for Prosecutor's Information Management System - was developed by [[inslaw|Inslaw]], which leased the software to the U.S. Justice Department in 1982 under Attorney General [[edwin-meese-iii|Edwin Meese III]].[2] The software enabled information analysis and the integration of disparate databases on an unprecedented magnitude.[2] It caught the eye of [[rafi-eitan|Rafi Eitan]], then head of [[lekem|Lekem]], who was introduced to it by the former CIA covert operations officer [[earl-brian|Earl Brian]].[2] Brian and Eitan devised the plan to install a "trapdoor" or backdoor and sell the product globally, giving Israel and allied elements of the U.S. security state a digital peephole into the affairs of enemies and allies while generating enormous profits; Badlands notes that such access could also, hypothetically, help those holding it circumvent authorities investigating financial crimes.[2]

## Distribution through Robert Maxwell

The first attempt failed, with the Eitan-Brian-Meese collaboration leading to Inslaw's bankruptcy.[2] Eitan then recruited [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]], who marketed PROMIS through the computer firm [[degem-systems|Degem Systems]] and other Maxwell-owned companies in Israel and abroad.[2] The effort gave Israeli intelligence access to the innermost workings of countless governments, corporations, banks and intelligence services worldwide; the software was also sold to the military dictatorships of Chile and Argentina, where it was used to facilitate the mass murder of dissidents.[2] Eitan then tasked Maxwell with placing PROMIS in the U.S. laboratories of the Los Alamos complex, including [[sandia-national-laboratories|Sandia National Laboratory]], in 1984, concurrent with Eitan's handling of the spy [[jonathan-pollard|Jonathan Pollard]].[2] Badlands describes the whole affair as one of the most brazen and successful operations ever conducted by Israeli intelligence.[2]
