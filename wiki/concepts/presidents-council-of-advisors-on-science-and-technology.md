# President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology

The President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST) is a federal advisory body that provides the President of the United States with independent advice on science, technology, and innovation policy. Its membership is typically drawn from academia, industry, and research institutions, blending public- and private-sector expertise, and it operates within the framework of the [[federal-advisory-committee-act|Federal Advisory Committee Act]], meaning its work is structured to produce formal recommendations to the president.

## March 2026 appointments

The [[white-house|White House]] announced a slate of appointments to PCAST in March 2026, reported in the March 26 Badlands Brief. Appointees were tasked with advising on artificial intelligence, national security technology, economic competitiveness, and scientific research priorities, with the announcement framing the council's role as guiding policy on emerging technologies and maintaining US leadership in science and innovation.

The named appointees were [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]], [[sergey-brin|Sergey Brin]], [[safra-catz|Safra Catz]], [[michael-dell|Michael Dell]], [[jacob-dewitte|Jacob DeWitte]], [[fred-ehrsam|Fred Ehrsam]], [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], [[david-friedberg|David Friedberg]], [[jensen-huang|Jensen Huang]], [[john-martinis|John Martinis]], [[bob-mumgaard|Bob Mumgaard]], [[lisa-su|Lisa Su]], and [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].

## Reception in Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the roster as roughly half figures associated with [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]]'s network — naming Zuckerberg, Andreessen, Ehrsam, Ellison, Friedberg, and Huang — and half not. While distancing herself from the "Thiel Doomer" thesis that Thiel is "shadow running our government through his vast network of insider placements," she raised pointed concerns about Zuckerberg's inclusion given the history of the [[center-for-tech-and-civic-life|Center for Tech and Civic Life]], writing that "public trust is conditioned on accountability." She described the announcement as resembling "the seeming celebration of the birth of Westworld, and its infamous board of governors," and argued that if power is genuinely returned to the People, it falls to the People to refuse technocratic overreach.
