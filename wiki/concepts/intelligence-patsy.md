# Intelligence Patsy

"Intelligence patsy" is the term used in the Badlands Media essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI" for an individual publicly blamed for an act of political violence who, in the essay's account, was in fact recruited, manipulated or manufactured by the agencies that later accuse him.[1]

## The defining common feature

The essay poses its own question — what do most [[cia|CIA]] and [[fbi|FBI]] patsies have in common? — and answers that most of them think they are actually working for or with the CIA or FBI.[1] They are not randomly blamed by the agencies; most of them have a history with them.[1] A second common feature is added later: most also have alleged ties to CIA mind control through the [[mk-ultra|MK ULTRA]] program, described as using LSD along with physical and psychological torture to break people's minds and reprogram them as assets.[1]

## The roster and its taxonomy

The article names [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]], [[jack-ruby|Jack Ruby]], [[sirhan-sirhan|Sirhan Sirhan]], [[charles-manson|Charlie Manson]], [[jim-jones|Jim Jones]], [[john-hinckley-jr|John Hinckley Jr.]], [[mark-david-chapman|Mark Chapman]], [[david-koresh|David Koresh]], [[timothy-mcveigh|Timothy McVeigh]] and [[john-salvi|John Salvi]] as suspected MK ULTRA participants, and sorts them by function.[1] Oswald, Ruby, Sirhan and Hinckley were used to eliminate — or try to eliminate — people in power who opposed the CIA.[1] Manson and Jones were used to create fear and race wars.[1] Chapman took out a man who was influential and vocal against the CIA agenda.[1] Koresh and McVeigh were used to make anti-government groups look violent and crazy.[1] Salvi was used to make pro-life supporters seem like violent extremists.[1] [[james-earl-ray|James Earl Ray]] is treated separately, as a patsy established by a jury verdict.[1]

## The infiltration mechanism

Beneath the individual cases the article describes a standing method. When a group of people who are anti-government, or for limited government, or who simply want government held accountable begins to form, the agencies infiltrate it and try to turn it into a hate group, or at least make it appear to be one.[1] In some cases they do not infiltrate but start such groups themselves, to create the appearance that anti-government people are violent and crazy.[1] [[ruby-ridge|Ruby Ridge]] — where FBI informants are said to have pushed race hate at church picnics, to [[randy-weaver|Randy Weaver]]'s incredulity — and the [[waco-siege|Waco siege]] are the two cases offered as illustration.[1] The stated objective is for the public to equate anti-government sentiment with hate groups.[1]

## Application to January 6

The essay applies the concept to [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6]], arguing that the release of Capitol footage on [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s program showed that government agencies possibly coordinated the whole thing as a staged show to make [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his supporters seem like violent nut jobs.[1] [[jacob-chansley|Jacob Angeli]], the QAnon Shaman, is the central case: the author says he does not know whether Angeli worked with the agencies, was set up as their patsy, or was simply an attention seeker taken advantage of.[1] [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]] is named as someone who should be exposed as a CIA/FBI stooge.[1]

## Wider claims

The article argues that brainwashing does not occur only in laboratories with LSD and torture but reaches the masses through propaganda in mainstream media, with the CIA vital to the manipulation of Americans' minds through created narratives and agendas.[1] It states that the two biggest and most impactful CIA and FBI activities are trafficking — drug and human — and the overall brainwashing of a nation.[1] It concludes that it would be a shorter list to ask what the CIA has not been involved in, from political assassinations to government overthrows, wars and mass-casualty events.[1]
