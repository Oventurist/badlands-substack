# Defence Scheme No. 1

Defence Scheme No. 1 was a military plan drafted by the British Empire in 1921 providing for an invasion of the United States from Canada in the event of a future war between the two English-speaking powers.[1]

## Relationship to War Plan Red

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] uses Defence Scheme 1 to correct what he regards as a widespread historical misreading. Many historians cite the American [[war-plan-red|War Plan Red]] as evidence that the United States has harboured aggressive imperial ambitions to take over Canada for nearly a century.[1] In fact, Ehret argues, War Plan Red was drafted as a RESPONSE to the already-existing Defence Scheme 1.[1] The sequencing matters to his broader thesis: American contingency planning against Britain was defensive and reactive, not expansionist.[1]

## Wider scope

Although typically treated as a narrow Canada-versus-US war plan, Defence Scheme 1 is described as part of a much larger British program that also involved Japanese attacks on the American West Coast and British-directed incursions into the United States via Mexico from the south.[1]

## Wartime echo

Ehret contends that after British plans to support the Nazi project suffered setbacks — the ousting of the pro-Nazi king [[edward-viii|Edward VIII]] and then of pro-Nazi prime minister Neville Chamberlain — the invasion concept outlined in Defence Scheme 1 was modified only slightly, with the Germans and Japanese colluding on plans one through six to invade the United States.[1] These were set out in the March 2, 1942 edition of Life Magazine, from which plans 2, 4, and 6 are reproduced in the article.[1]
