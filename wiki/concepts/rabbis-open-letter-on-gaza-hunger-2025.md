# Rabbis' Open Letter on Gaza Hunger (2025)

In late July 2025 more than 1,000 rabbis from around the world signed an open letter accusing [[israel|Israel]] of weaponizing hunger in [[gaza|Gaza]] and urging West Jerusalem to allow humanitarian aid into the enclave.[1]

## The letter

Rabbis and Jewish scholars from the United States, United Kingdom, European Union and Israel signed the letter, which declared that the Jewish people "face a grave moral crisis."[1] It stated that "the severe limitation placed on humanitarian relief in Gaza, and the policy of withholding food, water, and medical supplies from a needy civilian population, contradict essential values of Judaism as we understand it."[1]

The appeal came against a backdrop in which, according to [[unrwa|UN Palestinian refugee agency]] chief Philippe Lazzarini, around 90,000 women and children were suffering from malnutrition in what relief groups described as a man-made famine caused by the Israeli blockade.[1]

## Trump's response and Israeli politics

On Sunday, July 27, 2025, while on a four-day visit to Scotland, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said that "Israel will have to make a decision" on Gaza, even as Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] denied that Jerusalem bore any responsibility for the widespread aid shortages.[1] Asked about images of starving children emerging from Gaza, Trump called them "terrible," but promptly pivoted to say "They're stealing the food," without specifying whom he meant — likely an allusion to [[hamas|Hamas]], which Israel has regularly accused of diverting aid.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that as the political landscape in Israel deteriorates, the narrative shielding via religion that has long protected it from objective criticism dissolves along with it.[1] He suggested Trump was buying Netanyahu as much time as possible while the government descended further into "irrational hysteria," pointing to National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] calling Netanyahu "morally bankrupt" for allowing food into Gaza and insisting they should only be "sending shells" — "to bomb, to conquer, to encourage emigration, and win the war."[1] He concluded that such rhetoric does not sound like interest in a two-state solution.[1]
