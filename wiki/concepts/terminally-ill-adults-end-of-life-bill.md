# Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill

The Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill is proposed British legislation that would legalise assisted suicide in England and Wales.[1] Introduced as a Private Members' Bill, it has passed key parliamentary votes in 2024 and 2025 and, if enacted, would permit assisted suicide for terminally ill adults expected to live six months or less, subject to court approval.[1]

## Legal background

Euthanasia remains illegal throughout the United Kingdom under the Suicide Act 1961, which carries a maximum prison sentence of 14 years.[1] The bill would create a statutory exception to that regime for a defined class of terminally ill adults.[1]

## Pregnancy exclusion debate

During committee stage debates in the House of Lords in December 2025, peers conducted detailed scrutiny of amendments to the bill, including how it should treat pregnant women.[1] Lord [[charles-falconer|Charles Falconer]], a [[labour-party|Labour]] peer and former Secretary of State for Justice, argued that pregnancy should not be a bar to accessing assisted dying, despite the unborn child also being killed by the process.[1] Comparative approaches were raised in debate: Oregon requires efforts to keep the mother alive if the unborn child is "viable," while the Netherlands allows for aborting babies before euthanising their mothers.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because pregnancy-related hormonal fluctuations can cause suicidal ideation, extending euthanasia to pregnant women inverts the duty of care owed to vulnerable mothers, and characterised the movement behind the bill as a "death cult" that should be removed from power.[1]
