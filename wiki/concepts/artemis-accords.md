# Artemis Accords

The Artemis Accords are a framework announced on May 15, 2020 committing signatories not only to return humans to the Moon for the first time since 1973 but to permanently develop a Lunar and Mars-based economy with a focus on international cooperation.[1]

## Context

In Badlands Media commentary the Accords are treated as one component of a larger shift toward [[open-systems-vs-closed-systems|open-system]] thinking in space policy, alongside America's successful return to manned spaceflight on May 28, 2020.[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents space diplomacy as one of the most fruitful arenas for [[win-win-cooperation|win-win cooperation]] among the nations of the UN Security Council during a period of intense crisis.[1]

## Parallel programs

The Accords' outlook is described as dovetailing with Russia's commitment to permanent lunar colonization and resource development, which began with Luna 25 in 2021 and was to be followed by Luna 26, 27 and 28, with a plan for a permanent manned base alongside China in the early 2030s.[1] China, though banned from the [[international-space-station|ISS]] and U.S. cooperation since 2011, has become a pioneer in space, signing a lunar cooperation alliance with Russia in September 2019; its Chang'e program landed on the far side of the Moon and envisages colonization and Helium-3 mining for [[fusion-power|fusion power]].[1]
