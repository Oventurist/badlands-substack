# Artemis Accords

The Artemis Accords are a framework announced on May 15, 2020 committing signatories not only to return humans to the Moon for the first time since 1973 but to permanently develop a Lunar and Mars-based economy with a focus on international cooperation.[1]

## Context

In Badlands Media commentary the Accords are treated as one component of a larger shift toward [[open-systems-vs-closed-systems|open-system]] thinking in space policy, alongside America's successful return to manned spaceflight on May 28, 2020.[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents space diplomacy as one of the most fruitful arenas for [[win-win-cooperation|win-win cooperation]] among the nations of the UN Security Council during a period of intense crisis.[1]

## Parallel programs

The Accords' outlook is described as dovetailing with Russia's commitment to permanent lunar colonization and resource development, which began with Luna 25 in 2021 and was to be followed by Luna 26, 27 and 28, with a plan for a permanent manned base alongside China in the early 2030s.[1] China, though banned from the [[international-space-station|ISS]] and U.S. cooperation since 2011, has become a pioneer in space, signing a lunar cooperation alliance with Russia in September 2019; its Chang'e program landed on the far side of the Moon and envisages colonization and Helium-3 mining for [[fusion-power|fusion power]].[1]

## Origins and structure

The Artemis Accords were first unveiled in 2019 as an effort to put substance onto the bones of the 1967 [[outer-space-treaty|Outer Space Treaty]], which had been signed by 109 UN members but, in Matthew Ehret's judgment, never evolved beyond the vaguest of generalities.[2] The accords form part of the broader Artemis Program signed into law by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2017, whose preamble stated that "while [[nasa|NASA]] is leading the Artemis program, international partnerships will play a key role in achieving a sustainable and robust presence on the Moon while preparing to conduct a historic mission to Mars."[2] NASA administrator [[jim-bridenstine|Jim Bridenstine]] announced them by declaring, "It's a new dawn for space exploration!… We go, together!"[2]

The ten accords deal with: the primacy of peaceful purposes; transparency; interoperability of technologies; emergency assistance between nations; registration of space objects; release and sharing of scientific data among nations; protecting heritage sites such as the Apollo 11 landing site; the development of space resources within the lunar regolith; de-confliction of activities; and orbital debris and spacecraft disposal.[2]

They followed an April 6, 2020 executive order signed by Trump calling for an accelerated push toward lunar, Mars and asteroid resource development; three days later the first of three phone calls between Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] took place, including lengthy discussions on space cooperation, and twelve days after the last call the two presidents published a Joint Declaration on the 75th Anniversary of the Meeting on the Elbe on April 25.[2]

## Russian participation and its collapse

Ehret argues that, contrary to Western mainstream media narratives, Russia was not excluded but invited, and had already begun cooperating on Artemis through the [[lunar-gateway-project|Lunar Gateway Project]].[2] NASA spokesman Michael Gold complained that "there were a lot of media leaks that did not properly describe what the Artemis Accords were," adding that he expected a great deal of Russian cooperation on Artemis, likely centred on the gateway.[2] One day after the May 15, 2020 accord, [[roscosmos|Roscosmos]] international cooperation deputy director general Sergei Savalyev said that "ambitious projects connected to Moon colonization could become a serious factor for the interaction of the two countries in difficult times."[2] Ehret contends that the original design of the accords was abandoned under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], after which Russia left the gateway project and turned to China, and that this design must be revived if the United States is to survive what he calls the coming storm.[2]
