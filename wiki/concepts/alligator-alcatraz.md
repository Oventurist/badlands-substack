# Alligator Alcatraz

"Alligator Alcatraz" is the nickname given to a proposed immigrant detention facility in the Florida Everglades, floated in June 2025 by Florida Attorney General [[james-uthmeier|James Uthmeier]] as a way to assist [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] with mass deportation efforts.[1]

## Proposal

Uthmeier, a Republican, posted the idea to X, formerly Twitter, dubbing the proposed facility "Alligator Alcatraz" because it would be sited in the Everglades, which is home to more than 200,000 alligators.[1] Newsweek reported that it had reached out to Uthmeier's office and to ICE for comment.[1] The name evokes the former federal island prison at Alcatraz, whose isolation was reinforced by the surrounding waters — in this case, the natural hazards of the swamp substituting for the bay.[1]

## Political context

The proposal intertwines with the politically divisive issue of immigration and the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's continued efforts to mass deport immigrants and prevent entry for some others.[1] Critics of the administration have protested ICE raids, while supporters view the enforcement effort as necessary to keep the country secure.[1] Polling has consistently shown that immigration remains one of Trump's strongest issues with voters, despite significant backlash from critics.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] joked that the proposal resembled a border-security design concept he had emailed to the Trump White House in 2017 — conceding differences between his idea (a wall, with sharks) and the Everglades detention center (a prison, with alligators) — while praising "Alligator Alcatraz" as a "next-level name."[1]
