# El Paso Cartel Drone Incursion (2026)

The El Paso cartel drone incursion was a February 2026 incident in which drones linked to Mexican criminal organizations entered United States airspace near El Paso, Texas, prompting an emergency airspace closure and a joint federal counter-drone response.[1]

## Airspace closure

The [[federal-aviation-administration|FAA]] issued a surprise notice shutting down the airspace above El Paso, halting all flights up to 18,000 feet for ten days, citing "special security reasons."[1] The restricted zone covered a ten-mile radius around the west Texas border city.[1] The restrictions were lifted Wednesday night, far earlier than the notice indicated.[1]

## Federal response

The Trump Administration confirmed that cartel drones had entered US airspace and had been disabled by the FAA and the [[department-of-war|Department of War]].[1] Transportation Secretary [[sean-duffy|Sean Duffy]] posted on X that the agencies "acted swiftly to address a cartel drone incursion," that "the threat has been neutralized," and that there was no danger to commercial travel in the region.[1] *Business Insider*, citing anonymous officials, reported that counter-drone measures were used; the exact technology was not publicly specified.[1]

## Disputed accounts

Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] rejected the reports outright, saying "There is no information about the use of drones on the border."[1] Representative Tony Gonzales, by contrast, treated the event as routine, saying daily drone incursions by criminal organizations are ordinary life on the border — "It's a Wednesday for us."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] connected the incident to his prior speculation that the large military buildup in the eastern Mediterranean might be a head-fake concealing a Western Hemisphere focus.[1] He noted that Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] had recently visited Washington — and nearly been assassinated on the way home — while vowing that he and Trump were working together to bring down cartels and the bankers who finance them.[1] Citing a question raised by Eric Rice — "Who sold Mexico the drones?" — he pointed to a 2015 article on drone sales to the Mexican government, conceding it concerned the government rather than the cartels while asking whether there is an actual difference.[1] He concluded that Mexico appears to be the last bastion of the Hispanic narcotics cartels and that Trump has signaled he considers the Mexican government to be in bed with them and therefore a threat, characterizing the drone activity itself as "definitely an invasion."[1]
