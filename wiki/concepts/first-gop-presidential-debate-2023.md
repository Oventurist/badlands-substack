# First GOP Presidential Debate 2023

The first Republican presidential primary debate of the 2024 cycle was scheduled for August 23, 2023, in Milwaukee, hosted by [[fox-news|Fox News]] and beginning at 9 p.m. ET.[1]

## Outcome and viewership

The debate went ahead on August 23, 2023, without Trump, who instead sat for a pre-recorded interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] released on X the same evening.[2] News media reported that an "unprecedented" 24 million people watched the debate, against a prior record for a primary debate of 19.7 million set by an MSNBC-hosted Democratic primary debate in 2020, according to AdWeek.[2] By comparison, the [[donald-trump|Trump]]–Carlson interview passed 200 million views before noon the following day, less than 24 hours after airing — a margin over Carlson's previous record-holding interview with [[andrew-tate|Andrew Tate]] that alone exceeded the debate's entire audience.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the contrast was decisive: "Trump was Presidential. The GOP was hysterical and visionless," and she expected his polling margin to widen further.[2] She predicted small "normie bumps" for [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], [[chris-christie|Chris Christie]] and [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] while judging the field as a whole irrelevant, concluding that "The Grand Ol' Party is dead. America First is now the party."[2] She identified the other winner of the night as the roughly 4,000-person live audience, which she said responded with overwhelming support for Trump despite his absence from the stage, and the biggest loser as [[fox-news|Fox]]: "Mainstream media is dead. New media is now mainstream."[2]

A companion essay published by [[american-greatness|American Greatness]] and republished by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized the Milwaukee event as a "sideshow" that would be forgotten if Trump's mugshot were taken, ranking [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] a "dud" and Ramaswamy the runner-up of the evening.[2]

## Trump's posture

Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who led the GOP primary field, had not decided by late July whether he would participate.[1] On Monday, July 31, 2023, he teased on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that the debate might help him narrow his choices for a running mate, posting: "Let them debate so I can see who I MIGHT consider for Vice President!"[1] His stance had not changed much by Tuesday, August 1.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump's public communications, however crass or spontaneous they appeared, reflected a major paradigm shift that had been building slowly and then arrived all at once.[1] By turning the GOP debates into a meme he wielded full control over, Burning Bright wrote, Trump simultaneously elevated himself and the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]] while denigrating the GOP, the two-party system, and "the Establishment clown show American politics has become" — an outcome the contributor judged "a good thing."[1]
