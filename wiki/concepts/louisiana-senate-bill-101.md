# Louisiana Senate Bill 101 (2024)

Senate Bill 101 is Louisiana legislation prohibiting the use of [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked-choice voting]] in state elections, advanced by the Republican-controlled Louisiana Senate on Wednesday, March 20, 2024.[1]

## Text and scope

The bill stipulates that a "ranked-choice voting or instant runoff voting method … shall not be used in determining the election or nomination of any candidate to any local, state, or federal elective office in this state."[1] It carves out an exception permitting the system for absentee voting by military and overseas voters under the Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act.[1]

## Passage

SB 101 cleared the Senate on a 29–9 vote, with two Democrats joining 27 Republicans in voting "yea."[1] All "nay" votes came from Democrats, and Republican Senator Michael Fesi was absent.[1] The House was set to consider the measure next.[1]

## Debate

Pressed by Democratic Senator Edward Price on the need to ban RCV given that no Louisiana locality currently uses it, bill sponsor and Republican Senator Blake Miguez stressed the importance of preventing the system from being adopted in the future and disenfranchising Louisiana voters.[1] "We are trying to ensure that ranked-choice voting doesn't become a reality here in Louisiana," Miguez said. "America is about … one person, one vote, and to have these complex tabulated ballots is un-American, and I think both sides of the aisle understand this. And that's why we don't want it here in Louisiana."[1]

Miguez is a member of the Louisiana Freedom Caucus and previously sponsored the state's enacted constitutional amendment banning "Zuckbucks" in elections.[1] The bill's progress was reported by [[the-federalist|The Federalist]].[1]
