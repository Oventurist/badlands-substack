# Gender Dysphoria

Gender dysphoria is a clinical term for a deep sense of discomfort with one's body and biological sex.[1] Its treatment in children became one of the most contested questions in Western medicine during the early 2020s, particularly the choice between therapy-focused approaches and the affirmation model paired with [[puberty-blockers|puberty blockers]].[1]

## Treatment models

Early pediatric gender clinics, including the one founded by Canadian psychiatrist [[susan-bradley|Susan Bradley]] in 1975, took a therapy-focused approach; under that model most patients reportedly outgrew their feelings of being transgender over time.[1] Around 2000, prescribing puberty blockers to gender-dysphoric children as a way of alleviating distress began at Bradley's clinic, and the practice subsequently became widely adopted by medical establishments around the world, including in the United States.[1]

## Criticism and reassessment

In March 2023, Bradley publicly reversed her position, saying the field had been wrong and expressing regret over her clinic's role in administering puberty blockers.[1] She argued that blockers can cement a child's confusion out of which they would otherwise likely grow, and raised concerns about drug side effects.[1] Commentary from [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] framed pediatric gender medicine as an irreversible set of interventions performed without adequate informed consent, and predicted rising parental resistance.[1]
