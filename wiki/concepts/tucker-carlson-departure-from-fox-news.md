# Tucker Carlson Departure from Fox News

The departure of [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] from [[fox-news|Fox News]] on Monday, April 24, 2023, ended the tenure of the network's highest-rated and most popular host and touched off intense speculation about the reason for the split, which remained unexplained publicly.[1] The network announced only that it and Carlson had "agreed to part ways."[1]

## Market fallout

[[fox-corporation|Fox Corporation]] shares dropped to a low of $29.27 on the day of the announcement, a 5.2 percent decline representing more than $800 million in lost market value, before partially rebounding.[1] The stock slipped again Tuesday and recovered slightly Wednesday, trading near $29.88 against a Monday peak of $30.98.[1] The losses followed by days Fox's $787 million defamation settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], reached on the eve of trial.[1]

## Psychological-operations interpretation

Former U.S. Army psychological warfare officer [[scott-bennett|Scott Bennett]] argued that Carlson was fired to preserve an "uninformed semi lobotomized quasi retarded population," saying the host had turned his audience into "researchers and thinkers" and therefore threatened the "powers and principalities, institutions and agendas" that depend on an unquestioning public.[1] Bennett credited Carlson with an intellectualism and analytical depth he said no other American news personality had matched.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] read the episode as confirmation that Fox's priority was narrative rather than profit, and linked the Dominion settlement to overlapping [[blackrock|BlackRock]] holdings in both companies, describing the payout as Kabuki theater intended to certify the 2020 election as fair.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that although he had long viewed Carlson skeptically as part of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]], the host's segments had grown increasingly poignant and anti-establishment, pushing the [[overton-window|Overton window]] toward truth and justice — and that the firing, alongside CNN's ratings decline, left alternative media more comfortable than the legacy outlets.[1]
