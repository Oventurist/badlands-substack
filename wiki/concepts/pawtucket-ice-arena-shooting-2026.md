# Pawtucket Ice Arena Shooting (2026)

The Pawtucket ice arena shooting was a murder-suicide committed on Monday afternoon, February 16, 2026, inside the Dennis M. Lynch Arena in Pawtucket, Rhode Island, during a crowded Senior Night high school hockey game.[1]

## The attack

A 56-year-old man opened fire inside the arena, killing his ex-wife and one of their adult sons before taking his own life.[1] The attack occurred while the family was watching another son compete on the ice.[1] The shooter, Robert Dorgan — who also went by Roberta Esposito and had undergone gender reassignment surgery several years earlier — carried out the targeted shooting while dressed in women's clothing.[1] He fatally shot his ex-wife Rhonda Dorgan, 52, and 23-year-old son Aidan Dorgan.[1] Three other relatives, including Rhonda's parents Linda and Gerald Dorgan and a family friend, Thomas Geruso, were critically wounded and remained hospitalized at Rhode Island Hospital in Providence as of a 4:30 p.m. press conference the following Tuesday.[1] Rhonda died of gunshot injuries at the scene, while Aidan later died at Rhode Island Hospital.[1]

A bystander intervened and initially disarmed the shooter, but he produced a second firearm and killed himself.[1]

## Police account

Police described the incident as domestic-related.[1] Family members noted the shooter's history of mental health struggles and long-standing disputes tied to his transition, which contributed to the couple's divorce around 2020.[1] The Pawtucket police chief asked publicly for respect for the family in the aftermath.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America described the story as particularly gruesome even by the standards of mass shootings, emphasizing that the surviving son who was playing in the game will carry for life the knowledge that his father publicly murdered his mother and brother at his own hockey game.[1] She declined to name the shooter on the grounds that he wanted to be remembered and that terrorists should never be given what they want.[1] She characterized the event as the second act of trans-involved terrorism that week, asked how many people must die "before we reopen the asylums," and anticipated that the response from the political mainstream would consist of calls for disarmament and for greater social acceptance rather than scrutiny of mental health policy.[1]
