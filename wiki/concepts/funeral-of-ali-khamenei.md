# Funeral of Ali Khamenei

The funeral of Iranian Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] was a multi-city mourning event that began in Tehran in early July 2026 and concluded with burial in Mashhad, and which became the focus of both U.S.–Iran diplomacy and violent online rhetoric documented in the July 6, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]].[1]

## The procession

[[iran|Iran]] began the funeral procession on a Monday, with ceremonies moving through Tehran before continuing later in the week in other cities and religious centers.[1] Arab News, citing AP and AFP reporting, said Khamenei's coffin and those of family members killed in a February 28 airstrike were carried through the capital toward Mehrabad International Airport, and that thousands gathered at Tehran's Grand Mosalla the preceding Sunday to pay respects.[1] Funeral events were scheduled to continue in Qom and in Iraq's holy cities of Najaf and Karbala, concluding with burial in Mashhad.[1] Iranian authorities presented the ceremonies as a major national event and implemented crowd-control measures during the mourning period.[1]

## U.S. and Israeli responses

[[donald-trump|Trump]] told Axios he was monitoring developments around the funeral and said U.S.–Iran talks would pause for about a week, with neither side engaging in hostilities during that period.[1] "They are all there. One shot [and we can take them all out], but we are not going to do that because then we would have nobody to negotiate with," Trump said.[1] He added that he was surprised to see some Iranians crying at the funeral, saying he thought people hated Khamenei, and said of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], "We get along very good. He knows who the boss is."[1]

RT reported that Ali Abdollahi, commander of Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, warned the United States and [[israel|Israel]] against any attacks during the ceremonies, and that Israeli Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] had said [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] was "marked for death."[1] Far-right activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] posted on July 2 that the funeral was a "target rich environment," adding a bomb emoji.[1]

Attendance included hundreds of world leaders and diplomats; the Brief noted that [[saudi-arabia|the Saudis]] — historic rivals of Iran who had not formally announced plans to attend — surprised observers by showing up to pay respects.[1]

## Badlands framing

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that granting an enemy "a week off" from war to mourn the very leader one purportedly killed in an airstrike signals that the conflict "isn't what it seems," particularly for an administration that champions total war.[1] The burial falling on the Fourth of July was flagged as symbolism, and Trump's remark to Barak Ravid of Axios about crying Iranians was read as a deliberate use of the Streisand Effect to draw attention to the possibility that Western audiences have been misled about Khamenei's relationship with the Iranian people.[1]

The commentary restated the Badlands position that the Ayatollah died years earlier of natural causes and that the successor figure is not a real person, with the martyrdom story serving as a face-saving exercise allowing Iranian leadership to end its theocratic age; it referenced a [[continuity-of-government|continuity of government]] protocol implemented the previous August that removed the Ayatollah from the chain of command and effectively dissolved the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] over concerns of [[mossad|Mossad]] infiltration and an Israeli decapitation attempt.[1] The section closed with the assertion that "world peace is coming," but that some will do everything in their power to stop it.[1]
