# 2020 Election Fraud Claims

The claim that the 2020 United States presidential election was stolen is the factual predicate underlying both criminal prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arising from the post-election period.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) Badlands Media commentary inverts the usual framing: rather than the claims being disproven in court, they argue the claims have never actually been adjudicated.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## The standing problem

Of 55 cases filed in relation to the 2020 election, 50 were thrown out on standing rather than on the merits.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) Standing and jurisdiction — territorial, personal, and subject-matter — must be established before a court may hear evidence or testimony at all.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The article argues that commentators who say "there's no evidence because the courts threw out the cases" misunderstand procedure, comparing them to someone commenting on a chess tournament blindfolded.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The author further suggests, without proof, that some dismissals may reflect corruption or compromise operating in the background.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Criminalizing disagreement

Because the indictments rest on the allegation that Trump lied about the election, the article contends that "the very act of disagreeing with the mainstream is being criminalized."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) Drawing an analogy to defamation law — where an untrue statement made in sincere belief is not actionable — it argues that error is not a crime, that disagreeing with advisors is not a crime, and that attempting to stop a perceived theft is not a crime.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) If Trump believed in good faith the election was stolen, his efforts to prevent it were consistent with his presidential oath.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Discovery as a vehicle

As a criminal defendant, Trump gains discovery powers and, the article argues, must under due process be given the opportunity to prove his statements true.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) This would put election-fraud evidence onto the court record for the first time.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) Should the state courts mishandle the cases, the author speculates the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] might weigh in — an outcome that "could open a massive can of worms for the Biden administration."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The [[deep-state|Deep State]], on this reading, has the strongest possible interest in preventing exactly that.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## DOJ investigations confirmed (May 2026)

In May 2026 Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] publicly stated that "there's a ton of evidence that the election was rigged" in 2020 and confirmed multiple ongoing [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] investigations in Arizona and in Fulton County, Georgia.[2] Blanche described the inquiry as focused on "whether the right people voted, whether people who were supposed to vote voted, whether there was one vote cast per voter" in multiple states, and said the subjects were "very good at hiding misconduct."[2] He attributed the slow pace to conventional police work and said the Department had "great prosecutors" assigned, promising that the public would learn the results "whether it's charges, whether it's a report, whether it's the results of an investigation."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Blanche's phrasing suggested a focus on voter fraud rather than systemic election fraud, but argued that in Arizona and Georgia the individual bad-actor and systemic-design dimensions are inextricably connected, and that evidence of the sort Blanche described had been public for years.[2] She characterized the pace as indicating a justice phase while warning that a report issued without charges "would be quite the rugpull."[2]

## Republican messaging split, March 2026

In March 2026, [[politico|Politico]] reported that [[republican-party|Republican]] strategists and officials were divided over whether President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should keep emphasizing claims about the 2020 election or shift messaging toward current policy issues and future elections.[3] Multiple officials and strategists said continuing to focus on election fraud in 2020 and beyond could distract from policy issues ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterm elections]], while others remained supportive of Trump raising election integrity concerns and viewed the issue as important to many Republican voters.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the pivot argument outright, satirizing it as "Stop saying true things! We're gonna lose the next fake election!" and insisting that "no one wins a fake election."[3] She noted that the same election systems and processes are currently being exposed in Georgia and Arizona, with investigations and legal action underway in a couple dozen more states, and argued that real elections require honesty about, and accountability for, the fake ones.[3] She also observed that Trump himself never stopped calling elections fake, questioning why some allies now insist the public pretend otherwise absent any change to the underlying machinery.[3]

## Trump's Davos prosecution announcement (January 2026)

Speaking at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos on January 21, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said of the Russia-Ukraine war that "it wouldn't have started if the 2020 US presidential election weren't rigged," adding: "It was a rigged election. Everybody now knows that. They found out. People will soon be prosecuted for what they did. It's probably breaking news, but it should be."[4] He tied the claim to a broader prescription of "strong borders, strong elections and ideally, a good press."[4]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the statement as genuine news and speculated about which individuals Trump had in mind, arguing that while much recent attention had gone to Venezuelans, the prosecution priority should be American — with foreign-directed conduct noted in charges such as treason — and that foreigners should be pursued as well but second.[4] Within that ordering she named the "[[colorado|Colorado]] Election Fraud Test Kitchen" and its collaborators as her top target, contending that the record accumulated across cases since 2020 should suffice to dismantle the entire corrupt ecosystem in the Centennial State, and that what is lacking is only "the spine and will."[4] She discussed the options with [[canncon|CannCon]] on Badlands Daily immediately following the speech.[4]

## White House declassification task force (2026)

In July 2026 a [[white-house|White House]] task force was reported to be gathering intelligence documents related to the 2020 presidential election for declassification from the [[cia|CIA]], the [[national-security-agency|NSA]], the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|ODNI]], the [[fbi|FBI]], and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], in addition to ongoing Justice Department probes.[5] The documents focus on ballot irregularities, potential voting machine issues, and possibly other topics, and members were encouraged to limit redactions even where that means retaining the names of government officials.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the anticipated disclosure as a check on whether the movement is "actually winning," arguing that there can be no Golden Age without a Justice Phase, that the core of any Justice Phase must be elections, and that real elections require accountability and honesty about "how fake they've been."[5] She added that a genuine investigative effort into 2020 would also yield disclosure on the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]], since the government was weaponized to prevent the truth about fake elections from emerging.[5]

## Wisconsin: the Milwaukee Central Count inquiry (2026)

In May 2026, Townhall — citing unnamed sources and the Wisconsin-based Heartland Post — reported that the [[fbi|FBI]] was preparing to interview Milwaukee police officers, as many as 30, as part of an investigation into the administration of the 2020 presidential election in Wisconsin.[6] The officers were described as potential witnesses rather than targets, and investigators were said to be examining whether police assigned to Milwaukee's [[milwaukee-central-count-facility|Central Count facility]] had been instructed to avoid certain areas while election operations were underway.[6] The reporting revisited a 2020 episode in which former [[milwaukee-election-commission|Milwaukee Election Commission]] director [[claire-woodall-vogg|Claire Woodall-Vogg]] said a flash drive of absentee ballot tabulation data had been left at Central Count before being retrieved and transported by police, and cited a sworn affidavit attributed to county election commission employee [[david-j-bolter|David J. Bolter]] describing a November 4, 2020 announcement about the arrival of additional ballots.[6] Milwaukee County Executive [[david-crowley|David Crowley]] and Mayor [[cavalier-johnson|Cavalier Johnson]] called the inquiry "an act of intimidation," while the report also referenced comments by FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] in an April Fox News interview about 2020 election allegations.[6]

Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] identified Woodall-Vogg as the official who delivered "just the margin needed at 3am" and referred to an alleged 4:30 a.m. infusion of 138,000 votes.[6] She argued that Woodall-Vogg and Ryan Chew — the latter affiliated with [[the-elections-group|The Elections Group]], as was [[ryan-macias|Ryan Macias]] in Georgia's 2020 recount — needed to be interrogated, and that the recurrence of the same "sources and methods and actors across states" was the pattern of interest.[6] Ashe observed that the disclosure operations and prosecutions were not restoring trust in elections but justifying distrust of them, with the "overwhelming evidence of 2020 election fraud" being dragged into the light and affirmed as real; she reasoned that if it were merely a rug-pull the only coherent goal would be civil unrest, which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had worked to avoid, and therefore hoped it presaged genuine honesty and accountability as a precondition for real elections.[6]

## The 2024 Heartland mail-in ballot study

In February 2024 the [[heartland-institute|Heartland Institute]] published a study concluding that fraudulent [[mail-in-voting|mail-in ballots]] "significantly" impacted the 2020 presidential election and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would "almost certainly" have been re-elected had the election been conducted primarily in person.[7] The study drew on a December Heartland/[[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] survey in which roughly one in five mail-in voters admitted to potentially fraudulent actions.[7] Its authors emphasized that the pandemic expansion of mail balloting was often implemented without legislative approval.[7]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized the framing as insufficient, arguing that attributing 2020 to individual voter fraud rather than systemic election fraud "is a distracting lie that ultimately serves the regime."[7] She noted the study's silence on algorithms, non-competitive technology contracts, certification gaps, the role of NGOs, voter roll inflation, and post-election purges of people who had voted — omissions she attributed to the fact that such issues cannot be explained without criminal intent.[7] Blaming anonymous voters, in her account, produces conclusions that are shocking yet unactionable, guaranteeing that no accountability follows.[7] She argued the system's centralization creates complexity, and that complexity "breeds and hides corruption," making the system designed for fraud.[7]

## Postal ballot transport allegations

One strand of the fraud claims concerns the alleged interstate movement of completed ballots by the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]].[8] The [[amistad-project|Amistad Project]] of the [[thomas-more-society|Thomas More Society]] presented sworn declarations claiming over 300,000 ballots at issue in Arizona, 548,000 in Michigan, 204,000 in Georgia and over 121,000 in Pennsylvania, and alleged multi-state illegal efforts by USPS workers to influence the outcome in at least three of six swing states.[8] Contract driver [[jesse-morgan|Jesse Morgan]] said a trailer of completed ballots he brought from New York vanished in Harrisburg, and attorney [[phill-kline|Phill Kline]] put the figure at 130,000 to 280,000 ballots shipped from Bethpage to Lancaster.[8] A [[united-states-postal-service|USPS]] report completed in June 2022 and briefly posted in December 2022 was said by Badlands commentators to confirm the interstate transport and to have been referred to federal prosecutors.[8]

## Supreme Court denial, February 2023

On February 21, 2023, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] again declined to hear a lawsuit alleging that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]], 291 House members and 94 senators had violated their oaths of office by refusing to investigate evidence of fraud before accepting the electoral votes on January 6, 2021.[9] The justices considered a petition to reconsider at a private conference and released the denial the following Tuesday; four votes are required to grant review.[9]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that courts had consistently declined to reach the evidence, frequently on standing grounds, and that [[congress|Congress]] had refused to conduct hearings even as ordinary citizens continued to surface material casting doubt on the result.[9] He argued that the recurring dismissals pointed to a problem larger than a rigged election — a system he described as at best defunct and at worst tyrannical — and warned that if the matter were allowed to fade, future generations "won't know enough to ask for the truth."[9]

## Trump's May 2025 restatement

Amid the release of the [[robert-hur|Hur]] audio in May 2025, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] declared on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that "the biggest crime of all is that THE 2020 PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION WAS RIGGED!"[10] Badlands writers treated the statement as vindication of their own long-running position and argued that celebrating the 2024 result while conceding the legitimacy of the 2020 count would "retroactively codif[y] the System's win in 2020."[10] Burning Bright argued that ceding the 2020 election to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had itself been part of a plan that accelerated public awakening while affording space for operations that would have been impossible with Trump publicly in power, while maintaining that the election was nonetheless fraudulent.[10]
