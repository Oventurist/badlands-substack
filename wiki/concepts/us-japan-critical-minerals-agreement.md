# US–Japan Critical Minerals Agreement

The US–Japan critical minerals agreement is a cooperation pact on producing and supplying critical and rare-earth minerals, signed by the United States and Japan on Tuesday, October 28, 2025.[1]

## Background

The agreement followed China's decision to tighten export controls on rare earths and chipmaking equipment in response to tariffs imposed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Signing

The deal was concluded during Trump's trip to Tokyo, where he met for the first time with Japan's new prime minister, [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]].[1] According to the [[white-house|White House]], the two sides agreed to boost joint projects "necessary to support domestic industries, including advanced technologies and their respective industrial bases," and to use "economic policy tools and coordinated investment to accelerate the development of diversified, liquid, and fair markets for critical minerals and rare earths."[1] The leaders also signed a document directing their governments to take further steps toward "a new golden age of the ever-growing US-Japan alliance."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary treated the deal as a major indicator of a deeper shift in the US–Japanese economic relationship, noting that Trump also referenced a significant military equipment arrangement that could signal an end to the post–World War II restriction on Japan maintaining its own military — which in turn would relieve the US Navy of its obligation to defend the Japanese islands.[1] The same commentary situated Takaichi as a political protégé of [[shinzo-abe|Shinzo Abe]] and contrasted her with predecessor [[shigeru-ishiba|Shigeru Ishiba]].[1]
