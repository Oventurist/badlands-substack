# 2023 DOJ Vaccine Injury Attorney Hiring

In November 2023, the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] posted a jobs advertisement seeking eight new attorneys to defend the federal government in vaccine injury cases, a move widely read as anticipating a surge of COVID-19 vaccine litigation.[1]

## The job posting

The advertisement, placed by the Torts Branch of the DOJ on the USAJobs website, stated that "the office is currently expanding to address workload created by an increase in cases filed under the Vaccine Act."[1] Commentary in the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] framed the hiring spree as preparation for lawsuits from people who were compelled by government mandates to receive the shot and who suffered serious side effects as a result, and who were now attempting to extract compensation "from a system that is stacked against them."[1]

## Related litigation

The recruitment drive followed a little-noticed lawsuit filed in Louisiana in October 2023 by six vaccine-injured plaintiffs against the federal government.[1] That suit aims to overturn the legal immunity that pharmaceutical manufacturers such as [[pfizer|Pfizer]] and [[moderna|Moderna]] enjoy in connection with their COVID-19 shots.[1] The item appeared as a bonus entry in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief of November 14, 2023, presented without contributor commentary.[1] The underlying compensation framework is the [[national-vaccine-injury-compensation-program|National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program]] and its associated statutory scheme.[1]
