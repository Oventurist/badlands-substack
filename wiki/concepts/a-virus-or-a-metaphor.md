# A Virus, or a Metaphor?

"A Virus, or a Metaphor? Decoding Donald Trump's COVID Comms" is an opinion essay published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on July 17, 2023 and written by contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]. The piece advances the interpretive claim that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] speaks in deliberate double meanings, and that his repeated public references to the "virus" during the COVID-19 period functioned as coded communication about [[communism|Communism]] spreading inside the United States. The article carries the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributor features represent the opinions of their authors and not necessarily the views of Badlands Media itself.

## Trump as a layered communicator

The article opens by describing what the author takes to be points of consensus within the MAGA movement: that Trump is highly intelligent, that his messages are frequently confusing, and that he likely loves his country more than any contemporary politician. Building on this, the source argues that many of Trump's statements carry multiple meanings aimed at distinct audiences. It identifies four such audiences: an audience that is "completely asleep, fully brainwashed"; a half-awake audience that senses something is wrong but is easily manipulated or discouraged; a fully awake audience that recognizes manipulation and avoids the "rabbit holes dug to distract the extremely motivated away from finding truth"; and, in many cases, a fourth audience consisting of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] itself.

## The case against Trump, as summarized by the source

The essay catalogues the objections raised by former or wavering supporters. Some, it says, find Trump exhausting and would prefer a less controversial figure who would not be attacked by the Deep State "24/7"; this group agrees with his policies but not with him as a communicator, believing he cannot control his tongue. Others withdrew support because they believe he "pushed the 'jab'" on Americans, because they consider him a poor judge of character given how many members of his inner circle appear to have "turned" on him, because they regard his ego as too large, or because they believe he is ultimately controlled by the same interests that control Liberals, RINOs and Neocons.

The author responds that these critics never answer two questions: why Trump is attacked so intensely if he is himself a member of the Deep State, and why he and his family would endure sustained abuse if he were not genuinely trying to dismantle what the article calls the Deep State [[cabal|Cabal]]. The piece notes that Trump is a billionaire married to a supermodel with a family that adores him, and therefore has no material need to spend "the twilight years of his life being abused from every angle." It observes that his net worth declined after becoming president, that the SEC took what the author calls the unprecedented step of holding up the merger between DWAC and Trump Media — restricting his ability to grow his media holdings and inject money into Truth Social — and that the mainstream media uniformly demonizes him. Allegations that Trump is controlled by the banks, Wall Street or Israel are rejected on the grounds that there is "literally no actual evidence of it."

## The vaccine question

The article treats the COVID 19 vaccines as the single most prominent reason former supporters cite for abandoning Trump. Its central defense is strategic: the author states his firm belief that Trump knew the Cabal's plan was to keep America and the world locked down for a far longer period — "maybe up to five years," or however long was necessary to destroy the middle class and create a two-tier society of working-class poor and elite, ending in a one-world totalitarian government.

On this reading, Trump forced Big Pharma to accelerate vaccine development because rapid availability was the only thing that could end the lockdowns of the COVID 19 pandemic before permanent social damage was done; otherwise, the author argues, drug makers would have spent years developing vaccines in order to keep everything shut down longer. Trump is described as having weighed potential vaccine harm against lockdown harm and concluded the lockdowns were significantly worse. The article stresses that Trump made clear he thought vaccines should be a choice rather than a requirement, and treats this as the decisive tell: "If Trump truly believed vaccines were effective and completely safe, he would have required they be taken by everyone. He didn't do this." It further argues that had Trump not appeared to embrace vaccines, the media would have blamed him for millions of deaths. The section closes with the maxim that in war "sometimes you can't avoid casualties; sometimes the best you can do is to minimize them."

## Q and the decoding framework

The essay next introduces [[q-anon|Q]], described as, besides Trump himself, "probably the most attacked and vilified entity on the planet." Q is characterized as a small group of patriots working behind the scenes to support Trump and assist in the destruction of the Deep State, communicating with Anons first on 4chan and then 8chan in a deliberately cryptic manner so as not to illegally divulge classified information. Q, the article says, asked many questions and led Anons to investigate topics and people; followers believe Trump is "Q+" and leads the Q team while feigning ignorance of the subject, and that the drops carry both information and disinformation. The author declines to argue for Q's reality, saying only that the pattern attributed to Q — cryptic messages, multiple meanings, multiple audiences — closely matches the pattern attributed to Trump.

## Red October and The Sum of All Fears

Two Tom Clancy properties repeatedly referenced in Q drops are given fresh readings. The Hunt for Red October is commonly understood as the novel and film about a Soviet submarine captain who defects with his crew to the United States, and the author previously read it as an allegory for Trump defecting from a corrupt system while under attack from all sides. He now argues the larger meaning lies in the title itself: "Red October" refers to the October 1917 [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik uprising]] that overthrew and later murdered the Russian monarch and ushered in Communism and the Soviet Union — an event the author classifies as a color revolution in which a small group seizes a country through propaganda and chaos, funding street violence, buying off the media and rigging elections. The claim is that Q was signaling that America is undergoing a Communist takeover driven by narratives and agendas that divide, discourage and demoralize.

The Sum of All Fears receives a parallel treatment. Its plot — modern-day Nazis playing Russia against the United States by unleashing a bioweapon on Ukraine, attacking a U.S. Naval ship and detonating a nuclear bomb in Baltimore — is read as Q warning that "the real modern-day Nazis are trying to create a war between Russia and the US." The article asserts that the two people the Nazis have never been able to control are Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], and promises a follow-up post identifying who the Nazis "really are."

## "Virus" as a metaphor for Communism

The core thesis follows: that Trump places a double meaning on the word "virus," with the hidden referent being Communism. Communism, the article argues, "has been unleashed on the US to destroy it from within. It is not organic; it is a contrived plan." Just as Trump said COVID was intentionally created in a lab and released on the world, the narratives and agendas now circulating were "essentially created in a lab and dropped on us."

The author points to Trump's repeated statement during the "Covid Plandemic" that it was the worst thing to happen to America since the Spanish Flu in 1917. Trump was mocked for the apparent error, since the Spanish Flu occurred in 1918, yet — the article emphasizes — he continued to say 1917 even after public ridicule. Since October 1917 is the date of the Bolshevik uprising, the author reads the persistent "mistake" as a deliberate signal: "Communism, a virus, is spreading throughout America, with the sole intent of dividing and destroying the country."

## "Chyna virus" and the question of origin

The same decoding is applied to Trump's use of "Chyna" and the "Chyna virus," for which he was widely called racist. The author states his own belief that a bioweapon was released from a lab in Wuhan, China, but that it did not originate there: he claims it originated in a lab in North Carolina and was moved to Wuhan to maintain secrecy and control, that [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and Anthony Fauci funded and ran the program, and that Trump's arrival in office forced them to move it overseas. Other bioweapons, the article speculates, were being created in biolabs elsewhere, specifically in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and possibly Taiwan.

Given that Trump, in the author's view, knows the true origin, the continued use of "Chyna" is again read as code: a reference to Communism and to the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] infiltrating America. The article contends the bioweapon "wasn't as deadly as they hoped," but that sustained messaging by communist-controlled media and government officials would achieve the same destructive effect. It argues the COVID narrative forced lockdowns, produced overreaching control of people and an abandonment of human rights, and supplied the justification for mail-in ballots, through which "a duly-elected President was overthrown and removed from power."

## Two-tier society and the closing argument

Describing Communism as a system with a ruling elite and a working-class poor and "nothing in the middle" — "You are the 'served' or the 'server.' You are the slave or the master" — the article suggests that being classified as non-essential during the lockdowns may have indicated that the ruling elite had no place for that person in their two-tiered system, adding that when the Soviet Union had no place for people, "they faced a firing squad."

The essay frames the reader's choice as binary: Trump either vacillates between genius and idiocy, or everything he says and does carries meaning and strategy; he is either controlled by the Deep State despite gaining nothing from the alleged relationship, or he is attacked precisely because he stands up to the system. Readers are invited to revisit past speeches and posts substituting "Communism" for "virus," noting that hidden meanings are easiest to spot when a statement is conspicuously random or strange and hardest to find in messages that appear straightforward.

The article closes by qualifying its own target: Communism is "just a tool—a tool used by the Cabal to control. It's like an axe that is being swung by an axe-murderer." The real enemy, in the author's framing, is the Cabal wielding the axe, described as modern-day Nazis; fighting Communism therefore amounts to keeping the Cabal from getting its hands on the tool.
