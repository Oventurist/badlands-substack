# 2024 Wall Street Trump Trade

The 2024 "Trump trade" describes the repositioning of hedge funds and institutional investors around an expected victory by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the November 2024 presidential election.[1]

## The JPMorgan report

[[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]]'s Positioning Intelligence team, in a report dated October 17, 2024, summarized recent hedge fund positioning as "all-in on Trump themes," in the words of the bank's John Schlegel.[1] The report found that as odds of a Trump presidency and a Republican "Red Wave" rose over the preceding weeks, themes perceived as Republican winners (tracked as JPREPWIN) outperformed Democratic winners (JPDEMWIN) by roughly 7% over the prior month.[1] Crypto stocks and small caps performed better, while renewables underperformed, even as the broader US equity market continued making new all-time highs with elevated positioning.[1] Schlegel cautioned that the combination of thematic shifts, historical election-period returns and elevated positioning left "room for a bit of disappointment and reversal in coming weeks if odds start to shift the other way."[1] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], which covered the report, concluded that the "smart market" was increasingly going all-in on a Trump victory.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media's analysis argued that polls are fake and that elections have been for some time, but that "real" votes exist in the form of bets and hedges.[1] It cautioned that betting sentiment favoring Trump was not a measure of his true support levels — which the outlet claimed were closer to 80% than 60% — but did mark a shift in how large capital expected the election to resolve.[1] Rather than believing that smart money had suddenly grasped Trump's populism or Harris's falsity, the author read the shift as evidence that Wall Street did not believe "the machine is going to get away with it this time," and noted that markets tend to sense such turns early.[1] The commentary further framed breakouts in gold, silver and Bitcoin as mass psychological votes against the survival of the existing system.[1]
