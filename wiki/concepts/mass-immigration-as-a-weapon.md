# Mass Immigration as a Weapon

"Mass immigration as a weapon" describes the thesis, advanced in Badlands Media commentary, that large-scale legal and illegal migration into Western nations is not a humanitarian accident or a policy failure but a deliberate instrument for destabilizing target countries from within.[1]

## The internal-collapse premise

The argument opens with remarks by El Salvador's president [[nayib-bukele|Nayib Bukele]] on [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s program on X, in which Bukele said the erosion of major U.S. cities into crime and chaos is "by design" and the product of "enemies" within the American political system.[1] "The demise of the U.S. has to come from within. No external enemy can cause this much damage," Bukele said, adding that cities that were "pristinely beautiful 30 years ago" had become a wasteland, and that as a Salvadoran he now saw American cities he would not live in — unthinkable three decades earlier.[1]

## The lifeboat analogy

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] framed the situation with a lifeboat analogy: a boat built for twelve is loaded with ten or twelve more and capsizes, drowning everyone.[1] In his version the boat also has a hole in it, the captain is a saboteur, and more than half of those brought aboard past capacity would prefer to throw the original passengers overboard.[1] The prescription that follows is to identify and remove the saboteur and repair the boat, after which the vessel can safely carry additional passengers.[1] A parallel analogy holds that a household that cannot meet its own expenses should not be financing another family — least of all one that wishes its demise — and that jumping into the water to save a drowning person when one cannot swim simply produces two drownings.[1]

## Sweden as the test case

Carlson argued that [[sweden|Sweden]] functioned as a "test country" for the model, citing its asylum intake rising from roughly 20,000 between 1960 and 1982 to about 278,000 between 1982 and 2024, including 163,000 Syrians in 2015 alone, and the subsequent emergence of [[swedish-no-go-zones|no-go zones]] and a shift from one of Europe's lowest to its highest per-capita rate of deadly shootings.[1] He declined to name the architect of the model but gave the initials "G.S.," pointing at [[george-soros|George Soros]].[1]

## Application to the United States

Applied to America, the thesis holds that both illegal and legal channels serve the same end.[1] Carlson compared an open border to panning for gold: a mass of unvetted illegal entrants provides cover for terrorists and potential terrorists, after which the ordinary economic migrants are deported and the intended arrivals remain.[1] On the legal side, he argued that arrivals during the Obama years came increasingly from countries and cultures hostile to the United States, settling in areas where they rapidly became the majority and where allegiance ran to another country, culture and religion.[1] He contrasted this with immigrants of the 1970s and 1980s from Vietnam, Cambodia, Korea, Poland and the Philippines, who he said embraced American culture without abandoning their own.[1]

## Division as the objective

The claimed goal is fragmentation: "A divided America is a weak America."[1] Beyond immigration, Carlson listed the categories into which Americans have been sorted — gay, lesbian, trans, straight, liberal, conservative, Christian, Muslim, Jew, rich, poor, pro-abortion, pro-life, feminist, vegan, climate activist — arguing that anyone out of lockstep with a group's ideology becomes subhuman in its eyes.[1] Support for [[hamas|Hamas]] and Palestine expressed by [[black-lives-matter|BLM]] and progressive activists after October 2023 is presented as the intended result rather than an unintended side effect.[1] Carlson noted that the United States is about 61% non-Hispanic white and called it "a miracle" — by which he meant God's guiding hand — that so diverse a nation functions at all.[1]

## Proposed remedy

The remedy offered is a return to American identity as the primary identifier, with race, religion and sexual orientation secondary, and defense of the Constitution as the common project that defeats the [[deep-state|Deep State]] design.[1] Carlson endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s position of barring entry to people who hate America, arguing the stance names no race or religion and could as easily apply to England or France, and that those offended by it are the real racists.[1] He wrote that a nation genuinely built on Christian values is open to all beliefs so long as they are respectful in return — the Golden Rule works only if both sides abide by it.[1] Absent that, he warned, America will fall as Rome fell.[1]
