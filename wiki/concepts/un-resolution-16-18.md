# UN Resolution 16/18

UN Human Rights Council Resolution 16/18 is a measure adopted with United States support in late 2011 that seeks to limit speech viewed as "discriminatory" or which involves the "defamation of religion" — specifically speech that can be characterized as "incitement to imminent violence."[1] It was an initiative of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (formerly the Organization of Islamic Conferences), a confederacy of 56 Islamic states.[1]

## Origins

The resolution was initially proposed in response to alleged discrimination against Muslims in the aftermath of the September 11, 2001 attacks and in an effort to clamp down on anti-Muslim attacks in non-Muslim countries.[1] It went through a number of revisions over the years in order to make it palatable to American representatives concerned about U.S. constitutional guarantees of free speech.[1]

## U.S. adoption

Previous versions of the resolution, which sought to criminalize blasphemous speech and the "defamation of religion," were regularly rejected by the American delegation and by the [[state-department|U.S. State Department]], which insisted that limitations on speech — even speech deemed racist or blasphemous — were at odds with the Constitution.[1] The version incorporating the "incitement to imminent violence" phrase, criminalizing speech that incites violence against others on the basis of religion, race, or national origin, won U.S. approval despite indirectly placing limits on speech considered blasphemous.[1] Forbes noted the adoption occurred quietly over the 2011 holiday season.[1]
