# Project Cassandra

Project Cassandra was a long-running [[drug-enforcement-administration|Drug Enforcement Administration]] campaign targeting the narcotics trafficking, weapons dealing and money laundering networks of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1] Reporting cited in the Badlands Media essay "Panic in DC" gives its launch variously as 2007 and 2008, following an earlier investigation, Operation Titan, begun in 2004 against the Medellín-based Oficina del Envigado.[1]

## Origins

American law enforcement discovered Hezbollah's involvement in the drug trade by accident in 2006, when Colombian wiretaps monitoring the Oficina del Envigado picked up conversations in Arabic.[1] A translator brought in by the DEA determined that Hezbollah was arranging multi-ton cocaine shipments to the Middle East.[1] The article notes that this occurred during the presidency of [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]], and asserts that the underlying networks dated to the 1980s, when [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]] allegedly helped establish them in Latin America.[1]

## Scope and findings

Cassandra's central finding was that Hezbollah's cocaine shipments were not a fringe activity by loosely affiliated individuals but a multibillion-dollar global enterprise directed by senior figures in the organization's inner circle.[1] Politico reporting quoted in the article describes the DEA amassing evidence that Hezbollah had transformed from a Middle East-focused military and political organization into an international crime syndicate that some investigators believed collected roughly $1 billion a year from drug and weapons trafficking, money laundering and other criminal activity.[1]

Over eight years, agents working from a top-secret DEA facility in Chantilly, Virginia used wiretaps, undercover operations and informants, with the assistance of some thirty U.S. and foreign security agencies, to map the networks.[1] They tracked cocaine flowing from Latin America to West Africa and onward to Europe and the Middle East, and through Venezuela and Mexico into the United States, and followed the laundering of proceeds — including through the purchase of American used cars for shipment to Africa.[1] Investigators believed the conspiracy reached Hezbollah's innermost circle and its state sponsors in [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## The Joumaa network and used cars

A key figure identified was Hashem Safieddine, described as a low-key, bespectacled man of diplomatic bearing operating from a base in Tehran, whom investigators regarded as the point of connection to numerous smuggling and laundering networks.[1] The largest of these was run by Medellín-based Lebanese businessman [[ayman-joumaa|Ayman Joumaa]], who worked with Mexico's [[los-zetas|Los Zetas]] cartel to move multi-ton cocaine loads into the United States and washed roughly $200 million a month through approximately 300 used-car dealerships, with the vehicles shipped to Benin on Africa's west coast.[1]

## Alleged obstruction

The article's most serious allegation, drawn from Politico, is that as Project Cassandra reached higher into the conspiracy's hierarchy, Obama administration officials erected an increasingly insurmountable series of roadblocks.[1] The [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]] declined requests to file criminal charges against major players including Hezbollah's envoy to Iran, a Lebanese bank alleged to have laundered billions in drug profits, and a central figure in a U.S.-based [[quds-force|Quds Force]] cell; the [[state-department|State Department]] rejected requests to lure high-value targets to countries where they could be arrested.[1]

The article attributes this to the administration's broader policy posture. [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] had entered office in 2009 promising rapprochement with Iran and the Muslim world, and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] — later Obama's top counterterrorism adviser and then CIA director — had argued in a policy paper for "greater assimilation of Hezbollah into Lebanon's political system," describing Hezbollah as an organization that had evolved from "purely a terrorist organization" into a militia and ultimately a political party.[1] Combined with the desire for a negotiated settlement on Iran's nuclear program, this produced what Cassandra members described as a reluctance to act aggressively against top Hezbollah operatives.[1]

## Interpretation in the article

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents Cassandra as evidence for the Q-derived question "Why is Iran protected? Why is Iran funded by the US?" and as proof that the world's largest terrorist organization ran global drug trafficking undetected for decades because of protection at the highest levels of the U.S. government.[1] He connects it directly to [[cash-for-clunkers|Cash for Clunkers]], arguing that the program was designed to feed used vehicles into Hezbollah's laundering pipeline.[1]
