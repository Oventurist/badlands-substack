# Staff Accounting Bulletin 121

Staff Accounting Bulletin 121, commonly known as SAB 121, is a [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] accounting rule governing how cryptocurrency assets are accounted for, which makes it very expensive for financial institutions to participate in the crypto custody business.[1]

## Congressional rebuke

In May 2024 a resolution condemning the rule passed both houses of the U.S. Congress, setting up a likely veto by the [[white-house|White House]].[1] The [[us-senate|Senate]] voted 60-38 to strike down SAB 121, following earlier [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] passage.[1] The White House indicated it would veto any such legislation.[1] In response to a request for comment on the vote, SEC staff sent Axios a description of SAB 121 without commenting on the legislative action.[1]

The episode marked an extension of the crypto industry's fight against SEC chairman [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]] from the courts onto a new field: the legislature.[1] The SEC had been feeling more confident after a procedural win on most points in its case against [[coinbase|Coinbase]], the country's largest cryptocurrency exchange, and on key points in the case against Terraform Labs — though those followed a string of major losses, including on the characterization of the cryptocurrency XRP and the approval of a spot bitcoin ETF.[1]

## Veto threat

On 8 May 2024 the office of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] issued a formal statement informing lawmakers and the public that the administration planned to veto the joint resolution affecting SEC crypto policy if it reached his desk.[2] The [[white-house|White House]] said it "strongly opposes" members of the [[house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] seeking to pass a resolution it claimed would disrupt the SEC's efforts "to protect investors in crypto-asset markets and to safeguard the broader financial system."[2] The measure at issue, H.J.Res. 109, had been introduced in the House in February and would have overturned SAB 121, which requires banks to keep customers' digital assets on their balance sheets with capital maintained against them.[2]

The administration argued that "SAB 121 was issued in response to demonstrated technological, legal, and regulatory risks that have caused substantial losses to consumers," and that by invoking the Congressional Review Act the resolution "could also inappropriately constrain the SEC's ability to ensure appropriate guardrails and address future issues related to crypto-assets including financial stability," warning that limiting the agency's regulatory framework would introduce substantial financial instability and market uncertainty.[2]

## Further Badlands analysis

Writing in the Badlands News Brief of 9 May 2024, Burning Bright called the veto threat's implications "pretty massive" and classified the episode as a "Devo/Control proof."[2] The argument advanced was that the central banking apparatus has historically leveraged control through the valuation system, including gold, and cannot sell derivatives without holding the underlying assets that back them.[2] If central banks control the underlying asset they can build derivative markets atop it, whereas if independent custodians hold the asset and are publicly audited, the shell game ends.[2] On this reading, cabal elements wanting to custody bitcoin would have to do so on the same terms as sovereign individuals, and the spot ETFs represent a "rugpull ON the cabal" that drains financial energy into an asset class visible on a public ledger.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized SAB 121 as "one of the best Bicameral stories currently percolating in the Info War."[1] On one hand, decentralization of crypto custody for exchange-traded funds makes sense; on the other, the party most affected by the rule *not* being overturned is the banking apparatus that has subsisted on selling the American people derivatives off assets they traditionally have not even held on their balance sheets.[1] Under the view that Coinbase is acting as a custodian for an eventual seizure of corrupt assets, the vote is good news — as is the spectacle of the entire crypto space and banking sector unified in fury at the Biden presidency.[1] The commentary anticipated that [[donald-trump|Trump]] would "come forward with the magical solution circa 2025," concluding with a one-word verdict: "Controlled."[1]
