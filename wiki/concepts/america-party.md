# America Party

The America Party is a political party announced by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] in July 2025, with which he said he would take on both Republicans and Democrats by focusing on House and Senate seats over the following 12 months. Musk wrote on X that after that period, backing a candidate for president was not out of the question.[1]

## Market reaction

The announcement, made over a weekend, deepened Musk's pursuit of a political project that had already been a drag on his most valuable business. Shares of [[tesla|Tesla]] Inc. fell 6.8% on the Monday following the announcement in New York, wiping out $15.3 billion from Musk's net worth according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index.[1]

## Trump's reaction

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly attacked [[elon-musk|Musk]] over the announcement on July 6, 2025, writing that he was "saddened to watch Elon Musk go completely 'off the rails,' essentially becoming a TRAIN WRECK over the past five weeks" and noting that third parties "have never succeeded in the United States."[2] The party was announced on July 5, the day after Trump signed the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill]] into law; Musk had condemned that legislation, which the [[congressional-budget-office|Congressional Budget Office]] projected would add $3.4 trillion to the national debt.[2] Badlands contributors were unimpressed with the venture: [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued that "you can't fix a broken system by adding another layer to it," and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that "the only acceptable number of parties is zero."[2]

## Consultation with Curtis Yarvin

In July 2025 The New York Times reported that among the people [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] consulted while studying how to start the America Party was [[curtis-yarvin|Curtis Yarvin]], the right-wing blogger best known for advocating monarchism and for his distaste for traditional American democracy.[3] The paper noted that Yarvin is not an expert on the mechanics of forming third parties or on third-party campaign strategy, yet the two had spoken late the previous week about the task ahead according to two people briefed on the conversation.[3] A representative for Musk did not immediately respond to a request for comment and Yarvin declined an interview.[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] read the episode as part of a "loud MAGA-tech divorce" that would expose anti-Trump, pro-Regime elements in Silicon Valley much as earlier cycles exposed RINO Republicans, arguing that tech oligarchs use MAGA to advance their own aims before splitting off, and that they build instruments of top-down control while posing as freedom fighters.[3]
