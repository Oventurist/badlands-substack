# Domestic Terrorism

Domestic terrorism is the legal and rhetorical category used by United States authorities for politically motivated violence committed within the country. Badlands coverage treats it as a genuine category that is nonetheless persistently vulnerable to expansion beyond violent conduct.[1]

## The 2026 Antifa convictions

The label gained new weight in March 2026 when a federal jury in Fort Worth convicted nine members of what prosecutors called a "North Texas [[antifa|Antifa]] cell" over the July 4, 2025 attack outside the [[prairieland-detention-center|Prairieland]] ICE facility, on charges including providing material support to terrorists and explosives offenses.[1] HSI Dallas Special Agent in Charge Travis Pickard framed the case as the nation's first federal indictment of a coordinated Antifa cell and a demonstration of commitment to confronting domestic terrorism.[1]

## Civil-liberties objection

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] accepted that planting explosives for political purposes is domestic terrorism and that those responsible deserve prison, while warning that the label is generally overused as a vehicle for overreach.[1] Her central question was whether the category could be expanded to capture speech, assembly and association without underlying criminal conduct, and whether the public would accept that expansion simply because the accused were Antifa — noting that the same public rejects it in cases such as [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1] The argument connects the domestic-terror debate to the concurrent fight over the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] and the use of statutes such as the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]] against journalists.[1]

## FBI–IRS nonprofit task force (March 2026)

In March 2026 [[fbi|FBI]] and [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] agents were reported to be creating a joint task force to investigate nonprofits suspected of funding domestic terrorism or political violence.[2] The operation, described to CBS News as a "mission control command center," was to be based at the FBI with IRS Criminal Investigations agents serving on temporary one-year assignments.[2] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was separately assembling a task force to probe [[antifa|antifa]]-linked funding, and US attorney offices were directed to assign domestic terrorism coordinators.[2]

The initiative followed a December memo from Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] instructing federal agencies to prioritize investigations of antifa and other extremist groups, which highlighted potential violence, anti-American and anti-religious ideologies, and tax fraud as key concerns and ordered law enforcement to compile intelligence on such groups for the FBI.[2] That memo built on a September executive order from [[donald-trump|President Trump]] on domestic terrorism, issued after high-profile incidents including the 2025 assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[2]


## Trump's proposed designation of left-wing groups (September 2025)

On 15 September 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would consider naming [[antifa|Antifa]] and other left-wing groups as domestic terrorists, previewing a crackdown on what he cast as a widespread network of left-wing radicals inciting violence.[3] Asked whether he would apply the label, he said "It's something I would do, yeah," conditioning action on support from Cabinet officials and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], and adding in the Oval Office, "Antifa is terrible. There are other groups."[3]

It was not clear who or what exactly would be designated, since Antifa is a loosely organized movement without a distinct leader, membership lists or structure.[3] The move followed signals from administration officials after the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] that they would target an alleged coordinated left-wing effort to incite violence, though no public evidence had emerged that the alleged assassin acted as part of a larger network.[3] White House Deputy Chief of Staff [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] pledged to use every federal resource to "identify, disrupt, dismantle and destroy these networks."[3] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] recalled Trump's 5 January 2021 memo ordering an assessment of whether to classify Antifa as a terrorist organization.[3]
