# 2023 United Auto Workers Strike

The 2023 United Auto Workers strike was a coordinated labor action launched by the [[united-auto-workers|United Auto Workers]] against all three Detroit automakers simultaneously — described by Reuters as the most ambitious U.S. industrial labor action in decades.[1]

## Course of the strike

About 12,700 UAW workers remained on strike into a second day, targeting three U.S. assembly plants, one at each of the Detroit Three.[1] Union negotiators and representatives of [[general-motors|General Motors]], [[ford-motor-company|Ford Motor]] and [[stellantis|Stellantis]] resumed talks on Saturday, September 16, 2023, with no sign of a breakthrough as of that afternoon.[1]

The union said negotiators for the UAW and Ford had held "reasonably productive discussions" toward a new contract — a notably more positive characterization than its assessment before the strikes began.[1] Officials at Chrysler parent Stellantis said a proposal to resume work at an idled Illinois factory had fallen through.[1] Days earlier, Ford chief executive [[jim-farley|Jim Farley]] said the UAW's proposals would push the company into bankruptcy and that there was "nothing going on" in negotiations.[1]

## Automation commentary

Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the strike shared a single root cause with the [[2023-hollywood-labor-strikes|Hollywood strikes]]: automation. Reductions in force at major auto manufacturers were, per the commentary, a key decision point behind the UAW walkout, because automated production lines require fewer workers.[1]

The commentary contended that the time to strike over automation had passed roughly a decade earlier, when automation was still a coming threat rather than a realized one delivering record productivity and profitability gains through optimization.[1] Corporations, it argued, would not relinquish those gains after more than a decade of long-game investment in hardware, software, robotics, IoT sensors and artificial intelligence — an openly stated corporate strategy.[1] Global corporations knowing they will put most of their workforce out of work was offered as the explanation for their support of universal basic income and of "upskilling" and "reskilling" programs.[1]

While the cross-industry strikes were framed as a worker awakening to the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] and the [[new-world-order|New World Order]], the commentary concluded that any deal reached would be unsustainable and short-lived, since the corporations remain committed to an automated and more profitable future in both streaming and AI content creation and in robotic manufacturing.[1]
