# Khan Shaykhun Chemical Attack

The **Khan Shaykhun chemical attack** of 2017 was an alleged chemical weapons incident in Syria that prompted U.S. airstrikes under the Trump administration.[1] Badlands Media presents it as the second major instance — after the 2013 [[ghouta-chemical-attack|Ghouta attack]] — in which the [[military-industrial-complex|Military-Industrial Complex]] relied on chemical weapons allegations as a justification for military intervention in Syria.[1]

## Disputed evidence

Whistleblowers from the [[organization-for-the-prohibition-of-chemical-weapons|Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons]] later revealed that evidence had been manipulated to support the intervention narrative, according to the article.[1] This pattern of internal dissent within the OPCW recurred in the 2018 [[douma-chemical-attack|Douma incident]], where insiders similarly reported that findings had been altered.[1] Taken together, these disclosures are described as significantly undermining the propaganda used to justify Western intervention and highlighting the MIC's tactic of manufacturing consent through false flag operations.[1]

## Context

The Syrian conflict, framed in Western media as a civil war sparked by pro-democracy protests in 2011, is characterized in the article as engineered to destabilize the region, secure strategic resources, and counter Russian and Iranian influence.[1] Chemical weapons allegations against the government of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] served as the recurring trigger for escalation within that framework.[1] Independent journalists and alternative media platforms are credited by [[white-hats|White Hats]] with exposing the staged nature of these alleged attacks.[1]
