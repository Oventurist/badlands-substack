# Jerusalem Conference on Terrorism

The Jerusalem Conference on Terrorism was a 1979 gathering held in [[israel|Israel]] to discuss the origins, nature, and future of terrorism and to propose measures for combatting and defeating international terror movements.[1] Attendees produced numerous essays on the subject, and in 1981 a collection of these works was compiled and released by [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

## Significance in Badlands analysis

In the May 21, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the conference as an origin point for what he described as a decades-long campaign to seed the Western public mind with the image of radical Islamic terrorism as civilization's principal antagonist.[1] He linked the conference to the 1986 Hollywood film Delta Force starring Chuck Norris, which he identified as the first mainstream film casting radical Islamic terrorists as villains, released six years after the conference.[1]

He further traced a line from the conference through Netanyahu's later writing on terrorism, in particular the 1995 book Fighting Terrorism: How Democracies Can Defeat Domestic and International Terrorists, which explicitly warned that [[iran|Iran]] was on the cusp of developing a nuclear weapon and argued that the West had a moral obligation to stop Iran by kinetic intervention.[1] The commentator noted that the book's cover depicted an X-ray image of a traveler's bag resembling the TSA scanning equipment later mandated by Congress in every U.S. airport after the September 11 attacks — an attack he asserted was most likely orchestrated by Netanyahu and associates.[1] Concluding that "thirty years later, Netanyahu is still singing the same song with the same lyrics," he framed contemporary reporting on a possible Israeli strike on Iranian nuclear facilities as the latest iteration of the same psychological operation.[1]

These claims are contested interpretations advanced by Badlands commentators rather than established findings.[1]
