# Israel-Only Aid Bill

The Israel-only aid bill was a standalone supplemental spending measure unveiled by [[us-house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] Republicans on October 30, 2023, providing $14.3 billion in aid to Israel while offsetting the cost by cutting [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] funding and providing nothing for Ukraine.[1]

## Legislative context

The bill was the first major legislative action under new Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[1] It stood in contrast to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s request for a $106 billion package combining aid for Israel and Ukraine with funding to boost competition with China in the Indo-Pacific and security along the U.S. border with Mexico.[1]

Republicans held a 221–212 majority in the House, while Democrats controlled the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] 51–49; to become law the bill would have to pass both chambers and be signed by Biden.[1] Senators of both parties voiced doubts on October 31, and Senate Democratic Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] declared it dead on arrival in the upper chamber even if the House passed it, telling reporters, "The bottom line is it's not a serious proposal."[1] The administration said Biden would veto the bill were it to reach his desk.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] said that he and [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] had speculated that week on the outcomes of Speaker Johnson's maneuvering from an America First perspective.[1] One scenario he described would deliver a narrative win to the America First movement at no actual cost: the unified Israel–Ukraine package would be dead on arrival in the House while Johnson's bifurcated proposal would be dead on arrival in the Senate, leaving the Biden administration and the establishment-controlled Senate to absorb blame from both pro-Israel and pro-Ukraine war hawks while no money flowed to either proxy war.[1] Adding that he counted himself among "those kooks who believes much of what we're being told about spending packages is purely for optics," he concluded that regardless of who won, "the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] loses in the eyes of the people."[1]

The item was reported by [[reuters|Reuters]].[1]
