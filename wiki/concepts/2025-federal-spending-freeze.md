# 2025 Federal Spending Freeze

The 2025 federal spending freeze was a directive issued overnight on January 27–28, 2025 by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s budget office instructing federal agencies to temporarily halt paying out grants and loans as of 5 p.m. Tuesday, January 28.[1]

## Immediate fallout

The [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] memo threw Washington into chaos amid a scramble to determine precisely what programs were affected.[1] As the Capitol reeled, many Democratic lawmakers responded by voting against [[sean-duffy|Sean Duffy]]'s nomination to lead the Transportation Department — an otherwise uncontroversial nominee whom they had unanimously voted to advance less than 24 hours earlier.[1] Senator [[chris-coons|Chris Coons]] said he would vote against nominees "until I get clarity from this administration about their plans to continue implementing this order," adding that a federal court injunction would be necessary to ease his concerns and questioning whether Duffy would simply be a vessel for implementing the freeze.[1]

Badlands commentary observed that Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, that Senate Democrats have no power to block nominees without Republican help, and that "advice and consent" does not mean "approve," characterizing the protest as performative — while allowing that a blocked nominee could produce useful legal clarity.[1]

## Judicial block

Minutes before the directive was to take effect, U.S. District Judge [[loren-alikhan|Loren AliKhan]] halted the freeze, barring the administration from suspending disbursement of congressionally appropriated funds until February 3 at 5 p.m.[1] She described the order as a "brief administrative stay" to preserve the status quo, citing "the specter of irreparable harm."[1] The ruling was a victory for nonprofit and public health groups that had sued earlier that day.[1]

## Rescission

On Wednesday, January 29, 2025, the Trump administration's [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] released a new memo rescinding the controversial directive issued late that Monday, which had frozen a wide swath of federal financial assistance, paralyzed many federal programs and caused a large uproar on Capitol Hill.[2] The decision followed strong behind-the-scenes pushback from Republican lawmakers, according to a GOP senator apprised of the reversal.[2] The rescission was signed by [[matthew-vaeth|Matthew Vaeth]], acting director of the White House budget office, and one Republican senator expressed relief, remarking that "chaos is never good."[2] GOP senators had been flooded with calls from community leaders confused and concerned about losing federal grants.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode through the frame of a long-running "War of Stories," arguing that the establishment has sold a narrative of a broken future through climate doomism and AI apocalypse warnings, and that readers should weigh the source and incentive behind such fear-mongering.[2]
