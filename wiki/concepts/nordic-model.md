# Nordic Model

The "Nordic Model" refers to the combination of large-scale welfare provision, high taxation and extensive state intervention practiced by the Nordic countries, frequently cited in American political debate as proof that social democracy can work at scale.[1]

## Critique in Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that globalists and their allies in the United States had for decades used the Nordic countries as the gold standard for expanding the American welfare state while ignoring the model's costs: high taxes, a high degree of government intervention, and low GDP and worker productivity.[1] In this reading, the model redistributes assets, limits money available for individual spending and consumption, and incentivizes reliance on government.[1]

The commentary held that the Nordics remained "the gold standard of the open society" until mass migration — characterized as a coordinated global program to denationalize the world — undermined the arrangement.[1] [[sweden|Sweden]], previously a showcase of the model, was described as having become a cautionary tale about open borders, with the government's new mandatory reporting requirement for illegal immigrants presented as belated vindication of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2017 warnings.[1]

Ashe in America further warned that the proposed remedies carried their own costs, pointing to the center-right Swedish government's plan — supported by the Sweden Democrats — to extend biometric screening including fingerprinting and facial recognition to people already living in the country, and arguing that a population conditioned by dependence on the state might not notice its "slow and ongoing descent into digital slavery."[1]
