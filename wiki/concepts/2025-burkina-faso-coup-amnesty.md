# 2025 Burkina Faso Coup Amnesty

The 2025 Burkina Faso coup amnesty was a decree by interim president Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] pardoning 21 soldiers convicted of attempting to overthrow the country's government nearly a decade earlier, published by local media on Monday, March 31, 2025.[1]

## Background

Traoré had announced an amnesty the previous December for several people sentenced over a 2015 plot to topple the transitional government installed after the forced resignation of former president [[blaise-compaore|Blaise Compaoré]].[1] Compaoré had himself come to power in a 1987 coup during which Pan-Africanist icon [[thomas-sankara|Thomas Sankara]] was assassinated.[1]

## The decree

"The following persons, who have been convicted or prosecuted before the courts for acts committed on September 15 and 16, 2015, are granted amnesty," Traoré declared in the order, dated March 24, naming 21 soldiers.[1] The officers, including two former unit commanders of the former presidential guard, had been convicted by a military tribunal in Ouagadougou in 2019 for "harming state security," murder, or treason.[1] According to AFP, Compaoré's former chief of staff Gilbert Diendéré and head of diplomacy Djibril Bassolé — two generals sentenced to 20 and ten years respectively for allegedly masterminding the failed coup — were not included in the amnesty.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that while it is nearly impossible to discern who the earnest actors are in the recent history of African coups, the amnesty appears plausibly to be an act of justice.[1] He argued Sankara was overthrown for refusing loans from the [[imf|International Monetary Fund]], speculated that [[nato|NATO]] may have been involved in the 2014 uprising against Compaoré given its proximity to the 2011 uprising against Muammar Gaddafi in Libya, and suggested the 2015 plotters may have been acting in earnest to reclaim their country from foreign influence.[1] The exclusion of the two planning generals while pardoning subordinates following orders, he wrote, demonstrated "a just and measured man in Ibrahim Traore."[1]
