# 2024 Kharkiv Offensive

The 2024 Kharkiv offensive was a Russian military advance into northern Ukraine launched on Monday, May 13, 2024, with attacks on towns and villages in the Kharkiv region.[1]

## Missing fortifications

The offensive struck precisely the sector where Ukrainian defensive fortifications had been budgeted but, according to anti-corruption reporting, never built. [[martyna-bohuslavets|Martyna Bohuslavets]], head of the Mezha Anti-Corruption Center, reported in Ukrainian Pravda that millions of dollars intended for fortification construction had been "transferred to Kharkiv OVA to front companies of avatars," with the Kharkiv Regional Military Administration paying fictitious firms out of a total of 7 billion hryvnias spent in the region.[1]

Ukrainian commander [[denys-yaroslavskyi|Denys Yaroslavskyi]], head of the Ukrainian Special Reconnaissance Unit in the area, told the BBC that "there was no first line of defence" and that "the Russians just walked in… without any mined fields," concluding: "It wasn't a failure. It was a betrayal."[1]

## Oversight context

The episode coincided with continued US funding of the war and with a March 2024 [[government-accountability-office|Government Accountability Office]] finding that the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] lacked quality data to track delivery of defense articles to Ukraine.[1] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the combination pointed to something other than routine incompetence in the aid pipeline, raising [[continuity-of-government|continuity of government]] speculation and proposing the topic for discussion with [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] on the Power Hour.[1]

## US authorization of strikes inside Russia

On May 30, 2024, Politico reported that the Biden administration had quietly given Ukraine permission to strike inside Russia — solely near the area of Kharkiv — using U.S.-provided weapons, a major reversal intended to help Ukraine defend its second-largest city.[2] "The president recently directed his team to ensure that Ukraine is able to use U.S. weapons for counter-fire purposes in Kharkiv so Ukraine can hit back at Russian forces hitting them or preparing to hit them," a U.S. official said, adding that the policy barring long-range strikes inside Russia "has not changed."[2] Ukraine had asked for the change only after the Russian offensive on Kharkiv began that month, and in the preceding days Washington decided to allow "flexibility" for defense against attacks near the border.[2]

In practice Ukraine could now use American rockets and rocket launchers to shoot down Russian missiles heading toward Kharkiv, strike troops massing just over the border near the city, or hit Russian bombers launching munitions toward Ukrainian territory — but not to hit civilian infrastructure or to launch long-range missiles such as [[atacms|ATACMS]] against military targets deep inside Russia.[2] Politico characterized it as a stunning shift for an administration that had initially said such a step would escalate the war by more directly involving the United States, a change of mind driven by worsening battlefield conditions and Russia's improved position around Kharkiv.[2]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] analysed the reversal through his "Overton's Goalpost" cypher, which he uses to track what he calls the enemy's pattern of narrative retreat over previously held psychological ground in the Info War.[2] He argued that although the cypher is most easily applied to admissions of wrongdoing or policy retreats, the escalating posture of the Globalist West toward [[russia|Russia]] reflects the same pattern: a declared policy of non-intervention — sanctions and proxy funding aside — steadily shifting until the mainstream press was carrying water for the administration as it authorized its "Prussian Proxy" to strike Russian territory with American arms, qualified for now as short-range only.[2] His conclusion: "Normies most affected."[2]
