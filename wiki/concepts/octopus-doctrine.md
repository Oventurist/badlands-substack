# Octopus Doctrine

The **Octopus Doctrine** is an Israeli strategic concept associated with former Prime Minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]], holding that Israel should strike the "head of the octopus" — the Iranian state itself — rather than confining itself to fighting the "tentacles," the network of regional proxies such as [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and allied militias that [[iran|Iran]] sponsors.

## 2026 revival

Bennett returned the doctrine to public debate in June 2026, in the aftermath of the reported [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding-2026|U.S.-Iran agreement]] that excluded Israel from its terms. Speaking to The Times of Israel, Bennett said Israel faced "an existential moment" and warned that another four years under the incumbent government would leave the country without an economy, a society, or international standing.

He said that if elected he would advance the Octopus Doctrine as a multiyear plan aimed at accelerating the collapse of Iran's government while ensuring it does not acquire nuclear weapons. In the same interview he set out a Haredi policy that would end state funding for schools refusing to teach the core curriculum and cancel certain benefits for those who neither serve in the military nor work.

Bennett's intervention came alongside criticism of the U.S. deal from opposition leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]], his ally, who called it "terrible for Israel," and from National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]], who argued that Israel was not a party to the agreement and that operations should continue until Hezbollah is dismantled. Badlands commentary read the convergence as evidence that Israeli politics was preparing to pursue the war without American participation.
