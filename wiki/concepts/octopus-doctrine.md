# Octopus Doctrine

The "Octopus Doctrine" is a multiyear Israeli strategic plan described by former Prime Minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] as aimed at accelerating the collapse of [[iran|Iran]]'s government while ensuring that Tehran does not acquire nuclear weapons.[1] The metaphor treats Iran as the "head" of a regional octopus whose "arms" are its allied militias, implying that pressure should be directed at the Iranian state itself rather than only at its proxies.[1]

## Political context

Bennett invoked the doctrine in June 2026 while positioning himself against the sitting Israeli government during the fallout from the United States–Iran peace framework.[1] He told The Times of Israel that Israel faced "an existential moment" and warned that another four years under the current government would leave the country without an economy, a society, or international standing.[1] He said that, if elected, he would advance the Octopus Doctrine as a central plank of his security program.[1]

Alongside the doctrine, Bennett outlined a domestic Haredi policy that would end state funding for schools refusing to teach the core curriculum and cancel certain benefits for those who neither serve in the military nor work.[1]

## Relationship to the 2026 peace framework

The doctrine's revival coincided with sharp Israeli criticism of the U.S.–Iran agreement. National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] argued that Israel was not a party to the deal and that military operations should continue until [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] is dismantled, while Opposition Leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] called the deal "terrible for Israel."[1] Badlands commentary framed this cluster of positions as evidence that Israeli leadership intended to continue pursuing war independently of Washington.[1]
