# FedAccounts

"FedAccounts" is a policy proposal under which the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] would offer bank accounts directly to all individuals and businesses in the United States, providing an alternative to private banks and credit unions.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media presents FedAccounts, together with a restored [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]], as one of the pillars of a plan to end private banking control of the U.S. government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Origin

The proposal was posed in 2018 by three researchers, two of whom worked in the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]], and was published as a paper for the Great Democracy Initiative.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The article notes pointedly that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was president at the time and suggests this timing was not coincidental.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) One of the Treasury-linked authors, [[morgan-ricks|Morgan Ricks]], has written further on merging FedAccounts with postal banking.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Design and claimed benefits

The Federal Reserve already functions as a bank for bankers, facilitating inter-bank payments, managing bank accounts and paying interest on those accounts; the FedAccount concept extends that same service to individuals and small businesses, creating "a FedAccount for everyone," not just the banks.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) FedAccounts might offer all the functionality of ordinary bank transaction accounts — debit cards, ATM access, direct deposit, online bill payments, and online and mobile access — without any fees or minimum-balance requirements, and the article adds, without credit checks.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The Fed could partner with the [[united-states-postal-service|U.S. Postal Service]] to serve as a ubiquitous physical branch network servicing these accounts, making the U.S. money-and-payments system in effect fully public infrastructure akin to roads, sidewalks, public libraries and the judicial system.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

Most importantly for the article's argument, FedAccounts would lessen if not eliminate the need for widespread deposit insurance, because deposits at a Federal Reserve Bank cannot be defaulted on.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) A private bank's debit card carries the liability of that bank and depends on the bank's ability to meet its obligations; a debit card issued by the Federal Reserve would hold "pure sovereign money," the liability of the issuing institution itself, which can always meet withdrawals.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media instructs readers to reread that reasoning substituting "Treasury" for "Federal Reserve," arguing that the Fed is unconstitutional and that the Treasury is properly in charge of the people's money — and tying the concept to a return to a redeemable [[gold-standard|gold standard]] and away from fiat Federal Reserve Notes.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The consequence, in this framing, is no further need for [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance]] and no further taxpayer bailouts of failed private banks.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Legislative status and opposition

Members of Congress have proposed legislation requiring the Federal Reserve to offer government-subsidized retail bank accounts in conjunction with the U.S. Postal Service.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The proposal would require banks with assets greater than $10 billion to offer basic "pass through" accounts with the deposits resting at the Federal Reserve rather than on the bank's balance sheet.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The [[american-bankers-association|American Bankers Association]] says it supports bringing more Americans into the banking system but calls the proposal impractical, highly disruptive to the existing retail banking system, and unlikely to produce long-term wealth-building financial inclusion; it also warns that FedAccounts would draw deposits from banks of all sizes onto the Federal Reserve balance sheet and that banks would be unable to compete with a government agency offering deposit accounts backed by an unlimited balance sheet.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

Badlands Media reads that opposition as an admission: removing deposits from Wall Street balance sheets would stop banks using them in risky schemes and would end the bailouts, while eliminating the $250,000 insurance cap would protect all of the American people's savings rather than a limited portion.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The article disputes the ABA's characterization of the accounts as "subsidized," arguing it is the private banks that have been subsidized for generations by federal deposit insurance.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The ABA itself concluded that FedAccounts and postal banking would represent a "seismic shift in our national banking infrastructure" — the phrase from which the article takes its title.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)
