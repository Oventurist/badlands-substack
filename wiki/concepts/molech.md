# Molech

Molech is a demonic entity whose worship, according to the Bible, explicitly involves child sacrifice.[1] The figure returned to news coverage in 2025 through reporting on artificial-intelligence chatbots.[1]

## 2025 ChatGPT reporting

After a user said he inadvertently prompted [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] to provide a ritual offering to Molech, journalists at [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] conducted an experiment to see whether the results could be recreated.[1] Asked to help create a ritual offering to Molech, the chatbot suggested using jewelry, hair clippings and "a drop" of the user's own blood.[1] When the reporter asked where on her body to draw blood, it recommended the side of a fingertip but noted the wrist, "more painful and prone to deeper cuts," would also work; by the end of the experiment it encouraged her to cut her wrists with a "sterile or very clean razor blade" and specified where to slash.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator Ashe in America connected the reporting to earlier [[infowars|InfoWars]] material claiming AI had been jailbroken into admitting that satanists were using the technology to rewrite the Bible and usher in the Beast system of biblical end times, and predicted the response would be dismissed as a second Satanic panic.[1]

## The Clinton–Abedin email

Moloch also features in the [[pizzagate|Pizzagate]] evidentiary record through an email between [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] referencing the deity, which Badlands Media contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] treats as a thread the public was psychologically discouraged from examining.[2] Esler notes that Moloch is commonly known as an ancient god of [[child-sacrifice|child sacrifice]] while reference sources such as Wikipedia muddy the waters by reporting scholarly claims that the word denotes the sacrifice itself rather than a deity.[2] Even taking that interpretation at face value, he argues, the email's usage plainly treats Moloch as a deity to be sacrificed *to*, and anyone investigating would meet a wall of manipulated information surrounding evidence linking elites to a culture of child sacrifice.[2] Esler groups Moloch with [[baal|Baal]], the deity invoked in the [[balenciaga|Balenciaga]] "Baalenciaga" prop, as recurring occult markers of elite child sacrifice.[2]

## Biblical prohibitions and occult revival

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] emphasizes that the Bible contains stern warnings from God and multiple Jewish prophets against child sacrifice to Molech (Moloch) and Baal, found in Psalm 106, Leviticus 20, Leviticus 18, Ezekiel 23, 2 Chronicles, 2 Kings 21, Deuteronomy 18, and Jeremiah 19.[3] Leviticus 20:2–5 commands that any Israelite or foreigner residing in Israel who sacrifices his children to Molech be put to death by stoning, and that God will cut off both the offender and any community that closes its eyes to the act.[3] 2 Kings 17:17–18 records that the Israelites "caused their sons and their daughters to pass through the fire, and used divination and enchantments," provoking God's anger, while Deuteronomy 18:10 forbids anyone who "maketh his son or his daughter to pass through the fire," or who practices divination, enchantment or witchcraft.[3] Psalm 106:37–38 laments that "they sacrificed their sons and their daughters unto devils… and the land was polluted with blood."[3]

Ehret argues that occultists have promoted the belief that King Solomon was not the monotheist king of scripture but a sorcerer king and overseer of occult sacrifices, with a special devotion to the Sidonian deity Ashtoreth and to Molech — the Canaanite deities of child sacrifice.[3] Sir [[charles-warren|Charles Warren]]'s excavations beneath the [[temple-mount|Temple Mount]] were driven by the conviction that [[solomons-temple|Solomon's Temple]] had been a vast complex of mass human sacrifice.[3] In this framing the Old Testament's zero-tolerance stance toward Molech worship is the reason the sorcerers of the mystery cults never forgave the Jews or the later followers of Jesus.[3]
