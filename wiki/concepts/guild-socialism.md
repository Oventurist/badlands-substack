# Guild Socialism

Guild socialism is a political-economic doctrine advocating the devolution of productive power to a confederation of trade guilds, in opposition to centralized state planning.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats it as the deliberately manufactured "bottom-up" pole of a dialectic whose "top-down" pole was Fabian technocratic planning.[1]

## Origins

The doctrine descends from the nineteenth-century writers John Ruskin and William Morris, whom Ehret characterizes as British imperialists who advocated guild socialism under a system of world control.[1] [[alfred-richard-orage|Alfred Richard Orage]] became its leading promoter after breaking with the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] in 1912, abruptly rejecting all state planning and arguing that anarchistic bottom-up self-organization would emerge across the earth if people were left free to act as they wished, moderated only by medieval trade guilds and free markets.[1] Orage paired this with a demand for artisanal, low-technology production and a "back-to-nature" ethos that Ehret says was later revived in the modern environmental movement, and which travelled under the alternative name of anarcho-syndicalism.[1]

## A manufactured dialectic

The London Fabian Society founded The New Statesman in 1913 to promote top-down technocratic planning in direct opposition to Orage's New Age, and [[walter-lippmann|Walter Lippmann]] founded the American counterpart, The New Republic, in 1914.[1] Ehret argues that the opposition was staged, since the other leading guild-socialist theorists working with Orage — George Douglas Howard Cole, Samuel George Hobson and Arthur Joseph Penty — were themselves Fabians and followers of Ruskin and Morris.[1]

[[bertrand-russell|Bertrand Russell]] endorsed the system in *Proposed Roads to Freedom*, calling pure anarchism "the ultimate ideal" while judging guild socialism "the best practicable system," a federation among trades analogous to federalism among nations.[1] Ehret reads Russell's support as concerned not with freedom but with control, since the doctrine would dissolve sovereign nation states into a chaos justifying a top-down world-government response.[1] He compares this to modern populations content with local control of a fifteen-minute city while unable to influence the wider system.[1]

## Modern echo

Ehret notes that on 23 June 2019 [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] tweeted "Accelerating Starship development to build the Martian Technocracy," followed hours later by "Anarcho-Syndicalism FTW!!" — a pairing he treats as the same joke Russell was making when he simultaneously endorsed anarcho-syndicalism and a League of Nations world security order, and comparable to Musk's self-description as a socialist in 2018.[1] He offers [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]]'s simultaneous promotion of libertarianism and membership of the Bilderberg steering committee as a parallel case.[1]
