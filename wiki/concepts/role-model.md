# Role Model

The **role model** is the central mechanism of child development described by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in "Parenting For Freedom" (May 12, 2023).[1] Unlike animals, Deschamps argues, people must learn from an example — a role model — how to live life properly.[1]

## Characteristics

A healthy role model wants to be there for the child and to interact in a healthy way, remaining curious about the child's thoughts, emotions, experiences, and dreams; encouraging when needed, disciplined when called for, and ever protective.[1] Deschamps compares the role model to a wise driver: a car may be in tip-top condition, but without a competent driver it will be destroyed in no time.[1] Role models provide the guidance that allows children to drive their lives successfully, avoiding pitfalls and surmounting obstacles.[1]

## Social nutrition

Just as children need healthy food to keep their bodies disease-free, Deschamps writes, they also need "social food" and "parenting nutrients" to be mentally, emotionally, and physically healthy.[1] He illustrates the consequence of deprivation with the image of a dog locked in a closet from birth, which emerges extremely dysfunctional, easily manipulated, and unable to achieve its full potential — and argues children are no different.[1] Children worldwide, he claims, suffer intense spiritual, emotional, and mental malnutrition: their bellies are full of food, albeit of poor quality, while they remain empty of the guidance, support, and encouragement they need most.[1]

## Modeling and transmission

Parents and what Deschamps calls "parenting allies" — the community — model behaviour for children continuously.[1] Children quietly and subconsciously record and catalog adult responses to life's difficulties, drawing on them when they encounter similar situations themselves.[1] Role models who encounter challenges and grow from them teach children that transcendence and success are possible regardless of circumstance.[1] Adults who have faced the truth of their own poor parenting and cultural conditioning, and who courageously choose to fight on and recover what was lost, can in turn show others the way.[1]

The role model concept underpins the article's treatment of the [[zone-of-proximal-development|zone of proximal development]], [[courage-and-radical-acceptance|courage and radical acceptance]], and the [[nuclear-family]].[1]
