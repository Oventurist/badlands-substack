# Gettysburg Address

The Gettysburg Address was delivered by [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] on November 19, 1863, near the end of the American Civil War.[1] In it Lincoln memorialized those who had died defending the Union by exhorting that through their sacrifice, "government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth."[1]

## Influence on China

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies this tricolon as the direct inspiration for [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]]'s 1924 tract [[three-principles-of-the-people|Three Principles of the People]], in which Sun laid out the corresponding principles of *minzu*, *minquan* and *minsheng*.[1] Sun restated the formula almost verbatim in his first lecture on nationalism, describing "a state belonging to all the people, a government controlled by all the people, and rights and benefits for the enjoyment of all the people."[1]

The same phrase was invoked by [[henry-wallace|Henry A. Wallace]] in his 1944 book *Our Job in the Pacific*, where he dated the march of the peoples of the East to the 1911 Chinese revolution inspired by Sun's teachings, which he said set out "courageously toward the attainment of democracy – government of the people, by the people and for the people."[1]
