# Bitcoin 2024 Nashville Conference

The Bitcoin 2024 conference, held in Nashville, Tennessee, in late July 2024, became a significant campaign event when Republican presidential nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] delivered its keynote address on Saturday, July 27, 2024.[1]

## Trump's keynote

Trump told hundreds of cheering cryptocurrency supporters that he intended to embrace [[bitcoin|bitcoin]] wholeheartedly in a second term, saying he wanted the United States to become a "bitcoin superpower" and the "crypto capital of the planet."[1] He pledged to create a bitcoin "strategic reserve" built from the holdings the federal government already controls, to remove Securities and Exchange Commission chair [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]] if elected, and to establish a crypto advisory council — teasing the crowd by asking who wanted to join.[1] "We will have regulations, but from now on the rules will be written by people who love your industry, not hate your industry," he said.[1]

## Badlands reaction

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the Nashville remarks as "some of the most SIGNAL" Trump had ever put out, singling out his aside about "My Uncle, Dr. John Trump. He would have fit in this room very nicely ... that's why I mention it."[1] The commentary framed bitcoin adoption and the "Awakening" as the same movement — inviting both crypto maximalists and anons onto "the same train" — and connected Tesla the inventor, Tesla the company, bitcoin and an "American Renaissance" as parts of a plan larger than commonly understood.[1] The writer argued that after a decade spent tearing down a world of falsity, the coming period would require Americans to build, and that Trump "is nothing if not a Builder."[1]

## Trump's Bitcoin stockpile pledge

Speaking at the conference, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he wanted the US government to stockpile [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]], using it to protect the US dollar and the American economy, and that he wanted Bitcoin to be produced domestically in the United States.[2] Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cited the remarks as evidence that Trump had become a genuine backer of Bitcoin and was partnering with leaders in [[big-tech|Big Tech]] and cryptocurrency ahead of a possible return to office.[2] Carlson linked the pledge to a broader thesis that America must produce Bitcoin in-house and build up domestic [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] capacity or risk "a digital invasion."[2]
