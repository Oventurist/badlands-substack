# Juliana v. United States

Juliana v. United States was a long-running climate-change lawsuit that ended in March 2025 when the U.S. Supreme Court denied the plaintiffs' petition for certiorari, concluding litigation the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] had defended across three presidential administrations.[1]

## Procedural history

The case, filed in 2015, sought to have the courts force the federal government to implement more stringent, remedial measures related to climate change.[1] The U.S. District Court for the District of Oregon agreed to hear the case; the Justice Department moved to dismiss and sought an interlocutory appeal with the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals.[1] The Ninth Circuit remanded the case to the district court with instructions to dismiss.[1] The district court then accepted an amended complaint, and the circuit court again instructed that the case be dismissed.[1] The plaintiffs subsequently filed a petition for certiorari in the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], which was denied.[1]

## Justice Department response

Acting Assistant Attorney General Adam Gustafson of the Justice Department's Environment and Natural Resources Division (ENRD) said: "For nearly a decade, lawyers for the plaintiffs in the Juliana case have tied up the United States in litigation, persisting even after the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals twice instructed the case to be dismissed because the plaintiffs lack Article III standing."[1] He added that the cert denial "brings this long saga to a conclusion" and that through ENRD's work the Department is enforcing the nation's environmental laws and safeguarding America's air, water, and natural resources, while "cases like Juliana distract from those enforcement efforts."[1]
