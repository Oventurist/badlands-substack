# Hunter Biden v. Giuliani

Hunter Biden v. Giuliani is a civil lawsuit filed on 26 September 2023 by [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] against [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], alleging that the former attorney to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] violated his privacy rights by illegally disseminating content taken from his infamous laptop.[1]

## The complaint

The complaint was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California and states that Giuliani is "primarily responsible" for the "total annihilation" of Biden's digital privacy.[1] It also names [[robert-costello|Robert Costello]], a former federal prosecutor who had previously represented Giuliani, as a defendant.[1]

Biden's attorneys wrote that "[f]or the past many months and even years, Defendants have dedicated an extraordinary amount of time and energy toward looking for, hacking into, tampering with, manipulating, copying, disseminating, and generally obsessing over data that they were given that was taken or stolen from Plaintiff's devices or storage platforms, including what Defendants claim to have obtained from Plaintiff's alleged 'laptop' computer."[1] The filing claims the data did not come from a laptop at all but from an "external drive."[1] The contents of that drive — pictures, videos, emails and other data — had, since their initial publication by the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] in 2020, placed Biden in legal jeopardy and caused political problems for his father, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] The case was reported by [[fox-news|Fox News]].[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the suit as "Overton's Goalpost in full effect," arguing that Hunter Biden was not yet ready to concede that Giuliani's account of how he obtained the data was true, but was willing to concede that the data had been "hacked."[1] Burning Bright laid out a progression of narrative positions — deny (the laptop doesn't exist or wasn't mine), diminish (nothing important was on it), qualify (nothing incriminating was on it), distract (it was illegal to scrape the data), and finally admit.[1]

He further speculated that if Hunter Biden had flipped on his family and the broader [[deep-state|Deep State]] before or after the recovery of the [[hunter-biden-laptop|laptop]], the episode looked more like a deliberate data dump on the Biden family and the "System of Systems" than a fortuitous mistake, and suggested the lawsuit could be the "'Go' order on the next phase of Disclosure."[1]
