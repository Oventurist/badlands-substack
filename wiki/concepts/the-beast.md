# The Beast

**The Beast** is the figure introduced in Revelation 13 and, in the Badlands Media essay "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?", is read less as an individual than as a system of power and control.[1]

## Relationship to the Antichrist

The article notes that many people use "the Beast" and "[[the-antichrist|the Antichrist]]" interchangeably, and distinguishes the Antichrist — a specific figure primarily associated with the New Testament — from the broader condition of being "anti-Christ" described in 1 and 2 John, while conceding the Antichrist would undoubtedly be anti-Christ.[1] Paul's "son of lawlessness" in 2 Thessalonians is presented as the Antichrist: one who opposes God, exalts himself, and performs deceitful signs and wonders to deceive, whose power is temporary and whose defeat is certain.[1]

## Old Testament antecedents

Although the Antichrist is not explicitly named in the Old Testament, the article points to two passages read as descriptions of the figure.[1] Daniel's visions include a beast and oppressive ruler who gathers forces and defiles the sanctuary, replacing sacrifices with abomination; many are corrupted by flattery while those who know God remain strong, face persecution, and accomplish great feats; the ruler exalts himself, speaks blasphemies, worships a foreign god, conquers nations, and prospers until the appointed time of wrath, whereupon the Ancient of Days arrives, passes judgment, removes his dominion, and grants the everlasting kingdom to the saints (Daniel 7:19–27, 9:24–27, 11:31–45).[1] Ezekiel's message to the prince of Tyre concerns a mere man who exalted himself as a god and whose pride and wealth bring foreign nations to destroy him (Ezekiel 28:1–19).[1] The article observes that both Testaments picture believers present in the world as these events unfold.[1]

## Reading the Beast as a system

Regardless of its exact makeup, or whether it can be identified with a single face, the author concludes the Beast symbolises a system of power and control aligned with Satan that positions itself as God and demands His worship.[1] Because the personifications in scripture are likely attempts to describe the spiritual nature of Satan, he declines to invest much effort in identifying "who," noting there are many human faces.[1]

Applied to the present, he argues there is no shortage of data to support the conclusion that globalist, communist, technocratic, warmongering, perverted, lying "god-kings" have declared themselves rulers of the world and demanded complete worship in the form of compliance.[1] If they are not the Beast referenced, he writes, they are arguably the closest thing seen to date.[1] The Beast's defining action is the imposition of the [[mark-of-the-beast|Mark of the Beast]], and the fall of [[babylon-revelation|Babylon]] is read as a more detailed look into the same Beast system during [[the-tribulation|the Tribulation]].[1]

## "Death Blossom" (November 2022)

In "Death Blossom," published at Badlands Media on November 23, 2022, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the Beast as his controlling metaphor for the globalist "System of Systems" that the Fifth-Generation War is being fought against.[2] He described the Beast as trapped in a ravine at the edge of the world — tired, bleeding from many wounds, harried by hounds and shadowed by hunters from the cliffs — and therefore paranoid, reactive and "more prone to lunging and swiping and biting than of thinking."[2] Its movements are fast and its attacks erratic, and every outward move it manages to action only pushes it one step closer to going over the edge or collapsing in the bloody trenches it has dug in the ice and snow.[2]

Central to the essay is the observation that the Beast remains lethal precisely because it is dying: while it knows it is doomed, to the hounds nearest its snapping maw and splitting claws its power appears magnified as it approaches its inevitable ending.[2] Burning Bright identified the Beast explicitly as the System of Systems spanning the Political, Narrative, Cultural and Social domains, and argued that those systems must first be recognized for what they are before they can be taken down.[2] The essay's title refers to the indiscriminate, all-directions expenditure of psychological, narrative and kinetic ammunition that he read as the signature of a cornered enemy — the reason writers at Badlands Media describe the globalist side as the "Finite" or losing player in the conflict.[2]
