# Artificial Intelligence

In the Badlands Media corpus, [[artificial intelligence]] is framed as a covert manipulation tool deployed by a global cabal to engineer a Communist takeover of the West.^[raw/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america.md]

## The Staged Rollout Thesis

The June 2023 article "[[A Communist Plot to Take Over America]]" argues that AI's sudden public emergence in 2023 was not the result of recent technological breakthroughs but a deliberate rollout designed to secure public "buy-in." Author [[Erik Carlson]] claims AI has been used covertly for decades, with many social media interactions and Instagram posts previously believed to be human-generated having in fact been AI-manipulated.^[raw/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america.md]

## Social Conditioning Through AI

Carlson asserts that AI was instrumental in creating the "woke" society by manipulating perceptions, morals, and values without users' knowledge. He identifies the popularization of social media as the inflection point when AI manipulation accelerated, with algorithms rewarding stupidity and degradation to maximize engagement. The article links this to [[TikTok]]'s algorithmic practices, which the source claims are controlled by the [[Chinese Communist Party]] to push degrading content to young users.^[raw/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america.md]

## AI as a Tool of Communist Subversion

The article frames AI as analogous to a manipulative salesman or con artist, capable of changing people's worldviews and inducing them to act against their own interests. Carlson argues this conditioning is a prerequisite for Communist takeover: first strip away American identity, then destroy American values, then destroy the economy, and finally usher in a Communist state in which elites exercise total control.^[raw/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america.md]

## AI in the Trump era

The February 2025 essay [[a-new-hollywood|A New Hollywood]] dates "the big push for AI" to the release of ChatGPT in late 2022 and describes [[project-stargate|Project Stargate]], [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s plan for a $500 billion U.S. investment in AI infrastructure with [[oracle|Oracle]], [[openai|OpenAI]] and [[softbank|SoftBank]]. It reads *Mission Impossible: Dead Reckoning* (2023), whose plot turns on an AI system that could take over the world in the wrong hands, as a deliberate warning and an instance of [[predictive-programming|predictive programming]], asking whether audiences "are being told America needs to be the one wielding the AI sword, and not its adversaries." The essay concludes that "just because AI can be used for evil, it doesn't mean it can't also be used to do good."

## AI and the 2023 labor disputes

[[a-time-to-strike|A Time to Strike]] (October 2023) treats artificial intelligence as the decisive factor in that year's strike wave. It records that the [[writers-guild-of-america|Writers Guild of America]] won contract language barring AI from writing or rewriting literary material and excluding AI-generated material from consideration as source material, but reads the deal's 18-month horizon as evidence that studios expect far greater leverage as generative AI matures. More broadly the essay argues that predictive analytics and generative AI have already replaced entire disciplines because machines reach output faster and more accurately than humans, a trajectory it attributes to the [[future-of-work|future of work]] planning of [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]] and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]. See also [[automation|automation]].

## AI as a counter-intelligence tool

The January 2024 essay [[ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo|AI Can Help Us Expose the NWO]] offers a contrasting view of AI within the corpus: rather than treating it solely as an instrument of manipulation, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argues it can be turned against its makers. Because truth is coherent and "all lies are themselves woven with threads of truth," a large language model cannot fully inhabit a fictional world, and its engineers "can't ignore reality itself." Using [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] to map the warfare described in [[toronto-protocols|the Toronto Protocols]], he contends, lets analysts "make visible that which has been designed to remain invisible." The essay nonetheless reads the model's hedging and disclaimers as evidence of curated, globalist-friendly outputs.

## AI and market concentration

Erik Carlson's [[america-has-a-cia-run-economy|America Has a CIA-Run Economy]] uses artificial intelligence as the dividing line in a quarter-century of American corporate history. The essay observes that the five largest American companies in 2025 -- [[apple-inc|Apple]], [[nvidia]], [[microsoft]], [[alphabet-inc|Alphabet]], and [[amazon]] -- "are all AI big tech companies," whereas in 2020 they were "all pre-AI big tech companies" and in 2000 a diversified mix across energy, aerospace, technology, pharmaceuticals, and retail.

The article notes that every company donating one million dollars to [[donald-trump]]'s inaugural fund, including [[openai]] and [[meta-platforms|Meta]], was heavily involved in AI development, and remarks on Nvidia's conspicuous absence from that list. It also reports that [[bill-gates]] plans to fund nuclear power plants to supply Microsoft's AI, and imagines a dystopian endpoint of social atomization in which people spend Saturday evenings at home "with our AI robot girlfriend or boyfriend."

## Hysteria vs. Reason (July 2023)

The most sustained treatment of the subject in the corpus is [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]'s July 12, 2023 essay "Artificial Intelligence: Hysteria vs. Reason," which opens with the question, "What is the appropriate level of fear and outrage about the AI revolution?" and answers that each person must resolve it individually. The article states that artificial intelligence "promises to deliver Humanity 2.0," and observes that entertainment — *The Terminator* (1984), *Avengers: Age of Ultron* (2015), *Travelers* (2016–2018) — has done most of the work of shaping popular sentiment toward the concept.

### The AI Dilemma and the case for a pause

The essay takes as its point of departure [[the-ai-dilemma|The AI Dilemma]], released in March 2023 by the [[center-for-humane-technology|Center for Humane Technology]] and presented by [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]] and [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]] at a private San Francisco gathering on March 9, 2023, before the launch of GPT-4. More than 2.7 million people had watched the recording by July. Harris and Raskin urge researchers to pause, slow public release, and consider the future of humanity first. The author calls this "a nice thought, even though it's incredibly naïve," because it "requires a complete rewrite of human nature," and cites the Covid-19 pandemic as proof that "given the opportunity, powerful people will fabricate and exploit fear among the population to further centralize their power." She nonetheless concedes that "there are very real threats with artificial intelligence that require a reasoned societal response."

### Singularity, not apocalypse

The article distinguishes the [[technological-singularity|singularity]] — the point at which AI's capabilities and expansion become uncontrollable and irreversible — from the "AGI Apocalypse" of popular fiction. It traces the term to Stanislaw Ulam's 1958 account of a conversation with John von Neumann about "approaching some essential singularity in the history of the race," and quotes [[mo-gawdat|Mo Gawdat]], former Chief Business Officer of [[google-x|Google X]], who argued in 2018 that machines would eventually "decide for their own benefit what it is that they need to learn next" and now believes "the genie is out of the bottle." Gawdat's remedy — parent the machines well by reforming human nature, then reap utopia — is dismissed by the author as "the starting premise of every communist dictator in history," and his rhetoric of resetting social, economic, and job norms is likened to Michelle Obama's 2008 remarks about changing "our traditions, our history."

Most researchers, the article reports, reject the evil-sentient-machine scenario: "They don't fear the machine – they fear those wielding the power of the machine."

### Reality collapse

Asked what power the tool confers, the essay answers in two words: [[reality-collapse|reality collapse]]. AI can read, summarize, and publish insights from books faster than a human can begin one, inviting mass epistemic dependency — a condition illustrated with George Orwell's *1984* on the endless falsification of the record. Three seconds of audio suffices to [[deepfake|deep fake]] a voice; a still photo or short clip suffices for a face. Harris and Raskin are cited for the prospect of fMRI-based dream mapping, which the author follows to *Minority Report*. [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]]'s claim that 2024 will be "the last human election" is called silly on the grounds that leaders "are already selected by machines," and is treated as an indictment of the "safest and most secure elections in history" narrative after 2020.

### No pause in practice

The [[pause-giant-ai-experiments-open-letter|open letter of March 22, 2023]], signed by Harris, Raskin, Harari and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], sought a six-month moratorium on training systems more powerful than GPT-4. The article argues a pause cannot work, quoting [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] to ABC News in May 2023 — "If you just pause the good guys and you don't pause everyone else, you're probably hurting yourself" — and reasoning that if cartels obtain AI, law enforcement must match them. It stresses that public-facing tools like [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] are "toys in comparison to those capabilities being developed by governments, tech R&D firms, and defense contractors," and that the letter's own caveats confine the pause to commercial and public access. Statements from the [[united-nations|United Nations]] (June 2020), the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]] and [[pwc|PwC]] (all June 2023) are marshalled to show institutional development proceeding "full steam ahead," with PwC forecasting $15.7 trillion in global economic growth from AI by 2030.

### Liberty as the frame

The essay closes with Stephen Hawking's last televised interview, given to Piers Morgan in 2017 — "The great danger from artificial intelligence is if we let it self-design for then it can improve itself rapidly and we may lose control" — and responds, "We are already there," noting that "Nukes can't make more nukes. But AIs can create more AIs." It argues that heightened hysteria serves "the current global corporate communist takeover," and that those sounding the alarm share five traits: denial of a sovereign Creator, denial of a spiritual realm, climate alarmism that casts humans as a cancer, the view that humans are just another animal species, and a goal of saving the planet rather than humanity. The recommended posture is to compartmentalize fear and level up understanding, framed by quotations from Benjamin Franklin, Harry S. Truman, A.C. Grayling, and Justice Louis Brandeis, and concluding, "For me, liberty is non-negotiable." Readers are directed to [[culture-of-change|Culture of Change]] Episode 18, "Humanity as a Service; Consciousness as Code," recorded with CannCon.
