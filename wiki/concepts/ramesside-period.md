# Ramesside Period

The **Ramesside Period** (c. 1292 B.C.E. to 1189 B.C.E.) spans the 19th and 20th Egyptian dynasties within the New Kingdom era.[1] It is noted for its grandeur, military might, and monumental building programs, and its close is a dramatic turning point in Egyptian history — a fracturing of the kingdom and the end of Egyptian imperial power.[1]

## 2025 Luxor tomb discovery

In May 2025 an all-Egyptian research team announced the discovery of three tombs of prominent statesmen at Dra' Abu el-Naga on the West Bank of [[luxor|Luxor]], reported through the Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities and the [[supreme-council-of-antiquities|Supreme Council of Antiquities]].[1] The first tomb was dated to the Ramesside Period and belonged to Amum-em-Ipet, who worked in either the temple or the estate of Amun; although much of the tomb had been destroyed, the team could still make out depictions of a banquet, offerings, and funeral furniture carriers.[1]

## Commentary and the Exodus

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted that many scholars place the Biblical Exodus in the 13th century B.C., contemporaneous with the newly discovered tomb, and drew a parallel between the plagues of Exodus and the "Wrath of God" prophecies described in Revelation and the writings of the Old Testament prophets.[1] The commentary framed the discovery of a funeral-appointed tomb from that era as a possible symbolic notice — "Let My People Go" — rather than coincidence.[1]
