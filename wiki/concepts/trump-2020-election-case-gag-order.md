# Trump 2020 Election Case Gag Order

The Trump 2020 election case gag order was a restriction imposed in October 2023 by U.S. District Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]] on the public statements of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the federal 2020 election conspiracy prosecution brought by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]].[1]

## Terms of the order

Earlier in October 2023, Chutkan ordered Trump not to make posts or statements regarding possible witnesses, Smith, Smith's family, or court staff, while permitting general statements about the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and about Washington itself.[1] Prosecutors argued that Trump's social media posts could incite violence or contaminate the jury pool; Trump's attorneys countered that such an order would violate his right to free speech, particularly given his status as a leading candidate in the 2024 presidential election.[1]

## Temporary lift and Trump's response

Chutkan subsequently granted Trump a temporary hold on her own ruling while his legal team appealed the order as unconstitutional.[1] Days after that hold took effect, Trump criticized Smith on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], calling him "deranged" and accusing his team of leaking to the New York Times a story about Australian businessman [[anthony-pratt|Anthony Pratt]] claiming Trump had told him classified secrets; Trump denied those reports.[1]

Trump also wrote that "there is NO WAY I can get a fair trial on a Biden, Election Interference Indictment, in D.C.," adding that if the case were allowed to proceed — which he said legal scholars considered doubtful — he would demand a change of venue at the appropriate time.[1]

## En banc rejection, January 2024

On January 23, 2024, the [[dc-circuit-court-of-appeals|D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals]] rejected [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s requests for reconsideration of its decision largely upholding the partial gag order in the Washington, D.C. election-subversion case.[2] The court issued a pair of one-page orders denying petitions for a rehearing before the original three-judge panel and for en banc review before the full court.[2] Such en banc petitions are often the last step before federal appellants seek [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] review, and a Trump spokesman did not immediately respond to a request for comment.[2]

Badlands commentary from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] held that the order's practical effect was to strengthen Trump politically, given that the panel consisted of two Obama appointees and one Biden appointee and that restricting his speech on the campaign trail directly illustrated his own claim about the weaponization of the Justice Department.[2]
