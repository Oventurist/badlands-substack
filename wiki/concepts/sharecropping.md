# Sharecropping

Sharecropping is an arrangement in which a landowner hires people to work his land and, as payment, allows the workers to keep the yield of a small portion of what the land produces.[1] It emerged in the American South with the ending of legalized slavery after the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]].[1]

## Contrast with chattel slavery

Under legalized slavery, Badlands Media commentary argues, it was in the slave owner's interest to keep slaves healthy enough to work, because a slave was an investment purchased for a sizable upfront fee and treated as property for life; the owner bore responsibility for shelter, food and care once a slave could no longer work through injury or age.[1] Sharecropping removed both the upfront cost and the responsibility: if a sharecropper died in the field, another worker could simply be hired, making the arrangement, in the author's characterization, a win-win for former slave owners.[1]

## Scope and consequences

The article stops short of calling all Southern landowners evil, but describes sharecropping as an opportunity to reap rewards without responsibilities in a country decimated by civil war, and as a system that opened the door to enslaving more of the population — noting that two-thirds of sharecroppers at the time were White.[1] For most former slaves, it concludes, life after abolition became harder and more cruel rather than enriched.[1]
