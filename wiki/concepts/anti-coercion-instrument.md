# Anti-Coercion Instrument

The Anti-Coercion Instrument is a [[european-union|European Union]] trade mechanism designed to allow the bloc to retaliate against third countries that use economic pressure to coerce EU member states into policy changes.[1]

## First invocation against the United States (January 2026)

In the aftermath of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s January 2026 Davos remarks on [[greenland|Greenland]] — including his statement that Europe could "say no, and we will remember" — French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] floated the Anti-Coercion Instrument for the first time against the United States.[1] The move accompanied the [[european-parliament|EU Parliament]] trade committee's indefinite freeze of a US trade deal on grounds of "coercion" and "territorial integrity," and Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]]'s statement that the bloc was "prepared to act."[1]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the invocation as part of a European response that amounted to bluster and posture rather than leverage, noting that markets shrugged and that Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] openly described the EU as "not taken completely seriously" by world leaders.[1]
