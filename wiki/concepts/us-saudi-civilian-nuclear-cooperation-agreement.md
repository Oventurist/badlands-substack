# US–Saudi Civilian Nuclear Cooperation Agreement

The US–Saudi civilian nuclear cooperation agreement is a draft accord under which the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration has tentatively agreed to permit [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to enrich uranium in support of its civilian nuclear program, according to CNN reporting citing sources familiar with the matter and documents it reviewed.[1] The agreement remains unsigned by the president despite U.S.–Saudi negotiations concluding in October 2025, and has not been submitted to [[congress|Congress]] for the review required after presidential signature.[1]

## Safeguards omissions

CNN reported that the draft would not require Saudi Arabia to adopt the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]]'s Additional Protocol, an enhanced safeguards framework intended to strengthen international nuclear inspections.[1] Reuters previously reported that critics argue the framework omits both the Additional Protocol and the longstanding U.S. "gold standard" commitment under which partner countries forgo domestic uranium enrichment and spent-fuel reprocessing.[1]

## Delay and opposition

Unnamed sources told CNN the ongoing war with [[iran|Iran]] contributed to the delay in presidential approval, while another source cited concerns about possible bipartisan congressional opposition; neither explanation has been publicly confirmed by the White House.[1] The proposal has drawn criticism from Democratic lawmakers and nonproliferation advocates, while the administration says the agreement would strengthen strategic and economic ties while complying with applicable legal safety requirements.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the story the least surprising headline in months, saying Badlands had tracked it for years.[1] He wrote that Saudi Arabia will obtain nuclear power plants and will need enrichment to fuel them, and said he was untroubled by the prospect because he understands what drives Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1] The commentary recalled that MBS told Bret Baier during the Biden administration that he wanted both nuclear power and nuclear weapons; that Trump told MBS in Riyadh he would help Saudi Arabia become a nuclear power; and that during MBS's September White House visit he signed the documents and treaties needed to legally possess nuclear technology under international rules, with Trump publicly promising at a high-profile dinner to help realize those ambitions.[1]

## Abraham Accords conditionality, July 2026

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States was prepared to move forward with the civilian nuclear cooperation agreement only if Saudi Arabia eventually joined the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and normalized relations with [[israel|Israel]], tying together two tracks that had previously been pursued separately.[2] Saudi officials replied that normalization remains contingent on meaningful progress toward a Palestinian state and an end to the Gaza conflict, and that Riyadh's conditions had not changed.[2]

A Saudi source, responding to the linkage, said the nuclear arrangement "was signed. So nothing to renegotiate," adding that "tweets don't overturn signed agreements," while the Saudi Energy Ministry released its own statement.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he did not believe Trump intended to enforce the ultimatum, reading it as posturing for Zionist constituencies, and noted that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] does not need the United States to build reactors: it has already received proposals from the South Korean contractor KEPCO and a Japanese company and intends to build two plants regardless, with US firms merely its preferred partner.[2]

## Israeli opposition (2025)

In April 2025 Saudi state media, citing US Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]], reported that Washington was edging closer to a landmark energy partnership with Riyadh that could pave the way for commercial nuclear development in the kingdom.[3] Israeli opposition leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] rejected any deal permitting uranium enrichment on Saudi soil, saying Israel must demand an explicit prohibition and recalling that his government had taken the same position in 2021 and 2022.[3] Badlands commentary noted that opposition to Saudi enrichment spanned the whole Israeli political spectrum, including opponents of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], and contrasted it with [[israeli-nuclear-program|Israel's own undeclared nuclear program]].[3]
