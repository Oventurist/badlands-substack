# Atrocity Propaganda

Atrocity propaganda is the deliberate circulation of accounts of enemy cruelty — real, exaggerated or fabricated — in order to generate public support for war.[1]

## Function in the war economy

Badlands Media treats atrocity propaganda as the ignition mechanism of the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial-congressional complex]]. Despite rising public awareness of the complex's exploits, [[dwight-eisenhower|Dwight Eisenhower]]'s farewell warning "falls on deaf ears every time a new war with a good marketing campaign comes along," because all it takes is "a few traumatic rounds of atrocity propaganda" before the public rallies behind what the publication calls a war-economy cash cow.[1]

## Delivery system

The effectiveness of atrocity propaganda depends on the [[military-industrial-media-complex|military-industrial-media complex]] — the commercially controlled press that Badlands Media argues is wedded, through ownership and contracting, to the interests of the [[permanent-war-economy|permanent war economy]].[1] Programmes such as [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]] are cited as evidence of long-standing intelligence penetration of that press.[1]

## Case: the War on Terror

Badlands Media points to the [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]] as a worked example: Americans were told the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]] occurred because the attackers "hated our freedoms," a rationale the publication attributes to neoconservatives such as [[bill-kristol|Bill Kristol]] and [[david-frum|David Frum]], and contrasts with [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]]'s stated grievance over decades of US violence in the Middle East.[1]

## Israel–Palestine, 2023

The article applies the same lens to the 2023 Israel–Palestine conflict, arguing that establishment forces want the public "hungry for this war" and may not care which side a person takes so long as they pick one.[1] The manufactured binary — support Israel or support Palestine — is itself described as a propaganda structure, producing enthusiastic constituencies for war on both flanks of the political spectrum.[1]

## Carlson's mechanism of propaganda (October 2023)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] set out a four-step model of how atrocity propaganda works as a psychological tool: an outrageous claim unsupported by evidence is published; anyone declining to accept it immediately is attacked and silenced; the claim is eventually shown to be baseless; but the emotions it generated persist and are layered under the next false claim.[2] Recipients may know the claims were untrue yet remain driven by the residue of feeling, and often defend the people who deceived them with formulas such as "their claims were wrong, but their hearts were in the right place."[2] Carlson suggested that most people who hate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] cannot say why, the hatred being an accumulated residue of false claims.[2]

He listed historical cases: German soldiers accused of killing babies in the First World War; the Nazis accused of making lampshades from skin and soap from the fat of murdered Jews in the Second; Iraqi soldiers accused of throwing Kuwaiti babies from hospital incubators in the first Gulf War; and Iraq and Afghanistan accused of holding weapons of mass destruction in the second.[2] He stressed he was not defending any of those parties, only noting that propaganda succeeded against them and still colours perception.[2] To these he added the [[covid-19-narrative|Covid]] era — the virus as a death sentence, alternative treatments as poison, the vaccines as 100% safe and effective — and the claim that Ukraine was winning its war with Russia.[2]

Carlson identified two lasting harms. Propaganda is used to justify actions that cannot be undone once the accusations collapse: "One couldn't take back the bombing of Bagdad, or the vaccines that were administered."[2] And attacks launched on knowingly false claims manufacture genuine hatred — a person who loses their family to a drone strike justified by a lie may become an actual terrorist.[2]

## Application to October 2023

Carlson applied the model directly to the opening weeks of the [[israel-hamas-war|Israel–Hamas conflict]], citing the reports of hundreds of decapitated Israeli babies and of hundreds of Palestinians killed by an Israeli strike on the [[al-ahli-arab-anglican-hospital|Al-Ahli Arab Hospital]].[2] In both cases, he wrote, the media ran the stories without proof; he judged both to be propaganda designed to escalate the conflict into a world war, one story demonising Israelis and the other demonising Palestinians.[2] Asked which side the [[deep-state|Deep State]] media wanted the public to back, his answer was that it did not care — it simply wanted a war.[2]
