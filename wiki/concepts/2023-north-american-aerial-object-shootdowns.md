# 2023 North American Aerial Object Shootdowns

The **2023 North American aerial object shootdowns** were a rapid sequence of U.S. and allied military engagements against high-altitude objects in North American airspace in early February 2023, beginning with a Chinese high-altitude balloon and followed within a week by three additional unidentified objects.[1]

## Sequence of events

A Chinese high-altitude balloon was shot down in the first week of February 2023.[1] On Friday, February 10, U.S. fighter jets were scrambled and downed an object over Alaska.[1] On Saturday, February 11, a U.S. Air Force F-22 Raptor operating under [[norad|NORAD]] control shot down an object over Canada's Yukon Territory; Canadian Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] announced the engagement and said he had spoken with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that afternoon and that Canadian forces would recover the wreckage.[1] It was the F-22's third "kill" ever, after the Chinese balloon a week earlier.[1] On Sunday, February 12, another unknown object — described as "octagonal" — was shot down over Lake Huron by an F-16, according to Michigan Representatives [[elissa-slotkin|Elissa Slotkin]] and [[jack-bergman|Jack Bergman]].[1] Slotkin wrote that "the object has been downed by pilots from the US Air Force and National Guard" and that "we're all interested in exactly what this object was and it's purpose."[1] In total, F-22s under NORAD control downed two objects within a single day and three within a week.[1]

## Transparency controversy

The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and White House released few details about the objects, prompting criticism even from establishment media.[1] [[nbc-news|NBC]]'s [[chuck-todd|Chuck Todd]] opened "Meet the Press" on February 12 by faulting the administration and [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] spokesman [[john-kirby|Adm. John Kirby]], who had not volunteered information about the Alaska shootdown until a reporter asked.[1] The "Meet the Press" account posted that in both cases "the White House and Pentagon missed opportunities for transparency."[1]

## Badlands Media interpretation

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], described the episode as occurring "not even a week after what many believe to be a failure of the Biden administration to handle the Chinese Ballon fiasco," and said the sequence "wreaks of state-sponsored media stunts."[1] He asked how prevalent such overflights had been historically and whether they had gone unremarked for years until the administration became "beset by scandals."[1] Deschamps outlined two competing readings within the Truth Community: that the shootdowns were an intentional operation to damage Biden ahead of the selection of a 2024 DNC candidate, or that they were a "white hat operation designed to expose intelligence activities that have been quietly taking place for years."[1] His conclusion was that coordinated media behavior itself signals an agenda.[1] Fellow contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] summarized his own position on the shootdowns simply as "We agree with Flynn," a reference to [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]].[1]
