# Russian Space Nuclear Weapon Program

The **Russian space nuclear weapon program** refers to an alleged Russian effort to place nuclear weapons in orbit for use against satellites, which surfaced in American public debate in February 2024 when the chairman of the House Intelligence Committee issued a cryptic public warning about a "serious national security threat."[1]

## The February 2024 disclosure

On Wednesday, February 14, 2024, [[mike-turner|Rep. Mike Turner]] (R-OH), chair of the [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]], issued a public warning of a "serious national security threat" that he said might require help from "our allies" to address.[1] Turner cited unspecified "information" about the threat that he said all members of Congress had already been briefed on, and called on President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to declassify it.[1] In his statement to House members he said he was "requesting that President Biden declassify all information relating to this threat so that Congress, the Administration, and our allies can openly discuss the actions necessary to respond."[1]

Capitol Hill sources told ABC News that the threat concerned Russia wanting to put nuclear weapons in space for use against satellites.[1] An unnamed Democratic source told NBC News that it was "a serious issue that could lead to a destabilizing situation and a national security threat," describing it only as a "potential foreign threat" without further detail.[1] CNN separately cited unnamed sources saying the threat was related to Russia and was "highly concerning and destabilizing."[1]

## Official response

House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-LA) said in a statement that there was "no need for public alarm," adding that he would press the Biden administration to take "appropriate action."[1] "Steady hands are at the wheel, we're working on it, there's no need for alarm," Johnson cautioned.[1]

At the White House press briefing on the same Wednesday, National Security Adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] appeared alongside Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] and said he had reached out to the Gang of Eight the previous week, with a meeting scheduled for Thursday.[1] Sullivan expressed puzzlement that Turner had taken threat rhetoric public before that meeting, but declined to confirm whether the meeting concerned the new threat, telling reporters: "Well, I — again, I'll — I'll leave it to you to draw whatever connections you want. All I can say is I've reached out to see Turner. Turner has gone out publicly. I'm going to go see Turner tomorrow. That's where I want to leave things for today."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as timed political theatre, arguing the warning arrived "right on cue" as pressure built for the House to pass the Senate's national security supplemental aid package, calling it "a vague, but super scary and totally not made up threat to national security."[1] She predicted the proposed solution to the vague threat would be for the House to pass the supplemental, and characterized the sequence as "political theatre intended to enhance and expand the power and resources of the criminal oligarchy."[1] The framing connects the disclosure to the broader [[uniparty]] critique advanced across the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]
