# Maricopa County 2022 Ballot on Demand Printer Investigation

The Maricopa County 2022 General Election Ballot on Demand Printer Investigation was a review conducted by [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona into the widespread failures of [[ballot-on-demand-printers|Ballot on Demand (BOD) printers]] on election day, November 8, 2022.[1] Critics characterized it as the Arizona government investigating itself in the wake of the election disaster.[1]

## Findings

The report disclosed that "Runbeck and County technical workers traveled to approximately 70 vote centers — roughly 50% — to troubleshoot the reports of problems with the [Ballot on Demand] BOD printers," referring to vendor [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]].[1]

Despite months of county denials regarding 19-inch versus 20-inch ballots, the county's own report confirmed that a change had been made prior to the election in which the ballot image was "increased in length from 19 inches to 20 inches to accommodate the number of contests."[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that this change in image size was the root cause of the widespread tabulator failures on midterm election day, and that the county had been prepared for those failures because it had the so-called "Drawer Three" contingency ready to deploy.[1] In the report the county maintained that it did not know how the change had been made and therefore could not conclude whether it amounted to intentional sabotage.[1]

## Timing

A county statement on the matter was released on January 6, 2023, two weeks after the close of the first trial in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], and the report of findings followed in April 2023 — four months after the original trial, a sequence critics questioned as more than coincidence.[1]

## Related litigation

In the first trial, Maricopa County Elections Director [[scott-jarrett|Scott Jarrett]] testified on day one that he had heard no reports of a 19-inch ballot image being placed on 20-inch ballot paper, claiming "no knowledge" of this occurring and stating, "I don't believe that that occurred," before changing his testimony the following day to say that he had learned of a fit-to-paper issue "a few days after Election Day."[1] Jordan Conradson of [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] documented the reversal.[1]
