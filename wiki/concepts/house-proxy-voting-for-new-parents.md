# House Proxy Voting for New Parents

House proxy voting for new parents was a 2025 rules dispute in the U.S. House of Representatives over allowing members who are new parents to cast votes by proxy for three months.[1]

## The discharge petition

Rep. [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]] (R-Fla.) launched a [[discharge-petition|discharge petition]], the procedural device that can force a floor vote on any measure without leadership support if 218 members sign on.[1] Luna obtained the signatures — including a dozen Republicans — meaning the vote would have to occur by the end of that week.[1]

## Defeat of leadership

On April 1, 2025, the House voted to defy Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-La.) and force the proxy-voting vote, a loss described as brutal for Johnson, who had poured considerable political capital into snuffing out Luna's effort.[1] In a rare move, Johnson had tied a provision killing the vote to unrelated Republican legislation prohibiting non-citizens from voting in federal elections.[1] Nine Republicans voted against Johnson, including Reps. Tim Burchett (R-Tenn.), Mike Lawler (R-N.Y.) and Jeff Van Drew (R-N.J.) — all signatories of Luna's petition — as well as Luna herself.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded that "[t]he Congress is fake and illegitimate" and that its stories about its own rules do not matter.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], recounting her own experience traveling for work as a new mother, argued the episode showed members of [[us-congress|Congress]] fighting hardest when their own perks are at stake — comparable to their healthcare, tax treatment and insider trading — rather than for constituents' interests.[1] She also noted that Luna's much-anticipated government secrets task force had failed to reveal a single government secret at its hearing the same day, reportedly ending early because of the proxy-voting vote.[1]
