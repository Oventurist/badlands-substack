# Thirteenth Amendment

The Thirteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution provides that "Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted, shall exist within the United States, or any place subject to their jurisdiction."[1]

In his April 27, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Economy: Weaponized," [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the punishment clause means the government can and does deprive convicted criminals of all their rights, to the extent that they may be considered slaves subject to involuntary servitude, with prisoners forced to work and forced to comply or suffer.[1] He acknowledged that well-intentioned legal scholars and debunkers assert this is the wrong way to read the amendment, but contended that those debunking efforts are in error and that the reading holds true in practice.[1]

Deschamps noted that convicted persons are deprived of nearly all choice in their lives and are often encouraged to work for pennies; while some argue this is not slavery because inmates retain a modicum of choice and earn small sums, he held that inmates have almost no ability to govern or direct their lives, which he described as one of the best tests of real freedom.[1] He further argued that the American penal system produces more criminals than it reforms, and that the best forms of justice satisfy the social demand for recompense while maintaining the dignity of the prisoner so that reform is possible.[1]
