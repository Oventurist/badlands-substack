# Order of Abdulaziz Al Saud

The Order of Abdulaziz Al Saud is Saudi Arabia's highest civilian honour, awarded in its gilded "Collar" grade to foreign heads of state.[1] It was established on March 20, 1971 by [[king-faisal|King Faisal]], half-brother of the later monarch King Salman.[1]

## 2017 award to Donald Trump

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] received the gilded Collar of the Order of Abdulaziz Al Saud from King Salman on May 20, 2017 — the same day as the [[sword-dance|Sword Dance]] in Riyadh, an event Badlands commentary treats as a hinge point in modern geopolitics.[1] Badlands framed the ceremony as a Muslim leader bestowing a Muslim accolade on a Christian leader, the mirror image of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s later conferral of the [[order-of-st-andrew-the-apostle|Order of St. Andrew the Apostle]] on Hindu Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]].[1]

## Historical framing

Badlands links the order's founding to a secret 1961 meeting between President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and then-Crown Prince Faisal, in which Kennedy argued that investing the Royal Family's wealth in its people would create a middle class and build [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] into a great world power while securing the bonds of trust between the people and the House of Saud.[1] Three years later, on becoming king, Faisal implemented a ten-point plan of social reforms including the abolition of slavery and universal healthcare funded by the Royal Family.[1] Faisal was assassinated in 1975, which Badlands attributes to the same forces held responsible for Kennedy's assassination twelve years earlier.[1] In this framing, Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] is the spiritual successor of Faisal just as Trump is the spiritual successor of Kennedy.[1]
