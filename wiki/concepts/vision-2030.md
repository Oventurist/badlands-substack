# Vision 2030

Vision 2030 is the long-range economic transformation program driven by Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], which he has been implementing for roughly eleven years as of 2026.[1] The plan requires historic levels of development and investment in the kingdom and aims to convert the economy toward tourism, hospitality and high technology.[1]

## Strategic implications

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used Vision 2030 as the central argument against claims that Saudi Arabia had secretly backed or waged war on [[iran|Iran]]: none of the plan's ambitions could be achieved if the Middle East were destroyed in a regional war.[1] In this reading, the crown prince's well-documented animus toward Iran is real but subordinate to a development agenda that requires regional stability.[1]

Achieving the economic vision, the argument continues, required partners and a security architecture capable of stabilizing the region — hence Saudi coordination with [[pakistan|Pakistan]], [[turkey|Turkey]] and Egypt and the proposed expansion of the [[saudi-pakistan-defense-pact|Saudi-Pakistan defense pact]].[1] The commentary frames Saudi Arabia's success in uniting the Muslim world behind a prosperity-based vision, rather than a destructive caliphate, as the reason the [[deep-state|Deep State]] is said to be targeting the kingdom.[1]

## Entertainment and Hollywood (2026)

Saudi investment in Hollywood is framed as part of Vision 2030's diversification agenda, encompassing the Public Investment Fund's roughly $12 billion contribution to the financing of Paramount's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, a PIF-led $55 billion acquisition of [[electronic-arts|Electronic Arts]] in 2025, full control of MBC Group, a $1 billion studio partnership with Arena SNK, domestic film production funding, festivals such as the Riyadh Comedy Festival, Soundstorm and the Red Sea International Film Festival, and theme-park IP development.[2] Saudi officials and backers describe the strategy as soft-power rebranding, entertainment for a young domestic population, and political influence in Washington.[2]


## Badlands framing, June 2026

Commenting on Saudi Arabia's role as guest of honor at the [[2026-st-petersburg-international-economic-forum|2026 St. Petersburg International Economic Forum]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that when the Middle East achieves Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s Vision and becomes a first-world society, it will "forever change the rest of the world" — drawing people toward the region rather than driving them out, ending forever wars as commonly understood, and reducing the radicalization of vulnerable populations.[3]

## Mining and Lithium Diversification (January 2025)

Saudi Arabia's January 2025 announcement of a joint venture between Aramco and the state-owned mining company [[maaden|Ma'aden]] to extract lithium was presented as aligned with Vision 2030, the initiative to reduce the kingdom's dependence on oil by developing its mining sector and other industries.[4] The partnership targeted high-concentration deposits and cost-effective direct extraction technologies, with commercial lithium production expected to begin by 2027.[4] Aramco's president of exploration and production, Nasser al-Naimi, said the venture would leverage "the world's leading upstream enterprise" with a view to meeting the kingdom's and potentially the world's projected lithium demand.[4]
