# Tower 22 Drone Attack

The Tower 22 drone attack was a one-way drone strike on a small United States outpost in northeast Jordan, near the Syrian border, during the overnight hours before Sunday, January 28, 2024. It killed three U.S. Army soldiers and injured more than thirty other service members, marking the first time American troops had been killed by enemy fire in the Middle East since the beginning of the Gaza war.[1]

## The Attack

[[us-central-command|U.S. Central Command]] confirmed in a statement on January 28 that three service members were killed and at least 34 injured in a one-way drone attack that "impacted at a base in northeast Jordan."[1] Eight of the injured were medically evacuated to receive higher-level care.[1] Two U.S. officials said the number of wounded was expected to rise as additional service members sought treatment for symptoms consistent with traumatic brain injury.[1]

Officials said the drone was fired by Iran-backed militants and appeared to have come from Syria, though which militia group specifically was responsible remained undetermined at the time of reporting.[1]

## Response

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], speaking in South Carolina on Sunday, said simply: "We shall respond."[1] The killing of three Americans at Tower 22 was described as a significant escalation of an already precarious regional situation.[1]

## Biden's decision on retaliation (January 30, 2024)

On Tuesday, January 30, 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] told reporters he had made a decision about how the United States would respond to the drone strike, answering "Yes" when asked by [[cnn|CNN]]'s Arlette Saenz whether he had decided, but declining to provide details.[2] Administration officials had feared such an outcome since the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack on Israel, because it would require a strong American response that they worried could widen the conflict.[2] Preparing to depart for a fundraising swing in south Florida, Biden reiterated that he hoped to deter escalation: "I don't think we need a wider war in the Middle East. That's not what I'm looking for."[2]

The attack was also cited in a [[cnn|CNN]] opinion column arguing the United States was already in the midst of a Middle East regional war, listing routine [[houthis|Houthi]] drone and missile strikes in the Red Sea, retaliatory U.S. and U.K. strikes in Yemen, near-daily exchanges between Israel and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon, more than 150 drone and missile attacks on U.S. troops in Iraq and Syria with responding U.S. airstrikes on Iran-backed militias, Israeli strikes on Iran-linked Syrian targets, an ISIS terrorist attack inside Iran, and reciprocal Iran–Pakistan strikes.[2]
