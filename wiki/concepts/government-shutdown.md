# Government Shutdown

A **government shutdown** is the lapse in federal appropriations that occurs when Congress fails to pass a budget or continuing resolution, suspending the operations of non-essential federal departments and furloughing large numbers of federal employees. In Badlands Media commentary the shutdown is treated less as a budgetary mechanic than as a political taboo: an event the permanent federal bureaucracy cannot permit because it would demonstrate to the public how little of the government's day-to-day activity is actually necessary.[1]

## The Badlands argument

Writing in January 2025, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that the American government sustains itself through fear and intimidation, persuading citizens that without federal management infrastructure would crumble, foreign invaders would arrive, streets would go unpoliced, the elderly would go unprovided for, and health care would collapse.[1] In this framing the government does not merely fail to deliver those protections; it manufactures many of the dangers it claims to defend against, inciting foreign and domestic enemies, producing the ill health it later treats, and — through taxation and inflation — destroying the savings capacity that would otherwise make old-age dependency on federal programs unnecessary.[1]

The shutdown is therefore dangerous to the state precisely because it is harmless to the public. If federal operations ceased for a sustained period and ordinary livelihoods went unaffected, Americans would conclude that the apparatus is oversized and that cutting it back is feasible.[1] Carlson argued this is why every credible shutdown threat produces what he described as an Armageddon narrative from establishment Republicans, neoconservatives, liberals and the media, urging rapid passage of a new budget.[1]

## The December 2024 budget fight

Carlson pointed to the pre-Christmas 2024 budget scramble as the clearest illustration. Legislators worked late into the holiday period to secure appropriations rather than simply waiting for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to take office weeks later, even though the government would have been on holiday for much of the intervening period and little substantive work was scheduled.[1] The urgency, in his reading, was the point: a month-long shutdown running into the inauguration would have coincided with the arrival of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] team and its planned elimination of federal positions and departments, and the public would have drawn the obvious inference that a government it had not needed for a month was not needed at scale afterward.[1]

## Extortion analogy

The article frames the relationship between the federal government and the citizenry through two extended analogies. The first is a high-school bully extorting lunch money for "protection," whose power collapses the moment a single payment is missed and the victim discovers the threat was hollow.[1] The second is organized crime: the Mafia extorts protection money from businesses under the same pretext, and Carlson notes that if taxes go unpaid the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] will ruin a family's life, an act he equates with the mob burning down a business.[1] He treats it as significant that 1913 saw the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the federal income tax, and the IRS in the same year.[1]

## Related propaganda expectations

Carlson predicted that each reform attempted after the inauguration would be preceded by a fear campaign: warnings that making Social Security income tax-free would collapse the [[social-security-administration|Social Security]] system, that [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]'s health overhaul would cost lives, that replacing leadership at the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]], [[fbi|FBI]], [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and [[national-security-agency|NSA]] amounted to weaponization against opponents, that new military leadership would invite war, and that defunding the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] would leave students further behind.[1] The recommended response is inoculation — telling people in advance what the propaganda will say, so that when it arrives it confirms the prediction rather than the panic.[1]
