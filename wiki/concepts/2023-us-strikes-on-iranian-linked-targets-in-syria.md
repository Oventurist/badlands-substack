# 2023 U.S. Strikes on Iranian-Linked Targets in Syria

The 2023 U.S. strikes on Iranian-linked targets in Syria were two American airstrikes carried out in eastern Syria in the early hours of Friday, October 27, 2023, against facilities used by [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps]] and its proxy forces.[1] The strikes were framed by Washington as retaliation for a flurry of recent rocket and drone attacks on American forces stationed in [[iraq]] and [[syria]].[1]

## The strikes

The operation was conducted by U.S. Air Force F-16 jets, which hit a weapons storage facility and an ammunition storage facility.[1] U.S. officials described the strikes as intended to send a strong signal to [[iran]] to rein in attacks that the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration attributed to Tehran's proxies in Syria and Iraq, while stopping short of escalating the broader conflict in the Middle East.[1] Although limited in number, the targets represented an escalation, because they involved facilities used by Iran's own forces rather than solely the militias that Tehran arms, trains, and equips.[1]

Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd J. Austin III]] characterized the operation in a statement as "precision self-defense strikes" responding to "a series of ongoing and mostly unsuccessful attacks against U.S. personnel in Iraq and Syria by Iranian-backed militia groups."[1] Austin added that the United States "does not seek conflict and has no intention nor desire to engage in further hostilities," but that the Iranian-backed attacks on U.S. forces were "unacceptable and must stop."[1]

## Context

The strikes came in the weeks following the October 7, 2023 [[hamas]] surprise attack on [[israel]], during which President Biden and his aides sought to contain the Israel–Hamas war and prevent it from spilling into a regional conflict involving Iran and its proxies in Lebanon, Syria, and Iraq.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media]] commentary in the Badlands News Brief treated the episode as an example of the difficulty of establishing motive in kinetic events. Writing as [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], the contributor argued that adversarial information operations work in two directions — reporting false actuals based on real narratives, and reporting false narratives based on real actuals — so that while kinetic events may indeed be occurring, the underlying "why" can only be inferred directionally by tracking "the whip and lash of the enemy's words."[1] The recommended heuristic was to ask what the messaging apparatus wants audiences to conclude about a given event, narrative or actual, and to act accordingly.[1]
