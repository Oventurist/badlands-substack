# 2023 DeSantis Presidential Campaign Launch

On Wednesday, May 24, 2023, Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] formally entered the 2024 Republican presidential race, filing paperwork with the Federal Election Commission and announcing his bid publicly that evening.[1]

## The Twitter Spaces event

DeSantis took the unconventional step of launching his campaign on [[twitter]] in a live conversation with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], the platform's owner.[1] The discussion was moderated by [[david-sacks|David Sacks]], a tech entrepreneur, Musk confidant and strong DeSantis supporter.[1] Musk's involvement had been quietly negotiated over several weeks by DeSantis's inner circle of political advisers and was first reported by [[nbc-news|NBC News]].[1] Musk had amassed an intense conservative following since buying Twitter and remaking it into a platform more welcoming to right-wing voices, including accounts previously banned for alleged disinformation.[1]

The campaign hoped Musk's star power would create a moment capable of altering trend lines that in preceding months had seen [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] build an early advantage in key states.[1] In the event, the rollout was widely described as glitch-riddled, stalling amid technical difficulties.[1]

## Same-day counterprogramming

As DeSantis launched, the [[washington-dc-young-republicans|Washington, D.C. Young Republicans]] unanimously endorsed Trump — an endorsement whose board included Kingsley Cortes, daughter of DeSantis PAC spokesman [[steve-cortes|Steve Cortes]], who had defected from Trump's camp earlier in May and been accused by [[steve-bannon]] of taking "thirty pieces of silver."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the central question as whether the DeSantis entry was part of a coordinated plan or a purely independent ambition, suggesting that a "faux spat" between Trump and DeSantis could bridge the gap between pro-Trump conservatives and what he called pro-RINO conservatives.[1] He argued that [[con-inc|Con, Inc.]] retains a grip on the conservative mind, that globalists engender false division by creating siloed political groups around surface-level issues, and that open, expressive conflict in the public square destroys that illusion.[1] On this reading, a public primary battle functions as a "positive psyop" whose spectators come to recognize the divide between conservative values as illusory in time for the 2024 election, while the genuine divide lies between truthful, pro-freedom values and their opposites.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] offered a simpler assessment, calling the same-day Trump endorsement "pretty indicative of how a Trump vs DeSantis primary will go."[1]
