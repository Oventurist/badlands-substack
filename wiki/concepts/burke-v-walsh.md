# Burke v. Walsh

Burke v. Walsh is a religious-liberty lawsuit filed on August 8, 2023 by Mike and Kitty Burke, a Catholic couple from [[massachusetts|Massachusetts]], against state officials after the Commonwealth denied them the ability to foster children because of their conservative Catholic beliefs on marriage and gender.[1] The couple is represented by [[becket-law|Becket Law]].[1]

## The plaintiffs

According to Becket's press release, Mike and Kitty Burke "have long wanted to become parents." Mike is an Iraq war veteran, Kitty is a former paraprofessional for special-needs children, and together they run a small business and perform music for Mass.[1] The Burkes learned early in their marriage that they would not be able to have children of their own, and began exploring foster parenthood through the state's foster care program with the hope of caring for and eventually adopting children in need of a stable, loving home.[1] They completed hours of training in 2022 and underwent extensive interviews and a home study to qualify for the program before being denied.[1]

## Context: the Massachusetts foster shortage

At the time of filing there was a shortage of foster families in Massachusetts, with over 1,500 children without foster families.[1] Becket stated that "the crisis has become so extreme that the state has resorted to housing children in hospitals for weeks on end—not because the children need medical attention, but because the Commonwealth has nowhere else to put them," concluding that "now more than ever, Massachusetts needs loving couples like the Burkes to foster children in need."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], argued that although Christians are typically "mocked and shamed for not understanding their privilege" when they claim their belief system is under attack, in some jurisdictions traditional Christian beliefs are indeed under attack.[1] She contended that "the state cannot tolerate citizens that believe in a power higher than government," that children are "the prize," and that the Burkes were denied because they would likely oppose what she termed the societal grooming of those children.[1] In her view the Commonwealth "would rather maintain a shortage of loving homes than allow the Christians to foster vulnerable kids," and she concluded that "this discrimination case must succeed."[1]
