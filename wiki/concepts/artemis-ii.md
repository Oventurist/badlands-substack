# Artemis II

Artemis II is the [[nasa|NASA]] mission that carried a crew of four astronauts around the Moon in 2026, the first crewed flight to lunar distance since the Apollo program. It is discussed in the April 6, 2026 Badlands Brief, "Jesus Walks", which reports NASA's release of the first images of Earth captured from lunar distance during the flight.

## Mission profile

The mission is designed as a flyby rather than a landing. Its four astronauts test spacecraft systems aboard the Orion capsule during the journey around the Moon before returning to Earth, and NASA describes the flight as a critical step toward future lunar landing missions under the wider Artemis program. NASA and its partners made mission tracking tools available to the public, allowing users to follow the spacecraft's trajectory and receive near real-time updates. Among the new onboard technologies is a redesigned space toilet intended for long-duration spaceflight.

## Reception in the corpus

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treats the mission's public presentation with scepticism. She notes that the toilet allegedly broke again over the weekend and earned a Space.com feature on Sunday, quoting the article's description of the crew "closing in on their historic lunar flyby — the first by a human crew in more than 53 years" while "an unusual star has risen aboard their Orion moon ship. But it's not an astronaut — it's the space toilet." The Space.com piece recounts that the device's urine function went offline on day one of the mission and was repaired by mission specialist [[christina-koch|Christina Koch]] with guidance from Mission Control.

Ashe objects to the term "moon loo" on the grounds that the toilet is not on the Moon but merely en route, "allegedly," and asks whether it is strange that plumbing is stealing the thunder of a historic flyby. She further asks why the mission does not include a landing and a moonwalk, which she would expect to be the priority "for this narrative," and jokes about wanting "a wellness check on the camera man they left behind in 1969," picturing him "like Number One from Umbrella Academy" and speculating about whether he would let the crew use his actual moon loo "or if he's still pissed off." The passage is characteristic of the corpus's broader posture of treating official spaceflight milestones as narrative products rather than settled events.

## Launch night announcement (April 1, 2026)

The Badlands Brief of April 2, 2026 reports the launch of Artemis II on the evening of April 1, quoting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s statement that "for the first time in over 50 YEARS, America is going back to the Moon!" with the crew traveling "farther into Deep Space than any human has EVER gone." Trump's post described the rocket as "among the most powerful rockets ever built." Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] asked on Badlands Daily who decided on "a sudden manned moon mission on April Fool's Day," adding that the launch video "is the most real thing to happen since we bombed Iran via video bowling and Wii Golf" — a reference to the corpus's skepticism about the presentation of both the space program and the war with [[iran|Iran]].
