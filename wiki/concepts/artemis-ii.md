# Artemis II

Artemis II is the [[nasa|NASA]] mission that in April 2026 conducted the first crewed flight around the Moon since the Apollo program.[1] The mission is designed as a flyby rather than a landing and carries four astronauts aboard the Orion spacecraft, who test spacecraft systems during the journey around the Moon before returning to Earth.[1] NASA released the first images of Earth captured from lunar distance during the flight and, with its partners, made public mission-tracking tools available for near real-time trajectory updates.[1]

## Onboard technology and the space toilet

The mission includes new onboard technologies, among them a redesigned space toilet intended for long-duration spaceflight.[1] The toilet's urine function went offline on day one of the mission and was repaired by mission specialist [[christina-koch|Christina Koch]] with guidance from Mission Control; it reportedly broke again over the Easter weekend and was featured on Space.com.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] objected to the toilet being called a "moon loo" — noting it is not on the Moon but en route — and asked whether it was strange that plumbing was "stealing all the thunder" of the first human lunar flyby in more than 53 years.[1] She further questioned why the mission does not include a landing and a moonwalk, arguing that walking on the Moon would seem a priority for the narrative.[1]

## Launch and presidential statement

The mission launched at 6:24 p.m. EST on April 1, 2026, marking the first American return to the Moon in more than 50 years.[2] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued a statement calling Artemis II "among the most powerful rockets ever built" and saying it was "launching our Brave Astronauts farther into Deep Space than any human has EVER gone," adding that America is "WINNING, in Space, on Earth, and everywhere in between — Economically, Militarily, and now, BEYOND THE STARS."[2] He closed by blessing the astronauts, [[nasa|NASA]] and the United States.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned on Badlands Daily who had decided to schedule a sudden crewed Moon mission on April Fool's Day, while praising the NASA video the president posted as "awesome."[2] She remarked that the launch was "the most real thing to happen since we bombed Iran via video bowling and Wii Golf," a reference to Badlands' [[fake-iran-war|fake Iran war]] framing, ending with the single word "Accelerate."[2]

## Return and skepticism (April 2026)

Following the flyby, the Artemis II crew splashed down and reunited with their families, footage of which was released by [[abc-news|ABC News]].[3] Coverage of the mission emphasized contributions from across the United States, including graduate students and a professor at the University of Alabama who developed software slated for use on the mission, and participants in southern [[colorado|Colorado]].[3] [[nasa|NASA]] was already planning subsequent missions beyond Artemis II as part of its broader lunar exploration goals.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the mission's messaging as the beginning of "a very long story," noting the reported timeline of roughly four days out, one day around the Moon, and three days back, and responding with a one-word "Sure."[3] She argued that instead of pragmatic justification, the public receives emotional propaganda about beating Chinese adversaries to lunar dominance and resources, asked how Moon exploration could be more accessible than exploration in Antarctica, and pointed to an estimated per-mission cost of $4.1 billion that advocates avoid publicizing because, in her view, the public would refuse it.[3]
