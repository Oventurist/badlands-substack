# Critical Infrastructure Cyber Attack Warnings

Mark Edwards, chief information security officer at Digital Health and Care Wales, predicted in June 2024 that cyber attacks on critical national infrastructure were likely to increase because of global conflict.[1] Speaking at the NHS Confed Expo in Manchester on June 12, 2024, Edwards said it was "widely acknowledged" that "the first thing that's going happen is there's going to be an increase in cyber attacks," and that those attacks "won't be to just ransom or extort money" but would aim at causing disruption and bringing systems down.[1]

His comments followed the ransomware attack on Synnovis, which disrupted pathology systems at hospitals in south east London and led to thousands of appointments being cancelled.[1] Edwards told the conference it was important to be "not only mindful of our own security but supply chain security."[1]

## February 2024 warning on Chinese pre-positioning

On Wednesday, February 7, 2024, federal agencies announced that Chinese hackers had at times secretly hidden inside United States infrastructure for as long as five years, positioned to conduct a potentially destructive cyberattack should the two countries go to war.[2] The claim appeared in a public cybersecurity advisory — among the largest and starkest of its kind — issued by six U.S. agencies together with allied cybersecurity and intelligence agencies from Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the United Kingdom.[2]

Over the preceding year U.S. officials had repeatedly warned that hackers working for [[china|China]]'s intelligence services kept gaining stealthy access to American infrastructure, fearing that such access could be converted into a destructive attack during a major conflict such as a Chinese invasion of Taiwan, which the United States has said it would resist.[2] The report named no specific victims but said the "PRC state-sponsored" hackers had targeted key infrastructure "primarily in Communications, Energy, Transportation Systems, and Waste and Wastewater Systems Sectors — in the continental and non-continental United States and its territories."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the advisory landed one day after the Cyber Infrastructure subcommittee of the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Homeland Security Committee]] held a hearing on technology-led vulnerabilities in the American water supply, much of which centered on Iran's November 2023 hack of a municipal water authority in Pennsylvania.[2] Citing the report's assessment that the campaign appeared to consist of hackers positioning themselves "purely to have the potential capability to wreak havoc across the country," she argued that these cyber threats should not be considered detached from the influx of military-aged males across porous northern and southern borders, suggesting that the bigger analytical leap would be to assume cyber capabilities and boots on the ground are not coordinated.[2]
