# Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo

Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo is the United States Supreme Court decision that ended [[chevron-deference|Chevron deference]], the doctrine under which courts deferred to federal agencies' own interpretations of ambiguous statutes.[1]

## Significance in the "Spear of Justice" Framing

An August 2025 Badlands Media essay identified Loper Bright, together with [[sec-v-jarkesy|SEC v. Jarkesy]], as the decisive change in legal terrain governing the [[administrative-state|administrative state]].[1] Where Jarkesy curtailed agencies' ability to serve as their own judge and jury in major penalty cases, Loper Bright removed the interpretive presumption that had allowed agency power to expand automatically; in the essay's systems language, the two rulings together "change the governing equations."[1]

The author argued that Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]]'s administrative-law cases were already flowing into this altered terrain, locking in interpretations that shrink agency reach — with examples including the nationwide vacatur of the [[equal-employment-opportunity-commission|EEOC]]'s expanded harassment guidance and the injunction against the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]]'s [[title-ix|Title IX]] rewrite.[1] Describing the work as "fighting in the footnotes," the essay contended that a ruling limiting one agency today becomes the foundation for dismantling an entire category of overreach tomorrow.[1] In the piece's phased escalation model, the end of Chevron deference belongs to the convergent "Tsunami" stage, in which Supreme Court terrain changes combine with discovery material from pharmaceutical, [[esg|ESG]], and censorship-collusion litigation as other states replicate the precedents.[1]
