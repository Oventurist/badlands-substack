# Trump Hush-Money Case

The Trump hush-money case is the New York state criminal prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over allegations that he falsified business records to conceal a pre-election payment to adult film actress [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]].[1]

## Charges

On April 4, 2023, Trump was arraigned on 34 counts of falsifying business records.[1] Prosecutors alleged he covered up a $130,000 non-disclosure payment made roughly two weeks before the 2016 election to silence Daniels over an alleged affair.[1] Trump denied the affair and any wrongdoing.[1]

## Gag order

In May 2023, State Supreme Court Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] granted prosecutors' request for a social-media gag order, banning Trump from posting any evidence in the case after prosecutors accused him of a history of targeting witnesses.[1] The order additionally barred him from publicly discussing details about witnesses or other evidence prosecutors planned to use.[1] It was issued on a Monday, as Trump's legal team simultaneously sought to remove the state case to federal court.[1] The judge had earlier been placed under heightened protection.[1]

## Trial and opening statements (April 2024)

The trial opened in earnest in Manhattan in April 2024 after jury selection the previous week, with opening statements delivered on Monday, April 22.[2] Court wrapped up around 12:30 that day, earlier than initially anticipated, after a juror experiencing a toothache required a dental appointment.[2] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley]] said the proceedings left him "in utter disbelief," arguing that "the fact that we are actually talking about this case being presented in a New York courtroom" was an embarrassment and that the day's arguments "did in fact capture all the problems here."[2] Turley described the theory of the case as a lapsed state misdemeanor "zapped back into life" by alleging a federal campaign finance violation "that doesn't exist," tied back to the 2016 election.[2]

Polling cited alongside the trial's start found that 31% of Americans considered Trump's behavior at the crux of the case unethical but not illegal, 14% said there was "nothing wrong" with his actions, and 35% said his actions were illegal.[2] Turley argued the case should not have been brought and, if brought, should not have been scheduled immediately before the election, describing a "split screen election" in which Trump is held in a courtroom while [[joe-biden]] campaigns in key states such as Pennsylvania.[2] He called the prosecution an instance of "the weaponization of the criminal legal system" that Americans across the political spectrum should be able to condemn.[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[burning-bright]] treated the day's absurdity — a nationally consequential trial adjourning over a juror's toothache, before a judge who had refused Trump exceptions to depart proceedings early even to attend his son's graduation — as evidence that the "War of Stories" had transitioned from thriller to drama to black comedy.[2] He argued that such absurdity is a feature rather than a bug of the Patriot Plan and the Great Awakening, predicting that Trump will be tried and probably convicted while the Deep State will in the end be the party most affected.[2]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued the restriction would fail: "The gag won't work. The evidence will come out eventually," adding that in the interim what he described as "the Soros-backed Deep State AGs of the world look the fool."[1]

## Pre-indictment signals

On March 9, 2023, The New York Times reported that prosecutors under Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] had signaled that criminal charges against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were likely, informing him that he could appear before a Manhattan grand jury the following week if he wished to testify — an invitation widely read as a strong indication that an indictment would soon follow.[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded that "if America earned a nickel every time the 'mainstream media' hinted that Trump was definitely facing an indictment, America just might be out of debt," arguing that Trump had represented the greatest threat the establishment ever faced since descending the escalator and that, lacking the ability to beat him directly, it relied on propaganda to delegitimize him.[3] Herold predicted more such stories and dismissed the report as "another nothing-burger," reasoning that genuinely incriminating material would have leaked and saturated prime-time coverage during the first investigation.[3]

## Sentencing fight (January 2025)

On January 6, 2025, Trump's lawyers [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and [[emil-bove|Emil Bove]] urged Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] to pause that week's sentencing, arguing in a court filing that their planned appeal to the Appellate Division of the state's trial court triggered what is known as an automatic stay of the proceedings.[4] Merchan rejected the bid for a pause later the same day.[4]

The lawyers were simultaneously asking a state appeals court to reverse rulings keeping intact Trump's conviction on 34 felonies for concealing a payoff that hid a sex scandal before the 2016 presidential election.[4] Merchan had set the sentencing date for January 10 — ten days before Inauguration Day — after rejecting Trump's earlier claim that the verdict should be nullified because of presidential immunity.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded by publishing a sworn statement from [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] that had been provided to NBC News for a story run on January 17, 2018, and argued that the dishonesty and malice of lawyers within the American "justice" system had reached an untenable point, the law industry being barely policed and peer accountability amounting to little more than a slap on the wrist.[4] He described [[lawfare]] as one of the most destructive forces in modern society, wielded most effectively by what he called the most morally depraved individuals in the civilization, and recounted his own five-year experience of legal proceedings following his brother's murder — including a prosecutor with a hidden criminal history who argued that victims "have no right to the truth" — as evidence that victims' rights are nearly nonexistent while the state is permitted to lie, cheat, and steal in the pursuit of justice.[4] He rejected the standard defense of the system ("at least it's not [foreign country]") as woefully cynical and blissfully naive, questioning how anyone could claim certainty without studying the law of all 192 UN-recognized countries, and suggested the deeper problem may be the moral decay of lawyers rather than the legal structure itself.[4]

## Contempt finding over the gag order (April 2024)

On April 30, 2024, Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] held Trump in contempt of court for violating the gag order limiting what he could say about those involved in the case, and warned that he could be jailed if he violated the order again.[5] Merchan found that Trump had violated the order nine times in recent weeks through posts on Truth Social and his campaign website, many of them targeting [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] and [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]], key witnesses in the case.[5] The judge fined Trump $9,000 — $1,000 for each violation — and ordered the posts deleted; all nine were taken down by Tuesday afternoon.[5] In an accompanying written order, Merchan wrote that Trump "violated the Order by making social media posts about known witnesses pertaining to their participation in this criminal proceeding and by making public statements about jurors in this criminal proceeding."[5] Badlands commentary described the proceeding as the "NY Election Interference Trial," casting it as one of many examples of election-interference accusations flying between both wings of the [[uniparty|uniparty]] ahead of November.[5]

## Trump declares the "witch hunt" dead (March 2023)

Before any indictment was returned, [[donald-trump|Trump]] publicly declared the case finished. On Saturday, March 25, 2023, he suggested that the [[manhattan-district-attorneys-office|Manhattan DA]] had been tricked by "star" witness [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]], a disbarred lawyer who pleaded guilty in 2018 to felonies including five counts of tax evasion, lying to a financial institution, lying to Congress, and two campaign finance violations.[6] Trump pointed to a 2018 letter that had emerged the prior week in which Cohen's lawyer told the [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]] that Cohen used his own funds for the $130,000 hush payment to [[stormy-daniels|Stephanie Clifford]] and was not reimbursed by Trump.[6] Speaking to reporters on his plane after the [[2023-waco-rally|Waco rally]], Trump said "I think they've already dropped the case … they have absolutely nothing," and called it "a fake case."[6] He wrote separately that "The Manhattan D.A. Witch Hunt against me is DEAD, no evidence at all, & it has been conclusively proven that I did nothing wrong!"[6]

Badlands commentator [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the collapse as "yet another attempt to take Trump out of the race" that had failed, arguing that globalist actors "put their money on multiple horses" and would push every available pathway toward their agenda in 2024 and beyond, while Trump would continue pushing MAGA and alerting the public to systemic fraud.[6]

## March 2024 gag order

On March 26, 2024, Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] issued a gag order against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Manhattan criminal case.[7] The order from the New York State Supreme Court for New York County — the county trial court — bars Trump from speaking publicly about witnesses and counsel involved in the case, with the exception of New York County District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], and prevents him from making public comments about staff of the court and the district attorney's office.[7] The order extends to family members of both court staff and Bragg's staff, and prohibits public comment "about any prospective juror or any juror in this criminal proceeding."[7]

The order came hours after Trump criticized Merchan and his daughter in a Truth Social post on the morning of March 26, and a day after Merchan set the trial date for April 15.[7] Trump faces 34 counts in the case related to alleged falsification of business records.[7] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the order as a further instance of election interference, arguing that with [[arthur-engoron|Arthur Engoron]]'s credibility destroyed it was "Marchan's turn."[7]

## Jury Selection (April 2024)

Jury selection in the Manhattan case began on Monday, April 15, 2024, and was still under way on its third day that Thursday, with court observers expecting the process to conclude early the following week.[8] Prospective jurors were put through a battery of 42 questions and had their social media accounts scrutinized before being seated.[8] Excused juror Kara McGee, dismissed on account of her work in the cybersecurity sector, told reporters outside the courthouse that "we all have prior opinions on the defendant, unless you've been living in a cardbox," while maintaining she personally could have been impartial.[8] McGee said the questionnaire probed whether jurors held views on the ability of a former sitting president to be tried in a court of law and on legal limits for campaign finance donation amounts — items she read as designed to gauge feelings about the case itself.[8]

On Thursday, April 18, Justice Juan Merchan abruptly excused a juror who had already been approved after she raised concerns that her identity was becoming public; although names were withheld, jurors' employers and other identifying details had been disclosed in open court.[8] Merchan responded by instructing reporters to stop detailing where prospective jurors work, asserting "I have the legal authority to do it."[8] Badlands commentator Ashe in America argued the episode confirmed that a fair trial for Trump in New York was unlikely if not impossible, attributing the tainted jury pool to media hoaxes and to what she called Trump-deranged activism by the state Attorney General and District Attorney.[8]

## Daniels's offer to testify (April 2023)

Two days after [[donald-trump|Trump]] pleaded not guilty, [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] told [[piers-morgan|Piers Morgan]] of TalkTV in an interview aired April 6, 2023 that she was "absolutely" willing to testify at the New York criminal trial.[9] She said being called to the stand would legitimize "my story and who I am," while declining to call her would make it feel as though prosecutors were hiding her and lead the public to assume she was not a credible witness.[9]

Badlands commentary treated the development with suspicion rather than alarm, with [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noting that Daniels's social-media "Redemption" and "Movie" posts and her media appearances could serve either side, and pointing to the fate of her former lawyer [[michael-avenatti|Michael Avenatti]] during the previous effort to use her against Trump.[9] Citing [[just-human|Just Human]], he raised the unresolved question of what Daniels was actually paid for by Trump-owned LLCs and posed the same question about [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]].[9]

## Second contempt finding and comptroller testimony (May 2024)

On Monday, May 6, 2024, Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] found Trump in contempt for a further violation of the gag order and warned that continued violations could result in jail time for the former president.[10] Testimony that afternoon featured retired [[trump-organization|Trump Organization]] comptroller [[jeffrey-mcconney|Jeffrey McConney]], who described the business records and accounting practices central to the prosecution, after which Merchan sent the jury home shortly after 4 p.m.[10]

Outside the courthouse Trump called Merchan "a disaster" and framed the gag order as a constitutional question, telling reporters that "our Constitution is much more important than jail" and that he would make that sacrifice any day.[10] He also objected to prosecutors seeking two to three additional weeks of trial time, saying the aim was to keep him off the campaign trail ahead of a rally scheduled in New Jersey.[10]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the confrontation as "a major game of chicken," reading Trump's repeated violations as an intentional baiting strategy consistent with his broader narrative deployments.[10] In this analysis the prosecutions establish precedent Trump wants codified for future investigations of his own opponents, while each escalation by the court strengthens his position within what Burning Bright calls the "Martyr-Champion" template.[10]

## Appellate challenge to the gag order (May 2024)

On Thursday, May 9, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] confirmed that his lawyers had asked a New York state appellate court to rule on the trial judge's gag order barring him from speaking about certain individuals connected to the proceeding.[11] The motion was filed on Wednesday and sealed, making it inaccessible on the court docket; the Manhattan district attorney's office filed a response that was also sealed.[11] Anonymously sourced reports described it as a motion to expedite a ruling.[11] Speaking to reporters outside the courthouse, Trump said, "we've just filed a major motion in the appellate division concerning the absolutely unconstitutional gag order, where I'm essentially not allowed to talk to you about anything meaningful that's going on in the case," adding that "many good things are going on with the case" and that "it shouldn't have been filed."[11] He did not discuss specifics of the motion.[11]

## July 2024 sentencing postponement

On Tuesday, July 2, 2024, Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] postponed sentencing in the hush-money case until at least September, agreeing to weigh the impact of the Supreme Court's new ruling on [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]].[12] Trump had been scheduled for sentencing on July 11, just days before the Republican nominating convention, on his New York convictions for felony falsification of business records; he denied any wrongdoing.[12] The postponement set September 18 as the earliest possible date — if sentencing happened at all, since Trump's lawyers argued the immunity ruling merited not only delay but tossing out the conviction entirely.[12] Manhattan prosecutors offered no immediate comment.[12]

Trump celebrated on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], writing in all capital letters that "the impact of the Immunity Ruling is a loud and clear signal for Justice in the United States" and claiming the decision amounted to "total exoneration" in this and other criminal cases he faced.[12] Badlands writer Ashe in America framed the delay as evidence that "the process is the punishment" was beginning to boomerang, arguing that the executive, legislative and judicial branches were visibly "checking and balancing the execution of power against the Constitution's guardrails" in the context of the 2024 election.[12]

## The April 2023 arraignment

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] flew from Florida to New York on Monday, April 3, 2023, for the first arraignment of a former American president, his movements tracked minutely from his departure from Palm Beach to his arrival at Trump Tower in Midtown.[13] Live flight trackers followed his red-white-and-blue plane to La Guardia Airport, and helicopters broadcast the motorcade that carried him to his Manhattan home, which was surrounded by press, police, and protesters.[13] The next day he was to be taken downtown by police officers and Secret Service agents to surrender at the office of Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] and be arraigned in the Manhattan Criminal Courts Building, where supporters planned a rally outside.[13]

Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] warned Trump's supporters to keep the peace and said the dignity of the proceedings would not be disrupted; barricades were deployed and the Police Department issued a stand-ready order to its roughly 35,000 officers, a force described as larger and better trained than some national armies.[13]

### Precedent and commentary

Commentators argued that no former president had ever been indicted for good reason: such prosecutions would be viewed as persecutions, render every former president a target of publicity-hungry or politically hostile prosecutors, and reduce the presidency to Third World norms.[13] They further noted Trump led the polls for the Republican nomination and outpolled the Democratic incumbent [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] head to head, making a same-party prosecutor's attempt to destroy a likely opponent's viability a first in U.S. history.[13] [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] was criticized for a post suggesting Trump had a right to prove his innocence, as if presumed guilty.[13]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the indictment would provide "rocket fuel" to Trump's 2024 campaign and the legal and psychological shielding needed to guard both Trump and the justice system from charges of partisanship when what he called the true witch hunt eventually commenced.[13] He separately wrote that chaos does not happen to Trump but is his narrative weapon of choice, and advised observing the net effects of any narrative deployment on the collective mind and on the demeanor of its target.[13]

## Defense rests (21 May 2024)

The defense rested its case on Tuesday, 21 May 2024, without [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] taking the witness stand.[14] Attorney [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] closed the defense after testimony from a former federal prosecutor who challenged the credibility of the prosecution's key witness, and Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan M. Merchan]] dismissed the jury until 28 May, when closing arguments were scheduled.[14] After more than four weeks of testimony, jurors were expected to begin deliberating on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records as early as the following week.[14] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that on the evidence the case should have been dismissed, that [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] and [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] should be charged with perjury, and that the attorneys who put them on the stand should be disbarred.[14]

## Opening of the trial, April 2024

The trial was scheduled to begin on Monday, April 15, 2024, with [[donald-trump|Trump]] taking his 2024 campaign to New York to sit in a Manhattan courtroom facing 34 counts of falsifying business records in connection with the [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] hush-money episode, a proceeding he decried as a "Fake Biden Trial."[15] The start followed an unsuccessful defense bid to adjourn the case over what Trump's lawyers described as overwhelming pretrial publicity, which Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] denied as "not tenable."[15]

On [[truth-social|Truth Social]], Trump described Merchan as "perhaps the most highly conflicted Judge in New York State history," accusing him of giving the defense insufficient time to analyze "hundreds of thousands of pages of documents that D.A. [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] illegally hid, disguised, and held back from us."[15] The Wall Street Journal reported that the 34 felony counts were all tied to records prosecutors said Trump falsified while reimbursing [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] for the Daniels deal, consisting of 11 invoices, 12 general ledger entries and 11 checks.[15]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the "Fake Biden Trial" phrasing as a demonstration of Trump's command of [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]], arguing that his public communication style was among the reasons he was "likely chosen as the 'face' of the most complex military intel operation of all time," and that from Operation Warp Speed to the mugshot "heard around the world," Trump's adversaries were the ones trapped in the information war with him.[15]

## Grand jury hiatus (March 2023)

After weeks of media anticipation of an imminent arrest of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], reporting on 29 March 2023 indicated that the New York grand jury hearing evidence in the hush money probe was not scheduled to meet on the case again until late April.[16] Politico, citing a person familiar with the proceedings, said the break would push any indictment to late April at the earliest and attributed the pause to a "previously scheduled hiatus," an explanation ZeroHedge treated as a convenient after-the-fact admission.[16]

The delay coincided with a report by Jack Phillips of The Epoch Times that an attorney in Trump's orbit who had testified before the Manhattan grand jury earlier that month believed there had been a shift in [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg's]] case against the 45th president, saying, "I think I got through to them."[16] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] mocked the pause as "the Trump indictment version of 'two weeks to flatten the curve.'"[16]

## Bid to move the case to federal court (August 2024)

Late on August 29, 2024, lawyers for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked a federal court in Manhattan to intervene in the New York hush money prosecution, seeking to overturn his felony conviction and indefinitely delay the sentencing scheduled for the following month.[17] They argued that the federal court should seize the case from the state court where it was tried because the prosecution violated his constitutional rights and conflicted with the Supreme Court's recent presidential immunity ruling.[17] Having failed in a pretrial attempt to move the case in the previous year, Trump's attorneys contended that a transfer would give him an "unbiased forum, free from local hostilities," asserting that in state court he had been subject to "bias, conflicts of interest, and appearances of impropriety."[17] If the case were moved, they said they would then seek to have the verdict overturned and the case dismissed on immunity grounds.[17]

## Reaction to the indictment (April 2023)

When the Manhattan grand jury indicted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the hush money payment to a pornographic film actress, Trump issued a lengthy statement calling the case "Political Persecution and Election Interference at the highest level in history" and placing it in a sequence with "Russia, Russia, Russia; the Mueller Hoax; Ukraine, Ukraine, Ukraine; Impeachment Hoax 1; Impeachment Hoax 2" and the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]].[18] He argued that while Democrats had "cheated countless times over the decades," weaponizing the justice system to punish a political opponent who was also the leading Republican presidential candidate had "never happened before. Ever."[18]

Trump singled out [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] as "hand-picked and funded by [[george-soros|George Soros]]," accusing him of doing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s "dirty work" instead of addressing violent crime in New York City, and predicted the "Witch-Hunt" would "backfire massively on Joe Biden."[18] [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] signalled Florida would not cooperate with extradition and [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] denounced the indictment as an injustice.[18]

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] compared the dynamic to a counter-striker in combat sports and to the "Matador's Cloak" concept used by Just Human, arguing that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had long underestimated Trump's narrative deployments.[18] He noted that Trump was the figure pushing the indictment story hardest and that critics within the [[media-industrial-complex|media industrial complex]] and the cultural left were themselves calling the prosecution a miscalculation likely to boost Trump's standing ahead of the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 campaign]].[18]

## Cameras in the courtroom

Trump's April 4, 2023 arraignment drew more cameras to Manhattan than perhaps any case in New York state history, but they were largely kept out of the courtroom itself under a state law dating to the 1930s that is among the most restrictive camera bans in the nation.[19] Only Washington, D.C. is more stringent on in-court media coverage, according to a 2022 report by The Fund for Modern Courts; some state lawmakers mounted a renewed push to change the law amid the arraignment.[19] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that cameras are barred from federal courtrooms but permitted in most state courtrooms, and suggested that securing a televised trial might be preferable to having the indictment dismissed.[19] The rule is discussed further at [[new-york-ban-on-cameras-in-courtrooms|the New York ban on cameras in courtrooms]].[19]

## Aftermath of the conviction

Following the May 30, 2024 verdict convicting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] of 34 felony counts of falsifying business records, celebrations broke out across Manhattan and in the press; former U.S. Attorney Harry Litman told [[msnbc|MSNBC]]'s Nicolle Wallace that it was a "majestic day" and "a day to celebrate."[20] Constitutional scholar [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]], who attended the trial and live-tweeted it daily, wrote in [[the-hill|The Hill]] that the case was "a target-rich environment for an appeal, with multiple layers of reversible error," while rejecting the argument that the verdict could be overturned on venue grounds in a district that voted roughly 90 percent against Trump — in his view the problem was the prosecutors and the judge, not the jury.[20] Sentencing was set for July 11, 2024, with multiple emergency appeals expected, and Republican politicians pressed to move up the [[2024-republican-national-convention|Republican National Convention]] then scheduled for July 15.[20]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the conviction produced an "America First ripple effect," with online sentiment moving from anger to doom-posting to resolve over the weekend and former Trump critics declaring themselves supporters because "justice should be blind and they can see that it's not."[20] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode alongside the 2020 election dispute and the broader "Trump Witch Hunt" as an Info War inflection point testing whether audiences would "enjoy the show" or react emotionally within the system's own rules.[20]

The conviction also triggered a fundraising surge: per Eric Trump, the campaign raised more than $200 million in three days, including roughly $70 million from small-dollar donors and $53 million within 24 hours of the verdict, with Trump telling Fox News that at least 30 percent of donors were first-time political donors — figures far exceeding the [[joe-biden|Biden]] campaign's $51 million raised across all of April.[20] Actor [[dennis-quaid|Dennis Quaid]] cited the trial as the reason he decided to vote for Trump, describing it as "a weaponization of our justice system."[20] The article noted Trump faced 88 charges across four criminal cases at the time, all of which he had pleaded not guilty to.[20]
