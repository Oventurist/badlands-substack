# Trump Hush-Money Case

The Trump hush-money case is the New York state criminal prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over allegations that he falsified business records to conceal a pre-election payment to adult film actress [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]].[1]

## Charges

On April 4, 2023, Trump was arraigned on 34 counts of falsifying business records.[1] Prosecutors alleged he covered up a $130,000 non-disclosure payment made roughly two weeks before the 2016 election to silence Daniels over an alleged affair.[1] Trump denied the affair and any wrongdoing.[1]

## Gag order

In May 2023, State Supreme Court Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] granted prosecutors' request for a social-media gag order, banning Trump from posting any evidence in the case after prosecutors accused him of a history of targeting witnesses.[1] The order additionally barred him from publicly discussing details about witnesses or other evidence prosecutors planned to use.[1] It was issued on a Monday, as Trump's legal team simultaneously sought to remove the state case to federal court.[1] The judge had earlier been placed under heightened protection.[1]

## Trial and opening statements (April 2024)

The trial opened in earnest in Manhattan in April 2024 after jury selection the previous week, with opening statements delivered on Monday, April 22.[2] Court wrapped up around 12:30 that day, earlier than initially anticipated, after a juror experiencing a toothache required a dental appointment.[2] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley]] said the proceedings left him "in utter disbelief," arguing that "the fact that we are actually talking about this case being presented in a New York courtroom" was an embarrassment and that the day's arguments "did in fact capture all the problems here."[2] Turley described the theory of the case as a lapsed state misdemeanor "zapped back into life" by alleging a federal campaign finance violation "that doesn't exist," tied back to the 2016 election.[2]

Polling cited alongside the trial's start found that 31% of Americans considered Trump's behavior at the crux of the case unethical but not illegal, 14% said there was "nothing wrong" with his actions, and 35% said his actions were illegal.[2] Turley argued the case should not have been brought and, if brought, should not have been scheduled immediately before the election, describing a "split screen election" in which Trump is held in a courtroom while [[joe-biden]] campaigns in key states such as Pennsylvania.[2] He called the prosecution an instance of "the weaponization of the criminal legal system" that Americans across the political spectrum should be able to condemn.[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[burning-bright]] treated the day's absurdity — a nationally consequential trial adjourning over a juror's toothache, before a judge who had refused Trump exceptions to depart proceedings early even to attend his son's graduation — as evidence that the "War of Stories" had transitioned from thriller to drama to black comedy.[2] He argued that such absurdity is a feature rather than a bug of the Patriot Plan and the Great Awakening, predicting that Trump will be tried and probably convicted while the Deep State will in the end be the party most affected.[2]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued the restriction would fail: "The gag won't work. The evidence will come out eventually," adding that in the interim what he described as "the Soros-backed Deep State AGs of the world look the fool."[1]

## Pre-indictment signals

On March 9, 2023, The New York Times reported that prosecutors under Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] had signaled that criminal charges against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were likely, informing him that he could appear before a Manhattan grand jury the following week if he wished to testify — an invitation widely read as a strong indication that an indictment would soon follow.[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded that "if America earned a nickel every time the 'mainstream media' hinted that Trump was definitely facing an indictment, America just might be out of debt," arguing that Trump had represented the greatest threat the establishment ever faced since descending the escalator and that, lacking the ability to beat him directly, it relied on propaganda to delegitimize him.[3] Herold predicted more such stories and dismissed the report as "another nothing-burger," reasoning that genuinely incriminating material would have leaked and saturated prime-time coverage during the first investigation.[3]

## Sentencing fight (January 2025)

On January 6, 2025, Trump's lawyers [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and [[emil-bove|Emil Bove]] urged Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] to pause that week's sentencing, arguing in a court filing that their planned appeal to the Appellate Division of the state's trial court triggered what is known as an automatic stay of the proceedings.[4] Merchan rejected the bid for a pause later the same day.[4]

The lawyers were simultaneously asking a state appeals court to reverse rulings keeping intact Trump's conviction on 34 felonies for concealing a payoff that hid a sex scandal before the 2016 presidential election.[4] Merchan had set the sentencing date for January 10 — ten days before Inauguration Day — after rejecting Trump's earlier claim that the verdict should be nullified because of presidential immunity.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded by publishing a sworn statement from [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] that had been provided to NBC News for a story run on January 17, 2018, and argued that the dishonesty and malice of lawyers within the American "justice" system had reached an untenable point, the law industry being barely policed and peer accountability amounting to little more than a slap on the wrist.[4] He described [[lawfare]] as one of the most destructive forces in modern society, wielded most effectively by what he called the most morally depraved individuals in the civilization, and recounted his own five-year experience of legal proceedings following his brother's murder — including a prosecutor with a hidden criminal history who argued that victims "have no right to the truth" — as evidence that victims' rights are nearly nonexistent while the state is permitted to lie, cheat, and steal in the pursuit of justice.[4] He rejected the standard defense of the system ("at least it's not [foreign country]") as woefully cynical and blissfully naive, questioning how anyone could claim certainty without studying the law of all 192 UN-recognized countries, and suggested the deeper problem may be the moral decay of lawyers rather than the legal structure itself.[4]

## Contempt finding over the gag order (April 2024)

On April 30, 2024, Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] held Trump in contempt of court for violating the gag order limiting what he could say about those involved in the case, and warned that he could be jailed if he violated the order again.[5] Merchan found that Trump had violated the order nine times in recent weeks through posts on Truth Social and his campaign website, many of them targeting [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] and [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]], key witnesses in the case.[5] The judge fined Trump $9,000 — $1,000 for each violation — and ordered the posts deleted; all nine were taken down by Tuesday afternoon.[5] In an accompanying written order, Merchan wrote that Trump "violated the Order by making social media posts about known witnesses pertaining to their participation in this criminal proceeding and by making public statements about jurors in this criminal proceeding."[5] Badlands commentary described the proceeding as the "NY Election Interference Trial," casting it as one of many examples of election-interference accusations flying between both wings of the [[uniparty|uniparty]] ahead of November.[5]

## Trump declares the "witch hunt" dead (March 2023)

Before any indictment was returned, [[donald-trump|Trump]] publicly declared the case finished. On Saturday, March 25, 2023, he suggested that the [[manhattan-district-attorneys-office|Manhattan DA]] had been tricked by "star" witness [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]], a disbarred lawyer who pleaded guilty in 2018 to felonies including five counts of tax evasion, lying to a financial institution, lying to Congress, and two campaign finance violations.[6] Trump pointed to a 2018 letter that had emerged the prior week in which Cohen's lawyer told the [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]] that Cohen used his own funds for the $130,000 hush payment to [[stormy-daniels|Stephanie Clifford]] and was not reimbursed by Trump.[6] Speaking to reporters on his plane after the [[2023-waco-rally|Waco rally]], Trump said "I think they've already dropped the case … they have absolutely nothing," and called it "a fake case."[6] He wrote separately that "The Manhattan D.A. Witch Hunt against me is DEAD, no evidence at all, & it has been conclusively proven that I did nothing wrong!"[6]

Badlands commentator [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the collapse as "yet another attempt to take Trump out of the race" that had failed, arguing that globalist actors "put their money on multiple horses" and would push every available pathway toward their agenda in 2024 and beyond, while Trump would continue pushing MAGA and alerting the public to systemic fraud.[6]

## March 2024 gag order

On March 26, 2024, Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] issued a gag order against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Manhattan criminal case.[7] The order from the New York State Supreme Court for New York County — the county trial court — bars Trump from speaking publicly about witnesses and counsel involved in the case, with the exception of New York County District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], and prevents him from making public comments about staff of the court and the district attorney's office.[7] The order extends to family members of both court staff and Bragg's staff, and prohibits public comment "about any prospective juror or any juror in this criminal proceeding."[7]

The order came hours after Trump criticized Merchan and his daughter in a Truth Social post on the morning of March 26, and a day after Merchan set the trial date for April 15.[7] Trump faces 34 counts in the case related to alleged falsification of business records.[7] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the order as a further instance of election interference, arguing that with [[arthur-engoron|Arthur Engoron]]'s credibility destroyed it was "Marchan's turn."[7]

## Jury Selection (April 2024)

Jury selection in the Manhattan case began on Monday, April 15, 2024, and was still under way on its third day that Thursday, with court observers expecting the process to conclude early the following week.[8] Prospective jurors were put through a battery of 42 questions and had their social media accounts scrutinized before being seated.[8] Excused juror Kara McGee, dismissed on account of her work in the cybersecurity sector, told reporters outside the courthouse that "we all have prior opinions on the defendant, unless you've been living in a cardbox," while maintaining she personally could have been impartial.[8] McGee said the questionnaire probed whether jurors held views on the ability of a former sitting president to be tried in a court of law and on legal limits for campaign finance donation amounts — items she read as designed to gauge feelings about the case itself.[8]

On Thursday, April 18, Justice Juan Merchan abruptly excused a juror who had already been approved after she raised concerns that her identity was becoming public; although names were withheld, jurors' employers and other identifying details had been disclosed in open court.[8] Merchan responded by instructing reporters to stop detailing where prospective jurors work, asserting "I have the legal authority to do it."[8] Badlands commentator Ashe in America argued the episode confirmed that a fair trial for Trump in New York was unlikely if not impossible, attributing the tainted jury pool to media hoaxes and to what she called Trump-deranged activism by the state Attorney General and District Attorney.[8]

## Daniels's offer to testify (April 2023)

Two days after [[donald-trump|Trump]] pleaded not guilty, [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] told [[piers-morgan|Piers Morgan]] of TalkTV in an interview aired April 6, 2023 that she was "absolutely" willing to testify at the New York criminal trial.[9] She said being called to the stand would legitimize "my story and who I am," while declining to call her would make it feel as though prosecutors were hiding her and lead the public to assume she was not a credible witness.[9]

Badlands commentary treated the development with suspicion rather than alarm, with [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noting that Daniels's social-media "Redemption" and "Movie" posts and her media appearances could serve either side, and pointing to the fate of her former lawyer [[michael-avenatti|Michael Avenatti]] during the previous effort to use her against Trump.[9] Citing [[just-human|Just Human]], he raised the unresolved question of what Daniels was actually paid for by Trump-owned LLCs and posed the same question about [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]].[9]

## Second contempt finding and comptroller testimony (May 2024)

On Monday, May 6, 2024, Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] found Trump in contempt for a further violation of the gag order and warned that continued violations could result in jail time for the former president.[10] Testimony that afternoon featured retired [[trump-organization|Trump Organization]] comptroller [[jeffrey-mcconney|Jeffrey McConney]], who described the business records and accounting practices central to the prosecution, after which Merchan sent the jury home shortly after 4 p.m.[10]

Outside the courthouse Trump called Merchan "a disaster" and framed the gag order as a constitutional question, telling reporters that "our Constitution is much more important than jail" and that he would make that sacrifice any day.[10] He also objected to prosecutors seeking two to three additional weeks of trial time, saying the aim was to keep him off the campaign trail ahead of a rally scheduled in New Jersey.[10]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the confrontation as "a major game of chicken," reading Trump's repeated violations as an intentional baiting strategy consistent with his broader narrative deployments.[10] In this analysis the prosecutions establish precedent Trump wants codified for future investigations of his own opponents, while each escalation by the court strengthens his position within what Burning Bright calls the "Martyr-Champion" template.[10]

## Appellate challenge to the gag order (May 2024)

On Thursday, May 9, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] confirmed that his lawyers had asked a New York state appellate court to rule on the trial judge's gag order barring him from speaking about certain individuals connected to the proceeding.[11] The motion was filed on Wednesday and sealed, making it inaccessible on the court docket; the Manhattan district attorney's office filed a response that was also sealed.[11] Anonymously sourced reports described it as a motion to expedite a ruling.[11] Speaking to reporters outside the courthouse, Trump said, "we've just filed a major motion in the appellate division concerning the absolutely unconstitutional gag order, where I'm essentially not allowed to talk to you about anything meaningful that's going on in the case," adding that "many good things are going on with the case" and that "it shouldn't have been filed."[11] He did not discuss specifics of the motion.[11]

## July 2024 sentencing postponement

On Tuesday, July 2, 2024, Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] postponed sentencing in the hush-money case until at least September, agreeing to weigh the impact of the Supreme Court's new ruling on [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]].[12] Trump had been scheduled for sentencing on July 11, just days before the Republican nominating convention, on his New York convictions for felony falsification of business records; he denied any wrongdoing.[12] The postponement set September 18 as the earliest possible date — if sentencing happened at all, since Trump's lawyers argued the immunity ruling merited not only delay but tossing out the conviction entirely.[12] Manhattan prosecutors offered no immediate comment.[12]

Trump celebrated on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], writing in all capital letters that "the impact of the Immunity Ruling is a loud and clear signal for Justice in the United States" and claiming the decision amounted to "total exoneration" in this and other criminal cases he faced.[12] Badlands writer Ashe in America framed the delay as evidence that "the process is the punishment" was beginning to boomerang, arguing that the executive, legislative and judicial branches were visibly "checking and balancing the execution of power against the Constitution's guardrails" in the context of the 2024 election.[12]

## The April 2023 arraignment

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] flew from Florida to New York on Monday, April 3, 2023, for the first arraignment of a former American president, his movements tracked minutely from his departure from Palm Beach to his arrival at Trump Tower in Midtown.[13] Live flight trackers followed his red-white-and-blue plane to La Guardia Airport, and helicopters broadcast the motorcade that carried him to his Manhattan home, which was surrounded by press, police, and protesters.[13] The next day he was to be taken downtown by police officers and Secret Service agents to surrender at the office of Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] and be arraigned in the Manhattan Criminal Courts Building, where supporters planned a rally outside.[13]

Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] warned Trump's supporters to keep the peace and said the dignity of the proceedings would not be disrupted; barricades were deployed and the Police Department issued a stand-ready order to its roughly 35,000 officers, a force described as larger and better trained than some national armies.[13]

### Precedent and commentary

Commentators argued that no former president had ever been indicted for good reason: such prosecutions would be viewed as persecutions, render every former president a target of publicity-hungry or politically hostile prosecutors, and reduce the presidency to Third World norms.[13] They further noted Trump led the polls for the Republican nomination and outpolled the Democratic incumbent [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] head to head, making a same-party prosecutor's attempt to destroy a likely opponent's viability a first in U.S. history.[13] [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] was criticized for a post suggesting Trump had a right to prove his innocence, as if presumed guilty.[13]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the indictment would provide "rocket fuel" to Trump's 2024 campaign and the legal and psychological shielding needed to guard both Trump and the justice system from charges of partisanship when what he called the true witch hunt eventually commenced.[13] He separately wrote that chaos does not happen to Trump but is his narrative weapon of choice, and advised observing the net effects of any narrative deployment on the collective mind and on the demeanor of its target.[13]

## Defense rests (21 May 2024)

The defense rested its case on Tuesday, 21 May 2024, without [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] taking the witness stand.[14] Attorney [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] closed the defense after testimony from a former federal prosecutor who challenged the credibility of the prosecution's key witness, and Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan M. Merchan]] dismissed the jury until 28 May, when closing arguments were scheduled.[14] After more than four weeks of testimony, jurors were expected to begin deliberating on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records as early as the following week.[14] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that on the evidence the case should have been dismissed, that [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] and [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] should be charged with perjury, and that the attorneys who put them on the stand should be disbarred.[14]

## Opening of the trial, April 2024

The trial was scheduled to begin on Monday, April 15, 2024, with [[donald-trump|Trump]] taking his 2024 campaign to New York to sit in a Manhattan courtroom facing 34 counts of falsifying business records in connection with the [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] hush-money episode, a proceeding he decried as a "Fake Biden Trial."[15] The start followed an unsuccessful defense bid to adjourn the case over what Trump's lawyers described as overwhelming pretrial publicity, which Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] denied as "not tenable."[15]

On [[truth-social|Truth Social]], Trump described Merchan as "perhaps the most highly conflicted Judge in New York State history," accusing him of giving the defense insufficient time to analyze "hundreds of thousands of pages of documents that D.A. [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] illegally hid, disguised, and held back from us."[15] The Wall Street Journal reported that the 34 felony counts were all tied to records prosecutors said Trump falsified while reimbursing [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] for the Daniels deal, consisting of 11 invoices, 12 general ledger entries and 11 checks.[15]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the "Fake Biden Trial" phrasing as a demonstration of Trump's command of [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]], arguing that his public communication style was among the reasons he was "likely chosen as the 'face' of the most complex military intel operation of all time," and that from Operation Warp Speed to the mugshot "heard around the world," Trump's adversaries were the ones trapped in the information war with him.[15]

## Grand jury hiatus (March 2023)

After weeks of media anticipation of an imminent arrest of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], reporting on 29 March 2023 indicated that the New York grand jury hearing evidence in the hush money probe was not scheduled to meet on the case again until late April.[16] Politico, citing a person familiar with the proceedings, said the break would push any indictment to late April at the earliest and attributed the pause to a "previously scheduled hiatus," an explanation ZeroHedge treated as a convenient after-the-fact admission.[16]

The delay coincided with a report by Jack Phillips of The Epoch Times that an attorney in Trump's orbit who had testified before the Manhattan grand jury earlier that month believed there had been a shift in [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg's]] case against the 45th president, saying, "I think I got through to them."[16] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] mocked the pause as "the Trump indictment version of 'two weeks to flatten the curve.'"[16]

## Bid to move the case to federal court (August 2024)

Late on August 29, 2024, lawyers for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked a federal court in Manhattan to intervene in the New York hush money prosecution, seeking to overturn his felony conviction and indefinitely delay the sentencing scheduled for the following month.[17] They argued that the federal court should seize the case from the state court where it was tried because the prosecution violated his constitutional rights and conflicted with the Supreme Court's recent presidential immunity ruling.[17] Having failed in a pretrial attempt to move the case in the previous year, Trump's attorneys contended that a transfer would give him an "unbiased forum, free from local hostilities," asserting that in state court he had been subject to "bias, conflicts of interest, and appearances of impropriety."[17] If the case were moved, they said they would then seek to have the verdict overturned and the case dismissed on immunity grounds.[17]

## Reaction to the indictment (April 2023)

When the Manhattan grand jury indicted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the hush money payment to a pornographic film actress, Trump issued a lengthy statement calling the case "Political Persecution and Election Interference at the highest level in history" and placing it in a sequence with "Russia, Russia, Russia; the Mueller Hoax; Ukraine, Ukraine, Ukraine; Impeachment Hoax 1; Impeachment Hoax 2" and the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]].[18] He argued that while Democrats had "cheated countless times over the decades," weaponizing the justice system to punish a political opponent who was also the leading Republican presidential candidate had "never happened before. Ever."[18]

Trump singled out [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] as "hand-picked and funded by [[george-soros|George Soros]]," accusing him of doing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s "dirty work" instead of addressing violent crime in New York City, and predicted the "Witch-Hunt" would "backfire massively on Joe Biden."[18] [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] signalled Florida would not cooperate with extradition and [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] denounced the indictment as an injustice.[18]

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] compared the dynamic to a counter-striker in combat sports and to the "Matador's Cloak" concept used by Just Human, arguing that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had long underestimated Trump's narrative deployments.[18] He noted that Trump was the figure pushing the indictment story hardest and that critics within the [[media-industrial-complex|media industrial complex]] and the cultural left were themselves calling the prosecution a miscalculation likely to boost Trump's standing ahead of the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 campaign]].[18]

## Cameras in the courtroom

Trump's April 4, 2023 arraignment drew more cameras to Manhattan than perhaps any case in New York state history, but they were largely kept out of the courtroom itself under a state law dating to the 1930s that is among the most restrictive camera bans in the nation.[19] Only Washington, D.C. is more stringent on in-court media coverage, according to a 2022 report by The Fund for Modern Courts; some state lawmakers mounted a renewed push to change the law amid the arraignment.[19] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that cameras are barred from federal courtrooms but permitted in most state courtrooms, and suggested that securing a televised trial might be preferable to having the indictment dismissed.[19] The rule is discussed further at [[new-york-ban-on-cameras-in-courtrooms|the New York ban on cameras in courtrooms]].[19]

## Aftermath of the conviction

Following the May 30, 2024 verdict convicting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] of 34 felony counts of falsifying business records, celebrations broke out across Manhattan and in the press; former U.S. Attorney Harry Litman told [[msnbc|MSNBC]]'s Nicolle Wallace that it was a "majestic day" and "a day to celebrate."[20] Constitutional scholar [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]], who attended the trial and live-tweeted it daily, wrote in [[the-hill|The Hill]] that the case was "a target-rich environment for an appeal, with multiple layers of reversible error," while rejecting the argument that the verdict could be overturned on venue grounds in a district that voted roughly 90 percent against Trump — in his view the problem was the prosecutors and the judge, not the jury.[20] Sentencing was set for July 11, 2024, with multiple emergency appeals expected, and Republican politicians pressed to move up the [[2024-republican-national-convention|Republican National Convention]] then scheduled for July 15.[20]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the conviction produced an "America First ripple effect," with online sentiment moving from anger to doom-posting to resolve over the weekend and former Trump critics declaring themselves supporters because "justice should be blind and they can see that it's not."[20] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode alongside the 2020 election dispute and the broader "Trump Witch Hunt" as an Info War inflection point testing whether audiences would "enjoy the show" or react emotionally within the system's own rules.[20]

The conviction also triggered a fundraising surge: per Eric Trump, the campaign raised more than $200 million in three days, including roughly $70 million from small-dollar donors and $53 million within 24 hours of the verdict, with Trump telling Fox News that at least 30 percent of donors were first-time political donors — figures far exceeding the [[joe-biden|Biden]] campaign's $51 million raised across all of April.[20] Actor [[dennis-quaid|Dennis Quaid]] cited the trial as the reason he decided to vote for Trump, describing it as "a weaponization of our justice system."[20] The article noted Trump faced 88 charges across four criminal cases at the time, all of which he had pleaded not guilty to.[20]

## Pre-Indictment Legal Debate (March 2023)

In the days before the expected indictment, the legal basis of the case was widely disputed.[21] Legal scholars including George Washington University professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] predicted an indictment for falsifying business records under New York Penal Law Section 175.05, a charge that would rest on Trump's labeling of his reimbursement to [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] as a legal expense.[21] Turley argued the district attorney was "struggling to twist state laws to effectively prosecute a federal case long ago rejected by the Justice Department against Trump," noting that he had written in 2018 about how difficult such a federal case would be under existing election laws and that six years later the same theory was being "shoehorned into a state claim."[21] He also observed that legal experts insisting such concealment was clearly criminal "were conspicuously silent when [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] faced a not-dissimilar campaign-finance allegation."[21]

Trump's attorney [[joe-tacopina|Joe Tacopina]] countered that the payment to Cohen was a legitimate legal fee, emphasizing that Trump never paid [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] directly.[21] "The payments were made to a lawyer, not to Stormy Daniels," Tacopina said on MSNBC, adding that Cohen "was his lawyer at the time and advised him that this was the proper way to do this to protect himself and his family from embarrassment."[21] The MSNBC host countered that the money Trump paid Cohen went on to Daniels and therefore was not a legal fee.[21] Constitutional lawyer [[alan-dershowitz|Alan Dershowitz]] wrote in his Substack that the former president "should not be indicted for novel and unprecedented technical crimes for which no one else would be prosecuted."[21]

## Summations and jury deliberations (May 2024)

Ahead of closing arguments, [[abc-news|ABC News]] reviewed the testimony of the 22 witnesses heard over five weeks of trial, a roster that included Trump's former lawyer, an adult film actress, two of Trump's executive assistants, a tabloid executive, a top White House aide and a flurry of custodial witnesses.[22] Prosecutors used them to argue that Trump falsified business records to hide the reimbursement of the hush money payment [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] made to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] in order to boost Trump's prospects in the 2016 presidential election, while defense lawyers pinned responsibility for the invoices on Cohen and argued that Trump — who denied all wrongdoing — was simply protecting his family from false allegations.[22]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that closing arguments in [[juan-merchan|Judge Juan Merchan]]'s "upside down clown rodeo" were scheduled for May 28, 2024, and that the ABC summary itself demonstrated why the case never should have been brought.[22] She argued that the inner workings of Trump's political campaign had undergone "a colonoscopy" over six weeks and that prosecutors had "essentially suborned perjury" in an effort to make their case, with unreliable witnesses impeached multiple times and conflicting testimony supplying sufficient reasonable doubt for acquittal.[22] She warned that a precedent had now been set for "political campaign colonoscopies," reasoning that if the presidential front-runner was not off limits, no one was.[22] With deliberations expected to begin the following day, she questioned whether a New York jury already the subject of bias accusations following voir dire could rule fairly on the facts, the evidence and the law, closing with the line "It's 12 angry thems in NY this week."[22]

## Trial date set for March 25, 2024

On February 15, 2024, a New York judge ruled that the hush-money trial would proceed as scheduled with jury selection starting March 25, rejecting defense demands for delay on the grounds that a trial would interfere with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign to retake the White House.[23] The ruling made the case the first of Trump's four criminal prosecutions to reach trial, centered on years-old accusations that he sought to bury stories about extramarital affairs during his 2016 presidential run, while the other cases charged him with plotting to overturn the 2020 election result and with illegally hoarding classified documents at his Florida estate.[23]

Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Manuel Merchan]] pointed to the delay in the separate Washington prosecution over efforts to undo the 2020 election, originally set for trial on March 4 but effectively frozen pending Trump's appeal on the legally untested question of presidential immunity from prosecution for official acts.[23] Noting that he had resisted defense urgings months earlier to postpone, Merchan said, "In hindsight, frankly, I'm glad that I took that position, because here we are and the D.C. case did not go forward," adding that he decided to keep the date after speaking the previous week with the Washington judge, [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]].[23] Merchan said the trial was expected to last six weeks.[23]

## Aftermath of the verdict (June 2024)

On May 30, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was found guilty on 34 counts of falsifying records, a charge Modernity News characterized as something "no one really quite knows."[24] A week later, on June 6, Trump announced at a Turning Point Action town hall in Arizona that his campaign had raised roughly $400 million since the verdict, an unprecedented amount that commentators judged unlikely to be matched by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] before the election.[24] Trump said he had "just went through a rigged trial in New York with a highly conflicted, and I mean highly conflicted, judge where there was no crime," and argued that prosecutors "could have brought the case seven years ago" but that "it's only when you run for office they bring cases."[24]

In the same period Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] disclosed to counsel a Facebook comment on the court's page claiming that a juror's cousin had said Trump was going to be convicted, prompting speculation that Merchan was either diluting a larger story, baiting Trump into a gag order violation, or laying groundwork for a mistrial.[24]

## Closing arguments and the start of deliberations

Jurors sat through about eight hours of closing arguments on Tuesday, May 27, 2024, as lawyers for both sides made their final cases before deliberations on whether [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was guilty of 34 counts of falsifying business records.[25] Trump's lead attorney [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] argued that [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]], the prosecution's key witness, was the "greatest liar of all time" and had acted alone in paying [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] $130,000 in 2016 for her silence about an alleged sexual encounter, telling jurors they could not convict on Cohen's testimony and that there were ample other reasons to doubt the prosecution's narrative.[25]

Prosecutors alleged that Trump signed off on a scheme to falsify records to conceal the payment, designed to subvert election law, and that reimbursements for the $130,000 were falsely recorded as monthly checks for Cohen's ongoing legal services during the first year of the presidency; Trump pleaded not guilty and his defense maintained the checks and records were accurate.[25] Prosecutor [[joshua-steinglass|Joshua Steinglass]] delivered the closing for the Manhattan District Attorney's Office, defending Cohen's credibility as corroborated by a voluminous record of texts, emails and call records, and urging jurors to hold Trump accountable.[25]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who had previously likened the trial to "a colonoscopy for the Trump campaign," argued that the prosecution's recasting of the evidence rested wholly on Cohen's credibility, that much of the supposed evidence was simply Cohen's testimony, and that because he was impeached multiple times the state had not proved its case beyond a reasonable doubt.[25] Court was to resume the following morning for roughly an hour of jury instructions before deliberations began.[25]

## March 2023 Pre-Indictment Narrative Phase

In the days before any indictment was returned, reporting that [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] would charge the former president dominated national and social media, shaping the political environment in which the case unfolded.[27] Coverage focused on Bragg's decision to take the matter to a grand jury and on the invitation to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to testify, a step traditionally read as a sign that jurors were prepared to indict.[27] Bragg publicly attributed the surrounding spectacle to Trump while refusing to cooperate with a House inquiry into his handling of the case.[27]

Badlands commentary treated the pre-indictment phase as an information-warfare event rather than a purely legal one, arguing that Trump had seeded the indictment narrative himself and thereby extracted "all of the optical benefits of an Actual Event" from a merely potential one — smoking out political adversaries, blunting any coordinated media campaign, and further positioning himself as anti-establishment.[27] Polling conducted between March 17 and March 21, 2023 by [[mclaughlin-and-associates|McLaughlin & Associates]] was cited as evidence that the prosecution was backfiring politically, with Trump's standing improving in both general-election and primary matchups.[27]


## Jury instructions and deliberations

Jury deliberations opened on May 28, 2024 amid widespread criticism of Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]]'s instructions.[28] [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] called one element "the coup de grace instruction," noting that Merchan told jurors they need not agree on what occurred and could disagree over which of the three predicate crimes applied, meaning a 4-4-4 split would still be treated as unanimous.[28] Turley also pointed out that jurors would not be given written copies of the instructions but could ask for them to be read again, prompting Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] to call the arrangement "absolute insanity."[28] Mid-afternoon the jury sent a note with four specific requests to the court.[28] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the case as "the NY Election Interference" trial and wrote that the world was bracing for a guilty verdict.[28]

During the same week the [[joe-biden|Biden]] re-election campaign abandoned six weeks of distance from the courthouse at 100 Centre Street and held its own surrogate event outside, with communications director [[michael-tyler|Michael Tyler]] telling reporters the campaign came "because you all are here."[28] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the move meant Democrats were doubling down on a prosecution that had raised [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s poll numbers.[28]

## October 2025 appeal

On October 28, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] submitted a 96-page appeal to a New York court seeking to overturn his conviction in the Manhattan hush money case, with his lawyers outlining multiple arguments for dismissal.[29] The brief argued federal law preemption — that the state statute used to convict him conflicts with federal campaign finance rules, so New York should not have prosecuted him under it.[29] It also invoked [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]], contending the trial wrongly admitted evidence and testimony about Trump's actions while president, citing Supreme Court rulings affirming immunity for official presidential acts.[29]

The appeal further claimed the jury was not properly instructed to agree unanimously on which "unlawful acts" Trump allegedly committed, a potential violation of New York law, and that prosecutors failed to prove he intended to defraud anyone or cover up a crime.[29] Trump's team also argued that Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] should have recused himself for perceived partiality, citing his political contributions and statements.[29] The brief asked the court either to dismiss the charges entirely or to reverse the conviction.[29] Badlands contributor Chris Paul characterized the case as "a fake indictment based on mainstream media reporting and novel legal theories" that was never going to survive appellate review.[29]

## The grand jury indictment (March 30, 2023)

On Thursday, March 30, 2023, a Manhattan grand jury voted to indict [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for his alleged role in organizing hush money payments made to the adult film performer [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] during his 2016 campaign, capping a yearslong probe.[30] [[cnn|CNN]] described the indictment as historic, marking the first time a former US president and major presidential candidate had ever been criminally charged.[30] Trump, a Florida resident, was expected to be arraigned the following week.[30]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the prosecutors had chosen the worse of two options: declining to indict would have let Trump exhaust the political energy of a merely potential event, whereas indicting him "kicked off the greatest—and eventually most cathartic—presidential campaign in U.S. history ... and almost two years early."[30] He also read CNN's emphasis on the precedent as an inadvertent admission of the psychological effect of the move, asking which other names such a precedent might "come back around on in the coming years."[30]

## Jury selection, April 16, 2024

On Tuesday, April 16, 2024, the first seven jurors were seated in the hush-money criminal trial of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], as the selection process continued toward a panel of twelve jurors and six alternates who could judge the former president fairly.[31] The presiding judge warned counsel that he would not tolerate any effort to intimidate prospective jurors, after stating that Trump — the Republican nominee for the November 5 election — had been audibly muttering while a member of the venire was being questioned.[31] Trump faced 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to conceal a hush-money payment to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] shortly before the 2016 election, pleaded not guilty, denied that any sexual encounter took place, and called the case brought by Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] a partisan "witch hunt" intended to interfere with his campaign against President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[31] [[reuters|Reuters]] noted the hush-money case was one of four criminal prosecutions Trump faced, the others arising from efforts to overturn the 2020 election result and the alleged mishandling of classified information, with the remaining three unlikely to reach trial before the election.[31]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], following the Inner City Press live coverage of day two for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], described jury selection as "fiery," with several prospective jurors stating outright that they could not be impartial while others claimed they could despite social-media posts revealing bias; the judge excused many and rejected challenges to others before seven were seated, with selection of the remaining five jurors and six alternates set to resume Thursday.[31] She argued that Reuters had "effectively already convicted President Trump," which she called convenient for the prosecution given how many readers still regarded the wire service as journalism, and concluded that whether Trump could get a fair trial in New York was an "outlook uncertain."[31]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a contrasting reading, arguing that while the legacy press and what he called their Deep State handlers spent their remaining sociopolitical capital urging audiences to despair, Trump had produced two of the most memetic images in modern American history during the same "Witch Hunt" and for opposite reasons — the defiant mugshot "Game Face" and the courtroom sketch communicating calm under fire, or what Anons call "comfiness and coziness."[31] His closing question was whether Trump seemed afraid.[31]

## Arraignment logistics and expected delay (March 2023)

Law enforcement officials met behind closed doors on Monday, March 20, 2023 to discuss the logistics of arraigning [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] following his possible indictment over the hush-money payments made on his behalf during the 2016 presidential campaign.[32] A law enforcement source told Fox News that the [[manhattan-district-attorneys-office|Manhattan District Attorney's Office]] and different branches of law enforcement had discussed closing streets, erecting lights with generators, and deploying extra barriers and police.[32] The source said law enforcement did not expect the former president to be arraigned until the following week, because the Manhattan grand jury — which had been meeting secretly to hear evidence for weeks — had another witness scheduled for that Wednesday.[32]

A virtual arraignment was ruled out, reportedly because the District Attorney opposed it, though another source familiar with the discussions said a virtual option had never been seriously considered.[32] Officials expressed concern about safety, indicating that if Trump came up to Manhattan there would be a major police presence and the surrounding area would be shut down; Trump had called on supporters to protest ahead of a possible indictment.[32]

[[just-human|Just Human]] predicted the office would keep pushing the date back "again and again, until the heat is off," then quietly dismiss the grand jury after failing to procure an indictment.[32] He compared the situation to that of [[cyrus-vance-jr|Cy Vance]], the previous Manhattan DA who failed to "get Trump," an effort whose collapse prompted the resignations of prosecutors Mark Pomerantz and Carey Dunne and preceded Vance's decision not to seek re-election.[32] Just Human argued [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] faced "fire on both sides of the door": no indictment would mark a public failure that had dragged media and politicians over a cliff, while an indictment would be easily dismissed and hand Trump more credibility and support than ever.[32] Invoking the "Matador" framing, he said the pursuers were headed full speed into a brick wall behind Trump's cape, and added that a "spicier dish" remained possible — an indictment of someone other than Trump that would invert the entire saga.[32]

## Prosecutors defer on delaying sentencing (August 2024)

In August 2024, New York prosecutors took no position on Trump's request to delay sentencing until after the November election, with the Manhattan district attorney's office saying in a letter that it would defer to Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]].[33] Trump was at that point scheduled to be sentenced on September 18, after a jury convicted him of all 34 felony counts of falsifying business records related to a payment to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]]; the original July 11 date had been moved after Merchan said he would rule on Trump's immunity claim on September 16 and impose sentence two days later.[33]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] found the non-position puzzling, noting that lawyers love to argue and that sitting out the motions cycle waived the prosecution's right to put its view on the record in what the regime regarded as one of its greatest perceived victories against Trump.[33] She flagged the prosecution's observation that "significant public safety and logistical steps by multiple agencies are necessary to prepare for court appearances in this matter," predicting the narrative would pivot to claims that MAGA would riot if Trump were sentenced — a hard sell, she argued, against the backdrop of leftist agitators then occupying Chicago during the [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]].[33] She also quoted Trump's legal team arguing that "setting aside naked election-interference objectives, there is no valid countervailing reason for the Court to keep the current sentencing date on the calendar."[33] Sentencing after early voting began would, in her view, be obvious election interference, while Merchan faced a dilemma over the immunity claim that could render much of the evidence inadmissible and overturn the conviction — in which case the "convicted felon" narrative would fall away as the "oath-breaking insurrectionist" framing had after the Supreme Court ruled.[33]

## The verdict, May 30, 2024

The verdict arrived just as everyone was about to leave for the day.[34] Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan M. Merchan]] had already summoned [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], his legal team and prosecutors into the courtroom, saying he planned to send the jury home at 4:30 p.m. with deliberations to resume the next morning; Trump looked upbeat, having animated chats with his lawyers, and the bell used by the jury to signal the court had been silent all day.[34] The signal came instead from the jingling of court officer Maj. Michael McKee's keys as he hustled past the bench, after which the judge returned to announce a note signed at 4:20 p.m. stating that the jury had reached a verdict and wanted an extra 30 minutes to fill out the verdict form.[34] Trump thereby became the first former president to be convicted of felony crimes, as a New York jury found him guilty of falsifying business records in a scheme to illegally influence the 2016 election through hush money payments to a porn actor who said the two had sex.[34]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the jury convicted on all 34 counts after a controversial trial in which the defense and most media claimed not to have fully understood the charges until jury instructions were provided.[34] The pro-Trump crowd outside the courthouse was described as livid but hopeful, reacting in real time on [[steve-bannon|Bannon]]'s [[war-room|War Room]]; the news broke at the top of the program, which Ashe — invoking Chris Paul's theories on Bannon — argued allowed Bannon and his team to reinforce messages of hope and guide the emotional response immediately following the verdict, with Bannon concluding "I'm long MAGA."[34] Trump told reporters afterward that "everyone knows what happened… We'll fight to the end and we'll win… This was a rigged decision, right from day one… This is long from over."[34] Citing [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]], Ashe reported that Merchan set July 11 for sentencing, that a probation interview would be scheduled for Trump, that all motions had to be filed by June 13, and that there was no bail.[34] A second Badlands take, developed with [[just-human|Just Human]] on the [[defected|DEFECTED]] program, held that the conviction was expected by most Anons paying attention — and therefore by Trump and patriots — meaning "everything is pretty good, actually."[34]

## Day one of trial and the graduation dispute (April 15, 2024)

The first day of the trial, Monday April 15, 2024, was largely procedural, taken up with evidence questions, scheduling and the opening of jury selection, with more than 50 prospective jurors dismissed.[35] Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] barred [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from attending his son's high school graduation and warned that he could be arrested if he went, and also refused to let him attend a Supreme Court hearing in Washington concerning presidential immunity.[35] Speaking after court, Trump said "as you know my son is graduating from High School, and looks like the judge will not let me go to the graduation for a son who's worked very, very hard," adding that he had been "looking forward for years to having graduation with his mother and father there" and calling the trial a "scam" and a "political witch hunt."[35] He continued: "We got a real problem with this Judge... that I can't go to my son's graduation, or that I can't go to the United States Supreme Court, that I'm not in Georgia or Florida or North Carolina campaigning like I should be... it's perfect for the radical left Democrats — that's exactly what they want," describing the situation as "election interference."[35]

Merchan also left the door open to admitting evidence from the [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] case, saying it would be unfair to bind the state's hand.[35] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], following the Inner City Press live thread, argued that the rulings on evidence and scheduling conflicts showed again a pattern of judicial discretion prejudicing Trump, and said she was "looking forward to the boomerang."[35]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a macro reading, noting that colleagues including Ashe in America and [[just-human|Just Human]] would cover the granular detail of what he called a sham trial — valuable work, since studying the micro allows the community to "learn, dissect and ultimately disarm enemy tactics" in the lawfare realm.[35] His own view was that despite short-term damage, Trump's growing portrayal even among non-MAGA alternative media as a martyr, victim and quarry of the establishment was a net positive heading into what he called the most consequential election in American history, closing with the line that "Trump's not trapped in there with them. They're trapped with him."[35]

## Capitol security preparations and the "prison presidency" debate (March 21, 2023)

On Tuesday, March 21, 2023 — the day [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had publicly predicted he would be arrested — the U.S. Capitol moved to raise security levels in anticipation of a reported indictment, with the [[senate-sergeant-at-arms|Sergeant at Arms]] stating that the [[united-states-capitol-police|United States Capitol Police]] anticipated demonstration activity across the country related to the possible indictment.[36] The statement said law enforcement was not tracking any specific, credible threats against Capitol or state offices but that a greater law enforcement presence would be visible on Capitol Hill, and that the Senate Operations Center and State Office Readiness Program were monitoring the situation.[36] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that videos of NYPD barricades and reports of police cars set on fire ahead of the anticipated arrest bore "all the hallmarks of a production" reminiscent of January 6 stagecraft, while conceding that the extent of the preparations suggested authorities genuinely expected something to happen.[36] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the situation reduced to two possibilities: either an actual indictment was unlikely and Trump knew it, or an indictment was likely and would prove beneficial to his 2024 campaign.[36]

In the same period, [[alan-dershowitz|Alan Dershowitz]] — a onetime member of Trump's first impeachment defense team — told [[newsmax|Newsmax]] that he expected Trump to be indicted and convicted in New York City because of what he viewed as the city's unfair legal system, but that Trump could still be reelected in 2024 and run the country "from prison."[36] Coverage summarized the underlying probe led by Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] into the 2016 payment of $130,000 to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]], which Trump denied any wrongdoing over while attacking the investigation.[36] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] responded that the claim a president could not govern from prison was "Deep State" spin, that Trump would likely never actually be jailed for security reasons, and that his willingness to keep fighting despite the threat of losing his freedom was itself a proof of character.[36]

## Arraignment and the "fake case" response (April 4, 2023)

Following his Tuesday, April 4, 2023 arraignment in New York before what he called a "Trump-hating judge," former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] denounced his prosecutors in a speech to a crowd at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]].[37] "This fake case was brought only to interfere with the upcoming 2024 presidential election and it should be dropped immediately," Trump said, adding, "I never thought anything like this could happen in America."[37] He asserted that "the only crime that I've committed is to fearlessly defend our nation from those who seek to destroy it," before turning on Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], saying, "The criminal is [Alvin Bragg] … he should be prosecuted, or at a minimum, he should resign."[37] Trump also attacked the presiding judge, saying he faced "a Trump hating judge with a Trump hating wife and family, whose daughter worked for [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and now receives money from the Biden/Harris campaign," and concluding, "It's right out of the old Soviet Union. That's where we are!"[37]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that little had happened beyond what was expected and that Trump had arguably never intended to plead guilty, arguing that the legitimacy of the indictment was questionable because the payments made to [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] were not "known" to Trump — knowledge being critical to establishing the conspiracy charge levied by Bragg.[37] He contended that the substance of the charges was repeatedly obscured by pundits and the district attorney, a pattern he read as telling: by substance he meant what events occurred, what evidence proved them, and whether those events demonstrated wrongdoing.[37] Because the case rested on Trump's presumed knowledge that payments to Cohen were compensation for "campaign contributions," Deschamps argued, Bragg and allied media inflated the charges rhetorically while avoiding details, so that — as in a witch hunt — the accusation carried more persuasive power in the court of public opinion than the alleged conduct itself.[37] Most people, he wrote, would not evaluate the actions and would instead assume guilt, an assumption that held only because it went critically unexamined, which he took as sufficient to characterize the affair as a smear paired with an attempt to make charges stick that would not survive reasonable scrutiny.[37] Drawing the analogy to historical witch trials, in which the methods of testing a suspect all but guaranteed suffering or death, he argued the goal was to do maximum damage to Trump on a guilty-until-proven-innocent basis; he nonetheless suspected Trump and his allies had anticipated the whole sequence, and that the episode revealed the defunct condition of the legal and justice system.[37]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a contrasting reading, asking whether Trump looked worried or rather "expectant, even content as a plan plays out in full view of the American Public."[37] He enumerated what he saw as the outcomes: legal precedents set; a spotlight on the weaponized, partisan nature of "Swamp AGs" that drew bipartisan criticism; legal and mass-psychological shielding stripped from former heads of state and politicians; and narrative shielding established for the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] and local and state attorneys general for future RICO investigations, indictments and convictions — the last credited to [[just-human|@justhuman]].[37] "The REAL Witch Hunt rolls on," he concluded.[37] In a further "Deep Cut," Burning Bright speculated that with narrative shielding in place and precedents set for state attorneys general and district attorneys to pursue former heads of state for alleged felonies, New York prosecutors might find those same tools turned against others — and that "the greatest DOJ asset of all time," a New Yorker and former president who had helped establish those precedents, might simultaneously lead an America First revival presidential campaign.[37]

## Dershowitz on venue and a "fixed case" (April 2023)

On the Monday, April 3, 2023 broadcast of Fox News Channel's "Hannity," Harvard law professor emeritus [[alan-dershowitz|Alan Dershowitz]] vowed to fight any gag order placed on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in connection with the sealed grand jury indictment.[37] Dershowitz said he could not imagine an elected judge in the borough of Manhattan throwing out the case and then having to tell family, friends or business associates, "I'm the lawyer who freed Donald Trump," citing his own experience of losing friends, clients and business associates after defending Trump in the Senate impeachment trial.[37] "No judge is going to want to be, quote, 'Dershowitz-ed,'" he said, concluding that there would be no fair assessment of the statute of limitations or a change of venue and that "this is a fixed case if it's tried in Manhattan."[37]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded that Dershowitz of all people would know, remarking that "Jesus had a fairer trial in Jerusalem," and noted reports that the judge had banned live television coverage of the courtroom — a ruling some defended on the grounds that cameras would encourage political posturing by the prosecution and the judge.[37] DeLarme wrote that the general consensus was that the matter could only be justly resolved on appeal, said he fully expected a conviction that would function as a ready-made Democrat campaign advertisement and would have to be overturned by a higher court, and framed the prosecution as aimed ultimately at Trump's supporters because Trump remained "in the way."[37] He asked rhetorically whether the indictment should be declared as a campaign donation from [[george-soros|Soros]], and, while disagreeing with Dershowitz politically, applauded his defense of the Constitution and asked where the other constitutionally minded liberals had gone.[37]

## Sentencing delayed until after the 2024 election

On Friday, September 6, 2024, Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] delayed Trump's sentencing on the felony charges until November 26, writing that "this is not a decision this Court makes lightly but it is the decision which in this Court's view, best advances the interests of justice."[39] The ruling followed a request from Trump's attorneys to postpone the September 18 sentencing until after the November 5 election so that they could appeal a pending ruling involving presidential immunity, which had been expected by September 16 — two days before what would have been the first ever sentencing of a former president on criminal charges.[39]

Trump's attorneys [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and [[emil-bove|Emil Bove]] had argued in an August 14 filing that "a single business day is an unreasonably short period of time" for such an appeal and that "there is no basis for continuing to rush."[39] Trump had been convicted in May on 34 counts of falsifying business records related to a hush money payment to adult film star [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] near the end of the 2016 presidential campaign.[39]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that although Trump had faced the possibility of four criminal trials that year, the New York case was the only one to reach the trial phase — and that it was now on hold until after the election, a sequence she read as evidence that hyper-politicized prosecutors were not actually in control of the game board.[39]
