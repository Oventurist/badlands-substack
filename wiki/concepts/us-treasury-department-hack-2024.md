# US Treasury Department Hack (2024)

In December 2024, the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|US Treasury Department]] disclosed that it had been hacked by a Chinese state-sponsored actor through a third-party software service provider, according to a letter the agency sent to Congress on Monday, December 30.[1] Treasury described the intrusion as a "major cybersecurity incident," a classification triggered by its attribution to a state-sponsored actor.[1]

## The intrusion

Treasury was notified on December 8 by third-party software provider BeyondTrust Inc. that a hacker had gained access "to a key used by the vendor to secure a cloud-based service used to remotely provide technical support for Treasury Departmental Offices (DO) end users."[1] The department was assisted in its response by the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]], the FBI, the intelligence community, and third-party forensic investigators.[1] Based on available information, advanced hackers tied to [[china]] were assessed to be behind the incident.[1] The breach was reported amid a broader wave of telecom hacking attributed to Chinese actors.[1]

## Chinese denial and Badlands commentary

China denied hacking the Treasury Department.[1] The Chinese embassy in Washington said in an emailed statement that it opposes US "smear attacks against China without any factual basis," adding: "The US needs to stop using cybersecurity to smear and slander China, and stop spreading all kinds of disinformation about the so-called Chinese hacking threat."[1]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America questioned the attribution, writing that "it is most likely our own government that hacked the US Treasury Department."[1]
