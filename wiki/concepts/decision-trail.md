# Decision Trail

The "decision trail" is an investigative heuristic advanced by Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in his May 2023 essay "Poison Fruit of the Decision Tree," offered as a complement to the more familiar practice of following the money.[1] Deschamps wrote that following the money helps identify where the resources for policy flow from — an essential facet of any good investigation — but that the best way to identify insidiousness at a causal rather than a merely resource level is to look at who made the policy decisions and how those decisions were pushed down through the layers of society to everyone else.[1]

## Policy as the unit of analysis

Central to the method is the definition of policy as "a course or principle of action adopted or proposed by a government, party, business, or individual."[1] Deschamps stressed that the phrase "course or principle of action adopted or proposed" means someone is suggesting what everyone else should do — that a decision or choice has been made affecting the country, the people, an industry, or the world at large.[1] He named policy as the key word linking bodies such as [[chatham-house|Chatham House]] and the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] to concrete outcomes in citizens' lives.[1]

In a lawful, rule-of-law, genuinely free society, he argued, policies affecting citizens must be reviewed, debated and approved by those citizens; that is the ideal and the only real standard of lawfulness.[1] He allowed that not every citizen can, wants to, or should participate, and proposed a standard of civil or lawful competence — arguing for example that a corrupt politician found to be working for the Chinese Communist Party should not be relied on for insight into a policy proposal, and that an MS-13 illegal alien who has already demonstrated disrespect for US immigration laws should have no voice in whether US immigration policy is changed, at least until trust has been re-established.[1]

## Supporting definitions

Deschamps supplied working definitions of the terms his framework depends on. Power, in the most important sense, is about control: with power one can exert some degree of control or influence, and without it one cannot.[1] A decision is the ability to choose — to use reason, logic, feelings, insights and intuition to act in the world of ideas or of physical things and so influence social, personal or cosmic events — which he also called **discretionary power** and named the most important aspect of a truly free society regardless of the social system used.[1] Society is a collective of individuals who pool their collective power and decision-making capacities for the advancement of that society's interests, values and goals.[1] Tyranny is a societal system in which the power to influence the whole of society is aggregated into a small population that exerts unfair and dishonorable discretionary power over others, with "dishonorable" referring to lawful dishonor — the refusal to give voice and ear to other equal-standing members of a contractual arrangement, the contract in question being the social contract that forms the bedrock of civilized society.[1]

## Application

Applying the heuristic, Deschamps described a pipeline in which tyrants meet to draft new policy that is then crammed down the public's throat: leaders return home and push the policy to lobbyists, the media, think tanks, propagandists, social media influencers, city councils, school boards, Hollywood and beyond, presenting it to citizens as a foregone conclusion in which they had no role.[1] He asserted that acts of Congress are written by think tanks, lobbyists and globalists in foreign lands within shadowy conferences like the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg]] meetings; that school board policies and corporate culture initiatives are formulated by professional propagandists — naming [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[esg|ESG]] and [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]] initiatives; and that standards of medical care and public health are drafted by industry leaders and NGOs loyal to the globalists.[1]

The conclusion he drew was that the United States has a false democracy in which citizens do not influence government but are governed, their only real choice being to accept or reject decisions made without their involvement or consent — the same degree of involvement, he wrote, that a slave has with a slave master.[1] Against this he invoked what he called a universal, natural and divine law: "that which we are affected by we have the right to influence with our voice and defend with our arms," adding that a right not invoked has no power and that inaction constitutes consent to tyranny.[1]

## Proposed remedies

Deschamps closed with an organizing program: citizens should create their own think tanks and councils to strategize against globalist decision-makers, take back city councils, school boards and state legislatures, hold elected officials to account, and remove those who violate the public trust using the recall provisions that exist in every state — enacting such provisions where they do not exist.[1] Anticipating a hard and dirty fight, he called for support networks for patriots willing to do the fighting.[1]
