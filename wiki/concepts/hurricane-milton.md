# Hurricane Milton

**Hurricane Milton** was a major Atlantic hurricane that threatened Florida's Gulf Coast in October 2024, arriving less than two weeks after [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]] flooded communities across the Southeast.[1] Late on Monday, October 7, 2024, Milton was a Category 5 storm with winds reaching 180 mph and was tracking toward a possible landfall in Tampa Bay; it was subsequently downgraded to a Category 4 while remaining aimed at the Florida Gulf Coast.[1]

## Political conflict over storm preparation

The storm became the focus of a political dispute between Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] after reports that DeSantis had not taken Harris's phone calls ahead of landfall.[1] Harris accused DeSantis of "political gamesmanship" and said he was being selfish and "utterly responsible" for not taking her call.[1] In a Monday evening interview with Fox News, DeSantis said he was "leveraging all resources available, including from the federal government" and had been in contact with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and with officials from [[fema|the Federal Emergency Management Agency]], calling the suggestion that his focus on Floridians was selfish "delusional."[1]

The criticism of Harris was bipartisan in tone: CNN senior political commentator [[scott-jennings|Scott Jennings]] said "she's attacking the governor of a state that's about to get lashed with one of the worst storms" and asked, "Is she crazy?"[1] On Tuesday Biden said DeSantis was doing a great job and that he had given the governor his personal phone number.[1]

## Strain on federal disaster programs

Reporting cited in the brief described the federal government as potentially nearing a collapse of its capacity to respond to major disasters as Milton approached.[1] FEMA's disaster relief fund, the [[small-business-administration|Small Business Administration]]'s loan programs for stricken businesses and homeowners, and FEMA's flood insurance program were all described as possibly within weeks of running out of cash, based on remarks by Biden, Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] and insurance analysts — even as FEMA sought to assure the public it had enough money for its "life-saving" responsibilities for Helene and Milton.[1] Lawmakers of both parties called for Congress to return to Washington before the November election to approve additional disaster funding, but fiscal conservatives in the House balked and Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said he had no plans to bring members back.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright framed the episode as evidence that "the politicos of the DC Swamp" were signalling to the Harris campaign that its narrative strategy was failing, noting Biden's decision to side with DeSantis amid "the Hurricane Milton chaos" as part of a broader hedging by establishment figures ahead of what the brief called the true October surprises.[1]
