# Stop-and-Frisk

Stop-and-frisk is a policing practice in which officers stop, question and pat down individuals on the street based on reasonable suspicion, most closely associated with New York City policing in the 1990s and 2000s and long contested by civil liberties advocates.[1]

## 2024 Trump campaign proposal

On August 20, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidential campaign rolled out a set of tougher-on-crime proposals that included a return to stop-and-frisk strategies nationwide, alongside pursuing the death penalty for child rapists and felony charges for doctors who perform transgender surgeries on children without parental consent.[1] Trump unveiled the package in a law-enforcement-centered speech flanked by local Michigan sheriffs, in which he accused Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] of waging an "anti-police crusade" and of supporting "extremist, pro-crime, anti-police" policies such as "defund the police."[1]

"We will never even think about defunding the police," Trump said. "The concept is so chilling and so horrible," adding a pledge to "re-fund the police."[1] He criticized low arrest rates under President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Harris, saying the administration "goes after guys like me but they don't go after people that kill people," and blamed Harris for a crime spike since he left office.[1] The rollout was reported by the [[new-york-post|New York Post]].[1]

Badlands commentary treated the death-penalty element of the platform, rather than stop-and-frisk itself, as the headline threat to what it calls the [[deep-state|Deep State]], framing it as a promise to execute pedophiles and [[child-trafficking|child traffickers]].[1]
