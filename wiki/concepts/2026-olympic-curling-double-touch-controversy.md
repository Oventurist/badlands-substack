# 2026 Olympic Curling Double-Touch Controversy

The **2026 Olympic curling double-touch controversy** was a rules dispute that engulfed the curling competition at the [[2026-winter-olympics|2026 Winter Olympics]], in which both the Canadian men's and women's teams were accused of committing "double-touch" infractions during round-robin play.[1]

## Origins

The dispute erupted during Canada's 8–6 men's victory over Sweden, when Swedish curler [[oskar-eriksson|Oskar Eriksson]] alleged that Canadian third [[marc-kennedy|Marc Kennedy]] had touched his stone after release — a violation that, if confirmed, requires the stone's removal under [[world-curling|World Curling]] rules.[1] Kennedy vehemently denied wrongdoing, responding with profanity on the ice ("I haven't done it once. You can fuck off"), to which Eriksson replied that he would produce video showing the touch occurring "two meters over the hog line."[1] Kennedy later suggested his team may have been deliberately targeted in what he characterized as a planned effort to catch violations.[1]

According to commentary in the Badlands Brief, the Swedes were sufficiently frustrated by what they described as serial double-touching that they set up what amounted to a surveillance sting operation to capture the infractions on video.[1]

## Spread of the dispute

In a subsequent women's match against Switzerland, Canadian skip [[rachel-homan|Rachel Homan]] was penalized for the same double-touch violation, resulting in the removal of her stone.[1] Homan protested the call, but officials upheld it without video review, citing the sport's current officiating standards.[1] The controversy widened as other nations, including the British men's squad, also had stones removed under the same rule enforcement.[1]

## Governing-body response

World Curling acknowledged that umpires cannot visually monitor every delivery infraction, and clarified that while accredited Olympic Broadcasting Services may film within venues, the viral footage circulating on social media was not authorized.[1] Officials indicated that no violations were recorded during designated umpire observation periods.[1] World Curling also issued a verbal warning to Canadian officials over Kennedy's language, noting that further inappropriate behavior under rule R.19 would bring additional sanctions.[1] In response to the uproar, the body adjusted its officiating procedures by increasing the number of monitoring officials at subsequent matches to better enforce the hog-line rule.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the affair as the biggest scandal of the Games to that point and drew an analogy to American election disputes, joking that "if this were an election investigation, they'd prosecute the Swedes" — a line she said was borne out when Canada accused the Swedes of improperly recording violations inside the arena, which she paraphrased as "How dare you catch us cheating?!"[1]
