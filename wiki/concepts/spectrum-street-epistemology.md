# Spectrum Street Epistemology

Spectrum Street epistemology is a discussion technique in which a series of lines is inscribed on the floor to represent a spectrum ranging from agreement through neutrality to disagreement; participants physically stand at the point matching their view of a stated proposition and are then invited to explain and examine their reasoning.[1] It is intended to foster critical thinking by making positions visible and requiring their justification, and versions of the exercise have circulated widely online.[1]

## Use in the Rivers case

The method became a subject of controversy in Canada when [[leigh-rivers|Leigh Rivers]], who teaches chemical and physical sciences at the [[university-of-toronto|University of Toronto]], ran the exercise with his students and was sanctioned by his department chair for doing so.[1] An opening prompt — "Vegetarians are morally superior to omnivores" — drew no objection, but later statements did, including one referring to Prof. [[jordan-peterson|Jordan Peterson]], two concerning political bias and non-Western approaches in science, and the proposition "Men can become women," which the chair described as traumatic.[1]

The episode has been used to argue that a technique explicitly designed to surface and test disagreement is now itself treated as transgressive within institutions ostensibly committed to inquiry — the concern that animates bodies such as the [[heterodox-academy|Heterodox Academy]].[1]
