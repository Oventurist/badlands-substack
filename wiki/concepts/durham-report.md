# Durham Report

The Durham Report is the final report of former Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s investigation into the origins of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]]/[[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] inquiry, portions of which remain withheld or redacted.[1]

## Missing and withheld material

In January 2026, [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] filed a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] seeking fully unredacted records from the Durham probe.[1] Among the specific items targeted were the 14 missing pages of the Durham Report appendix, [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]'s 2016 White House briefing notes, the DOJ Inspector General report critiquing the [[fbi|FBI]]'s handling of Crossfire Hurricane, and all unredacted transcripts or notes from Durham's interviews with key figures including [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], Brennan, [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and [[john-podesta|John Podesta]].[1]

The suit further seeks unredacted records of Durham's referrals to other federal agencies, emails between [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]] and major media outlets, the Clinton campaign or the [[democratic-national-committee|DNC]], and communications involving [[british-intelligence|British Intelligence]] head Sir [[richard-dearlove|Richard Dearlove]], U.S. intelligence officials, [[stefan-halper|Stefan Halper]] and [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]].[1]

## Public reception at the June 2023 hearing

The report's findings were aired publicly when [[john-durham|John Durham]] testified before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on June 21, 2023.[2] Durham told members that the report established that the [[fbi|FBI]] had relied on the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] and FISA applications "knowing there was likely material originating from a political campaign or political opponent," and that the [[cia|CIA]] was aware of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s plan to tie [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Russia.[2] Democratic members argued the report's small number of prosecutions — the [[michael-sussmann|Sussmann]] and Danchenko trials — showed it had produced nothing, while Truth Community commentators argued the opposite: that the damning material was deliberately held back and the hearing served as a deliberate "nothingburger" absorbing the left's attacks.[2] See [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|Durham House Judiciary Testimony (2023)]].[2]


## Findings on Crossfire Hurricane

The Durham report alleged that the FBI and the Justice Department had no proper basis for launching their investigation into alleged ties between Donald Trump's 2016 presidential campaign and Russia.[3] Durham concluded that "based on the evidence gathered in the multiple exhaustive and costly investigations of these matters," including the special counsel's own inquiry, "neither U.S. law enforcement nor the Intelligence Community appears to have possessed any actual evidence of collusion in their holdings at the commencement of the Crossfire Hurricane investigation."[3] House Intelligence Committee Chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] said shortly after the report's release that it outlined "serious errors and shortcomings" in the FBI's handling of the investigation.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarized the report's implications bluntly: officials knew the Steele dossier was fake, knew the Clinton campaign and the DNC had commissioned it, and fabricated the case against Trump anyway, tearing the country apart with a narrative they knew to be false in order to obstruct a sitting president and, by extension, the will of the people.[3] She argued this amounted to treason rather than the "made-up crime" of obstruction of government used against figures such as [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[3]

## Release and initial coverage (May 2023)

The report was released on Monday, May 15, 2023, after a review compiled over three years by the Trump-administration appointee [[john-durham|John Durham]].[4] CNN reported that the 300-plus page document concluded the [[fbi|FBI]] should never have launched a full investigation into connections between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign and Russia during the 2016 election, and that the bureau had used "raw, unanalyzed, and uncorroborated intelligence" to open [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] while applying a markedly different standard when weighing concerns about alleged election interference involving [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s campaign.[4]

## Findings on the Clinton Foundation investigations

The report also revealed that the FBI had three concurrent investigations touching the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] and the Clinton campaign, which were shut down by top bureau leadership during the 2016 election year.[4] [[techno-fog|Techno Fog]] noted at The Reactionary that the first involved a confidential human source reporting that a foreign government intended to funnel a contribution to Clinton's anticipated campaign to gain influence, and that FBI headquarters delayed a field office's [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|FISA]] request for four months because Clinton was "involved."[4] The second involved the same source reporting in November 2015 that another foreign government sought to contribute in exchange for protection of its interests, and subsequently making a $2,700 donation on behalf of a foreign insider in violation of the ban on foreign-national contributions.[4]

## Narrative management around the report

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that coverage of the Durham Report illustrated [[overtons-goalpost|Overton's Goalpost]] in full force — the sequence by which a claim is first denied and its evidence suppressed, then partially conceded, then admitted as mostly true but immaterial, then admitted as entirely true and damning while insisting everyone already knew it and that conspiracy theorists were right only by accident, before distraction operations are deployed.[4] He characterized the dialectic as a sign the Truth Community was winning rather than cause for frustration.[4]

## Trump's response to the final report

Responding through a spokesman on Monday, May 15, 2023, former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the report "proves" a coordinated effort by the federal government to interfere with the 2016 U.S. election.[5] Writing on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], Trump said: "WOW! After extensive research, Special Counsel John Durham concludes the FBI never should have launched the Trump-Russia Probe! In other words, the American Public was scammed, just as it is being scammed right now by those who don't want to see GREATNESS for AMERICA!"[5] He added that "The Durham Report spells out in great detail the Democrat Hoax that was perpetrated upon me and the American people," compared it to "2020 Presidential Election Fraud, just like 'stuffing' the ballot boxes, only more so," and said that "with an honest Media, we are looking at the Crime of the Century!"[5] The reaction was reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] under the framing that the report concluded the [[fbi|FBI]] had opened the Trump probe despite having no verified intelligence.[5]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that while the report did less than some had hoped, dismissing it as meaningless was "a doomer's take," predicting that legacy media efforts to spin the findings would fail to persuade anyone not already hostile to Trump.[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] emphasized what he called the temporal significance of the report: because the Russia collusion narrative marked the start of the Deep State's "narrative blitzkrieg" against Trump and the MAGA movement, its exposure might mark the start of something equally consequential in the other direction.[5]
