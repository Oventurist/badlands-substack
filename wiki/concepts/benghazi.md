# Benghazi

Benghazi is Libya's second-largest city and the principal port of the Cyrenaica region. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," Benghazi is framed as a central hub in what the source describes as a CIA-led operation to overthrow [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], train Islamist fighters, and funnel taxpayer money to terrorist organizations.

According to the article, Benghazi served as the headquarters of the anti-Gaddafi rebel movement in 2011. It cites a 2011 Guardian report describing how [[john-mccain|John McCain]] visited the city in April of that year and praised the rebel fighters as "heroes," calling them "not Al-Qaeda" despite evidence that they were members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], an Al-Qaeda affiliate. The source claims that McCain was the most senior U.S. official to visit Benghazi, and that his visit was designed to provide legitimacy to the rebel movement and draw media attention to build support for NATO intervention.

The article asserts that after Gaddafi's fall, Benghazi became "a regional hub for Islamist extremists seeking to hone their combat skills." Citing a 2013 McClatchy DC report, the source claims that about a dozen Syrians arrived at Benghazi's airport each week for insurgent training, carrying fake Libyan passports when they departed. It notes that fighters from Tunisia and Algeria also trained there, and that the Syrian training pipeline was "better organized and financed than had been realized."

According to the article, the [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]]—commanded by [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]]—was assigned security at the U.S. consulate in Benghazi, and "literally did nothing to stop the attack." The source claims that Benghazi's importance to the CIA and shadow government was that it served as a relocation site for Al-Qaeda training camps after 9/11, allowing the intelligence apparatus to continue feeding a constant supply of new fighters to kill American soldiers in Afghanistan. In the source's framework, Benghazi was "vitally important because we were now fighting a war in Afghanistan because of 9/11. That's where most of the Al-Qaeda training camps were before 9/11, and now the CIA needed to relocate them."
