# Civil Rights Act

The **Civil Rights Act** is cited in the Badlands Media essay "On Nations and Natural Law" (5 October 2025) as a notable example of government legislating in the moral sphere when, on the author's reading of [[lysander-spooner|Lysander Spooner]], it should be confined to the legal sphere.[1]

[[natural-law|Natural Law]] as Spooner frames it distinguishes the legal — pertaining to property rights — from the moral, which concerns the ethical dimension of social relationships and is adjudicated in the court of public opinion rather than by a prescribed system of enforcement.[1] The essay argues, while acknowledging the position is controversial, that the Act's prime legacy has been usurpations of personal liberty such as a baker being compelled to bake a cake for homosexual customers, thereby infringing his legal right of property over his own time and resources and depriving him of freedom of association exercised in accordance with his morality.[1]

The author adds that a purely social remedy would remain available in such cases: a baker who set up shop in a predominantly homosexual community and insisted on refusing service would not likely be in business for very long.[1]
