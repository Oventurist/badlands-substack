# 2019 Hong Kong Anti-Extradition Protests

The Anti-Extradition Law Amendment Bill Movement was the mass protest campaign that swept [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]] in 2019 in opposition to a proposed law giving the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] the ability to extradite Hong Kong citizens to the mainland for trial.[1]

## Origins

The extradition bill was proposed while participants in the 2014 [[umbrella-movement|Umbrella Movement]] were awaiting sentencing, which came in 2018.[1] Opponents objected that mainland trials would take place within what is widely regarded as a highly corrupt and unfair judicial system.[1] Activist [[samuel-chu|Samuel Chu]] observed that his father's two-year sentence in Hong Kong courts for organizing peaceful protests would have been a life sentence had the case been tried on the mainland.[1]

## The protests

Two million Hong Kongers took to the streets to voice distrust of the CCP and to state as loudly as possible that they did not want their citizens extradited.[1]

## Consequences

In response to the protests on the ground and to overseas advocacy efforts, [[china|China]] implemented the [[hong-kong-national-security-law-2020|National Security Law]], which was subsequently used to jail [[hong-kong-pro-democracy-movement|pro-democracy]] leaders and to destroy the newspaper [[apple-daily|Apple Daily]].[1]

## Timeline as recounted in The Patriot Chronicles

[[ghost-of-patrick-henry|Ghost of Patrick Henry]] used the protests as the opening illustration of his June 2023 introduction to [[patriot-chronicles|The Patriot Chronicles]].[2] He dated the crisis to March 2019, when the Hong Kong government — described as acting under CCP influence — introduced legislation permitting the extradition to China of Hong Kong citizens and Chinese defectors wanted by Beijing for their vocal opposition.[2] A protest outside the legislature on 12 June 2019 succeeded in having the bill temporarily suspended from consideration, which immediately led to a larger protest of two million people demanding full withdrawal.[2]

The resulting government crackdown, he wrote, was witnessed by the world: escalating violent force by Hong Kong police, mass arrests, crackdowns on pro-democracy media outlets, the exile of prominent activists and lawmakers, and accusations of murders and disappearances of prominent protest leaders.[2]

## The American flag demonstration

On 1 December 2019 protesters assembled waving American flags and singing the American national anthem, which the author called a heart-warming display of unity and defiance — as if seeking to conjure the ghosts of America's Founding Fathers to vanquish their oppressors.[2] He argued that the demonstrators were in fact attempting to invoke the American Spirit and imbue it within modern-day Americans, trusting those patriots would awaken in time to save them, or at least their children, from what he called the soul-crushing despotism of a transnational cartel.[2] Noting that every participant knowingly risked their life, he observed that within a few months the entire world was brought to its knees by COVID-19, bringing "a convenient and swift end to the Hong Kong freedom protests."[2]
