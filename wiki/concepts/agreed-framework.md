# Agreed Framework

The Agreed Framework was the 1994 nuclear accord between the United States and [[north-korea|North Korea]], announced by President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] on October 18, 1994 and described by him as a landmark nuclear agreement that was "good for the United States, good for our allies, and good for the safety of the entire world."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article treats it as the founding fraud of the modern [[sanctions|sanctions]] racket.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Terms

Citing the History Channel, the article notes the agreement was just four pages long.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) North Korea would shut down its main nuclear reactor at Yongbyon, abandon two others, and seal fuel that could potentially be used to create a nuclear weapon.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) In exchange, the United States would provide oil to make up for the fuel lost from the dismantled plants and would build two new "light fuel" plants from which extracting nuclear material would be harder.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The deal was designed to put the brakes on North Korea's nuclear program and end years of rising tension that had approached war.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## The article's critique

North Korea broke the agreement almost immediately, resuming weapons development at an underground facility.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article asks why the United States would help North Korea build nuclear plants if the goal was to prevent nuclear weapons, and concludes the deal was never meant to prevent proliferation — that was the scam.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The key to the deal, the author argues, was oil.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) North Korea was under sanctions at the time; Clinton promised to lift them and supply oil in return for a halt to weapons work.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) But supplying oil allowed Pyongyang to reallocate cash toward its nuclear weapons program, actually accelerating development — in the article's words, "we purposely sped up their development of nuclear weapons."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The deal, it concludes, was always about money.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Connection to Iran

The article's pivotal question is where the oil promised to North Korea would come from.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Five months after the Agreed Framework, in March 1995, Clinton imposed severe sanctions on [[iran|Iran]]'s oil industry under Executive Order 12957, followed by Executive Order 12959 in May 1995 ending all U.S.–Iran trade.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The author reads this as no coincidence: Clinton and the CIA/shadow government intended to source Iranian oil to deliver to North Korea, with [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]] — the world's foremost sanctions-busting oil trader with contacts in both countries — the plausible intermediary.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The alleged Clinton share of that oil deal is offered as the real reason for Rich's presidential pardon.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
