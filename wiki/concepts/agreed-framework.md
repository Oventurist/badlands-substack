# Agreed Framework

The Agreed Framework was the nuclear accord announced by President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] on October 18, 1994 between the United States and [[north-korea|North Korea]]. It is treated in the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]] as the founding transaction in a decades-long pattern of using nuclear diplomacy as cover for commodity deals.

Quoting the History Channel, the article recalls Clinton's announcement — "This agreement is good for the United States, good for our allies, and good for the safety of the entire world" — and describes the deal as designed "to put the brakes on North Korea's nuclear program" and end years of rising tension, including a near war.

The agreement itself ran only four pages. Under its terms North Korea would shut down its main nuclear reactor at Yongbyon, abandon two others, and seal fuel that could be used to create a weapon. In exchange, the United States "would provide oil to make up for the fuel lost from the dismantled plants and would build two new 'light fuel' plants from which it would be harder to extract nuclear materials."

The Badlands author argues that the framework was never intended to work. North Korea, the article states, broke the agreement almost immediately and resumed weapons development at an underground facility. The essay questions why the United States would help North Korea build nuclear plants at all if the goal was non-proliferation, and identifies oil as the real key: by lifting [[sanctions|sanctions]] and supplying fuel, Washington freed Pyongyang's cash for weapons work, in the author's words purposely speeding up its nuclear development. Five months later Clinton imposed sweeping sanctions on [[iran|Iran]]'s oil industry, a juxtaposition the article reads as evidence that Iranian oil was to be routed to North Korea, plausibly through the sanctions-busting trader [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]].

The essay further argues that the 1994 negotiation established the "working relationship" between Clinton and [[kim-jong-il|Kim Jong Il]] that explains why, in 2009, Kim specifically requested Clinton as the negotiator for the release of two detained American journalists.
