# Arms Export Control Act

The Arms Export Control Act (AECA) of 1976 is the United States statute governing American arms exports and military assistance, requiring that US arms transfers align with US foreign policy, national security interests, and international peace and stability.[1]

## Relation to Israel's Qualitative Military Edge

The AECA is one of the legal pillars of the doctrine protecting [[israel|Israel]]'s [[qualitative-military-edge|Qualitative Military Edge]].[1] The doctrine's origins are traced to the Foreign Military Sales Act of 1968 — under which the United States approved the sale of F-4 Phantom II fighter jets to Israel, establishing a precedent for maintaining its qualitative edge over neighboring countries — and to the AECA itself.[1] In 2008, Congress passed the Naval Vessel Transfer Act, authorizing the sale and transfer of excess US naval vessels to foreign allies; contained within that legislation was an amendment to the AECA that explicitly established and defined Israel's Qualitative Military Edge under Section 36(h), codified at 22 U.S. Code § 2776, "Reports and certifications to Congress on military exports."[1]

These provisions were cited in May 2025 as the likely constraint on the [[2025-us-saudi-arms-deal|$142 billion US–Saudi arms deal]], particularly any transfer of [[f-35|F-35]] fighter jets to Riyadh.[1]
