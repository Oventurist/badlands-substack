# 2019 Baghdad Embassy Attack

On December 31, 2019, protesters attacked the United States embassy in Baghdad, scaling the walls and forcing the gates of the compound as hundreds demonstrated against American airstrikes on an Iran-backed militia group in [[iraq|Iraq]].[1]

## Cause

The attack was a response to five United States airstrikes carried out on Sunday, December 29, 2019 against facilities in Iraq and Syria controlled by [[kataib-hizballah|Kataib Hezbollah]], which killed at least 25 people and wounded 51.[1] Those strikes were the first significant American military response to weeks of deadly rocket attacks by the Iran-backed group on US-Iraqi targets.[1] The militia formally sits under the control of Iraq's prime minister, and the strikes caused tension with Baghdad, where officials accused the United States of attacking without sufficient evidence, violating Iraqi sovereignty and threatening the country's security.[1]

## Participants

The pro-Iranian demonstrators were mostly from the [[popular-mobilization-forces|Popular Mobilization Units]], a coalition of predominantly Shiite militias formed in 2014 to fight ISIS and recognised under a 2016 Iraqi law as an independent military force answering directly to the prime minister.[1] Three leaders of powerful militia groups were seen at the protest, including Kataib Hezbollah leader [[abu-mahdi-al-muhandis|Abu Mahdi al-Muhandis]].[1] Questions were raised as to whether Iraqi forces had allowed the protesters to reach the embassy, a highly fortified building in an area that is usually restricted.[1]

## Trump's response

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened [[iran|Iran]] the same day, tweeting from Florida that "Iran will be held fully responsible for lives lost, or damage incurred, at any of our facilities. They will pay a very BIG PRICE! This is not a Warning, it is a Threat."[1] Later that evening, entering a New Year's Eve gala at Mar-a-Lago, he told reporters he did not want war but that conflict "would go very quickly" and would not be a good idea for Iran.[1] Trump also emphasised in a call with Iraqi Prime Minister [[adil-abdul-mahdi|Adil Abdul-Mahdi]] that Iraq had a responsibility to protect the embassy and American personnel.[1]

Three days later, on 3 January 2020, the United States killed [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]] and al-Muhandis in a drone strike near Baghdad International Airport, an event Badlands Media treats as the direct sequel to the embassy attack and the culmination of Trump's warning.[1] The article emphasises the chain of connections it sees running Iraq–Iran–[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1]
