# Disaster Opportunism

Disaster opportunism is a term used in Badlands Media commentary, defined by contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] as "taking advantage of disasters for personal or financial gain."[1] The concept was elaborated at length in the August 17, 2023 Badlands News Brief in response to the post-fire land-buying frenzy following the [[lahaina-wildfires|Lahaina wildfires]].[1]

## Ethical framing

Deschamps argued that disaster opportunism is not inherently wrong: damaged property must be restored, and those performing restoration are entitled to reasonable compensation.[1] The ethical line, in his framing, is crossed when a party charges exorbitant fees or actively hampers recovery efforts in order to increase profit — conduct he described as ethically dubious but not necessarily criminal.[1] Investors who "swoop in to gain off the losses of others" — described as vultures — fall into the same ethically questionable but lawful category.[1]

## The disaster creation question

The commentary's central claim is that the more serious question is whether disasters themselves are engineered: "Was this tragedy curated for the vultures and disaster opportunists? And how deep and dark is the disaster creation machine in general?"[1] The cited precedent is Hurricane Katrina, where post-event findings indicated that the Army Corps of Engineers and other regulators had failed to properly maintain the New Orleans levees.[1]

## The development pattern

Deschamps outlined a five-step pattern he associated with speculative redevelopment: settle and slowly develop an area; build public squares, markets, and high-population services that make it attractive; manipulate the market so housing supply stays below demand and values rise; zone for lower-income workers and use that workforce to build higher-income commercial or industrial zones; then gentrify by displacing the lower-income population through rising costs, eminent domain, or disaster.[1] A destroyed district such as Lahaina, he argued, checks nearly every box a whale investor seeks — high population attraction, high growth potential, low-cost distressed property, and recent vacancy.[1]

## Accountability critique

The essay linked disaster opportunism to what it called psychopathy and parasitism — the willful harming, deceiving, or coercing of others for personal gain — and asked whether individuals demonstrating such capacity should be disqualified from positions of trust such as government office or fiduciary roles.[1] Modern societies, it argued, operate "disbursed culpability systems" in which institutional policies survive the resignation of figureheads and no one is truly held responsible, a condition it presented as evidence that the public has been defanged and root causes are never addressed.[1]
