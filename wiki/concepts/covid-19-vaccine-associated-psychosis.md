# COVID-19 Vaccine-Associated Psychosis

**COVID-19 vaccine-associated psychosis** refers to reported cases of new-onset psychosis occurring in individuals shortly after receiving COVID-19 vaccines, a phenomenon examined in a peer-reviewed systematic review published in the journal Frontiers in Psychiatry on April 12, 2024.[1] Psychosis denotes symptoms arising when a person has difficulty differentiating reality from fantasy, with hallucinations and delusions the two key types.[1]

## The systematic review

The review examined 21 articles describing 24 cases of psychosis symptoms following vaccination.[1] Its authors concluded that the "data suggest a potential link between young age, mRNA, and viral vector vaccines with new-onset psychosis within 7 days post-vaccination," and added that "collecting data on vaccine-related psychiatric effects is crucial for prevention, and an algorithm for monitoring and treating mental health reactions post-vaccination is necessary for comprehensive management."[1]

Of the 24 cases, 13 involved female patients and the median age of participants was 36 years.[1] Twenty-two patients — reported as 91.2 percent — had no specific history of somatic illness or comorbidities.[1] In 33.3 percent of cases the [[pfizer|Pfizer]] mRNA vaccine "potentially induced adverse psychiatric events," while the viral vector [[astrazeneca|AstraZeneca]] vaccine, marketed as [[vaxzevria|Vaxzevria]], was linked to psychotic symptoms in 25 percent of cases.[1] Psychotic symptoms were reported after the first shot in 45.8 percent of incidences and after the second dose in fifty percent.[1] The findings were reported in the United States by [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]].[1]

## Commentary

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], treated the findings as a crystallization of what he calls the mind war — an instance in which an adversary's biological weaponry operates directly on the minds of citizens.[1] Drawing on his background as a social worker, Esler argued that the individuals captured in such studies represent only those fortunate enough to have a support network that eventually got their condition recorded in the medical system, leaving unknown numbers of isolated victims without any record of harm.[1]

Esler further contended that such cases became publishable only because the Overton Window around COVID had begun to expand, permitting medical academics to consider the possibility that the vaccines were something other than safe and effective.[1] He described the review as "the trickle before the downpour," warning that so long as what he termed vaccine mind control retained its grip, injuries would continue to accumulate without the possibility of a vaccine-injury explanation even being whispered.[1]
