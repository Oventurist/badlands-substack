# Threshold State

A **threshold state** is a country that has acquired the technical capacity to build a nuclear weapon but has not actually assembled one. Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] applies the term to [[iran|Iran]] in his analysis of the [[twelve-day-war|12-Day War]], arguing that Iran's status as a threshold power — rather than any demonstrated weapons program — is what drives the crisis.[1]

## The enrichment benchmark

By all accounts Iran possesses the capability to enrich uranium to 60 percent; enrichment to 90 percent is the point at which weapons-grade material is being produced.[1] Iran therefore is not a nuclear-capable state, and it remains unclear whether it was even pursuing nuclear weapons, but it has, in DeLarme's football metaphor, "basically made it to the five-yard line."[1] As a party to the [[treaty-on-the-non-proliferation-of-nuclear-weapons|Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons]], Iran has the right under international law to conduct the enrichment activity it is conducting.[1]

## Strategic implications

DeLarme argues that Israeli leadership views Iran's threshold status as an existential crisis but also as an opportunity.[1] [[israel|Israel]] currently carries the "big stick" in the region and leverages that fact, in the view of some observers acting as the regional "bully"; if Iran were to achieve or be supplied nuclear weapons, that outcome would take Israel down several pegs and would be perceived as an existential threat.[1]

He also notes that there are two diverging schools of thought on whether more nuclear-armed states make the world safer: those who hold that the fewer nuclear states the better, and those who hold that nuclear weapons exert a generally stabilizing effect on a region.[1]

## The "suicide" objection

A common claim is that a nuclear-armed Iran would immediately strike Israel — the bill of goods, in DeLarme's phrasing, sold behind so much "Iran can't have nuclear weapons" rhetoric.[1] He counters that such an action would mean the immediate suicide of the Iranian regime and could mean the deaths of over 90 million Iranians should Israel and the United States respond with scorched-earth retaliation, noting that not all Iranians are allegiant to the current regime.[1] The public is expected to believe that, unlike the rest of the world's elite, Iranian leadership is so unhinged and unconcerned with self-preservation that it would use a weapon despite certain doom — a posture DeLarme says is not reflected in the Iranian regime's behavior in recent conflicts.[1]
