# Control Through Language

"Control through language" is the thesis of a February 5, 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], subtitled "Words Matter," arguing that the vocabulary a population is given — and the vocabulary taken away from it — determines the range of thoughts that population is capable of having.[1]

## Origin in Orwell

Carlson traces the idea to [[george-orwell|George Orwell]]'s *1984*, in which the protagonist Winston works for the government media rewriting history to fit each new narrative while destroying evidence of prior ones, a process Carlson calls "memory-holing."[1] He highlights a scene in which Winston speaks with a colleague compiling a dictionary designed to contain fewer and fewer words, on the premise that limiting words limits thought.[1] Carlson finds the concept "really interesting": in theory, removing words alters not only how something is described but prevents certain thoughts from ever forming.[1]

He tests the idea against examples — eliminating racist words to eliminate racist thoughts, removing hateful words to remove hate, deleting the vocabulary of freedom so that freedom is never sought, comparing it to being unable to crave a food never heard of, seen or tasted.[1] His qualification is that humans possess inherent needs, desires and expectations that do not disappear with the words: lacking a word for "hunger" does not stop hunger pangs, and the inherent need for justice and freedom survives linguistic manipulation.[1]

## Adding words

The inverse operation, Carlson argues, is introducing words to make audiences "buy in" to concepts or narratives.[1] He offers several cases:

- "Conspiracy theory" functions as a shut-off valve, causing people to stop pursuing a subject once it is so labelled — though he says this worked better formerly than now.[1]
- "Cis," denoting identification with one's birth sex, reframes normality as one option among two, so that the opposition is no longer normal versus trans but cis versus trans, normalising the latter.[1]
- "Denier" labels — "climate denier," "transphobe" — shift the perceived craziness from the person holding an unusual belief to the person refusing to accept it, so that healthy belief becomes merely an alternative and logic becomes hateful and repressive.[1]
- "Plus size model" alters perception of the person described; Carlson asks why the qualifier is needed at all, while noting that the unqualified word "model" itself conveys thinness.[1]
- "Birthing people" in place of "mothers" reduces motherhood to a one-time, factory-like event, stripping out ongoing love and sacrifice, weakening the mother–child bond and, in his view, enlarging the role of government and government schools in shaping a child's life.[1]

## Compliance and thought

Carlson argues that compliance with narratives diminishes the ability and desire to think independently: displaying pronouns on a social media profile "shrinks the pasture" one's thoughts have to roam, until thoughts are effectively imprisoned.[1] He asks how one argues with a person whose mind is incarcerated, and describes speech emptied of meaning as "a duck quacking" — a disconnect between thought and words, in which people parrot approved language to avoid friction without recognising that resistance is what makes them stronger.[1]

## Prescription

His remedy is personal rather than confrontational: resist the pressure applied to oneself, refuse to comply, stay true to what one knows, and avoid arguing with people who have lost the capacity for logic, since argument only drives them deeper into irrationality.[1] He states he will not use another person's chosen identity terms while also declining to insult them, and closes with the line from the 1983 film *WarGames*, "The only winning move is not to play," reasoning that the game cannot be played without an adversary and that emotion-driven actors cannot be fuelled if others refuse to engage.[1]

## Political correctness as antecedent

Carlson also asserts that "political correctness" was created as a weapon of control in pre-Nazi Germany and later "dropped on" US university campuses in the early 1990s, and attributes the creation of [[wokeism|wokeism]] to Europe, especially think tanks run by the [[city-of-london|City of London]].[1]
