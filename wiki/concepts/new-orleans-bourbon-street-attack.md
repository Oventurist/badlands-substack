# New Orleans Bourbon Street Attack (January 1, 2025)

The New Orleans Bourbon Street attack was a vehicle-ramming attack carried out in the early hours of Wednesday, January 1, 2025, when a man drove a pickup truck through a crowd of New Year's revelers on Bourbon Street in the French Quarter of New Orleans.[1] At least 15 people were killed and dozens more were injured before the driver died in a shootout with police.[1]

## The attacker and the ISIS link

The driver was described as a 42-year-old Army veteran from Texas.[1] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said in a short address from Camp David on the evening of January 1 that the attacker was "inspired by" the Islamic State terrorist organization ([[isis]]).[1] Biden said he had been briefed by federal investigators, who told him the suspect had posted videos to social media shortly before the attack indicating that he had a "desire to kill."[1] Investigators were examining the man's possible ties to the Islamic State.[1]

## State response

Louisiana Governor [[jeff-landry|Jeff Landry]] issued a State of Emergency hours after the attack, citing the need to marshal state resources ahead of the Sugar Bowl, Super Bowl LIX and Mardi Gras, and pledging that his administration would not "look the other way."[1]

## Related incidents and Badlands commentary

Authorities separately investigated whether the attack was connected to the [[las-vegas-cybertruck-explosion]] outside the Trump International Hotel in Las Vegas the same morning; Sheriff Kevin McMahill of the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department said the Las Vegas blast was believed to be "an isolated incident" but that a connection had not been ruled out.[1]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright wrote that days like these were why many believed "the ONLY road to mass catharsis comes on the back of an exhaustive and transparent justice campaign against the most subversive and expansive criminal network in history," adding, "Friends must respect us. Enemies must fear us. Foreign AND Domestic."[1] Contributor Jon Herold predicted that more terror events would likely occur before Inauguration Day, then set for January 20, 2025.[1]

## FBI "acted alone" assessment

On January 2, 2025, [[fbi|FBI]] officials publicly identified the attacker as [[shamsud-din-jabbar|Shamsud-Din Jabbar]] and said they believed he acted alone in what they described as a terror attack inspired by ISIS.[2] Christopher Raia, deputy assistant director of the FBI's counterterrorism division, said, "Let me be very clear about this point, this was an act of terrorism. It was premeditated and an evil act," and added that the Bureau did not assess that anyone else was involved and was confident there were "no accomplices."[2] That statement marked a departure from the agency's warnings the previous day, when officials said Jabbar may not have acted alone and cited significant new developments; Raia said Jabbar had posted several videos before the attack professing support for ISIS.[2]

## Mosque guidance and CAIR

A mosque near Jabbar's Houston home, Masjid Bilal, urged its congregation not to respond to media inquiries and, if approached by the FBI, to refer to the [[council-on-american-islamic-relations|Council on American-Islamic Relations]] and the [[islamic-society-of-greater-houston|Islamic Society of Greater Houston]].[2] Breitbart described CAIR as a radical organization that had previously assisted terrorists' families, pointing to its local branch's aid to the families of the ISIS-affiliated attackers in the 2015 San Bernardino mass shooting.[2]

## Skeptical readings

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued the coverage amounted to a psychological operation preparing the public for renewed Middle East intervention, and catalogued what he saw as evidence of a sloppily staged scene: the ISIS flag on the truck was mounted upside down, which he said implied either that the attacker could not read Arabic or that those staging the scene were poorly trained.[2] He also questioned reporting that bomb-making materials were found beside a Quran opened to a passage on martyrdom, and the apparent lack of crime-scene control that allowed a reporter to walk through the property before forensics.[2]

Burning Bright, responding to the question of how "patriots" could be in control if such attacks were allowed to occur, wrote that Q drops and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] himself had repeatedly said the enemy would not go down without a fight, and that resorting to such obvious means of national subversion signaled a faction "hemmed in and boxed out."[2] He observed that the Bourbon Street attack was followed by an absurdly memetic Tesla Cybertruck incident outside a Trump property, arriving directly on the heels of a media effort to split Trump and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] optically.[2]

## FBI release of the attacker's POV video

In early January 2025 the [[fbi|FBI]] released video footage of attacker [[shamsud-din-jabbar|Shamsud-Din Jabbar]] apparently planning the New Year's Day attack, saying he had visited the city months beforehand and filmed the streets with Meta smart glasses.[3] The nearly four-minute compilation, which has no sound, combines clips from area surveillance cameras with scenes Jabbar recorded on his glasses; the agency said he had visited New Orleans twice, in October and November, before staging the attack.[3]

The first clip, dated October 31, shows Jabbar recording from his Meta glasses while riding a bicycle through the French Quarter, and a second October clip shows him testing the glasses by recording himself looking into a mirror at the home he rented.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] remarked that the POV video could serve as an advertisement for Meta glasses, pairing it with the Tesla incident in Las Vegas and asking why emerging tech featured in two terror attacks on the same day, describing it as unexpectedly obvious product placement within mass trauma events.[3] She also noted that Trump ally [[dana-white|Dana White]] joined the board of [[meta-platforms|Meta]] that same Monday, weeks after Mark Zuckerberg "bent the knee" at Mar-a-Lago, and asked where the line falls between a "golden age" and a "brave new world" for emerging technology.[3]

## Conservative media response

In "Hysteria and Error," Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the attack was exploited by figures in the conservative movement to advance an unrelated immigration narrative.[4] He described the incident as a man driving his car down Bourbon Street on New Year's morning knowing crowds would be present and intending maximum loss of life, an intention made explicit in his manifesto.[4]

DeLarme stressed that the driver, [[shamsud-din-jabbar|Shamsud-Din Jabbar]], was a 42-year-old United States citizen and Army veteran, and that the border therefore had nothing to do with the attack.[4] He criticized House speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s [[fox-news|Fox News]] appearance, Louisiana attorney general [[liz-murrill|Liz Murrill]]'s January 1 interview, and Fox's since-corrected claim that the vehicle had crossed the border, when it had merely been driven around Eagle Pass a month earlier.[4] He further noted that [[isis|ISIS]] had not claimed responsibility as of his writing, and paired the incident with the [[las-vegas-cybertruck-explosion|Las Vegas Cybertruck explosion]] as events whose perpetrators were both American military veterans.[4]

DeLarme's broader thesis was that the reaction reprised the post-9/11 consensus-manufacturing playbook — the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]], the invasions of Iraq and Afghanistan, and the wider [[war-on-terror|global war on terror]] — this time with conservatives rather than liberals as the target audience.[4]
