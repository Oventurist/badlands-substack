# Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC)

The **Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children**, commonly known as **WIC**, is a federal food assistance program whose funding was placed on hold during the government shutdown that began on October 1, 2025.[1]

## Shutdown suspension

According to Rep. [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]], R-Fla., the shutdown cut aid to 6.7 million women, infants, and children.[1] "The WIC program is now on hold because these people can't put aside their egos and political games," Luna said, adding that "WIC gives food to the most vulnerable in this country, and as a result American children are starving," and calling for the programs to be reopened immediately.[1] Coverage of the suspension framed the lapse as the "Schumer Shutdown," attributing it to Democrats led by [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]].[1]

In an interview with One America News Network, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he had not wanted the government to shut down but warned that "there could be firings, and that's their fault," and that favored Democratic projects "could be permanently cut."[1] Trump added that although "a lot of people are saying, 'Trump wanted this,'" he did not, but that he was "allowed to cut things that should have been approved in the first place, and I will probably do that."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America called it "wild that politicians shutdown the government and still get paid while benefits for infants and children are immediately cut."[1] She stated that she did not think WIC should be a federal program, on the grounds that social safety nets are not the role of the federal government, and likewise did not think legislators should be paid despite their failures.[1] She described the use of vulnerable infants and children to score political points as "gross," while adding that "the conduct of the governing class is rarely ever not gross," and expressed hope that the shutdown would continue until the federal government stopped "coloring outside its constitutional lines."[1]
