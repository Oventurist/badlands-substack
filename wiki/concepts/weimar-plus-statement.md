# Weimar+ Statement

The Weimar+ Statement was a joint declaration issued on February 12–13, 2025 by Germany, France, Poland, Italy, Spain, the United Kingdom, the European External Action Service, and the [[european-commission|European Commission]], published by the UK Government.[1] The "Weimar+" label extends the older Weimar Triangle grouping of Germany, France, and Poland to include additional European powers and European Union institutions.[1]

## Content

The signatories declared themselves "ready to enhance our support for Ukraine," committing to Ukraine's independence, sovereignty, and territorial integrity "in the face of Russia's war of aggression."[1] They said they shared the goal of supporting Ukraine until "a just, comprehensive and lasting peace is reached" — a peace guaranteeing the interests of Ukraine and of Europe itself.[1]

The statement said the group looked forward to discussing the way ahead with "our American allies," and set out three conditions: that the shared objective should be to put Ukraine in a position of strength; that Ukraine and Europe must be part of any negotiations; and that Ukraine should be provided with strong security guarantees.[1] It concluded that a just and lasting peace in Ukraine was "a necessary condition for a strong transatlantic security."[1]

## Context and commentary

The declaration came in the immediate aftermath of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s call with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]]'s address in Brussels telling European allies to "take ownership of conventional security on the continent" — developments widely read as sidelining Europe in any Ukraine settlement.[1]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] said he had not been aware there was a "Weimar+" alliance at all, and asked what exactly the United States had been defending in Europe after "winning" World War II.[1]
