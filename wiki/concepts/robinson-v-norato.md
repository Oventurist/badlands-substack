# Robinson v. Norato

*Robinson v. Norato* (July 25, 1945) is a Rhode Island Supreme Court decision cited in Badlands Media legal commentary for its distinction between how public and private international law treat the relationship among the several American states.[1]

## The cited holding

The article quotes the court as stating: "In the sense of public international law, the several states of the union are neither foreign to the United States nor are they foreign to each other. But such is not the case in the field of private international law..."[1]

## Significance in the Badlands argument

For the author, writing as [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], the quotation is the most unsettling item in an otherwise cumulative argument, because it implies that the same states can be simultaneously domestic and foreign to one another depending on which body of law is being applied.[1] The article declines to resolve the puzzle, with the author stating that he has "hunches" but is "unwilling to state them out loud just yet," pending further research, and inviting readers to supply their own analysis in the comments.[1]

The case sits alongside [[cherokee-nation-v-georgia|Cherokee Nation v. Georgia]], [[chisholm-v-georgia|Chisholm v. Georgia]], and [[people-ex-rel-attorney-general-v-naglee|People ex rel. Attorney General v. Naglee]] in the piece's survey of what it calls obscure case law raising fundamental questions about whether the United States is properly described as a nation.[1] The public/private distinction is presented as evidence that legal terminology is far more technical — and far less well understood by the public — than civic education suggests.[1]
