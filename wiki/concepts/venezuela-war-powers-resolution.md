# Venezuela War Powers Resolution

The Venezuela war powers resolution was a bipartisan measure in the 119th Congress, introduced as Senate Joint Resolution 90 and led by Sen. [[tim-kaine|Tim Kaine]], that would have required President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to obtain congressional approval before undertaking further military action in or against [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[1]

## Legislative history

The resolution advanced on January 8, 2026, on a 52–47 procedural vote, with support from several Republicans joining Democrats.[1] Under pressure from the Trump administration, the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] reversed itself on January 14, 2026, voting 51–50 to block the resolution; Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] cast the tie-breaking vote in his constitutional capacity as president of the Senate.[1] Senators [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] and [[todd-young|Todd Young]], both of whom had earlier supported advancing the measure, voted against it on final passage.[1] The resolution did not become law, and Democrats publicly decried the outcome.[1]

## Context

The vote occurred against the background of months of speculation about a US military campaign against Venezuela that never materialized. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the Venezuela buildup as a narrative that Trump ultimately "rugpulled," and drew a parallel to the escalating war rhetoric around [[iran|Iran]] in mid-January 2026, arguing that Trump's public threats function as posturing rather than telegraphed war plans.[1]
