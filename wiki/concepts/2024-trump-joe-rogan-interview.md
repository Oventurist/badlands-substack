# 2024 Trump Joe Rogan Interview

The **2024 Trump–Joe Rogan interview** was the long-form podcast conversation between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[joe-rogan|Joe Rogan]], recorded in Austin, Texas on Friday, October 25, 2024, and described by Badlands Media as "the podcast that broke the internet this weekend."[1]

## Media reaction

[[cnn|CNN]] responded with a fact-check article headlined "32 false claims Trump made to Joe Rogan."[1] The Badlands editor declined to quote or even read the CNN piece, characterizing it as "funny, and super lame," and instead directed readers to the full podcast and to commentary from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] on episode 116 of *The Liberty Den*.[1]

## Kennedy symbolism

Burning Bright focused on Trump's first aside in the conversation, concerning [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]]'s inauguration-day dress.[1] As widely noted among Anons, the dress was a replica of Jacqueline Kennedy's "Tiffany Blue" outfit, which Badlands links to Trump's recurring references to Tiffany's diamonds in the context of the 2020 election.[1]

Patrick Gunnels noted on the Friday broadcast that one of Trump's grandchildren was named Arabella, likely after the stillborn firstborn of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], prompting the question of whether the name represented "one of many promises to counter."[1] Burning Bright added that Kennedy had himself been accused of election fraud by the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] he subverted, and had been accused of being a Russian puppet of Nikita Khrushchev — a pattern he treated as directly parallel to accusations against Trump.[1] He concluded that current events represent "the continuation of a plan that went into effect in the 1960s," one intended to lead to "mass unification through historic catharsis."[1]

## Harris declination

The same brief noted that Harris campaign spokesman [[ian-sams|Ian Sams]] confirmed [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] had declined an invitation to appear on Rogan's show, citing scheduling — despite Harris being in Houston, roughly a two-and-a-half-hour drive from Rogan's Austin studio, on the same weekend.[1]
