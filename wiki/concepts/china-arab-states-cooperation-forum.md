# China-Arab States Cooperation Forum

The China-Arab States Cooperation Forum is a multilateral ministerial framework linking [[china|China]] with the member states of the [[arab-league|Arab League]]. Its 10th ministerial meeting opened in Beijing on Thursday, May 30, 2024, and was reported as the largest Arab summit China had yet hosted.[1]

## 2024 Beijing meeting

Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] told the opening ceremony that China was "ready to work with the Arab side in order to jointly defend an equal and orderly multipolar world."[1] He pledged to strengthen cooperation in oil, gas, trade, infrastructure and high technology, and to intensify promotion of cooperation in artificial intelligence, investment and the new power industry with Arab countries.[1] Xi also said China was ready to intensify cooperation with Arab states in the field of manned space flight, proposing a joint space debris observation centre and the development of space aircraft for scientific and passenger missions, and announcing Arab investment and participation in the [[beidou-navigation-satellite-system|BeiDou Navigation Satellite System]] operated by the China National Space Administration.[1]

The summit culminated in the signing of the Beijing Declaration.[1] All 22 Arab nations in attendance signed individual agreements with China focused on the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], described as "implementing over 200 major projects benefiting nearly 2 billion people."[1]

## Economic and financial dimension

Silk Road Fund chairman Zhu Lun highlighted the geopolitical risks of the western financial system, noting that a significant portion of Saudi-Chinese oil trade is conducted in "petro-yuans" and arguing that more effort was needed toward [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] in order to limit exposure to what he characterized as a collapsing western central banking system.[1] China disclosed that it sources over one-third of its oil from six Gulf Cooperation Council members, with only Russia supplying more than [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], which delivered 85.9 million metric tons in 2023.[1] Delegates also reviewed a $5.6 billion joint venture signed between China and Saudi Arabia in June 2023 for an electric car company to be based in Arabia.[1]

## Palestinian question

UAE President [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohamed bin Zayed]] spoke on the importance of bilateral agreements and asked Xi to support the Two-State Solution.[1] Xi responded by organizing a joint statement signed and presented by Chinese Foreign Minister [[wang-yi|Wang Yi]] and Arab League Secretary General Mohamed Salem Ould Merzouk, with China pledging $69 million in aid to Gaza; Wang said that "the Palestinian issue is the core of the Middle East problem."[1] In what Badlands commentary framed as a snub to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], Xi pledged that China would seek a larger role in the Gaza conflict, calling for an international peace conference resembling one proposed days earlier by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and King Hamad of Bahrain.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized the meeting as a gathering of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] in Beijing and read it, alongside Saudi Arabia's announcement of a domestic space technology company and a $2 billion investment in Elon Musk's [[xai|xAI]], as evidence that the alliance "is dismantling the Old Guard."[1]
