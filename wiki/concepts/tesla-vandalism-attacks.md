# Tesla Vandalism Attacks

The Tesla vandalism attacks were a wave of property destruction in early 2025 targeting [[tesla|Tesla]] automobiles, charging stations and dealerships, widely understood as protest against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s role in the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] and the second Trump administration.[1]

## Character of the attacks

Vehicles were keyed and in some cases set ablaze, with charging infrastructure and dealer lots also targeted.[1] Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued the campaign was driven by a belief among protesters that Musk was "stealing everyone's personal information" and exercising unchecked power, a perception he attributed to media conditioning rather than to the facts of DOGE's work.[1]

## Claims about participants

Musk stated that roughly half of the people caught vandalizing Tesla property were either transgender or nonbinary.[1] Carlson wrote that this claim was unsurprising to much of his audience, but stressed that the phenomenon extended beyond any single demographic: in his estimation, for every person who actually vandalized a vehicle there were perhaps a hundred who would do so if they believed they could escape consequences, including moderate liberals and some who had voted for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2024.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

Carlson treated the vandalism as the sharp edge of a broader wave of fear among Americans who were "barely left of center," fear he attributed to sustained mainstream-media messaging about economic collapse, [[2025-trump-tariffs|tariffs]], and the dismantling of federal programs.[1] He contrasted the attacks with the behaviour of Trump supporters, asserting that celebrity critics such as [[joan-baez|Joan Baez]] and other entertainers faced no comparable risk of having their property destroyed or being assaulted.[1]
