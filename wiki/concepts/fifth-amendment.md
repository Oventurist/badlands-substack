# Fifth Amendment

The Fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution guarantees several protections in criminal process, including the privilege against compelled self-incrimination. Witnesses before congressional committees routinely invoke this privilege — colloquially "pleading the Fifth" — to decline to answer questions whose answers might expose them to prosecution.

The amendment features prominently in the July 30, 2026 Badlands Brief, which reported that Anthony Fauci invoked it throughout a [[senate|Senate]] hearing on Covid origins and the pandemic response. Fauci said he feared Republicans would use his testimony to prosecute him for perjury, accused Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] of an "obvious obsession" with imprisoning him, and stated that on advice of counsel he would not answer questions despite decades of cooperating with [[congress|Congress]].

The Brief highlighted an unusual legal dimension: Rand Paul argued from the dais that the chairman had denied the assertion of privilege and directed Fauci to answer "despite the existence of the pardon," implying that a valid pardon would eliminate the jeopardy on which a Fifth Amendment claim rests. Badlands commentary connected this to the [[autopen-scandal|Autopen scandal]], arguing that challenging the validity of Fauci's pardon is a necessary step toward resolving the wider question, and that continued refusal to testify could support a [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] referral.
