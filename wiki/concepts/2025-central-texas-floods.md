# 2025 Central Texas Floods

The 2025 Central Texas floods were a catastrophic flash-flooding event that struck central Texas over the Fourth of July weekend in 2025, killing at least 82 people according to officials cited in contemporaneous reporting.[1] The death toll included at least 28 children in [[kerr-county|Kerr County]], the hardest-hit jurisdiction.[1]

## Casualties and missing persons

At the time of the reporting summarized in the Badlands News Brief of July 7, 2025, ten girls and one counselor from [[camp-mystic|Camp Mystic]], a summer camp located in the community of Hunt in Kerr County, remained unaccounted for.[1] Dozens of other people were also still missing across the affected area.[1]

## Ongoing flood threat

Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] warned that flash flooding continued to pose a danger to parts of the state, with additional rain forecast for several areas.[1] Officials urged residents of Hunt to move to higher ground on the afternoon of July 6 because of the potential for further flooding.[1]

## Emergency preparedness questions

Records reviewed by reporters showed that Kerr County officials had previously considered installing flood-warning sirens, but the plan was never carried out.[1] Although the [[national-weather-service|National Weather Service]] issued a series of alerts in advance of the storm, questions were raised about the agency's staffing levels and its ability to reach residents in time.[1]

## Weather modification claims and the Rainmaker response

As the story spread, theories circulated online attributing the flooding to [[weather-modification|weather modification]].[1] The most viral of these implicated [[rainmaker-technology-corporation|Rainmaker Technology Corporation]], a cloud-seeding company.[1] Rainmaker chief executive [[augustus-doricko|Augustus Doricko]] responded publicly on X, stating that the company had suspended operations in accordance with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation's suspension criteria in order to prevent any risk of flooding.[1] Doricko said the company's last seeding mission before the July 4 event took place in the early afternoon of July 2, when two clouds were seeded over the eastern portions of south-central Texas; those clouds dissipated between roughly 3:00 and 4:00 p.m. CDT, more than 24 hours before the storm complex that produced the flooding rainfall developed.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the devastation recalled the Hurricane Helene disaster in western North Carolina and noted that an eerily similar disaster had occurred in the same area in 1987.[1] She criticized what she described as gaslighting by commentators who attributed the disaster instead to climate change and to alleged cuts to NOAA funding by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Related controversy

The disaster generated a secondary controversy when [[sade-perkins|Sade Perkins]], a former Houston city board appointee, posted a viral TikTok video characterizing Camp Mystic as "White-only, conservative [and] Christian."[1]

## Casualties, warnings, and the cloud-seeding controversy

Flash floods swept across central Texas on Friday, July 4, 2025, killing more than 80 people with many still missing, as the Guadalupe River surged by more than 20 to 26 feet within 90 minutes and forced mass evacuations.[2] The [[national-weather-service|National Weather Service]] activated flood warnings across Texas expected to last at least into the following Monday morning; by NWS data, flooding is the second-deadliest weather hazard in the United States after extreme heat.[2]

[[rainmaker-technology-corporation|Rainmaker]] chief executive [[augustus-doricko|Augustus Doricko]] publicly stated that his cloud-seeding company "unequivocally had nothing to do with the flooding."[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributors were unpersuaded: [[canncon|CannCon]] mocked Doricko's football analogy about moving forward after 109 deaths, and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] jabbed at establishment critics who "were against man made climate change before they were for man made climate change" when [[weather-modification|weather modification]] entered the conversation.[2]
