# Nutritious SNAP Act of 2025

The Nutritious SNAP Act of 2025 is legislation introduced in March 2025 by U.S. Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] to restore what he described as personal responsibility and state flexibility to the federal food assistance program.[1]

## Purpose

The bill's stated purpose is to confront the argument that while the [[snap|Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program]] was created to fight hunger, it now fuels a public health crisis of obesity and chronic disease by subsidizing junk food purchases with taxpayer dollars.[1]

"SNAP was designed to fight hunger and improve nutrition, not to subsidize soda and junk food," Paul said. "It makes no sense that taxpayer dollars are being used to fund an epidemic of obesity and diet-related illness in low-income communities. My bill ensures that this assistance program actually supports health and wellness, not chronic disease."[1]

## Context

SNAP provides food-purchasing assistance to more than 42 million individuals.[1] Studies cited in support of the bill indicate that nearly one-quarter of SNAP benefits are spent on junk food — sodas, chips, snack cakes and desserts — contributing to worse diets and higher obesity rates among recipients than the general population.[1] Previous efforts by states including Minnesota, California and New York to limit SNAP junk food purchases were rejected by the [[usda|U.S. Department of Agriculture]].[1]

Paul framed the measure as a common-sense step toward improving public health, protecting taxpayer dollars, and respecting state decisions to combat chronic disease.[1] The bill sits alongside state-level [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] measures such as West Virginia's food dye ban.[1]
