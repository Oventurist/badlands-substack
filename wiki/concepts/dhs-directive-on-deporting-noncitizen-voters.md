# DHS Directive on Deporting Noncitizen Voters

The **DHS directive on deporting noncitizen voters** is guidance announced by the United States [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] on June 9, 2026, instructing [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] to pursue deportation proceedings against noncitizens who illegally vote in American elections or who falsely claim United States citizenship.[1]

## Legal basis

DHS General Counsel [[james-percival|James Percival]] said the policy reflects existing provisions of the [[immigration-and-nationality-act|Immigration and Nationality Act]] rather than creating new authority, and that it follows the administration's broader election-integrity initiatives.[1] Under the directive, ICE attorneys may seek removal based on evidence of illegal voting or of a false claim of citizenship even where a criminal conviction has not yet occurred.[1] Federal law already prohibits noncitizens from voting in federal elections, and several recent cases involving noncitizens accused or convicted of illegal voting had drawn national attention.[1]

Departmental officials argued that unlawful voting by noncitizens undermines election integrity and can independently trigger grounds for removal under federal immigration law.[1] The announcement came amid a wider administration push focused on voter-eligibility verification and election security.[1]

## Reception

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the directive and anticipated litigation, writing that she could not wait for the lawsuits in which leftist NGOs would argue that criminal invaders who diluted the votes of Americans should be allowed to stay.[1] She predicted that Colorado Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]], then running for governor, would be first in line to sue.[1] Framing the coming midterms through Alexander Solzhenitsyn's [[live-not-by-lies|"Live Not by Lies"]], she argued that real elections require honesty and accountability about past fraud and described removing "criminally-invading voter-fraudsters" from the country as a good step in that direction.[1]
