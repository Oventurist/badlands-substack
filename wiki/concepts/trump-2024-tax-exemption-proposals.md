# Trump 2024 Tax Exemption Proposals

The Trump 2024 tax exemption proposals were a set of income tax breaks floated by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during the 2024 presidential campaign that, according to a CNBC analysis of several estimates, could offer total or partial income tax exemptions to roughly 93.2 million Americans — a meaningful share of the U.S. electorate.[1] The proposals formed the core of Trump's economic pitch to voters and were presented as part of a sweeping tax overhaul.[1]

## Proposed exemptions

As of October 2024 the Republican presidential nominee had officially proposed eliminating income tax on tips, on Social Security benefits, and on overtime pay.[1] In an interview on the sports media site OutKick, Trump said he would additionally consider tax exemptions for firefighters, police officers, military personnel and veterans.[1] Badlands commentary noted that the sequence of announcements also included a "No Tax on Loans" proposal, and that Trump had answered a question about abolishing all federal taxes "once the country is back on its feet" with the phrase "There is a way."[1]

## Tariffs as replacement revenue

The exemptions were framed as part of a larger vision to transition away from the income tax system entirely and replace the lost revenue with receipts from a hardline tariff regime.[1] Trump pledged a 20% universal tariff on all imports from all countries, with a specific 60% rate for Chinese imports.[1] Speaking with voters in New York for "Fox & Friends," he argued that "in the old days when we were smart, when we were a smart country, in the 1890s and all, this is when the country was relatively the richest it ever was. It had all tariffs. It didn't have an income tax."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing for Badlands Media, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the rollout as the seeding of a "Reverse Hegelian Dialectic" and as mass psychological game theory: by granting tax relief to discrete constituencies one at a time, Trump would generate demand from every group left out, which he would then oblige, forcing the existing tax system to justify its own existence.[1] The analysis tied this to Trump's sit-down with Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal, in which he told the assembled financial press, "you people have been wrong about [currency] your whole lives."[1]
