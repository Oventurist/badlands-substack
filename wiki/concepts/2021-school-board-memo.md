# 2021 School Board Memo

The 2021 school board memo was a directive issued by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] under Attorney General Merrick Garland in October 2021, instructing the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] and United States Attorneys to coordinate with state and local leaders on what the memorandum described as a rising tide of harassment, intimidation, and threats of violence against school board members, teachers, and school staff.

## Background

The memo followed a letter from the National School Boards Association that likened certain parental protests at school board meetings to "domestic terrorism." Those protests had been driven by disputes over pandemic-era mask and closure policies and over curriculum controversies associated with [[critical-race-theory|critical race theory]]. The association later retracted the language, but the DOJ directive remained in force, and it became one of the defining flashpoints of the Biden administration's domestic law-enforcement record.

## Internal warnings

Newly released DOJ documents reported in the June 16, 2026 *Badlands Brief* show that officials inside the department raised concerns about the memorandum at the time it was drafted. According to the records, some officials questioned how the directive would affect parents speaking at local school board meetings — that is, whether ordinary protected speech at a public forum would be swept into a federal threat-assessment posture. Those internal objections were not reflected in the memo as issued.

## Interpretation in the Badlands corpus

Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] places the disclosure alongside similar reporting about the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]], in which internal warnings were also said to have been raised and overridden. She reads the pattern as evidence that the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]] was not a series of isolated excesses but a coordinated program, and that the sequential release of such records is preparing the public to accept that conclusion.

The memo is thus treated in the corpus as a domestic-front counterpart to the surveillance abuses associated with [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] and the prosecution of political opponents — the application of federal counterterrorism machinery to parents rather than to foreign adversaries.
