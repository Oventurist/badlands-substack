# Cancel Culture

"Cancel culture" describes the practice of withdrawing support from, deplatforming, or seeking the termination of individuals for expressed opinions. In "A Glaring Double Standard," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] uses the concept as the central test of whether self-described free-speech champions on the American right hold their principles consistently.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## The conservative anti-cancel-culture movement

In the years preceding October 7, 2023, numerous free-speech champions emerged on the right, including [[bari-weiss|Bari Weiss]] and [[dave-rubin|Dave Rubin]], along with outlets such as the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]] and the Babylon Bee.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) These figures billed themselves as opposed to cancel culture, advocated freedom of thought, and objected in particular to conservatives being fired from their jobs for expressing their beliefs.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme cites three examples. [[christopher-rufo|Christopher Rufo]] tweeted on December 20, 2021, in defense of Indianapolis teacher Tony Kinnett, who was fired after posting a video about critical race theory in public schools.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) In February 2021 the Daily Wire's Tim Pierce quoted [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] calling Disney's firing of actress Gina Carano "indicative of where we are in the culture" and part of "a mass social movement to expel half of the American population from the body politic."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) Bari Weiss, in a 2021 Commentary Magazine op-ed titled "We Got Here Because of Cowardice. We Get Out With Courage," wrote that persuasion had been replaced with public shaming, debate with de-platforming, and forgiveness with punishment, quoting Douglas Murray on the complicity and silence that permit reputational destruction.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## The reversal

DeLarme lists Americans fired for dissenting views on Israel's military actions in Gaza: [[maura-finkelstein|Maura Finkelstein]], a tenured Jewish professor at Muhlenberg College; Hesen Jabr, a nurse at NYU Langone Health dismissed for comparing the ethnic cleansing of Gaza to that of European Jews by the Nazis; and Nikki Noor Aytoglu, a dental hygienist terminated over social media posts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He observes that after these firings, none of the right-wing free-speech advocates warned about the dangers of cancel culture as they had when the "PC police" targeted people and ideas they agreed with.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

The advocacy group [[stopantisemitism|StopAntisemitism]] was the primary force pressuring employers in these cases, often doxxing targets through its X account, while [[chaya-raichik|Chaya Raichik]] of Libs of TikTok amplified those posts with comments such as "Consequences for hate are necessary."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) DeLarme's conclusion is that the commitment of figures like Shapiro, Weiss, and Rubin to free speech was always conditional, tethered to tribal loyalty rather than principle.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Comedy as a cancel-culture battleground (2023)

An earlier front in the same fight ran through comedy. Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] reported in May 2023 that comedians had long objected that "woke activists are killing comedy," describing "a group of perpetually pissed off, humorless people... remaking the world in their own image."[2] Campuses were said to be "dead as venues" because no joke is safe unless aimed at white straight males, Christians or conservatives; hate-speech laws had produced prosecutions for "malicious communications" and for insulting groups or religious figures; and six out of ten students reportedly viewed offensive jokes as hate speech.[2]

The May 2023 flashpoint was the demand that a scene be cut from [[monty-pythons-life-of-brian|The Life of Brian]] on the grounds that it mocked transgender people, a demand [[john-cleese|John Cleese]] publicly refused.[2] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cast the refusal as heroism against tyranny, grouping Cleese with J.K. Rowling as figures "refusing to bend the knee to the corporate-sponsored woke mob" and predicting sustained attacks from "ideologically possessed supplicants to tyranny."[2]

## Ivan Provorov and the NHL (2023)

The January 2023 backlash against [[ivan-provorov|Ivan Provorov]], the [[philadelphia-flyers|Philadelphia Flyers]] defenseman who declined to wear an LGBT pride jersey on religious grounds, was cited by [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributors [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] and [[absolute-1776|Absolute1776]] as a case study in cancel culture attempting to silence dissenting voices.[3] They argued that public support for Provorov showed the silent majority becoming loud, and that the [[national-hockey-league|NHL]]'s refusal to fully capitulate placed it on the same slippery slope previously travelled by the NFL and MLB.[3]

## Ye and Musk as sequential test cases (December 2023)

A December 2023 Badlands Media essay by Erik Carlson used the year separating the cancellation of [[kanye-west|Ye (Kanye West)]] from the campaign against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to argue that cancel culture functions as an enforcement mechanism rather than a spontaneous social reaction.[4] In this account the [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]] acts as arbiter of what is offensive and who should be offended, spearheading campaigns designed to make examples of high-profile targets and deter others from stepping out of line.[4]

The essay observed that by early December 2022 essentially every individual and business had cut ties with Ye, that few defended his free speech rights, and that people who privately agreed with his claims kept silent rather than be cancelled alongside him.[4] It argued the mechanism had been used to keep both wings of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] in lockstep.[4] Carlson also described the reflexive deployment of Nazi accusations as a debate-ending device applied to positions such as border security, America First, criticism of riots, and support for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4] By late 2023, he argued, the same machinery was being aimed at Musk over his statements on illegal immigration and on "anti-white" messaging, with manufactured outrage including performative threats to trade Teslas for Rivians.[4]
