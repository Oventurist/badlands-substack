# App Tracking Transparency

App Tracking Transparency (ATT) is an [[apple|Apple]] privacy policy that forces apps to obtain a user's permission before collecting data to target that user with personalized advertising.[1] Apple rolled the feature out beginning in April 2021 as part of an update to the operating system powering the iPhone and iPad.[1]

## Criticism and competition concerns

While ATT was designed to tighten privacy protections, it faced criticism from Big Tech rivals who argued it would make it harder for smaller apps to survive without charging consumers.[1]

## Italian antitrust fine (December 2025)

On December 22, 2025, [[italy|Italy]]'s antitrust authority fined Apple 98.6 million euros ($116 million) after determining that the operation of ATT restricted App Store competition, holding that Apple had abused its dominant position.[1] The authority did not criticize the policy as such, but rather the fact that Apple's system requires third-party app makers to ask users for consent twice in order to comply with Europe's strict privacy rules.[1] "As a result, such double consent requirement is harmful to developers, whose business model relies on the sale of advertising space, as well as to advertisers and advertising intermediation platforms," the authority said.[1] Apple announced it would appeal the sanction.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Europe's privacy laws are strict and carry real consequences for technology companies, observing that Apple appeared to have designed ATT to ensure "informed consent."[1] She argued that regulations always hurt competition — even those intended to spur it — by imposing unnecessary compliance burdens, and that in fining Apple, Italy was prioritizing competition over privacy in a way that undermines the European Union's broader position on privacy.[1]
