# Globalism

In Badlands Media commentary, globalism is presented not as a recent development in international economics but as the continuation of British imperialism under a new name.[1]

## Continuity with imperialism

Writing in "The British Cabal" (August 7, 2023), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] stated flatly that "England invented globalism as we know it today; it used to be called Imperialism."[1] He posed the question of what, in substance, distinguishes colonialism from modern-day globalism, treating the two as the same programme of extraction and control operating through different instruments.[1]

Carlson refused to accept the conventional account in which the United Kingdom, having ruled roughly a quarter of the world in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, was never militarily defeated yet simply relinquished that empire and withdrew to "their little island in the North Sea."[1] He argued instead that control was retained covertly, and pointed to the worldwide rise of [[communism|communism]] immediately following the supposed voluntary surrender of empire as evidence that the mechanism of control had merely changed form.[1]

## Colonial economic template

The economic content Carlson attributed to the older imperialism is a manufacturing monopoly: before the [[american-revolutionary-war|American Revolutionary War]], he wrote, England forbade Americans to manufacture finished goods of any kind, whether for domestic use or export, while permitting the colonies — especially the South — to produce raw commodities such as cotton for shipment to England, where they were manufactured into clothing and other goods and sold back to the world, America included.[1] He noted his surprise at never having encountered this in a high school American history class, which he took as further evidence of narrative control.[1]

After the war, on his account, British producers dumped manufactured goods on the open market below the cost of production with the explicit purpose of destroying the young American economy, which could not compete; early American leaders responded with tariffs.[1] Carlson identified the same template in the post-WWI settlement imposed on Germany, whose export capacity was deliberately constrained, and in China's 1990s rise on cheap exports that triggered the exodus of American manufacturing — drawing an explicit parallel to Trump's later tariffs on China.[1]

## Modern instruments

Carlson listed the wider apparatus he associates with the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]]'s globalism: the creation of communism and its use to control the world, the running of drug trafficking and human trafficking, and the development of modern mind control and propaganda through [[tavistock-institute|Tavistock]].[1] He described the [[brics|BRICS]] grouping as a Cabal creation and named [[satanism|Satanism]] as the common bond among those at the top of the pyramid.[1]
