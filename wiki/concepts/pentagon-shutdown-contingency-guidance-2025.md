# Pentagon Shutdown Contingency Guidance 2025

With less than two days remaining before a potential United States government shutdown at the end of September 2025, the Department of War issued contingency guidance outlining six departmental priorities and its contracting plans during a lapse in appropriations.[1]

## Contents

The document warns that activities not deemed "excepted," and unable to be performed by military personnel in place of furloughed civilian staff, will be suspended once funding lapses, though the defense secretary retains authority to designate additional activities as excepted.[1] Fiscal 2025 funding was set to expire at midnight Wednesday, and absent a continuing resolution approved by [[congress|Congress]] and signed by President [[donald-trump|Trump]], the government would shut down.[1]

Active-duty troops, including reserve personnel on federal orders, will continue to serve and may be tasked with duties normally handled by furloughed civilians, while only the minimum number of civilian employees needed to carry out excepted activities will remain at work.[1]

## Stated priorities

The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] identified its highest priorities as securing the US southern border, supporting operations in the Middle East, advancing the [[golden-dome|Golden Dome]] initiative, and maintaining depot work, shipbuilding, and critical munitions.[1] Contractors may continue work on previously awarded projects, but new contracts cannot be executed during a lapse in appropriations.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] characterised the document simply as the Department of War's continuity of operations plan for the shutdown.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] extended the point, noting that the "Department Priorities" look a lot like Devolution, and that in the context of narrative warfare and the ongoing trend of asymmetric disclosure it would be interesting to see how much of the public narrative around a looming shutdown came to surround continuity of government.[1]
