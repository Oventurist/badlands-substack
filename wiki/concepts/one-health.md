# One Health

**One Health** is a framework, formally adopted by the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] and the G20 health ministers in 2017, which holds that human health, animal health, and environmental conditions are inseparable and must be governed together.[1] In Badlands Media's coverage, One Health is treated not as a benign scientific principle but as the mechanism by which the WHO's remit could be expanded almost without limit under the proposed pandemic instruments.[1]

## Origin

The term "One Health" was first coined by the executive vice president of the [[ecohealth-alliance|EcoHealth Alliance]] — the same organization that, according to the article, appears to have had a hand in the creation of SARS-CoV-2.[1] Ryan DeLarme presents this overlap as evidence that the same names and institutions recur across both the origin of the pandemic and the governance apparatus proposed in its aftermath, remarking that "it's almost as if there's some kind of conspiracy."[1]

## Scope and criticism

Because One Health recognizes that a very broad range of aspects of life and the environment can affect health, virtually any activity can be classified as having the "potential" to cause harm and therefore fall within the WHO's jurisdiction.[1] Under the agenda as described in the article, the WHO would be empowered to make decisions concerning diet, agriculture and livestock farming, environmental pollution, and the movement of populations, among other domains.[1]

The article argues that this open-ended scope becomes dangerous when combined with an affiliated media apparatus capable of shaping public discourse: as long as sufficient fear and propaganda are produced, the scope of the WHO's power "will be limited only by their imagination."[1] Incorporation of the One Health agenda is listed among the core proposals of the [[who-pandemic-accord|WHO CA+]] instrument.[1]
