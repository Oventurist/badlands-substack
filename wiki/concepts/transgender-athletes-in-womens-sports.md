# Transgender Athletes in Women's Sports

**Transgender athletes in women's sports** refers to the ongoing dispute over whether athletes born male but identifying as female should be permitted to compete in female categories, a controversy that by 2023 had become one of the most prominent fronts in the American and Canadian culture war.[1] Critics argue that male puberty confers durable advantages in strength and power that make such competition unfair; supporters frame exclusion as discrimination.[1]

## The 2023 Alberta powerlifting demonstration

The dispute drew fresh attention in March 2023 when [[avi-silverberg|Avi Silverberg]], for more than a decade the head coach of Team Canada Powerlifting, entered the women's division of the Heroes Classic tournament in Lethbridge, Alberta, after identifying as a female.[1] Still bearded and wearing a men's singlet, he bench-pressed nearly 370 pounds and broke the Alberta women's record of 275 pounds by almost 100 pounds.[1]

That record had been held by transgender lifter [[anne-andres|Anne Andres]], who was volunteering at the event and watched the lift.[1] Andres also held the Alberta women's deadlift record at 544 pounds and the combined three-lift total at 1,245 pounds, and had won eight of nine women's competitions entered over four years, according to the [[independent-council-on-womens-sports|Independent Council on Women's Sports]].[1]

## Commentary

Video of the protest was distributed by ICONS and reported by the [[new-york-post|New York Post]].[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] argued that the demonstration "should be happening in every place this woke nightmare" was permitted, contended that the pre-existing transgender record was itself an affront to female competitors, and pointed to the swimmer [[lia-thomas|Lia Thomas]] as a parallel case.[1] Getz further argued that liberal women, having helped advance the policies at issue, would be the constituency whose outrage ultimately reversed them.[1]
