# Fusion Power

Fusion power is a form of carbon-free energy derived from fusing two atomic nuclei. It is widely regarded as an enticing alternative to nuclear fission, which powers traditional nuclear plants but carries drawbacks such as the production of radioactive waste.[1]

## Commercialization and the Google agreement

In June 2025 [[google|Google]] struck a deal to buy 200 megawatts of fusion power as the technology company struggled to reach its sustainability goals in the face of the growing energy demands of artificial intelligence.[1] The company announced that it was partnering with [[commonwealth-fusion-systems|Commonwealth Fusion Systems]] to offtake power from that firm's first commercial plant in Virginia, which is set to bring 400 megawatts of power online starting in the early 2030s.[1]

## Outlook

Michael Terrell, Google's head of advanced energy, wrote in a blog post that "fusion holds huge potential as an energy source of the future: it's clean, abundant and inherently safe, and it can be built just about anywhere."[1] He cautioned that "commercializing fusion is immensely challenging, and success is not guaranteed," but added that "if it works, it could change the world by providing a more secure and clean energy future."[1]
