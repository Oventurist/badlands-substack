# Counterman v. Colorado

Counterman v. Colorado is a First Amendment case decided by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in 2023 that established the prevailing legal precedent for determining what constitutes a "true threat" as a predicate for criminalizing and punishing speech.[1]

## Significance

The decision set out a test — described in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as a four-part test, and referred to as the "Colorado Test" — that, in the assessment of Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], should have substantially curtailed plaintiffs' ability to bring speech-related lawfare claims.[1]

## Subsequent use

Ashe in America wrote that the year after Counterman was decided she presented a First Amendment defense in the federal District of [[colorado|Colorado]], citing Counterman and its test, when the League of Women Voters recruited the [[naacp|NAACP]] to accuse her and her co-defendants of voter suppression, intimidation, coercion and threats under the Voting Rights Act and the Ku Klux Klan Act.[1] She won at the district level; the plaintiffs appealed, and the case was scheduled to be argued in the 10th Circuit Court of Appeals within days of the November 2025 brief.[1]

The case was invoked in Badlands commentary alongside the [[colorado-social-media-warning-label-law|Colorado social media warning label law]] injunction as evidence that Colorado has been a recurring venue for [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] conflicts, involving both the punishment of disfavored speech and the compulsion of state-preferred speech.[1]
