# Freedom Cities

**Freedom Cities** is a proposal advanced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in March 2023 calling for the design and construction of ten new American cities on federal land, presented as part of what his campaign described as a "quantum leap to revolutionize the American standard of living."[1] The plan was paired with proposed "baby bonuses" intended to spark a new American baby boom.[1]

## Framing in Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] treated the Freedom Cities proposal as a direct counter-offer to the urban model promoted under the [[great-reset|Great Reset]], particularly the [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]] concept, which he characterized as a high-density, low-self-sufficiency arrangement descended from two centuries of deliberate rural depopulation.[1] In this reading, Freedom Cities are not merely a housing program but a competing vision of civilization — an attempt to demonstrate in physical form what an American city built around property, self-sufficiency, and the [[rule-of-law|rule of law]] could look like.[1]

Deschamps noted that establishment coverage of the proposal was comparatively muted relative to other planks of Trump's 2025 agenda, and argued that the plan cuts against the climate-and-overpopulation narrative used to justify compact megacities.[1] He conceded that no modern example of a well-run large-scale city exists to serve as a template, since in his view all such cities have been captured by [[globalism|globalist]] administration, and framed the Freedom Cities idea as valuable precisely because a superior visible alternative would expose the deficiencies of the existing model.[1]

## Relation to the 250th anniversary celebration

Deschamps linked Freedom Cities to Trump's May 2023 announcement of a year-long celebration of the 250th anniversary of American independence, including a "Great American State Fair" that would "put forth innovative visions for America's future."[1] He interpreted the pairing as a deliberate "white-hat" inversion of the nineteenth- and twentieth-century world's fair tradition — events such as the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition — which he argued had historically been used to sell populations on centrally planned technological futures.[1]
