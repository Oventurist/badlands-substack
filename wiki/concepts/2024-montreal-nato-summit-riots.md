# 2024 Montreal NATO Summit Riots

Riots broke out in downtown Montreal on the night of Friday, November 22, 2024, when pro-Palestinian and anti-NATO protesters clashed with police, smashed shop windows, and set vehicles on fire.[1] The unrest coincided with the 70th Annual Session of the [[nato|NATO]] Parliamentary Assembly, held in Montreal from that Friday through Monday and attended by roughly 300 delegates from member states and partner countries.[1]

## Course of events

According to the Montreal Gazette, an initial group of protesters gathered at Émilie-Gamelin Parc at about 4:30 p.m. before marching toward the Quartier des Spectacles, per Montreal police spokesperson Manuel Couture.[1] By 5:30 p.m. a second group had converged at Place des Arts and the two demonstrations merged, then marched down St-Urbain Street.[1] At 6:10 p.m. demonstrators set an effigy of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] alight in the middle of the crowd, and objects including small explosive devices and metal items were thrown at police.[1] By 6:40 p.m. protesters had smashed shop windows near St-Urbain and René-Lévesque Blvd. and set two vehicles ablaze.[1] Police deployed chemical irritants and conducted crowd-dispersal manoeuvres; three protesters were arrested for allegedly assaulting officers and obstructing police work.[1]

## Trudeau's absence

Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] was attending a Taylor Swift concert in Toronto at the time of the riot, a detail highlighted by ZeroHedge and widely circulated.[1] Badlands Media called the framing "a bit of a cheap shot," saying it cared less about Trudeau's taste in music than about his government's asymmetrical treatment of protesters.[1]

## Double-standard argument

Badlands contrasted the Montreal violence with the conviction, on the same Saturday, of [[pat-king|Pat King]] for organizing the peaceful 2022 [[freedom-convoy|Freedom Convoy]] blockade of Ottawa.[1] The convoy participants, it argued, caused no damage and did no harm beyond frustrating the government, while the NATO protesters destroyed property and assaulted police — and the difference in treatment turned on the content of the protest rather than the conduct.[1] The commentary noted Canada lacks First Amendment protections that in the United States render content-based speech restrictions presumptively invalid, and predicted leftist rioters would receive at most a slap on the wrist while King faced five years.[1] It concluded that Canadians should replace Trudeau with [[pierre-poilievre|Pierre Poilievre]] at the election due in October 2025.[1]
