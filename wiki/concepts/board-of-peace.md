# Board of Peace

The Board of Peace is the transitional governance mechanism associated with the October 2025 United States-brokered [[gaza|Gaza]] ceasefire framework advanced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].

The February 11, 2026 [[badlands-brief-05f|Badlands Brief]] refers to the Board's "Phase Two Plan" to take control of Gaza. Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reports that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] intended to tell Trump during his Washington visit that the Phase Two plan "will not happen," and that the United States should instead move immediately to war with [[iran|Iran]]. The Brief separately describes Phase 2 of the ceasefire — requiring [[hamas|Hamas]] disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawal — as stalled because of Hamas's refusal to relinquish weapons, with Israel arguing that renewed military operations are needed to enforce demilitarisation.

## Formal launch and charter (January 2026)

The January 20, 2026 [[badlands-brief-144|Badlands Brief]] describes the Board as a new international body chaired by Trump, backed by a November 2025 [[united-nations|United Nations]] Security Council resolution adopted with Russian and Chinese abstentions. Its mandate is officially limited to Gaza until the end of 2027, supervising a Palestinian technocratic administration and a separate 11-member Gaza Executive Committee responsible for day-to-day operations. The draft charter, however, omits any specific mention of Gaza, criticizes past "failed" institutions — implicitly the UN — and presents the Board as a "nimble and effective" alternative for global conflict resolution with scope to expand beyond the Middle East.

A signing ceremony was scheduled for Thursday at 10:30 a.m. during the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, to launch phase two of the Gaza peace effort. Invitations went to leaders of roughly 60 countries across Europe, the Middle East, Asia and the Americas. [[hungary|Hungary]]'s [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] announced unequivocal support; Kazakhstan's Kassym-Jomart Tokayev, Belarus's Alexander Lukashenko, Argentina, Vietnam and Morocco also accepted. Italy's Giorgia Meloni and Canada's [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] expressed readiness in principle, and the United Kingdom under [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] signaled willingness on Gaza's second ceasefire phase. France declined, citing conflicts with its UN commitments and the charter's expansive scope; Poland objected to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s inclusion; and many governments withheld commitment out of concern that the Board could undermine the UN's authority. Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said Putin's invitation was under review pending clarification with Washington, while Russian Special Envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]] confirmed on X that Putin had accepted. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was invited and his office confirmed receipt, but he did not attend the Davos ceremony.

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] frames the launch as an assault on the WEF and a de facto rival to the United Nations, and connects it to Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s call to dismantle the [[civil-military-coordination-centre|Civil Military Coordination Centre]] at Kiryat Gat, which hosts the [[international-stabilization-force|International Stabilization Force]].
