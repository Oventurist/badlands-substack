# Open Borders Ponzi Scheme Thesis

The **open borders Ponzi scheme thesis** is an argument advanced by Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in a November 12, 2024 essay titled "Open Borders Finally Make Sense," subtitled "Spoiler Alert: It's All a Scam."[1] The thesis holds that mass illegal immigration under the [[biden-administration|Biden administration]] was not a policy failure or an act of humanitarian sentiment but a deliberate financial operation designed to supply taxpayer-funded tenants to institutional investors that had accumulated large quantities of residential real estate.[1]

## Premise

Carlson opens from the observation that the vast majority of Americans do not want open borders, do not want millions of unvetted entrants, and do not want migrants flown into the country and housed in hotels, apartments and houses at taxpayer expense — and asks why, then, it is happening, and why the shift from border protection to border opening occurred on the first day of the Biden administration.[1] His answer is that the policy makes sense only once one identifies who profits from it.[1]

## The mortgage and home-sales anomaly

The empirical core of the argument rests on two figures Carlson says he retrieved by searching the internet.[1] Roughly six million homes were sold in the United States in 2021, a higher-than-usual figure, falling to about four million in 2023 — a decline of two million.[1] Over the same period mortgage originations fell from $4.51 trillion to $1.5 trillion, a roughly threefold contraction.[1] Because the fall in mortgage lending was proportionally far larger than the fall in home sales, even allowing for reduced refinancing at higher rates, Carlson concludes that cash buyers accounted for the majority of homes sold in 2023.[1]

He identifies the typical cash buyer as a large investment company such as [[blackrock|BlackRock]], which purchases homes and rents them out.[1] When he searched Google on the question, its AI told him BlackRock was not buying houses and the top result was a BlackRock publication saying the same; Carlson compared trusting AI to trusting the mainstream media, arguing that "information is preselected for the AI to use" and that AI draws its conclusions from false material already on the internet — "garbage in, garbage out."[1]

## Foreknowledge of the rate cycle

Carlson argues that buying residential property at an all-time-high price violates the maxim "buy low, sell high" unless the buyer possesses information others lack, drawing an analogy with [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s investment record.[1] He proposes two possibilities: either BlackRock and comparable firms were informed by "the people who control the world" that interest rates would be raised in 2022, or they deduced it from simulations run on [[aladdin|Aladdin]], the supercomputer BlackRock built in the 1990s.[1] With foreknowledge of a rate shock, buying rental properties at elevated prices becomes rational, because rising rates would price ordinary Americans out of ownership and into rentals, allowing rents to be raised across the board.[1]

## Migrants as demand

The remaining requirement, in Carlson's account, is occupancy. Filling the units required "opening up the borders to let 20+ million illegals into the country and paying them to live in America."[1] Because migrants would not compete with investors for home purchases but would occupy rental stock, they raise rents further for Americans who can no longer buy.[1] Carlson relays a claim he had recently heard that [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] was controlled by BlackRock, and notes her role as border czar and the administration's practice of flying migrants in directly from their home countries.[1] He cites [[springfield-ohio-haitian-migrant-controversy|Springfield, Ohio]] — a city of roughly 50,000 that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said received 30,000 Haitians — as an illustration of the resulting strain on medical and educational systems, jobs, crime and housing prices, while the media dismissed the account as a conspiracy theory.[1]

The distribution of costs and benefits is summarized directly: American taxpayers pay, working-class Americans suffer most, and investment companies such as BlackRock and the politicians they pay off benefit most.[1] Carlson ties the scheme to taxation, asking how the [[deep-state|Deep State]] could run such operations if Americans were not paying [[federal-income-tax-elimination-proposal|income tax]], and how migrants would pay rent without government money.[1]

## The three "I"s

Carlson labels the components of the operation "the three 'I's" — illegals, inflation, and interest rate hikes — arguing that what appeared to be unrelated simultaneous phenomena were in fact a single mechanism serving "Deep State asset accumulators."[1] He connects this to his earlier claim that banks profited a trillion dollars from the 2022–2023 rate hikes, arguing that such profits are inexplicable given a threefold fall in mortgage lending unless the banks were themselves financing the asset collectors.[1]

## Unwinding under Trump

The thesis predicts that a second [[donald-trump|Trump]] term collapses the scheme.[1] Removing 20 million renters lowers both rents and property values, leaving investors holding vacant stock.[1] The scheme can only be unwound safely if interest rates fall quickly enough for middle-class Americans to buy the properties before they lose too much value — but rates can only fall fast if the economy is visibly suffering and inflation is portrayed as low, which Carlson links to the downward manipulation of oil prices, the largest input to inflation.[1] He points to the poor jobs report released by the Biden government four days before the election, after years of what he calls blatantly fudged numbers, arguing it was timed to justify rate cuts once a Trump victory looked certain: if America is creating jobs the [[federal-reserve|Fed]] will not cut, and without cuts the asset collectors are stuck.[1]

Carlson frames the multiple assassination attempts against Trump as a consequence of this exposure, and speculates that the Ponzi schemers may have been deliberately set up — allowed to accumulate assets so that the rug could be pulled from under them.[1] He anticipates further "[[kayfabe|Kayfabe]]" — staged tension — between [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] and Trump, with Trump demanding cuts to stimulate the economy and Powell insisting rates must stay high to contain inflation.[1] He suggests the institutions deemed "too big to fail" in [[2008-financial-crisis|2008]] and the "monolithic asset collectors" may now do exactly that, comparing their pleading to the demons Jesus cast out of Legion begging to enter a herd of swine.[1]

## Framing and reception

The argument is embedded in a broader discussion of the [[overton-window|Overton Window]], with which the essay opens.[1] Carlson notes that he set aside the effect of migration on swing-state voting — which he says could be an article by itself — in order to focus on the financial mechanism.[1] The essay closes with the claims that "the Deep State is totally screwed," that it will fight back as though its existence depends on it, that "the war isn't over, it's just beginning," and that "my money is on the good guys to win."[1] The piece carried [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]'s standard disclaimer that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors, and recorded 331 likes.[1]
