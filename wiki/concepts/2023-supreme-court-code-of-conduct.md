# 2023 Supreme Court Code of Conduct

The 2023 Supreme Court Code of Conduct is the first formal ethics code adopted by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]], announced on Monday, November 13, 2023, as the justices faced mounting pressure over undisclosed travel and gifts.[1]

## Background

Concerns about alleged ethical improprieties by the justices intensified in April 2023, after the nonprofit outlet ProPublica reported that Justice [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]] had taken trips paid for by Republican megadonor Harlan Crow.[1] The controversy fed a broader campaign for binding ethics rules governing a court that, unlike the lower federal judiciary, had operated without a written code.[1]

## Contents

The Court released 14 pages of guidance on the afternoon of November 13, 2023, including rules for accepting travel opportunities.[1] The guidance appears to allow justices to exercise their own discretion in accepting certain travel gifts, stating that they "may accept reasonable compensation and reimbursement of expenses for permitted activities" provided the source of the gift does not seem intended to influence a justice's duties "or otherwise appear improper."[1]

Under a section titled "COMPENSATION, REIMBURSEMENT, FINANCIAL REPORTING," the guidance states that "expense reimbursement should be limited to the actual or reasonably estimated costs of travel, food, and lodging reasonably incurred by the Justice and, where appropriate to the occasion, by the Justice's spouse or relative."[1] The same section records that the nine justices have "agreed to comply with the statute governing financial disclosure," with the undersigned members of the Court each individually reaffirming that commitment.[1]

The adoption was carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief of November 14, 2023, drawn from reporting by the [[washington-examiner|Washington Examiner]], without accompanying contributor commentary.[1]
