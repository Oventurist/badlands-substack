# Golden Escalator Campaign Launch

The golden escalator campaign launch was the June 16, 2015 event at Trump Tower in New York at which [[donald-trump]] descended a gold-rimmed escalator into the building's atrium and announced his first presidential campaign.[1] The June 2025 Badlands News Brief marked the tenth anniversary of the event, drawing on an Atlantic retrospective titled "A Decade of Golden-Escalator Politics."[1]

## The event and its memory

The Atlantic observed that Trump's seconds-long descent has lingered in the national memory more vividly than the 45-minute speech that followed, and that the moment is cited so widely — "since Trump rode down the golden escalator" — that it borders on cliché.[1] A decade later the escalator functions as something of a tourist attraction, carrying a steady stream of sightseeing fans into the Trump Tower atrium, where they can visit Trump Grill (with the Southwest Taco Bowl still on the menu at $25), the Trump Store, Trump Sweets, and a souvenir shop.[1] A floor above, visitors pose before a flag-flanked, gold-plated "45" insignia that had yet to be updated for Trump's second term, and no plaque commemorates the campaign launch itself.[1]

The magazine's correspondent noted that hardly anyone who attended the 2015 event, himself included, thought it would take Trump remotely close to the White House, and that his becoming — in the magazine's own estimation — "the most consequential American leader of the 21st century" was then unthinkable.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul]] used the anniversary to argue that Trump "has been the greatest RINO hunter in the history of the world" and that "the neocons of the Republican Party are all extinct," while noting that [[tucker-carlson]] had joined [[war-room]] to press the same case, particularly against [[fox-news]] and [[mark-levin]].[1] Paul insisted the real battle is not against Democrats or the left but against the establishments of all political parties, and that the [[rino|RINO]] hunt therefore continues.[1] He criticized audiences for resuming the pretense that televised Republicans are allies, for extending the benefit of the doubt to "rich strangers on a screen," and for groveling before figures who lie constantly while supporting the globalist cause.[1]
