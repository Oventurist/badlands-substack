# 2024 Harris Houston Rally

The **2024 Harris Houston rally** was a [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] campaign event held in Houston, Texas on October 25, 2024, widely publicized as featuring an appearance by the singer [[beyonce|Beyoncé]] and remembered chiefly for the crowd backlash when no performance materialized.[1]

## The event

The rally had been billed, following an announcement earlier in the week, in terms that led the public and the media to believe Beyoncé would perform.[1] Roughly 30,000 people attended after waiting in line, and by some accounts a million people attempted to obtain tickets, believing they were attending a Beyoncé concert.[1] Instead the singer walked out and delivered a short, barely audible speech; some outlets received a direct microphone feed while others did not, leaving much of the coverage inaudible.[1]

When it became clear there would be no performance, the rally "descended into complete chaos," with some attendees leaving and others protesting loudly.[1] Harris was booed and heckled through the remainder of the evening.[1] Media coverage recast the disruption as protesting Trump supporters, and Harris repeated a line directing hecklers to the "smaller rally down the road," a reference to a [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] event.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the more interesting question was not the bait-and-switch itself but *why* Beyoncé did not sing.[1] She noted that lying to voters is a poor decision for a candidate already struggling with authenticity, and that the campaign obtained a large-crowd photograph at a heavy net cost.[1] Attendees received what she described as "four minutes of emotional blackmail about abortion from the pop icon," without a single lyric, alienating apolitical fans who felt tricked — a repeat of dynamics from the 2024 Democratic National Convention.[1]

Her stated conclusion was financial: widespread rumors held that the campaign paid the performer $10 million for four minutes, and that a campaign obliged to pay for fabricated support, excitement and joy could not afford an actual performance.[1] If money was indeed the reason, she argued, it was "a fatal blow to the counterfeit joy movement," since a true believer would have sung for free.[1]

## Related declination

The same weekend, Harris campaign spokesman [[ian-sams|Ian Sams]] confirmed that Harris had declined an invitation to appear on [[joe-rogan|Joe Rogan]]'s podcast, citing campaign scheduling despite her presence in Texas.[1] Rogan's studio in Austin is about two and a half hours' drive from Houston.[1]
