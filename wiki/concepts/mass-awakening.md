# Mass Awakening

Mass awakening is the term used in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary for the anticipated large-scale public recognition that mainstream narratives about politics, medicine, elections and the economy were manufactured — a process treated less as a political event than as a psychological one with predictable casualties.[1]

## The flash-flood model

In "The Aftermath of a Trump Victory" (October 2024), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that the difficulty of mass awakening is absorptive rather than informational.[1] He offered the image of an arid desert whose clay ground has hardened to the consistency of concrete receiving two feet of rain in three hours: almost none soaks in, and the result is flash flooding.[1] A population fed lies its entire life has, in his framing, brain matter hardened the same way, so a sudden downpour of truth largely runs off and floods the psyche instead.[1]

Carlson rejected the assumption that the misinformed are simply untold, noting that most have already heard dissenting claims many times and concluded the teller is the crazy one, with television always available to validate them.[1] He cited a Substack commenter who said they followed him only to hear what crazy people think, and compared the situation to a person who has memorised the Bible and still regards Christianity as absurd.[1] The reachable group, he argued, is not those who know and reject the claims but those who do not yet understand they have been lied to.[1]

## Cognitive defences

Carlson described the human brain as designed to protect existing belief, deploying excuse ("the people I support are bad, but the other side is worse"), justification ("it's bad, but it's for the greater good") and outright blocking ("it never happened, it's fake news").[1] He argued the media can dismiss most inconvenient subjects — child trafficking, border crossings, stolen elections — as [[fake-news|fake news]], but where denial is impossible, as with inflation, it deflects blame onto greedy business rather than the government's money printing.[1]

He characterised the media-backed public as never having had to win an argument through logic and facts, likening them to cowards who only fight in groups and to Japanese Wagyu cattle that are massaged and kept from moving before being asked to pull a heavy plough, and asked whether neural pathways damaged by constant messaging, drugs and toxins would function at all.[1]

## Expected costs

Carlson predicted the number of Americans traumatised by the truth could be massive, comparing it to a population of alcoholics going cold turkey at once and forecasting suicides, depression and anxiety, along with [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]] hires facing a level playing field for the first time.[1] He rated the shock at "Covid times ten," noting that the [[covid-19-lockdowns|Covid]] narrative left many permanently disabled through psychological rather than physical damage — fear of illness, refusal to leave home, fear of being breathed on or touching anything.[1]

He also warned of a short-term economic reckoning: gas prices held down to suppress inflation and job numbers fabricated to flatter the economy, with no reason for the [[deep-state|Deep State]] to continue manipulating figures once [[donald-trump|Trump]] returned to office, potentially revealing an economy far worse than believed before matters were fixed in the long run.[1]

## Pacing and the role of the awake

Carlson wrote that Trump allowed the 2020 election to be stolen because the American public was not ready for the mass awakening required to dismantle the system, and asked whether the transition to a new system would require baby steps — a question he left undetermined while urging patience.[1] He framed the awakening as following the stages set out at [[five-stages-of-grief|five stages of grief]], with some making the transition easily, some advancing two steps forward and one back, and some retreating permanently into a false reality in search of a safe space.[1]

For those already awake, he prescribed restraint: no gloating, no demands for acknowledgment, no ridicule, and a triage-tent ethic of helping those capable of recovery while ignoring the mortally wounded and refusing to argue with them.[1] Being awake early is presented as an honour and a responsibility carrying battle scars, summarised in the essay's closing image that the awake "have been called to brew the coffee, fry the bacon and crack the eggs" for the many who will soon wake up hungry for the truth.[1]
