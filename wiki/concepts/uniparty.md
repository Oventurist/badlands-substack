# Uniparty

**Uniparty** is the term used across the Badlands corpus for the contention that the Republican and Democratic parties, notwithstanding their public antagonism, operate as two wings of a single governing establishment whose core commitments — on war, finance, surveillance and the permanent bureaucracy — do not change with elections. On this reading the visible partisan contest is a managed spectacle, while substantive policy continuity is maintained by the shared donor class, leadership and institutional interests that sit behind both parties.

## Usage in the corpus

The framing appears as shorthand rather than as an argued thesis in most installments of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]]. In [[badlands-blitz-episode-23|Badlands Blitz Episode 23]], published on July 3, 2026, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] devote a substantial part of the episode to what the post calls "the intra-party Civil Wars afflicting both the Right and Left sides of the Uniparty." The phrasing is characteristic: the Right and the Left are described not as separate parties but as sides of one entity, and the news event of interest is the factional conflict occurring inside each of them at the same time.

That simultaneity is treated in the corpus as significant in itself. Where a conventional account would read internal party disputes as ordinary coalition politics, the Badlands framing reads concurrent fracture on both sides as evidence that the establishment arrangement holding the two wings together is itself under strain — a development the outlet generally interprets as favourable to the populist realignment it associates with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].

## The campaign against Joe Kent (March 2026)

In the March 18, 2026 [[badlands-brief-1cd|Badlands Brief]], contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that "we are already seeing the Uniparty figures all participating in a communist struggle session against [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]]," the counterterrorism director who resigned over the [[iran|Iran]] war, and that [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] "may be their next target." In the same edition [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the adjective "unipartisan" to describe the lawmakers using the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] files subpoena for "public character assassinations" ahead of the midterms.
