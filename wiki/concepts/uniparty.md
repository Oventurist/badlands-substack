# Uniparty

"Uniparty" is the term used in Badlands Media commentary for the contention that establishment Republicans and Democrats function in practice as a single governing bloc, differing in rhetoric but not in outcomes.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## The debt ceiling as proof

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] invokes the concept directly in the context of the 2023 [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt limit agreement]], asking whether "more evidence of a 'Uniparty' need[s] to occur to wake more people up."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) The agreement — an increased debt limit with virtually no spending cuts — is the specific fact he treats as difficult to reconcile with genuine two-party opposition.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

Carlson pairs the Uniparty frame with the possibility that [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s apparent capitulation to the Democrats was engineered to expose the RINOs and [[neoconservatives|Neocons]], noting that America First members had already secured rules making it easier to impeach the Speaker.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) On this reading the exposure of the Uniparty is not merely commentary but an objective that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] may be actively pursuing.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Related usage

The label "RINO" — Republican In Name Only — is used interchangeably in the article with Uniparty membership; Carlson's first instinct about McCarthy was that he was "just another weak Republican, maybe even a RINO."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) The same test is applied to [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], with the observation that whatever his motives, he is exposing the RINOs, Neocons and the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Intra-party civil wars

By mid-2026 the Uniparty frame had been extended on Badlands Media's weekly program [[the-blitz|The Blitz]] to describe factional conflict inside each of its halves.[2] Episode 23, "Generation America," published 3 July 2026 and hosted by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], focused on what the post called the intra-party civil wars afflicting both the Right and Left sides of the Uniparty.[2] The framing treats the two parties as wings of one bloc, each now fighting internally rather than against the other, a dynamic the hosts linked in the same episode to the [[generational-divide|generational divide]] between Boomers, Millennials and Gen Z that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is being forced to navigate.[2]

## The 2026 midterm convergence

Badlands commentary in March 2026 applied the uniparty frame to the Democratic Party's internal fight ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterms]], arguing that the "uniparty left" was discarding the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]] and allied radicals because their zeal had become inconvenient to a predictable shift toward the middle.[3] In this reading, leadership needed to appear stable and sane in order to portray [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his supporters as extremists, making uncontrollable ideologues a liability and producing the [[2026-democrat-primary-civil-war|midterm civil war]].[3] The same brief described a [[neoconservatives|NeoCon establishment]] on the other side of what it called the false divide, shifting from celebration to concern-trolling as the [[iran-war|Iran campaign]] failed to trend toward the regime-change occupation it had wanted.[3]

## Revealed by the dueling Iran headlines (2026)

In July 2026 [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the media protectorate had accidentally exposed uniparty unity through two [[politico|Politico]] headlines published in the same week — one reporting Democratic desperation to stop [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s continuation of the Iran campaign, the other reporting Republican hysteria over the same thing.[4] Because, in his premise, the Democratic Party has never cared about the human cost in the Middle East, the convergence of both parties against a Trump policy is read not as principle but as the signature of the uniparty itself.[4]

The formulation offered is that whenever the so-called Left and the so-called Right agree on something anathema to Trump's moves, they are reminding the public "that the real enemy has been in the gates all along," and that the only people they treat as threats are the American people and the man acting as their tribune.[4] The episode is presented as a moment when the [[fake-iran-war|Fake Iran War]] narrative let the real war beneath it show through.[4]

## Republican defections of summer 2026

In June 2026 [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] extended the frame to Republican lawmakers who broke with [[donald-trump|President Trump]] on spending, executive authority and redistricting, whom Trump publicly called "unpatriotic" and "grandstanders."[5] Burning Bright argued that what was unfolding was not the external assault long anticipated from legacy media, institutional holdouts or lawfare, but a breach from within the walls of the MAGA coalition by elements that had worn its colors only when doing so served narrower interests.[5]

He traced the pattern to the 2022 midterm cycle, when voices positioning themselves as guardians of the movement — many orbiting the DeSantis operation — declared Trump's 2024 announcement premature and destabilizing.[5] In his reading, Trump's early declaration was a disarmament operation against a weaponized "unity" play designed to sideline the one figure preventing the movement from being herded back onto partisan rails.[5] Many of those figures, unelected and unaccountable to the base, secured their positions in that cycle and now break ranks again because the movement they only pretended to serve is an existential threat to the Uniparty architecture they were cultivated to protect.[5]

Burning Bright described the defectors as seizing on the public discombobulation generated by Trump's ongoing [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] campaign as cover, noting that every assertion of sovereignty and every exposure of rules-based illusions creates stress in the system — stress he framed as opportunity.[5] He also faulted the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] for the same failure, observing that those who continue to point out the structural falsity of American elections have been mocked and attacked for refusing to outsource their discernment to partisan outcomes, and warning that pinning the movement's future on the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]] repeats the 2022 trap by reducing American sovereignty to electioneering and handing initiative back to the mechanisms that produced the tyranny in question.[5]

## October 2023 exposure

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued in October 2023 that the Israel–Hamas escalation and the concurrent paralysis of [[congress|Congress]] had exposed Uniparty war hawks, and that the American electorate was being shown the positives of an inept and inert legislature.[6] He identified one point of genuine bipartisan agreement in the moment: "NO NEW WARS."[6]

## Fox News and the pre-Trump era

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] applied the Uniparty frame to [[fox-news|Fox News]] in February 2023, after filings in the [[dominion-v-fox-news-defamation-lawsuit|Dominion defamation suit]] revealed that the network's hosts privately disbelieved the 2020 fraud claims they platformed.[7] In his reading, the network "misses the pre-Trump days," when it was much easier to hide the existence of the Uniparty and when election fraud "was just part of the political game."[7] The same disclosures were offered as an explanation for Fox's eagerness to call Arizona early and its reluctance to air [[2020-election-fraud-claims|fraud claims]] it did not value.[7]

## Application to the November 2023 funding fight

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] invoked the Uniparty in November 2023 when assessing Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s two-step [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]], writing that another extension would likely be reached "if only so we can watch the Uniparty continue to kick the can down the road in increasingly-dramatic (and exposing) fashion."[8] He argued that even a Speaker seen as pushing an America First agenda operates inside an apparatus that appears broken to the American people — a perception that, in his framing, supplies the public mandate to fix it.[8]

## February 2024 illustrations

The February 15, 2024 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], subtitled "Russian Space Nukes & Uniparty Shenanigans," used the week's news as a case study in uniparty mechanics.[9] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]]'s vague national security warning about the alleged [[russian-space-nuclear-weapon-program|Russian space nuclear weapon program]] was timed to pressure the House into passing the Senate's $95 billion supplemental, describing it as "political theatre intended to enhance and expand the power and resources of the criminal oligarchy."[9] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized House Democrats' [[discharge-petition|discharge petition]] maneuver as the establishment "using every trick in the book" to fund a foreign proxy war the American people had not agreed to fund, "to say nothing of whatever kickbacks they get in return."[9]
