# ATF Stabilizing Brace Rule

The ATF stabilizing brace rule is a firearms regulation issued in January 2023 by the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives]] that reclassified pistols equipped with stabilizing braces as short-barreled rifles — a category heavily regulated since the 1930s and legally comparable to sawed-off shotguns.[1] The rule took effect on June 1, 2023.[1]

## Legal basis

Under the reasoning published in the Federal Register, the agency held that "under the [[gun-control-act-of-1968|Gun Control Act of 1968]] and the [[national-firearms-act-of-1934|National Firearms Act of 1934]], a firearm equipped with a stabilizing brace or other rearward attachment is considered a 'rifle' or 'short-barreled rifle' if other factors indicate that the weapon is designed, made, and intended to be fired from the shoulder."[1] The rule was one of several firearms measures announced by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in 2021 following a mass shooting in Boulder, Colorado, in which a gunman using a stabilizing brace killed ten people at a grocery store.[1] Braces were also reported to have been used in the 2019 Dayton, Ohio shooting that left nine dead and in the Nashville, Tennessee school shooting.[1]

## Congressional repeal effort

On June 13, 2023, House Republicans passed a resolution to repeal the rule by a vote of 219–210, nearly along party lines, with two Democrats voting in favor and two Republicans against.[1] The resolution was introduced by Rep. [[andrew-clyde|Andrew Clyde]] of Georgia and moved to the [[united-states-senate|Senate]], which was expected to take it up within the week.[1] Floor debate was contentious: Republicans accused the administration of "executive overreach," while Democrats condemned the measure as a bill that would "help kill people."[1] Biden promised a veto; overriding it would require two-thirds majorities in both chambers.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], argued that "every gun law is an infringement" and characterized the brace rule as self-defeating policy.[1] She noted that stabilizing braces are widely used by disabled veterans to improve accuracy and reduce recoil, and that a rule aimed at "a tiny psychopathic fringe" of mass shooters instead degrades the accuracy of a far larger population of lawful firearm users.[1] Anticipating that a veto override would fail because "Republican legislators are largely feckless," she closed by invoking Chief Justice John Marshall in [[marbury-v-madison|Marbury v. Madison]] (1803): "A Law repugnant to the Constitution is void."[1]
