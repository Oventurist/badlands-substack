# Vaccines for Children Program

The Vaccines for Children (VFC) program is a federal initiative administered by the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] that supplies free vaccines to Medicaid-eligible children.[1] The program began including COVID-19 shots during the pandemic, a change that became the subject of litigation in 2025.[1]

## 2025 lawsuit

[[childrens-health-defense|A nonprofit formerly led]] by Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] sued the CDC, accusing the agency — then under Kennedy's own oversight — of pressuring doctors to administer COVID-19 vaccines to disadvantaged children through the program.[1] The suit sought to force the CDC to abandon what it called a "scientifically untethered" vaccination policy, specifically challenging the VFC program's inclusion of COVID-19 shots.[1]

The lead plaintiff was Dr. Samara Cardenas, a pediatrician from Anaheim who said her refusal to give COVID-19 shots based on medical judgment cost her nearly 2,000 Medicaid patients and ultimately forced the closure of her practice.[1] The complaint, filed April 25 in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California, alleged that the CDC's actions "punish ethical physicians" and promote an "unnecessary mass vaccination" campaign targeting the nation's poorest children.[1] Cardenas contended that the CDC was compelling doctors to offer "an investigational COVID-19 vaccine, which has never been shown to confer any clinical benefit to healthy children," despite the end of the pandemic and what she argued was minimal risk to children.[1]
