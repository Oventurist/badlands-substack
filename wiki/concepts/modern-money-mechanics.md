# Modern Money Mechanics

**Modern Money Mechanics** is a workbook on bank reserves and deposit expansion published in 1961 by the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, roughly ten years before the United States abandoned the [[gold-standard|gold standard]].[1] Its opening page states that its purpose "is to describe the basic process of money creation in a fractional reserve banking system," making it one of the few official documents in which a [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] institution plainly describes how deposits are multiplied into new money.[1]

## Contents

The booklet explains, in the banking terminology of the late 1950s, that "a bank must maintain legally required reserves … equal to a prescribed percentage of its deposits," and that "under current regulations, the reserve requirement against most transaction accounts is 10 percent."[1] Under that rule a $10 billion deposit obliges the bank to hold $1 billion, leaving $9 billion of excess reserves usable as the basis for new lending.[1]

The document is most frequently cited for its acknowledgement that banks do not lend out existing deposits: "Of course, they do not really pay out loans from the money they receive as deposits. If they did this, no additional money would be created. What they do when they make loans is to accept promissory notes in exchange for credits to the borrowers' transaction accounts."[1] In other words, loan money is created at the moment the loan is written, because demand exists and the reserve requirement is satisfied.[1]

## Significance

Badlands Media used the workbook as the technical backbone of its explanation of [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve banking]], tracing how an initial $10 billion deposit can cascade through successive rounds of lending — $9 billion, then $8.1 billion, then $7.29 billion, and so on — until roughly $90 billion of additional money has been created from the original sum.[1] Because each newly created dollar draws its value from dollars already circulating, the article treats the process described in the booklet as the primary mechanism of [[inflation|inflation]].[1]
