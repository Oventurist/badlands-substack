# Business Plot

The Business Plot refers to the 1934 coup d'état plans against President [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] laid by the pro-fascist Wall Street establishment and thwarted by General [[smedley-butler|Smedley Butler]].[1]

Matthew Ehret situates the plot within a longer campaign that began the day the anti-fascist Roosevelt was elected in 1932.[1] An attempted assassination of Roosevelt in February 1933 had already failed; the coup plans of the following year were exposed by Butler, whom Ehret describes as a patriotic general.[1] With both the violent and the electoral routes closed, Wall Street's remaining strategy ran through organizations such as the [[american-liberty-league|American Liberty League]], which sought to keep America out of the coming war until Pearl Harbor made that position untenable.[1]

## Relationship to the Pecora hearings

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the [[pecora-commission|Pecora Commission]] shaped American politics so intensely "by its simple power of speaking the truth" that the effort to run a fascist coup against [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] using General [[smedley-butler|Smedley Butler]] came undone before it could succeed.[2] Butler played along with Wall Street's plans for some months before publicly blowing the whistle in Congress, exposing the intention to use him as a "puppet dictator" leading thousands of [[american-legion|American Legion]] legionnaires in a storming of the White House to displace Roosevelt.[2] Ehret notes that the Legion of that era was modeled on [[benito-mussolini|Mussolini]]'s squadristi, as its leader [[alvin-owsley|Alvin Owsley]] had made explicit in 1921.[2] Butler's revelations amplified Roosevelt's popular support and inoculated much of the population against the fake news pouring out of Wall Street propaganda agencies.[2]
