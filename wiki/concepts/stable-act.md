# STABLE Act

The Stablecoin Transparency and Accountability for a Better Ledger Economy Act, known as the STABLE Act, is proposed United States legislation to bring issuers of payment [[stablecoin|stablecoins]] under a bank-like regulatory perimeter.[1]

## March 2025 House hearing

The bill was the subject of a [[house-financial-services-committee|House Financial Services Committee]] hearing on Tuesday, March 11, 2025, at which the pro-digital-assets witnesses notably included a senior executive from BNY and a Davis Polk & Wardwell lawyer who had spent his career representing Wall Street.[1] [[coindesk|Coindesk]] framed the composition of the witness panel as a marker of how far the politics of crypto had shifted: after years in which crypto insiders struggled to get the attention of [[us-congress|Congress]], representatives of the traditional financial system were now putting a foot on the scales to tip the balance toward stablecoin regulation.[1]

## Guynn's testimony

Lawyer [[randy-guynn|Randy Guynn]] argued that the safeguards imposed by the STABLE Act should put stablecoin issuers under protections similar to those governing banking.[1] "If a permitted stablecoin issuer has a properly calibrated reserve of liquid assets, capital buffer and no material amount of liabilities other than its stablecoin liabilities, as contemplated by the STABLE Act, its payment stablecoins should be as safe as insured bank deposits and central bank money," Guynn testified.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor GMONEY opened his response with the quotation attributed to Mayer Amschel Rothschild — "Give me control of a nation's money supply, and I care not who makes its laws" — and characterized the hearing as an attempt by the banking class, working through [[maxine-waters|Maxine Waters]], to protect itself through permissioned control and issuance of human energy.[1] He described the US dollar as "a centralized shitcoin for over 100 years" and argued that crypto was exposing the fraud of the dollar and the centralized scheme behind it, concluding that "[[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] fixes this."[1]
