# Minneapolis ICE Shooting of Renee Nicole Good

On January 7, 2026, a 37-year-old woman, identified in Minnesota Executive Order 26-01 as Renee Nicole Good, was fatally shot during an immigration enforcement operation involving [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|US Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] in south Minneapolis.[1] The shooting followed a federal surge of masked immigration officials into Minnesota.[1]

## Demonstrations

Protests escalated in Minneapolis on January 8, with demonstrators gathering near the Bishop Henry Whipple Federal Building, which houses ICE offices.[1] Confrontations occurred between protesters and law enforcement; authorities reported crowd-control measures including pepper spray and partial highway closures, though no arrests were announced by local officials.[1] Additional demonstrations and solidarity actions were reported in other US cities.[1]

Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] responded by issuing Executive Order 26-01 and placing the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] on standby, alongside 85 members of the Minnesota State Patrol Mobile Response Team.[1]

## FBI takeover of the investigation

The [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] assumed control of the shooting investigation, limiting access by state investigators.[1] Minnesota investigators claimed the FBI was blocking their access to information.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Contributor Burning Bright framed the episode as "distraction theater" erupting in the same battlespace — Minneapolis — where the Somali-linked fraud revelations had, in his account, shattered plausible deniability.[1] He argued the NGO "protest industrial complex," surfaced in public consciousness partly through the 2025 [[doge|DOGE]] initiative, was activating in the same theater where the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] storyline detonated in 2020 and produced nationwide riots under the "Summer of Love" framing.[1] He described the timing — days before a mass mobilization he characterized as a domestic insurgency — as too convenient to be coincidence.[1]

Contributor Ashe in America likewise said "it all feels very George Floyd," joked that paid protesters charge more in winter, and called the story "a distraction" from the Minnesota Somali fraud hearings.[1]

## Aftermath and precedent

Subsequent Badlands coverage treated Good's death as establishing a precedent in the Minneapolis confrontations between residents and federal immigration officers. She was described as a 37-year-old fatally shot on January 7, 2026 while attempting to ram an [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agent with her car.[2] Roughly a week later an ICE agent shot and wounded a Venezuelan national who authorities said attacked him with a shovel during a traffic stop in north [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]].[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the rules of the game had been confirmed with Good on January 7 — that one cannot ram law enforcement with a car — and that the second incident added that one cannot attack officers with shovels.[2] Continued protests and the looting of federal vehicles prompted President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to say he was weighing invocation of the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act]].[2]


## Federal response and protests

Federal officials, including Department of Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], defended the agent's actions as self-defense and announced the deployment of hundreds of additional federal officers to Minnesota to support ongoing operations.[3] Local leaders rejected the federal narrative, pointing to video they said contradicted claims that Good posed a threat.[3] The shooting drew tens of thousands of demonstrators locally and more than 1,000 rallies nationwide under the banner "ICE Out For Good," with activists demanding accountability and an end to [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]]'s presence in the city.[3]

Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] drew both support and criticism for an on-camera profanity-laced statement telling ICE to leave the city, which he later defended, while Hennepin County Attorney [[mary-moriarty|Mary Moriarty]] called on the public to share recordings and evidence with her office amid concerns that the federal government was handling the investigation without local participation.[3] Despite some arrests and isolated clashes, city officials described most demonstrations as peaceful.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] offered a caustic commentary, writing that Good "seems to have believed her own propaganda that white people have different experiences with the justice system" and that attacking law enforcement will get a person killed regardless of skin color.[3]

## Federal surge context

The shooting occurred during what the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] described as the largest immigration enforcement operation ever conducted in Minneapolis–St. Paul, involving roughly 2,000 federal personnel deployed in early January 2026 from [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]], [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] and other DHS components.[4] DHS said the surge was focused on enforcing federal immigration law and investigating alleged fraud, unlawful employment practices and human smuggling, and followed ongoing investigations in [[minnesota|Minnesota]].[4] Local officials confirmed the shooting occurred during federal enforcement activity while noting they had no involvement in the operation.[4]

DHS said the agent acted in self-defense after [[renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]], 37, allegedly attempted to strike officers with a vehicle, and stated that the wider operation remained ongoing with further arrests possible.[4] The shooting site lay less than a mile from where [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] was killed in 2020.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] emphasised the timing, noting the death coincided with a [[minnesota-somali-fraud-hearings|House Oversight Committee hearing]] on Minnesota pandemic fraud that was subsequently displaced from news coverage.[4]
