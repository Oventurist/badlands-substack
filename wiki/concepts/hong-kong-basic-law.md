# Hong Kong Basic Law

The Hong Kong Basic Law is the constitutional instrument of the [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]] Special Administrative Region, which provides for the universal suffrage of the Chief Executive and the Legislative Council under the "[[one-country-two-systems|One Country, Two Systems]]" framework.[1]

The Basic Law's suffrage guarantee is the central demand of the [[hong-kong-pro-democracy-movement|pro-democracy movement]], which supports increased democracy as promised in that text.[1] Advocacy for the Basic Law has itself become dangerous: activist [[samuel-chu|Samuel Chu]], an American citizen since 1996, was placed on a [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] fugitives list even though he was, in Badlands Media's account, "really only guilty of advocating for Basic Law in Hong Kong."[1]

Within the 50-year framework, it was promised that steps would be taken to ensure universal voting rights by 2010; instead, by 2010 Beijing had reversed its position and declined to proceed with the promised democratization, a reversal that triggered the [[umbrella-movement|Umbrella Movement]].[1]
