# Flooding of Hamas Tunnels in Gaza

The flooding of Hamas tunnels in Gaza refers to an Israeli military tactic, reported publicly in December 2023, of pumping seawater from the Mediterranean into the underground tunnel network used by [[hamas|Hamas]] beneath [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## Reporting

The Wall Street Journal reported the operation, citing United States officials briefed on the matter, and said the flooding had begun sometime in the previous month.[1] Two U.S. officials confirmed the account to ABC News but cautioned that only some of the tunnels were being flooded, with the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] seeking to compare the tactic's effectiveness against other approaches previously used against the network, including airstrikes, explosives, drones and robots.[1]

IDF chief of staff [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]] said the previous week that flooding the tunnels was "a good idea, but I won't comment on its specifics," according to The Times of Israel.[1] A spokesperson for the Israeli defense minister declined to comment to the Journal, saying tunnel operations were classified.[1]

The item was carried in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief of December 13, 2023, as a bonus item drawn from The Daily Beast's account of the reporting.[1]

## Earlier reporting on the plan (December 5, 2023)

A week earlier, [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] reported that Israel's military had put in place controversial and high-risk plans to flood the tunnel network with sea water, with high-powered pumps already set up at key locations in the Gaza Strip.[2] The stated aim was to force Hamas militants above ground or else drown them, using pumps to draw water from the Mediterranean in volumes expected to fully flood the tunnels within weeks.[2] According to the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]], US defense officials had been briefed on the "option," though it was not believed the Israelis had yet pulled the trigger.[2] The report said the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] finished assembling large seawater pumps roughly one mile north of the Al-Shati refugee camp around the middle of November, with each of at least five pumps able to move thousands of cubic meters of water per hour.[2]

Some US officials expressed "concern" about the plan given its potential to kill more civilians and further destroy the Strip's infrastructure.[2] A prime issue was that some 137 Israeli and foreign hostages remained in [[hamas|Hamas]] captivity after the end of the previous week's ceasefire and prisoner-swap deal, and very likely many were being held within the tunnel system and its cavernous rooms, which include subterranean offices and command centers.[2] Israel had identified at least 800 tunnels but believed the network to be much larger than what was known.[2] One unnamed security source told the Journal, "We are not sure how successful pumping will be since nobody knows the details of the tunnels and the ground around them," adding that it was "impossible to know if that will be effective because we don't know how seawater will drain in tunnels no one has been in before."[2]
