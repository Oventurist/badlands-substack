# Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence for Science, Security and Technology (FASST)

The Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence for Science, Security and Technology (FASST) initiative is an agency-wide effort proposed by the US [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] to harness and advance artificial intelligence for the public's benefit.[1]

## Announcement and scope

First mentioned in May 2024, the program's roadmap was unveiled on Tuesday, July 16, 2024, with details shared first with Axios.[1] The initiative is aimed at helping the United States achieve top capabilities in AI for scientific, energy, and national security aims, and would leverage the department's seventeen national laboratories.[1]

A central technical goal is the construction of energy-efficient AI supercomputers, addressing the challenge of advancing the technology without causing energy consumption to skyrocket — a concern Energy Secretary Jennifer Granholm identified as a top priority.[1] The department already operates some of the world's fastest supercomputers, used for climate change and energy research as well as nuclear weapons modernization.[1] DOE envisions its role as helping its own scientists and the broader scientific community create trustworthy AI models to yield breakthroughs across fields including clean energy technologies.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the announcement as more than a routine technology program, framing it as a disclosure or timed release indicative of long-term "patriot plans."[1] He connected FASST to three themes: [[operation-warp-speed|Operation Warp Speed]], "The Switch" theory, and the Q drops, arguing that the program's name has "Warp Speed and 'The Switch' written all over it" and that its reference to seventeen national laboratories was potentially a Q communication.[1]

He further argued that the plan comes into sharp focus when considered alongside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s rapid public turn in favor of Bitcoin adoption — and particularly mining adoption — viewed through the lens of national defense and energy infrastructure.[1]
