# War on Fraud

The "War on Fraud" is a federal initiative announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during his February 2026 [[state-of-the-union|State of the Union]] address and led by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], aimed at rooting out waste, fraud, and abuse in federal benefit programs.[1]

## Minnesota Medicaid suspension

Vance moved immediately after the announcement: the administration suspended [[medicaid|Medicaid]] payments to [[minnesota|Minnesota]], citing concerns about fraud in the state's program.[1] Federal officials said approximately $259 million in Medicaid funding was being withheld while the review proceeded.[1] Vance appeared alongside [[centers-for-medicare-and-medicaid-services|Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services]] Administrator [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]] when announcing the action.[1] Minnesota officials were given 60 days to address federal concerns and implement corrective measures in order to potentially restore the suspended funds.[1] Minnesota Democratic leaders criticized the suspension as politically motivated and warned it could harm beneficiaries.[1]

## Relationship to DOGE

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the initiative as the human follow-through phase of a technology transformation program begun by [[doge|DOGE]], comparing it to the standard cycle of plan, design, test, deploy, review, and launch.[1] She noted that the executive order creating DOGE explicitly framed it as a technology endeavor, directing the USDS Administrator to commence a Software Modernization Initiative covering government-wide software, network infrastructure, and IT systems, and requiring agency heads to give USDS "full and prompt access to all unclassified agency records, software systems, and IT systems."[1]

"DOGE was tooling," she wrote, contrasting corporate environments — where employees are tracked to the keystroke and waste is hard to hide — with a federal government built on plausible deniability.[1] Among the findings she attributed to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and his team: Treasury payment approval officers instructed to always approve payments, with zero denials across entire careers, even for known fraudulent or terrorist-linked recipients; budget codes optional or left blank for nearly 70% of federal spending (about $4.7 trillion), making tracing nearly impossible; agencies buying far more software licenses than needed, such as 37,000 licenses for 13,000 employees; 47 redundant financial systems across 24 major agencies and 111 instances of 59 different HR products; retirements hand-processed in a Pennsylvania limestone mine by more than 700 workers using manila envelopes and cardboard boxes at roughly 10,000 applications per month; and an incomplete website modernization project that burned more than $5 million with no output.[1] Musk described most government systems as "shockingly primitive."[1]

In this reading the headline fraud dollar figures were "the shiny objects," while systems modernization — installing tooling to monitor agencies and prevent waste, fraud, and abuse — was the actual goal.[1] With Musk departed from government service since May and the temporary DOGE organization shut down in November, the tooling was presumed to be operating, requiring only human follow-through; "apparently that person is JD Vance."[1] Ashe added that if the effort is more than theater, it should produce new cycles of disclosure and accountability with trackable actuals, and asked whether the DOGE tooling came from [[palantir|Palantir]].[1]
