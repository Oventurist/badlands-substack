# Chiles v. Salazar

Chiles v. Salazar is a United States Supreme Court case concerning whether Colorado's ban on so-called "conversion therapy" violates the First Amendment. The [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] agreed to review the case on Monday, March 10, 2025, in a grant of certiorari described in contemporaneous coverage as potentially having "blockbuster repercussions."[1]

## Background

The case involves Kaley Chiles, a Colorado licensed professional counselor who wishes to provide services to Colorado youth suffering from gender dysphoria.[1] Colorado law forbids Chiles from providing any counseling — that is, from speaking — if it concerns the concept of a young person not seeking to affirm their transgender status.[1] Under the statute, if a young person who has transitioned from one sex to another questions that decision and approaches Chiles for guidance about potentially detransitioning, she is legally forbidden from providing that guidance.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the Colorado statute as legal madness following on medical madness, arguing that the law permits minors to undergo irreversible surgical and hormonal interventions while forbidding anyone from helping them reverse course — summarized as an "once trans, always trans" doctrine.[1] She predicted that history would harshly judge the era, with future generations questioning how society convinced itself that sterilizing and operating on children was moral.[1]
