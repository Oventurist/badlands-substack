# Jekyll Island Meeting

The 1910 meeting on Jekyll Island, Georgia, is the secret gathering of bankers and legislators that produced the blueprint for the American central banking system and, ultimately, the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1] In Badlands Media's framing it stands as the reference point for monetary upheaval: the digitization of financial assets is described as "one of the most transformative and impactful moments in money since the 1910 meeting on Jekyll Island."[1]

The comparison anchors the article's central argument — that the rise of [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and other digital assets after the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]] represents the first serious challenge to the century of central banking that Jekyll Island inaugurated, with early adopters functioning as a pilot program for whatever monetary order comes next.[1] The open question the article poses is whether Bitcoin can free people from what it calls the regime and its private banking cartel, or whether apathy, fraud, and the appetite for power will instead accelerate the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] toward a [[new-world-order|New World Order]].[1]

## The 1910 Gathering in Badlands Media's Account

Following the establishment of the [[national-monetary-commission|National Monetary Commission]], Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson Aldrich]] embarked on a two-year fact-finding tour of Europe, consulting at length with private central bankers in England, France and Germany — all of which, the article asserts, were controlled by the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]].[2] Aldrich returned on the 22nd of November, and shortly afterward some of America's wealthiest and most powerful men boarded his private railcar in the strictest secrecy, presumably intending to capitalize on recent instability by creating a new central bank that would withstand the test of time.[2]

The article notes that the story as popularly told was recounted by G. Edward Griffin in *The Creature from Jekyll Island*, and cautions that while Griffin took many creative liberties, some aspects of the book remain true while others are contested.[2]

## Participants

Among the group was [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]], then earning a $500,000 annual salary from [[kuhn-loeb-and-co|Kuhn, Loeb & Company]] — described in the article as Rothschild-owned — ostensibly to lobby for a privately owned central bank in America.[2] [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] was also present.[2] The article emphasizes that because the Rothschilds, Warburgs and Schiffs were interconnected by marriage, these participants were essentially the same extended family.[2] Secrecy was said to be so tight that participants were cautioned to use only first names to prevent servants from learning their identities.[2]

## Confirmation and Criticism

Critics have dismissed the Jekyll Island story as conspiratorial nonsense, but the article responds that none of these critics were present and that the documentation left by those who were suggests plenty of conspiracy.[2] Years later, participant [[frank-vanderlip|Frank Vanderlip]], president of National Citibank and a representative of the Rockefeller family, confirmed the trip in the Saturday Evening Post, writing: "I was as secretive indeed, as furtive as any conspirator ... Discovery we knew, simply must not happen ... If it were to be exposed that our particular group had got together and written a banking bill, that bill would have no chance whatever of passage by Congress."[2]

## Agenda Beyond a Central Bank

Setting up a central bank was not the only item on the agenda.[2] The bankers also needed to determine how to bring the other 20,000 national banks under their control, and were alarmed that the nation's economy had grown strong enough that corporations were financing their own expansions out of profits rather than taking large bank loans — meaning American industry was becoming independent of the money changers.[2] The product of the meeting was the [[aldrich-plan|Aldrich bill]], presented to Congress for debate.[2]
