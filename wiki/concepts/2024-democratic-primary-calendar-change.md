# 2024 Democratic Primary Calendar Change

The 2024 Democratic primary calendar change was the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]]'s decision, taken on Saturday, February 4, 2023, to make South Carolina the first state to hold a 2024 Democratic presidential primary, in order to prioritize black voters.[1]

## The decision

The DNC's vote dethroned Iowa and New Hampshire from their traditional positions as the first states to host presidential nominating contests.[1] South Carolina, a battleground Southern state that had traditionally been among the first four states to vote, was scheduled to hold its 2024 Democratic presidential primary on February 3.[1] The decision was reported by [[just-the-news|Just the News]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that election fraud and manipulation extend to nearly every arena, with voter rolls, mail-in voting and gerrymandering being only the better-known examples.[1] He described an election, as a matter of law, as the process by which citizens who have rights select a steward to represent them in government through a contest determining who is most qualified — a contractual, trust-based relationship between citizen and elected official.[1] The qualifications of an ideal steward, he wrote, fit a common-sense definition of trustworthiness: good faith, transparency and clean hands, making the citizen's task substantial given rampant breach of trust and negligence among politicians.[1]

Deschamps read the reordering of Iowa and New Hampshire behind South Carolina as an attempt to sway black voters by producing the appearance of serving them first, and doubted that DNC candidates would genuinely represent South Carolina voters' rights and interests where those conflicted with the party's globalist agenda.[1] He predicted the DNC would instead pander to groups and races to induce blind loyalty, after which deceived masses would vote on identity politics rather than on whether a candidate truly represented them.[1]
