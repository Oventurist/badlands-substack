# Rural Digital Opportunity Fund

The Rural Digital Opportunity Fund is the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] program through which the agency awards subsidies to providers that commit to deliver high-speed internet service to underserved rural households and businesses in the United States.[1] Awards are conditional: recipients must satisfy program requirements and demonstrate the technical capacity to deliver the promised service, and the FCC can rescind provisional awards when it concludes an applicant has not met its burden.[1]

## The Starlink award and its rescission

[[spacex|SpaceX]]'s satellite internet unit [[starlink|Starlink]] had provisionally been allocated $885.5 million under the program in exchange for a commitment to serve 642,000 rural homes and businesses across 35 states.[1] In August 2022 the FCC rescinded that funding on the basis of speed-test data, and on December 12, 2023 the commission reaffirmed the denial after SpaceX challenged it, finding that Starlink had failed to meet basic program requirements and could not demonstrate that it would deliver the promised service.[1]

Among its reasons, the commission cited SpaceX's failure to successfully launch its Starship rocket, stating that "the uncertain nature of Starship's future launches could impact Starlink's ability to meet" its obligations.[1] FCC Chair [[jessica-rosenworcel|Jessica Rosenworcel]] said the agency "followed a careful legal, technical and policy review to determine that this applicant had failed to meet its burden."[1]

## Dispute over politicization

SpaceX said it was "deeply disappointed and perplexed" by the outcome, maintaining that Starlink "is demonstrably one of the best options - likely the best option" to accomplish the program's goals.[1] The two Republican members of the five-member commission dissented, arguing that the FCC was improperly holding SpaceX to 2025 targets three years early and that the Biden administration's anger toward [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] explained the decision.[1] Commissioner [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] said the commission had joined "the growing list of administrative agencies that are taking action against Elon Musk's businesses," describing the ruling as fitting "the Biden Administration's pattern of regulatory harassment."[1] Badlands Media commentary treated the episode as public evidence of administrative-state retaliation against political and cultural opponents.[1]
