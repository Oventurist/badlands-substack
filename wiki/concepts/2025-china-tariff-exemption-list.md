# 2025 China Tariff Exemption List

The 2025 China tariff exemption list refers to a quietly compiled Chinese roster of US-made goods exempted from Beijing's 125% retaliatory tariffs, reported by Reuters on Wednesday, April 30, 2025 as an effort to ease trade tensions without making public concessions.[1]

## Contents and expansion

The list appeared to be expanding over time: China recently waived tariffs on US ethane imports, and reports the previous week indicated Beijing had quietly rolled back tariffs on some US semiconductor products — easing pressure on its own technology sector — along with certain US pharmaceuticals.[1] The approach allowed Beijing to maintain a firm public posture while offering behind-the-scenes relief to its economy.[1]

## Trump's response

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defended the 145% US tariffs on Chinese imports, saying China "deserves it" and would likely "eat" the costs in order to shield American consumers.[1] He acknowledged on the same Wednesday, however, the possibility of supply shortages and higher prices for consumers.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright noted that Trump had recently defined "INTELLIGENCE" as the ability to predict the future, and argued that whether through overt control, game theory, or both, Trump's political career is defined by that trait — a point the writer said many observers still fail to grasp.[1] Separately, Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said Congress could intervene to curtail the tariff regime if an imbalance developed between executive and legislative power, but that the policy was only about three weeks old and it was not yet appropriate for Congress to legislate.[1]
