# Trump Broadcast License Revocation Controversy (2026)

In July 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said that television networks which declined to air his primetime White House address on election security should have their broadcast licenses revoked.[1] During the speech Trump criticized ABC and NBC by name and alleged that media organizations refusing to carry the address were part of a "plot."[1] Reuters reported that the speech included renewed claims about election security and alleged Chinese interference that U.S. intelligence has not substantiated.[1]

## Network conduct

ABC and NBC did not interrupt their primary broadcast schedules to carry the address live but streamed it on their digital news platforms.[1] [[cnn|CNN]] also declined to air the speech on its main television channel, while CBS and Fox News carried the address live.[1]

## Regulatory and legal reaction

[[federal-communications-commission|FCC]] Commissioner [[anna-gomez|Anna Gomez]] said the proposal was unconstitutional and that broadcasters face no FCC penalty for declining to air a presidential speech.[1] Legal experts cited by Reuters said broadcasters hold First Amendment discretion over programming and that license revocation would require a formal administrative process.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode within the broader information war, recalling Trump's 2015 characterization of the "Fake News Media" as the "enemy of the people" and describing that statement as a literal declaration of war that was initially read as hyperbole.[1] He wrote that [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] was founded expressly to combat the engineered noise of what he called the Media Industrial Complex.[1] The commentary argued that Trump targets the media not out of annoyance but because media constitute "the key power projection arm of the invisible enemy," and that the narrative battlespace is the mass psychological battlespace that determines political reality.[1] It concluded that as the disclosure phase accelerates, the media machine becomes more exposed and legally compromised, and that time is running out for media figures to choose "between truth and lies, loyalty and treason."[1] The commentary situated this within the [[deep-state]] framework long advanced by the outlet.[1]
