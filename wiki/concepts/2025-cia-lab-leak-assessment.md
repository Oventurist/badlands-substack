# 2025 CIA Lab Leak Assessment

The 2025 CIA lab leak assessment was a revised judgment issued by the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] in late January 2025 stating that its analysts now favored the theory that the Covid-19 pandemic originated from an accidental leak at a research laboratory in Wuhan, China, rather than emerging naturally from a wet market there.[1] For years the agency had said it lacked sufficient information to choose between the two explanations.[1]

## Basis of the shift

Officials said there was no new intelligence behind the change; the assessment rested on the same evidence the agency had been reviewing for months.[1] The analysis was based in part on a closer look at conditions in the high-security laboratories in Wuhan province before the outbreak, according to people familiar with the agency's work.[1] An agency spokeswoman said the natural-origin theory remained plausible and that the CIA would continue to evaluate any credible new intelligence reporting.[1] Some American officials argued the debate mattered little because the Chinese government had failed either to regulate its markets or to oversee its laboratories, while others considered it an important intelligence and scientific question.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentator Jordan Sather credited the shift to Trump's new CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], describing him as already at work in declassifying a CIA report affirming what the community had long asserted, and recalling that his own videos had been removed by YouTube and Facebook for making the same claim in February 2020.[1]

Commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry connected the assessment to a parallel development — the stated willingness of Russian [[foreign-intelligence-service-of-russia|SVR]] chief [[sergey-naryshkin|Sergey Naryshkin]] to meet Ratcliffe — and to claims by the late Russian Lt. General [[igor-kirillov|Igor Kirillov]] that Covid-19 originated in a US-funded bioweapons laboratory in Ukraine.[1] He noted that confirmation of the existence of such laboratories had come through congressional testimony by [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]], elicited by then-Senator [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], who had just been sworn in as Secretary of State at the head of the [[state-department|State Department]].[1] On this reading, Trump's cabinet selections were "starting to make more and more sense."[1] See also [[lab-leak-theory|the lab-leak theory]].[1]
