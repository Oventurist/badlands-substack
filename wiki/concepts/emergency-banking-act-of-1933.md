# Emergency Banking Act of 1933

The Emergency Banking Act, signed in March of 1933, is one of the two statutes identified as providing the authority for [[executive-order-6102|Executive Order 6102]], issued the following month by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]] to forbid the hoarding of gold coin, gold bullion, and gold certificates within the continental United States.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] describes the Act, together with the [[trading-with-the-enemy-act-of-1917|Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917]], as repugnant to the spirit of freedom embedded in the founding documents of the United States.[1] In his account these statutes belong to a wider pattern in which declared emergencies are used to justify assertions of near-total federal control over commerce, and in which the suspension of gold's monetary role produces financial chaos that falls hardest on the less well-off while tipped-off elites profit from the resulting transfer of wealth.[1]
