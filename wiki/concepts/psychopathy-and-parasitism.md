# Psychopathy and Parasitism

"Psychopathy and parasitism" is the analytical frame used in the Badlands Media article "Q is Our Best Fren" to profile the alleged global [[cabal]].[1] The article argues that these two concepts together are sufficient to explain nearly everything about the cabal's behaviour, and that once they are understood a great many finer details of the cabal's plans can be inferred.[1]

## Core claim

At the archetypal level, the article defines a parasite as a being that sustains itself by feeding off other beings.[1] Psychopathy is then characterised as a terminally advanced form of parasitism — a formulation the author concedes clinical research does not state so bluntly.[1] Ordinary humans are said to be mildly parasitic in that they kill to eat, but the decisive difference is that the psychopath's existence is defined by endless hunger, with satiation experienced only while actively harming people or animals.[1]

## The cabal as parasite

The cabal's entire system is described as designed to siphon energy and resources from the population back to itself, which it then uses to further enslave and torture the masses; the propaganda directed at the public is characterised as a form of psychological torture.[1] Its occultism is framed as an elaborate mechanism for consuming other people's energy, its banking systems as serving the same purpose, and its media as designed to hypnotise people into accepting exploitation.[1] Poisoning the population is presented as another means of making it easier to exploit.[1]

Drawing on [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]]'s book [[shadow-men|Shadow Men]], the article reports that cabal elites see themselves as gods above ordinary people, and that some reportedly refuse to eat food that has not been subjected to torture.[1] Unlike atheists, the cabal is said to acknowledge a Divine Creator but to hate Him, wishing to take His place — an ambition the author calls the ultimate parasitic act and one supposedly shared by all psychopaths.[1] The article invokes the adage that the Devil's greatest trick was convincing the world he does not exist, noting that modern secular society has a corresponding blind spot for psychopaths.[1]

## Infiltration

Psychopaths are described as born infiltrators: aware from childhood that they are different, they spend their lives learning to superficially fit in and honing manipulation.[1] They band together when interests align, but without genuine affection, and rely on mutual blackmail rather than promises to hold their alliances together.[1] The resulting networks are likened to a deadly parasitic infection of society.

The article compares the cabal specifically to the *Strongyloides stercoralis* parasite, which can colonise a host for decades without symptoms before producing deadly complications once the immune system finally responds.[1] Like that organism, the cabal is said to have infiltrated nearly all the "organs" of society, enabling a system-wide response whenever a local branch of its network is threatened.[1]

## Related further reading

The article recommends [[karen-mitchell|Dr. Karen Mitchell]]'s work on the [[persistent-predatory-personality|Persistent Predatory Personality]], the book *Psychopath Proof*, and Napoleon's *Shadow Men* for readers pursuing the topic.[1]

## Treatment in The Werewolf Game

The opening installment of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series, published March 18, 2024, develops the same equation at length, stating flatly that "psychopathy IS parasitism" and defining evil itself as unbounded parasitism.[2] It attributes to psychopaths an *eternal power struggle worldview* in which the whole of existence is a Darwinian contest for domination, identifies this as the central belief of Marxism, and argues that it functions as a unifier for the world's slavers and dark occultists.[2] The essay concludes that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] "amounts to organized psychopathy."[2]

The article lists traits agreed upon across the psychopathy literature: an early awareness of being different that leads to mask-wearing, pure appetite without conscience or moral restraint, inner deadness relieved only by parasitizing others, fear of exposure requiring operation "under the cover of night," hubris that leads to self-exposure, and an ability to weasel into trust before the mask slips.[2] It identifies the inability to achieve satisfaction as a driving feature of the type, contrasting it with healthy people who "consume to satisfaction and no more," and notes that psychopaths are said to feed on the energy generated by causing pain to other living beings, with childhood animal torture given as a classic sign.[2] Trauma-induced cases are described as "secondary psychopathy," toward which compassion is permissible but which does not oblige innocent people to tolerate psychopathic disruption of their lives — a point illustrated with the fable of the frog and the scorpion.[2]

The same essay extends the analysis to systems, arguing that a large-scale psychopathic enterprise reveals itself by inflicting pain on the masses without rational justification and by hiding its own existence, and citing [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]], [[andrew-lobaczewski|Andrew Lobaczewski]] and [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]]'s [[psychopath-proof|*Psychopath Proof*]] as key sources.[2] It maintains that accurate knowledge of psychopathy is one of the master keys both to defeating the Deep State and to preventing its re-emergence.[2]
