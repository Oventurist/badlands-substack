# Ukraine victory plan

The "victory plan" was a document prepared by Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] in 2024 outlining terms for Ukraine's victory over [[russia|Russia]], which he intended to present to the White House and to both American presidential candidates ahead of the November election.[1]

## Presentation

Zelensky was expected to meet again with U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in September 2024, and revealed on 27 August that he intended at that meeting to present the White House with the plan.[1] He specified that the plan would also be presented directly to Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the two candidates headed into the election.[1]

"The plan is prepared. I think it's right that I first present this plan to the US President," Zelensky was quoted as saying in Ukrainian media, adding that "the success of this plan depends on whether we get what is outlined in it, or if we are free to use what is included."[1] He also stated that "Kursk region is part of our plan—Ukraine's victory plan. It may sound overly ambitious to some, but for us, it's an important plan," tying the [[kursk-incursion|Kursk incursion]] directly to the initiative.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the plan as a negotiating instrument rather than a military programme: on a surface level, Ukraine was conducting a possibly successful, possibly catastrophic incursion into Kursk, but was realistically using the counteroffensive to set terms at a negotiating table whose existence Kyiv had refused to acknowledge as recently as six months earlier.[1] Along the way Zelensky continued to narratively "bite the hand that feeds" by casting aspersions on the Establishment — now represented by Harris — accusing it of withholding aid and support.[1]

Reframing Zelensky as either covertly captured or overtly planted, Burning Bright argued that each deployment damaged both the state he leads and the Western globalist hegemon that propped it up.[1] He noted that Zelensky increasingly seeded Trump's name into his statements, moving the Overton window from scoffing at Trump's offers to broker peace toward openly appealing for his input, thereby preparing previously unreachable layers of the collective mind to accept Trump as a peacemaker in waiting.[1] He predicted that the eventual peace deal signed by Zelensky and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] — likely publicly brokered by Trump — would closely resemble the original terms Putin laid out in February 2022 alongside the announcement of the Special Military Operation he framed as a denazification effort, terms Trump himself softly cosigned by suggesting Ukraine leave the Donbas.[1]

## Tomahawk request and the White House leak (October 2024)

On Wednesday, October 30, 2024, Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] confirmed that he had asked the United States for Tomahawk long-range missiles to help defeat Russia, and criticised the [[white-house|White House]] for leaking secrets to the American media.[2] Tomahawks can fly up to 1,500 kilometres and would allow Ukraine to strike targets deep inside Russia if Washington permitted it, a component described as key to the Victory Plan for beating back [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[2]

Zelenskyy objected to the request being divulged to [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] for a story in which an anonymous senior US official called the Ukrainian ask totally unfeasible.[2] "And this was confidential information between Ukraine and the White House. How should we understand these messages? So, it means between partners there's nothing confidential?" he said in an interview with Nordic media outlets.[2] The Times reported that senior US officials did not believe Ukrainian leadership had made a convincing case for how the long-range missiles would turn the tide on the battlefield.[2]

The Times piece also suggested the plan may have been designed to fail: analysts and diplomats quoted in the report argued the real audience was domestic, with Zelenskyy using a hard sell — including an address to Parliament — to show Ukrainians he had done all he could, prepare them for a possible deal, and supply a convenient scapegoat in the West.[2] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] found the episode darkly comic, describing Zelenskyy as playing a character "straight from Central Casting" and noting the irony of the media's former darling turning on the press and on his backers at Langley.[2]
