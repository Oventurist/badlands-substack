# H-1B Visa

The H-1B is a United States non-immigrant visa category permitting employers to hire foreign workers in specialty occupations. Certification of the underlying labor conditions runs through the [[united-states-department-of-labor|Department of Labor]], which makes the programme both an immigration and a labor-market instrument. In the Badlands Media corpus the H-1B is treated as a central test of whether the [[america-first|America First]] agenda can be implemented against entrenched corporate and globalist interests.

## The 2024 controversy

Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] describes a public argument over H-1B visas at the end of 2024 as a scandal "that nearly tore us apart and ultimately cost us [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek]]." Quoting the December 30, 2024 Badlands News Brief, she restates the position that "you can't have 'America First Globalism'," that [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s promise to reinvest in American workers, companies and industries "is at odds with the global world order," and that "we are not trading one set of globalists for another." The Brief's original assessment held that the week Americans spent arguing about H-1Bs was "a good thing," because "everyone elevated their knowledge of visa types and talent pools and skill gaps."

## The 2026 fraud and trafficking investigation

The July 10, 2026 Badlands Brief "Mossad Bait & Labor Tackles" reports that the Department of Labor's [[office-of-inspector-general|Office of Inspector General]] launched a major investigation into fraud and human trafficking tied to the H-1B and [[program-electronic-review-management|PERM]] systems. The OIG stated that such abuses "undermine the integrity of Department of Labor programs designed to address genuine labor shortages — not to line the pockets of bad actors at the expense of American jobs," and pledged to "dismantle human trafficking and forced-labor networks that exploit the foreign guest worker visa system." Inspector General [[anthony-desposito|Anthony P. D'Esposito]] characterised the conduct under investigation as forced labor, worker exploitation and displacement of American workers rather than mere paperwork violations.

Ashe in America reads the investigation as a direct consequence of the 2024 debate, arguing that public outrage then "seeded the conditions to build legitimate American support for accountability and change."
