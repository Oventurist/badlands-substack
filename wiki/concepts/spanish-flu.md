# Spanish Flu

The Spanish Flu features in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s *A Virus, or a Metaphor?* as the clearest example of what he considers a deliberate coded error by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## The "1917" statement

During what Carlson calls the Covid Plandemic, Trump said the pandemic was the worst thing to happen to America since the Spanish Flu in 1917.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He was viciously mocked for historical ignorance, because the Spanish Flu occurred in 1918 — and yet, even after being publicly ridiculed, Trump continued to mention the Spanish Flu of 1917.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## Carlson's decoding

Carlson notes that, like Covid, the Spanish Flu was sold to the public as a deadly virus, and that in his reading Trump uses "virus" references to refer to Communism.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) The remaining question is the date: what happened in 1917?[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) In October of that year the [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik uprising]] occurred, in which the far-left Bolsheviks overthrew the Russian monarch and murdered his family, bringing about Communism and the formation of the Soviet Union largely through the use of propaganda and chaos, and fomenting a civil war by dividing the country between the "whites" and the "reds."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

Carlson concludes that these comms are Trump letting the public know what is really happening in America: that Communism, a virus, is spreading throughout the country with the sole intent of dividing and destroying it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)
