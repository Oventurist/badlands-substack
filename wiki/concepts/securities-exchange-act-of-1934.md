# Securities Exchange Act of 1934

The Securities Exchange Act of 1934 is the federal statute governing secondary trading of securities in the United States and the law under which the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] polices market conduct.[1]

## Section 10(b)

Badlands Media invoked the Act in the context of cryptocurrency fraud, noting that in financial services "deliberately misleading investors is a violation of Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934."[1] The article applied that standard to the [[saitama-token|Saitama Token]] affair, in which the project advertised a [[fair-launch|fair launch]] while reserving undisclosed team tokens later used, according to investigators, to manipulate the market.[1] It also cited the SEC's statement that "victims in these cases have the right to recover their investment losses they suffered as a result of the fraud."[1]

The broader argument advanced was that existing law is adequate to address [[rug-pull|rug pulls]] and other digital-asset fraud — but that enforcing it means inviting government intervention into a sector whose entire premise is decentralization, at a moment when governments are "itching to lay claim to — and neutralize — the power of Bitcoin and other digital assets."[1]
