# Great Tribulation

The Tribulation is the period of divine judgment on a sinful and wicked world described in the [[book-of-revelation|book of Revelation]]. It is examined at length in the Badlands Media feature [[are-we-entering-the-millennial-reign|"Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?"]], which asks whether the present era qualifies.

## Duration and structure

The article notes debate over whether the Tribulation is a literal seven-year period — an interpretation of the seventieth week of Daniel 9 — or symbolic. Revelation makes reference to three separate three-and-a-half-year periods (11:2–3, 12:6 and 13:5), which many combine into a seven-year span by treating two as sequential and one as overlapping. John describes the judgments in three sets of seven: 7 seals, 7 trumpets and 7 bowls, or 21 judgments in total, with continuing debate over their uniqueness, overlap, timing, duration, and literal or symbolic meaning.

As summarized in the article, the seals symbolize a progression of events leading to final judgment and the establishment of God's Kingdom, representing conquest, conflict, bloodshed, warfare, violence, scarcity, economic hardship, famine, poverty, death, pestilence, beasts, suffering, persecution, martyrdom and cosmic upheaval. The trumpets signal both divine judgment and the culmination of God's plan of redemption, representing devastation of natural resources and beasts, contaminated and bitter water, reduced light, increased darkness, spiritual torment and anguish, war and death. The bowls represent the culmination of God's wrath, more intense and specific than the preceding judgments.

## The threshold test

Patriots in Progress argues that to conclude the present qualifies as the Tribulation, one would need credible data showing consequences on the scale of the fourth seal and sixth trumpet: the death of one quarter and one third of humanity respectively. The text is unclear, he notes, whether these are one event within a range or sequential events totalling one half, and whether the deaths are immediate or spread over time. Examining the root words for the powers granted — sword, hunger, death, beasts, fire, smoke and brimstone — he concludes they are broad enough to encompass all forms of destruction, warfare, famine and death.

The closest historical parallel offered is the Black Death of around 1351, which killed an estimated one tenth to one quarter of the global population — and which was plainly not the end. Against a present population of roughly 8 billion, the author proposes counting those "who would be here but aren't," citing abortion, cancer, sterilization, infertility, destruction of the nuclear family, starvation, thirst, overdose, suicide, murder, war, psyops, genocide, eugenics, slavery and trafficking, along with the roughly 70% of humanity that received at least one dose of a [[covid-19-vaccines|Covid vaccine]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s warnings of population collapse.

## The church during the Tribulation

Against the common teaching of a pre-Tribulation [[rapture]], the article argues that believers are pictured in the world throughout the eschatological narrative, receiving warnings and encouragement — notably the blessing pronounced on "the dead who die in the Lord from now on," positioned after the seal and trumpet judgments and just before [[the-beast|the Beast]] and the [[mark-of-the-beast|Mark of the Beast]].
