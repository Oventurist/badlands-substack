# 1998 Gallup Poll on Predictions for 2025

The 1998 Gallup poll on predictions for 2025 was a survey conducted by Gallup and USA Today in which 1,055 Americans were telephoned on landlines and asked for their best predictions about the distant future year of 2025.[1] The poll was taken in a year when Bill Clinton was facing impeachment proceedings, "Titanic" was dominating the Oscars, and most households still relied on landline phones.[1] The results are memorialized in the polling archives maintained by the Roper Center at Cornell University, and were revisited by CNN as 2025 drew to a close.[1]

## Accurate predictions

Several forecasts proved surprisingly prescient. Most Americans predicted that within the next 27 years the country would have elected a Black president, that gay marriages would be legal and commonplace, and that a "deadly new disease" would have emerged.[1] Most respondents also correctly doubted that space travel would become common for ordinary Americans or that alien life forms would have made contact.[1]

## Inaccurate predictions

Other predictions did not hold up. About two-thirds of respondents thought the country would have elected a female president by 2025; more than half expected a cure for cancer; and 61 percent expected that "people will routinely live to be 100 years old."[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for the [[badlands-media|Badlands]] News Brief, reflected that 1998 was a good year — she was 18 or 19 and in college, there was no war on terror, existence was affordable, and the world seemed full of possibility.[1] Focusing on the poll finding that most respondents believed race relations would improve, she argued that they already had: in her account America was largely post-racial by 1998 after tumultuous years in the 1980s and early 1990s, a claim she noted contemporary commentators would themselves call racist.[1] She contended that the election of "the first black president" reignited race relations in an institutionalized way, producing mandatory instruction on white fragility and "struggle sessions," and described Barack Obama's presidency as "the long march through the institutions."[1] She concluded that it proved easy to overthrow America by dehumanizing critics as racists, that many countrymen still deny it happened, and that "there's no statute of limitations on treason."[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a contrasting reading, arguing that 2025 would go down as the first year of a new American Golden Age, whether readers recognized its victories at the time or only in hindsight.[1]
