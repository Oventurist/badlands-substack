# FDA Adverse Event Reporting System

The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) is the U.S. [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]]'s database for collecting reports of undesirable experiences associated with medical products.[1] The agency defines an adverse event as "any undesirable experience associated with the use of a medical product in a patient."[1]

## Shift to real-time reporting

In August 2025 the FDA began "real-time reporting of adverse event data" for the first time, a change the agency called a "significant step forward in modernizing" FAERS.[1] FDA Commissioner [[marty-makary|Marty Makary]] described the move as "radical transparency" on his X account, stating that "adverse event reporting should be fast, seamless and transparent" and that people navigating the government's clunky reporting websites "should not have to wait months for that information to become public. We're closing that waiting period and will continue to streamline the process from start to finish."[1] The FDA operates under the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], led by Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that it would be a revelation for ordinary Americans to learn that FDA adverse event data had not previously been reported in real time, given that the agency costs Americans billions of dollars annually — roughly $60 billion over the preceding decade by conservative estimates — and that the public has been told vaccines are known to be safe and effective because of the FDA.[1] Asking why real-time tracking was not already in place, she wrote that with accurate health data the [[make-america-healthy-again|MAHA]] revolution would accelerate, since it is far easier to drive change with reliable data.[1]
