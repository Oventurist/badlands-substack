# Fortress Economy

"Fortress economy" is the term used to describe the economic model advanced by Chinese Communist Party leader [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] to bolster national self-sufficiency and resilience against external shocks, including geopolitical conflict and global pandemics.[1]

## Origins and documentation

The concept was documented in a study released on July 30, 2024 by Jimmy Goodrich, a fellow at the University of California Institute on Global Conflict and Cooperation, which analyzed official CCP speeches and policy documents to assess the Party's progress in implementing the policy across critical sectors.[1] The paper asserted that the strategy is "designed to bolster national self-sufficiency and resilience against external shocks, and ultimately allow the nation to withstand 'extreme situations' including protracted armed conflict."[1]

The impetus for the pivot was traced to a sequence of global upheavals: rising tensions between the United States and China, the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and the disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, which collectively exposed the vulnerability of interconnected global supply chains and prompted Beijing to reduce dependency on foreign markets.[1]

## Dual-economic circulation

Central to the recalibration is the concept of [[dual-circulation|"dual-economic circulation"]], a policy of reorienting China's economy away from its historical reliance on exports toward a more balanced model that strengthens domestic industry while continuing to engage in international trade.[1] By fostering robust internal economic activity, the Party aims to mitigate the impact of global disruptions.[1]

The approach dovetails with Xi's broader national security agenda, encompassing food and energy security, supply-chain robustness, civil defense mobilization, and the development of strategic reserve infrastructure — all designed to prepare the nation for "extreme-case" scenarios.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] connected the fortress-economy turn to a broader thesis that the COVID lockdown era accelerated the transition toward a multipolar, sovereign world envisioned by nationalist leaders from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Xi and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1] In this reading, the shortages of 2020 demonstrated in cartoonish fashion how reliant the United States was on foreign supply chains, an effect mirrored in China, whose economy had depended heavily on American purchases.[1] Because each side was reliant on the other — the definition of globalism, which both Trump and Xi publicly oppose — the commentary argued that their "trade war" was in substance a mutual arrangement, a narrative deployment meant to paper over the short-term pain of the actual transition beneath it.[1]
