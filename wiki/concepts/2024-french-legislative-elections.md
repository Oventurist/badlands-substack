# 2024 French Legislative Elections

The 2024 French legislative elections were snap parliamentary elections whose decisive round was held on Sunday, July 7, 2024, producing a hung National Assembly.[1]

A coalition of the French left won the most seats, beating back a surge by the far right but failing to secure a majority.[1] All three main blocs fell far short of the 289 seats needed to control the 577-seat National Assembly, the more powerful of France's two legislative chambers, according to official results released early the following Monday.[1]

President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] had called the election on June 9, 2024, after the far right surged in French voting for the European Parliament, saying a return to the voters would provide "clarification."[1] On almost every level that gamble appeared to have backfired.[1]

The outcome left France — a pillar of the European Union, host of the 2024 Olympics, and home to the EU's second-largest economy — facing political paralysis.[1] Associated Press coverage warned that the turmoil could rattle markets and the French economy and carry far-ranging implications for the [[russia-ukraine-war|war in Ukraine]], global diplomacy and European economic stability.[1]

## First round results, June 30, 2024

Projections released after polls closed in the first round on Sunday, June 30, 2024 showed French voters boosting the prospects of the far right while potentially shattering the centrist alliance of President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]].[2] France's public broadcaster projected the far-right [[national-rally|National Rally]], guided by [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]] and her protégé [[jordan-bardella|Jordan Bardella]], comfortably in first place with 33 percent of the national vote; an alliance of leftist parties, the New Popular Front, was second on 28 percent; and Macron's Together alliance trailed with 21 percent.[2]

The projections showed National Rally narrowly falling short of a majority of seats.[2] If the party expanded its lead in the second round on July 7, it could form France's first far-right government since World War II, with the 28-year-old Bardella as prime minister, replacing Macron's pro-Europe, pro-business agenda with a populist, euroskeptic and anti-immigration platform.[2] A second-round result producing no clear majority, by contrast, could paralyze French politics.[2]
