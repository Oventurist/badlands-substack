# Fauci Senate Covid Origins Hearing (2026)

The Fauci Senate Covid origins hearing was a July 2026 congressional proceeding at which Dr. [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] refused to answer questions about the origins of Covid-19 and the pandemic response, invoking the Fifth Amendment.[1]

## Proceedings

Fauci declined to testify on the Wednesday of the hearing, saying he feared Republicans would use his testimony to prosecute him for perjury. In his opening statement he accused Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] of an "obvious obsession" with putting him "behind bars," citing the senator's calls for prosecution, "slanderous comments," and release of Fauci's personal journal. Under advice of counsel, Fauci said he would not answer questions despite decades of cooperating with Congress.[1]

After Fauci pleaded the fifth, Paul stated: "The chairman has denied your assertion of privilege, and directed you to answer them. But you nonetheless refuse and stand on privilege, despite the existence of the pardon. The committee will have to consider after this hearing what appropriate action should be taken against you for the failure to testify after being directed to do so. It's against the law to obstruct an investigation of congress. There will be repercussions to your refusal to testify today."[1]

Ahead of the hearing Paul had warned of possible perjury charges if Fauci lied. Asked whether he was trying to trap Fauci into perjury, Paul replied: "There's no risk to perjury if you tell the truth. … The only thing he can't do is lie again."[1]

## Disclosures and aftermath

Despite Fauci's silence, Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that new information entered the record — including the claim that [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] held Q clearance during the pandemic. The hearing trended in news coverage on X, and further reporting by [[natalie-winters|Natalie Winters]] surfaced Fauci's 2023 move to a stealth mRNA vaccine startup later acquired by [[eli-lilly|Eli Lilly]].[1]

Paul indicated he would pursue Fauci for contempt of Congress, the charge that imprisoned Steve Bannon and Peter Navarro. Ashe in America argued that the case is strategically valuable because resolving the [[autopen-scandal|Autopen scandal]] requires challenging the validity of at least one Biden-era pardon, and Fauci is the ideal test case given broad public support for prosecuting him. She speculated the case could reach the Supreme Court, forcing Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] to recuse.[1]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] separately remarked that presidential pardons are very powerful and that Fauci could not be touched apart from the Autopen angle — comments that generated further controversy.[1]
