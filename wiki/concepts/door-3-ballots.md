# Door 3 Ballots

"Door 3" is the term for the auxiliary ballot receptacle used at [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona voting locations during the November 8, 2022 general election. When on-site tabulators could not read a voter's ballot — a widespread problem attributed to non-uniform ballot-on-demand printer configuration settings at at least 60 locations — voters were directed to deposit the ballot in Door 3 rather than feed it into the tabulator. Door 3 ballots were to be transported to the [[maricopa-county-tabulation-and-election-center|Maricopa County Tabulation and Election Center]] for central-count tabulation.

## Procedure as described to voters

[[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] recounts in An SOS From Ground Zero that at her polling place the receptacle was labeled "BOX 3" rather than "Door 3," and that she and her husband were instructed to use it because no working tabulation machine was available. Poll workers reassured them that the ballots would be escorted to MCTEC by both a Republican and a Democrat. Gillespie found the assurance hollow, noting that partisan escort provides no guarantee where "RINOs or DINOs" may be involved, and writes that her "radar instantly went up."

## Allegations of statutory non-compliance

The November 19, 2022 letter from Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] to [[tom-liddy|Tom Liddy]] of the [[maricopa-county-attorneys-office|Maricopa County Attorney's Office]] treated Door 3 handling as one of three principal areas of concern. Wright wrote that "Maricopa County appears to have failed to adhere to the statutory guidelines in segregating, counting, tabulating, tallying, and transporting the 'Door 3' ballots," and that "Maricopa County has admitted that, in some voting locations, 'Door 3' non-tabulated ballots were commingled with tabulated ballots at the voting location." She further reported "a sworn complaint from an election observer indicating that more than 1700 'Door 3' non-tabulated ballots from one voting location were placed in black duffle bags that were intended to be used for tabulated ballots."

Because commingling defeats the segregation on which the statutory close-out and reconciliation duties of election boards depend, Wright framed the issue as bearing directly on whether the county could lawfully certify its results in the canvass due to the [[arizona-secretary-of-state|Secretary of State]] on or before November 28, 2022.
