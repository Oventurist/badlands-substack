# Too big to fail

"Too big to fail" is the rhetoric that arose around large American banks whose collapse was considered unaffordable for the country, and which Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] identifies as one cause of the risk-taking that produced the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]].[1]

In Carlson's account, big banks understood to a certain extent that the country could not afford for them to fail, and knew they would be bailed out by the government if they got into serious trouble.[1] He argues, however, that the deeper problem was psychological rather than structural: bankers became disillusioned and came to believe they could do no wrong and that there was no need for caution, since caution merely ate away at profits.[1]

This attitude compounded the effects of predictive risk software. Because systems such as [[blackrock|BlackRock]]'s [[aladdin|Aladdin]] — built on [[monte-carlo-simulation|Monte Carlo simulation]] — made the financial system appear more predictable, investment banking firms took on more risk with confidence, and were left holding those portfolios when markets collapsed in 2008.[1]
