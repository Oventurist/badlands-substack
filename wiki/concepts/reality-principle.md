# Reality Principle (CTMU)

The **reality principle** is the starting axiom of [[chris-langan|Chris Langan]]'s [[cognitive-theoretic-model-of-the-universe|Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe]] as presented by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] in Part 1 of [[the-white-pill-emporium|The White Pill Emporium]] (22 October 2024): reality contains everything that is real, excluding nothing.[1] "Real," in this context, is defined as anything empirically or theoretically capable of influencing the contents of reality in any way — physical objects, but also metaphysical concepts and cognitive operations.[1] If something is real enough to be identifiable in any way whatsoever, the author writes, it has its place in reality somehow.[1]

## Consequence for parallel-universe theories

The principle is used to argue that "parallel universe" theories require tweaking, since anything that influences reality is by definition contained within reality — thoughts, dreams and emotions plainly affect a person and are therefore real, if not in a traditional physical sense.[1] The notion that electrons blip in and out of other universes implies a bridge between those realities, which the author holds instantaneously renders them one and the same universe.[1] The very concept that parallel realities can interact negates their separateness; at best they could be conceived as different rooms in one gigantic house.[1]

## Reality theory as a search for a singular answer

Accordingly the author states that a main purpose of reality theory is to reduce the answer to "what is reality?" to a single thing or process, since saying reality amounts to a collection of sub-realities is a circular definition that explains nothing.[1] His own advertised answer — offered as a spoiler ahead of the argument — is that reality is the self-simulation of God, in which all lifeforms partake and are granted a level of agency appropriate to their development.[1] The [[syntactic-metaverse|syntactic metaverse]] is introduced immediately afterwards as the CTMU feature that carries this structure.[1]

## Related two-valued logic argument

The exposition of the principle follows an argument from two-valued logic and ontological boundaries: a person cannot simultaneously observe a table and its absence, nor read a sentence while not reading it, and such boundaries are described as limits reality imposes on its own content to maintain intelligibility.[1] The author extends the same logic to the existence of God, arguing that reality either is or is not the creation of a single creator with no room for fuzzy logic, and concludes that God's existence is logically demonstrable and His memory perfect — meaning, in his words, that no person will ever be forgotten.[1] He further argues that because the universe cannot reach outside itself to fetch materials, it is inescapably self-created.[1]
