# Deepfake

A deep fake is synthetic audio, image, or video generated by [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] that convincingly depicts a real person saying or doing something they never said or did.[1]

Badlands Media's July 2023 treatment of AI identifies the collapse of the evidentiary value of recordings as one of the core mechanisms of [[reality-collapse|reality collapse]]. Three seconds of audio is all it takes for AI to simulate a person's voice and create a deep fake of them saying anything the operator wants; a still photo or a brief video clip is all that is required to deep fake the face delivering those words.[1] The article emphasizes the triviality of the input requirement — "Just three seconds."[1]

The significance of this capability in the Badlands framing is not that machines have become malevolent but that the tool is in the hands of men: most AI researchers dismiss the sentient-machine apocalypse and instead fear those wielding the machine's power.[1] Combined with AI's capacity to summarize and publish authoritative-sounding insight faster than any human can read, and with anticipated fMRI-based dream mapping, synthetic media contributes to a condition in which the historical record can be falsified continuously, echoing the passage from George Orwell's *1984* quoted in the article.[1]
