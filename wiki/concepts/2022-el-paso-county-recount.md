# 2022 El Paso County Recount

The 2022 El Paso County recount was a candidate-requested machine recount of results from Colorado's June 2022 Republican primary, conducted in [[el-paso-county|El Paso County]] and covered on site by Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].[1]

## Origins

Problems in the primary were significant enough that four candidates paid what Ashe in America described as extortion-level fees to obtain machine recounts.[1] [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], the Secretary of State candidate, was one of the four, and because her race was statewide her recount took place in all 64 Colorado counties.[1] The other three were El Paso County candidates, though jurisdiction over their recounts was split: recounts for County Clerk and Recorder candidate Peter Lupia and Coroner candidate Dr. Rae Ann Weber were overseen by El Paso County Clerk and Recorder Chuck Broerman, while recounts for State Senate candidate Lynda Zamora Wilson, along with Peters, were overseen by Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]].[1]

## Logic and Accuracy Testing

The El Paso County recount, which encompassed all four races, was described as a disaster from the start.[1] Logic and Accuracy Testing ahead of the recount produced a ballot adjudication rate of roughly 57%, with approximately 2,260 of 4,000 test ballots sent to adjudication.[1] Officials stated the high rejection rate was expected and had declared as much before publicly pronouncing the test a success.[1] Witnesses in the room reported the opposite: that the error rate was not expected, and that officials scrambled to summon additional judges and watchers to handle the volume of rejected ballots, each of which had to be adjudicated for every machine under test.[1]

## Alleged alteration of original records

After fixing nothing but declaring the test a success, officials proceeded with the recount.[1] Ashe in America attended part of it and reported witnessing, and capturing on video, an election worker modifying original batch totals from the primary election to match the totals produced by the recount.[1] Drawing on more than a decade working for an SEC audit firm, she characterized the alteration of original count documentation to match a secondary count intended to verify that documentation as a massive red flag resembling a cover-up.[1] The incident was recorded and reported, and, she wrote, nothing happened — the observers were simply ignored.[1]

Observers also documented ballot boxes with broken seals.[1] That issue was likewise ignored, though officials replaced the box lid and resealed it while acting bothered and rolling their eyes.[1] The recount was ultimately declared a success.[1]

## Outcome

The candidates who had petitioned onto the ballot rather than face Republican voters at the [[2022-colorado-republican-state-assembly|state assembly]] won their primaries, and subsequently lost the general election.[1] Ashe in America presented the episode as evidence for her broader argument that Colorado functions as an "election fraud test kitchen" whose methods spread to other states.[1]
