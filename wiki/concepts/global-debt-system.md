# Global Central Bank Debt System

The "global central bank debt system" is the term used in Badlands Media commentary for the network of national central banks, reserve-currency arrangements and sovereign borrowing that the author [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues has functioned as an instrument of control over governments and populations.[1]

## The thesis

In the January 2023 essay "Master and Commander," Lange writes that the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]] completely controlled the entire global financial system for many generations, and created the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] to be the most powerful central bank on the planet in order to control every government in every country.[1] By controlling the money supply of every nation, he argues, they made the world's population debt slaves, since every government borrowed from Rothschild-controlled central banks to finance everything up to and including wars — the most profitable venture of all.[1]

## Historical claims

The essay traces the system to the Napoleonic Wars, alleging manipulation of English pound sterling bonds through a panic sell and buyback that delivered control of the Bank of England while burdening the English Empire with massive debt.[1] It further claims both sides of the First and Second World Wars were financed by the same interests before the US dollar was established as the global reserve currency under the [[bretton-woods-agreement|Bretton Woods Agreement]].[1] The abandonment of the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] in 1971 (see [[nixon-shock|1971 abandonment of the gold standard]]) is presented as the point at which the reserve currency became a [[fiat-currency|fiat currency]] backed by nothing and control over money printing and debt issuance became total.[1]

## Debt saturation

Lange states that the world is drowning in roughly three hundred trillion dollars of debt, none of which can be paid back, which is why he believes "the enemy" is desperate for a global reset that would allow confiscation of assets.[1] US debt alone is described as approaching 32 trillion dollars and growing.[1] Because borrowing cannot continue forever on minimal interest payments, he argues, default was always inevitable; the only open question is who goes bankrupt — the United States, as Germany did after World War I and Venezuela more recently, or the global central bank debt system itself.[1]

## Signs of collapse

The essay cites the 2022 losses of the [[riksbank|Swedish Riksbank]] and the record loss at the [[swiss-national-bank|Swiss National Bank]] as cracks in the system, both driven by globally rising market interest rates eroding the market value of central bank assets.[1] It notes that three central banks had recently required emergency bailouts because a strengthening dollar undermined their currencies, and that central banks and countries — especially the [[brics|BRICS]] alliance — were buying record amounts of gold in response.[1] The dollar, in this reading, is the debt system's Achilles heel, and the $65 trillion of hidden dollar debt in [[foreign-exchange-swaps|foreign exchange swaps]] is its largest unexploded charge.[1]

## Drivers of dollar strength

The essay attributes recent dollar strength to three factors: rapid rate rises and balance-sheet tightening by the Federal Reserve making dollars a scarcer, yield-bearing asset; rising yields on savings accounts and fixed-income securities such as US Treasuries drawing investors out of falling equity markets, especially technology; and the comparative resilience of the US economy against a Europe facing an energy crunch amid the Russia-Ukraine conflict and a China whose zero-COVID policies hampered manufacturing.[1]

## Oil prices and the brink of collapse (November 2022)

Joe Lange's November 2022 essay "Money and Oil" argues that the entire global debt system stands on the brink of collapse because the dollar is strengthening while every other world currency weakens.[2] He notes that three central banks — those of the United Kingdom, [[japan|Japan]] and Switzerland — had recently required [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] bailouts for this reason, and that the whole world was pressuring the Fed to stop raising rates because oil must be purchased in U.S. dollars.[2]

The exchange rate, in this account, is what bankrupts commercial banks: they lack sufficient dollars on hand and must convert weakening local currencies into stronger dollars, so they need the Fed to print money and kill the dollar to make [[oil|oil]] cheaper.[2] Lange reads a Q post as referring not to the Federal Reserve but to a "Federal Exchange Department" for commercial banks, concluding that the [[cabal|cabal]] controls the world's money supply through exchange rates for the dollars needed to buy oil, and that the Fed's strong dollar is dismantling that mechanism.[2] Because the world is at the end of the debt cycle and drowning in debt, he argues, inflationary oil prices — with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] cutting output rather than raising it — will be what pushes the system over the edge.[2]
