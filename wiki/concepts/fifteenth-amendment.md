# Fifteenth Amendment

The Fifteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution prohibits the denial of the right to vote on the basis of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.[1] It was formally ratified on April 1, 1870, exactly five years after the Union victory at the Battle of Five Forks marked the beginning of the end of the Civil War.[1]
