# Fifteenth Amendment

The Fifteenth Amendment to the [[united-states-constitution|United States Constitution]] prohibits denial of the right to vote based on race, color, or previous condition of servitude. It was formally ratified on April 1, 1870 — a date Badlands commentary links to both the end of the Civil War and the 2026 birthright citizenship argument at the Supreme Court.

## Ratification date and Civil War anniversary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] notes in the Badlands Brief of April 2, 2026 that April 1, 1865 marked the beginning of the end of the Civil War with the Union victory at the Battle of Five Forks, and that exactly five years later, on April 1, 1870, the United States formally ratified the Fifteenth Amendment. Exactly 156 years after that ratification, on April 1, 2026, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] heard oral arguments in [[trump-v-barbara|Trump v. Barbara]], the challenge to the administration's effort to limit birthright citizenship under the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].

## Connection to the 2026 election orders

The same Brief reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued a new executive order on elections — [[ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections|"Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections"]] — on the Tuesday before the article's publication, extending the administration's citizenship-verification agenda into federal election administration. The commentary presents the Fifteenth Amendment's history as part of the same Reconstruction settlement being relitigated in the current disputes over citizenship and voting.
