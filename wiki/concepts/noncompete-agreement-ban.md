# FTC Noncompete Agreement Ban (2024)

The FTC noncompete ban was a rule adopted by the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]] on April 23, 2024, by a narrow vote, prohibiting nearly all noncompete agreements across American employment.[1] Noncompetes are contractual clauses that typically prevent workers from joining competing businesses or launching businesses of their own after leaving an employer.[1]

## Rulemaking

The Commission received more than 26,000 public comments in the months leading up to the vote, an unusually large response reflecting the breadth of workers covered by such clauses.[1] Chair [[lina-khan|Lina Khan]] cited worker testimony describing employees trapped in abusive workplaces, including one who could not leave after an employer merged with an organization whose religious principles conflicted with the worker's own.[1] Khan argued that stripping people of economic liberty also strips them of other freedoms.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

The rule was announced in the same week as the Department of Transportation's automatic airline refund requirement, and Badlands commentary grouped the two as rare wins for the Biden administration in the public mind.[1] Writers acknowledged the substance — agreeing that noncompetes should be rendered unenforceable — while warning that the pairing amounted to a tacit endorsement of administrative agency authority over private enterprise and the free market, a Trojan Horse embedded in otherwise popular consumer- and worker-friendly measures.[1]
