# 2025 St. Paul Cyberattack

The 2025 St. Paul cyberattack was a breach of the city government of Saint Paul, Minnesota, detected on 25 July 2025, after which hackers posted stolen city data online and the city undertook a mass in-person password reset for its workforce.[1]

## Response

For a second consecutive day in August 2025, a steady stream of St. Paul employees passed through Roy Wilkins Auditorium, which had been rapidly converted into a password reset center.[1] Eighty laptops were arranged in two rows, accommodating eighty people every thirty minutes; of 3,500 employees, more than 2,000 had passed through by late Monday, using a single clearinghouse for security and efficiency.[1] Deputy Chief Information Officer Mary Gleigh-Matthews said the recommendation from all the cybersecurity experts involved was that a physical, in-person reset was the most secure option.[1]

Upon detecting the breach on 25 July the city immediately shut off systems and backed up its data to prevent further damage.[1] The FBI, the Department of Homeland Security and the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] all worked with the city to scour its systems and bring them back online.[1]
