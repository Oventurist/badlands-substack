# 2025 Trump Address to a Joint Session of Congress

On the evening of Tuesday, March 4, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] delivered an address to a joint session of Congress at a contentious moment in his second term, days after enacting sweeping 25 percent tariffs on Mexico and Canada.[1]

## Content of the address

Trump said he was "fighting every day" to lower prices for consumers despite the onset of a trade war, and promised to tariff other countries at whatever rate they tariff the United States.[1] He pledged to expand energy production and took a victory lap over dramatically lowered illegal border crossings and plans to carry out the largest deportation operation in US history.[1] He said he was working "tirelessly" to end the war in Ukraine and read part of a letter from Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] stating that Ukraine was ready to come to the negotiating table as soon as possible to bring lasting peace closer, and was ready to sign a minerals deal as part of the peace process.[1]

Trump also proposed getting rid of the [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS Act]] and using the savings to reduce the deficit, arguing the law had been a giveaway that actually harmed the US chip manufacturing industry.[1]

## Made-for-TV moments and the terror arrest

The speech included staged emotional moments, notably the naming of 13-year-old cancer survivor Devarjaye "DJ" Daniel as an honorary US Secret Service agent, who embraced Secret Service Director [[sean-curran|Sean Curran]].[1] Trump also announced the capture of one of the terrorists who masterminded the [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate bombing]] in Afghanistan, which killed 13 US servicemembers.[1]

## Democratic protests

Democrats protested throughout, holding up signs and jeering; Representative [[al-green|Al Green]] was removed by the Sergeant at Arms and others walked out.[1] Badlands commentary described the Democrats as bringing shirts, signs and other props while refusing to applaud regular Americans honored in the gallery.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Burning Bright wrote that watching opponents react to the prospect of peace, truth and prosperity "like vampires to holy water" was sobering, and probably the point of the exercise.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] counted 95 standing ovations and contrasted the speech with the fictional politics of the television show *Scandal*, noting that in 44 days in office Trump had reshaped immigration, education, foreign policy, trade policy, economic policy, innovation policy and health policy, and had signed the Laken Riley Act into law on day nine — evidence, she argued, that governmental gridlock had been intentional.[1]
