# Hillary Clinton private email server

The private email server used by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] while she served as Secretary of State is treated in the Badlands Media article "The Biggest Boomerang In History" as the opening that exposed the wider [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration's concealed communications and, ultimately, as the instrument of Clinton's own defeat.[1]

## Discovery and subpoena

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] writes that [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] discovered in 2015 that Clinton had been using a private server to avoid the [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]] as Secretary of State, and that the [[state-department|State Department]] therefore did not hold her emails — they resided on a server she fully controlled.[1] Her emails were placed under congressional subpoena; she initially refused to hand them over, then had aides and lawyers sort them and produced roughly half, claiming the remainder were personal and largely concerned her daughter Chelsea's wedding.[1] About 33,000 subpoenaed emails were deleted, which Lange characterises as obstruction of justice through destruction of evidence.[1]

The article adds that the deletions were carried out using the file-erasure tool BleachBit and were accompanied by the physical destruction of her BlackBerry phones; Lange notes the BlackBerry's built-in VPN made it the most secure phone of its era for anyone wishing to keep secrets.[1] He treats these as two obstruction-of-justice acts Clinton effectively admitted to.[1]

## The FBI investigation

The [[fbi|FBI]] was forced to investigate because the server carried classified information — in Lange's telling, far more than [[james-comey|James Comey]] would concede — and because Congress and the Inspector General were demanding it.[1] He argues Clinton used the server not only to conceal the Benghazi affair but also the truth about the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] deal and the spying operation against the Trump campaign.[1] Comey, Lange contends, was never going to prosecute her: doing so would have exposed that Obama and many of his officials used the same arrangements, and Clinton "owned" Comey through the [[pay-for-play|pay to play]] network.[1]

Comey took no action over the deletions or the smashed phones, instead confining the investigation to whether classified emails were on the server and how they were handled, with the mainstream media sustaining that focus while ignoring obstruction charges.[1] Q drop 4845 of October 9, 2020, quoted in the article, links Clinton's classified [C] markings to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] and a long list of subjects including Soros, Africa, Saudi Arabia, Special Access Programs, China, Russia, Uranium One, Benghazi, the Muslim Brotherhood and CIA assets, and states that "nothing is ever TRULY DELETED."[1]

## Congressional findings

Reporting by [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] in The Hill, cited at length, states that congressional investigators secured written evidence that the FBI believed laws had been broken, that the "sheer volume" of classified information flowing through the insecure server was itself proof of criminality, and that a key witness had made false statements.[1] That witness — an employee of the computer firm maintaining the server — belatedly admitted permanently erasing an archive of her messages in 2015 after they had been subpoenaed by Congress; Lange believes this witness has flipped and is cooperating with the [[racketeer-influenced-and-corrupt-organizations-act|RICO]] investigation.[1] Investigators also confirmed the FBI began drafting an exoneration statement while subpoenaed evidence was outstanding and before more than a dozen key witnesses, including Clinton, had been interviewed.[1]

[[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] told a December 21 closed-door House Judiciary Committee briefing that the investigation and charging decisions were controlled by a small group at Washington headquarters rather than by the New York field office where the server was located — a arrangement known in bureau parlance as a "special."[1] Lange presents this as the reason FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] is decentralising power away from headquarters and returning employees to field offices.[1]

## The reopening and the election

Comey was forced into the spotlight to exonerate Clinton in July 2016 after the [[clinton-lynch-tarmac-meeting|tarmac meeting]] story broke that June.[1] Afterwards, further Clinton emails were discovered on [[anthony-weiner|Anthony Weiner]]'s laptop in October 2016 — emails Lange attributes to [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]]'s access — and police involved in the Weiner investigation threatened to go public if the FBI did not reopen a criminal investigation.[1] Comey reopened the case on October 26, 2016, which Lange calls the biggest [[october-surprise|October Surprise]] in history and the cause of Clinton's defeat.[1] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
