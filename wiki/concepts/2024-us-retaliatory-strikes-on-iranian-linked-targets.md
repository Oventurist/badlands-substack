# 2024 U.S. Retaliatory Strikes on Iranian-Linked Targets in Iraq and Syria

In late January and early February 2024, U.S. officials confirmed that plans had been approved for a series of strikes to be carried out over a number of days against targets inside Iraq and Syria, including Iranian personnel and facilities.[1] The campaign was framed as a response to sustained drone and rocket attacks on U.S. forces in the region, above all the [[tower-22-drone-attack|drone attack]] on a base inside Jordan near the Syrian border that killed three U.S. service members.[1]

## Announcement and rationale

Speaking at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] on 1 February 2024, Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] told reporters that the United States would not tolerate attacks on American troops.[1] "This is a dangerous moment in the Middle East," Austin said, noting that Israel's ongoing war against [[hamas|Hamas]] in the Gaza Strip and attacks by [[houthis|Houthi]] forces in Yemen on commercial shipping in the Red Sea were also unfolding in the region.[1] He added that Washington would "continue to work to avoid a wider conflict in the region," while taking "all necessary actions to defend the United States, our interests and our people," responding "when we choose, where we choose and how we choose."[1]

U.S. officials told CBS News that weather would be a major factor in the timing of the strikes: while the military retained the capability to strike in poor conditions, it preferred better visibility of selected targets as a safeguard against inadvertently hitting civilians who might stray into the area at the last moment.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that what would be a defining moment in most presidencies was instead being treated as the latest in a long line of blunders by the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], criticizing the decision to telegraph the strikes publicly before carrying them out as a major escalation.[1] Aligning himself with [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], he expressed doubt that Biden meaningfully controlled the U.S. military, suggesting that any actual operations on the ground might better be described as strikes on "Deep State proxies" rather than Iranian ones — a pattern he compared to the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] in Ukraine and to Hamas, and one he speculated might later be repeated in Taiwan.[1] He conceded this was guesswork, arguing that the operative element was the narrative being presented to the public: that an aging president in the [[white-house|White House]] was stumbling unimpeded into a great war.[1]
