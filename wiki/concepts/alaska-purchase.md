# Alaska Purchase

The Alaska Purchase was the 1867 transfer of Russia's North American possessions to the United States for $7.2 million.[1] Matthew Ehret treats it not as a commercial curiosity but as the culmination of a deliberate Russian-American strategic alignment formed in reaction to British imperial policy.[1]

## Origins

Ehret traces the logic back to 1853, when Russia was being drawn into the [[crimean-war|Crimean War]] and the United States toward its own civil war.[1] In that year [[nikolai-muraviev|General Nikolai Muraviev]] wrote to the Czar that "due to the present amazing development of railroads, the United States will soon spread over all North America," and that Russia would have to cede its North American possessions.[1] [[grand-duke-constantine|Grand Duke Constantine]] made the same argument to Ambassador [[eduard-de-stoeckl|Eduard de Stoeckl]] before the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] began.[1]

## Consummation

In 1866 Stoeckl was recalled to Russia and, after a lengthy meeting with Czar [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]], received approval to initiate the sale.[1] On the evening of 29 March 1867 he delivered the news to Secretary of State [[william-seward|William Seward]] at Seward's private residence; Seward asked "why wait until tomorrow what could be done that very evening?"[1] At midnight the State Department was opened for a small group including Senator [[charles-sumner|Charles Sumner]], Seward, Stoeckl and trusted members of the Russian consulate.[1] By sunrise on 30 March the treaty was written and finalized, and before the ink was dry it was presented to a shocked Congress, which passed it in the following weeks.[1]

## Consequences

The purchase made the isolated colony of [[british-columbia|British Columbia]] suddenly valuable, since American territory now flanked it to the north and south.[1] Ehret connects the sale to the parallel project of connecting the continents by rail and telegraph across the Bering Strait — a vision advanced by [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]] in his December 1864 message to Congress and later by [[william-gilpin|Governor William Gilpin]] in his 1890 [[cosmopolitan-railway|Cosmopolitan Railway]].[1] British strategy, in this reading, responded by locking British Columbia into the new Canadian federation.[1]

## Symbolism at the 2025 Alaska summit

In August 2025 [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argued that the choice of Alaska as the venue for the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin meeting]] carried major symbolic value for those who know their history, since Russia's 1867 sale of Alaska to the USA "had everything to do with a special US-Russia relationship that saved the Union during the Civil War."[2] He described the same period as one that nearly saw America's new transcontinental railway extended across [[british-columbia|British Columbia]], Alaska and onward to the new [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]], a trajectory he links to the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] corridor first proposed by [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] in 1864.[2]
