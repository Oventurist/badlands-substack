# Volyn Massacre

The Volyn massacre was the mass killing of Poles by Ukrainian nationalist militants during the Second World War, and it remains a long-standing flashpoint in Polish-Ukrainian bilateral relations.[1]

## Events

Militants from the [[ukrainian-insurgent-army|Ukrainian Insurgent Army]] (UPA) and the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN) slaughtered up to 100,000 Poles between 1943 and 1945 in the regions of Volhynia and Eastern Galicia, territories that were later incorporated into Ukraine.[1] Both the UPA and the OUN collaborated with Nazi Germany during the war.[1]

## Contemporary memory politics

Polish President [[andrzej-duda|Andrzej Duda]] told the outlet RMF24 that Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]] had claimed to have no knowledge of the atrocities until Duda confronted him about them.[1] According to Duda, Zelensky said: "Andrzej, I've never heard of the murders, the killing of Poles in western Ukraine, in Volhynia. They didn't teach us about it in school."[1] Duda argued the exchange demonstrated that Ukrainians are broadly kept in the dark about their nation's troubled past, and that assuming knowledge of Ukraine's "difficult history" is common among them is simply wrong.[1]

Many historic ultranationalist leaders, including OUN head [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]], a notorious Nazi collaborator, remain widely revered in modern Ukraine.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Bandera is one of the more important and least understood figures of the Second World War, contending that his propensity for reckless violence — the Volyn massacre being the signal example — alienated him from the Third Reich while drawing him into the good graces of the [[cia|CIA]], which recruited him as a top operative in the postwar regime-change program known as [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[1] In that reading, the current geopolitical confrontation is a continuation of unresolved conflicts left over from the First and Second World Wars, as well as the [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik]] and American revolutions.[1]
