# Fourth Industrial Revolution

The Fourth Industrial Revolution is the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] framing for a coming fusion of artificial intelligence, digital identity, biotechnology and networked infrastructure. In Truth Community analysis it is read as the technical delivery mechanism for technocratic governance rather than a neutral technological trend.[1]

## Schwab on mastering the technologies

In February 2023, WEF founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] discussed AI, chatbots and digital identities and stated that "who masters those technologies — in some way — will be the master of the world," a clip circulated widely on social media and reported by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that the use of AI to run society on behalf of globalists is, as WEF-aligned thinkers such as [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] state, a boon for despotic elites.[1] He described a historical method of population control with three components: dumbing down the population; making life hard so that time and energy are consumed and leisure for self-education is reduced; and supplying media-backed experts to whom people willingly surrender their sovereign rights of self-governorship.[1] In his view this WEF model is the technocratic dream of 1960s and 1970s globalists, and the Fourth Industrial Revolution takes it to the next level by casting AI as an incorruptible, unbiased "uber expert" that is fair, precise, efficient and abundant.[1] Deschamps read Schwab's remark as "open, naked disclosure" that the technologies are servants of masters, warning that inaction leaves the door open for total control through technocracy.[1]

## The "new priests" of the Fourth Industrial Revolution

Badlands contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes the promoters of the Fourth Industrial Revolution as "new priests" preaching a gospel derived from Marshall McLuhan, [[julian-huxley|Sir Julian Huxley]], cybernetics founder [[norbert-wiener|Norbert Wiener]], the Jesuit transhumanist [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] and Chardin's intellectual heir [[ray-kurzweil|Ray Kurzweil]].[2] Their creed, in his account, holds that technology grows with a life all its own as a deterministic synthetic *élan vital*, that mind is nothing but the atoms of the brain, and that humans are "hackable animals" destined for irrelevance.[2]

[[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] is presented as the movement's leading spokesman, promising happiness, justice and even eternal life through technology rather than through a supernatural being, while proposing drugs and computer games for the resulting [[useless-eaters|useless class]].[2] Ehret treats this as a [[transhumanism|transhumanist religion]] descending from the [[cybernetics|cybernetics]] revolution of the 1960s and institutionalized at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[2]

## Worldcoin and biometric identity

The Badlands Media article "Dark Equation" invoked the fourth industrial revolution as one half of the Davos rhetoric it accused [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]]'s [[worldcoin|Worldcoin]] project of repackaging, alongside the [[great-reset|Great Reset]].[3] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the "globalist Davos crowd" had been consistently open about wanting AI-powered biometric digital identities, digital currencies and the transhuman integration of the Internet-of-Things into human bodies — ideas that seem far-fetched but are advanced by people with the power and connections to realize them.[3]
