# 2024 Trump Tariff Threat

The 2024 Trump tariff threat was President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement, made on the night of Monday 25 November 2024, that he intended to impose steep tariffs on all exports from [[canada|Canada]], [[mexico|Mexico]] and [[china|China]] to the United States until those countries reduced migration and the flow of drugs into the country.[1]

## Reaction

Trade experts and economists said the threat set the stage for a bitter global trade war, warning consumers and companies to brace for steep costs.[1] Keith Rockwell, a former director at the [[world-trade-organization|World Trade Organization]], predicted the move could spark a trade war, noting that "the United States exports hundreds of billions of dollars worth of goods to these countries" and that "anyone who expects that they will stand pat and not retaliate has not been paying attention."[1]

China responded that both sides would lose from escalating economic tensions, with Liu Pengyu, a spokesperson at the Chinese embassy in Washington, writing on X that "no one will win a trade war or a tariff war."[1] Canada's deputy prime minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] and public safety minister [[dominic-leblanc|Dominic LeBlanc]] touted their country's "balanced and mutually beneficial" economic ties with the United States.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the episode in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as "Trump's Tariff [[game-theory|Game Theory]]" kicking off in earnest before the second administration had even taken office, blurring the line between actual and narrative deployments of power.[1] In his framing, Trump first threatens tariffs and attaches the threat to a deal, as he did throughout his business career; the leaders of Canada and Mexico respond with bluster and bravado; and their currencies then flash crash relative to the U.S. dollar.[1]

Burning Bright stressed that the currencies were responding to narrative rather than actual policy, because "the money knows who wins a trade war between the most powerful economic empire in world history and, well, literally anyone else" — an open secret he argued the globalist hegemon wanted to keep from the American people and from its own.[1] Observing what he called capitulation already underway, he concluded that Trump might not even need tariffs to realize their net effects.[1]
