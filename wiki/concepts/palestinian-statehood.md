# Palestinian Statehood

Palestinian statehood — the establishment of a sovereign Palestinian state — is the central unresolved condition in Middle East diplomacy and, as of late 2025, the principal obstacle to Israeli–Saudi normalization.[1]

## Netanyahu's rejection (November 2025)

In an interview aired on the evening of Thursday, November 20, 2025 with the Telegram channel Abu Ali Express, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] insisted there would be no Palestinian state, even at the cost of normalization with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[1] "There will not be a Palestinian state. It's very simple: it will not be established," he said.[1] Asked whether his opposition held even if it jeopardized normalization with Riyadh — which insists on a credible pathway to statehood in exchange for ties — Netanyahu answered: "The answer is: a Palestinian state will not be established. It is an existential threat to Israel."[1] Asked what had prevented normalization with the Saudis, he said the war in Gaza had strained progress but that "the conditions could develop" now that the war was winding down.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu had spent years leveraging what Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] wanted — [[saudi-f-35-deal|F-35s]], a nuclear deal and a defense agreement — to force capitulation on normalization without Palestinian statehood, and that the Saudis nonetheless secured their objectives without conceding the point.[1] He concluded that the Arabs are not the ones seeking conflict or subverting the peace process, and framed [[israel|Israel]] itself as the outstanding liability blocking a [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]].[1]

## French recognition (July 2025)

On July 24, 2025, French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] announced that France would recognize Palestine as a state in pursuit of what he called a "historic commitment to a just and lasting peace" in the region, saying in a surprise statement on X that he would make the formal announcement at the United Nations General Assembly that September in New York.[2] "The urgent need today is to end the war in Gaza and to rescue the civilian population," he added.[2]

Badlands commentary observed that roughly 140 countries already formally recognized Palestine, making the move appear minor at first glance, but argued its significance lay in French history: France and Britain carved up the Middle East and Africa over a century ago, and while Palestine was a British protectorate, France has played a key role in the region's security architecture.[2] On that reading, Macron's move — judged unlikely to be altruistic in motive — signaled that the geopolitical calculus of the West was rapidly changing and that Israel was no longer the priority it once was, raising the question of how soon the United States and United Kingdom would follow.[2]

## May 2024 General Assembly vote

On May 10, 2024, the United Nations General Assembly voted by a wide margin to grant new "rights and privileges" to Palestine and to call on the [[united-nations-security-council|Security Council]] to reconsider Palestine's request to become the 194th member state of the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[3] The Arab and Palestinian-sponsored resolution passed 143-9 with 25 abstentions; the United States voted against it alongside Israel, Argentina, Czechia, Hungary, Micronesia, Nauru, Palau and Papua New Guinea.[3]

The vote reflected broad global support for full Palestinian membership, with many countries citing the escalating death toll in Gaza and fears of a major Israeli offensive in Rafah, where roughly 1.3 million Palestinians had sought refuge.[3] It also marked growth in support relative to the General Assembly's October 27, 2023 humanitarian ceasefire resolution, which passed 120-14 with 45 abstentions weeks after Israel launched its offensive in response to the Hamas attack of October 7, which killed 1,200 people.[3]

## Israeli reaction

Israeli finance minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] warned settlement mayors that the establishment of a Palestinian state had become a "tangible danger" driven by "international involvement" and the "consent of leftist elements" inside Israel, and pressed Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to convene a body able to approve new West Bank construction.[3] [[israel-ganz|Israel Ganz]] of the [[yesha-council|Yesha Council]] and mayors of the largest settlements attended that meeting.[3]

## September 2025 wave of recognitions

On Monday, September 22, 2025, leaders from across the globe convened in New York to support Palestinian statehood over the opposition of Israel and the United States, as the Gaza Strip neared the two-year mark of a war that had brought mass death, destruction and hunger.[4] A summit organized by France and Saudi Arabia ahead of the annual [[united-nations-general-assembly|United Nations General Assembly]] was portrayed as an urgent effort to salvage the long-deferred vision of a two-state solution, though with Israel continuing its offensive in Gaza City against [[hamas|Hamas]] and rapidly expanding West Bank settlements, the notion seemed more distant than ever.[4]

French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] told the meeting, "We must pave the way for peace… Today, France recognizes the state of Palestine," drawing applause and a standing ovation from the Palestinian delegation, and noted recent statements of recognition — some not yet formalized — by Britain, Canada, Australia, Portugal, Belgium and several others.[4] Israel's ambassador to the United Nations, [[danny-danon|Danny Danon]], said "We will take action" in response, declining to specify whether that meant expanded settlements or outright annexations, and said Israel's response would be announced by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[4]

[[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], leader of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], said the authority would form the government of the West Bank and Gaza with no role for Hamas; denied a US visa to attend, he addressed the meeting by video and told the people of Israel, "Our future and yours depends on peace. Enough war."[4] Badlands contributor Jon Herold asked what happens when the entire world recognizes the state of Palestine other than the one country trying to erase it from existence, while GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that country by country the world is adopting a supporting posture toward Palestine and warned that Israel, as a "cornered animal," is at its most dangerous.[4]
