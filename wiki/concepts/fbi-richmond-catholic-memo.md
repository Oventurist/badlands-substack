# FBI Richmond Catholic Memo

The FBI Richmond Catholic memo was an intelligence product issued by the [[fbi|FBI]]'s Richmond field office suggesting that some Roman Catholics be viewed as radical extremist threats and urging agents to infiltrate certain groups within the world's largest Christian faith.[1] The Bureau retracted the document on Thursday, February 9, 2023, after it became public.[1]

## Significance

The episode was described as the latest to sully the FBI's reputation, raising fresh concerns about political bias and civil liberties.[1] It came a little over a year after an earlier bungled effort that sought to treat parents protesting at school board meetings as domestic terrorism threats.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the FBI had already been functioning as "an arm of the globalist culture police," acting as the authority that decides who qualifies as a domestic terrorist.[1] He noted that traditional Catholics who wish to hear the Mass in Latin were painted as white supremacists, and that no clear methodology was offered for how the Bureau reached that determination — an omission he called revealing.[1]

Deschamps framed the memo as an instance of "cult-like social weaponization tactics," relying on the tendency of people to defer to authorities within a social group who tell them what to think and how to act.[1] Absent critical evaluation, he argued, people go along out of precaution to avoid backlash, which is precisely what he said the cabal counts on.[1] He concluded that such tactics work only on those who do not think critically, underscoring the importance of speaking truth openly, "because lies naturally crumble in the minds of those who are exposed to the truth."[1]
