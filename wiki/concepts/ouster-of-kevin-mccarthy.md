# Ouster of Kevin McCarthy

The ouster of Kevin McCarthy was the removal of the Speaker of the House of Representatives by floor vote on Tuesday, October 3, 2023 — the first successful removal of a sitting Speaker in United States history. The vote was forced by a contingent of hard-right conservatives and threw the House and its Republican leadership into chaos.[1]

## The vote

[[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] was voted out of the speakership in what the Associated Press described as an extraordinary showdown. The motion was engineered by right-flank Republicans, led publicly by [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]], and carried when House Democrats piled on rather than rescue him. Neither faction appeared open to negotiating a reprieve.[1]

McCarthy, who had said repeatedly that he never gives up, found himself with almost no options remaining, and told lawmakers that evening that he would not run again for Speaker, putting the gavel up for grabs.[1] At a press conference at the Capitol he alternated between an upbeat assessment of his speakership and angry score-settling against those who removed him: "I may have lost this vote today, but as I walk out of this chamber I feel fortunate to have served."[1]

The removal was recorded as a 216-to-210 vote on Tuesday evening, October 3, 2023.[2] Hardline Republicans were angered by McCarthy's willingness to fund Ukraine's war while arguing that the money could have been better spent to protect the southern border and restore law and order in imploding major US cities.[2]

## Market and funding consequences

The historic ouster weighed on defense stocks as traders anticipated that a new Speaker would face greater difficulty securing further funding for Ukraine.[2] [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] reported that "the conservative revolt that ousted McCarthy has left the chamber in a state of paralysis until a new speaker is found," raising the chances of a US government shutdown the following month and a delay in further Ukraine assistance.[2] In a note to clients, [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] analyst Alec Phillips wrote that the leadership change raised the odds of a November shutdown, that a $120 billion gap separated the parties on preferred FY2024 spending levels, and that it was difficult to see how [[congress|Congress]] could pass the twelve necessary full-year spending bills before funding expired on November 17.[2] Phillips added that the next Speaker was likely to be under even more pressure than McCarthy to avoid passing another temporary extension or additional funding for Ukraine.[2] On Wednesday morning, European defense stocks including Rheinmetall, Saab, BAE Systems, and Leonardo slid in the cash market, which Bloomberg attributed to the ouster.[2]

Burning Bright welcomed the outcome, arguing that the era of "easy money" was ending not only for large banks amid what he called a stagflationary spiral in the fiat petrodollar, but also for the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]].[2] Setting aside competing theories about McCarthy's replacement, he pointed to the two net effects legacy media itself identified — higher odds of a November government shutdown and lower odds of additional Ukraine funding packages — and concluded that with defense stocks hemorrhaging he was "struggling to find the bad news."[2]

## Aftermath

Next steps were highly uncertain, with no obvious successor to lead the House Republican majority. Action in the chamber was halted until the following week, when Republicans would attempt to elect a new Speaker — the process that became the [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election|2023 Speaker of the House election]].[1]
