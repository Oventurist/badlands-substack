# New York City Migrant Prepaid Card Program

The **New York City migrant prepaid card program** was a reported $53 million pilot approved by Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] to distribute prepaid cards to migrant families housed in city hotels.[1]

## Design and rationale

Adams defended the initiative during a state legislative budget hearing in Albany, clarifying that the program would issue prepaid food cards rather than American Express credit cards as critics had suggested.[1] He argued that the approach would improve food distribution efficiency and reduce waste, cutting migrant support costs by roughly 20 percent.[1] The pilot involved 500 individuals and was projected to save approximately $6.7 million annually.[1]

## Criticism

The cards were valued at about $350 per month per migrant, an allocation that exceeded the assistance available to low-income New Yorkers under SNAP benefits — migrants receiving about 40 percent more than the state offers its own residents in need — and also exceeded the monthly services provided to single veterans in the state, according to reporting cited by Sara Carter.[1] The disparity drove much of the public uproar over the program.[1]
