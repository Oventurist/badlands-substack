# Pocket Rescissions

A "pocket rescission" is a budgetary maneuver by which a president can effectively cancel previously appropriated federal funding without obtaining affirmative congressional approval.[1] The tactic drew renewed attention in July 2025 when [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] Director [[russ-vought|Russ Vought]] said the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was considering using it.[1]

## Mechanics

The strategy works by exploiting the timing rules of the Impoundment Control Act. A president submits a rescission proposal — a formal request that [[congress|Congress]] cancel appropriated funds — within 45 days of the end of the fiscal year on September 30.[1] The funds are withheld during the 45-day congressional review period, and if Congress does not act before the fiscal year ends, the funds simply expire.[1]

By contrast, when a rescission request is made outside the fiscal year-end window and Congress declines to act within 45 days, the funding is released and spending on the impounded program resumes.[1] The difference in outcome is entirely a function of when the request is filed.

## Vought's comments and the legal dispute

"It's very possible that we might use pocket rescissions. It's one of our executive tools. It's been used before," Vought told CNN's "State of the Union" on Sunday, July 27, 2025.[1] Critics characterize the maneuver as an overreach of executive power, and Congress has expressed concerns, but Vought said the Government Accountability Office "has said it was legal in the 1970s."[1]

Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] noted that he and co-host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] had anticipated the move weeks earlier, and quoted Vought describing it as "a fully legal approach to using the impoundment control act... to send up a recissions bill later in the year and be able to have that evaporate at the end of the fiscal year," adding that "nothing is taken off the table for us to be able to deal with the deficits we have."[1]
