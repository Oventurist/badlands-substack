# New York Times v. Microsoft and OpenAI

On December 27, 2023, [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] filed a lawsuit against [[microsoft|Microsoft]] and [[openai|OpenAI]], the company behind [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]], accusing them of copyright infringement and of abusing the newspaper's intellectual property.[1]

In its court filing, the publisher said it sought to hold the two companies to account for "billions of dollars in statutory and actual damages" that it believed it was owed for the "unlawful copying and use of The Times's uniquely valuable works."[1] The Times accused Microsoft and OpenAI of building a business model founded on "mass copyright infringement," alleging that their AI systems "exploit and, in many cases, retain large portions of the copyrightable expression contained in those works."[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that whatever the underlying legal merits, the memetic power of the headline spoke for itself: for years independent citizen journalists had compared corporate media output to that of non-player characters, and now the self-described paper of record — in his framing, the principal narrative-setter for the establishment — was suing AI developers for training on its content.[1] He described this as a continuation of an "Ouroboros" pattern in which legacy media, politics and culture consume themselves, dating the acceleration to roughly 2016 and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s entry into presidential politics.[1] His conclusion for writers and creatives facing displacement by AI was blunt: "Be better. Be human."[1]
