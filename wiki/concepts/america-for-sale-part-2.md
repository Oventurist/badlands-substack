# America for Sale — Part 2

"America for Sale - Part 2," subtitled "The Foundation of Corruption," is a Badlands Media essay published on January 30, 2025 by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]. It is the sequel to [[america-for-sale-part-1|America for Sale — Part 1]] and argues that [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] and [[doug-band|Doug Band]] — both former interns in the [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] White House — became the twin hinges of a pay-for-play network linking the [[state-department|State Department]] under Secretary [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], and the consulting firm and investment bank [[teneo|Teneo]]. The article closes by connecting that network to a 2009 hostage mission to [[north-korea|North Korea]] and to Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]]'s custody of congressional evidence of what the author calls treason.

## "Follow Huma"

The essay opens from the Q maxim "follow the money," but insists that the parallel instruction was "Follow Huma." It quotes Q drop 71, posted November 4, 2017 — the day before [[prince-alwaleed|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]]'s arrest in Saudi Arabia — which asks: "FOLLOW HUMA. Who connects HRC/CF to SA? Why is this relevant? Who is the Muslim Brotherhood? Who has ties to the MB?"

According to the source, Abedin was a "handler" for Hillary Clinton dating to Bill Clinton's presidency and the chosen liaison between Clinton and Prince Alwaleed. The article stresses that Abedin's family moved to Jeddah — Alwaleed's home city — while her birth in Michigan made her an American citizen and therefore, in the author's words, "the perfect choice" for the liaison role. Q, the essay notes, tied Abedin to Clinton, the Clinton Foundation, the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] and Alwaleed, but omitted one further connection: Teneo.

## Abedin, Teneo and "special government employee" status

The article quotes *The Washington Times* (September 29, 2015) reporting that while still at the State Department, Abedin was paid by the private consulting firm Teneo Holdings to help stage a September 20, 2012 reception at the Essex House in New York City — days after the [[benghazi|Benghazi]] attack — at which Bill Clinton, [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and former British Prime Minister [[tony-blair|Tony Blair]] appeared as speakers before potential Teneo clients. The paper reported that Abedin earned a $15,000-a-month consulting fee from Teneo over a seven-month period while simultaneously drawing pay as a "special government employee" advising Clinton at the State Department.

The source treats this as an obvious conflict of interest, noting that Abedin was not an ordinary employee needing a second job but an aide privy to secret information at the highest levels. It further quotes the *Times* stating that special government employee status allowed Abedin to take on consulting work with both Teneo and the Clinton Foundation, where she assessed the charity's programs "to pave the way for Mrs. Clinton's return there after she left the State Department in early 2013." Throughout this period, the article adds, Abedin remained assistant editor of the [[journal-of-muslim-minority-affairs|Muslim Brotherhood journal]] her parents helped start in Jeddah.

The author's inference is that Abedin served as liaison between Clinton Foundation donors seeking favors and Hillary Clinton's State Department, and that Alwaleed's access to Abedin gave him direct access to the department, the Foundation and Teneo. Citing Q drop 299 (December 7, 2017) — "SA Controls (assigned) US / UK POLITICIANS / Tech Co's (primary)" — the essay argues that Alwaleed had controlled leading American and British politicians for decades, that he was among the Clinton Foundation's largest donors while Clinton was Secretary of State, and that Clinton's expected presidency was the second half of a "sixteen year plan" that began with the installation of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].

## Doug Band

The article introduces Doug Band by way of Wikipedia: born October 28, 1972, an American businessman and lawyer, founding partner and former president of Teneo, previously Deputy Assistant and then counselor to President Bill Clinton, the "key architect" of the Clinton post-presidency and creator of the [[clinton-global-initiative|Clinton Global Initiative]]. The same entry notes that Band traveled to North Korea to orchestrate the release of two Americans and to Cuba to help secure the release of Alan P. Gross — a detail the essay flags for later.

Drawing a parallel with Part 1's treatment of [[monica-lewinsky|Monica Lewinsky]], the article observes that Band, like Abedin, entered the Clinton orbit as a White House intern. The *Gainesville Sun* (2008) is quoted describing Band as a Sarasota native who earned a bachelor's degree in English at the University of Florida in 1995 and became a White House intern, rising to Clinton's personal aide. *The Atlantic* (October 2016) is quoted at length: Band "helped launch the Clinton Foundation, came up with the idea for the Clinton Global Initiative, brokered deals for paid speeches that enriched Clinton, and then started a private consulting firm called Teneo," and "Band, now 44, was to Bill Clinton what Huma Abedin has been to Hillary."

A Breitbart report from October 2016, based on WikiLeaks material, is cited for the claims that Teneo obtained State Department approval for a paid consulting arrangement between Bill Clinton and Band; that Teneo also paid Abedin while she served as senior adviser at the department; and that a few months after Hillary Clinton took office in 2009, Abedin began communicating with Clinton Foundation donors who were asking favors of the State Department. The article states that Teneo paid Bill Clinton $2.5 million per year as a client and paid adviser.

## Declan Kelly, Paul Keary and the founding of Teneo

The essay turns to [[declan-kelly|Declan Kelly]], the Teneo co-founder whom Hillary Clinton handpicked as her economic envoy to Northern Ireland. Quoting *Politico* (April 2016), it reports that Clinton personally sought out Kelly — then 41 and a former fundraiser — giving him "a special status outside normal diplomatic channels," and that while serving as special envoy soliciting corporate investment he was simultaneously a private consultant to two of those corporations, earning about $2.4 million from [[dow-chemical|Dow Chemical]]. *Politico* also reported that during this period Kelly and Band were preparing to launch Teneo Holdings, which would employ numerous Clinton associates including Abedin and, for a time, Bill Clinton as "honorary chairman."

The article emphasizes that Kelly took no State Department salary and hired his own staff, and quotes *Politico* reporting that despite official-sounding titles and regular interaction with State Department staff and Irish diplomats, "none of them were official government employees," with the department stating it had no record of them. Some of Kelly's envoy-office employees simultaneously did consulting work for Declan Kelly Consulting and would become among Teneo's first employees, escaping the oversight applied to State personnel. The author reads this arrangement as deliberate concealment.

Teneo was formally launched in June 2011 by Kelly, Band and a third partner, [[paul-keary|Paul Keary]]. *Politico* is quoted reporting that planning began in 2009 — the year Clinton became Secretary of State — with Kelly creating a private consulting firm weeks before taking his unpaid State post and Keary creating two others that later combined under the Teneo umbrella. The two-year delay, the article explains, was due to a noncompete agreement Kelly signed on leaving [[fti-consulting|FTI Consulting]] in July 2009, which nonetheless allowed him to keep consulting part-time for Dow Chemical and Coca-Cola; between August 2009 and January 2011 Dow paid Kelly $2.4 million through FTI.

## FIFA, Qatar and UBS

The article describes Keary as building a client list during the same interval. *Politico* is quoted noting that the USA bid committee for the World Cup hired Keary in 2010, that Band had joined that committee's board of directors earlier the same year, and that Bill Clinton personally lobbied [[fifa|FIFA]] that autumn to bring the tournament to the United States.

Citing *Nonprofit Quarterly*, the source reports that FIFA — whose leadership was arrested in Switzerland in 2015 on U.S. Department of Justice charges — gave the Clinton Foundation between $50,000 and $100,000 as a Clinton Global Initiative membership fee, plus a commitment to build twenty community centers in South Africa tied to the 2010 World Cup; that the [[qatar|Qatar]] 2022 Supreme Committee gave between $250,000 and $500,000 as a CGI sponsor in 2013; and that the government of Qatar has given the Clinton Foundation between $1 million and $5 million. The essay asks whether the timing of the FIFA arrests was a coincidence and notes that the Clintons' involvement was never investigated.

Keary is also credited with winning the business of the Swiss bank [[ubs|UBS]], whose Zurich branch came under fire in 2009 for helping wealthy Americans evade taxes. *Politico* reported that Teneo helped arrange a "Revitalizing America" event series beginning in May 2011 pairing UBS executive Bob McCann on stage with Bill Clinton and George W. Bush; McClatchy reported Clinton received $1.5 million for participating, and *The Wall Street Journal* reported UBS donations to the Clinton Foundation grew from $60,000 in 2008 to $600,000 by 2014.

*The Guardian* (July 2015) is quoted describing the February 2009 IRS suit demanding UBS disclose 52,000 possible American tax evaders, and the August 19, 2009 settlement — reached, in the paper's account, with the involvement of Clinton as Secretary of State and Swiss lawmakers — under which UBS would pay no fine and provide information on 4,450 accounts. The same report lists a Clinton Economic Opportunity Initiative partnership offering $32 million in loans, a $350,000 donation in June 2011, a $100,000 charity golf donation, and more than $1.5 million in speaking fees paid to Bill Clinton between 2001 and 2014. The article notes that UBS nonetheless paid $780 million in fines, interest and restitution to avoid prosecution, citing *Investment News*.

## The Obama DOJ "slush fund"

Asking why the Obama [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] would fine UBS so heavily, the essay pivots to the [[doj-slush-fund|DOJ settlement slush fund]]. Quoting *Forbes* (October 2017), it reports that internal DOJ documents confirmed the existence of a departmental "slush fund" under the Obama administration and that officials "went out of their way" to exclude conservative groups, according to [[bob-goodlatte|House Judiciary Chairman Bob Goodlatte]]. Goodlatte's committee found that the DOJ had systematically subverted Congress's budget authority by using settlements with financial institutions to funnel money to "left-wing activist groups." Hot Air is quoted reporting Goodlatte's "smoking gun" emails and the finding that roughly $1 billion was directed to outside organizations over two years.

The article links this to Q drop 36 (November 2, 2017), which asked what "SLUSH FUND" Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] had ended and how George Soros, Obama, Clinton, Eric Holder and Loretta Lynch all net millions within a single tax year.

## Goodlatte, Gowdy, Huber and Horowitz

Goodlatte and [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] are presented as central to criminal investigations conducted out of public view before both resigned from Congress. Citing Q drop 2462 — "Who briefed GOODLATTE & Gowdy on classified intel re: DOJ & FBI?" — the author identifies [[ezra-cohen-watnick|Ezra Cohen Watnick]], Michael Flynn's former deputy, as the briefer, and claims Donald Trump installed him at the DOJ specifically for that purpose. The essay states that Trump publicly thanked both congressmen and that their work "will never be forgotten."

The article recounts that Sessions had secretly appointed U.S. Attorney [[john-huber|John Huber]] in November 2017, with the Clinton Foundation as his first investigative focus, and quotes Breitbart citing Professor Jonathan Turley calling the move "brilliant" for combining the DOJ inspector general's powers with a prosecutor able to bring charges faster than a second special counsel. Q drop 1517 (June 16, 2018) is quoted — "IG started long before Huber setting stage… Think: IG = FBI, Huber = DOJ (no DC)" — and read as evidence that record CEO and political resignations reflect knowledge of what is coming.

The author explains the strategy as routing around a corrupted [[fbi|FBI]] and DOJ: Sessions assigned Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]] to investigate instead of the FBI, and Huber to prosecute instead of Main Justice. Breitbart is quoted reporting that Sessions told Congress the matters recommended by Goodlatte, Gowdy and Senator Chuck Grassley were "fully within the scope of [Huber's] existing mandate"; that Horowitz commanded a staff of 470 investigators; that inspector general jurisdiction reaches "actions taken by former employees after they have left government service"; and that Huber, as a U.S. attorney, could empanel a grand jury anywhere — potentially in Utah rather than Washington. The article asserts that hundreds of thousands of sealed indictments await unsealing, and links the sequence to Alwaleed's arrest in Saudi Arabia on November 5, 2017.

## Grassley and the Senate Judiciary Committee

Goodlatte and Gowdy, the essay says, handed their evidence to Senate Judiciary Chairman Chuck Grassley. It enumerates the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]]'s oversight powers — overseeing the DOJ, considering executive and judicial nominations, reviewing pending legislation, holding hearings and investigations, and legislative jurisdiction covering constitutional amendments, federal criminal law, human rights law, immigration, intellectual property, antitrust and internet privacy — and maps them onto Trump's stated goals: ending the two-tier justice system, an executive order on crimes against humanity, ending illegal immigration, protecting intellectual property, breaking up monopolies, and an internet bill of rights.

The author predicts Grassley will be placed at center stage to expose "treason at the highest levels," evidence of which he says is connected to the Clinton Foundation, Huma Abedin and Doug Band, and to the money-laundering operation running between Hillary Clinton's State Department and Teneo. He characterizes the whole arrangement as a "pay-for-play spider web."

## The 2009 North Korea mission

The final section returns to a favor Band asked of Abedin in 2009. The article cites Q drop 227 (November 24, 2017), a wordless image, and identifies it as a photograph from Bill Clinton's secret 2009 mission to Pyongyang to free two American journalists sentenced to twelve years of hard labor — with [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] standing behind Clinton and Doug Band standing behind North Korean leader [[kim-jong-il|Kim Jong Il]], who had requested Clinton by name.

Quoting the *Chicago Tribune*, the essay describes a trove of emails released by [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] under the headline "Bill Clinton/Doug Band Sought State Department Favors for Foundation Supporters," including Band's July 27, 2009 email to Abedin — "Need get me/ justy and jd dip passports" — and her reply, "Okay. Will figure it out." The request concerned diplomatic passports for Band and two younger Clinton aides, Justin Cooper and Jon Davidson, none of whom worked for the State Department; the article argues the passports would let them skip security and scrutiny.

The author then generalizes: hostages, he writes, have been "an enemy playbook strategy for many decades," pointing to the Iran hostages as cover for the Iran-Contra operation and to hostages in Obama's Iran nuclear deal as cover for a secret money transfer, referencing his earlier essays "Shadow Government Exposed" and "Panic in DC." He asks what the 2009 hostage story was covering for, points to Q drop 228 and to leadership changes in three other countries following Trump's 2017 inauguration, and defers the answer to "America for Sale — Part 3."

## Reception and framing

The piece carries Badlands Media's standard disclaimer that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors. It recorded 317 likes on publication. Its claims about Teneo, Abedin's dual employment, the UBS settlement and the DOJ settlement practice rest on mainstream reporting, while the interpretive frame — Alwaleed's control of Western politicians, the sixteen-year plan, sealed indictments and a coming prosecution phase — derives from [[q-anon|Q]] and is contested.
