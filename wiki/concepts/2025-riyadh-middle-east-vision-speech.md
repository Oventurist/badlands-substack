# 2025 Riyadh Middle East Vision Speech

The 2025 Riyadh Middle East vision speech was an address delivered by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia on May 13, 2025, which Badlands Media commentary describes as less a speech than a blueprint for a post-cabal Middle East.[1]

## Content

Trump declared: "After so many decades of conflict, finally it is within our grasp to reach the future that generations before us could only dream about — a land of peace, safety, harmony, opportunity, innovation, and achievement right here in the Middle East."[1]

The speech offered Iran a "new path" — economic partnership and sanctions reversal in exchange for denuclearisation and withdrawal from Yemen and Syria.[1] Trump also outlined plans to help Lebanon rebuild its infrastructure, to support Jordan's banking sector, and to offer Syria a path to United Nations-monitored elections.[1] Even critics writing in Foreign Affairs acknowledged that the framework would force the Gulf states to choose commerce over chaos.[1]

## Investment forum remarks

At the associated Saudi-U.S. Investment Forum the same day, Trump articulated the economic corollary of the doctrine: "We will export commerce, not chaos, technology, not terrorism, and peace, not war."[1] He also defended his trade agenda at the forum, saying "We're fighting for America's future with trade, and we will win."[1]

## Significance

The article treats the Riyadh address as one of three pillars of a 2025 peace strategy, alongside the Ukraine peace framework and a series of hostage recoveries, all of which apply the same principle that peace is structural rather than symbolic and profitable rather than merely virtuous.[1] The Riyadh plan in particular was expected to attract investment capital to the region and reduce defence spending, feeding what the author calls a peace-dividend model backed by hard capital.[1] The doctrine is closely tied to the [[reciprocal-tariff-doctrine|Reciprocal Tariff Doctrine]], under which trade routes and capital flows are attached to diplomacy so that nations have more to lose by choosing war.[1]
