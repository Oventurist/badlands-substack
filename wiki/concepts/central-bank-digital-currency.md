# Central Bank Digital Currency

A central bank digital currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a nation's sovereign currency issued directly by its central bank. Within the Badlands Media corpus the subject is unusually contested: the June 2023 article [[are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom|"Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?"]] by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] is written explicitly against the prevailing view in alternative and conservative media that CBDCs constitute the [[cabal]]'s instrument for total financial surveillance and control.

## The fear narrative and the counter-argument

The standard critique, as the article summarizes it, holds that central banks are constructing digital money in order to monitor and control every transaction by every individual and business, abolishing cash in the process. Lange rejects both the premise and the assumption behind it, arguing that the fear "focuses on the enemy's plans, assuming their outcome will prevail and that they are in control of what's happening." In his account the cabal already controls the financial system completely and would gain nothing new; CBDC development is instead a reaction to forces outside its control.

Two such forces are named. The first is bitcoin and the blockchain technology beneath it — a decentralized, distributed ledger the article calls "the opposite of what the cabal wants." Because Bitcoin preceded CBDCs, Lange argues that central banks were forced to compete with cryptocurrency and fintech innovation rather than acting on a long-laid plan. The second is [[brics|BRICS+]], whose members are said to have built a parallel financial system and to be preparing a gold- and commodity-backed common currency, with a digital cross-border settlement layer of the kind [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] described as "completely independent of banks and third-party interference."

## Claimed advantages

Citing Forbes, the article lists faster payments, cheaper international transfers, 24/7 availability, inclusion of the unbanked (the FDIC's 2019 survey counted more than seven million American households without a bank account), and more efficient government disbursement of tax refunds, child benefits, and food stamps. Lange contrasts digital currency with the existing ACH system, in which settlement takes days and a third party retains power to cancel transactions or freeze accounts. He also distinguishes CBDCs from FedNow, which he describes as a peer-to-peer payment rail rather than a currency.

## Scale of adoption

Research from the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]]'s CBDC tracker, quoted via CNBC, is cited for the finding that at least 87 countries representing over 90 percent of global GDP were exploring a CBDC, compared with only a handful in mid-2019. Lange notes that many of the same countries are buying record quantities of gold, which he reads as preparation for a coordinated move to a [[gold-standard|gold standard]].

## The American fight over values

The article treats a U.S. digital dollar as inevitable and reframes the question as what values it will encode. It credits jared kushner with quietly advocating a Fed-issued digital dollar in 2019 and J. Christopher Giancarlo, founder of the Digital Dollar Project, with driving the public case. Giancarlo's stated values — free enterprise, free economic activity, individual privacy, and freedom from government censorship of lawful activity — are presented as the American position in a global contest over the design of digital money.

Lange concedes that a CBDC is merely a digital [[fiat-currency|fiat]] currency unless backed by gold, and argues that the state-level CBDC bans and congressional opposition are deliberately engineered to produce litigation that will take the definition of money, and the constitutionality of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].
