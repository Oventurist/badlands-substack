# SAVE Plan Litigation

The SAVE plan litigation was the series of legal challenges that culminated in July 2024 with the total blocking of the Biden administration's Saving on a Valuable Education (SAVE) student-loan repayment plan, which was intended to lower borrowers' monthly payments and shorten the timeline to loan forgiveness for many of them.[1]

## Course of the litigation

Two separate groups of Republican state attorneys general filed lawsuits earlier in 2024 seeking to block the SAVE plan.[1] At the end of June 2024, two federal courts placed preliminary injunctions on the plan.[1] Days later the 10th Circuit appeals court granted the Biden administration's request to stay one of those rulings, allowing provisions set to take effect in July — including cheaper payments — to proceed.[1]

On Thursday, 18 July 2024, the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-eighth-circuit|8th Circuit Court of Appeals]] blocked all provisions of SAVE in a one-sentence ruling: "Appellants' emergency motion for an administrative stay prohibiting the appellees from implementing or acting pursuant to the Final Rule until this Court rules on the appellants' motion for an injunction pending appeal is granted."[1] The effect was to prohibit borrowers from receiving cheaper payments and debt cancellation while the legal process continued.[1] An [[education-department|Education Department]] spokesperson said the department was "assessing the impacts of this ruling and will be in touch directly with borrowers with any impacts that affect them."[1]

## Commentary

The ruling landed the same day the administration announced $1.2 billion in cancellations under [[public-service-loan-forgiveness|Public Service Loan Forgiveness]].[1] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s decrees "hold about as much water today as his bladder does," presenting the outcome as evidence that dramatic executive announcements often fail to produce effects "in the realm of the real."[1]
