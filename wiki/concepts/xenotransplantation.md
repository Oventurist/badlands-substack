# Xenotransplantation

Xenotransplantation is the practice of using animal organs in human patients.[1] Research in the field has centered on genetically modified pigs, whose organs are altered to reduce immune rejection in human recipients.[1]

## State of the field

Scientists have had early success transplanting genetically modified pig kidneys and hearts into living humans, and pig livers have been transplanted into people who were brain-dead.[1] Experts had nonetheless expressed concerns about whether the liver would be a good candidate for the procedure, because unlike hearts and kidneys — which have relatively narrow functions — the liver is large, has a dual blood supply, and performs multiple functions.[1]

## 2025 pig liver case in China

In October 2025 doctors in [[china|China]] reported that they had transplanted a genetically modified pig liver into a 71-year-old man who lived 171 days after the procedure, spending 38 of those days with the pig organ in place.[1] It was the first such case to be published in a peer-reviewed journal.[1]

Dr. [[beicheng-sun|Beicheng Sun]], president of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University and a co-author of the study, argued that the result should change expert assumptions: "Everyone always says, 'oh, liver is too complicated to transplant, compared to the heart or kidney,' but after this, in the future, I think people will think differently. I think liver is good if we can get enough human genes in the pig."[1]
