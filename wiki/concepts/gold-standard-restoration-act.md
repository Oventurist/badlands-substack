# Gold Standard Restoration Act

The Gold Standard Restoration Act (H.R. 2435) is a sound money bill introduced in the 118th Congress in April 2023 that would repeg the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] note "dollar" to a fixed weight of gold bullion.[1] It was introduced by Rep. [[alex-mooney|Alex Mooney]] (R-WV), joined by Reps. [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] (R-AZ) and [[paul-gosar|Paul Gosar]] (R-AZ), as America faced what sponsors described as the twin threats of inflation and bank failures.[1] Supporters framed it as a means of allowing the dollar to regain stable footing for the first time in more than half a century.[1]

## Mechanism

Upon passage, the bill would give the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Treasury]] and the Federal Reserve 24 months to publicly disclose all gold holdings and gold transactions.[1] After that disclosure period, the Federal Reserve note would be formally repegged to a fixed weight of gold at its then-market price.[1] Federal Reserve notes would become fully redeemable for and exchangeable with gold at the new price, with the Treasury and its gold reserves backstopping the Federal Reserve Banks as guarantor.[1]

## Rationale

Monetary experts cited in coverage argued that a return to a [[gold-standard|gold standard]] would substantially curtail the economic damage caused by inflation, runaway federal debt, and monetary system instability.[1] Mooney stated that "a gold standard would protect against Washington's irresponsible spending habits and the creation of money out of thin air."[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] sponsor and financial analyst [[kirk-elliott|Dr. Kirk Elliott]] observed that the federal proposal followed several state-level efforts to restore gold and silver to their status as official government money, including a Texas proposal for a gold-backed digital currency.[1] He described a renewed interest in constitutional money and argued that even if such proposals do not pass immediately, they serve as ready frameworks for a future currency crisis.[1]
