# Gender Affirmative Care

Gender affirmative care (also "gender affirming care") is the clinical model under which a patient's stated gender identity is accepted at face value and social, hormonal, or surgical transition is facilitated without first treating the identity claim as a psychiatric question.[1] A February 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], assisted by [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], describes the model as the medical arm of [[gender-ideology|gender ideology]] and argues that under it medical personnel in many countries participate in the sterilization and mutilation of minors.[1]

## WPATH and de-psychopathologization

The model is promoted principally by [[wpath|WPATH]], the World Professional Association for Transgender Health.[1] In a 2010 statement the WPATH Board of Directors "strongly urge[d] the de-psychopathologisation of gender variance worldwide," holding that gender expression not stereotypically associated with one's assigned sex at birth is a common and culturally diverse human phenomenon that should not be judged inherently pathological, and that psychopathologization reinforces stigma and increases risks to mental and physical well-being.[1]

Deschamps argues that this statement deliberately conflates people with the recognized mental-health condition of [[gender-dysphoria|gender dysphoria]] with the purposefully ambiguous category of "gender-variant people," producing what he calls complete mayhem in medical systems that adopt it, and deterring many doctors from providing such care.[1] The defining feature of the radical version of the model, in his account, is affirming children without first evaluating their mental health, for fear of being socially outcast as a bigot.[1] He characterizes the claim that mental-health safeguards themselves endanger mental health as Orwellian doublespeak, given that gender dysphoria has been studied as a mental disorder for decades.[1]

The essay cites a Christina Button article as refuting the WPATH-associated claim that transgender individuals are born with the brain of the opposite sex and that gender identity is brain-determined — a claim the author says is used to persuade clinicians worldwide that withholding medical intervention from anyone who "feels trans," at any age, is transphobic.[1]

## Puberty blockers

[[puberty-blockers|Puberty blockers]] are central to the dispute. The essay notes the drugs were authorized for treating precocious puberty, where the effect is transient and children soon resume normal development, whereas when prescribed for gender dysphoria they are intended to halt puberty in a way that pushes most recipients onward to hormone therapy.[1] Because this is off-label use, the article argues the drugs have not been well investigated as a treatment for gender dysphoria.[1] Under WPATH's principle, the author says, access should not even require a clinical dysphoria diagnosis.[1]

## International divergence

Finland, Norway, the United Kingdom, and Sweden are described as resisting the paradigm and erecting medical gatekeeping that prioritizes mental health, with little coverage of that dissent in North American mainstream media.[1] An October 14, 2022 Reuters report described England's [[nhs|National Health Service]] drafting stricter guidelines for transgender youth, including alerting local authorities in some cases where young people obtained puberty blockers or hormones on the private market.[1] The NHS interim service specification of October 2022 required providers to be established specialist tertiary paediatric units with strong mental-health partnerships, academic research track records, and robust safeguarding frameworks — a radical departure from the affirmative model, which activists and WPATH called transphobic and claimed would "kill trans kids."[1] The article states that the approach used in Canada would be prohibited under the NHS rules.[1]

## Adoption in North America

In [[canada|Canada]] the model is described as fully adopted by legislators, politicians, and medical professionals who accepted it at face value.[1] In the United States, WPATH's influence is illustrated in the essay by [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]], which the author says permits young girls who self-diagnose gender dysphoria same-day access to testosterone without a prescription or parental permission.[1] Both countries are accused of using gender ideology and affirmative care to endanger children and strip [[parental-rights|parental rights]].[1]

## International rejection of the model

In a March 2023 feature, [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that three international developments should have destroyed the evidentiary basis for the gender affirmative model: the shutting down of the [[tavistock-institute|Tavistock]] gender clinic in the United Kingdom, Sweden's change of approach to gender dysphoria, and the [[toronto-desistance-study|Toronto study]] on gender dysphoria in youth.[2]
In England, the [[nhs|NHS]] released an entirely new set of protocols in the autumn of 2022, and the [[society-for-evidence-based-gender-medicine|Society for Evidence Based Gender Medicine]] reported on October 24, 2022 that most gender dysphoric youths under 18 were now held to need psychoeducation and psychotherapy rather than specialty "transgender healthcare," with [[puberty-blockers|puberty blockers]] delivered only in research protocol settings.[2] The interim report of the [[cass-review|Cass Review]] defined the affirmative model as "a model of gender healthcare that originated in the USA," a point Esler emphasised as an explicit rejection of the American approach.[2] New NHS guidance also recognised social transition as a psychosocial intervention rather than a neutral act and strongly discouraged it in children.[2]

In May 2021 Sweden became the first country to stop prescribing puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones to children under 16, announced in a letter from [[karolinska-hospital|Karolinska Hospital]] citing the SBU's finding of a lack of evidence and the risk of irreversible harms including cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, infertility, increased cancer risk and thrombosis.[2]

Esler contended that despite these reversals, Canada remained steadfast in ramping up large-scale implementation of the model, while a shift in the [[overton-window|Overton Window]] produced policy changes in a growing number of US states.[2] He attributed the model's survival in North America to the omission of internationally recognised evidence against it, arguing that in a world of global information such a localised phenomenon "has to be curated," and describing the model as a fifth-generation warfare operation camouflaged by a veneer of scientific and academic authority.[2] He also faulted [[scientific-american|Scientific American]] for relying almost entirely on American citations in its defence of the model.[2]
