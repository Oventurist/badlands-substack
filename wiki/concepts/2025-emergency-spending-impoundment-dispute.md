# 2025 Emergency Spending Impoundment Dispute

The **2025 emergency spending impoundment dispute** was a confrontation between [[us-congress|Congress]] and the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] over whether the president may unilaterally decline to spend funds appropriated by law.[1]

## The dispute

In late March 2025, Senator [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]], Republican of Maine and chairwoman of the Senate Appropriations Committee, accused President [[donald-trump|Trump]] of illegally refusing to spend $2.9 billion approved by Congress.[1] Collins initiated a letter to the White House, co-signed by Senator Patty Murray of Washington, the panel's senior Democrat, asserting that the administration had violated the six-month spending law Congress had approved earlier that month.[1]

The letter pointed to a memo Trump had sent to Congress declaring that only a portion of the $12.4 billion designated as emergency funding in the legislation would actually be spent, "because I do not concur that the added spending is truly for emergency needs."[1]

The appropriators vigorously contested that assertion, arguing that the law requires the administration either to spend all emergency money or none of it, and does not permit the president to decide for himself which appropriated funds to release.[1] The episode was described as an early salvo in the simmering struggle between Congress and the [[white-house|White House]] over which branch holds ultimate power over federal spending.[1]
