# EU Digital Identity Wallet

The EU Digital Identity Wallet (EUDI) is a digital credential system developed by the [[european-commission|European Commission]] and intended to be made available to all EU citizens.[1] The Commission describes the wallets as "personal digital wallets allowing citizens to digitally identify themselves, store and manage identity data and official documents in electronic format," including items such as a driving licence, medical prescriptions or educational qualifications.[1]

## Pilot programs

Through 2023 the Commission advanced pilot deployments largely outside public attention, with more than 250 private corporations and public authorities participating in four large-scale pilot projects.[1] The EU had invested €46 million into these pilots at the time of reporting, while legislation to streamline the wallets' use across Europe was being finalized.[1]

Use cases already under test included accessing government services, registering and activating SIM cards for mobile network services, signing contracts, facilitating travel and presenting educational credentials — a spread of functions covering services essential to ordinary daily life.[1]

## Criticism

The [[brownstone-institute|Brownstone Institute]] argued that while the Commission emphasized convenience, security and breadth of use, the less-discussed dimension was the tool's potential for ethical and surveillance-related problems.[1]

Commentator [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] framed the rollout as globalist infrastructure advancing while public attention was consumed by war abroad and domestic culture conflict, noting that institutions such as the World Economic Forum had long gamed out such technologies.[1] He argued that the critical implication is inverted from the marketing: if a digital ID can "open up the digital world" for an individual, it necessarily carries the capacity to close that world off as well.[1] DeLarme tied the timeline to the stated ambition of making the 2030s a "Digital Decade," meaning core technocratic systems would need to be entrenched well before that decade begins, and suggested that resistance consists of spreading awareness and reducing personal dependence on government systems.[1]
