# 2024 Thuringia State Election

The 2024 state election in Thuringia, in eastern Germany, produced the first state-level election victory for the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD) since the Nazi era, a result reported by Newsweek and carried in the September 2, 2024 Badlands News Brief.[1]

## Result

The AfD emerged as the clear victor in Thuringia, securing between 32 and 33 percent of the vote according to projections by the public broadcasters ARD and ZDF.[1] That placed the party well ahead of the center-right [[christian-democratic-union|Christian Democratic Union]], which took roughly 24 percent.[1] Projections showed the AfD also in close contention in the neighboring state of Saxony, where the CDU had held power since reunification in 1990 and where both parties hovered around 31 to 32 percent — see the [[2024-saxony-state-election|2024 Saxony state election]].[1]

## Political context

Newsweek framed the outcomes as underscoring deepening discontent with the government of Chancellor [[olaf-scholz|Olaf Scholz]], which had been mired in unpopularity and internal conflict.[1] The headline tied the result to the 85th anniversary of the Nazi invasion that started World War II.[1]

## Badlands commentary

The Badlands take rejected the Nazi framing, arguing that "the Nazis didn't lose World War 2, Germany did," and that the Nazi high command subsequently ruled continental Europe through the bureaucracies of [[nato|NATO]] and later the [[european-union|European Union]], created in 1993.[1] The writer asserted that the EU's founding documents closely resemble — "basically a photo-copy" of — the Third Reich's postwar plans for continental Europe.[1] As supporting evidence for continuity rather than return, the commentary cited the standing ovation given to a Waffen SS veteran in the Canadian Parliament the previous year.[1]
