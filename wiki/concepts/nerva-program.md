# NERVA Program

NERVA (Nuclear Engine for Rocket Vehicle Application) was a United States nuclear rocket program unveiled under President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], developed alongside Project Rover.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes the program as promising humanity "a means of flying not only to the moon and mars, but to the furthest reaches of the solar system and beyond."[1]

According to Ehret, the NERVA and Phoebus rockets passed all tests with flying colors, and programs were outlined to begin Mars colonization with plans to land on the red planet by August 1982.[1] The nuclear rocket programs were nonetheless dismantled in 1972 despite having beaten all expectations — one of a cluster of anomalies Ehret attributes to the rise of [[neo-malthusianism|neo-Malthusian]] statecraft in Western science policy, alongside the dissolution of the [[apollo-program|Apollo Program]] by 1973 and the collapse of [[nasa|NASA]]'s budget after its 1964 peak of 4% of GDP.[1]

Ehret situates NERVA within a broader postwar pattern in which nuclear technology "that emerged in the fires of war similarly found itself forged into tools of creation as well as destruction," with civilian reactors producing cheap, abundant energy capable of lifting billions out of poverty.[1] He notes that Russia pursued a parallel path, applying nuclear reactor technology to spacecraft and sending out thirty nuclear reactor units in thirty years.[1]
