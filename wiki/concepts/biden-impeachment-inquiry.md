# Biden Impeachment Inquiry

The Biden impeachment inquiry was a formal investigative process opened by the United States House of Representatives against President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], announced by Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] on 12 September 2023.[1]

## Announcement

McCarthy declared that the House would open an impeachment inquiry into Biden over a variety of alleged crimes, and did so without holding the formal House floor vote he had previously teased as a precondition.[1] He designated Oversight Committee chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] and Judiciary Committee chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] to spearhead the effort.[1]

The stated basis for the inquiry centred on the president's family's foreign business dealings. "Biden used his official office to coordinate with [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s business partners, about Hunter's role in [[burisma|Burisma]] — the Ukrainian energy company," McCarthy said, adding that "it appears that the president's family has been offered special treatment by Biden's own administration."[1] Reporting at the time noted that Senate Republicans were circling the wagons around Biden and discouraging impeachment of "the Big Guy."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writers argued that the inquiry's value lay in disclosure rather than removal. One contributor, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], wrote that "you can't impeach an illegitimate president" but that impeachment inquiries carry power regardless of outcome, recalling that the Trump impeachment hearings drew historic ratings, dominated headlines and saturated everyday conversation.[1] She predicted that hearings would return the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Biden laptop]] material to the mainstream, "awaken a whole new cohort" to what she termed the Biden Crime Family racketeering operation, and expose those who had helped cover it up — while cautioning that Republicans might instead deliver a show trial designed to pacify the public and let the Bidens off the hook.[1] She counted "at least 459 crimes" that could now be tried in the court of public opinion.[1]

A second contributor, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], described the situation as favourable in either direction: a successful impeachment would embarrass the establishment and the Democratic National Committee, while a failure would expose what he called the UniParty's ineptitude or corruption and hand [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] a "controlled, disastrous symbol" to wield during what the community terms the awakening.[1]

## Formalization vote of December 13, 2023

On Wednesday, December 13, 2023, the Republican-led House of Representatives voted to formalize the impeachment inquiry into President Joe Biden, converting an inquiry that had previously been opened by leadership fiat into one authorized by a floor vote.[2] GOP leadership was careful to frame the vote as a procedural strengthening of the investigation rather than a commitment to impeach, even as pressure to proceed grew within the party and among the Republican base.[2] House Speaker Mike Johnson told reporters the day before the vote, "We're not going to prejudge the outcome of this because we can't," adding, "It's not a political calculation. We're following the law and we are the rule of law team and I'm going to hold to that."[2]

Biden responded on Wednesday night by condemning the vote as a "baseless political stunt that even Republicans in Congress admit is not supported by facts."[2] Badlands Media commentary framed the vote against the constitutional standard of "Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors," arguing that election theft, national-security sabotage, treasury looting, solicitation of bribes and RICO-style conduct should each be weighed against that bar.[2] The commentary also argued that show-trial impeachments carry an upside for the Truth Community, because televised political proceedings attract mass audiences and therefore create an unmatched opportunity for public disclosure.[2]

The same day's proceedings were complicated by Hunter Biden's refusal to sit for the closed-door deposition sought as part of the inquiry, which prompted committee chairmen to announce contempt of Congress proceedings.[2]

## Turley on the "moment of maddening truth"

Writing on December 11, 2023, legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] argued that the House vote was only to continue looking into allegations that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] knew of his family's influence-peddling operation and fostered those efforts.[3] He described the final line of Democratic defense as acknowledging that influence peddling occurred while insisting Biden was only trying to support his son.[3] Turley posed the central question — "how do you know?" — noting that millions of dollars were raised in what most view as corrupt influence peddling, with many of those payments now confirmed by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] in the second [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] indictment.[3] Only an investigation, he wrote, could establish the truth about the president's knowledge and involvement, leaving Democratic members who had opposed any investigation for years facing "a moment of maddening truth."[3]

## March 2024 Oversight hearing

On March 20, 2024, the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] held a nearly six-hour public hearing into the Biden family's alleged influence peddling as congressional Republicans pushed the inquiry forward.[4] Former [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] business associates [[tony-bobulinski|Tony Bobulinski]] and [[jason-galanis|Jason Galanis]] testified — Bobulinski in person on Capitol Hill and Galanis virtually from a federal prison camp in Montgomery, Alabama — while Hunter Biden himself refused to attend.[4] Bobulinski accused Hunter Biden of "blatant lies" and of lying under oath about his father's involvement in his business dealings, and said he had "begged" Rep. [[ro-khanna|Ro Khanna]] to review text-message evidence on his BlackBerry.[4]

Democrats invited [[lev-parnas|Lev Parnas]] to testify about his Ukraine work with [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], and Rep. [[jared-moskowitz|Jared Moskowitz]] challenged chairmen [[james-comer|James Comer]] and [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] to hold an immediate impeachment vote if they had truly found high crimes and misdemeanors.[4] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] mocked the proceeding over the preceding weekend, calling the inquiry "the biggest joke of the night" and something "so baseless that has a zero chance of succeeding."[4]

Badlands Media commentary held that, given the low bar established during the 117th Congress, impeachment should have been a straightforward matter, but that "uniparty" pressure on the thin Republican majority kept the effort stuck in hearings; it further argued that "you cannot impeach an illegitimate president" and that the exposure itself benefits the American people, while the bickering left the forum with no credibility.[4]

## December 2023 authorization vote and subpoenas

By early December 2023 House Republicans were inching toward a floor vote to formally authorize the impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], a step described as marking a new phase of the probe.[5] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] of the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] had subpoenaed [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], the president's brother [[james-biden|James Biden]] and other relatives and business associates for depositions, scheduling Hunter for December 13 and James for December 6.[5] Hunter, through attorney [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]], insisted on testifying publicly rather than behind closed doors, while James failed to appear for his interview.[5] In parallel, the [[house-ways-and-means-committee|House Ways and Means Committee]] again interviewed IRS whistleblowers, and emails released around the same time were said to show Joe Biden using aliases to contact Hunter's business partners.[5]

## James Biden's closed-door interview

On February 21, 2024, [[james-biden|James Biden]] appeared before House GOP investigators from the Oversight and Judiciary Committees for an hours-long closed-door interview, testifying that his brother President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had no involvement in any of his financial deals.[6] His 10-page opening statement, obtained by Politico, asserted a 50-year business career in which "Joe Biden has never had any involvement or any direct or indirect financial interest in those activities. None."[6] The inquiry to that point had focused largely on deals cut by the president's brother and his son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]].[6]

Badlands commentary treated the denial as implausible, noting that even Republicans conducting the inquiry appeared to struggle to press the central claim that the president was a participant rather than a bystander in the family's dealings, and pointing to the [[patrick-ho|Patrick Ho]] episode and the Hunter Biden laptop material as circumstantial corroboration.[6]

## First Oversight Committee hearing, September 28, 2023

The [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] held the inquiry's first hearing on Thursday, September 28, 2023, a session devoted largely to reviewing the evidence Republicans had assembled about foreign business ventures involving the president's son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], then 53, which they said showed family members selling access.[7] Republicans detailed foreign payments to members of the Biden family but did not present evidence that the president had personally benefited.[7] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] declared that "the American people demand accountability for this culture of corruption," charging that Biden had lied about his relatives' business dealings and had failed to wall them off from his official duties.[7]

Democrats and several independent witnesses countered that no proof existed that Biden received any of the payments or otherwise behaved improperly during his 2009-2017 vice presidency, and the [[white-house|White House]] denied wrongdoing and dismissed the probe as politically motivated.[7] The panel's ranking Democrat, [[jamie-raskin|Jamie Raskin]], told the hearing, "If Republicans had a smoking gun or even a dripping water pistol they would be presenting it today. But they've got nothing."[7] The hearing unfolded as Biden campaigned for reelection in a likely rematch with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who was himself preparing for four criminal trials.[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the hearing "a surreal experience," writing that Republican members methodically laid out direct evidence of Joe Biden's knowledge of and participation in influence-peddling schemes spanning Russia, China and Ukraine while Democrats responded by shouting "No evidence! Debunked!" and pivoting to Trump.[7] She noted that all Republican witnesses agreed the inquiry should continue, and that Comer confirmed Thursday evening that investigators were moving forward with subpoenas for [[jim-biden|Jim]] and Hunter Biden's bank records.[7] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the launch as a rare combination of "Overton's Goalpost" and "Narrative Whiplash," predicting Democrats would defend an indefensible candidate on national television while their media allies quietly spun up alternative candidates, and suggesting that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] would end up "caught holding the Biden Bag" if Biden survived the drama in office.[7]
