# GeoSpatial Politics

**GeoSpatial Politics** is a term coined by retired Air Force General [[steven-kwast|Steven Kwast]] and Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] to describe the extension of geopolitics into outer space — "geopolitics in space," as the Badlands Brief of April 20, 2026 puts it.

## Origin

The concept was introduced on episode 14 of the Rumble program Space Revolution, "The Dawn of GeoSpatial Politics," in which Kwast and GhostofBasedPatrickHenry discussed a new field of study for the orbital domain. The framing begins from the observation that geography has largely shaped the development of culture, empires, and modern nation-states, and continues to constrain geopolitics today — the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and its connection to worldwide energy prices being the recurring example. The question the pair pose is what happens to diplomacy and trade in space, where there is no "geography" in the terrestrial sense: what shapes power, access, and conflict when the terrain itself is manufactured?

## Significance in the corpus

The coinage reflects the broader Badlands interest in the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]'s agenda of building new institutions, and in space as the next arena of great-power competition. It also models the network's characteristic method of generating analytical vocabulary — naming a phenomenon in order to make it visible — which the Brief applies equally to narrative warfare concepts like the [[forward-defense-zone|Forward Defense Zone]] and the [[rico-grande|RICO Grande]].
