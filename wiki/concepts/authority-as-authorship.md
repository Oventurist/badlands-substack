# Authority as Authorship

**Authority as authorship** is the etymological argument advanced in the Badlands Media essay "The Extraordinary Power of Digital Soldiers" (July 31, 2023), in which the author writing as [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] contends that core legal concepts encode an ancient recognition that words possess creative power.[1]

## The argument from etymology

The essay observes that the word "authority" contains both *author* and *write*, and glosses authority as "author-write-y" — "one who can make things happen with words."[1] The paradigm case offered is the judge, who *pronounces verdicts*: since *ver* means true (as in veracity, verify) and *dict* means speech (as in diction, dictionary), to render a verdict is literally to speak the truth.[1] Correspondingly, *jurisdiction* is parsed as the *right* (juris) to *speak* (diction) about a given matter, so that a man without jurisdiction is simply one not permitted to pronounce verdicts upon it.[1]

When an authority possessing sufficient jurisdiction renders a verdict, the essay argues, this constitutes a miniature act of creation.[1] Two illustrations are given. In a wedding, a *rite* (homophonous with *write*), the minister pronounces two beings — bride and groom — into two new beings, husband and wife.[1] A court hearing is likewise a rite in which people may be assigned new identities such as prisoner or criminal, at the discretion of an author-ity holding the right to assign them through writing.[1]

## Right, rite and write

The essay treats *write*, *right* and *rite* as a cluster of words each connected to power over creation.[1] It adds a geometric argument: dimensions are necessarily created at *right* angles, since generating a plane from a line, or a cube from a plane, requires perpendicular motion, so that three-dimensional reality is "utterly dependent on right angles to be brought into existence at all."[1] Human rights are then presented as a micro-version of that creation event — as the universe uses literal right angles to create itself, so individuals use rights to create their own world.[1] The author suspects the Masonic compass and square alludes to this idea, among others.[1]

*Right* is further linked to correctness and truth: a person who answers a question correctly is said to be "right," which returns to the notion of producing a *ver-dict*, and one who is frequently right becomes an author-ity.[1] The essay's conclusion is that using language skillfully to arrive at true statements is how genuine authority is achieved, and that such authority amounts to a limited power over creation — limited because human beings are not God, but real to the extent that they can generate true statements through their own mental processes.[1] The formula "In the beginning was the Word" is invoked as the theological warrant.[1]

## Application to the occult and to anons

The author states that he does not regard the dark occultists as especially "high" authorities, but that they do recognize language's power to influence creation — the theory behind ritual spellcasting being that local symbolic acts influence nonlocal events through *spelling words*.[1] He calls this recognition one of their greatest advantages over everyone else.[1] Against it he sets the training of [[anons]] by [[qanon|Q]]: by inducing readers to generate true information themselves rather than receiving answers, Q is said to have trained them to become genuine authorities in the sense developed above.[1] For the broader creative principle see [[law-of-attraction|the law of attraction]].[1]
