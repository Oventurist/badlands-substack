# Sound of Freedom

Sound of Freedom is a 2023 American independent film about child sex trafficking, starring [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]] and directed by [[alejandro-monteverde|Alejandro Monteverde]].[1] The film is based on the life of [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]], founder of [[operation-underground-railroad|Operation Underground Railroad]], an organisation dedicated to rescuing children from sex trafficking operations.[1]

## Box office performance

Despite a run of negative coverage from mainstream outlets including Rolling Stone and Bloomberg News, the film grossed $100 million at the box office within several weeks of its debut.[1] According to [[variety|Variety]], it became the first independent film to reach $100 million at the North American box office since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic.[1] Made on a budget reported at $14 million, it ranked as the 16th highest-grossing film of 2023 at the time of the milestone.[1] Audience and critical response were both strongly positive: the film received an A+ CinemaScore and a 100 percent score on Rotten Tomatoes.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the film's performance against the backdrop of a depressed industry and the concurrent Writers Guild and Screen Actors Guild strikes, which he described as threatening to burn Hollywood down on a permanent basis.[1] That same weekend produced one of the strongest box offices in years on the strength of the counter-programmed hits Barbie and Oppenheimer — the former characterised by Burning Bright as one of the most openly neo-feminist films ever made, the latter as an attempt to imprint the mainstream narrative on nuclear war into the collective mind.[1] Beneath that programming, he wrote, Sound of Freedom continued to take the third spot and to accumulate well over $100 million on what he described as a $15 million budget, making it arguably the most financially successful film of the year on a per-screen basis relative to cost — and, he suggested, not a coincidence.[1]

## Mainstream criticism and the QAnon framing

In July 2023 [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] published an opinion piece arguing that Sound of Freedom and [[qanon|QAnon]] "both rely on tired Hollywood tropes," contending that the conspiratorial right and Hollywood's default storytelling conventions are not meaningfully different, which the author said helps explain the difficulty of rejecting QAnon, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and fascism.[2] The piece noted that Trump planned to screen the film at his New Jersey golf club, that the film had been embraced by the far right and by Christians, and that it had passed $50 million in United States box office receipts.[2] Bloomberg located the controversy in the QAnon links attributed to [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]], the former Homeland Security operative the film is about, and to [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]], the actor who portrays him: Ballard had promoted the claim that the furniture retailer Wayfair was involved in child trafficking, while Caviezel had spoken at multiple QAnon events and, in an interview with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]], repeated the claim that traffickers drain victims' blood to produce an anti-ageing serum.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] read the campaign against the film as a Streisand effect.[2] He argued that the earlier weaponisation against Q had been partially successful in making the operation radioactive to ordinary observers, but that attacking Sound of Freedom and child-trafficking exposure was instead producing a convergence between the film and Q that was causing many people to reconsider what anons had been saying for seven years.[2]

## Rolling Stone review and the July 2023 backlash

Shortly after the film's release, [[rolling-stone|Rolling Stone]] published a scathing review by [[miles-klee|Miles Klee]] describing Sound of Freedom as a "QAnon-tinged thriller about child-trafficking" that was "designed to appeal to the conscience of a conspiracy-addled boomer."[3] The review attacked [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]] as "a prominent figure on the conspiracist right" and dwelt on the actor's past claims about elite paedophile rings that kidnap, rape and murder children to harvest [[adrenochrome|adrenochrome]].[3] Klee wrote that the film was "a stomach-turning experience, fetishizing the torture of its child victims and lingering over lush preludes to their sexual abuse," adding that at times he "had the uncomfortable sense that I might be arrested myself just for sitting through it."[3] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], which aggregated the review under the headline "Why Do You Support Child Trafficking?", noted that the film held a 99 percent user rating on [[rotten-tomatoes|Rotten Tomatoes]] against 75 percent from professional reviewers.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that while audiences had made Sound of Freedom the top picture in America, critics were uniformly disparaging it — labelling it "QAnon," calling its substance conspiracy theory, and suggesting that anyone who saw it was a "boomer" — with the bottom line that Americans should not see the film.[3] She argued that protecting children is not a partisan issue and that the attempt to paint a verifiably true story about the exploitation and rape of children as unimportant right-wing propaganda said more about the journalists and their publications than about the film itself.[3]

## Disney and the distribution history

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] highlighted the film's backend connections to [[walt-disney-company|Disney]]: the picture was originally financed by a subsidiary of [[20th-century-fox|20th Century Fox]], and after Disney acquired Fox the film was reportedly shelved indefinitely, prompting a years-long battle by [[angel-studios|Angel Studios]] to reacquire the distribution rights.[3] By July 2023 it was supplanting recent Disney tentpoles, including the latest Indiana Jones instalment.[3] Burning Bright argued that when the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] goes out of its way to frame a film chronicling real-life child trafficking busts as Alt Right and QAnon-adjacent, the correct response is to "sit back and enjoy the show," because the enemy's attempts to obscure the truth spread it more effectively than advocacy could.[3]

## Justin Deschamps's analysis

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described the film as "a perfect storm" exposing the media and child sex trafficking, and suggested it might play a role in the eventual overthrow of the globalists.[3] He noted the film had grossed over $2 million in early ticket sales and was overtaking the new Indiana Jones picture, praised [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]]'s work through [[operation-underground-railroad|Operation Underground Railroad]] as exemplary, and argued that the film's years in development limbo was serendipitous: had it come out in 2018, the public would not have been ready.[3] Since then, he wrote, the Epstein scandal, the border crisis and the trans agenda had made industrial-scale child abuse credible to the average viewer.[3] He cited the sex-grooming character of the education system, the complicity of medical and psychology industries in the mutilation of children, and Big Tech's supply of hand-held devices that bypass parental gatekeepers as evidence the child abuse industry exists.[3] The [[2023-bud-light-boycott|Bud Light]] pushback, he argued, showed a line had been crossed and that the [[overton-window|Overton Window]] had shifted far enough for a film like this to land.[3] He also raised the possibility that the release was coordinated with the political warfare efforts of white hats and Trump-aligned patriots, while declining to answer his own question.[3] Ballard had previously been attacked by [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] in a piece entitled "The Great (Fake) Child-Sex-Trafficking Epidemic," which Deschamps said failed utterly to provide facts or evidence.[3] He framed the controlled media as reality gatekeepers operating in the interests of criminals who engage in sin for its own sake — a reference to [[sabbateanism|Sabbateanism]] and [[frankism|Frankism]] — for whom the very notion of a global child-sex trafficking network must be destroyed.[3]

## As a cultural opening

Writing in July 2023, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated the film's box-office run as the occasion for Badlands Media to begin a long series on child [[ritual-abuse|ritual abuse]] and trauma-based mind control, arguing that as *Sound of Freedom* "rocks the box office and ignites righteous indignation within large pockets of the American population, the perfect opportunity has presented itself" to discuss that subject.[4]
