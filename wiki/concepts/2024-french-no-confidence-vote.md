# 2024 French No-Confidence Vote

The 2024 French no-confidence vote was a parliamentary motion tabled against the minority government of Prime Minister [[michel-barnier|Michel Barnier]], scheduled for Wednesday, December 4, 2024, and widely expected to pass — an outcome that would leave France "staring into the unknown."[1]

## Background and stakes

Barnier's administration had taken office only in September 2024.[1] If the motion carried, it would be the first French government ousted by a motion of no confidence since 1962, and its fall at the hands of the far-right and leftwing parties would represent a significant blow to Europe weeks before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] returned to the White House.[1] Two separate no-confidence motions had been tabled, by the far-left and far-right opposition, with the former widely predicted to pass; a parliamentary debate was due to start at 4 p.m. local time, with the vote roughly three hours later.[1]

## Reactions

Barnier told MPs the vote risked making "everything more difficult and more serious," noting France's situation was already "difficult in budgetary and financial terms" and "very difficult in economic and social terms," and later warned of "a lot of tension in France" while arguing he should remain in office to restore stability.[1] Hardline interior minister Bruno Retailleau said toppling the government would "throw France and the French into an insufferable situation" and accused backers of the motion of playing "Russian roulette."[1]

President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], then visiting Saudi Arabia, was expected to return to France for what media described as a "moment of truth" that risked "plunging France into the great political and financial unknown."[1] The crisis unfolded in the same week that Macron hosted the reopening of [[notre-dame-cathedral|Notre Dame]], which Trump planned to attend.[1]
