# Bread and Circuses

"Bread and circuses" is an expression dating to the Roman Empire, coined by the Roman poet Juvenal to describe how Rome's rulers kept the population under control.[1] The underlying belief was that if the people could be kept fed and entertained, they would be far more easily governed; a population without hunger pangs and with its attention occupied will tolerate a great deal without standing up and demanding change.[1]

## Modern application

In his September 17, 2024 Badlands Media essay "The Media is the Enemy of the People," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that the [[cabal|Cabal]] continues to use the tactic successfully today.[1] Welfare, food stamps and other financial assistance are provided to the poor in amounts calibrated to keep them dependent on government and to discourage revolt, while the working and middle classes are preoccupied and entertained through television, movies, video games, music and sports.[1] Carlson observed that polling shows elites consume considerably less media than lower- and middle-income Americans.[1]

## What Rome lacked

Carlson's central claim is that the Roman version of the formula lacked three modern components.[1] The first is a scientific understanding of the human mind and how to manipulate it, which he attributed to [[carl-jung|Carl Jung]], [[sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]] and Freud's nephew [[edward-bernays|Edward Bernays]], who he says developed techniques capable not only of keeping people from thinking but of changing how they think.[1] The second is unlimited funding, which he traced to the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in 1913.[1] The third is a dedicated and powerful institution to run the operation, a role he assigned to the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] after its creation in 1947.[1] In this account modern distraction has escalated beyond entertainment into outright manipulation and mind control.[1]
