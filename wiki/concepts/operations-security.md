# Operations Security (OPSEC)

Operations security, commonly abbreviated OPSEC, is the practice of denying adversaries information about ongoing operations. Within Badlands Media commentary it functions both as a national-security discipline and as an interpretive key: participants in a live operation are expected to refuse to discuss it and may deny it outright.[1]

## Badlands framing

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] has repeatedly emphasized the importance of OPSEC in his research, stating that "the bottom line with OPSEC is that anybody involved in ops will NOT openly discuss their involvement and maybe even deny the op altogether."[1] He deploys the concept against the common rebuttal that a named figure "denied it so it can't be true," arguing that denial is precisely what operational secrecy requires.[1]

## Institutionalization under NSPM 28

Herold points to [[nspm-28|NSPM 28]], the National Operations Security Program memorandum signed January 13, 2021, as evidence that OPSEC — a discipline typically reserved for military operations — was formally established and implemented at the Executive Branch and extended "beyond to trusted partners" in the final days of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s term.[1] In his view this makes the expectation of official denials provable rather than merely assumed.[1]
