# Interactive Internet Activities

"Interactive Internet Activities" (IIA) is a term used by the U.S. Department of Defense for online engagement conducted as part of information operations.[1] The Badlands Media essay "Our 'Greatest Ally'" invokes IIA and the related concept of cyber influence operations to explain what its author sees as an inorganic surge in openly antisemitic content on social media during 2023–2024.[1]

## The article's claim

DeLarme observes that alongside the rise of young protesters reacting emotionally to events in [[gaza|Gaza]], antisemitic tropes have become commonplace online, spread by accounts that are "almost always anonymous" or by edgy young female posters forming what he calls a "simp-to-anti-Semite pipeline" for weak-willed men.[1] He argues the pattern has "all the hallmarks of an influence op" rather than organic sentiment.[1]

His stated reasoning is one of asymmetric moderation: users could not discuss rigged elections, Hunter Biden's laptop, or COVID origins without being shadow banned, yet overtly vile antisemitic profiles have been allowed to explode in number and popularity on platforms such as Facebook.[1] Combined with a congressional push for censorship legislation that would penalize criticism of Israel, he treats the surge as an effort to make the scourge of [[antisemitism|antisemitism]] appear more real and pervasive than it is.[1] The author frames these as personal observations rather than established fact.[1]
