# Kenya Finance Bill 2024 Protests

In late June 2024 the Kenyan capital of Nairobi descended into violence as mass street protests against new tax policies and a harsh "Eco-Austerity" program culminated in the parliament building being set on fire.[1]

Legislators evacuated after anti-tax protesters breached parliament, quickly overwhelming police shortly after lawmakers voted to pass a bill introducing new nationwide taxes, including an eco-levy that raises the price of basic goods such as diapers as part of an effort to curb waste and improve environmental performance.[1] The levies were contained in [[kenya|Kenya]]'s Finance Bill 2024 and directly affect the import, pricing and sale of diapers, batteries and dry cells, smartphones, earphones, clocks, radios, television sets, cameras, staplers, printers, calculators, photocopying machines, keyboards, mice, projectors and LCD monitors.[1] The eco-levy falls especially heavily on importers of plastics, imposing a substantial fee per kilogram.[1]

Protesters entering parliament were reported shouting, "We're coming for every politician," and there were widespread reports that police used live fire against the crowds alongside riot-control measures such as tear gas.[1] President [[william-ruto|William Ruto]] was outside the capital attending an African Union event at the time but struck a conciliatory tone, praising the enthusiasm of the young people who formed the backbone of the demonstrations.[1] The episode was carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief.[1]
