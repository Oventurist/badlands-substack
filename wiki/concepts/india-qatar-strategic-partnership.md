# India-Qatar Strategic Partnership

The **India-Qatar Strategic Partnership** is a bilateral framework agreed on Tuesday, February 18, 2025, when [[india|India]] and [[qatar|Qatar]] elevated their relationship to the level of a strategic partnership.[1]

## Agreements

Alongside the elevation, the two countries signed a revised pact for the avoidance of double taxation and the prevention of fiscal evasion with respect to taxes on income, and inked five memoranda of understanding covering trade, energy, investments, innovation, technology, food security, culture, and people-to-people ties.[1] The accords followed talks at Hyderabad House between Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] and the Amir of Qatar, Sheikh [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]], covering a range of bilateral matters as well as global developments including the Israel-Hamas conflict and the situation in Afghanistan.[1] Randhir Jaiswal, official spokesperson of India's Ministry of External Affairs, announced the outcome on X.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary situated the partnership in the context of Modi's unsuccessful Washington visit the previous week, at which he had sought to dissuade [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from imposing tariffs or to win special terms for India; Trump instead publicly described India as among the worst offenders on import tariffs and vowed reciprocal tariffs.[1] The Qatar deal was read as Modi taking action favorable to Trump, given Trump's long-standing description of Amir Tamim as a friend since 2017 and his emphasis on Qatar's role in facilitating the Gaza ceasefire secured by US Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] the day before Trump's inauguration.[1] Modi publicly called Tamim "my brother" on X.[1] The commentary characterized such bilateral dealmaking as a subversive act against globalist hegemony, which it argued demands that nation-states route agreements through trade authorities and regional bodies such as the EU.[1]
