# Cultural Hypnosis

**Cultural hypnosis** is the term used by the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] for the process by which a culture's stories, symbols and assumptions install beliefs and behaviours in its members below the level of conscious awareness.[1] In his May 6, 2024 essay "The Hidden Hypnotic Factor," subtitled "Understanding Narratives, Identities, and Trance States in an Information War," he defined hypnosis broadly as any form of communication that bypasses the mind's "firewall" and stimulates an unconscious — that is, automatic — response.[1]

## Culture as meta-identity

The author's central model treats culture as a sort of *meta-identity* containing numerous narratives within itself, which in turn serve as the raw material out of which individuals assemble their own personal identities.[1] On this account many people have no conscious awareness that they did not invent their own personality but "collaged" it together from subliminal and non-subliminal influences.[1] He credits the hypnotist and cartoonist [[scott-adams|Scott Adams]] with the observation that people believe they arrive at their own opinions when in fact their opinions are assigned to them by their favourite media.[1]

He illustrates the invisibility of the medium with the question of whether a fish recognises the water in which it swims, describing the reader as swimming in an ocean of language — of symbols, words, myths and cultural assumptions.[1] Stories are singled out as an especially effective vehicle, since an entire belief system or moral perspective can be suggested to an audience simply by telling a tale whose protagonist holds it — which he offers as an answer to the question of what "entertainment" is actually for.[1]

## Hijacking and weaponisation

The essay is careful to state that there is nothing inherently wrong with belonging to a culture or identifying with its narratives; the problem arises when a culture is hijacked and then weaponised against itself.[1] Because all stories carry moral lessons, a population that does not consciously recognise those lessons cannot defend their essential structure and purpose against revision and degradation in the wrong hands.[1]

Alongside the mainstream press, the author identifies advertising, education, "news" and entertainment as the sectors of society concerned with moulding consciousness, noting that hundreds of billions of dollars per year are spent on advertising in America alone and asking what the chances are that intelligence agencies catalogue that research and wield it as a weapon against the public.[1] He also asks rhetorically how many companies control 99% of the world's media.[1]

## Related work

The author has spent roughly a year building a study-at-home course entitled *Self Defense Against Cultural Hypnosis*, built on the culture-as-meta-identity premise.[1] The related concept of the [[trance-state|trance state]] supplies the mechanism by which cultural suggestion is executed in individual behaviour, and the book [[escaping-cultural-hypnosis|Escaping Cultural Hypnosis]] is recommended elsewhere in his work for the underlying neuroscience.[1]
