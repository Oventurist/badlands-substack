# Thriving in the Transition

**Thriving in the Transition** is a morale and mindset framework advanced in a December 7, 2022 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay by the citizen journalist [[pepe-lives-matter|Pepe Lives Matter]], subtitled "An Appeal to Calm in the Chaos."[1] The piece argues that the awakened citizen — the [[anons|anon]] or truth-seeker — is living through a transitional "barren season" between an old order that is collapsing and a promised restoration that has not yet arrived, and that surviving this interval requires a deliberate discipline of expectation, perspective and faith rather than more information.[1]

## Core argument

The essay opens with the claim that the awakening mind needs "encouragement and faith more than ever," because in the absence of those things the [[black-pilling|black pill]], what the author calls the "Doomer's Refrain," and outright defeatism can embed themselves permanently in the psyche and refuse to let go.[1] Against this, the author prescribes what he terms the **Macro Viewpoint** or "eagle-eyed view": a habit of stepping back from the daily churn of news so that the noise of the day-to-day cannot drown out hope.[1] Cultivating that wider view is described as a core priority for emotional survival, not an optional luxury.[1]

A central distinction in the piece is between content aimed at the not-yet-awakened and content suited to the already-awakened. The author argues that the constant bombardment of "precipice" narratives is engineered to bring the normie and the half-awake into contact with their own [[red-pilling|red pill]] moment, and is therefore not always what the individual patriot is meant to digest daily.[1] Negative realities persist and must be addressed, but the reader's ability to notice and dwell on uplifting moments is presented as imperative to mental and emotional survival in a barren season.[1]

## The transition motif

The essay frames the present moment as a season of leaving one thing behind in order to reach another.[1] It identifies a tension: the reader must not surrender the promises and faith held for future good, yet living permanently inside the strain of not having arrived risks undoing all prior progress on the journey.[1] The proposed resolution is a change of mentality and of expectations — a mindset under which the reader is less likely to abandon belief that the world will be blessed, that God will come through in personal life, and that patriots will secure a future free of "globalist elitists hellbent on destruction."[1] Anons, citizen journalists and the awakening collective, the author writes, cannot give up after everything endured and after learning the truth about the world.[1]

## Trump, the precipice, and the plan

The author invokes [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 campaign announcement speech, in which — as characterized in the essay — Trump stated plainly that the precipice had not yet been reached, that not enough people were awake, and that unity and breakthrough would follow once the people awakened.[1] From this the essay concludes that the transition period for the weary anon is less about digging into information and more about digging deeper into God, or at minimum into faith in something greater.[1]

The piece contrasts the Trump presidency, when "all felt well" and inevitable victory was never doubted despite fierce opposition, with the subsequent period described as a fog in which doubt has overcome many.[1] The masses, the author argues, required a "red pill enema," while the anon already understood the evils at issue; the blatancy of the corruption is interpreted as an unveiling that is a prerequisite to [[trust-the-plan|"the plan"]] being truly implemented, and the author affirms that a plan exists.[1]

The essay addresses an apparent contradiction in Trump's messaging — that the best is yet to come while World War III is simultaneously described as imminent and the adversary as seemingly unbeatable.[1] Its answer is that if the best were not to come the fight would be futile, and therefore the World War III precipice messaging is likely aimed not at the awakened but at unconvinced spectators just beginning to question things and start down their own rabbit holes.[1]

## The pandemic as an accidental awakening

A further section argues that the 2020 "Plandemic" backfired on its architects in tangible ways.[1] Without that demonstration of tyranny, the author contends, many readers would never have arrived at the essay at all; isolated at home over a virus the piece characterizes as having an over 99 percent survival rate and an available cure, readers sensed something was wrong, began researching for themselves, and could not stop digging.[1] Reaching that stage of self-directed inquiry is described as the goal for all reachable people.[1]

Not every red pill lands the same way with every person, the author allows, but once truth latches on the experience is likened to Neo opening his "real" eyes for the first time in *The Matrix*.[1] The essay states as a governing principle that once a mind awakens it never goes back to sleep, and that the anon and truth-seeker cannot return regardless of how painful the journey feels.[1]

## Guarding hope as practice

Hope, in the essay's treatment, is not a mood but a craft: guarding it becomes a way of life, "an art form that needs to be carefully practiced before it can truly be mastered."[1] The author warns that a reader who is constantly and emotionally consumed by narratives designed to trigger other people's awakenings is "living on the precipice incorrectly."[1] Emotion, passion and righteous indignation are legitimate and should be felt, but permanently inhabiting them is described as a recipe for disaster and heartbreak.[1] That heartbreak is attributed to the continued assault on fundamental freedoms, which the essay credits with hardening popular resolve and showing people firsthand what would happen if the [[cabal|Cabal]] were allowed to complete its plans.[1]

## Transformation of the self

The closing movement answers how the truth-seeker endures while the wider world spins on, populated by people ignorant of the severity of the war in which all are engaged.[1] The answer given is that endurance comes from recognizing that the seeker's own nature is changing: difficult truths and reveals are said to expand the corridors of heart and mind and to strengthen the person who absorbs them.[1] Responding to the saying that "the end won't be for everyone," the author replies that the reader should not worry, because their old self will not be present at the end — they are being reborn from the ashes, with the heat of each trial crafting them for a new place.[1] To lose heart or give up at this point, the essay argues, would deny both the self and the world a precious treasure.[1]

The final instruction is to feel the longing, defend hope, do what one is called to do, and meanwhile allow God to work — placing faith in a higher power, in oneself, in one another, and in the truth.[1] The essay ends by suggesting that a simple change of perspective about the meaning of one's trial may itself be the mechanism by which God brings the reader somewhere new, expanding the heart and preparing the interior life — and that this, perhaps, "is the plan."[1]
