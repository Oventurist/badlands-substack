# Laches

Laches is an equitable doctrine under which a court declines to grant relief because the party seeking it waited too long to sue, and the delay has prejudiced the opposing party or third parties who have relied on the status quo.[1] Unlike a statute of limitations, laches is discretionary and turns on the fairness of allowing a late claim to proceed.

## Use in 2020 election litigation

Laches became one of the principal procedural grounds on which [[2020-election-lawsuits|2020 election lawsuits]] were disposed of without any examination of their factual merits.[1] In at least one case the court held that the "challenge was brought too late, voters are already relying on the process," warning that a change would risk leaving voters unable to timely resubmit applications and receive their absentee ballots.[1]

Laches sat alongside dismissals for lack of standing, lack of jurisdiction, untimeliness, and even public-health considerations, which together accounted for 62 of the 92 lawsuits specifically dealing with the 2020 election.[1] Attorney [[robert-barnes|Robert Barnes]] captured the sequence of procedural obstacles in a formulation quoted by [[rachel-alexander|Rachel Alexander]]: "Not Ripe in Spring, No Standing by Summer, Laches by Fall, and Moot by Winter."[1] Critics argue this pattern created a closed loop in which pre-election challenges were dismissed as premature and post-election challenges as too late.[1]
