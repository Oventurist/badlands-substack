# Specie Resumption Act

The **Specie Resumption Act** was the postwar law that ended the circulation of [[greenbacks|greenbacks]] and returned the United States currency to a gold basis. Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] dates the act to 1871 and describes it as the instrument by which the [[city-of-london|City of London]] and its American agents on Wall Street subverted American productive credit after the assassination of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]].[1]

According to Ehret's account, rather than imposing the full reconstruction of the defeated South that Lincoln had planned, the postwar political order permitted a new war to be waged against the greenbacks. The Specie Resumption Act killed the greenbacks and tied the republic's currency to gold, submitting the nation to London's speculative controls while contracting the means of credit available for large-scale, long-term infrastructure projects.[1]

Historian [[anton-chaitkin|Anton Chaitkin]] wrote that immediately after the President's assassination the Anglo-American financier faction ramped up their attack on Lincoln's national-sovereign economic policy, demanding "honest money" and clamoring for the "accursed greenbacks" to be taken quickly out of circulation so that gold could be restored to its throne. Chaitkin argued that shrinking the money supply through specie resumption would sharply reduce consumer demand, bringing hard times to workers, farmers and businessmen, but that America might thereby be stopped from what the financiers viewed as its dangerous race to an unnaturally high place in the world order.[1]
