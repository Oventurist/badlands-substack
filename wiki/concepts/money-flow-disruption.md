# Money Flow Disruption

**Money flow disruption** is a term drawn from Q drop #119, posted on November 6, 2017, and used in Badlands Media commentary to describe the deliberate interruption or diversion of established financial streams that allegedly sustain a global elite network.[1] In the framing advanced by Joe Lange, disruption is ordinarily destructive to a business, but becomes a strategic weapon when directed against "a cabal of elites who have controlled the global financial debt system."[1]

## The thesis

The argument holds that the wealth of the world has been continually stolen through a debt-based financial system, and that **fear** is the emotional instrument used to secure public consent for that theft.[1] Fear of foreign enemies is said to drive populations to support wars, which the article characterizes as "massive money laundering operations and theft of tax payers money."[1] Under this reading, the Middle East was deliberately converted into a war zone by [[central-intelligence-agency|the CIA]] and an entrenched shadow government, using fear of [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] as "the next Hitler" and fear of terrorism as the mobilizing narratives.[1] Both Saddam and Iran's proxy networks are described as CIA creations serving that purpose.[1]

## Q drop #119

Q drop #119 asked "What is money flow disruption?" and followed with the instruction "List the billionaires."[1] The drop went on to reference family histories predating the First and Second World Wars, to ask why the [[george-hw-bush|Bush]] family had recently broken its silence to attack the sitting president, and to pose the question "Coincidence pre SA arrests?"[1] The drop was published the day after the arrest of [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] in Saudi Arabia, a sequence the article treats as causally significant rather than coincidental.[1]

Further sections of the same drop asked "Who audits the billions paid for war?", "Where do the funds go?" and "Offshore?"[1] The article answers the first question with a single word — nobody — and argues that the absence of auditing is a designed feature rather than an oversight failure, concealing theft measured in trillions of dollars and loaded as debt onto successive generations of taxpayers.[1] Recipients of the diverted funds are said to include individuals, corporate entities, NGOs, institutes and foundations, routed through offshore accounts.[1]

## Application

The concept is applied to a series of specific mechanisms: the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] banking network as an off-books CIA funding channel, settlement payouts to third-party organizations terminated by Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]], pallets of cash shipped by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] to Baghdad, and weapons trafficking under [[operation-fast-and-furious|Operation Fast and Furious]].[1] Each is presented as a stream that the incoming [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration sought to close.[1] The arrest of Prince Alwaleed is characterized as the single most consequential act of disruption, severing funding to American politicians who promoted Middle Eastern wars and to proxy forces such as [[hezbollah]].[1]

The article summarizes the underlying accusation in the Q formulation "WAR = scam (trillions)," together with the assertion that "The US taxpayer is FUNDING the very people we are engaged in taking down. Slush funds everywhere."[1]

## Assessment

These claims originate in Q-adjacent commentary and are presented as interpretation rather than documented finding. Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors.[1]


## Chapter 18 reading of drop #119

Chapter 18 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series treats Q drop #119's opening reference to money flow disruption, paired with the instruction to list the billionaires, as evidence that the wealthiest people in the world as of 2017 were being targeted by white hats and potentially having funds frozen.[2] The author expects that such actions would not become publicly known immediately, reads the drop as indicating that these figures' income streams will be greatly diminished in future, and also takes it as a nod toward Bitcoin.[2] The same drop's questions about who audits war spending are answered with the claim that effectively no one does, the system being too tangled for anyone to trace the money.[2] Q's instruction to "reconcile" is read as pointing to officeholders whose wealth exceeds their lawful income, who are therefore presumed to be on the take.[2]
