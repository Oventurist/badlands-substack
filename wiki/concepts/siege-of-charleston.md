# Siege of Charleston

The Siege of Charleston was the British operation of spring 1780 that captured South Carolina's principal city and, with it, an entire American army — the event that opened the southern campaign of the [[american-revolution|American Revolution]] and set the stage for the partisan war of [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]].[1]

## Course and outcome

Rumor of the invasion reached Charleston society while [[henry-clinton|Sir Henry Clinton]] was landing an enormous army thirty miles south of the city, in the direction of Savannah, which the British had taken six months earlier.[1] The article portrays the city's elite as unconcerned, absorbed in the gratification of the moment even as a conquering force approached — the setting for Marion's decision to leap from a second-storey window and abandon the [[continental-army|Continental Army]] social world.[1]

When the city fell, the British trapped a five-thousand-man Continental Army inside; its soldiers spent the remainder of the war on prison ships.[1] A depiction of the siege by Alfonzo Chappel (1862) illustrates the article.[1] As the siege opened, Clinton ordered [[banastre-tarleton|Banastre Tarleton]] to sever all escape routes and ride down fleeing units, an order that produced the [[waxhaws-massacre|Waxhaws massacre]].[1]

Marion, hobbled by his broken ankle, escaped the city with the help of Buddy, a former slave he had freed, just as the British arrived and took control; the two spent the following months evading capture in the backcountry and recruiting militants.[1]
