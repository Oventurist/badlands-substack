# Free Speech Protection Act

The Free Speech Protection Act is legislation introduced by Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] of Kentucky as part of a Republican effort to curb federal collaboration with social media platforms on content moderation.[1]

## Background

The bill emerged from the controversy over the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] (CISA), a component of the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] that a Fifth Circuit federal appeals court found had likely violated the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] by telling social media platforms how to moderate content.[1] Republicans on the [[senate-homeland-security-committee|Senate Homeland Security Committee]] pushed a package of measures to address CISA's ability to collaborate with tech companies to censor content, introducing bills to mandate transparency and accountability regarding the agency's activities.[1]

## Paul's rationale

Paul told the Daily Caller News Foundation that "CISA has blatantly violated the First Amendment and colluded with big tech to censor the speech of ordinary Americans," and that although federal courts had ordered the agency to stop, "the trust CISA has abused cannot be restored until the agency gives a full accounting of what it has done, and Congress changes the law to create severe penalties for anyone who tries to do the same thing in the future."[1] He also said he would not back cybersecurity bills until CISA earned back Americans' trust, a stance reported to have stymied cyber legislation in the Senate.[1]
