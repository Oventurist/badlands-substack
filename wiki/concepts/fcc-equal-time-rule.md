# FCC Equal-Time Rule

The equal-time rule is a [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] broadcast regulation requiring licensed broadcasters that give airtime to one legally qualified political candidate to make comparable time available to that candidate's opponents.[1]

## 2026 Late Show controversy

The rule drew renewed public attention in February 2026 when [[stephen-colbert|Stephen Colbert]] criticized the FCC as "bullies" on his [[cbs|CBS]] program and said network lawyers had told him "in no uncertain terms" that Rep. [[james-talarico|James Talarico]] could not appear on the broadcast.[1] CBS responded that the show had not been prohibited from airing the interview, but had been "provided legal guidance that the broadcast could trigger the FCC equal-time rule for two other candidates, including Rep. [[jasmine-crockett|Jasmine Crockett]] [D-Texas]," and had been presented with options for satisfying the equal-time obligation.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] that equal-time regulations "have always been a thing" but simply "weren't enforced by the FCC during the age of Trump," and that broadcasters accepting government protections must accept the accompanying rules.[1] She added that the rules "were made for a different time," contending that broadcast networks persist chiefly because of government protections and pharmaceutical advertising.[1]
