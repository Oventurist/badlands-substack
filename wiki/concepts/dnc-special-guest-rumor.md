# DNC Special Guest Rumor

The DNC special guest rumor was a viral media speculation cycle preceding the final night of the [[2024-democratic-national-convention|2024 Democratic National Convention]], in which social media was abuzz on Thursday, August 22, 2024, over reports that an unnamed "special guest" would appear.[1] Coverage afterward characterized the episode as a con job likely designed to maximize viewership for [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s acceptance speech — and concluded that it probably worked.[1]

## Speculation

Speculation quickly centered on [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]], with fans tracking her plane to see whether she would be in the area.[1] Former CNN host [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] claimed that sources had told him the guest would be either George W. Bush, [[beyonce|Beyoncé]], or Swift.[1] Commentators noted that a Bush appearance made little sense, and a convention insider told Deadline that although Swift is beloved, her presence would overshadow Harris's acceptance speech and therefore would not benefit the campaign.[1]

Attention then shifted to Beyoncé as the most likely candidate, and TMZ went so far as to report the appearance as confirmed.[1] A representative for Beyoncé subsequently shot the rumor down, telling The Hollywood Reporter that "Beyoncé was never scheduled to be there" and that "the report of a performance is untrue."[1] The special guest turned out to be Harris herself.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentary held that rumors of a free Swift or Beyoncé concert brought in many viewers who would otherwise never tune into a political speech, describing the episode as a "big lie" deployed for ratings.[1] The convention as a whole was characterized as cringe and creepy: the low-budget set was read as implying that funds had gone toward paying attendees, and the widely promoted theme of "joy" was said to have come through clenched teeth and forced talking points.[1] The writer described crowd shots as giving off "two minutes of hate" vibes recast as "ragey joy," and framed the sequence as an escalation from Harris crashing concerts to appropriate their crowds to spreading false rumors of concerts to boost ratings, concluding that there was "nothing authentic about this woman or her manufactured popularity."[1]
