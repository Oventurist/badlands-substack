# Hegseth Sexual Assault Allegation

The Hegseth sexual assault allegation concerns a 2017 incident involving [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], then a Fox News weekend host and later President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee for secretary of defense, which resurfaced publicly during the November 2024 confirmation cycle.[1]

## The lawyer's account

Hegseth's attorney [[timothy-parlatore|Tim Parlatore]] told Breitbart News in an exclusive interview in November 2024 that the allegations, dating from seven years earlier, had been "completely investigated" at the time and that his client is "completely and totally innocent."[1] Parlatore stated flatly, "He is completely and totally innocent. It was completely investigated. She was the aggressor."[1]

According to Parlatore's account, Hegseth had been invited in 2017 to speak at a California Federation of Republican Women conference in Monterey.[1] After the event he was at a bar with organizers, where he had "too many drinks," and the accuser — described by Parlatore as sober at the time — took him back to his room.[1] Parlatore said that "by all accounts, both video and eyewitness, she was sober. He was drunk. She grabbed him. She took him to his room," and asserted that police reviewing the video considered charging the woman who had made the report, though nothing came of it because, in his words, "Pete's not gonna sit there and say, 'I was raped.'"[1]

The incident occurred at the height of the [[metoo-movement|Me Too movement]], when numerous high-profile men in media faced allegations of sexual assault and harassment.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media framed the story as the third narrative attack on Hegseth's nomination during what it called confirmation season.[1] The outlet said Hegseth had first been dismissed as "that Fox News weekend anchor" lacking requisite experience — a line it argued was preempted by his verifiable service record and his appearance on [[shawn-ryan|Shawn Ryan]]'s podcast — then attacked over his Jerusalem Cross tattoo, which some critics likened to a swastika, and finally targeted with a Me Too allegation.[1]

The commentary asserted that the accuser is already on record in a legal proceeding and, per Hegseth's lawyer, may be in violation of a prior legal agreement, arguing she should be prosecuted if so.[1] It concluded that resorting to a Me Too attack this early in the process indicated opponents had nothing substantive with which to sink the nomination, and predicted Hegseth would be confirmed.[1]
