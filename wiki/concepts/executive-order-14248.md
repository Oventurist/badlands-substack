# Executive Order 14248

Executive Order No. 14248 is an order signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on March 25, 2025, addressing federal guidelines, testing, and certification of voting systems in the United States.[1] The order required voting systems to "provide a voter-verifiable paper record to prevent fraud or mistake" and requested that all states assist in determining whether individuals are eligible to register to vote.[1] It charged the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] with taking "appropriate action" against states that failed to comply.[1]

## Enforcement through voter-list litigation

On September 25, 2025, the Justice Department announced that it was suing six states — California, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, New Hampshire, and Pennsylvania — for failing to produce their statewide voter registration lists after DOJ requested them.[1] The identical lawsuits followed responses in which each state provided only publicly available voter registration information rather than the more detailed records sought, which would have included driver's license and Social Security information of registrants.[1]

Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pamela Bondi]] said "clean voter rolls are the foundation of free and fair elections," adding that "every state has a responsibility to ensure that voter registration records are accurate, accessible, and secure — states that don't fulfill that obligation will see this Department of Justice in court."[1] The suits were described as part of the implementation of the executive order.[1]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America framed the litigation as one part of a wider shift on the election front, pointing to defunding across the bureaucracy — notably the [[multi-state-information-sharing-and-analysis-center|Multi-State Information Sharing and Analysis Center]], which could no longer provide free cybersecurity monitoring to every local jurisdiction and was soliciting membership fees instead.[1]
