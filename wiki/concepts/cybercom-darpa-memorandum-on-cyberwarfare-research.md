# CYBERCOM–DARPA Memorandum on Cyberwarfare Research

In May 2024, [[us-cyber-command|U.S. Cyber Command]] and the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency ([[darpa|DARPA]]) signed a binding memorandum creating pathways to accelerate research and development of advanced cyberwarfare technologies within the Department of Defense.[1]

## Terms

The agreement, announced by the two Pentagon agencies, establishes budgets, roles and governance structures intended to move cyber technologies swiftly "from the laboratory to the cyber battlefield."[1] It positions DARPA as the frontrunner entity supplying projects for loading into CYBERCOM's software suite.[1] Research and development programs would be selected by DARPA and executed by the Orion Consortium, a joint group comprising DARPA contributors and CYBERCOM engineers.[1]

The memorandum builds on the 2022 establishment of Constellation, the cornerstone pilot program focused on transmitting advanced hacking capabilities to American cyberspace combatants.[1]

## Statements

CYBERCOM deputy commander Lt. Gen. William Hartman described the arrangement during a keynote at the RSA Conference in San Francisco as "the rapid expansion of the art of the possible by partnering directly with the people persistently pushing the technological beyond its limits, conversely informing and challenging those advancements through an operational lens to maximize the balance between the art and science of cyber in support of national security."[1]

## Context

CYBERCOM is one of several unified combatant commands drawing staff from multiple service branches. According to April 2024 testimony to a Senate panel by command leader Lt. Gen. Timothy Haugh, it deployed cyber personnel on "hunt forward" missions 22 times to 17 countries during 2023 to disable cyber threats across global networks.[1]
