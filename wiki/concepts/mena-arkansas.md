# Mena, Arkansas

**Mena, Arkansas** is a small town in the Ouachita Mountains that in the 1980s became, in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], "a dark stage for a twisted drama involving drugs, guns, and government shadow games."[1]

## The airport operation

Mena Intermountain Municipal Airport, a modest airstrip ringed by pine forest and largely out of public view, allegedly became a hub where covert operators turned the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]] into a funding stream for their Contra project.[1] The pilot [[barry-seal|Barry Seal]] set up at Rich Mountain Aviation on the field in 1981 after smuggling for the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín Cartel]], and the airstrip became the centre of his operation.[1] Seal's aircraft was later tied to [[oliver-north|Oliver North]]'s Contra resupply network, drawing Mena into the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] scandal after a 1986 crash in Nicaragua.[1]

## Political cover and investigations

Then-Governor [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] is described as content to look the other way while the operation ran, leaving local residents to whisper among themselves — sometimes, DeLarme notes, at their own peril, a reference to the deaths documented in Mara Leveritt's *The Boys on the Tracks*.[1]

Arkansas State Police investigator Russell Welch and [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] agent Bill Duncan dug into Seal's operation and uncovered what DeLarme calls a swamp of drug smuggling and money laundering.[1] An FBI memorandum from 1986, later cited in a 1991 congressional hearing by Representative Bill Alexander, estimated that between $3 billion and $5 billion in cocaine had moved through the airport.[1] Declassified CIA material on Mena, including the 1996 CIA Inspector General report, was released through [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] in 2020.[1]

DeLarme concludes that the Mena story is too deep to be fully covered in a single article, but that the surviving record supplies more than enough dots to connect.[1]
