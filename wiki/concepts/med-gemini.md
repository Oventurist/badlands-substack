# Med-Gemini

Med-Gemini is [[google|Google]]'s healthcare-oriented artificial intelligence model, a medical adaptation of the company's [[google-gemini|Gemini]] family designed to interpret clinical material including medical imaging.[1]

## The "basilar ganglia" hallucination

A 2024 research paper introducing Med-Gemini contained a fabricated anatomical term in a section on head CT scans: the model reported a problem with the "basilar ganglia," conflating the basal ganglia — the brain region governing motor control, learning and emotional processing — with the basilar artery, which supplies blood to the brainstem.[1] No such structure as the "basilar ganglia" exists.[1]

Nobody at Google caught the error, either in the paper itself or in the accompanying blog post announcing the model.[1] The distinction matters clinically because a stroke or abnormality in the basal ganglia is typically treated very differently from one involving the basilar artery.[1] Reporting on the incident framed the central risk as automation bias: a radiologist would know not to confuse the two terms, but a clinician relying on an AI reading might not catch a plausible-sounding invented diagnosis.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America used the episode to argue that American "sick care" has been transformed into a demand-driven industry, noting that healthcare is now the number one employer in most states — a comparatively recent development — and dating the inflection to the intensification of pharmaceutical marketing in 1997.[1] The commentary posed the open question of what impact AI systems such as Med-Gemini will have on those trends.[1]
