# National Rifle Association of America v. Vullo

*National Rifle Association of America v. Vullo* is a unanimous decision of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] holding that government officials may not coerce private parties in order to punish or suppress views the government disfavors.[1] Decided in 2024, the case acquired renewed prominence in September 2025 amid debate over pressure on broadcasters by the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]].[1]

## Background

The [[national-rifle-association|National Rifle Association]] sued the New York Department of Financial Services, alleging that its superintendent, Maria Vullo, had violated the First Amendment by coercing DFS-regulated insurance companies and banks away from doing business with the NRA, in a bid to punish or suppress the group's gun-rights advocacy.[1]

## Holding

Writing for the Court, Justice [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] stated: "Six decades ago, this Court held that a government entity's 'threat of invoking legal sanctions and other means of coercion' against a third party 'to achieve the suppression' of disfavored speech violates the First Amendment. Today, the Court reaffirms what it said then: Government officials cannot attempt to coerce private parties in order to punish or suppress views that the government disfavors."[1]

## Relevance to FCC broadcast pressure

Suggestions by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and FCC Chairman [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] that broadcaster licenses could be revoked over disfavored views expressed on air were reported as potentially running afoul of the *Vullo* holding, which observers described as bearing striking similarities to the broadcast-licensing debate.[1] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] took a more sweeping position, arguing that the FCC is unconstitutional and should not exist, that the federal government does not own the airwaves, and that the agency has historically served the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] rather than the public.[1]
