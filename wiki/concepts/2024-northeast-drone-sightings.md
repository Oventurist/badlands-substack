# 2024 Northeast Drone Sightings

In December 2024, swarms of unidentified drones appeared over the New York–New Jersey tri-state region, alarming residents and baffling experts, and prompting federal action.[1]

## Federal response

New York Senator [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] called on Sunday, December 15, 2024, for the federal government to deploy recently declassified radar technology to identify the mysterious swarms.[1] The Senate Democratic leader spoke to mounting public frustration shortly after Governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]] announced that the federal government was finally "deploying a state-of-the-art drone detection system" to New York.[1] Hochul did not elaborate on what the system was, or how and where it would be used, but stressed that the region still needed more help.[1]

Schumer specifically urged the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to send the Robin, a 360-degree radar able to identify drones quickly and track them back to their landing zones.[1] "New Yorkers have tremendous questions about [the drones]. We are going to get the answers," Schumer said, adding: "You should not have to shake an eight ball to see what it is. There ought to be better technology — and there is. We need it here in New York."[1] He said he had told Homeland Security and the FBI that answers were needed quickly.[1] The recently declassified Robin radar, Schumer noted, can be mounted on another drone or an airplane and "can go anywhere" to trace the unidentified flying objects.[1]

## The "nuclear sniffer" theory

A competing explanation gained traction in mid-December 2024 when [[john-ferguson|John Ferguson]], founder of the military drone manufacturer [[saxon-aerospace|Saxon Aerospace]], published a video on TikTok linking the sightings to elevated radiation readings around New York City.[2] Ferguson recounted a conversation with a man who claimed to have handled a nuclear warhead left over from Ukraine and who believed the device was headed for the United States, and asserted that "everyone knows that this administration is pushing to get into war with Russia."[2] He drew a parallel with earlier unexplained drone flights along Interstate 70 from Colorado through Nebraska, Kansas and Missouri, which were believed at the time to be a search for radioactive material missing in the United States.[2]

Ferguson's technical argument was that drones "have no reason to fly at night" because mapping requires daylight unless thermal optics are fitted, and therefore that low, nocturnal flights indicated sensing rather than surveillance: the craft were "not nefarious in intent" but were "trying to smell something on the ground," whether gas leaks or radioactive material.[2] The theory was reported alongside jokes that observers now wished Paul Krugman's alien-invasion speculation had been correct instead.[2]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the drone panic as an instance of what he termed "Shatterpoints" — psycho-temporal convergence points at which the energy required to sustain cognitive dissonance in support of the central narrative is overwhelmed by logic, forcing a shattering in the observing mind at both individual and societal scales.[2] In his reading, the very idiocy of the official narrative is a feature of the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] rather than a bug, and the Fear Narrative must grow increasingly absurd to remain jarring to audiences already inoculated against catastrophism.[2] He pointed to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ability to meme about drone warfare while media and military institutions treated the threat with total gravity, asking readers whether they were watching a comedy or a horror movie.[2]
