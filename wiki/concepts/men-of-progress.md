# Men of Progress

*Men of Progress* is an 1862 group portrait by Christian Schussele, a European painter who emigrated to the United States and became a cultural leader in Pennsylvania and New York during the Civil War.[1] The painting assembles an array of American scientists and inventors and serves as the header and organising image of Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] on cultural warfare in the early republic.[1]

## Subjects

Figures depicted include William Thomas Green Morton (surgical anesthesia), James Bogardus (cast-iron construction), Samuel Colt (the revolving pistol), Cyrus Hall McCormick (the mechanical reaper), Joseph Saxton (the coal-burning stove and hydrometer), and [[joseph-henry|Joseph Henry]], the father of electromagnetism in America, shown with his elbow near a pillar at the rear; a painting-within-the-painting features [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]].[1] At the centre, with his right hand on the table beside a telegraph apparatus, stands [[samuel-morse|Samuel Morse]].[1]

## Interpretation

Ehret argues the picture showcases the paradox within contemporary America being exploited by the nation's historic enemies to destroy it from within.[1] Painted in the year of an existential civil war, it points to the unresolved tension between the unalienable rights of the individual as set out in the Declaration of Independence of 1776 and the principle of the general welfare set out in the Constitution of 1787 — a conflict the article says was only partly resolved in the fires of the war and is being reinflamed today.[1] In that context [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] was denounced as "a dictator and a tyrant" by partisans of the Lost Cause school, who omit that the rights he threatened were the rights of citizens to own other humans as property.[1] Ehret contends the solution to the paradox lies in the domain of culture, science, the arts and music.[1]
