# Union of States

The "union of states" is the term Badlands Media legal commentary uses to describe what the [[united-states-constitution|United States Constitution]] actually created, in contrast to the popular description of the United States as a nation.[1]

## Definition advanced

In "Is the United States a Nation?", [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] defines a union as "what you get when sovereign nation-states decide to delegate certain powers to a central authority whose action, legally, ought never extend beyond its enumerated powers."[1] The argument proceeds from the Constitution's preamble, which speaks of forming "a more perfect Union" and nowhere speaks of forming a new nation out of the independent American states.[1] If the United States were itself a nation, the article observes, it would have to be a nation of nations — a construction the author rejects.[1]

The conclusion offered is that an American resident lives in "a nation that has outsourced a small portion of its authority to the union."[1]

## Supporting authorities

The union reading is buttressed by several sources. [[blacks-law-dictionary|Black's Law Dictionary]], 4th Edition (1951), defines [[congress|Congress]] in international law as "an assembly of envoys... from different sovereignties," and its Revised 4th Edition (1968) distinguishes a national government from a federal one, the latter being "the government of a community of independent and sovereign states, united by compact."[1] Case law cited includes [[people-ex-rel-attorney-general-v-naglee|People ex rel. Attorney General v. Naglee]], holding each state possesses all the powers of an independent and sovereign nation except as ceded by the Constitution, and [[chisholm-v-georgia|Chisholm v. Georgia]], describing the several states as forming "a society, not a NATION."[1] The historical sequence — [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]] followed two years later by the [[articles-of-confederation|Articles of Confederation]] — is presented by [[gerald-brown|Gerald Brown]] as further proof that no single nation was ever constituted.[1]

The article concludes that the schoolbook claim that the United States is a democratic nation is "utterly false," and that social engineers benefit from the public never recovering the technical meanings of these words.[1]
