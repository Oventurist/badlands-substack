# January 2024 Stopgap Funding Bill

The January 2024 stopgap funding bill was a short-term appropriations measure passed by the United States [[congress|Congress]] on Thursday, January 18, 2024, to prevent a partial government shutdown that would otherwise have begun when funding expired at midnight on Friday. The measure extended federal funding through two staggered deadlines, March 1 and March 8, 2024.[1]

## Passage

The Democratic-led Senate approved the bill by a vote of 77–18 on final passage, after disposing of a small number of amendments. The Republican-led House followed shortly afterward, passing the measure 314–108, and sent it to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for signature before the funding lapse took effect.[1]

The vote exposed the continuing split within the House Republican conference: the bill passed with mostly Democratic votes, with 107 Republicans voting in favor and 106 voting against — an almost exactly even division of the majority party.[1]

## Context

The measure was the third stopgap bill enacted since September 2023, as a divided Congress repeatedly failed to agree on full-year appropriations legislation. A spending-level deal between Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-La.) and Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] (D-N.Y.) had renewed hope that the full-year process could be completed by the early-March deadlines, though right-wing House Republicans openly rebelled against that agreement, leaving completion far from guaranteed.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the recurring funding fights as an example of what [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] calls "reruns" — repeated narrative deployments recycled through the news cycle. Burning Bright argued that the public has been saturated with congressional complaints about a lack of funding even as the same body demonstrates why many Americans believe the money would be better withheld.[1]

The commentary emphasized a pattern: the funding "ALWAYS seems to arrive just in the nick of time," which Burning Bright read as evidence that audiences are watching the narrative destruction of the "Old Guard" without the actual disruptive effects of a shutdown being imposed on a fragile American system.[1]
