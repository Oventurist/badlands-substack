# Massachusetts Driver's Licenses for Illegal Aliens

The Massachusetts driver's license law is a state statute, approved by voters in a statewide ballot initiative in late 2022 and effective July 1, 2023, that extends driving privileges to persons present in the United States illegally.[1] With its passage Massachusetts became the 17th state to provide driver's licenses to illegal aliens, joining California, New York, Colorado and Connecticut among others.[1]

## Implementation and demand

Data from the state's [[massachusetts-registry-of-motor-vehicles|Registry of Motor Vehicles]], published by Chris Van Buskirk of the [[boston-herald|Boston Herald]], showed that roughly 100,000 requests for learner's permit appointments were placed in the period following the law's July 1, 2023 effective date.[1] Reporting indicated that the majority of those requests — tens of thousands — were likely from illegal aliens newly made eligible by the law.[1] State officials have said they expect close to 300,000 illegal aliens in Massachusetts to apply for driver's licenses within the law's first four years, and the implementation cost to taxpayers has been estimated at a minimum of $28 million.[1] The Registry hired 52 additional employees after the law took effect.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that millions of illegal aliens have crossed the border since [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] assumed the presidency, and that depending on the state they settle in they may qualify for driver's licenses.[1] She argued that the 17 states extending driving privileges to illegal aliens are thereby in violation of federal law while also generating additional bureaucracy, and that government grows with every law passed.[1]

Drawing on her own state of Colorado, which permits illegal aliens to hold driver's licenses, she pointed to the interaction with motor-voter registration, under which a driver's license registration creates a voter registration record.[1] While officials claim controls exist to prevent illegal aliens from voting, she noted that in the two preceding elections the Colorado secretary of state had "accidentally" sent election mail to tens of thousands of ineligible voters, and that voter roll hygiene in Colorado was poor enough that state leadership agreed to report cleanup efforts to [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] as part of the Judicial Watch v. [[jena-griswold|Griswold]] et al. settlement earlier that year.[1] Her conclusion was that the Massachusetts law, like Colorado's, creates bureaucratic complexity that erodes the integrity of American institutions — and that this is the intended effect, which she summarised as "Welcome to the Open Society."[1]
