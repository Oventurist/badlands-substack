# 2025 Federal Restrictions on Pediatric Gender-Affirming Care

On Thursday, December 18, 2025, health officials in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] announced measures that would have the effect of essentially banning gender-affirming care for transgender young people, even in states where such care remains legal.[1]

## Announcement

Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and Dr. [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]], who leads [[centers-for-medicare-and-medicaid-services|Medicaid and Medicare]], announced the measures at a press conference at the headquarters of the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] in Washington, D.C.[1] "So-called gender affirming care has inflicted lasting physical and psychological damage on vulnerable young people," Kennedy said. "This is not medicine. It is malpractice."[1]

## Mechanism

The restriction takes the form of two new proposed rules from Medicaid and Medicare.[1] The first prohibits doctors and hospitals from receiving federal Medicaid reimbursement for gender-affirming care provided to transgender patients younger than 18; the second blocks all Medicaid and Medicare funding for any services at hospitals that provide pediatric gender-affirming care.[1]

## Reaction

The [[american-academy-of-pediatrics|American Academy of Pediatrics]] pushed back strongly, with AAP President Dr. Susan J. Kressly saying that "These policies and proposals misconstrue the current medical consensus and fail to reflect the realities of pediatric care and the needs of children and families."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the policy a righteous cause, arguing that children cannot consent to permanently altering their bodies psychologically, chemically or surgically, and that parents should not be permitted to consent on their behalf either — suggesting that many mothers of trans-identifying children present with Munchausen syndrome by proxy.[1] She characterized "gender affirming care for minors" as the sexualization, sterilization, mutilation and psychological torture of children, praised the administration's courage in ending it, and called for prosecution of the American Academy of Pediatrics for opposing the measures.[1]
