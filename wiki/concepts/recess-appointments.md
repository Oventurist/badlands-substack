# Recess Appointments

Recess appointments are executive appointments made while the U.S. Senate is out of session, allowing a president to install nominees without the ordinary process of Senate confirmation. The mechanism became a central point of contention in the days following the November 2024 election, when President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] pressed Senate Republicans to commit to using it for his Cabinet nominees.[1]

## The November 2024 push

Trump took to social media to issue orders to Senate Republicans running in that week's majority leader election, demanding that they endorse recess appointments for his Cabinet nominees; all three candidates quickly signaled they were open to the idea.[1] CNN framed the episode as evidence that the president-elect was already flexing raw power before taking office, showing he might try to subvert Washington's checks and balances, and that he intended to rule a GOP monopoly on power with unchallenged authority — treating [[us-congress|Congress]] as a rubber stamp rather than a separate, co-equal branch of government.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the mandate delivered by voters — who gave Trump both the Electoral College and the popular vote — required that bureaucratic obstruction be neutralized, and that "the privately-incentivized legislature cannot be allowed to interfere with making America great again."[1] She wrote that the post-election shock among mainstream analysts had been replaced with panic and dread, and that CNN's warnings that Trump would "act with maximum force" read to the Truth Community as a promise rather than a threat.[1]

She also noted that while the public square argued about recess appointments and efforts to obstruct the incoming administration's agenda, Trump was moving quickly to place personnel able to execute on day one — pointing to border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], who reportedly would not require Senate confirmation because his role sat within the [[white-house|White House]] rather than at the head of a department.[1]
