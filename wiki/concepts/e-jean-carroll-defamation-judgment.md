# E. Jean Carroll Defamation Judgment

The E. Jean Carroll defamation judgment is the $83.3 million civil award entered against [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] in favor of writer [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]], and one of the highest-profile civil cases connected to Trump's post-presidency years.[1]

## Appellate stay

In May 2026 a federal appeals court temporarily halted Carroll from collecting the judgment while Trump's appeal proceeded. The ruling paused enforcement of the payout as appellate judges reviewed challenges tied to the underlying trial, the damages calculation, and the legal procedures used in the case.[1]

## Positions of the parties

Trump has continued denying Carroll's allegations and argues the verdicts were politically motivated and legally flawed. Carroll's legal team has sought to preserve the judgment and defend the jury's findings.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the stay as good for Trump and, if the verdict is ultimately overturned, good for justice generally, describing the trial as an egregious abuse of process and power.[1] She questioned the coherence of Carroll's account and criticized presiding judge Lewis Kaplan as a political actor, arguing that the appeal "must go all the way" because the process will be weaponized again absent consequences for those who participated.[1]

## May 2023 jury verdict

The litigation's first verdict came on May 9, 2023, when a New York City jury in U.S. District Court in lower Manhattan found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation but not for rape, after less than three hours of deliberation.[2] The jury awarded [[e-jean-carroll|Carroll]] $20,000 in punitive damages on a battery claim and roughly $5 million in compensatory damages for defamation.[2] Trump, who had long denied the allegation dating to the mid-1990s, accused Carroll of using false claims to promote her book, having told The Hill at the [[white-house|White House]] in June 2019: "I'll say it with great respect: Number one, she's not my type. Number two, it never happened."[2] Trump said he would appeal, calling the outcome "an affront to justice."[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump's demeanor toward the saga seemed "rather cavalier," and placed the verdict on the heels of what he called the most obvious [[lawfare|lawfare]] campaign waged against a former U.S. head of state, the New York case brought by [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]].[2] He argued the American public was being primed for former heads of state to be arraigned in criminal and civil trials, that "[they] are losing every last morsel of Narrative Shielding they've enjoyed as untouchables," and asked what would happen "when the real trials start" and "the real crimes (including sex crimes) are revealed."[2]

## Supreme Court petition (November 2025)

On Monday, November 10, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to throw out the jury verdict finding him liable for sexually abusing the writer [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] in the mid-1990s, according to his personal legal team.[3] Carroll had taken Trump to trial twice and won a total of $88.3 million in damages, and the petition marked the first time the long-running litigation reached the justices.[3] In 2023 the first jury had found Trump liable for sexually abusing Carroll in a Manhattan department store dressing room in the mid-1990s and for defaming her by denying her account when she came forward during his first presidency, ordering him to pay $5 million.[3] Trump's 33-page petition characterized Carroll's sexual assault claims as "facially implausible, politically motivated allegations" and urged the justices to intervene to reverse several evidentiary rulings that it said tainted the trial.[3]

## January 2024 damages trial

The damages-only trial in [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]]'s suit against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Southern District of New York (1:20-cv-07311), before Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]], was scheduled to begin on January 16, 2024 - the day after the [[2024-iowa-caucus|Iowa Caucuses]] on January 15.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the juxtaposition of the trial calendar with the primary calendar as evidence that the proceedings functioned as [[lawfare|lawfare]] entangled with the 2024 election.[4]
