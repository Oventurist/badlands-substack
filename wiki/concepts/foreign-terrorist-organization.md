# Foreign Terrorist Organization designation

A Foreign Terrorist Organization (FTO) designation is a United States government label applied by the [[state-department|State Department]] that layers terrorism-related sanctions onto a named group.[1]

## Use against Cartel de los Soles

On Monday, November 24, 2025, the United States formally designated Venezuela's [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel de los Soles]] as a foreign terrorist organization, adding terrorism-related sanctions to a network Washington says includes President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] and other high-ranking Venezuelan officials.[1] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had announced earlier in November that the designation would proceed because of the network's alleged role in importing illegal drugs into the United States.[1] Venezuela's government rejected the move as a "ridiculous" plan to designate a "non-existent" group.[1]

The designation came amid President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s military buildup in the Caribbean, raising concerns that it might be used to justify military action against Venezuela; sanctions experts, however, said that the statute underlying the designation does not authorize such a move.[1]

## Designation of Mexican cartels (January 2025)

On Monday, January 20, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order designating Mexican cartels and other criminal organizations as foreign terrorist organizations, a measure intended to apply maximum pressure on Mexico to rein in its drug trade.[2] More generally, the designation gave the administration greater power to impose economic penalties and travel restrictions, and potentially even to take military action in foreign countries.[2]

The [[new-york-times|New York Times]] reported that former government officials and analysts believed the order could force some American companies to forgo doing business in Mexico rather than risk U.S. sanctions — an outcome with major consequences given the two countries' deep economic interdependence and Mexico's status as the United States' largest trading partner in goods, with many American firms operating manufacturing there.[2] The paper argued that disentangling cartel operations from U.S. interests would be immensely complicated because the criminal networks had extended beyond drug trafficking and human smuggling into a wide swath of the legal economy, "from avocado farming to the country's billion-dollar tourism industry."[2]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] ridiculed the avocado framing and dismissed the Times as a [[cia|CIA]] asset, citing a 2017 Doha presentation by [[kevin-chalker|Kevin Chalker]] and [[global-risk-advisors|Global Risk Advisors]] that named the paper as a Muslim Brotherhood media asset.[2] He pointed to the [[military-commissions-act-of-2006|Military Commissions Act of 2006]], which defined "unlawful enemy combatant," noting that anyone providing material support to such a combatant risks the same designation, and recalled 1970s congressional investigations documenting CIA entanglement with drug cartels in Central and South America.[2]

## Revocation for Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, the United States formally revoked the foreign terrorist organization designation of Syria's ruling [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS), nearly two months after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met the group's leader, Syria's self-declared interim president [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmad al-Sharaa]], formerly known as Abu Mohammad al-Jolani.[3] *ZeroHedge* called it not merely ironic but scandalous that Trump had met a US-designated terrorist during his Gulf tour in Saudi Arabia, arguing that an ordinary American citizen doing the same would likely be investigated and prosecuted by the FBI, and noted that Sharaa was the "former" al-Qaeda in Syria figure who helped overthrow [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[3] The State Department memo effecting the revocation was signed by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and read: "In consultation with the Attorney General and the Secretary of the Treasury, I hereby revoke the designation of al-Nusra Front, also known as Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the Jolani/Syria situation one of the bigger head-scratchers of 2025, describing the spectacle of the Trump administration and the GOP anointing a "head-chopping jihadi" as a great national leader as almost cartoonish, and speculated either that Jolani had always been a well-trained television actor now under new management, or that he was a legitimate operator deployed by the CIA/Mossad who had simply been offered a better deal by Trump and the Saudis.[3]
