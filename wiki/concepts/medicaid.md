# Medicaid

**Medicaid** is a United States health coverage program jointly funded by the federal government and the individual states, providing insurance to tens of millions of Americans.[1] Because eligibility determination, enrollment, and payment administration are handled at the state level while a large share of the money is federal, the program has become a recurring focus of congressional fraud and improper-payment oversight.[1]

## 2026 fraud investigation

In March 2026 the [[house-energy-and-commerce-committee|US House Energy and Commerce Committee]] announced that it was expanding an investigation into potential Medicaid fraud and improper payments to cover ten states — New York, California, Colorado, Massachusetts, Maine, Nebraska, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Vermont, and Washington State.[1] Committee members requested records from state Medicaid agencies concerning eligibility verification, enrollment procedures, and improper payments, with the stated aim of determining whether states were adequately preventing fraud, waste, and abuse.[1]

## Narrative significance

Within Badlands commentary the Medicaid probe is read as part of a wider accountability arc that began with fraud cases in Minnesota and is expected to spread to other states with long-standing single-party control.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the limiting factor on exposure was not evidence but prosecutorial discretion — that elected prosecutors decline politically costly cases, and that the same discretionary logic protects election administration from scrutiny.[1] In her framing, restoring trustworthy elections would collapse the protective layer that allows other categories of fraud, including Medicaid fraud, to persist.[1]
