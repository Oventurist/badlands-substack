# United States v. Wong Kim Ark

United States v. Wong Kim Ark is the landmark [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] decision in which the Court held that children born in the United States to non-citizen parents are citizens under the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]]. In the Badlands corpus it is cited as the controlling precedent standing in the way of the Trump administration's effort to restrict birthright citizenship.

## Role in the 2026 birthright citizenship dispute

In its coverage of [[trump-v-barbara|Trump v. Barbara]], the Badlands Brief of April 2, 2026 identifies Wong Kim Ark as the key precedent in the challenge to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order limiting birthright citizenship. Lower federal courts that blocked the order and related efforts found that the Constitution's text and "long-standing precedent" — anchored in Wong Kim Ark — protect birthright citizenship regardless of the parents' immigration status.

Legal analysts cited in the coverage indicated that overturning or narrowing the decision would require the Court to reinterpret more than a century of constitutional law. The Brief observes that the current Court's conservative majority has shown willingness to revisit precedent in other areas, but that immigration and constitutional-text cases have produced mixed outcomes, leaving the fate of the precedent open.
