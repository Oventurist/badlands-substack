# 2024 US-Congo Gertler Sanctions Deal

In June 2024 the *[[financial-times|Financial Times]]* reported a controversial U.S. plan under which sanctioned Israeli mining billionaire [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] would receive hundreds of millions of dollars in exchange for selling his remaining mining interests in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]].[1]

## Terms reported

Gertler was sanctioned by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|US Treasury]] in 2017 over alleged corrupt dealing in Congo, but retained lucrative royalty streams from three mining projects there. Under the proposal, Treasury would grant him "special" licences to sell those three royalty streams back to the Congolese government and would eventually grant a "general" licence restoring his access to the U.S. financial system. The total was undetermined but U.S. officials expected it to be around $300 million, according to two people familiar with terms that had not previously been reported. Officials behind the proposal argued that removing Gertler from Congo would create more opportunities for U.S.-friendly companies to access copper and cobalt, both vital to clean-energy infrastructure buildout.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the article "some of the most shameless and brazen propaganda I've ever seen," attributing it to the *Financial Times* of London, which he described — crediting [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] — as "the paper of record for the Uniparty Establishment."[1] He recounted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had used [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] to seize Gertler's assets over maliciously acquired mineral rights in the DRC and the use of child slaves to mine diamonds, gold and cobalt, with proceeds allegedly disseminated to Israeli oligarchs.[1]

The commentary further asserted that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] had already coordinated to strip Gertler of some mineral rights, and cited years of investigative reporting alleging that Gertler was evading Trump-era sanctions through proxy companies used to launder money into accessible accounts, with some journalists saying Gertler and associates had personally threatened them.[1] On this reading the FT piece was a hollow attempt to explain away the restoration of seized assets and Gertler's presumed resumption of plundering Africa.[1]
