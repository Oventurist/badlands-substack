# Argo (film)

*Argo* is the Ben Affleck film dramatizing the [[iran-hostage-crisis|Iran Hostage Crisis]], described by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] as the means through which younger audiences typically learn about the 1979 seizure of American diplomats in Tehran.[1]

Carlson treats the film as an instrument of [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] narrative management in [[hollywood|Hollywood]], noting that he considers Affleck "one of the CIA's greatest assets in Hollywood" alongside Matt Damon and George Clooney, and that in his view the agency exerts wide control over the industry, including over writers and, in many cases, final approval of scripts.[1] In this account actors, directors, producers and writers are tasked with making the CIA look good — or at least less bad — and indispensable to democracy, while simultaneously altering public perception of history.[1]

The article places *Argo* within a list of films said to be produced to advance a CIA-preferred version of history, alongside *The Titanic*, *Schindler's List*, *Pearl Harbor*, *All the President's Men*, *Snowden* and *The Social Network*.[1] Carlson highlights the timing of two releases in particular: *Pearl Harbor* came out three months before 9/11, in his reading preparing audiences to accept the ensuing propaganda, and *Argo* appeared just before the CIA publicly admitted its involvement in the [[1953-iranian-coup|1953 Iranian coup]].[1] He stops short of Holocaust denial, stating explicitly that he is not claiming the Nazis were less than evil or that no one died in the Holocaust, only that "history didn't really happen exactly the way you saw it in a movie — any movie."[1]
