# Survey of Consumer Expectations

The Survey of Consumer Expectations is a monthly consumer survey published by the [[federal-reserve-bank-of-new-york|Federal Reserve Bank of New York]] that tracks American households' expectations for inflation, income growth, and their own ability to service debt.[1] Its one-year inflation expectation series is traditionally treated as a proxy for the price of oil.[1]

## September 2023 release

The September 2023 edition, published in October, showed the second consecutive increase in the one-year inflation expectation after several months of decline, rising slightly to 3.67 percent from the previous month's 3.63 percent.[1] Three-year-ahead expectations also reversed higher, bouncing from 2.79 percent in August to 3.00 percent in September after having fallen from 2.91 percent in July.[1] Five-year-ahead expectations declined from 3.00 percent to 2.84 percent, the lowest reading since May 2023.[1] The report also noted that median inflation uncertainty increased slightly across all three horizons.[1]

The most widely noted finding was an unexpected spike in household delinquency expectations — the share of respondents anticipating they would be unable to make a minimum debt payment — reaching its worst level since the April 2020 COVID-19 crash, despite a modest improvement in expected household income growth.[1] Commentators read the result as evidence that US households had "hit a brick wall" on how much debt they could pay down.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the share of people expecting to be unable to pay their bills rose 12.5 percent in September, and emphasized that this occurred before the outbreak of the latest Middle East war, which was expected to push energy prices and therefore inflation higher in subsequent months.[1] She contrasted the data with the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's messaging that the economy was working "from the bottom up and the middle out," arguing that Americans were demoralized by domestic conditions even as the executive branch promised billions in new foreign military assistance.[1]
