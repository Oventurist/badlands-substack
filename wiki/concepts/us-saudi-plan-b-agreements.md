# US–Saudi Plan B Agreements

"Plan B" refers to a fallback diplomatic framework advanced by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] in the spring of 2024, under which a package of bilateral agreements with the United States would proceed without being conditioned on a broader Middle East settlement involving [[israel|Israel]] and the Palestinians.[1]

## Background

The United States and Saudi Arabia had drafted a set of agreements on security and technology-sharing that were originally intended to be linked to a comprehensive regional settlement.[1] In the absence of a ceasefire in [[gaza]], and in the face of adamant resistance from [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s Israeli government to the creation of a Palestinian state — as well as its apparent determination to launch an offensive on Rafah — the Saudis pushed for a more modest option that excluded the Israelis.[1]

## Terms

Under the plan B option, Washington and Riyadh would sign agreements covering a bilateral defence pact, U.S. assistance in building a Saudi civil nuclear energy industry, and high-level cooperation in artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies.[1] An offer of normalisation of diplomatic relations with Riyadh would still be extended to Israel in return for Israeli acceptance of the two-state solution to the 76-year Israeli–Arab conflict, but completion of the U.S.–Saudi deals would not be made dependent on agreement from the Netanyahu government.[1]

Firas Maksad of the [[middle-east-institute|Middle East Institute]] described the logic as a "less-for-less model, so the relationship with the US need not be held hostage to the whims of Israeli politics or Benjamin Netanyahu."[1] The [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration would not obtain the historic regional settlement it had sought amid the wreckage of the Gaza war, but would cement a strategic partnership with Riyadh that would keep encroaching Chinese and Russian influence at bay.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the story as a signal that a "counterinsurgency" against Netanyahu and his regime was real and nearing completion, arguing that cheap and affordable energy in developing nations would foster prosperous middle classes, reduce migration pressure, and lower incentives for kinetic conflict — and that malevolent actors therefore work to prevent such development, a premise he attributed to the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] and the climate change narrative.[1] He recalled Netanyahu's 2002 congressional testimony urging military force to stop Iraq, [[iran|Iran]], and Afghanistan from developing nuclear energy programs, and his promise that removing Saddam Hussein would bring peace, calling the latter a lie paid for in lives and trillions of dollars.[1] He further asserted that Netanyahu was a key player in what he characterized as the 9/11 false flag, that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] both harbored a comparable zeal for justice over that event, and that Israel needed a leader prepared to seize the moment while Trump reforges the world with the help of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Israel was being isolated on the geopolitical game board, and that the episode was "another soft [[devolution|Devolution]] proof," with Trump conveniently out of the public seat of power while Uniparty war hawks converged on the Biden administration to press for war against Iran and its proxies.[1] In his framing, Russia held center stage in 2022–23 and the Saudis had now stepped up to exert asymmetric power projection in favor of peace while the U.S. military-industrial complex was temporarily hamstrung by the Devolution operation.[1]
