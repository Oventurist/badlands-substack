# Russia-Africa Partnership Forum

The Russia-Africa Partnership Forum is a diplomatic framework through which Moscow coordinates political and economic cooperation with African states.[1]

## Second Ministerial Conference (Cairo, December 2025)

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] addressed the opening plenary of the forum's Second Ministerial Conference in Cairo on Saturday, December 20, 2025.[1] He said that "Russian-African partnership is developing steadily across all areas, with substantive and trusting political dialogue continuing at the highest levels," and reported that trade with Africa rose 13% in the previous year to nearly $28 billion.[1] Describing that figure as "far from the limit," Lavrov said Moscow was "committed to fully unlocking the huge potential of our practical cooperation."[1]

Lavrov said Moscow had invited African partners to explore opportunities in Russian markets and highlighted prospects for boosting trade with sub-Saharan countries by building direct links with Russian counterparts.[1] He identified national-currency settlement mechanisms as key to "ensuring stable trade and economic transactions and protecting investments," noting a steady rise in the share of ruble settlements in mutual trade — an approach consistent with broader [[local-currency-settlement-system|local-currency settlement]] efforts.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read Lavrov's language about unlocking Africa's potential as a reference to Russian military and paramilitary forces, including the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]], liberating African populations from what he called colonial oppression.[1] He listed the forces he saw arrayed against that project: transnational cartels such as the Israeli diamond cartel, the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], [[mi6|MI6]], [[mossad|Mossad]], [[nato|NATO]], the United Nations, the European Union, and a range of NGOs and proxy militia groups including ISIS and M23.[1] He concluded that "Africa is the chessboard where the Sovereign War is playing out, kinetically."[1]
