# 2024 Israel–Iran Intelligence Leak

The 2024 Israel–Iran intelligence leak was the unauthorized disclosure of top-secret U.S. intelligence documents concerning [[israel|Israel]]'s preparations for an attack against [[iran|Iran]], described as one of the most serious security breaches within the U.S. intelligence community in years.[1]

## The disclosure

On Friday, October 18, 2024, a Telegram channel named "Middle East Spectator" claimed it had received documents from a source inside the U.S. intelligence community detailing Israel's attack preparations.[1] The channel routinely publishes pro-Iranian content, and the profile of the X account affiliated with it says it is located in Iran.[1]

Among the leaked material was an alleged Visual Intelligence report produced by the Department of Defense's [[national-geospatial-intelligence-agency|National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]] and circulated within the intelligence community the preceding week.[1] The report stated that, per U.S. signals intelligence, the Israeli Air Force had conducted a large exercise involving intelligence planes and likely fighter jets trained for a possible strike on Iran.[1]

## Initial reporting and classification

A separate Badlands brief published on October 21, 2024 described the material as an alleged leak of at least two TOP SECRET documents, both marked NOFORN — among the highest classifications, indicating the intelligence could not be shared with allied foreign intelligence agencies, with the exception of the "Five Eyes."[2] A breaking [[axios|Axios]] report by [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]], who maintains close Israeli military and intelligence sources, suggested the documents were authentic and said U.S. officials were "extremely concerned about a potentially major security breach" after the two alleged documents about Israel's preparations were published by a Telegram account affiliated with Iran.[2] The [[national-geospatial-intelligence-agency|NGA]] "Visual Intelligence" report was dated to October 14–16 and distributed among the U.S. intelligence community that week.[2] Neither the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] nor the NGA offered official confirmation or comment, and Badlands noted neither was likely to.[2]

The documents first appeared online on Friday, October 18, on the pro-Iran Telegram channel identified by Axios as "Middle East Spectator."[2] The region remained on edge as Israel prepared to retaliate against Iran for its October 1 ballistic missile attack, in which some 200 projectiles struck sites in central Israel, some reportedly Iranian hypersonics.[2] Axios noted that "the leak could be an attempt to disrupt the Israeli operation."[2]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that the disruption theory was his own first thought on seeing the headline, and argued that an earnest actor seeking to subvert an impending war might well resort to something as extreme as the leak; he added that its appearance specifically on a pro-Iran Telegram channel was not insignificant.[2] The same commentary dwelt on Ravid's role as the Israeli journalist [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has used to deploy narratives to the Israeli public, citing a December 2021 interview in which Trump described Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas as a "father figure," said Abbas wanted peace while [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] did not, implicitly endorsed Benny Gantz for prime minister, and cursed Netanyahu for being the first world leader to congratulate [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on the 2020 election.[2] In that interview Trump also said of Netanyahu's handling of Iran and Soleimani: "He did not do the right thing. I am very disappointed with Israel. I can't talk about it, right now. I will be talking about it, at the right time..."[2] The item was published alongside coverage of the [[2024-caesarea-drone-strike-on-netanyahus-residence|drone strike on Netanyahu's residence in Caesarea]].[2]

## Investigation

The [[fbi|FBI]] opened an investigation into the leak, with a spokesperson saying on Tuesday, October 22 that the bureau was "investigating the alleged leak of classified documents and working closely with our partners in the Department of Defense and Intelligence Community," and declining further comment on an ongoing matter.[1] Officials noted the leak exposed sensitive U.S. spying on one of its closest allies and potentially risked disrupting Israel's plan to retaliate for Iran's ballistic missile attack earlier that month.[1]

## New Zealand implicated

Reporting in late October 2024 placed New Zealand in the crosshairs of the U.S. investigation, with the country implicated in the leak of the classified documents outlining the American assessment of [[israel|Israel]]'s plan to attack [[iran|Iran]].[3] White House National Security Council spokesman [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] said President Joe Biden was "deeply concerned" about the leak.[3] One document was compiled by the [[national-geospatial-intelligence-agency|National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]] and the other appeared to have originated with the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]]; both were marked "top secret," carried the acronym FGI for Foreign Government Intelligence, and had been shared within the [[five-eyes|Five Eyes]] alliance of the United States, Britain, Canada, New Zealand and Australia.[3]

The documents were published on an Iranian-aligned Telegram account called "Middle East Spectator," though Kirby said there was no indication that further documents would surface publicly.[3] New Zealand had recently suffered several successful hacks by foreign powers, and its intelligence agency, the [[government-communications-security-bureau|Government Communications Security Bureau]], had absorbed a NZ$7 million (US$4.7 million) budget cut.[3] Because Iran maintains a large and sophisticated cyber-warfare capability, U.S. intelligence agencies and the [[fbi|FBI]] investigated the possibility of a hostile hack.[3]
