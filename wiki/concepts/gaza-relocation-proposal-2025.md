# Gaza Relocation Proposal (2025)

The **Gaza Relocation Proposal** refers to a plan floated publicly by U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Tuesday, February 4, 2025, under which displaced Palestinians in the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]] would be permanently resettled outside the territory and the United States would take "ownership" of the area in order to redevelop it.[1] Trump did not rule out deploying U.S. troops to support the rebuilding effort and described the arrangement as a potential "long-term" American ownership stake.[1]

## The proposal

Trump's remarks came as negotiations were ramping up over extending the fragile ceasefire between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] and securing the release of the remaining hostages held in Gaza, alongside promises of surging humanitarian aid and reconstruction supplies after more than fifteen months of devastating conflict.[1] The proposal contemplated moving roughly 1.8 million people out of the land they had called home, with settlements to be constructed for them in other countries, and claiming the territory for the United States, possibly with an American military presence.[1] Contemporary reporting characterized the proposal as brazen and likely to roil the next stage of ceasefire talks.[1]

## Saudi rejection

Within hours of the press conference, the Saudi Foreign Ministry issued a statement reaffirming that the Kingdom's long-held position — that Palestinians must have their own independent state — was firm and not open to negotiation, a stance Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] had reiterated many times before.[1] [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] restated that any formal relations between the Kingdom and Israel hinge on the creation of a viable Palestinian state on the 1967 borders, and rejected the displacement of Gazans outright.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentators treated the announcement as strategically ambiguous rather than straightforward policy. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] asked why, one day after Trump "drained the life blood of the Deep State" by halting [[usaid|USAID]] programs, he had "adopted the posture of a drunken NeoCon," and located the answer in a surge of online commentary from supporters asking why the United States blindly supports Israeli policy.[1] He argued that forcibly removing people from their property under threat of violence is despicable whether the setting is Lahaina, Los Angeles, Western North Carolina, or Gaza, framing it as theft in the biblical sense, and said that a genuine "Golden Age" requires Strength, Honor, and Dignity — including the strength to say "no" to allies.[1] He stated that if the plan amounted to stealing land, that is where his path with the administration would fork, adding that if [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] wanted the land he could take it himself.[1]

A second commentator, Chris Paul, offered a contrary reading, questioning why it should be considered stunning that "a construction project announced years ago was called a construction project today," and asking why the regime opposed the project so strongly that it was "giving the world a war story to stop it" — speculating about infrastructure motives such as a canal that would nullify the Suez.[1]

## Palestinian Authority threat to sever ties

In early March 2025 [[ahmed-majdalani|Ahmed Majdalani]], a senior Palestinian official, PLO Executive Council member and confidant of [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], said the PA would cut ties with the United States if [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] advanced the proposal.[2] Speaking to The Times of Israel from his Ramallah office, Majdalani said implementation would amount to the "expulsion" of Gaza's roughly two million residents and pose an "existential threat" to the Palestinian cause, answering "Of course" each time he was pressed on whether the PA would sever relations.[2] He clarified that Ramallah remained prepared to work with Trump if the idea were abandoned, and cited softening statements by top US aides who suggested openness to shelving the plan if Arab allies produced an alternative post-war arrangement removing [[hamas|Hamas]] from power.[2]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Abbas and the Fatah faction of the PA — at odds with Hamas politically and at times kinetically since Hamas took power in Gaza in 2006 — had reached the same conclusion he had: that Trump's "Gaza Plan" was a proposal rather than a real plan of action.[2] Because Trump had instructed regional Arab partners to produce a counter-proposal, the commentary held that the matter had become a negotiation in which Trump's position was an opening offer, not an intended outcome.[2]

In this reading, Trump's rhetoric removed [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the Israelis from the negotiating table, letting Trump absorb the heat for a radical idea Netanyahu had wanted all along; each time Trump declined an Arab counter-proposal, Israeli confidence grew that he would deliver the impossible.[2] Netanyahu was judged perceptive enough to know the final deal would differ from Trump's suggestion, which the author linked to his preparations to resume the war with Hamas, while still giving Trump space to negotiate on Israel's behalf.[2] What Trump needed, the piece argued, was time — to wrap up the Ukraine negotiation with [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and align with Russia so the two could present a united front against Netanyahu, in stages marked by the Riyadh peace summit and a forthcoming Putin-brokered Iran nuclear deal.[2] Meanwhile, Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s meeting with US Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] produced boilerplate rhetoric about "strengthening ties of economic interest," but his true purpose in Washington was said to be drumming up support for [[west-bank-annexation|annexation of the West Bank]], on which Tel Aviv expected Trump to endorse conquest.[2]
