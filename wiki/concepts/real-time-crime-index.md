# Real-Time Crime Index

The **Real-Time Crime Index (RTCI)** is a crime-data compilation drawing on reporting from 570 law enforcement agencies, used to estimate national crime trends well in advance of official federal statistics.[1]

## 2025 findings

RTCI data showed a nearly 20% decline in murders in 2025 compared with the same period in 2024, underpinning an analysis by crime-stats expert [[jeff-asher|Jeff Asher]] concluding that the United States was on pace for the largest one-year drop in murders ever recorded.[1] Other major crime categories tracked by the index also fell nationwide and across jurisdictions of all population sizes, including motor vehicle thefts (23.2%), aggravated assaults (7.5%) and robbery (18.3%).[1] The decline in killings was described as part of a broader decrease in violent crime following the COVID-era spike; mass killings also fell in 2025 to their lowest level since 2006.[1]

## Methodology and limitations

According to its online glossary, the database does not count manslaughter, self-defense, negligence or "accidental killings" in its statistics.[1] Its figures were available through October, ahead of the [[fbi|FBI]]'s official 2025 violent crime data, which was not expected until sometime the following year; RTCI estimates have historically tracked closely with federal figures.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had promised to make America safe again and that the data suggested he was succeeding, while cautioning that "the data has always been a problem" and questioning whether crime metrics had been fixed to track all categories of crime and their changes over time.[1] Citing the exclusion of manslaughter, self-defense, negligence and accidental killings, she concluded "Okay, so no," and added that even in Colorado things felt somewhat safer — an anecdotal impression she attributed to a momentary pause. Her summary: America is certainly safer, but still not safe.[1]
