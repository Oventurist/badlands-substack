# SAVE America Act

The SAVE America Act is proposed federal election-integrity legislation cited repeatedly in Badlands commentary during 2026.[1]

## Post-ruling advocacy

After the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] upheld [[mississippi|Mississippi]]'s late-arriving mail ballot law in a 5-4 decision, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the ruling breathed new life into legislative vehicles such as the SAVE America Act and [[clay-higgins|Clay Higgins]]' mail ballot act, since the Court had signalled it would not rewrite election rules from the bench.[1]

The same commentary treated a legislator's vote for the SAVE America Act as an insufficient credential on its own, criticizing Representative [[jeff-crank|Jeff Crank]] for treating that vote as proof of commitment to election integrity while campaigning on expansive spending.[1]

## Trump's March 2026 push

In March 2026 [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] urged Congress to pass the SAVE Act, describing it as "one of the most IMPORTANT & CONSEQUENTIAL pieces of legislation in the history of Congress, and America itself."[2] In a Truth Social post he said the bill was essential to ensure "NO MORE RIGGED ELECTIONS" by requiring proof of U.S. citizenship and government-issued photo ID for federal elections, while also banning unrestricted mail-in voting practices that he noted make the United States "the only Country in the World" that allows them.[2]

Trump explicitly tied the legislation to other priorities, stating it would address "Voter I.D., Proof of Citizenship, No Rigged Mail-In Voting … No Men in Women's Sports, No Transgender MUTILIZATION of our Children," calling these "90% to 99% ISSUES ALL!"[2] He warned that "only sick, demented, or deranged people in the House or Senate could vote against THE SAVE AMERICA ACT," adding that he would not endorse any lawmaker who opposed it and that doing so would mean "a guaranteed loss" in their future campaigns.[2]

## Senate debate

Bill sponsor [[mike-lee|Sen. Mike Lee]] (R-Utah) rebutted Democratic objections during Senate debate, directly addressing claims by [[dick-durbin|Sen. Dick Durbin]] that the measure would require expensive passports costing $186 and weeks of delay, or create burdens for married women needing extra documentation.[2] Lee explained that the act includes built-in accommodations such as free sworn attestations for voters lacking traditional paperwork, state verification using existing records, and reciprocity agreements between states, stressing that the process "will not cost them a dime" and "no one will be excluded."[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that while Republicans fear losing Congress in the midterms, the current Congress is already actively fighting against what the President calls the most consequential legislation in American history.[2] She argued that keeping the status quo of elections serves only the appearance of legitimacy: the SAVE America Act is good legislation, but the body called upon to pass it is illegitimate and invested in its demise.[2] She questioned why Trump repeatedly attaches gender-related issues to his posts about the bill if he were making a serious effort to change senators' minds, and — combining this with the video-game bombing videos released ahead of NDAA authorization — concluded that Trump is revealing the "apex parody of governance": the [[congress|United States Congress]].[2]

## H.R. 7296 (119th Congress)

The bill was introduced as H.R. 7296 by Representative [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]] on January 30, 2026 and referred to the House Committee on House Administration.[3] It would amend the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act of 1993]] to require documentary proof of US citizenship to register to vote in federal elections, listing acceptable documentation including a US passport, a REAL ID–compliant identification indicating citizenship, or other official proof of citizenship.[3] While no final House vote had occurred by early February 2026, House leadership took steps toward floor consideration and Roy indicated the chamber would vote that week; passage in the House would still require Senate consideration before the measure could reach the President's desk.[3]

On Monday, February 9, 2026, House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] criticized the SAVE America Act and broader voter ID efforts in a CNN "State of the Union" interview with Dana Bash, characterizing them as voter suppression and accusing Republicans of attempting to rig or steal elections.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that opposing voter ID is a losing issue, citing CNN chief data analyst Harry Enten's finding that 83% of Americans favor requiring photo ID to vote — including 71% of Democrats, 82% of Latinos and 76% of Black Americans — and argued that voter ID is not controversial but Democratic opposition to it should be.[3] She predicted a high likelihood that Republicans would nonetheless cave under accusations of suppression.[3]

## Trump endorsement in the nationalization push (2026)

In February 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] endorsed the Save America Act alongside the [[mega-act|Make Elections Great Again Act]] as part of a public call for Republicans to "take over" and "nationalize" election administration in at least 15 states.[4] As described in that coverage, the Save America Act would mandate proof of citizenship for voter registration and impose restrictions on mail-in voting processes, while Trump argued the measures were needed to prevent undocumented immigrants from voting and to address inaccurate vote counting.[4]

## June 2026 filibuster campaign

In June 2026 Trump escalated his advocacy on Truth Social, writing that "anybody who doesn't want to Terminate the Filibuster is a FOOL, a very stupid one, at that!" and predicting that Democrats would end the filibuster within minutes of taking office before adding two states, four senators, additional House members, and expanding the Supreme Court to 21 justices.[5] He warned that the Republican Party would "never win another Election," said he would "sadly, be the last Republican President," and called on Senate Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] and the Republican Senate to prevent that "carnage," closing with "TERMINATE THE FILIBUSTER, AND IMMEDIATELY APPROVE THE SAVE AMERICA ACT."[5]

In a companion post, Trump objected that Utah — a state he said he had won handily each time — was moving to an all-mail-in ballot format like Colorado's, argued that dishonestly handled mail-in ballots advantage Democrats whose "only Road to Victory ... is CHEATING," and again urged Republicans to either terminate the filibuster or pass the Act.[5] Badlands framed the episode with the observation that the bill had no chance of passing while media coverage claimed it was tying the Senate in knots — an outcome the outlet said only it had predicted.[5]

## March 2026 Senate weekend debate

The [[united-states-senate|Senate]] worked through the weekend of March 21–22, 2026, debating the bill, which would require individuals to provide proof of U.S. citizenship such as a passport or birth certificate to register for federal elections, impose stricter voter ID standards, and expand federal oversight of voter registration systems.[6] Supporters said the bill was needed to strengthen election integrity and ensure that only U.S. citizens vote, while opponents argued it could disenfranchise millions of eligible voters lacking ready access to required documents and burden state election systems.[6] The measure faced significant obstacles in the closely divided Senate, where most legislation requires 60 votes to advance and Democrats had largely opposed it, making passage unlikely in its current form.[6]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the act as legislation amending the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] to require proof of citizenship for voter registration, noting that most Americans cannot believe it is not already law and that the measure is supported by more than 80% of the electorate, including Democrats and minorities.[6] She argued that Democrat lawmakers were "using minorities as a narrative shield" and that the tactic should backfire spectacularly, since the arguments against the bill mirrored those made about [[real-id|REAL ID]] the previous year.[6] Press coverage cited the [[brennan-center-for-justice|Brennan Center for Justice]] finding that over 21 million Americans lack ready access to documents such as birth certificates or passports.[6] [[polymarket|Polymarket]] had the act's odds of becoming law in 2026 down to 11% over the weekend.[6]

## Noem–Mullin reshuffle question (March 2026)

When President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced on March 6, 2026 that Oklahoma Senator [[markwayne-mullin|Markwayne Mullin]] would replace [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] as Secretary of Homeland Security effective March 31, Badlands writer [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] asked whether Trump had just "rugpulled" the Save America Act by removing a Republican senator from the chamber.[7] He added that it would be comical to see the bill finally brought to the floor with a vote timed for March 31.[7]

## March 2026: Senate fight, the filibuster, and Murkowski's opposition

By March 2026 the SAVE America Act had passed the House in February and moved to the Senate, where [[donald-trump|President Trump]] told lawmakers it was a top priority ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]], urging Senate Republicans to pass the bill requiring documentary proof of US citizenship to register to vote in federal elections and directing states to remove non-citizens from voter rolls.[8]

Senate Republicans debated whether to prioritize the legislation even at the cost of the legislative filibuster.[8] Senator [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]] (R-TX) argued in an op-ed that the SAVE America Act mattered more than preserving Senate procedural norms, contending that election integrity should take precedence; Cornyn was at the time facing a difficult primary challenge from Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]].[8] Democrats and some strategists argued that internal Republican fighting over election legislation could work to Democratic advantage in the midterms.[8]

Senator [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] (R-AK) told a LindellTV reporter that she would not vote for the bill because citizenship and voter ID requirements would "disenfranchise" rural Alaskans.[8] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the argument as a progressive, expansionist talking point about the franchise and as implicitly demeaning to the 60–70% of Alaska's population that is indigenous or American Indian and the 20% living in rural communities, arguing Murkowski was effectively alleging they could not obtain identification or prove citizenship.[8] Ashe linked Murkowski's position to her support for [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice voting]] and predicted that getting every "no"-voting senator on record insulting their constituents would function as "an excellent boomerang."[8]

## March 19, 2026 Senate debate and Trump's naming complaint

The [[united-states-senate|Senate]] began floor debate on the SAVE America Act, the Republican-backed bill requiring documentary proof of US citizenship — such as a passport or birth certificate — to register to vote in federal elections.[9] Democrats opposed the measure and it faced procedural hurdles, with reporting at the time considering passage unlikely.[9]

On Wednesday, March 18, 2026, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]]: "For all of the Fake News out there, it's called THE SAVE AMERICA ACT, not the SAVE ACT. Nobody knows what the Save Act means!!!"[9]

## Trump's March 8, 2026 ultimatum

On Sunday, March 8, 2026, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] declared that he would not sign any other legislation until [[congress|Congress]] passed the SAVE America Act, escalating pressure on lawmakers to approve the Republican-backed voting measure.[10] In posts on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] he wrote that the bill "must go to the front of the line" and stated, "I, as President, will not sign other Bills until this is passed," while in a separate post urging lawmakers to "PASS THE SAVE AMERICA ACT, NOW."[10] His posts called for voter identification and proof-of-citizenship requirements along with restrictions on mail-in voting.[10]

Coverage at the time noted that a version of the legislation had passed the [[us-house-of-representatives|U.S. House of Representatives]] on February 11, 2026 by a 218–213 vote and faced consideration in the Senate.[10] Civil-rights groups argued the bill would require documents such as passports or birth certificates to register and could eliminate many forms of online or mail-in voter registration, making registration harder for some voters.[10]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the landscape surrounding the fight as "strange," calling the act an "80+% issue" popular even with minorities and Democrats, and summarizing its substance as proof of citizenship for registration plus state voter roll maintenance to identify and remove noncitizens.[10] She characterized opposition from what she called the "fake elections industry" as an admission that fake voters are a critical component of that industry.[10] Her sharpest commentary concerned [[scott-presler|Scott Presler]], whom Trump highlighted in a Truth Social post: if the act were properly executed, she argued, Presler's voter registration operation — which does not verify eligibility — "will be out of business."[10] Citing [[audit-the-vote-pa|Audit the Vote PA]] and reported 2020 turnout of 94.1 percent as a red flag for malfeasance rather than a turnout problem, she concluded that Trump was spotlighting the contradiction deliberately and that the episode had "galvanized the election integrity community in a big way behind the scenes."[10]

## March 2026 Doral conference push

At the House Republicans' annual Members Issues Conference on March 9, 2026, held at Trump National Doral Miami, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] urged the party to prioritize passage of the rebranded SAVE America Act, telling attendees the bill would "guarantee the midterms" for Republicans and warning of "big trouble" if it failed; he again threatened to withhold his signature from other legislation until the House acted.[11] As described in that coverage, the legislation would establish strict national voter ID requirements, ban mail-in ballots, and add new restrictions on transgender rights.[11]

House GOP leaders — Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], Majority Leader Steve Scalise, Majority Whip [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]], and Conference Chair Lisa McClain — instead emphasized pocketbook issues as the route to holding their narrow majority, highlighting tax cuts for families, energy independence, "Trump accounts" for newborns, and lower living costs.[11] Johnson insisted the party was "in lockstep" with Trump and the [[white-house|White House]], though the messaging split ran through the closed-door sessions.[11] An AP-NORC poll from December had found roughly one-third of Americans ranking inflation and personal finances as top priorities while almost none cited voting laws or election security, and the [[iran-war|war with Iran]] further complicated the GOP's economic message by driving up gas prices.[11]

## June 2026 stall and House floor revolt

By late June 2026 the stalled SAVE America Act had become the trigger for a procedural crisis in the House.[12] Frustration over the bill, described as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s top legislative priority on elections, drove a week-long impasse that culminated on June 30 when a small group of Republican hard-liners defeated the chamber's procedural rule 224-198, freezing most floor business and forcing leadership to begin the July 4 recess early.[12] Some rebels demanded amendment votes on the SAVE America Act itself alongside defense policy and Delphi retiree pensions.[12]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reiterated that the act is "not a magic bullet to secure elections" but that it moves the needle on the fake voters and, in part, fake ballots fronts.[12] She argued that without real elections consent is manufactured through coercion, deception, and fear-based propaganda, and that honesty and accountability for past fraudulent elections are required with or without the legislation.[12] See [[june-2026-house-rule-failure|the rule failure]].[12]
