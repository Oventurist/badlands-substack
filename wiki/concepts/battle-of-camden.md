# Battle of Camden

The Battle of Camden was the August 1780 engagement in which the second American army sent south to liberate Charleston was decimated and captured, completing the collapse of organized resistance in South Carolina during the [[american-revolution|American Revolution]].[1]

## Significance

Camden followed a few months after the [[siege-of-charleston|fall of Charleston]], where an entire [[continental-army|Continental Army]] had already been lost.[1] The defeated force was the army commanded by General [[horatio-gates|Horatio Gates]], into whose camp near Buffalo Ford [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]] and his militia volunteers had ridden on 25 July 1780.[1] With all colonial and militia forces driven from the colony, nothing appeared to stand between the [[british-empire|British]] and [[george-washington|George Washington]] in Virginia.[1]

Camden also served the British as a base of operations; when [[banastre-tarleton|Banastre Tarleton]] sought to lure Marion into an ambush at Nelson's Ferry, he spread rumors that he was withdrawing to Camden and ordered his scouts to leave "signs of sudden fear," such as campfires burning with food still cooking.[1]
