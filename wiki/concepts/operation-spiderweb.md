# Operation Spiderweb

**Operation Spiderweb** was the codename for a Ukrainian drone operation carried out on Sunday, June 1, 2025, which destroyed billions of dollars' worth of Russian military aircraft at air bases across Russia, including targets as distant as Siberia, in what Kyiv described as its longest-range assault of the war.[1]

## Planning and execution

According to reporting compiled by The Guardian, the operation was prepared in secret over roughly eighteen months.[1] Ukrainian agents moved short-range drones and explosives into Russian territory in advance of the strike, where they were staged for a coordinated remote launch intended to degrade Moscow's air superiority.[1]

Vasyl Maliuk, head of the [[security-service-of-ukraine|Security Service of Ukraine]] (SBU), said the drones were smuggled into Russia concealed inside shipping containers, which were subsequently loaded onto trucks.[1] With the trucks positioned near Russian air bases, the roof panels of the containers were lifted away by a remotely activated mechanism, allowing the drones to fly out and begin their attack.[1] The drones used first-person view (FPV) technology, permitting them to be piloted remotely — probably from Ukrainian territory.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright characterized Spiderweb as an "Actual-Narrative Deployment" — an event in the physical world that simultaneously functions as a move in what he calls the War of Stories.[1] In his reading, the Russia–Ukraine conflict had been publicly stuck in a holding pattern, with Kyiv signaling willingness to hold peace talks only if they were "serious," while Moscow maintained that the peace terms it offered in 2022 remained on the table and were both reasonable and morally right.[1]

Hovering over both narratives, in this account, is [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who commented from off stage during what Badlands writers term the Fake Biden Regime and who returned to office with a renewed mandate and the presumed capability to influence the international game board.[1] Burning Bright argued that while the Russian side has been complimentary of Trump's involvement, [[volodymyr-zelensky|Ukraine]] and what he calls its handlers have alternated between requesting aid and making what he describes as false overtures to peace while planning major escalations.[1]

He further argued that Trump's escalatory rhetoric toward [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] has led some in the alternative media sphere to question whether the president has the wrong advisers, but contended that long-standing backchannels between Trump and Putin point toward a shared end game of codifying a lasting peace between east and west — making Trump's public displays "entirely narrative."[1] The stated rationale for the theatrics is that the best way to expose "creatures of the dark" is to let them step into the light of public narrative when they believe it is safe to do so.[1] Burning Bright linked the metaphor to a piece he published two years earlier describing the trap — the "Spiderweb" — that he expected the "Prussian Proxy State" of Ukraine and its globalist backers to walk into.[1]

## Diplomatic aftermath

The strike immediately preceded direct Russian–Ukrainian talks in Istanbul, after which Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] renewed his offer to host a summit bringing Putin, Zelensky and Trump together.[1]
