# A Week to Remember

"A Week to Remember … But What Set This Chaos Into Motion?" is an opinion essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on July 25, 2024. Written largely in the interval between the June 2024 presidential debate and the July 13, 2024 assassination attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Butler, Pennsylvania, the article advances a single central thesis: that the political convulsions of July 2024 were not triggered by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s debate performance but by Trump's June 10, 2024 pledge to make tips tax-free, which the author reads as an opening move against the American financial system itself.

## The premise: a week of tectonic shifts

The essay opens by noting that [[q-anon|Q]] posted the phrase "a week to remember" on July 14, 2019, and that the attempt on Trump's life occurred on July 13, 2024 — five years later. In the days following the Butler rally, the author observes, Trump selected [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as his running mate, the [[republican-national-convention|Republican National Convention]] took place, and Biden withdrew from the race. The article describes this as "an epic week—a week in which one can't take a breath and reestablish their bearings because with every new day, a tectonic shift of the narrative occurs."

## Rejecting the debate explanation

Carlson argues that the debate cannot be the cause of the establishment's abandonment of Biden, because "everyone in the media knew of Biden's cognitive issues for years, and they've been making excuses for him for years." The speed and uniformity of the media turn is offered as evidence of prior coordination: "They didn't wait for their 4am talking points, as their orders were already given to them."

The article speculates that the drug regimen said to render Biden "temporarily semi-coherent" was deliberately withheld before the debate in order to expose his condition, contrasting his debate showing with his more coherent State of the Union address earlier in 2024. On this reading, the decision to replace Biden had already been made, and the debate was "the event that would be used to justify the decision." The author further asserts that Biden "fought the decision, kicking and screaming to the very end," and speculates that he had his assets frozen and feared for his life. Biden, the article argues, needed to be replaced "before people started asking the question, 'Biden obviously isn't running the country, so who is?'"

## The tax-free tips thesis

The essay's core claim is that on June 10, 2024, roughly two weeks before the debate, Trump publicly floated eliminating taxes on tips, and that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] read this as a declaration of intent to dismantle the financial order. Making tips tax-free, the author writes, "could be viewed as a first step towards abolishing income tax," after which "the money supply system would be next." Without private bankers controlling America's money supply, "the [[cabal|Deep State Cabal]] stands no chance of surviving."

The article notes the internal logic of tipping: some industries have "turned tips into wages," with employers paying employees little and shifting the burden of a living wage onto customers. It contends that [[federal-income-tax|income tax]] is similarly indefensible, that the founding generation viewed it as unconstitutional, and that the Constitution had to be amended in 1913 to permit it. The essay objects to the same dollar being taxed repeatedly through income, property, sales, and capital gains taxes — "double, triple and quadruple taxation, certainly without representation" — and offers the illustration of paying a tax to place purchases in one's trunk, remove them, and put them in a closet. Americans, the author estimates, retain only "around 30 to 40 cents" of each dollar once all taxes and [[inflation|inflation]] are accounted for: "In our minds, we think we pay 15%, but in reality, it's more like 60-70%."

## Taxation as illusion

The article claims taxes do not fund the federal government. Instead, "taxes are used to create the illusion that Americans pay for their government," which in turn supports "the illusion that the government in turn works for them." Fear of the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], the author writes, "is why it was created."

Common justifications are rejected: if income tax pays for roads and the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]], the essay asks, why are gas taxes, car tabs, and postage also charged? It recalls Trump's argument that Amazon should deliver its own packages rather than being subsidized through the USPS, noting the volume of Amazon trucks on the road today. The article observes that between the American Civil War and the founding of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in 1913, the United States built its railroads, power lines, much of its highway system, its military, schools, and hospitals without a federal income tax — asking, "When was the last time you saw a new freeway built? Where is the money really going?"

## Money printing, inflation, and the housing example

According to the source, the Federal Reserve prints money to fund itself and the financial system, passing it among banks, corporations, governments, and politicians, so that "the government represents and works for the banks and corporations, not the people." Were income tax abolished, Americans "might wake up to the scam" — likened to a drug dealer living lavishly with no reported income.

Inflation is described as "the biggest financial scam of them all." The essay walks through a housing example: the Fed prints money and gives it to banks to lend; the resulting dilution of purchasing power raises house prices; the bank lends the money at interest; the politicians are paid by the banks; and the buyer pays twice, through the inflated price and the loan interest. "Bernie Madoff is a lightweight compared to these Ponzi schemers," the author writes of the arrangement, invoking Bernie Madoff by name.

## Gold, the Petrodollar, and BRICS

The article recounts that in the early 1970s France sought repayment of a U.S. loan in gold rather than fiat currency, since gold "can't be created out of thin air." In response, the essay claims, the government of [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]] passed an amendment making it illegal to pay foreign entities in gold — an act said to have stood for fifty years before being "quietly repealed … a couple of years ago," just as the [[brics|BRICS]] nations began discussing a gold-backed settlement currency. The author notes pointedly that Trump met with BRICS leaders in 2017 and asks, "Coincidence?"

The [[petrodollar|Petrodollar]] arrangement, also dated to the early 1970s, established that oil could only be sold in U.S. dollars — an agreement the essay characterizes as reached at gunpoint: "someone pulling up their shirt a little to show the gun tucked into their belt and saying, 'are we cool?'" Requiring the world to settle transactions in dollars, the article argues, exported the cost of American money printing to every other nation and transformed the United States into "a true economic juggernaut." The essay compares the U.S. government to "a crack dealer who smokes half his supply and charges double for the crack he sells to make up the difference."

It further claims that immediately after the fifty-year Petrodollar agreement lapsed, mainstream media began attempting to tie [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to the September 11 attacks — echoing, in the author's telling, earlier coverage of the murder and dismemberment of a journalist when the Saudi royals grew "chummy" with Trump. The article asserts that coverage omitted that the journalist was the nephew of one of the world's largest arms dealers and a CIA asset.

## The Federal Reserve since 2006

The essay traces the Fed's post-2006 conduct as evidence of Deep State management of the economy. In 2007 the Fed cut rates by five percentage points ahead of the autumn 2008 banking collapse; once the system became insolvent it cut rates to zero and bought assets onto its balance sheet. The article likens this to a defibrillator that must eventually be withdrawn, yet says it was "used incessantly for the next eight years under [[barack-obama|Obama]]," during which Facebook, Amazon, Apple, Netflix, and Google grew into mega-cap companies the author describes as "created, funded and given preferential treatment by the CIA and their puppets."

The first rate hike, the article states, came after Trump's 2016 victory. Trump "inherited shroom-tripping [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] as his Fed Chairman" and had to keep her two years; in 2018 [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] took over and raised rates aggressively, contributing to a 20% stock market drop in the autumn of 2018 and rate cuts in 2019. When the COVID-19 lockdowns arrived in early 2020, the essay says, Trump forced the Fed to print money at an unprecedented rate, knowing it would create inflation — "fighting the Cabal with their own weapon, the Federal Reserve."

The author contends that much of the inflation visible after Biden took office in 2021 stems from that printing, and that the Deep State conspicuously declines to blame Trump for it, because "to accuse Trump is to draw attention to the Ponzi Scheme." Instead the media downplayed inflation and "told the public that they weren't really experiencing what they were experiencing." As the article puts it: "Who is going to continue to play at a roulette table if they know the wheel has a magnet, or play craps if they know the dice are loaded?"

## Cabal disarray and the Soros–Abedin engagement

The essay asks why establishment figures were not uniform in abandoning Biden, suggesting a power struggle among "Cabal families." It cites the announced engagement of [[alex-soros|Alex Soros]] and [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], comparing it to royal houses intermarrying "to keep peace and consolidate power," and asks whether the engagement was meant to calm the mayhem or was itself a product of an ongoing power grab.

Looking ahead, the author predicts continued exposure of the Deep State, groundwork for an American economic recovery in 2025 ("think Supreme Court decisions"), and a migration of Deep State power from America to Europe, beyond Trump's reach. As a data point, the article notes that [[boeing|Boeing]] stock stood at $323 the day Biden took office and fell to $95 within eight weeks, while no comparable bad news has emerged about [[airbus|Airbus]] — which is based in France. Within Europe, the essay foresees Cabal families in England, France, and Germany contesting for a larger share of power, and asks which families back [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].

## Predictions on tax policy

Carlson argues that revoking the federal income tax could not occur on day one — January 20, 2025 — without triggering financial panic and defunding legitimate programs. He predicts instead a sequence: tax-free tips first, then reduced rates, then eventual elimination, with the government funded by tariffs and corporate taxes and federal spending and employment reduced. "Less government, less government control."

The article allows that its thesis may be wrong — "one could argue the chaos comes from finally recognizing the severity of Biden's mental condition" — but suggests that tax-free tips may be "a shot across the bow—a warning to the Deep State that their days are numbered."

## Closing timeline and questions

The essay ends with a timeline. On July 13 the assassination attempt occurred; six days later [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson Lee]] died; two days after that Biden withdrew from the race; the next day [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]], director of the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]], testified before the [[house-committee-on-oversight-and-accountability|Committee on Oversight and Accountability]], mentioning Jackson Lee's passing and her role in oversight of the Secret Service. The following day Cheatle resigned.

The author closes with four questions for the reader: whether Jackson Lee was involved in planning the assassination attempt; whether, if so, she died because of the attempt or because it failed; whether Biden stepped down fearing he would be next; and "will Jackson Lee be buried wearing her infamous pedophile ring?"
