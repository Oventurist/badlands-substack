# TikTok Ban

The TikTok ban is the U.S. statutory deadline requiring the divestiture or prohibition of [[tiktok|TikTok]] in the United States, which in late 2024 was slated to take effect the day before the presidential inauguration.[1] The [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] agreed to hear TikTok's challenge to the potential ban on an expedited schedule.[1]

President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked the Supreme Court to delay the deadline, telling the justices that postponing the law until he returned to the White House could obviate the need for the court to weigh in.[1] The filing was written by [[d-john-sauer|D. John Sauer]], one of Trump's personal appellate attorneys and his nominee for U.S. solicitor general — a post that would have him take over managing the government's defense of the ban from the Biden administration.[1]

Sauer wrote that "President Trump alone possesses the consummate dealmaking expertise, the electoral mandate, and the political will to negotiate a resolution to save the platform while addressing the national security concerns," and that in light of those interests — "including, most importantly, his overarching responsibility for the United States' national security and foreign policy — President Trump opposes banning TikTok in the United States at this juncture, and seeks the ability to resolve the issues at hand through political means once he takes office."[1]

## Chinese response to the divestiture law (April 2024)

The divestiture requirement was enacted as part of a $95 billion foreign aid package signed into law in April 2024, which also included $2 billion to restock American weapons provided to [[taiwan|Taiwan]] and other Indo-Pacific allies in a direct attempt to deter Chinese aggression in the region.[2] The law demanded that TikTok's parent company [[bytedance|ByteDance]] sell the app to another company within nine months or see it banned from use in the United States.[2] The Chinese government threatened to retaliate against the United States over the package.[2] "China firmly rejects the U.S. passing and signing into law the military aid package containing negative content on China," Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman [[lin-jian|Lin Jian]] said in a briefing, adding, "We have lodged serious representations to the U.S."[2] Lin said the package "gravely infringes upon China's sovereignty," arguing that its large military aid to Taiwan "seriously violates the one-China principle, and sends a seriously wrong signal to 'Taiwan independence' separatist forces."[2]

## House passage and the Mnuchin bid (March 2024)

On March 13, 2024 the House of Representatives passed a bipartisan bill that, if enacted, would force [[bytedance|ByteDance]] either to divest its flagship global app or face an effective ban on [[tiktok]] within the United States.[3] The measure moved to the Senate, where its future was uncertain, though President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said he would sign it if it reached his desk.[3]

The day after passage, former Treasury Secretary [[steven-mnuchin|Steven Mnuchin]] announced on CNBC that he was assembling an investor group through his firm [[liberty-strategic-capital|Liberty Strategic Capital]] to acquire the app, saying the legislation should pass and the business should be sold.[3] Reporting noted that Masayoshi Son's [[softbank|SoftBank]] Vision Fund was both a 2018 investor in ByteDance and a limited partner in Liberty Strategic.[3] Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], drawing on research by [[ian-carroll|Ian Carroll]], questioned whether the divest-or-ban framing served as cover for other foreign influence interests in the platform.[3]

## April 2025 extension and tariff linkage

On 4 April 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced an executive order granting [[bytedance|ByteDance]] 75 additional days to sell its stake in [[tiktok|TikTok]] or face the U.S. ban, writing on Truth Social that "we do not want TikTok to 'go dark.'"[4] Speaking two days later, Trump said a sale had been "pretty close" before Beijing reversed course in response to his new 54 percent tariffs on Chinese goods, and claimed that "if I gave a little cut in tariffs, they'd approve that deal in 15 minutes."[4] Reporting characterized tariffs as Trump's bargaining chip to force Chinese assent to a divestiture.[4]

## House passage of the divestment bill, March 2024

On March 13, 2024, the House of Representatives passed the TikTok divestment bill by a vote of 352-65-1, with fifteen Republicans and fifty Democrats opposed.[5] The measure moved to the Senate, where it already had the backing of Senate Intelligence Committee Chairman Mark Warner and Vice Chairman Marco Rubio, who said in a joint statement that they were "encouraged by today's strong bipartisan vote" and looked forward to getting the bill through the Senate and signed into law.[5] President Joe Biden indicated he would sign the legislation if Congress placed it on his desk.[5]

Representative Thomas Massie opposed the bill on the House floor, calling it a possible "trojan horse" and warning of First Amendment implications, arguing that Americans have the right to view information and do not need government protection from it.[5]

Badlands writer Simon Esler read the episode as the Deep State building a pretext for further systems of control, observing that the pattern is clear when people known to be abusive and power-hungry announce their intention to protect the public.[5] He also noted Donald Trump's positioning in a "grey zone," speculating in light of Trump's recent meetings with ByteDance part-owner Jeffrey Yass and with Elon Musk that Trump's media conglomerate might be circling TikTok as an acquisition target, and raising the question of what parallel operations might be underway with respect to YouTube, Instagram and Facebook.[5]
