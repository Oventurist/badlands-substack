# Derivatives

Derivatives are the speculative financial instruments that [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies as a $700 trillion time bomb built into the trans-Atlantic banking system, warning that unless it is quickly addressed, efforts to re-industrialize American industry through protective tariffs will be for naught.[1]

## Origins

"Creative financial instruments" was the Orwellian name given to the asset class popularized by [[alan-greenspan|Alan Greenspan]] in the aftermath of the 23% Dow Jones collapse of October 19, 1987.[1] Supercomputing technologies originally developed for NASA nation-building and space exploration programs were repurposed to create complex formulas assigning values to price differentials on securities and insured debts, which could then be hedged on the spot and futures markets made possible by the destruction of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]] in 1971.[1]

## Growth

Ehret notes that derivatives grew to nominal values ten times larger than entire world GDP.[1] Over-the-counter derivatives expanded from $60 trillion in 2000 to $600 trillion by 2008 following passage of the [[gramm-leach-bliley-act|Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act]].[1] Attempts to regulate the market early, notably by [[brooksley-born|Brooksley Born]], were shut down.[1]

## After 2008

Contrary to popular belief, Ehret argues, nothing was resolved by the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 crash]]: speculation was not actually regulated under the [[dodd-frank-act|Dodd-Frank Act]] or the Volcker Rule of 2010, banks were not broken up, and derivatives grew by a further 40%, with the new bubble concentrated in the corporate and household debt sector.[1]
