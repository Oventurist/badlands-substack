# Holy Week

Holy Week is the week preceding Easter in the Christian liturgical calendar, beginning with [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] and culminating in the Paschal Triduum. In Jerusalem it centers on [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|the Church of the Holy Sepulchre]], traditionally identified as the site of the crucifixion and resurrection, and draws pilgrims from around the world.[1]

## Progressive restriction, 2020–2026

Badlands writers documented what they described as a "guided progression" of restrictions on Holy Week observance at the Holy Sepulchre.[1] In 2020 the church was closed to the public during Holy Week for the first time in modern history under COVID-19 health restrictions.[1] In 2021 a partial reopening allowed only limited access, reduced pilgrimages and controlled attendance.[1] In 2022 Israeli police imposed Old City entry limits that constrained Palm Sunday access although the church remained open.[1] In 2023 barriers and controlled entry further reduced attendance amid escalating tensions, and in 2024 a war-related security posture significantly reduced pilgrimages without a formal closure.[1]

## 2026 clergy exclusion

On March 29, 2026, [[israeli-police|Israeli police]] barred Latin Patriarch [[pierbattista-pizzaballa|Pierbattista Pizzaballa]] and other senior Catholic leaders from entering the church for a private Mass, an act Catholic authorities called unprecedented in centuries.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that at no previous point had church leaders themselves been denied access or prevented from holding Mass, framing the 2026 episode as a categorical break and characterizing it as Israel having "cancelled Holy Week for Christians, 'for your safety.'"[1] She connected the sequence to the theory that the 2020 global transformation was a ritual event, calling the seven-year pattern compelling.[1] Following international criticism, [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] directed that access be restored.[1]
