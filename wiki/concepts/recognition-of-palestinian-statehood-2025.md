# Recognition of Palestinian Statehood (2025)

In late September 2025, Britain, Canada and Australia, along with several smaller countries, officially recognized the state of Palestine in the run-up to a United Nations conference devoted to the two-state solution.[1]

The announcements came on a Sunday, with the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]], [[canada|Canada]] and Australia declaring recognition in the hope that a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]] would bring peace with Israel, over the objections of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[2] British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] said the move was intended "to revive the hope of peace for the Palestinians and Israelis," noting in a video message posted on X that "we recognized the state of Israel more than 75 years ago as a homeland for the Jewish people" and that London was now joining over 150 countries recognizing a Palestinian state.[2] Starmer insisted the recognition was not a reward for [[hamas|Hamas]], the group that carried out the 7 October 2023 attack on Israel in which some 1,200 people, mostly civilians, were killed and 251 abducted, and which remains the governing body in the Gaza Strip.[2] Netanyahu responded by vowing that "it will not happen," declaring that "a Palestinian state will not be established west of the Jordan River."[2]

## Criticism

For all the ceremony and celebration, it was not clear whether the pronouncements actually mattered.[1] Critics labeled the recognition effort "empty," "a distraction," or "even harmful."[1] The diplomatic declarations do nothing to help Palestinians in [[gaza|Gaza]] or those menaced by Israeli settler violence in the West Bank, and will not arrest the gradual, de facto annexation of occupied Palestinian areas under the successive governments of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

The recognizing countries insisted that [[hamas|Hamas]] should have no role in Palestinian governance, but such pledges do not change the fact that the group remains the dominant Palestinian power in Gaza and still holds dozens of Israeli hostages, despite the Gazan population's desperation for the war to end.[1] These complications suggest that recognizing a Palestinian state that does not actually exist, governed by people who are not currently in charge, is not a solution but rather a restatement of the problem.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that since 7 October 2023 key world leaders — among them Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Mohammed bin Salman — have endorsed a two-state solution, and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has now proposed a peace plan that paves the way to exactly that.[1] He added, sardonically, that the post was "obviously NOT paid for by Israel."[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted Netanyahu's refusal with claims made in October 2023 that Israel's leadership were earnest peace-seekers while Arabs were the intransigent party, asking where those apologists stood in light of the recognition wave.[2] He suggested the episode helped explain why [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] had felt the urge to deviate from "the issued talking points," arguing that the Zionist position was untenable on a long enough timescale.[2]
