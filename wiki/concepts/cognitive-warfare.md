# Cognitive Warfare

Cognitive warfare, also described as the "cognitive battlespace," is the domain of conflict in which competing factions fight for control of perception, belief and narrative rather than physical territory.[1]

## Framing in Badlands coverage

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the struggle between international "establishment" forces — the World Bank, the IMF, the military-industrial complex, NATO, the United Nations and the U.S. interagency "blob" — and populist forces, currently largely right-wing and independent, has manifested in many ways but is primarily fought in the cognitive battlespace.[1]

## Weapons of the domain

Drawing an explicit analogy to kinetic conflict, the article observed that in physical warfare a weapon of mass destruction delivers massive destructive force through the transfer of physical energy on impact; in cognitive or information warfare, the closest equivalent is the apparatus built over the preceding decade and commonly called the [[censorship-industrial-complex|Censorship-Industrial Complex]].[1]

## Actors and infrastructure

The infrastructure of cognitive warfare described in the article includes state bodies such as the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] and its successor [[counter-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-hub|R/FIMI]], allied foreign operations such as the UK's [[institute-for-statecraft|Institute for Statecraft]], and civil society funders including the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]], the [[tides-foundation|Tides Foundation]] and the [[rockefeller-brothers-fund|Rockefeller Brothers Fund]].[1] Beyond censorship, the article states that collaboration among these sectors extends into war propaganda, regime-change operations, public narrative seeding and social engineering.[1]

## Current phase

With U.S. government elements of the apparatus being dismantled under the Trump administration, the article contends that the centre of gravity in the cognitive contest has shifted to the European Union and NATO, chiefly through the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[1]
