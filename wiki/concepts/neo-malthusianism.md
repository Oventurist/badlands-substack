# Neo-Malthusianism

Neo-Malthusianism, in the usage of Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], is "a new breed of misanthropic statecraft" that captured the levers of Western foreign and domestic economic policy in the late 1960s and 1970s.[1] Ehret argues that it took power "over the dead bodies of pro-development leaders" including [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], [[enrico-mattei|Enrico Mattei]], Dag Hammarskjöld, Daniel Johnson Sr., [[robert-f-kennedy|Bobby Kennedy]] and [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]][1]

## Doctrine

As the neo-Malthusian revolution spread through Western governments, studies such as *[[limits-to-growth|Limits to Growth]]* (1972) promoted a "new wisdom" of de-growth and ecosystem conservation as a replacement for an "old wisdom" that cherished big infrastructure projects and scientific discovery as the driving ethic of humanity.[1] Under the new age of digital computer modeling, ecosystems were increasingly defined as closed thermodynamic processes bounded by mathematical homeostasis, with humanity expected to adapt to those supposed limits like any other beast within nature.[1]

Ehret cites [[maurice-lamontagne|Maurice Lamontagne]], a founder of the Canadian branch of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]] and former President of the Privy Council Office, whose 1968–1972 Science Policy Senate Reports asked: "Nature imposes definite constraints on technology itself... But then, an obvious question arises: How can we stop man's creativeness?"[1] Lamontagne further prescribed that additional R&D effort "be devoted to the life sciences and social sciences rather than the physical sciences… to economic and social objectives rather than curiosity and discovery."[1]

## Consequences for science policy

Ehret contends that the 1970s through the 1990s saw the largest onslaught on all domains of science policy involving discoveries into the universe of the immeasurably large (space science) and the immeasurably small (atomic science) — precisely those fields that threatened to disrupt the population-limit formulas neo-Malthusians required in their quest for total power under a New World Order.[1] He links this outlook to the dissolution of the [[apollo-program|Apollo Program]], the 1972 dismantling of the [[nerva-program|NERVA]] nuclear rocket programs, and the diversion of [[nasa|NASA]] funding into the war in Vietnam.[1] In contrast, he holds that outer space, being intrinsically open and composed almost entirely of undiscovered processes, is open to creative thought and unbounded growth — a prospect he says imperialists who locate humanity's causal nexus in genes or geography rather than ideas "obviously hate."[1]
