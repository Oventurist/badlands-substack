# Process Formalism

"Process formalism" is a legal-commentary term for strict adherence to procedural rules — venue, jurisdiction, class certification, adversarial fact-finding, and the ordinary sequence of appellate review — irrespective of the substantive merits or political stakes of a case. The concept became a focal point of conservative legal criticism in April 2025 in an essay published by Reason's Volokh Conspiracy, contrasting the treatment of Texas federal judges with the conduct of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]

## The Texas comparison

The essay argued that for much of the preceding five years, federal judges in Texas were slandered and maligned as rogue partisan hacks, targeted with non-stop attacks egged on by social-media pundits, blitzed with calls to their chambers, subjected to countless misconduct complaints, featured on billboards, and threatened with death — conduct that led to criminal indictments and that the piece described as far worse than the delivery of pizzas to judges.[1] The federal judiciary's institutional response, it charged, was to try to ram through an illegal rule stripping those judges of their cases while meeting the threats with silence.[1]

The piece conceded that plaintiffs forum shopped, but noted that the Biden administration never argued venue was improper; that when Texas judges issued national injunctions or vacaturs they stayed their rulings to permit timely government appeals; and that the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|Fifth Circuit]] moved promptly on its emergency docket to allow timely Supreme Court review.[1] While their rulings were fair game for substantive criticism, the author concluded that over four years the Texas judges largely followed fair procedures.[1]

## Alleged abandonment under the second Trump administration

The essay contended that the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration brought a different wave of problems: district judges permitted damages suits against the federal government that clearly belonged in the Court of Federal Claims; habeas actions were brought on the east coast when the prisoners were confined in Texas; suits seeking reinstatement of federal employees bypassed the MSPB and other civil-service forums; and judges certified class actions during ex parte TRO hearings without regard for Rule 23.[1] At every instance, the author wrote, judges abandoned any pretense of process formalism — denying Trump the presumption of substantive regularity while themselves abandoning any presumption of procedural regularity.[1]

The piece closed with a warning about Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]]: "John Roberts is ill equipped to keep the Court away from an actual constitutional crisis. At this point, he is squirming in a pit of quick sand. The more he flails his arms, the quicker he will sink. Anyone who reaches out to the Chief will descend just the same."[1]
