# Kobayashi Maru

"Kobayashi Maru" is a term borrowed from *Star Trek*, where it names a training simulation designed as an unwinnable no-win scenario. In Badlands analysis it is used as a framework for describing engineered strategic traps in geopolitics.[1]

## Application to the Iran war

In March 2026, as [[nato|NATO]] allies refused [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s calls for an international coalition to secure the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], parts of the alternative media described the situation as a classic Kobayashi Maru for the Trump administration: retreating risked a Suez Crisis–style geopolitical defeat, while continuing the campaign threatened prolonged energy-market chaos and higher global inflation.[1]

## Burning Bright's inversion

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the alternative media had adopted his framing — which he says he has employed since 2023 — but argued they had it backwards.[1] In his reading, the "Iranian Knot" is not Trump's Kobayashi Maru; it is the no-win scenario Trump has deliberately engineered for the globalist enemy that has long disguised itself as America's allies.[1] He describes the operation as placing the architecture of the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]], the [[neoconservatives|neoconservative]] war machine, the energy cartels and the "scarcity engineers" into a pincer movement from which there is no clean or face-saving exit.[1]

Burning Bright points to Europe and NATO — the entities he says spent decades weaponizing energy chokepoints against American interests — now twisting in winds they helped generate, while Trump calmly states the conflict will end "soon," that oil prices will drop dramatically, and that American interests never required allied support.[1] He argues this pattern is not a man falling backwards into victory but evidence of a deliberate method, resting on what he calls the complete inversion of the Axis and Allies paradigm.[1]

## Application to Biden and the Gaza crisis

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] invoked the Kobayashi Maru in October 2023 to describe the position of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] during the Israel crisis, writing that "the Kobayashi Maru is a training exercise in the Star Trek franchise designed to test the character of Starfleet Academy cadets in a no-win scenario," and that Biden had been placed — "wittingly or unwittingly" — into just such a scenario, in both its actual and narrative dimensions.[2] The framing followed the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|al-Ahli Baptist Hospital explosion]], which boxed Biden in before he had even landed in Israel, collapsing his Jordan summit and generating pressure to condemn an ally.[2] Burning Bright described the resulting terrain as one Biden had to navigate as "a 'leader' in name only who's being increasingly framed as feckless and incompetent by a bipartisan electorate," concluding: "Sucks to be him."[2]

## Trump and the Middle East (2025)

In a January 2025 Badlands News Brief essay, contributor Burning Bright described the Middle East as the Kobayashi Maru of both the Info War and the Shadow War, translated to the masses through what he termed the War of Stories.[3] He observed that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had begun routinely invoking the refrain to describe the position of the sociopolitical establishment as the Trump era rose to new heights.[3] Recounting the fictional Star Trek test in which prospective Starfleet captains face an impossible scenario, he emphasized that James T. Kirk refused to accept its parameters — which he judged nihilist and defeatist — and reprogrammed the simulation with new victory conditions, rescuing the stranded starship while keeping his own crew alive.[3]

On that reading, Donald Trump is "the James T. Kirk of the Info War and the Shadow War," and his Kobayashi Maru is the Middle East.[3] Burning Bright tied this to the "Saving Israel for last" comms threaded through the Q drops, argued that Trump's Gaza peace-deal narrative had placed several establishment factions into their own versions of the impossible test, and concluded that "from the Doha Agreement to the Abraham Accords to 'not so Greater' Israel, Trump isn't merely solving the Kobayashi Maru ... he IS the Kobayashi Maru."[3]
