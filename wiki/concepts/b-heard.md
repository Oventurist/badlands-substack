# Behavioral Health Emergency Assistance Response Division (B-HEARD)

The Behavioral Health Emergency Assistance Response Division, known as B-HEARD, is a New York City pilot program launched in 2021 that routes certain 911 calls to health-led teams rather than police officers.[1] As of late 2025 it operated only in some neighborhoods, with responding teams composed of two New York City Fire Department (FDNY) officers and EMTs plus one social worker.[1]

## Audit findings

A New York City comptroller report on the pilot produced unfavorable results.[1] More than 60% of calls received were ineligible for a B-HEARD response, and 35% of calls that were deemed eligible were not serviced by the division.[1] The reasons for calls receiving no response are unknown, because the Mayor's Office of Community Mental Health does not track that information.[1]

## Relevance to the Mamdani administration

The audit gained new salience because New York City Mayor-elect [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] announced plans to deploy social workers on 911 calls in place of police officers, expanding on the model B-HEARD had piloted.[1] The stated mission of Mamdani's initiative, per its proposal, is "to prevent violence before it happens by taking a public health approach to safety."[1] Reporting by One America News framed the existing program's performance as evidence that the plan "does not look promising."[1]

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the coming New York experiment would be politically instructive for the midterms and advised residents to take personal precautions because dialing 911 was likely to become even more unreliable.[1]
