# McCarthyism

McCarthyism is the term applied to the anti-communist investigations conducted by Senator [[joseph-mccarthy|Joseph McCarthy]] of Wisconsin during the 1950s, and by extension to the political climate those investigations produced.[1] In the Badlands Media account the word functions primarily as a slur devised by McCarthy's opponents, who possessed larger media platforms and greater numbers than he did.[1]

## The Badlands revision

The article argues that McCarthy was substantively correct: communists had in fact infiltrated Washington, D.C. and the media, and were planning to take over America from the inside.[1] Nearly everyone McCarthy accused of being a communist was, the article contends, actually a communist.[1] He nonetheless became the villain of the episode because "the media and other politicians had louder megaphones, and there were more of them" — an illustration of the article's broader claim that perceived truth often amounts to nothing more than the number of people espousing a position.[1]

McCarthy was ridiculed and demonized in his own time, dismissed as a conspiracy theorist and a fascist, and the history books have subsequently rendered him a pariah and "a stain on America."[1] The article draws an explicit parallel between this treatment and the contemporary demonization of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and more generally argues that Americans have been conditioned to reject any discussion of communist infiltration precisely because of how the McCarthy episode was written up.[1]

## Symington and the Army–McCarthy hearings

Among the enemies McCarthy made while pursuing suspected communists in the government and military was [[stuart-symington|Stuart Symington]], United States Secretary of the Air Force from 1947 to 1950 and later a senator, who became a major critic of McCarthy and of McCarthyism.[1] McCarthy reciprocated the disdain, referring to Symington as "Sanctimonious Stu" even in official Senate hearings.[1] McCarthy's chief counsel during the [[army-mccarthy-hearings|Army–McCarthy hearings]] of 1954 was [[roy-cohn|Roy Cohn]], who later mentored Trump.[1]
