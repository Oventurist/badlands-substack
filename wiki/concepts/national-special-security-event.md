# National Special Security Event

A National Special Security Event (NSSE) is a U.S. federal designation applied to events of the highest national significance, triggering a coordinated security plan led by the [[united-states-secret-service|U.S. Secret Service]] in collaboration with federal, state, and local partners.[1] Typical examples include the Super Bowl and the U.N. General Assembly.[1]

## Application to the January 6, 2025 certification

In September 2024 Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] designated the January 6, 2025 certification of the presidential vote as a National Special Security Event — the first time the federal government had applied the status to that proceeding, and an acknowledgment that a once-routine part of the democratic process now carried special risk.[1] The designation placed the [[congress|Congress]]ional session on the same security footing as the Super Bowl and authorized measures aimed at preventing a reprise of the 2021 Capitol riot by supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] seeking to overturn the 2020 election results.[1]

Eric Ranaghan, special agent in charge of the Secret Service's Dignitary Protective Division, said that "National Special Security Events are events of the highest national significance" and that the Service and its partners were committed to a comprehensive and integrated security plan for the event and its participants.[1] Reporting tied the announcement to a tense political environment, with polls showing Trump and Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in a close race before a bitterly divided electorate.[1]
