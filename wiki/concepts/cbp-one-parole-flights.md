# CBP One parole flights

The **CBP One parole flights** program refers to a [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration practice under which [[customs-and-border-protection|U.S. Customs and Border Protection]] approved flights carrying inadmissible aliens from foreign airports directly into U.S. airports, bypassing the land border, with travel pre-approved through the CBP One cell phone application.[1]

Disclosures obtained through Freedom of Information Act litigation by the [[center-for-immigration-studies|Center for Immigration Studies]] revealed that in 2023 alone the program moved 320,000 inadmissible aliens into roughly 43 American airports between January and December.[1] CBP declined to identify the receiving U.S. airports or the foreign airports of departure, with agency lawyers initially invoking a general "law enforcement exception" and the "sensitivity of the information."[1]

The litigation later produced a more specific government justification: the receiving airports could not be named because the volume of CBP-authorized arrivals had created "operational vulnerabilities" such that "bad actors" could undermine law enforcement efforts to "secure the United States border" if they knew how much CBP One traffic each port of entry processed.[1] Critics characterized this as an admission that a legally dubious program had generated law enforcement vulnerabilities too grave to disclose publicly.[1] Coverage also noted that large immigrant-receiving cities and much of the press had attributed the influx instead to Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]]'s migrant busing program.[1]
