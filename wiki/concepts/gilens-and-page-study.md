# Gilens and Page Study on American Politics

"Testing Theories of American Politics: Elites, Interest Groups, and Average Citizens" is a September 2014 study by Princeton University professor Martin Gilens and Northwestern University professor Benjamin Page, published in *Perspectives on Politics*, that Badlands Media cites as academic confirmation of elite capture of the United States government.[1]

The finding quoted is that "multivariate analysis indicates that economic elites and organized groups representing business interests have substantial independent impacts on U.S. government policy, while average citizens and mass-based interest groups have little or no independent influence."[1]

## Use in the Badlands argument

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] deployed the study to answer the question of whose interests the country serves, arguing that the people barely scrape by while their representatives and corporate partners take home handsome rewards, and that the public is made to serve a government that in turn serves people who do not have its best interests at heart.[1] "Influence," in his gloss, means power — the ability to contribute to outcomes affecting one's own life — and he observed that most citizens, asked how much of it they have, answer "little to none," despite modern civilization depending entirely on their participation.[1] The study sits alongside the [[first-inaugural-address-of-donald-trump|2017 inaugural address]] and the Thomas Jefferson banking quotation as pillars of the essay's case that [[donald-trump|Trump]] correctly identified an unrepresentative system.[1]
