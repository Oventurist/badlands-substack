# Conservatism

**Conservatism**, in the analysis advanced by the Badlands Media contributor [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], is defined not by policy platform but by disposition: it "ultimately comes from a love of one's own people and country."[1] True conservatism, the author writes, "is nothing other than the conviction that one's people, one's nation, and one's traditions shall go on" — asking rhetorically what else there could be to *conserve*.[1] It is summarized as being "fundamentally about preservation of oneself and one's people."[1]

## The Missing Ingredient

The article grants that conservatives have their share of mental illness — "it's a rare person who can maintain their sanity whilst living in Clown World" — but argues that conservatives possess something absent in liberals and leftists, and that this missing ingredient explains the higher rates of diagnosed mental illness and pharmaceutical use among the latter.[1] That ingredient is the attachment to one's own people and nation.[1]

The author explicitly excludes so-called neoconservatism from the category, describing it as "a total aberration" and noting that Badlands readers likely already understand that neocons are anything but conservative, however they present themselves on television.[1]

## In-Group Bias

Conservatives are described as having a higher in-group bias than liberals — which the author glosses as being "inherently proud of who they are" in a way liberals on average are not.[1] This is offered as the reason conservatives readily recognize the existential threat that transformation by mass migration poses to their people and culture, while liberals and leftists are comparatively easy to persuade that such transformation is desirable.[1]

## Evolutionary Framing

Conservatism and [[liberalism]]/[[leftism]] are presented as "two survival and replication strategies that are optimized for different environments," a thesis the author credits to [[the-evolutionary-psychology-behind-politics|The Evolutionary Psychology Behind Politics]].[1] Within [[r-k-selection-theory|r/K selection theory]], conservatism corresponds to the K-selected strategy: high parental investment, the competitive nuclear family, tight in-group bonds, restraint in reproduction, and conservation of resources against scarcity.[1]

## The Amygdala Question

The article addresses a long-circulating claim that conservatives are more bigoted because their amygdalas are larger, and that this proves them primitive and stupid. The author dismisses these conclusions as non sequiturs.[1] He accepts that conservatives have statistically been observed to have larger amygdalas, but argues this correlates with a *stronger ability to correctly perceive threats*.[1] Citing the same book, he identifies the anterior cingulate cortex — rather than the amygdala — as the region governing fear responses to adverse stimuli, noting as an aside that the anterior cingulate cortex figures prominently in measured hypnotic states.[1] He speculates in the reverse direction that liberals may have small or shrunken amygdalas, which could explain their difficulty perceiving the risks of mass illegal immigration or cultural erasure, and their taboo on the right to self-defense.[1]

## Historical Cycle

In the author's reformulation of the four-stage historical cycle, K-selected (conservative) men create conditions of material success and abundance; that abundance produces r-selected men who squander the civilizational inheritance; and hard times in turn regenerate K-selected men.[1] He locates America's decline phase in the 1960s and argues the country is now entering the regenerative fourth phase.[1]
