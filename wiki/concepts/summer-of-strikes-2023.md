# Summer of Strikes (2023)

The "Summer of Strikes" is the label applied to the wave of labor actions that swept across the United States during 2023, a surge described as unprecedented in recent memory and one that unfolded alongside new wars, a House "leadership crisis," escalating weaponization of government, and what the source characterizes as an irreparably broken economy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Scale

Citing CNBC, the reporting states that there were 312 strikes involving roughly 453,000 workers in 2023 to that point, compared with 180 strikes involving 43,700 workers over the same period two years earlier — figures attributed to Johnnie Kallas, a PhD candidate at Cornell University's School of Industrial and Labor Relations and project director of the ILR Labor Action Tracker.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Axios described the broader phenomenon as "worker activism," which extended well beyond the headline disputes: 84 Amazon workers, 1,400 electrical workers, 3,000 Starbucks workers, and 75,000 workers at Kaiser Permanente were among those taking action as calls to reshape the employee–employer relationship spread through 2023.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Headline disputes

The most visible actions were the [[writers-guild-of-america|Writers Guild of America]] strike in Hollywood and the [[united-auto-workers|United Auto Workers]] strike against the major automakers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The WGA strike lasted almost 150 days before writers voted to end it on Wednesday, September 27, with 99% of members ratifying the resulting contract the following Monday.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The UAW action made labor history when, on the same day the writers reached agreement with the studios, picketers were joined for the first time by a sitting US President, [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], in Wayne County, Michigan.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Interpretation

The article's central argument is that these strikes, however well-coordinated, on-message, and popularly supported, were fought against employers acting on "black or red economics" — the arithmetic of profit and loss — at a moment when it simply takes fewer people to deliver the same products and services.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Even as the UAW demanded a four-day work week, automakers continued issuing layoffs, with as much as 62% of some manufacturing workforces eliminated in the middle of the strike.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

The workers' expectations — better pay, better conditions, the ability to have a life — are framed as recalling a simpler America in which an electrician or steelworker could own a home and retire on a pension, a world the author considers unrecoverable without dismantling what is called the global corporate communist machine.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Supporting that view, the piece cites Bureau of Labor Statistics data showing dual-income households have been a majority for at least two decades, ranging from 52 to 58 percent between 1998 and 2017; declining US birth rates, down 20% over the past decade, with roughly 3.66 million live births in 2021 per Statista; and pension systems described as insolvent.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) These conditions are said not to have arisen organically, but to resemble groundwork for the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] and a New World Order, producing demoralization, disillusionment, and demands detached from reality.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

The article further argues the strikes came too late: the true "time to strike" would have been at the latest 2017, before the infrastructure of the [[future-of-work|future of work]] was laid by firms such as [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Because the demands across industries are judged self-interested rather than aimed at protecting liberty and way of life, the strikes are described as both a first and a final stand — one likely already too late, with any deals short-lived against a longer game.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)
