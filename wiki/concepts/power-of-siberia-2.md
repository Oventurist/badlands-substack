# Power of Siberia 2

Power of Siberia 2 is a long-discussed natural gas pipeline project intended to carry Russian gas to China, which Moscow views as critical for redirecting energy exports away from Europe.[1]

At the May 2026 Beijing summit, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] reportedly failed to secure final Chinese approval for the project despite the meeting producing more than 40 agreements covering trade, technology, energy, media cooperation, and strategic coordination.[1] Pricing and long-term dependency concerns reportedly remained unresolved, and the impasse was cited as evidence of the limits of the Russia–China partnership even as the two governments signed a joint declaration calling for a [[multipolar-world|multipolar world]].[1]

## Trans-Kazakh rerouting question (2025)

At the start of 2025, Russian Energy Minister [[alexander-novak|Alexander Novak]] confirmed that estimates, a feasibility study, and negotiations were underway for a Russian gas pipeline to China routed through [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]].[2] Analyst [[andrew-korybko|Andrew Korybko]] argued the statement functioned as a message to the United States rather than proof of a finalized project, and cautioned against the conclusion — advanced by Asia Times' Yong Jian — that the trans-Kazakh proposal represented an agreed rerouting of Power of Siberia II.[2] The underlying obstacle remained the Sino-Russian pricing dispute, with China demanding rates reportedly equivalent to Russia's domestic prices while Russia sought better terms.[2]
