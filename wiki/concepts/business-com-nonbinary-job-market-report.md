# Business.com Nonbinary Job Market Report

The **Business.com nonbinary job market report** is a study published in early March 2023 by the business resource platform Business.com examining discrimination against nonbinary job seekers, which was covered by CNBC under the headline that resumes including "they/them" pronouns are more likely to be overlooked.[1]

## Findings

The report found that more than 80% of nonbinary people believe that identifying as nonbinary would hurt their job search, and that 51% believe their gender identity has affected their workplace experience "very or somewhat negatively."[1] Coverage framed the results as evidence that inclusivity should be practiced during hiring as well as in the workplace, and that nonbinary job seekers face clear biases during their search.[1] Ryan McGonagill, director of industry research at Business.com and the report's author, said the statistics showed how much work remained to be done around diversity, equity, inclusion and belonging in the workplace.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] read the findings in the opposite direction, arguing that stereotypes serve as imperfect but useful rapid assessments of character and that the presence of preferred pronouns on a resume allows employers to make such an assessment.[1] He associated militancy about preferred pronouns with fragility, "renegade" temperament and reduced dedication to the duties of a job, and characterized it as indicative of selfish narcissism.[1]

Deschamps generalized the point into a claim about civic decline, describing dedication to duty as the most essential skill a citizen can bring to a community and framing civilization as a collective arrangement in which people accept obligations in exchange for security and freedom.[1] While allowing that compassion for what he called the victims of cultural warfare tactics is warranted, he argued that compassion must not come at the cost of society as a whole, that the rewards of modern life are the collective product of the labour of duty-executing men and women rather than an entitlement, and that a society permitting the young to claim unearned rewards replaces a culture of excellence with "an entitlement to mediocrity."[1] He concluded with the image of a beast that "will not be satisfied with just a pound of flesh" and must be tamed lest "the village lose everything to the monster of chaos."[1]
