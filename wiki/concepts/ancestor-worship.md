# Ancestor Worship

**Ancestor worship**, as used in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — What is Clown World?", refers to the practice attributed to the [[koryos|Koryos]] cult of allowing themselves to be possessed by "ancestor spirits," which the author identifies as demons rather than deceased relatives.[1]

The essay argues that the "ancestors" in question are not grandparents or great-grandparents but the first creators of the werewolf bloodline, who may not have been purely physical beings.[1] The Koryos are said to have channeled these entities because doing so granted superhuman strength; in exchange, the entities obtained what they wanted — access to a physical body.[1] This trade underpins the author's broader claim that demons must act through human beings because they possess no bodies of their own with which to enact their will in the physical realm.[1]

Extending the logic, the essay proposes that one can channel a demon simply by dressing up as it, and that clown costumes are therefore themselves a form of demon worship — a reading the author attributes to [[paul-stobbs|Paul Stobbs]].[1] On this view the Koryos are a "physicalized" version of ancient clown-demons, and the spread of clown aesthetics through popular culture has increased those beings' influence over mankind, in keeping with the maxim that "society is the devil's masquerade."[1] The author cautions that gambles with the dark never pay off forever, since the bill always comes due.[1]
