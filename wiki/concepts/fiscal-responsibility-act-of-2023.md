# Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023

The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 was a bipartisan bill negotiated between House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that suspended the federal [[debt-ceiling|debt ceiling]] until January 2025 while imposing certain spending limits.[1]

## Passage

The legislation moved through [[congress|Congress]] in late May and early June 2023, clearing the House on May 31, 2023 by a vote of 314-117.[1] Its path to the floor ran through the House Rules Committee, where Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] cast what was described as a pivotal vote to advance the bill despite opposition from other conservative members; Massie then voted in favor on final passage.[1]

## Significance in the Massie controversy

Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated the vote as the strongest evidence of inconsistency in Massie's record, noting that it marked the first time in Massie's congressional tenure that he had voted to increase the debt ceiling after consistently opposing such measures.[1] Carlson contrasted that accommodation of Biden with Massie's refusal to support a [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in March 2025, asking what conclusion other than [[political-grandstanding|grandstanding]] could be drawn: "When your vote matters, you go along with the group. But when your vote doesn't matter, you vote in opposition, parading around like you are constitutionally superior to others."[1] The account of the bill's terms and vote totals cited in the article was drawn from the Grok AI assistant rather than primary legislative records.[1]
