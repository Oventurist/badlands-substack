# Fannie Mae Minimum Credit Score Elimination

In November 2025 [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] announced that it would scrap the minimum credit score requirement of 620 for loans submitted through its Desktop Underwriter engine, a change taking effect that weekend.[1] The move followed a similar step taken several years earlier by fellow mortgage middleman [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]], and was expected to allow more Americans to qualify for loans from lenders that review borrower information through those automated underwriting systems.[1]

## Rationale and expected effects

Studies of Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) data identify credit histories — which credit scores are intended to represent — as one of the principal reasons property loans are denied, so eliminating the scoring floor was framed as a potential leg-up for buyers facing an increasingly inaccessible property ladder.[1] Bankrate financial analyst Stephen Kates told Newsweek that "prior to this change, a score of 619 would result in an automatic disqualification, regardless of a borrower's broader financial profile," and that "this person could now potentially qualify for a mortgage."[1]

Jake Krimmel, senior economist at Realtor.com, characterized the change as "a small positive demand shock" concentrated at the entry level of the American housing market.[1] He expected any lift in buying power to appear "in more affordable metros and lower price tiers, where credit-constrained buyers are clustered and particularly across the South where sub-620 credit scores are more common."[1] Krimmel also cautioned that widening access in this way could put upward pressure on prices: "while the policy change is trying to make homes more affordable for low-score borrowers, a jump in house values could price them out again."[1]

## Related policy: portable mortgages

The credit-score change was reported alongside remarks by [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]], director of the [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]], who said on November 12 that the administration was "actively evaluating [[portable-mortgages|portable mortgages]]," days after saying Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were examining how to offer assumable or portable mortgages "in a safe and sound manner."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the announcement as a procedural change to a trigger for automatic decline rather than an alteration of the overall creditworthiness calculus, ensuring that a score would not unjustly outweigh other factors.[1] She noted that a run of recent mortgage-related stories had all been messaged as making home *ownership* more accessible while none claimed to make *homes* more affordable, benefiting the financial services industries at the expense of the public.[1] In her framing, a low credit score unlocking "50 years of debt slavery" is a genuine win for lenders, and she expressed skepticism that portable mortgages would actually arrive given their impact on those industries.[1] She closed by invoking the previous relaxation of borrowing criteria by Fannie and Freddie two decades earlier: "slowly, then all at once."[1]
