# Canadian Electoral Reform Push (2024)

The Canadian electoral reform push of 2024 was a quiet negotiation between the governing Liberals and the New Democratic Party to table amendments to the Canada Elections Act before the next federal vote, undertaken within the parties' confidence-and-supply agreement.[1]

## Negotiations

As progress on other measures in the Liberal-NDP confidence-and-supply agreement played out publicly, the two parties held private talks on electoral reform legislation.[1] Leading the negotiations on the political front were Public Safety, Democratic Institutions and Intergovernmental Affairs Minister Dominic LeBlanc for the government and NDP MP and democratic reform critic Daniel Blaikie for his party.[1] In an interview with CTVNews.ca between NDP caucus retreat sessions in Edmonton, Blaikie said there had been "a fair amount of work done" toward drafting amendments to the Canada Elections Act.[1]

The package fell short of the full-scale overhaul of the federal voting system Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] had once promised, instead comprising a series of proposals aimed at expanding "the ability for people to vote."[1]

## Key Proposals

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] identified three headline items: allowing an "expanded" three-day voting period during general elections; allowing voters to cast ballots at any polling place within their riding; and improving the mail-in ballot process with both accessibility and integrity in mind.[1]
