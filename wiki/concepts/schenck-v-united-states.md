# Schenck v. United States

Schenck v. United States is the U.S. Supreme Court decision that produced the "shouting fire in a crowded theatre" formulation, a phrase repeatedly invoked in modern political debate to justify limits on speech.[1]

## Origin of the phrase

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the phrase originated in an attempt by the U.S. government to prosecute people for circulating leaflets opposing the use of the draft under Woodrow Wilson to enter World War One.[1] In his account, the only reason the phrase existed in the first place was the same reason it is deployed today: the government wanted to control speech and root out dissent.[1]

## Status as "bad law"

DeLarme described the decision from which the phrase originated as "bad law" — an outdated statute no longer applicable as a result of the comprehensive corpus of [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] law developed since.[1] He noted that the trope has nothing to do with political expression: while some limits on speech exist, particularly for speech facilitating fraud or defamation, the specific act of yelling fire in a crowded theatre is not necessarily illegal, though it might support a disorderly conduct charge.[1]

## Use in the 2024 campaign

The trope resurfaced in the [[2024-vice-presidential-debate|2024 vice presidential debate]], where [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] deployed it in response to a rebuttal from [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]] regarding the claim that misinformation constitutes a "threat to democracy."[1] DeLarme treated the reappearance of the phrase as evidence that the argumentative machinery of speech suppression is recycled across a century of American politics.[1]
