# WWG1WGA (Where We Go One, We Go All)

**WWG1WGA** — an acronym for the phrase "Where we go one, we go all" — is a slogan associated with the [[q-drops|Q posts]], used often by what adherents call the Q team, and has become a rallying cry within the "truth community" and MAGA to the point that people who have never read a single Q post may still recognise it as a Trump-supporting battle cry.[1]

## Origin

The phrase originates in [[white-squall|White Squall]], a 1996 film directed by Ridley Scott and starring Jeff Bridges.[1] In the film a group of young men sail a ship captained by Bridges's character and run into a massive storm that comes out of nowhere; before the storm the crew was disjointed, selfish, undisciplined and refused to work together, and the sudden squall forced them to put away petty disagreements and function as a cohesive team to keep the ship from going under.[1] [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on February 14, 2024, places [[ridley-scott|Ridley Scott]] among directors — including Scott's brother Tony, Mel Gibson, Christopher Nolan and Stanley Kubrick — whom he believes have used their films to communicate coded messaging about conspiracies.[1]

## Interpretations

Carlson identifies several layers of meaning. At the most immediate level, many read WWG1WGA as the Q team signalling that it is fighting for God-fearing, red-blooded Americans who want to restore the country to what it was intended to be, and offering support to conservative Trump supporters who believe the only way to save the nation is to drain the swamp of the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1] In this sense it functions as a pep talk to people who have put reputations and relationships on the line by speaking what they hold to be the truth and refusing to bend a knee to [[cabal|Cabal]] narratives, assuring them they are not alone and their pursuits are not in vain.[1]

A second reading is exhortative: those fighting for the country must stick together, set aside childish disagreements, and work as a team, because a divided side cannot survive the storm.[1]

The third and broadest reading — captured in the essay's subtitle "I Think They Meant Everyone" — treats patriot unity as merely a microcosm. On this view the nation as a whole must come together: conservatives and liberals, Christians and atheists, Blacks and Whites, gays and straights, young and old must find common ground to take back America and, through it, the world from what Carlson calls globalist, satanist elites.[1] This does not require agreement on everything, only cooperation, since division among the "peasants" is exactly what the Cabal needs to maintain control.[1]

## The mountain metaphor

Carlson reframes national restoration as a hike to a mountain peak rather than a race in which fellow Americans are competitors; the summit represents an America no longer run by mind-controlling globalists.[1] Because the majority of the country must reach the peak for the desired change to occur, and roughly 4–6% of people — the figure Q cites — will never make it, those who arrive first should descend to help the struggling: walking beside them, offering encouragement, carrying their backpacks and guiding them onto the best path.[1] Some hikers lag from lack of strength or stamina, others from poor navigation skills that have taken them off course.[1]

## Application and reconciliation

The essay applies the slogan practically. Carlson recounts being called irresponsible by a close family member for not vaccinating his family, and being told by acquaintances that [[donald-trump|Trump]] is a criminal, that there is no evidence the 2020 election was stolen, and that [[january-6-capitol-protest|J6]] was an attempted insurrection — sometimes angrily, sometimes condescendingly.[1] His standard reply is that he does not get his information from the [[mainstream-media|mainstream media]], which he considers compromised and paid to report disinformation, and that FOX, the Washington Post and the New York Times all fall within that category; he cites alternative media and trusted people on [[x-corp|X]], [[truth-social|Truth Social]] and [[rumble|Rumble]], followed by independent research.[1] He compares those who defend the media to Gollum from *Lord of the Rings*, guarding a ring that is slowly destroying them, and invokes "forgive them Father, for they know not what they do" to argue that most opponents have been manipulated rather than malicious.[1]

A section headed "Lukewarm" holds that comfortable Americans on both Left and Right — those who still have a job, social security or room on a credit card — never become motivated enough to act, and that hot and cold is where the action takes place.[1] The sincere vocal Right fights for justice while the sincere vocal Left, misled, unintentionally protects the system; the enemy is neither, but the Cabal elites.[1] Misled people of action, including BLM sign-holders, pink-hat marchers and pro-Hamas protesters, could become the Right's greatest allies if they broke through their conditioning, much as Saul became Paul in the New Testament.[1]

Because no one is converted by being yelled at and no one listens to someone they distrust, Carlson recommends attacking the media rather than people, noting that only one third of Americans retain any trust in the media, 40% have none, and 20,000 media jobs were lost in 2023.[1] He advises forgiveness, inviting the liberal neighbour over for dinner or a beer, buying a stranger a coffee and actually listening, rather than trying to win arguments with strangers online — an activity he likens to Don Quixote battling windmills.[1] Empathy is owed to those conditioned to accept immoral behaviour, he cautions, but not to those who commit it, especially against children.[1]

Carlson likens American division to a Chinese finger trap that tightens the harder each side pulls, and concludes that recognising that Americans are not each other's enemy is the first step.[1]
