# Trump–McCain Feud

The Trump–McCain feud was the public conflict between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Arizona senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] that began in mid-2015 and continued past McCain's death in 2018.[1] Badlands commentary treats it as a deliberate confrontation through which Trump exposed a corrupt political system and the media narratives that protected its figures.[1]

## Origins, June–July 2015

The feud began on June 30, 2015, only two weeks after Trump announced his presidential candidacy.[1] Per an AZ Central chronology, McCain distanced himself from Trump after a launch speech widely criticized for describing Mexican immigrants as "rapists" and drug runners, telling *The Arizona Republic* after a town hall at CAE Aviation Academy Phoenix at Mesa's Falcon Field airport: "I just disagree with his comments about the, quote, Mexicans."[1] On July 10, 2015 the paper published "McCain, Flake want no part of Trump's Phoenix rally," quoting McCain on MSNBC saying Trump's comments were "offensive to not only Hispanic citizenry, but other citizenry," while conceding "he's entitled to say what he wants to say."[1] Trump campaign sources confirmed Trump was aware of the article.[1]

The remarks at issue came from Trump's June 16, 2015 announcement, in which he said "When do we beat Mexico at the border? They're laughing at us, at our stupidity," and "When Mexico sends its people, they're not sending their best... They're bringing drugs. They're bringing crime. They're rapists. And some, I assume, are good people."[1]

## Escalation in Arizona

On July 11, 2015 Trump appeared at a rally at the Phoenix Convention Center and told the crowd, "We have incompetent politicians, not only the president. I mean, right here, in your own state, you have John McCain," drawing boos at the mention of McCain's name.[1] Afterward Trump added: "I've supported John McCain, but he's very weak on immigration... If the right person runs against John McCain, he will lose."[1] The Badlands account notes that Trump went into McCain's home state, called him an incompetent politician and connected him to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], and that the "weak on immigration" charge was corroborated by the Arizona Republican Party's 2014 censure of McCain and by primary challenger [[kelli-ward|Kelli Ward]]'s attacks on his support for "comprehensive immigration reform."[1]

## "He's not a war hero"

On July 18, 2015, Trump said of McCain: "He's not a war hero. He was a war hero because he was captured. I like people who weren't captured."[1] CNN reported that on March 19, 2019 — more than six months after McCain's death — Trump said, "I was never a fan of John McCain and I never will be," and treated the two comments as bookends showing how much Trump had changed Republican politics.[1] At the time of the 2015 remark, observers assumed that attacking McCain's five years as a prisoner of war in Vietnam was a "death sentence" for Trump's political ambitions, since no one had questioned McCain's service.[1] It did not destroy Trump.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The article frames the feud as information warfare, arguing that McCain started the fight by deploying the "racist" narrative against Trump as a supposed fellow Republican, which the media then echoed — an instance of what Q described as "All Assets Deployed" in drop 4645 of September 10, 2020.[1] It contends that McCain was one of the most dangerous puppets Trump faced precisely because he was a back-stabbing Republican who had cultivated an image as a war hero and bipartisan statesman through phrases such as "bipartisanship," "comprehensive" legislation, and working "across the aisle."[1] His media allies nicknamed him "Maverick" and cast him as "the voice of reason" whenever he voted against his own party.[1] The article asserts that Trump's willingness to attack him worked because a majority of Americans already knew the government was corrupt but had lacked the power to change it.[1] Trump's later insults after McCain's death are explained by the claim that Trump knew McCain was a traitor who did not deserve respect.[1]
