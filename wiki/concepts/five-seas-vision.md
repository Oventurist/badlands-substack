# Five Seas Vision

The **Five Seas Vision** is an infrastructure concept attributed to former Syrian President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], envisioning a massive integrated rail and transport network connecting five bodies of water: the Mediterranean, the Red Sea, the Black Sea, the Caspian Sea, and the Persian Gulf.[1]

## Revival through Turkish–Iraqi integration

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the 2026 sequence of energy and transport agreements across the region amounts to a de facto realization of the Five Seas Vision, notwithstanding the fall of the Assad regime.[1] Elements he cited include Turkish investment in Iraqi transportation infrastructure and a railway tying directly into [[turkey|Turkey]]'s existing rail network; an Iraq–[[iran|Iran]] railway agreement signed during [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali al-Zaidi]]'s visit to Tehran that would ultimately connect the two capitals and link Turkish and Arabian rail networks to those of Persia, the Caspian, and onward to [[russia|Russia]]; and U.S. backing for rebuilding pipelines running from the Kirkuk supergiant fields to the [[syria|Syrian]] coast and down to the al-Basra fields on the Persian Gulf.[1]

These pipelines, destroyed during the U.S. invasion of Iraq in 2003, are presented as an alternative to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Suez/Red Sea shipping route, both of which have proven troublesome in recent years.[1]

## Trump-administration linkage

The article contends that [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s "fingerprints are all over this situation," noting that U.S. envoy [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] visited [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdoğan]]'s administration the day before al-Zaidi arrived in Ankara.[1] The author concludes: "We are watching the Middle East be fixed," adding that even Iran appears to be a willing participant.[1]


## Origins and revival efforts

Badlands coverage of the [[fall-of-the-assad-regime|fall of the Assad regime]] recounted that, following the death of his older brother, the ophthalmologist [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] became the unlikely heir to his father Hafez al-Assad and chose to focus on cultivating prosperity for his people, proposing the Five Seas Vision in 2005.[2] The plan envisioned an enormous rail system connecting the Mediterranean with the Red Sea, the Black Sea, the Caspian Sea and the Persian Gulf, with Aleppo in Syria at the heart of the network.[2]

Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and the Chinese had, over the years preceding the regime's collapse, approached Assad about resurrecting the plan and implementing it as part of China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] while tying it into [[saudi-vision-2030|Saudi Vision 2030]].[2] Badlands argued that the original failure of the Five Seas Vision two decades earlier stemmed from Assad and his allies lacking the firepower to build something great in the face of adversaries who preferred to see them in stagnant subjugation, and framed the destruction of the Syrian state as an act of stifling prosperity.[2]
