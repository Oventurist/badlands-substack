# Netanyahu Corruption Trial

The Netanyahu corruption trial is the long-running Israeli criminal prosecution of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], whose charges were originally filed in 2019.[1] The proceedings have been repeatedly delayed — first by the COVID-19 pandemic, then by what Badlands Media described as a succession of excuses offered by the defendant, including a bold move to reorganize the entire judicial system so that Netanyahu's ruling party would be permitted to stack the Supreme Court with justices of its choosing.[1]

## December 2024 postponement

Three weeks before the end of December 2024, the judges presiding over the prosecution denied a request from Netanyahu's security cabinet to delay his testimony.[1] Days later, the Prime Minister's Office announced that Netanyahu had been examined at Hadassah Medical Center on a Wednesday, that a urinary tract infection caused by benign prostatic hyperplasia had been detected, that antibiotic treatment had successfully eradicated the infection, and that he would nonetheless undergo a procedure to remove the prostate the following day.[1] The office stated that the Sunday cabinet meeting would take place as planned.[1]

Netanyahu's attorney, [[amit-hadad|Amit Hadad]], obtained approval from the Attorney General's Office to cancel that week's scheduled testimony.[1] Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]] was to serve as caretaker prime minister during the incapacity, with Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] chairing the political-security cabinet.[1]

## June 2025 hearing cancellation

On Wednesday, June 11, 2025, the District Court of Tel Aviv canceled that day's hearing for Netanyahu after he requested a delay due to his health condition, according to Israeli media.[3] Public broadcaster KAN reported that the session was scrapped after Netanyahu asked for a postponement due to illness, without providing further details.[3] State prosecutors had begun cross-examining Netanyahu on June 3 after months of direct examination by his defense team; Israeli media described the cross-examination as a "dramatic shift," the first time the premier had appeared before representatives of the public prosecution in Case 1,000.[3] Netanyahu faces three separate corruption cases filed in 2019 — Case 1,000, Case 2,000 and Case 4,000 — encompassing accusations of bribery, fraud and breach of trust, all of which he denies, calling them "fake."[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] restated his view that President [[donald-trump|Trump]] collaborated with Israeli billionaire [[sheldon-adelson|Sheldon Adelson]] to bring the 2019 charges precisely because they become leverage limiting the moves available to Netanyahu, which improves planning against him from a game-theory standpoint.[3] He listed overlapping layers of leverage: the Orthodox community threatening to dissolve the government; public protests in Tel Aviv over the hostages; [[unit-8200|Unit 8200]] military intelligence officers threatening to mutiny; International Criminal Court arrest warrants against Netanyahu and his cabinet; economic sanctions from a growing list of countries; domestic political pressure to attack Iran; scandals involving Qatar, [[hamas|Hamas]] and ISIS; scandals over sadistic sex rituals and child grooming in the Orthodox community; and growing suspicion that Netanyahu himself was somehow behind the October 7th attack.[3]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the surgery as a pretext, writing that a leader willing to sacrifice his own citizens in a false flag for personal gain would also fake a prostate condition to delay a criminal proceeding.[1] The commentary linked the delays to Netanyahu's wartime posture after the [[october-7-attacks|October 7th attacks]], arguing that he had claimed to be too busy "defending Israel" while invading three foreign countries and launching airstrikes against a fourth, Iran.[1] It further noted Israel's continued aid to Ukraine following its unprecedented March 2023 decision to authorize the sale of defensive military equipment to Kyiv.[1]

## Trump's intervention, June 2025

In June 2025, days after the Israel–Iran ceasefire, U.S. President Donald Trump publicly demanded that the trial be "CANCELLED, IMMEDIATELY, or a Pardon given to a Great Hero," calling the case a politically motivated "WITCH HUNT" against a leader he described as a warrior unmatched in Israeli history.[2] Badlands analysts argued the intervention functioned in two directions at once — propping up an ally while spotlighting the corruption case as a leverage point over him.[2] Contributors also highlighted material about Hollywood producer and former Mossad agent Arnon Milchan, a central figure in the gift-giving allegations, including a claim that he delivered a Bugs Bunny doll to Yair Netanyahu at his parents' request.[2]

## June 30, 2025 hearing cancellation

Shortly after Trump publicly blasted the case, an Israeli court announced that hearings in the trial had been canceled for the week, a delay granted on the basis of pressing national security matters following Israel's military operation in Iran and the renewed campaign in Gaza.[4] The Jerusalem District Court ruled that "following the explanations given … we partially accept the request and cancel at this stage Mr. Netanyahu's hearings scheduled" for that week, a partial win for Netanyahu and his legal team in a case that had by then dragged on for four years.[4] His lawyers had sought cancellation of all hearings requiring his presence over the following two weeks, citing a schedule consumed by "managing national, diplomatic and security issues of the utmost importance."[4] The prime minister stands accused of bribery, fraud, and breach of trust after allegedly accepting tens of thousands of dollars' worth of cigars and champagne, allegations he denies.[4]

Israeli opposition politicians criticized Trump's intervention in one of the country's most controversial legal processes in years.[4] [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]], leader of the Yesh Atid party, said Israel was grateful to Trump for support in the war with Iran, during which U.S. planes bombed two Iranian nuclear facilities, but criticised "interference in the legal process" and suggested Trump was offering Netanyahu support before pressuring him to make concessions toward a new Gaza ceasefire.[4] Gilad Kariv, a parliamentarian from the Democrats party, said: "The Jewish tradition teaches us that no person is above the law. Not even a prime minister."[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the renewed delay was exactly what Trump wanted, because so long as the trial hangs over Netanyahu he remains leveraged by radical zealots in his security cabinet to continue the war and push Israel deeper into scandal.[4] He described Trump as inverting the [[strategy-of-tension|Strategy of Tension]] associated with [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] and using it against what he called the Deep State in Israel: publicly demanding peace while tacitly encouraging further conflict, thereby exposing the bloodlust of the hardline factions and generating internal tension intended to isolate the leadership rather than harm the Israeli people.[4]
