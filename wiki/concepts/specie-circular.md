# Specie Circular

The Specie Circular was an executive order issued by [[andrew-jackson|President Andrew Jackson]] in 1836 mandating that all public land sales over 320 acres be purchased in specie — that is, in gold and silver rather than paper or fiat money.[1] Like the [[deposit-act-of-1836|Deposit Act of 1836]], it diverted precious metals from the east of the country to the west.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] explains that large tracts of western land were being bought with money supplied by wealthy New York bankers, and that the order forced those buyers — including British purchasers — to surrender gold and silver, draining England's reserves.[1] British financiers expressed alarm in 1836, and when the directors of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] found their specie reserves reduced to four million pounds they adopted restrictive measures, raising the discount rate and refusing commercial paper from the houses financing Anglo-American trade.[1] Lange describes England's response as an attempt to recover its gold by accepting only precious metals for its exports to America — a trade war over real money and control that culminated in the [[panic-of-1837|Panic of 1837]].[1]

## Contemporary argument

Lange draws a direct parallel to the present, noting that for decades foreign investors such as China have purchased American land and corporations using fiat currency.[1] His proposed remedy is the Jacksonian one: demand gold and silver for such purchases.[1] He speculates that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] may issue a comparable executive order as a safeguard, arguing that gold and silver are not only honest money but a safeguard for national sovereignty.[1]
