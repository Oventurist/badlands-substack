# Keystone Pipeline

The Keystone Pipeline is a proposed cross-border pipeline project intended to carry Canadian crude oil to the United States. It became a long-running environmental flashpoint and was halted after President Joe Biden canceled a key permit on his first day in office.[1]

## Place in the 2024 Trump energy plan

In November 2024, reporting indicated that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s transition team was assembling a wide-ranging energy package for rollout within days of his taking office. The package would approve export permits for new liquefied natural gas (LNG) projects, increase offshore and federal-lands oil drilling, and repeal Biden-era climate measures such as electric vehicle tax credits and new clean power plant standards.[1]

Approving the Keystone Pipeline was described as a symbolic gesture within that plan. Any company seeking to build the multibillion-dollar project would have to start from scratch, because elements such as easements had been returned to landowners.[1]

Other early priorities included lifting Biden's election-year pause on new LNG export permits, expediting drilling permits on federal lands, and reopening five-year offshore drilling plans to add lease sales.[1] Transition spokesperson [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said "the American people can bank on President Trump using his executive power on day one to deliver on the promises he made to them on the campaign trail."[1] Many elements would require passage through Congress or the regulatory system; Trump promised to declare an energy emergency on his first day to test whether he could bypass those barriers.[1]

## In the case against Biden energy policy

The cancellation of the Keystone XL pipeline project is routinely cited as the opening item in a sequence of [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration decisions that critics say weakened American energy security — followed by draining the [[strategic-petroleum-reserve|Strategic Petroleum Reserve]] to low levels, pledges of no further oil drilling on U.S. soil, and the September 2023 cancellation of seven oil and gas leases in the [[arctic-national-wildlife-refuge|Arctic National Wildlife Refuge]].[2] The contrast drawn was with adversaries building their own energy independence, prompting the question circulated widely on X and used as a [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] headline: "Who is Biden working for?"[2]
