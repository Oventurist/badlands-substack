# Railway Safety Act of 2023

The **Railway Safety Act of 2023** was a bipartisan bill introduced in [[us-congress|Congress]] in March 2023 in response to the [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine train derailment]], with the aim of making American freight railroads safer.[1]

## Provisions and stall

Among its key provisions was a requirement that railroads install more detectors capable of identifying overheated wheel bearings — the mechanical failure that regulators concluded caused the derailment of the 1.75-mile-long train in East Palestine, Ohio.[1] Crucial parts of the legislation encountered resistance from rail lobbyists, who argued that such mandates would inhibit the industry's ability to introduce new practices and technologies for reducing accidents.[1] As of early 2024 the bill had not been brought up for a full vote in the [[us-senate|Senate]], and no rail-safety legislation had passed despite pledges from members of Congress in the wake of the disaster.[1]

## Accident trends

In the absence of legislation, accidents increased rather than declined. Derailments rose at the top five freight railroads in 2023 according to regulatory reports covering the first ten months of the year, and there was a steep increase in overheated wheel bearing failures specifically.[1] [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern]], the operator of the East Palestine train and owner of the track running through the town, was the only one of the five major railroads to report a decline in accidents over the period.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary argued that the Republican-backed train safety push was likely to miss root causes and create new problems, predicting that railroads would be fined and would shuffle paperwork to appear compliant while the people of East Palestine remained on their own.[1] The same commentary noted that those who had questioned the frequency of train derailments at the time of the accident were dismissed as conspiracy theorists, only for The New York Times to report a year later that derailments were in fact rising.[1]
