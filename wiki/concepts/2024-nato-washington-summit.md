# 2024 NATO Washington Summit

The 2024 summit of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization was held in Washington from July 9 to 11, 2024.[1] Its most contentious agenda item was the status of [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s bid for alliance membership, which was again deferred.[1]

## Ukraine's membership bid

At the previous year's summit, Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had been offered only "an invitation to Ukraine to join the Alliance when Allies agree and conditions are met."[1] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said at the time that "Ukraine isn't ready for NATO membership," citing "qualifications that need to be met, including democratization."[1] Zelensky called the outcome "unprecedented and absurd when time frame is not set neither for the invitation nor for Ukraine's membership," objecting to the "vague wording about 'conditions'" attached even to an invitation.[1]

At the 2024 Washington summit, corruption reform was added to democratic reform as a precondition.[1] Ukraine was to be offered neither membership nor a timeline, and instead told that it remained too corrupt to join, receiving "a list of reforms it will be expected to carry out before its membership ambitions can be realized."[1] A senior [[state-department|State Department]] official said the United States would "applaud everything that Ukraine has done in the name of reforms over the last two-plus years" while wanting "to talk about additional steps that need to be taken, particularly in the area of anti-corruption."[1] In an interview with TIME, Biden cited "significant corruption" as a reason he was not "prepared to support the NATOization of Ukraine."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media treated the corruption rationale as an unintentional admission, mocking the idea that Zelensky — accused in the commentary of embezzling American aid, conscripting civilians and prisoners, and recruiting mercenaries implicated in war crimes — could be indignant at the charge.[1] The commentary also stressed the stakes of the membership question under [[article-5|Article 5]] of the NATO charter: admitting Ukraine before the war with Russia was resolved would place the United States and the rest of the alliance automatically at war with Russia.[1]

The summit's timing coincided with the collapse of Biden's political standing at home, with critics noting that he had also boasted during his interview with [[george-stephanopoulos|George Stephanopoulos]] of having "expanded NATO" — an expansion Badlands identifies as the root cause of the [[russia-ukraine-war|Russia-Ukraine war]].[1]

## Escalation announcements

The summit produced a cluster of announcements that critics characterized as a collective escalation with [[russia|Russia]].[2] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] unveiled on Tuesday, July 9, 2024 that the United States was sending dozens of anti-air defense systems to Ukrainian forces amid stepped-up Russian aerial assaults.[2] A joint statement from Washington, The Hague and Copenhagen then confirmed that an initial batch of US-made F-16 fighter jets was en route to Ukraine, aircraft Moscow had already vowed to target.[2] On Wednesday, Polish army chief of staff General [[wieslaw-kukula|Wiesław Kukuła]] called for [[poland|Poland]] to prepare its soldiers for full-scale rather than asymmetric conflict.[2] Russian Security Council Deputy Chairman [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]], responding to the Washington Summit Declaration's language on Ukraine's "irreversible path" to membership, said Moscow must ensure that path "ends in the demise of either Ukraine or NATO. Or — even better — both."[2]

## Allied unease over Biden

Coverage of the summit dwelt on the contrast between an alliance described as never stronger and a president described as physically and politically frail, presiding over what could be his final NATO summit.[2] Officials from multiple European nations said they were alarmed by Biden's apparent decline and increasingly concerned at the prospect of an ardent champion of the alliance being replaced in November by a hostile [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a defeat to which they were said to be increasingly resigned.[2] One senior European diplomat said: "It's a very weird feeling to be in Europe listening to the president of the United States, and you're more stressed about whether he will go off script than being excited to listen to the leader of the free world ... You're worried if he knows which direction he's going or whether he's going to fall or what he's going to forget."[2] Latvian foreign minister [[baiba-braze|Baiba Braže]] countered that "the problem is not Ukraine. The problem is Russia."[2]

Wrapping up the 32-member summit, Biden said, "I've not had any of my European allies come up and say, 'Joe, don't run'. What I've heard them say is, 'You've got to win'."[2] Hungarian prime minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] attended the summit but did not request a bilateral meeting with Biden, flying afterward to Mar-a-Lago to meet Trump.[2]

## Biden's dual mission

Reporting ahead of the summit framed it as Biden's attempt to reassert leadership before two anxious audiences at once: his own Democratic Party and Western allies unsure what to make of the unfolding political crisis in Washington.[3] The gathering, which opened on Tuesday and commemorated 75 years since the alliance's founding, was Biden's first major international event since the June debate raised doubts about his capacity to lead NATO's most important member state.[3] More than 30 leaders came to Washington to discuss Ukraine's roadmap to membership, air defences against Russian missile and drone attacks, and the joint challenges posed by China.[3] Many NATO leaders and diplomats were privately eager to see Biden up close after tracking the debate fallout from afar; one European diplomat said, "People are coming to witness whether Biden is or is no longer [in charge]."[3]

Badlands commentary described the alliance as "limping into their annual meeting, licking their wounds," noting that China had just released footage of Chinese troops drilling in Belarus in apparent preparation to fight NATO in defence of Russia, weeks after [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] pledged personnel to the Ukrainian theatre.[3] The writer judged that the diplomat's remark made the event sound "more like a wake than a security summit."[3] Orbán's visit to Moscow became a central point of discussion at the gathering, where allies pledged additional air defences for Ukraine against the full-scale Russian invasion begun in 2022.[3]
