# Russian Invasion of Ukraine (Special Military Operation)

The war in Ukraine began on 24 February 2022, when [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] initiated what Russia terms the special military operation — an action "A Bright Red Line" attributes to Washington steadily applying pressure until a breaking point was reached, with full knowledge of the likely outcome.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Funding and American interest

From the outset the United States made it immediately clear it would take primary responsibility for funding and arming the Ukrainians.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article's framing question is why the American taxpayer should care who owns what land in Eastern Europe: U.S. funding of the war unquestionably harms Americans economically, yet it has never been made clear how the war serves the people's best interest.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Causes

Badlands rejects the account that Putin acted arbitrarily, arguing instead that Russia had watched U.S.-sponsored regime change in neighboring states despite promises to the contrary, arms flowing to those neighbors, and insistence that [[nato|NATO]] would expand to include Ukraine — a country on Russia's most sensitive border, the same approach through which Russia was attacked in both world wars.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The February 2008 cable of [[william-burns|William Burns]] had already identified Ukrainian NATO entry as the brightest of all redlines and forecast Russian meddling in Crimea and eastern Ukraine following a premature [[membership-action-plan|MAP]] offer.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Course of the war

The article maintains it was well known to anyone outside major-network audiences that this was a war Ukraine could not hope to win, coming down to manpower and to the abyssal gap in artillery production capabilities between Russia and the West.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Ukraine appealed on 27 February 2022 for foreign volunteers, a call remarkably few Americans heeded, and [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] later signed laws lowering the draft age to 25.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Badlands concludes that Ukraine essentially must join NATO to sustain the war of attrition, and that if it does, the [[us-military|United States military]] could be sent to the front lines.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Interpretive frames

The author offers two lenses. The first holds that all current and brewing conflicts are fragments of one great war — readable superficially as BRICS and allied nations against Western hegemony, or more deeply as "country-first" representatives worldwide fighting what Winston Churchill called the "High Cabal."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The second holds that everything being witnessed is theatre, a staged war designed for mass population awakening.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Whether one is in the black-pilled doomer camp or the "patriots are in control" camp, the article argues the conflict is worth observing and dissecting, since each earth-shaking event in the war of stories jars more people awake — much as COVID-19 did.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Compared to the 1979 Afghan intervention

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" argues that what happened in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] in 1979 was not much different from the later Russian invasion of Ukraine.[2] In both cases, the article contends, Russia was being threatened by a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-controlled [[shadow-government|shadow government]] that was not seeking peace but was instead trying to destabilize a neighboring country and threaten Russia with missiles.[2]

On this reading both invasions were defensive rather than wars of conquest, and the media have continued to lie about both.[2] The parallel is drawn from declassified Soviet material in which KGB chief [[yuri-andropov|Yuri Andropov]] and defense minister Dmitri Ustinov cited the danger of American missiles being deployed in Afghanistan on the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]]'s southern border.[2]
