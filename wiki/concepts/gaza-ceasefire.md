# Gaza Ceasefire

The Gaza ceasefire is the October 2025 U.S.-brokered agreement halting hostilities between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]], structured in phases.[1]

## Phase 2 impasse

Phase 2 requires Hamas disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawal.[1] Ahead of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s February 2026 Washington visit, an Israeli source indicated the prime minister would tell President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that Phase 2 "is not moving" due to Hamas's refusal to relinquish weapons.[1] Israel argued that a renewed military operation is needed to enforce demilitarization in line with Trump's Gaza vision, amid ongoing incidents and IDF strikes on suspected violators.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Netanyahu intended to tell Trump the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]]'s Phase Two plan to take control of Gaza will not happen, and that the United States needed to immediately start a war with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Hostage release and the October 2025 Knesset address

Under the U.S.-brokered deal, the final twenty living hostages held in [[gaza|Gaza]] were released on Monday, October 13, 2025, the same day President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] addressed the [[knesset|Knesset]].[2] Trump declared "the historic dawn of the new Middle East," describing the moment as "not only the end of war, but the end of an age of terror and death and the beginning of the age of faith and hope and of God."[2] He told Israeli lawmakers that Israel had achieved all it could "by force of arms" and should now translate battlefield victories "into the ultimate prize of peace and prosperity for the entire Middle East," and said he would carry a similar message to regional leaders in Egypt later the same day.[2]

Authorship of the plan became a point of contention. Former Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] wrote in a twelve-post thread that Trump had "adopted and built on the plan the Biden Administration developed after months of discussion with Arab partners, Israel and the Palestinian Authority," a credit-claim Trump dismissed as "a joke" and evidence of "weak leadership."[2] Badlands commentary used the exchange to argue that whoever had actually been negotiating with Arab partners during the [[joe-biden|Biden]] years was not the sitting administration.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the Knesset speech as an ultimatum rather than a celebration, arguing that Israel had not meaningfully "won" — its economy strained by IDF reservist mobilization and its international standing damaged — and that Trump was rewriting the story to give the Israeli political class a sense of accomplishment while telling it to stop warmongering.[2]

## January 2025 "finish line" push

On January 6, 2025, US Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] called for a final push to secure a Gaza ceasefire before President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] left office, after a [[hamas|Hamas]] official told Reuters the group had cleared a list of 34 hostages to be freed first under a truce.[3] "We very much want to bring this over the finish line in the next two weeks, the time we have remaining," Blinken said at a news conference in South Korea when asked whether a deal was close.[3]

Israel sent a team of mid-ranking officials to Qatar for talks brokered by Qatari and Egyptian mediators, and some Arabic media reported that [[david-barnea|David Barnea]], the head of [[mossad|Mossad]] and the lead negotiator, was expected to join them; the Israeli prime minister's office did not comment.[3] It remained unclear how close the two sides were, with some signs of movement but little indication of a shift in the key demands that had blocked any truce for more than a year.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] predicted flatly that there would be no truce deal before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office, on the grounds that there was zero upside for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and his administration, whose cabinet he described as motivated by the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] project and by Netanyahu's corruption trial to continue and expand the war.[3] He argued that the apparent dissent between Netanyahu and establishment Democrats in the Biden, Pelosi, and Schumer camp was political theater, and that with less than two weeks until an incoming Trump administration ostensibly loaded with proud Israel sycophants there was no incentive for the Netanyahu regime to pause.[3] In his reading, Israeli oligarchs feared Trump would stop their imperialism — which is why most did not back his candidacy — and hoped to use disloyal figures inside the administration to subvert peace efforts, while both pro- and anti-Trump analysts who assume Trump is beholden to that oligarchy are "standing on a rug" about to be yanked out.[3]

## January 2025 Framework

On January 15, 2025 Qatar's prime minister announced in Doha that a ceasefire-for-hostage deal had been struck between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]], with representatives of both the outgoing Biden administration and the incoming Trump administration present.[4] Multiple outlets reported a 42-day ceasefire paired with a prisoner exchange, including the release of all Israelis taken captive in the October 7, 2023 incursion from Gaza.[4] The final push was handled jointly by Brett McGurk, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s longtime Middle East negotiator, and [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Middle East envoy; a senior Biden official called the cooperation between the two administrations "almost unprecedented," enabled by a rare intersection of interests between bitter rivals after Trump's November victory.[4] Both presidents claimed full credit once the agreement was announced.[4] In Israel, President [[isaac-herzog|Isaac Herzog]] urged the security cabinet and full cabinet to approve the framework at votes expected the following day, while Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] threatened to leave [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s coalition over it.[4]

## February 2024 truce negotiations

In late February 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said he hoped a ceasefire in Israel's war on Gaza could be in place by the following Monday, as negotiations to halt hostilities and secure the release of captives appeared to gather pace.[5] His comments in New York came as Israeli media reported that an Israeli military delegation had flown to [[qatar|Qatar]] for intensive talks.[5]

The negotiations, mediated by Egypt, Qatar and the United States, sought a six-week pause in fighting between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] to allow aid into Gaza, where the United Nations said some 2.3 million people were on the brink of famine.[5] The proposed pause would also allow the release of dozens of captives held by Hamas in return for hundreds of Palestinian prisoners held by Israel.[5] Asked when a ceasefire might begin, Biden — speaking to reporters at an ice cream shop in New York — replied, "Well, I hope by the beginning of the weekend, by the end of the weekend," adding that his national security adviser had told him "we're close. We're close. We're not done yet. My hope is, by next Monday, we'll have a ceasefire."[5]

## October 8, 2025 first-phase breakthrough

[[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] announced on Wednesday, October 8, 2025 that [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] had reached a breakthrough on the first phase of his Gaza peace plan, which called for an immediate ceasefire, a hostage-for-prisoner release, Hamas disarmament and an Israeli troop withdrawal.[6] Writing on Truth Social, Trump said Hamas had agreed to release all Israeli hostages and Israel had agreed to withdraw troops "to an agreed upon line," calling the moves the "first steps towards a strong, durable and everlasting peace."[6]

He thanked mediators from [[qatar|Qatar]], [[egypt|Egypt]] and Türkiye, with indirect talks having run in Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt since Monday, October 6.[6] Trump subsequently told Axios that he was "likely going to Israel in the coming days" to address the [[knesset|Knesset]] at Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s invitation, saying "It's a great day for Israel and for the world."[6]

## Political strain on Netanyahu (January 2025)

Reuters reporting on January 23, 2025, described the ceasefire as trapping Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] between a new U.S. president promising peace and far-right coalition allies who wanted the war resumed.[7] One nationalist ally had already quit over the deal and another threatened to follow unless the war on [[hamas|Hamas]] resumed with even greater force than the 15-month campaign that devastated much of Gaza.[7] The first stage was intended to last six weeks, with negotiations on the second phase — whose stated aim was ending the war — due to begin by day 16, February 4, and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] sought to use the ceasefire's momentum to expand Israel's diplomatic ties across the Middle East.[7]

## October 2025 clashes and the ceasefire's survival

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Israeli officials confirmed in October 2025 that the Gaza ceasefire remained intact despite an exchange of fire between [[hamas|Hamas]] and Israeli forces over the weekend of October 18–19.[8] Speaking to reporters aboard [[air-force-one|Air Force One]], Trump said the ceasefire was "still in place" and that while Hamas had been "quite rambunctious," its leadership did not appear directly involved in the violations.[8]

Israeli airstrikes on Hamas positions in Gaza that Sunday reportedly killed 29 Palestinians, while Hamas militants killed two Israeli soldiers.[8] Israel responded by temporarily halting humanitarian aid deliveries and closing border crossings, but pressure from the Trump administration reportedly led Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to reverse the decision, with aid resuming Monday morning.[8]

The clashes followed Hamas's exchange of the remaining living hostages in Gaza for Palestinian prisoners held in Israel, after which Israeli soldiers withdrew from much of the Gaza Strip and aid began flowing in.[8] Tensions escalated as Israel demanded the return of deceased hostages' bodies and Hamas said it would need time to locate and return the remaining remains.[8] The United States warned Hamas against further violations as the group sought to consolidate power in Gaza.[8] Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] summarized the episode as "Neocons tried again and failed again."[8]

## 60-day ceasefire proposal (July 2025)

On Tuesday, July 1, 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] announced that [[israel|Israel]] had "agreed to the necessary conditions" to finalize a ceasefire deal in the Israel–Hamas war.[9] Writing on Truth Social, he said his representatives had held "a long and productive meeting with the Israelis today on Gaza" and that Israel had agreed to the conditions "to finalize the 60 Day CEASEFIRE, during which time we will work with all parties to end the War."[9]

Trump said the Qataris and Egyptians, "who have worked very hard to help bring Peace," would deliver the final proposal, and pressed [[hamas|Hamas]] to accept: "I hope, for the good of the Middle East, that Hamas takes this Deal, because it will not get better — IT WILL ONLY GET WORSE."[9] As of the announcement, Hamas had not responded.[9]

The proposal collided immediately with opposition inside the Israeli cabinet, where Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] declared the same week that the war must continue until "a decisive victory" without agreements or mediators.[9] Trump separately said he would be "very firm" with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] on ending the war when the premier visited Washington the following week, adding that Netanyahu "wants it too" and predicting "I think we'll have a deal next week."[9]

## January 2025 finalization dispute

On January 15, 2025, the United States and Qatar — the brokers of the agreement — proclaimed that a deal had been reached with Hamas to end the 15-month war in Gaza triggered by the October 7, 2023 onslaught.[10] Israel nonetheless held off through Thursday, January 16 from officially declaring the ceasefire-hostage release deal concluded, insisting details remained to be finalized and that Hamas was throwing last-minute wrenches into the negotiations.[10] Mossad chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]], head of Israel's negotiating team, remained in Doha as of that afternoon, having been dispatched to the Qatari capital the previous Saturday night.[10] The Prime Minister's Office issued a statement that morning accusing Hamas of backing out of some agreements and creating a "crisis" in finalizing the deal; no evidence was provided and Hamas denied the allegation.[10]

In the early hours of Friday, January 17, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s office said on X that the negotiating team had reported "agreements have been reached on a deal for the release of the hostages" in Doha, and that Netanyahu had ordered the security cabinet to convene later that day — a day later than originally intended.[10] It was not immediately clear whether the full cabinet would meet Friday or Saturday, with The Times of Israel reporting, citing a Netanyahu spokesperson, that the full cabinet meeting would not take place until Saturday night, potentially delaying the ceasefire's entry into force until Monday.[10] Netanyahu has been accused of deliberately sabotaging previous deals for his own political benefit.[10]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the sequence as a trap closing on Netanyahu: because Donald Trump had already spiked the football with repeated Truth Social celebrations, the delay registered publicly as Netanyahu pulling defeat from the jaws of victory, with the first hostage exchange falling on the day of Trump's inauguration.[10] He noted Netanyahu's coalition would retain a Knesset majority even without [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]]'s party, but would fall to a minority if [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s [[religious-zionism-party|Religious Zionism]] also followed through on threats to depart, while opposition parties promised to support the coalition so long as it advanced the deal.[10]
