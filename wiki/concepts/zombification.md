# Zombification

**Zombification** is the term used by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] in Part 8 of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] for what he presents as a real condition in which human beings lose the capacity for independent thought and become, in effect, running scripts.[1] The article's subtitle asserts that "zombification is a REAL phenomenon."[1]

## The zombie as symbol

A zombie is defined as a creature with no will of its own: it does not think, it merely consumes.[1] Zombie symbolism is said to refer to the state between life and death in which the natural processes of the body still run in some sense while the spirit is nowhere to be found, the defining characteristic being the absence of independent will.[1] On this reading the elite epithet "useless eaters" is a veiled reference to zombies, and the trope of zombies eating brains is a metaphor for the ideological indoctrination that [[npc-non-player-character|NPCs]] inflict on free thinkers.[1]

## Mass zombification

The author reports observing a major uptick in the mass zombification efforts of the cabal following the assassination attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], describing a distinct atmosphere on social media that felt like a last-ditch effort to maintain control over the minds of the masses.[1] The mechanism proposed is [[cyber-necromancy|cyber-necromancy]]: the combined use of ritual, malicious bots and what the essay calls disgruntled ghosts to reduce unique souls to replaceable automatons.[1]

## Resistance

Zombification is described as avoidable.[1] Thwarting it is said to require that people stop agreeing, whether in word or in deed, that they are replaceable cogs in another's machine, and that they look within to discover and then develop what makes them unique — a process that may take time, but in which even small effort is held to go a long way.[1] The essay adds that technological dependence accelerates the process, since faculties that go unused atrophy, and that [[qanon|Q]]'s insistence on independent research is a direct impediment to it.[1] Its closing formulation is that "EVIL is LIVE spelled in reverse," and that the mechanization of the living generates negative energy on which ghosts feed and which attack bots amplify.[1]

## Zombification as propagated blindness

Part 4 of [[the-white-pill-emporium|The White Pill Emporium]] (15 February 2025) gives the term a second and more explicitly propagandistic definition.[2] There the author argues that filling a mind with false beliefs, as he says the cabal has done to mankind, effectively switches off a person's access to their own [[intuition|intuition]] — carefully distinguishing thwarted access from any shutdown of intuition itself, since "the subconscious mind doesn't come with an 'off' button."[2] Cabal propaganda is described as installing a shield in the human mind that estranges it from its intuitive capacities, and as being additionally designed to spur the propagandized person to install that same shield in others.[2]

It is this transmission that the author names zombification, "because when one propagandized person propagandizes another, the metaphor of 'eating brains' applies," adding parenthetically that another word for zombie is [[npc-non-player-character|NPC]].[2] The corollary drawn is that the freer from self-deception a person becomes, the more able they are to access intuition and to receive specific, timely and actionable information from it.[2]

## Zombification as destruction of the will

An earlier installment of the [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series, "Hell is Organized" (August 8, 2024), gives the term its ritual sense: the process of destroying the will of a person and replacing it with the will of the cult.[3] The author writes that the [[koryos|Koryos]] sent bands of groomed young boys, "like mindless zombie hordes," to massacre nearby villages, and connects the imagery to the cult's practice of allowing possession by [[earthbound-spirits|earthbound spirits]] in order to acquire superhuman ferocity.[3] The same essay lists zombies alongside werewolves, vampires and demons as figures the author regards as real in some sense rather than purely fictional.[3]
