# Boxer Rebellion

The Boxer Rebellion of 1899–1901 was an anti-foreigner uprising that spread rapidly across China and, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, triggered a heated battle between imperial and anti-imperial forces in both Russia and the United States.[1]

Russia's Transport Minister [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]], who had spearheaded the [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] (1890–1905), attempted to avoid military entanglement, as did President [[william-mckinley|William McKinley]] in Washington.[1] When the Boxers attacked the Manchurian rail line connecting Russia to China by land, Witte succumbed to pressure and finally sent in troops.[1] The Chinese reformers who had attempted to modernize with American and Russian assistance under Emperor Kuang Hsu and Li Hung Chang fell from power as anarchy spread.[1]

The outcome of the Boxer chaos was that the imperial powers of France, Germany and England demanded immense financial reparations, ownership of Chinese territory and mass executions of the Boxers.[1] In China, McKinley — like Witte — worked to reject taking territory in the imperial free-for-all, producing fears within the British oligarchy that a US–Russia alliance led by McKinley and Witte was imminent.[1] [[alfred-thayer-mahan|Alfred Thayer Mahan]], by contrast, used the post-Boxer moment to champion an Anglo-American alliance to subdue and "civilize" China.[1] The subsequent "open door" carve-up of China was partially checked when [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]] overthrew the Manchu dynasty in 1911.[1]
