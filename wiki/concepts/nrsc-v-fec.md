# NRSC v. FEC

NRSC v. FEC is a case argued before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] in December 2025 challenging federal caps on coordinated political spending by parties under the [[federal-election-campaign-act|Federal Election Campaign Act]] of 1971.[1]

For the then-current election cycle, coordinated spending limits ranged from $61,800 to $123,000 for House races and up to $3.7 million for Senate races.[1] The plaintiffs — including Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]], former Congressman Steve Chabot (R-OH), the [[national-republican-senatorial-committee|National Republican Senatorial Committee]] (NRSC) and the National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC) — argued that the limits violate the First Amendment.[1] A federal appeals court had upheld the caps, relying on a 2001 Supreme Court ruling.[1]

## Oral argument

Justices Clarence Thomas, Samuel Alito and [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]] appeared inclined to strike the limits down.[1] Kavanaugh voiced concern about the weakening of political parties relative to outside groups such as super PACs, saying, "The parties have been weakened overall, and this case… starts to restore the strength of parties."[1] Chief Justice John Roberts and Justice Amy Coney Barrett questioned the lawyers sparingly, and Justice Neil Gorsuch remained silent.[1] Liberal justices, including [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]], warned that removing the caps could produce corruption; Sotomayor criticized the Court's campaign-finance jurisprudence, saying, "Every time we interfere with the congressional design, we make matters worse."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the case could "blow a hole in longstanding limits" on party coordination, and argued that although the ruling would nominally rebalance party power against PACs, it in fact "doubles down on the big money in politics — to the benefit of parties and PACs," concluding, "Likely by design. It's always by design."[1]
