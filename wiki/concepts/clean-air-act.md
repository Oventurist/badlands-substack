# Clean Air Act

The Clean Air Act is the principal United States federal statute governing air pollution, administered by the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]].

The February 11, 2026 [[badlands-brief-05f|Badlands Brief]] describes the Act as the vehicle through which the EPA's 2009 [[endangerment-finding|endangerment finding]] became operative: the finding that greenhouse gases endanger public health and welfare provided the foundational basis under the Clean Air Act for regulating emissions from vehicles, power plants, oil-and-gas facilities and other sources. The Trump administration's rescission of the finding is therefore presented as removing the statutory predicate for that body of regulation, beginning with motor vehicle emissions standards and potentially extending to power plant and stationary-source rules. The Supreme Court decision [[massachusetts-v-epa|Massachusetts v. EPA]] is cited as the ruling that brought greenhouse gases within the Act's definition of air pollutants.
