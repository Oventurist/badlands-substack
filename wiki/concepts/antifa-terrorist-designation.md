# Antifa Terrorist Designation

The Antifa terrorist designation refers to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement on Wednesday, September 17, 2025 that he would designate [[antifa|antifa]], a left-wing activist movement, a "major terrorist organization."[1] Trump described the movement as "A SICK, DANGEROUS, RADICAL LEFT DISASTER" in a Truth Social post announcing the move.[1]

## Funding investigations

Beyond the designation itself, Trump said he would be "strongly recommending" that those funding antifa be thoroughly investigated "in accordance with the highest legal standards and practices."[1] The announcement came just days after Trump said he would "100%" consider such a designation, and followed a period of heightened political tension in the wake of the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America framed the designation against her own reporting from 2020, recounting conversations with local Black Lives Matter activists who said the movement being funded nationally was not delivering money to their communities and that they felt used.[1] Those activists, she wrote, denied being antifa, denied being Marxists, said they would not give up their guns, and — like election-integrity marchers — believed the election was rigged.[1]

She characterized antifa's 2020 activity as selective rather than universal: the group did not attend every event, but nearly every event it did attend turned violent.[1] She pointed to Craigslist advertisements and recorded Zoom meetings surfacing over the years as evidence of a sophisticated, coordinated and well-funded effort behind street violence, which some — herself included — characterized as election interference.[1] Her open question about the funding probe was how far back the investigation would reach.[1]

## The September 2025 Executive Order

On the evening of Monday, September 22, 2025, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued a broadly worded executive order declaring [[antifa]] — described in press coverage as a decentralized antifascist movement — a "domestic terrorist organization."[2] The order characterized antifa as a "militarist, anarchist enterprise that explicitly calls for the overthrow of the United States Government, law enforcement authorities, and our system of law," and in an accompanying statement Trump called it a "SICK, DANGEROUS RADICAL LEFT DISASTER."[2] The order and an accompanying fact sheet asserted that antifa's "campaign of violence and terrorism nationwide" included the doxing of political figures and activists, assaults on Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers, and organized riots.[2]

The designation followed the murder of right-wing commentator [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] at a speaking event in Utah earlier that month, after which Trump and his allies vowed an aggressive crackdown on leftists — although prosecutors had not surfaced evidence that the shooter was aligned with far-left groups.[2] Critics, including activists and civil liberties experts, warned that because there is no formal entity called antifa — only a loose nationwide network of local far-left chapters — the label could be applied broadly to suppress First Amendment activity such as protests against ICE.[2]

## Badlands commentary on the designation

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] noted that there is no standalone federal criminal charge of "domestic terrorism," which functions instead as a sentencing enhancement at the federal level, and judged the executive order legally thin, expressing hope that investigators would establish foreign connections sufficient to support a foreign terrorist organization (FTO) designation.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump had been signaling a campaign against what he called the Domestic Terrorist Industrial Complex — funded, coordinated and controlled by NGOs — before Kirk's assassination, and therefore was not merely reacting to it.[2] He highlighted that on the same day as the antifa announcement, the [[capital-research-center|Capital Research Center]] published a report tying [[george-soros|George Soros]] to the funding of terror organizations, noting that CRC board member [[edwin-meese-iii|Edwin Meese III]] had received the Presidential Medal of Freedom from Trump in 2019.[2] He suggested the report could serve as a predicate for the designation and as a fulcrum for RICO charges against Soros and similar figures, and cited [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]]'s recent divestment from Arabella Advisors as another converging signal.[2] In his framing, the pattern indicated that Trump was cultivating the battlespace rather than reacting to it, with sustained help from the civilian side of what he termed the Military-Civilian Alliance.[2]

## November 2025 SDGT and FTO designations

On November 13, 2025 the United States [[state-department|Department of State]] announced that four militant groups would be designated Specially Designated Global Terrorists (SDGTs) and, effective November 20, 2025, added to the list of [[foreign-terrorist-organization|Foreign Terrorist Organizations]] (FTOs).[3] The action gave concrete legal form to the earlier antifa designation push by targeting named European anarchist and antifascist organizations rather than the amorphous domestic movement.[3]

The designated groups were [[antifa-ost|Antifa Ost]] (also known as Antifa East or Hammerbande), a German-based militant outfit that carried out numerous attacks in Germany between 2018 and 2023 and an attack in Budapest in February 2023, and which Hungary declared a terrorist organization on September 26, 2025; the Informal Anarchist Federation/International Revolutionary Front (FAI/FRI), an Italy-based anarchist militant group with self-proclaimed affiliates across Europe, South America and Asia that since 2003 has claimed responsibility for threats of violence, bombs and letter bombs against political and economic institutions; Armed Proletarian Justice, a Greek anarchist and anti-capitalist group that has attempted and conducted improvised explosive device attacks, including one near a Greek riot police headquarters in Goudi on December 18, 2023; and Revolutionary Class Self-Defense, a Greek anarchist anti-capitalist group citing opposition to "capitalist structures," state repression and solidarity with Palestine, which claimed two IED attacks — at the Greek Ministry of Labor on February 3, 2024 and the Hellenic Train offices on April 11, 2025.[3]

Under the designations, all property and property interests in the United States, or under the control of U.S. persons, belonging to the groups are blocked, and U.S. persons are generally prohibited from engaging in business with them.[3] Knowingly providing material support to the designated groups, or attempting or conspiring to do so, is a crime, and U.S. counterterrorism authorities warned that persons engaging in certain transactions or activities with the designated entities risk exposure to secondary sanctions.[3]
