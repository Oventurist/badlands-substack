# AurumNet

AurumNet is the name given to a joint gold-backed trade settlement ledger announced in March 2025 by Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, the five founding members of [[brics|BRICS]].[1]

## Context

The announcement is described as part of an acceleration of external monetary realignment running parallel to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s domestic economic reset.[1] Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and Iran joined BRICS+ in the same period, a development the article reads as signalling the end of the petrodollar, with oil increasingly traded in gold-backed yuan and rubles.[1] The African Union was reported to be discussing its own continental clearing system linked to BRICS commodity baskets.[1]

## Relationship to U.S. policy

Rather than confronting this bloc, the article argues, Trump's foreign policy pivoted toward engagement with it, quietly aligning trade corridors with BRICS-friendly nations such as Brazil, Hungary, and the United Arab Emirates.[1] The intended result is multipolar finance without a dollar collapse — a managed decoupling rather than a rupture.[1] Domestically, leaked Treasury Department memos were said to reveal working groups discussing a blockchain-based sovereign coin pegged to a hard asset basket of gold, silver, and rare earths, alongside quietly greenlit private gold audits of central bank vaults and executive discussions with BRICS central banks about interoperability of sovereign currencies.[1]

## Criticism

Economist Nouriel Roubini cautioned in a 2023 blog post that "commodity-based currencies or cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin could destabilize markets, amplifying volatility and constraining policy responses."[1] Critics further argue that gold and Bitcoin systems are prone to manipulation by powerful investors.[1] Supporters, including economist [[judy-shelton|Judy Shelton]], counter that "sound money anchored to real value restores confidence; fiat systems breed dependency on unelected officials."[1]
