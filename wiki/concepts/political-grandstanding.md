# Political Grandstanding

Political grandstanding is a pejorative term for public position-taking undertaken for visibility rather than legislative effect. It became a recurring frame in Badlands Media commentary after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] repeatedly applied the label to Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]].[1]

## Definition offered in Badlands commentary

In the March 2025 essay "Massie Maximalists," contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] defined a political grandstander as "someone who tries to take credit for something they didn't contribute to in any way, someone who leaches off of a successful movement after the momentum has already swung in that direction" — a coattail rider who climbs aboard only once it is safe and no risk remains.[1] He gave as examples people who began speaking against the 2020 election results or the [[covid-19-vaccines|Covid vaccines]] four years after the fact.[1] Al Sharpton was presented as the archetype of the behavior.[1]

## Contrast with deal-making

Carlson set grandstanding against deal-making, arguing that deal-makers put "skin in the game" and that by definition a deal requires both sides to compromise.[1] "It's easy to be on offense when you have nothing to defend," he wrote; "it's easy to be a critic and hold to your convictions when you have no track record to be critiqued."[1] He posed the essay's central question — whether "unwavering conviction" and "unwillingness to compromise" are actually distinguishable — and answered that if conviction prevents anything from getting done, the two are the same thing wearing different labels.[1]

The argument was illustrated with several analogies: the autistic character Raymond in *Rain Man* insisting on K-mart underwear as unreasonableness rather than principle; a wide receiver refusing to block because he disagreed with the play call; and a driver taking a family member to the hospital who does not stop for gas, an oil change, or water because emergencies justify temporarily relaxing ordinary rules.[1] Carlson concluded that politics as currently constituted is a team sport in which members may object in the huddle but must execute the play as called, and that the only conviction properly held without exception is religious faith.[1]

## Application to the March 2025 shutdown fight

The framing was applied directly to Massie's lone Republican vote against the March 2025 [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]], which Carlson said Massie cast knowing it would not change the outcome — grandstanding at zero risk while appearing "holier than thou."[1] He noted the paradoxical rewards: after Trump called for Massie to be primaried, liberal outlets praised the congressman's courage, influencers such as [[ian-carroll|Ian Carroll]] amplified him, and Massie reported raising $261,000 in under three days.[1] Carlson also observed that "sanctimonious," a synonym he applied to Massie, was the nickname Trump had given [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], the candidate Massie endorsed in 2024.[1]
