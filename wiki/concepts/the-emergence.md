# The Emergence

**"The Emergence: Living as the Coherence"** is an April 26, 2025 Badlands Media feature by the contributor [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]], published as a follow-up to the earlier essay "The Remembering" and presented by the author as the final movement of a seven-part arc on awakening, healing and integration.[1]

## Argument

The essay opens with the assertion that the reader "was never meant to stay in pieces," quoting *The Remembering*'s claim that the fragmented selves formed in childhood "are not mistakes" but "brilliant adaptations."[1] Where remembrance is described as the work of gathering fragments and sitting with their stories, emergence is defined as what happens after — the point at which the recovered pieces begin to harmonize and [[coherence|coherence]] becomes a default state rather than a peak moment.[1] The author distinguishes emergence from transcendence, calling it grounding, "where truth takes root," and insists that fractures are not erased but woven into the foundation.[1]

Emergence is figured through several images: the Phoenix that walks forward "with smoke still in your feathers," the Bodhisattva who declines transcendence in order to stay and serve, and the bridge between "what broke and what was rebuilt."[1] If the descent was the dark night, emergence is described as "the sacred morning after."[1]

## Crossing into embodied truth

The second section argues that the shift registers physiologically rather than intellectually: unshallow breath, an unclenched jaw, and the absence of bracing for betrayal at the first sign of closeness.[1] The essay cites [[andrew-newberg|Dr. Andrew Newberg]]'s neurobiological research on contemplative practice, in which brain regions governing self-identification and spatial orientation quiet down and the illusion of separation fades, as evidence that the state is "measurable" rather than metaphorical.[1] Coherence is characterised as emotional, physiological, chemical and electromagnetic at once.[1] The physicist [[carlo-rovelli|Carlo Rovelli]] is quoted — "We are not things, we are events" — to argue that identity is a dynamic unfolding rather than a static object.[1] The old selves, "the achiever, the adapter, the invisible one," are said to wave from the rearview mirror, at peace rather than erased.[1]

## The intelligence of embodiment

The third section reframes the body from battlefield to sanctuary, with the mind demoted from master to steward and the heart placed at the helm.[1] [[stephen-porges|Dr. Stephen Porges]] and [[polyvagal-theory|Polyvagal Theory]] are invoked for the claim that healing begins with safety rather than logic: until the body feels safe, transformation remains conceptual.[1] The biophysicist [[mae-wan-ho|Mae-Wan Ho]] is cited for the notion of quantum coherence in living systems, in which each part of the body communicates with the whole in real time.[1] [[shamini-jain|Dr. Shamini Jain]] is quoted that "when we live in coherence, we aren't just experiencing wellness, we are transmitting healing to our environment," and [[bruce-lipton|Dr. Bruce Lipton]] for the claim that perception rewrites the body's biology.[1] [[rachel-naomi-remen|Dr. Rachel Naomi Remen]] supplies the closing formulation that healing "may not be so much about getting better, as about letting go of everything that isn't you."[1]

## Resonance, leadership and the living grid

The fourth section holds that coherence sorts relationships without confrontation, quoting the therapist [[vienna-pharaon|Vienna Pharaon]]: "When you don't betray yourself, your relationships either rise to meet you or fall away with grace."[1] It recalls the question posed in the earlier essay "Walking the Path" — "Does this lift me or weigh me?" — and argues the answer is now felt bodily.[1] [[sue-johnson|Dr. Sue Johnson]] is cited for secure attachment as biology rather than luxury, [[joe-dispenza|Dr. Joe Dispenza]] for the "quantum signature" produced when thought, emotion and action align, [[bruce-greyson|Dr. Bruce Greyson]] for the finding that near-death experiencers return with clear purpose and reduced fear, and [[candace-pert|Dr. Candace Pert]] for the line "Your body is your subconscious mind."[1]

## The field awakens

The fifth section moves from the individual to the collective, claiming that coherent people begin to recognise one another and that the reader has become the bridge described in the earlier piece "The Shift Unfolding."[1] [[rollin-mccraty|Dr. Rollin McCraty]] of the [[heartmath-institute|HeartMath Institute]] is cited for research indicating that a regulated heart field can stabilize others within range; [[clare-graves|Dr. Clare Graves]] for a vision of humanity evolving from fear-based systems into collaborative synergy; and [[dean-radin|Dr. Dean Radin]] for experiments said to show that focused, emotionally coherent intention alters outcomes at a distance.[1] Leadership in "the age of resonance" is defined as leading "with signal" rather than strategy, and the reader's task is framed not as waking the world but as staying "awake in your own skin."[1]

## Living it forward

The final section quotes [[dan-siegel|Dr. Dan Siegel]] that "integration is the foundation of health" and [[jean-houston|Jean Houston]] that "we are the ancestors of a future age," presenting coherence as an offering transmitted into "a timeline still forming."[1] Coherence is said to mean harmony rather than sameness, and the piece closes on a collective register: "This is not the end of your becoming. This is the beginning of *ours*," describing "a frequency architecture built by those who remember" and addressing the reader as "one of the Builders."[1]

## The series

An afterword, "The Journey Continues," retraces the arc as a spiral staircase rather than a straight line, listing its stages: "The Bridge" (the rupture and first recognition), "The Shift" (orientation toward resonance and internal compass), "Walking the Path" (embodiment and practice), "The Liminal" (the sacred pause and gestation), "Transcending Ego" (integration rather than destruction of egoic identities), "The Remembering" (inner reunion with buried parts), and "The Emergence" itself, described as "the breath after the storm."[1] The author states that the series is the final piece but not a conclusion, arguing that awakening is a cycle that returns deeper each turn.[1]

## Author's note

In a closing note, Observing Consciousness writes that the series "was never meant to be a map" but "a mirror," composed while living its stages rather than after resolving them, and invites readers to pass it on "not as an answer, but as an offering."[1] The article carries the standing [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] disclaimer that features represent the opinions of contributing authors, and directs readers to the author's own Observing Consciousness Substack.[1]
