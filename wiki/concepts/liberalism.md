# Liberalism

**Liberalism** is treated in Badlands Media commentary by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] as distinct from, but continuous with, [[leftism]]. The two are not the same thing, the author insists, but liberalism "can easily give way" to leftism when it is fully divorced from certain indispensable values that he holds are found nowhere other than in right-wing [[conservatism]].[1] More pointedly, the environment created by liberalism is described as the necessary precondition for leftism to emerge, such that leftism may be characterized as "the out of control, spoiled child of liberalism."[1]

## Psychological Profile

Liberals are described as having lower in-group bias than conservatives — lacking, on average, the inherent pride in who they are that the author attributes to conservatives.[1] He argues that this deficit is why liberals and leftists are significantly easier to hypnotize into believing that the wholesale transformation of their society by mass migration is desirable.[1]

Liberalism and leftism are also associated in the article with significantly higher rates of diagnosed mental illness and consequently higher consumption of pharmaceuticals, a disparity the author attributes to the absence of attachment to one's own people and country.[1]

## Neurological Speculation

Addressing the claim that conservatives' larger amygdalas indicate bigotry, the author inverts the argument and speculates that liberals may have small or shrunken amygdalas.[1] He offers this as a possible explanation for why left-leaning people struggle to accept that admitting millions of illegal immigrants is unwise, why they appear unconcerned about cultural erasure or personal danger, and why they place a taboo on the right of self-defense — perhaps, he suggests, because a deficiency in threat perception makes it hard for them to believe others can accurately recognize and handle threats.[1]

## Evolutionary Framing

Within [[r-k-selection-theory|r/K selection theory]], liberalism belongs with leftism on the r-selected side of the ledger: a survival and replication strategy adapted to environments of material plenty, marked by lower parental investment, weaker in-group formation, and indiscriminate reproduction.[1] The article credits [[the-evolutionary-psychology-behind-politics|The Evolutionary Psychology Behind Politics]] with demonstrating that conservatism and liberalism amount to two survival and replication strategies optimized for different environments.[1]
