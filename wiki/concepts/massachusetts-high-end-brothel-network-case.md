# Massachusetts High-End Brothel Network Case

The Massachusetts high-end brothel network case is a federal prosecution announced in November 2023 concerning a multi-state prostitution ring whose alleged client list included elected officials, military officers, and executives at technology and pharmaceutical firms.[1]

## Arrests and allegations

On Wednesday, November 8, 2023, acting U.S. Attorney Joshua Levy of the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Massachusetts announced the arrests of three people: Han Lee, a 41-year-old woman from Cambridge, Massachusetts; James Lee, a 68-year-old man from Torrance, California; and Junmyung Lee, a 30-year-old man from Dedham, Massachusetts.[1] The three were suspected of operating multiple brothels in Massachusetts and Virginia.[1]

Federal investigators alleged that the defendants operated a pair of websites advertising professional photography appointments at high-end apartments they had rented.[1] Investigators believed the photography sessions functioned as a front service, with the designated properties providing a controlled location at which women hired by the defendants could transact illicit sex services.[1]

## The client list

Levy's office stated that it had identified "a wide array of buyers" of the ring's services but named no suspected clients at the Wednesday press briefing.[1] A November 6 affidavit described a client list including elected politicians, professors, lawyers, doctors, accountants, scientists, executives for technology and pharmaceutical companies, and other business executives.[1] Boston-based [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] agent Zachary A. Mitlitsky, who swore the affidavit, said the list also included military officers and government contractors holding security clearances.[1] Mitlitsky stressed that the professions identified were not an exhaustive list of the clientele, and that potentially hundreds of clients might yet be identified.[1] Levy told CNN: "Pick a profession, they're probably represented in this case."[1]

## Foreign honeytrap theory (January 2024)

By early January 2024 intelligence experts were reported to be increasingly convinced that the six high-end brothels in the suburbs of Boston and Washington, D.C. had been set up by a foreign nation as an espionage "honeytrap."[2] The network, allegedly masterminded by a 41-year-old South Korean woman, was believed to have targeted politicians, high-ranking government officials and defense contractors.[2] Which country stood behind the scheme remained the central mystery, with Russia, China, Korea itself and even Israel all seen as possibilities.[2] A one-time [[cia|CIA]] senior operations officer told DailyMail.com that "Having the Koreans out front could have been a false flag to give China or another country plausible deniability if the plot unraveled."[2] The brothels had been raided in November, and prosecutors said they were looking to charge 28 people in Massachusetts alone.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the intelligence-community framing skeptically, recalling that officials had once branded the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] Russian disinformation precisely because it revealed evidence implicating intelligence agencies.[2] While conceding the operation probably was foreign, she argued that such announcements are "the entire point of FVEY" — plausible deniability — and that in the post-COVID environment everything, including politician ownership and access, blackmail, bribery, extortion and crimes against children, is simultaneously global and local.[2] She noted that the sex-crime-and-blackmail-ring story coming into focus at the same time as the [[epstein-court-document-unsealing|Epstein document unsealing]] dominating [[daily-mail|The Daily Mail]]'s front page "doesn't feel coincidental," describing it instead as disclosures piling upon disclosures about the true nature of reality.[2]
