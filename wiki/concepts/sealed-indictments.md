# Sealed Indictments

"Sealed indictments" refers, in Badlands Media and [[qanon|Q]]-adjacent commentary, to the large and growing number of federal criminal charges filed under seal across United States district courts, interpreted as accumulated but not yet public prosecutions arising from investigations into the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]], the [[steele-dossier|Dossier]] and the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]].[1]

## Origin of the tracking claim

Q instructed followers to begin tracking sealed indictments, and Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] writes that their number "exploded and hasn't let up," reaching the hundreds of thousands where roughly 1,500 had previously been typical at any given time nationwide.[1] Q drop #2626 of December 19, 2018 quoted journalist [[sara-carter|Sarah Carter]] saying "There are a lot of sealed indictments" and [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] saying "It's all going to come out, U1, Dossier, CF, etc.," which Lange presents as confirmation that select journalists with good sources were signalling the existence of the hidden caseload while the rest of the press dismissed Uranium One as a conspiracy theory.[1]

## Why they remain sealed

Lange offers two explanations for the absence of public prosecutions.[1] First, [[donald-trump|Trump]] needed his own appointees in place at the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] and [[fbi|FBI]] — naming [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]], [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] as figures the enemy fears.[1] Second, the underlying matter is a [[racketeer-influenced-and-corrupt-organizations-act|RICO]] investigation rather than a series of individual corruption cases, so charges are held until the whole interconnected web can be charged together.[1] He adds that treason carries no statute of limitations and that under RICO the five-year limitations clock restarts after every connected crime, making the cover-up more legally dangerous to the participants than the original offence.[1]

Lange stresses that sealed indictments "don't just go away" and that those named in them are already indicted, awaiting only unsealing and the prosecution phase.[1] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than verified court statistics.
