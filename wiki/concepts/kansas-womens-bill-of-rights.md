# Kansas Women's Bill of Rights

The Kansas Women's Bill of Rights, filed as Senate Bill 180, is legislation defining a "woman" as a biological female under Kansas state law. The [[kansas-senate|Kansas Senate]] passed the act 26–10 in February 2023 without any Democratic support.[1]

## Content and rationale

The bill's sponsor, Republican Sen. [[renee-erickson|Renee Erickson]], said the legislation "does not deal with gender identity," but instead "simply codif[ies] in the law the definition of sex."[1] Erickson argued that the measure protects women's rights and that "there are legitimate reasons to distinguish between the sexes with respect to prisons, domestic violence shelters, rape crisis centers and other areas where safety and privacy are needed." She stressed that the bill "does not create any new rights or entitlements."[1]

## Prospects

The legislation was expected to pass the Republican-controlled Kansas House, but Democratic Governor [[laura-kelly|Laura Kelly]] was not expected to sign it into law.[1]
