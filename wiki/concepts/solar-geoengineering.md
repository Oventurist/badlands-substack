# Solar Geoengineering

Solar geoengineering refers to deliberate interventions intended to make the Earth's atmosphere a more effective reflector of sunlight, thereby cooling the planet.[1] The field became a subject of mainstream policy attention and of intense scrutiny within the Truth Community after a startup released sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere over Mexican territory.[1]

## Mexico's ban

[[mexico|Mexico]] banned solar geoengineering experiments after a startup sent sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere without authorization.[1] Coverage relayed by Badlands Media noted that it remains unknown whether solar geoengineering is a good idea at all, even as the [[white-house|White House]] was reported to be assessing how the atmosphere could be altered to cool the planet.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] wrote that "It appears Mexico isn't having any of the Bill Gates/Harvard created nonsense to block out the sun for 'climate change,'" adding that travelers to Cancun would not have to worry about planes dumping sulfur dioxide over the beaches.[1] He framed the episode as vindication, concluding that "aerosols spraying in our skies = yet another 'conspiracy theory' that's being proven true."[1] The reference implicates research programs associated with [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] and [[harvard-university|Harvard University]].[1]

## The San Francisco Bay marine cloud brightening test (2024)

In April 2024 a secretive project conducted from the deck of an aircraft carrier museum in San Francisco Bay began shooting trillions of aerosol particles into the sky to increase cloud cover in the name of preventing global warming, with details held back to "avoid (a) public backlash."[2] Dubbed America's "first outdoor test to limit global warming," the [[coastal-atmospheric-aerosol-research-and-engagement|Coastal Atmospheric Aerosol Research and Engagement]] project used specially built sprayers to disperse sea salt particles and increase the reflective capacity of marine clouds.[2] Reporting noted little data exists on the potential negative effects of such projects, which "could also harm communities and ecosystems in unexpected ways," and Shuchi Talati of the Alliance for Just Deliberation on Solar Geoengineering said the test had been "kept under wraps."[2] A comparable Harvard experiment over Sweden, previously funded in part by [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], had been cancelled the month before after opposition from campaigners and Indigenous groups.[2]
