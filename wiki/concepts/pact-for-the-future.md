# Pact for the Future

The Pact for the Future is the agreement that member nations of the [[united-nations|United Nations]] were slated to adopt at a landmark "Summit of the Future," scheduled for September 2024 — less than two months before the next U.S. presidential election.[1] The agreement was intended to solidify numerous policy reforms the U.N. had offered over the previous two years as part of its sweeping Our Common Agenda platform.[1]

## Emergency platform proposal

Among the agenda's proposals, the most consequential according to reporting by [[the-federalist|The Federalist]], relayed by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]], was a plan for a new "emergency platform" — a proposal to grant the U.N. significant powers in the event of future "global shocks," such as another worldwide pandemic.[1] The effort was described as proceeding with the support of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Coverage framed the U.N. as an institution many Americans deeply distrust, and drew a parallel to the domestic experience of emergency powers under governors such as [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] of Michigan, who critics said were reluctant to relinquish them.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that "the predatory class is desperate to establish a pathway for one of their institutions to gain emergency powers" that would elevate it into a temporary world-governing body, and that with Biden's support this could become a stepping stone toward a permanent world government.[1]

He identified three fronts in this push. First, the proposed [[who-pandemic-accord|Pandemic Treaty]], which would place the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] in the role of a temporary world governing body and which drew serious blowback.[1] Second, while attention focused on the treaty, a series of amendments to the [[international-health-regulations|International Health Regulations]] was proposed that would let the WHO supersede national governments during another pandemic.[1] Third, the United Nations itself — which DeLarme called "the granddaddy of all slimy, corrupt globalist institutions" — aiming at the same goal.[1] He expressed skepticism it would succeed, citing the large population of armed current and former U.S. military members willing to defend American sovereignty, and suggested this explained the intensity of gun-control efforts.[1]
