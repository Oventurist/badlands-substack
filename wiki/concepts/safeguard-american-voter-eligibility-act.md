# Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act (SAVE Act)

The **Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act**, commonly known as the **SAVE Act**, is federal legislation that would require documentary proof of United States citizenship — such as a passport or a birth certificate — as a condition of registering to vote in federal elections. The bill has been a signature election-integrity priority of congressional Republicans for several years.

## Passage in the House

The [[house-of-representatives|US House of Representatives]] passed the SAVE Act on a Wednesday in February 2026. House Republicans backed the measure as an election-integrity reform aimed at preventing noncitizen voting, arguing that verifying citizenship before registration is a minimal and self-evident safeguard. House Democrats opposed it, contending that a documentary-proof requirement would create practical barriers for eligible voters who do not have ready access to a passport, birth certificate, or other qualifying record — a burden they argued would fall disproportionately on the poor, the elderly, married women whose names have changed, and rural voters.

## Senate prospects

The bill faces significant obstacles in the [[senate|US Senate]], where it would need bipartisan support to overcome a [[filibuster]]. Absent sixty votes, House passage functions largely as a messaging exercise rather than a path to enactment.

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the *Badlands Brief*, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the SAVE Act as "a good piece of legislation," holding that proving citizenship before voting is basic and that opposition to it is either "stupid or evil." Her critique, however, was directed less at the bill's content than at its authors: a good law "passed by illegitimate 'lawmakers' that refuse to acknowledge their own legitimacy problem will not save the nation," which she characterized as a rug-pull.

The commentary framed opposition as coming from both wings of the [[uniparty]]. The "uniparty left" was cast as reflexively statist and prone to making every argument about itself — Ashe noted that [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] had never mentioned Democrats in the post that drew the criticism. The "uniparty right," by contrast, drew her sharper attention: she highlighted an exchange with a critic who blocked her shortly after posting, and pointed to the dates on successive posts to argue that Republicans in Congress have been promising to "save elections" with the SAVE Act for years while holding full control of government for over a year without delivering.

The underlying Badlands position is that procedural reform cannot precede accountability: "We cannot have real elections until we have accountability for the fake ones." Since the primary beneficiaries of contested past elections are the same officials who would have to authorize such accountability, the commentary treats reform-from-within as structurally improbable.
