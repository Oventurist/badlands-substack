# Pentagon Sustained Campaign Planning Against Iran (2026)

In February 2026, U.S. defense and administration officials were reported to be planning for the possibility of a sustained, weeks-long military campaign against [[iran|Iran]] should President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] order such action — a shift beyond limited strikes toward broader targeting of Iranian state and security infrastructure.[1]

## Reported planning and posture

The planning coincided with increased U.S. military deployments to the region, including the arrival of an additional aircraft carrier in the Middle East, and with ongoing diplomatic contacts between Washington and Tehran mediated by [[oman|Oman]].[1] Trump publicly suggested that regime change in Iran would be desirable, while Israeli leaders called for the dismantling of Iranian nuclear capabilities.[1] Taken together, the reporting underscored rising tensions and the complex interplay of diplomacy and military readiness in U.S.–Iran relations.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the reported planning as evidence for the contested claim that Israeli influence shapes U.S. policy, writing that he is accused of antisemitism for suggesting as much "and yet, things like this keep happening that support—if not affirm—the allegation."[1] He questioned why Iran requires a new government at all, arguing that even if its rulers were the worst tyrants imaginable in their treatment of Iranians, that would not by itself make the matter an American problem, and asserting that there is no evidence Iran poses a threat to the United States or to Israel.[1] He named [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]], [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] and Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] as prominent voices pressing the case for confrontation.[1]

He further argued that Iran, once geopolitically isolated, now maintains strong relations with major world powers — most notably Russia and China — and has become a geographic centerpiece of the [[north-south-transport-corridor|North–South Transport Corridor]], a trade route developed by the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organisation]] to compete with the Suez Canal.[1] In his reading, the deep economic entanglement among these states will itself hold Iran accountable, since economic pressure from inside and outside the country promotes peace and deters kinetic conflict.[1] The practical conclusion he drew is that Iran will not start or provoke a war with Israel — even granting hostility toward it — because Tehran knows such a war would draw in the United States and Europe, leading him to ask why the [[israel-lobby|Israel lobby]] is being permitted to drag the U.S. into the situation.[1]
