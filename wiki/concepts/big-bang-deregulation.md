# Big Bang Deregulation

The "Big Bang" was the 1986 deregulation of the [[city-of-london|City of London]] under [[margaret-thatcher|Margaret Thatcher]], which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes as announcing the beginning of a new era of economic irrationalism.[1]

## Content and effects

The reform swept aside the separation of commercial, deposit and investment banking that had been the post-World War cornerstone ensuring that the will of private finance would never again hold more sway than the power of sovereign nation-states.[1] Coming after decades of chipping away at the regulatory structure built by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]], the Big Bang set a precedent for similar financial deregulation into the "universal banking" model in other parts of the western world.[1]

## Context

The Big Bang followed the wave of liberalization that began in the 1970s and accelerated with the 1980–82 interest rate hikes of [[paul-volcker|Paul Volcker]].[1] Under Thatcherism and the Reaganomics of [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]], Ehret argues, western nations saw the destruction of national regulation, protectionism and oversight of private banking and corporate interests, accelerating the stripping of nations into consumer-society husks while transferring wealth to the 0.1%.[1] Together with [[nafta|NAFTA]], Euro integration and the rise of [[derivatives|derivatives]], universal banking induced an unprecedented cartelization of finance through newly legalized mergers and acquisitions.[1]
