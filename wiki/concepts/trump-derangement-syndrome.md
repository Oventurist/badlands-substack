# Trump Derangement Syndrome

**Trump Derangement Syndrome** is the pejorative label used in Badlands Media commentary for what its writers describe as an obsessive, hostility-driven reaction to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] among his political and media opponents.[1] [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] characterised those exhibiting it as "demonically-possessed," writing that for almost a decade they had "free roam to say whatever ridiculous or callous things that come into their minds" about Trump without penalty.[1]

## Claimed collapse after the Butler shooting

Carlson argued that the condition's social licence ended abruptly with the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] on July 13, 2024.[1] In his account, verbally attacking Trump became political suicide overnight, and insensitive comments began to carry consequences for people's relationships, influence and employment — a reversal of a decade in which such attacks had, if anything, been rewarded.[1] The counterpart to that shift, he wrote, was that publicly supporting Trump became acceptable for the first time, enabling "closeted" supporters to declare themselves openly.[1]

Carlson also claimed the incident forced a reassessment among Trump's detractors: those who regarded him as a [[deep-state|Deep State]] puppet saw him come within an inch of death, those who thought him hate-filled saw a humbled man grateful to be alive, and those who thought him motivated by money saw a man willing to die for his country.[1] He described this as walls being torn down and attitudes changing, framing the shooting as an event being used to heal and unite the nation.[1]

## Spring 2025 resurgence

Writing in April 2025, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] described a sharp revival of anti-Trump panic roughly a week after the president's early-March address to Congress, with liberals who had been dormant since the election "coming out swinging" over claims that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would kill Social Security, end Medicaid and Medicare, destroy the economy and expel non-white residents, that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had access to personal data, and that [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] would confiscate medicines.[2] He argued that a stock-market dip of barely 1% was all the proof these fears required.[2]

Carlson said he personally encountered the phenomenon through clients convinced a massive recession was imminent, and through celebrity examples such as a Netflix talk show on which [[joan-baez|Joan Baez]] and [[michael-keaton|Michael Keaton]] appeared and the audience applauded Baez's claim that Musk and Trump were attacking democracy.[2] He also cited Q's assertion that 4–6% of the population would be "lost forever" to conditioning, propaganda, toxins, drugs and abuse, while warning that a society cannot function if 10, 20 or 30% of Americans panic each time mainstream media announces the sky is falling.[2] He judged that although as much as 80% of the country appeared awake, half of those were still "half asleep" and liable to jump ship at any change in the tide — a fragility he said the [[tesla-vandalism-attacks|Tesla vandalism attacks]] and the backlash to [[doge|DOGE]] made visible.[2]
