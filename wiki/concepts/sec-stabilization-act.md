# SEC Stabilization Act

The SEC Stabilization Act was a bill introduced in the United States House of Representatives on June 12, 2023 by Republican Representative [[warren-davidson|Warren Davidson]] of Ohio and House Majority Whip [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] of Minnesota.[1] Its two central aims were the removal of [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]] from his position as chairman of the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] and a structural reorganization of the agency.[1]

## Provisions

The bill's sponsors justified the removal of the chairman by citing what they characterized as a "long series of abuses that have been permitted under the current SEC structure."[1] Beyond leadership, the legislation would expand the commission to six commissioners and prohibit any single political party from occupying more than three of those seats — a design intended to prevent partisan control of the agency and read by observers as a concession that the SEC had become too political.[1]

## Context and reaction

The bill arrived a week after the House passed a series of measures amending the Securities Act of 1933 with what commentators described as overwhelming bipartisan support.[1] Within the cryptocurrency community, where opposition to Gensler had become near-doctrinal, the announcement produced an immediate market and social-media reaction: the $GENSLR protest coin gained more than 25 percent and #FireGaryGensler climbed into the top three Twitter trends.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the bill also carried consequences for accountability over the "Russian Collusion" narrative, noting Gensler's prior role as chief financial officer of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s 2016 presidential campaign, the entity that funded the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]].[1]
