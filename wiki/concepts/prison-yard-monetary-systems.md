# Prison Yard Monetary Systems

Prison yard monetary systems are informal currencies — most famously cigarettes, but in practice any tradable good — created by inmates inside correctional institutions and war camps. In his April 27, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Economy: Weaponized," [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used them as the central illustration of his claim that ordinary people retain the power to create money without institutional permission.[1]

Deschamps observed that a cigarette used as money carries both a monetary value, since it can buy goods such as coffee in the prison black market, and a practical value, since it can be smoked.[1] The fact that a prisoner can imbue a cigarette with monetary value is, he wrote, a point that deserves underscoring.[1]

He offered a worked example: a prisoner who has received a box of candies may ask other inmates to trade goods or services for the candy. If the candy-holding inmate wants a bodyguard and a large, fit inmate wants candy, then at the moment the bargain is struck the candy has become money.[1] No permission from the prison was needed, no act of [[congress|Congress]] was required, no bankers were contacted, and no [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] meetings took place.[1]

Such systems are, according to Deschamps, everywhere: almost every prison has one or several operating at once despite the best efforts of the warden to stop them, and inmates often form strict policies around the money system to ensure that the monetary units used are fair and sustainable for the whole population.[1] He conceded the systems are imperfect, being run by criminals who engage in manipulations, but argued that if inmates in some of the most oppressive and resource-deprived environments can sustain enough collective coherence to run a grassroots monetary system, the general public can do the same.[1]

The argument connects to Deschamps's broader treatment of the [[thirteenth-amendment|Thirteenth Amendment]], under which he characterizes incarcerated persons as stripped of their rights and subject to involuntary servitude, and to his account of [[money|money]] as a symbolic token created by use rather than by decree.[1]
