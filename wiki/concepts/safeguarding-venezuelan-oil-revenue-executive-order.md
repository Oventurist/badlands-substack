# Executive Order on Safeguarding Venezuelan Oil Revenue

On January 9, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order titled *"Safeguarding Venezuelan Oil Revenue for the Good of the American and Venezuelan People,"* aimed at shielding Venezuelan oil revenues held in U.S. Treasury accounts from seizure or judicial attachment.[1] The White House said the action was meant to protect the funds so they could support U.S. foreign policy goals and help stabilize Venezuela's economy.[1]

## Context

The order came six days after a January 3, 2026 U.S. military operation that resulted in the capture of Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], and amid the elevation of [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] as acting president.[1] It was paired with the lifting of sanctions by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]].[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the order is best understood not as economic stabilization policy but as a move against the global banking system: by placing Venezuelan oil revenue in a [[qatar|Qatari]] bank account and prohibiting liens or sanctions against it, the administration removed the funds from the reach of bankers and creditors.[1] In this reading, Maduro's exit was "perfectly timed to coincide with a move against the global banking system."[1]

The mechanism matters because, with sanctions lifted, a civil action suit brought by Maduro against executives of banks, commodity trading houses, and oil companies — dismissed without prejudice in 2019 on national security grounds tied to Trump's own sanctions — could be resumed.[1] Discovery in such a suit, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry suggested, could expose the extent of money laundering at the highest levels of global finance, building on the 2018 arrest of [[mathias-krull|Mathias Krull]] and the asset forfeitures that followed, including those touching [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]].[1]

## Sullivan & Cromwell as an implied target

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that when [[donald-trump|President Trump]] signed the order — intended to protect [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s oil profits from creditors and "other vultures" — the law firm [[sullivan-and-cromwell|Sullivan & Cromwell]] was at least one of its intended targets.[2] The firm had been retained in 2019 by the U.S.-based Venezuelan opposition led by Juan Guaido to prepare a restructuring of Venezuela's roughly $60 billion in debt, and Badlands characterizes it as a [[deep-state|Deep State]] institution that functioned as the [[cia|CIA]] before the CIA existed.[2]
