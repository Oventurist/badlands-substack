# Executive Order 14217

**Executive Order 14217**, titled "Continuing the Reduction of the Federal Bureaucracy," was issued by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on February 19, 2025.[1] The order targeted the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] (USIP) and other entities for reduction to their "statutory minimums," labeling them "unnecessary" components of the federal government, and aimed to shrink the institute's operations, staff and functions significantly.[1]

## Implementation

Acting on the order, the administration fired 11 of USIP's 15 board members — apparently without providing legal or factual justification, in violation of the for-cause removal provisions of the USIP Act — and dismissed acting president and CEO [[george-moose|George Moose]], whose position was filled first by a [[doge|DOGE]] staffer and eventually by [[darren-beattie|Darren Beattie]].[1] On March 17, 2025, DOGE personnel backed by [[fbi|FBI]] agents, D.C. Metropolitan Police and the institute's own private security — whose contract had been canceled — forcibly entered USIP's Washington, D.C. headquarters after earlier unsuccessful attempts, amid reports of vandalism including removal of the institute's signage.[1]

## Litigation

USIP's leadership and staff described the takeover as illegal, asserting that the institute is a private nonprofit not technically under executive-branch control, and the fired board members filed lawsuits challenging the administration's actions.[1] In May 2025 Judge [[beryl-howell|Beryl Howell]] declared the firings, leadership replacements, staff terminations and asset transfers illegal and "null and void"; the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit stayed her ruling in late June, finding that USIP exercises "substantial executive power" and that the president likely holds at-will removal authority over its board.[1] The resulting disruption left most USIP staff fired, programs halted and the headquarters building repeatedly changing hands.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated the order and its aftermath as among the most consequential developments of Trump's second term, comparable to the dissolution of the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] and the termination of contractors associated with the [[counter-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-hub|R/FIMI hub]].[1] He argued that a successful outcome would furnish a working template for "seizing the institutions" that could then be applied to the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]] and the [[center-for-international-private-enterprise|Center for International Private Enterprise]].[1]
