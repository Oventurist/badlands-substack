# July 2025 Tariff Letters

The July 2025 tariff letters were a set of notices issued by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Monday, July 7, 2025, setting a 25 percent tax on goods imported from [[japan|Japan]] and [[south-korea|South Korea]], along with new tariff rates on a dozen other nations, all to take effect on August 1.[1]

## Form and mechanism

Rather than announcing the measures through conventional trade channels, Trump provided notice by posting letters on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] addressed to the leaders of the various countries.[1] The letters warned recipients not to retaliate by increasing their own import taxes, or else the administration would raise tariffs further: "If for any reason you decide to raise your Tariffs, then, whatever the number you choose to raise them by, will be added onto the 25 percent that we charge," Trump wrote to Japanese Prime Minister [[shigeru-ishiba|Shigeru Ishiba]] and South Korean President [[lee-jae-myung|Lee Jae-myung]].[1]

Coverage characterized the letters as not the final word on tariffs so much as another episode in a global economic drama in which Trump had placed himself at the center, noting that his moves had raised fears that economic growth would slow to a trickle, if not leave the United States and other nations more vulnerable to a recession.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that 25 percent tariffs on both Japan and South Korea were "very telling."[1] He framed the Japan tariff as the culmination of a 40-year odyssey, recalling Trump's appearances on multiple television programs in the 1980s — most notably with Oprah Winfrey — warning of the massive trade imbalance with Japan, explaining that most electronics, particularly VCRs, were by 1987 made in Japan, and that it was nearly impossible to bring a US-manufactured good such as a car into the Japanese marketplace.[1]

He considered the South Korea tariff even more telling, citing Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]]'s argument that South Korea has been a [[cia|CIA]] puppet state since the end of the Korean War, and that the South Korean government has long engaged in a "slap-tickle fight" with North Korea sustaining a constant existential threat from the ruling Kim dynasty — a threat exploited by the military and defense sector to extract wealth from the US taxpayer.[1] His conclusion was that the American people should never again be beholden to a foreign power that thinks it can dictate to the United States how things are going to be.[1]
