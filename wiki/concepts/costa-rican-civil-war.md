# Costa Rican Civil War

The Costa Rican Civil War of 1948 is presented in Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s "Illusions of Democracy" as the founding event of modern [[costa-rica|Costa Rica]] and as a historical template for how a people can reclaim a government stolen through election fraud.[1]

## Cause

According to the article, Costa Rica held an election in which the incumbent — a figure Lange characterizes as controlled by the cabal — lost. Rather than conceding, the incumbent directed the legislature controlled by his own political party to annul the election so that he could stay in power.[1] A separate source quoted in the piece confirms 1948 as "the year when the elections were deemed fraudulent and annulled by Congress, leading to the Costa Rican Civil War later that year."[1] The annulment triggered a popular uprising led by [[jose-figueres-ferrer|José "Pepe" Figueres Ferrer]].[1]

## Course

A 1990 [[new-york-times|New York Times]] editorial quoted in the article records that Figueres "plunged into politics in 1948 to challenge a coup carried out by the army in alliance with Communist-led guerrillas," and that "leading an irregular force of 700, [he] prevailed over them."[1] Lange notes the war lasted **only 44 days**.[1]

## Aftermath

Figueres headed a provisional junta that ruled for **18 months**. It enacted basic welfare legislation, promoted education, nationalized the banks and disbanded the armed forces — then stepped down, handing power back to the president who had actually been elected by the people.[1] Lange stresses how rare a voluntary return of power is, and argues it should be celebrated rather than hidden from history.[1] The junta also oversaw the election of a constitutional assembly that wrote the safeguards — non-consecutive term limits, the abolition of the military, and the [[cedula|cédula]] citizen ID — into a new constitution.[1] Since then, in the Times' phrasing, "Costa Ricans have settled their arguments constitutionally."[1]

## Legacy

The war and its settlement are, in Lange's argument, the reason Costa Rica escaped the dictatorship and repression that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and [[shadow-government|shadow government]] imposed on the rest of Central America through the [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]].[1] The [[legislative-assembly-of-costa-rica|Legislative Assembly]] later moved to declare Figueres a "Hero of peace" for the role he played.[1]
