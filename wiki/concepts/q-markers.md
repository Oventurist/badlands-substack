# Q Markers

A **marker**, in the interpretive vocabulary of the [[q-operation|Q Operation]], is something purposely left in a drop that can later be referred back to in order to demonstrate foreknowledge of a future event or scenario.[1]

## The 11.3 marker

Chapter 4 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series describes "11.3" as arguably the most famous Q marker to date, appearing in drop #25 of November 1, 2017.[1] Many anons speculated that the marker referred to the 2020 election itself, which took place on November 3 and which they regarded as a high-level crime against the country and its citizens.[1] The author writes that if the election was indeed the marker, no clear evidence of that has yet emerged, and that from where he stands the marker has not yet been seen — while allowing that if [[devolution|Devolution]] was initiated after the election, the election may very well have been the marker.[1]

## Deltas and "future proves past"

The chapter treats drop #24 as a strong "future proves past" case and borderline [[q-proofs|Q proof]], noting that far more politicians from both parties were stepping away and not seeking re-election in 2024 than ever before, whereas in 2017–18 only some chose not to run.[1] The author states that on [[eye-of-the-storm|Eye Of The Storm]] he and [[joe-lange|Joe]] both lean heavily toward many of the drops being six-year deltas, and that a great deal appears to line up with that framing.[1]

The chapter lists departures including [[george-santos|George Santos]] (expelled from Congress), Bob Menendez (under indictment for accepting bribes), [[mitt-romney|Mitt Romney]], Ken Buck, Kay Granger, Patrick McHenry, Debbie Stabenow, Laphonza Butler, Victoria Spartz, Debbie Lesko, Michael Burgess, Brad Wenstrup, Greg Pence, Jeff Duncan, Doug Lamborn, Larry Bucshon, Blaine Luetkemeyer, Drew Ferguson, Anna Eshoo, Grace Napolitano, Dan Kildee, Ben Cardin, Jennifer Wexton (retiring due to health), John Sarbanes, Earl Blumenauer, Derek Kilmer, Kathy Manning, Wiley Nickel, Brian Higgins, Bill Johnson (leaving to head Youngstown State University), Thomas Carper, and [[joe-manchin|Joe Manchin]] (a potential third-party run).[1] The author observes that the list is fairly balanced between Democrats and Republicans and that a number of the names are not America First or MAGA, while declining to claim that all of them are corrupt or being made to step away.[1]

Drop #25 is read as Q pointing back to the prior post, indicating that the resignations also related to what was then being seen with [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]].[1] The author notes that Pelosi later stepped down from her leadership role, and treats the eventual McCain outcome as the most undeniable Q proof; he also adopts the movement convention of referring to McCain as "No Name" thereafter, on the reading that Q withheld the surname because the senator was a traitor undeserving of having his name spoken.[1]
