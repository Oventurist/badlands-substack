# Military-Industrial-Media Complex

The military-industrial-media complex is described by Badlands Media as one of the sub-complexes of the broader [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial-congressional complex]], in which commercially controlled media promote war and militarism as an essential precondition for the system's operation.[1]

## Ownership as mechanism

The clearest evidence advanced for the arrangement is corporate cross-ownership between defence contractors and broadcasters. For years [[general-electric|General Electric]] owned 49% of NBC while simultaneously acting as a subcontractor for Tomahawk cruise missiles and Patriot II missiles, both used extensively during the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]].[1] GE also manufactured components for the B-2 stealth bomber, the B-52 bomber and the E-3 AWACS aircraft, likewise used extensively in the conflict.[1] The company received roughly $2 billion in defence contracts related to weapons used in the Gulf War and the 2003 invasion and occupation of Iraq.[1] Badlands Media presents this as one example among many of what it calls the historic marriage between defence contractors and corporate media.[1]

## Intelligence penetration

Beyond ownership, the article points to known CIA projects such as [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]], arguing that even setting those aside the case for Deep State control of the corporate press and its wedding to the interests of the [[permanent-war-economy|permanent war economy]] is compelling.[1]

## Effect on coverage

Badlands Media argues that most journalists and pundits worth their salt should be well aware of this system, yet talking heads repeatedly demonstrate a lack of understanding — or willful ignorance — of "the real monster lurking in the background behind so many headlines."[1] Where the mainstream can be expected to be largely in agreement, the publication finds the response of alternative media more interesting and more representative of the actual spread of public attitudes.[1]
