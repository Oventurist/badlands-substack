# Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS)

Poly- and perfluoroalkyl substances, commonly abbreviated PFAS, are a family of synthetic chemicals widely used to impart water-, grease- and stain-resistance to consumer goods.[1] They are popularly known as "forever chemicals" because they can persist in the environment for thousands of years and are not readily broken down or excreted by the human body.[1] Exposure to PFAS has been associated with a range of health problems including thyroid disease, increased cholesterol, liver damage, and kidney and testicular cancer, and the compounds are also regarded as environmentally harmful.[1]

## Detection in paper and bamboo straws

PFAS entered [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] coverage in August 2023 through a peer-reviewed study published in the journal Food Additives and Contaminants, which tested 39 straw brands sold by restaurants and retailers in Belgium for PFAS content.[1] The study found that a majority of the straws contained the chemicals, and that they were most prevalent in straws made from paper and bamboo — precisely the materials marketed as environmentally superior replacements for plastic.[1] Dr. Thimo Groffen, an environmental scientist at the University of Antwerp who participated in the research, observed that straws made from plant-based materials "are often advertised as being more sustainable and eco-friendly than those made from plastic," but that "the presence of PFAS in these straws means that's not necessarily true."[1] Groffen added that "small amounts of PFAS, while not harmful in themselves, can add to the chemical load already present in the body."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated the finding as illustrative of a broader pattern in which social engineers retire an imperfect legacy technology — in this case microplastic-shedding plastic straws — and substitute a replacement that sounds environmentally virtuous on paper while carrying a less visible harm.[1] He noted that although paper straws do biodegrade far faster than plastic ones, the presence of persistent fluorinated compounds means users absorb chemicals their bodies cannot filter out, and suggested that such substitutions ultimately serve to feed a medical-industrial complex.[1] DeLarme's commentary also promoted humic and fulvic acid supplementation as a mitigation, citing published research and a sponsor product.[1]
