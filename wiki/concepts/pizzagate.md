# PizzaGate

**PizzaGate** is the name of a long-running set of allegations, originating in 2016, concerning claimed child-exploitation networks tied to prominent Democratic political figures.[1] The allegations have been widely rejected by mainstream outlets, but remained a live subject within independent media through 2026.[1]

## 2026 congressional testimony

PizzaGate re-entered formal proceedings in March 2026 when [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was questioned about it during a congressional hearing connected to inquiries into the late financier [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1] Clinton answered questions related to her awareness of Epstein, PizzaGate, and related matters before storming out of the hearing after learning that a congressman had posted a photo of her on social media during her testimony.[1]

The questions on PizzaGate and the alleged [[frazzledrip|Frazzledrip]] video were brought by Representative [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]].[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Boebert "asked the main questions on the minds of many Americans" and, judging by the reaction of Clinton's attorneys, "she was over the target."[1]

## Origins in the Global Intelligence Files

The [[global-intelligence-files|Global Intelligence Files]], released by [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] beginning in 2012 and comprising thousands of emails dated 2004 to 2011, became the fodder for what came to be called PizzaGate.[2] Establishment media subsequently used the label to dismiss disfavoured or suppressed claims as "wild QANON Pizzagate conspiracy theories," though Badlands writers argue that despite an arguably billion-dollar effort to get ahead of the narrative, many people still believe there is truth in the material.[2]

A frequently cited item from that cache is a May 14, 2009 Stratfor email from Fred Burton stating that [[barack-obama|Obama]] "spent about $65,000 of the tax-payers money flying in pizza/dogs from Chicago for a private party at the White House," and asking whether the same "channels" were in use.[2] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] asked who spends $65,000 on hot dogs and why they must be flown in from Chicago through "channels," while conceding that nothing has been proven and that plausible deniability remains ample.[2]

## Clinton's 2024 October surprise warning

In an interview with "Firing Line" host Margaret Hoover published at the end of September 2024, former Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton]] invoked Pizzagate while warning of an October surprise that would "distort and pervert" Vice President [[kamala-harris]].[3] Describing "the crazy story about me running a child trafficking operation out of a basement of a pizzeria," Clinton rebuked the audience for laughing, noting that the theory "got one young man in North Carolina to get in his car with his, you know, assault rifle" and drive to Washington, D.C. to "liberate these nonexistent children and shoot up a pizzeria."[3] She characterized such narratives as starting online, often on the dark web, migrating to pro-Trump media, and then achieving near-total coverage once reported by everyone else, and warned that the "digital airwaves" would fill with misinformation before the election.[3]

Badlands commentary treated Clinton's remarks as a signal rather than a warning, noting the timing a month before the vote and reading the segment as foreshadowing a coming story about Harris.[3] The analysis layered in the simultaneous [[sean-combs-federal-trial|Sean Combs]] scandal piercing mainstream awareness — Combs described as "the Epstein of the music industry" — alongside Harris's alleged connections to and electoral support from the hip hop world, and recalled that Clinton had been a U.S. Senator from New York, the jurisdiction through which the highest-profile RICO cases had surfaced.[3]

## 2023 Instagram investigation and the "cheese pizza" code

The Pizzagate symbolism resurfaced in June 2023 when a joint investigation by the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] and the [[stanford-internet-observatory|Stanford Internet Observatory]] reported that [[meta-platforms|Meta]]-owned [[instagram|Instagram]] hosted a large organized pedophile network whose participants used coded emojis, including "cheese pizza" — sharing its initials with "child pornography" — and a map image standing for "minor-attracted person."[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the finding demonstrated the pizza association originally raised during the Comet Ping Pong controversy "wasn't invalid after all," while cautioning that this did not make every claim about elite child trafficking true.[4] The commentary contended that beneath a "well-manicured Potemkin village facade" there existed a sophisticated network servicing pedophiles that received far less attention from the [[justice-department|DoJ]] and [[congress|Congress]] than January 6 prosecutions or parents attending school board meetings.[4]
