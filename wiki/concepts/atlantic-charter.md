# Atlantic Charter

The **Atlantic Charter** is invoked in Badlands Media commentary as one of the founding statements of the post-war order whose original spirit contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] contrasts with its later revisions.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Relation to the Four Freedoms

Ehret argues that freedom from want, freedom from fear of war, freedom of speech and freedom of conscience — the [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]] outlined by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] in 1941 — are universal, and that they set the backbone of the spirit of the Atlantic Charter, of the [[united-nations|UN Charter]], and of the original designs of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) He specifies the Charter as it stood before what he calls the Biden–Bojo edits, a reference to the 2021 revision issued by the American and British governments.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Continuity with later declarations

In Ehret's telling these same universal freedoms were restated in the [[universal-declaration-of-human-rights|UN Declaration of Human Rights]] of 1947 and in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence that emerged from the 1955 [[bandung-conference|Bandung Conference]], which set the tone for the [[non-aligned-movement|Non-Aligned Movement]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) He notes the irony that the Four Freedoms are now, in his view, being advanced by Eurasian powers widely considered the most "un-American" groups on earth.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Wallace and the post-war design

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that [[henry-wallace|Henry A. Wallace]] worked closely with [[franklin-d-roosevelt|FDR]] and other New Deal Democrats to shape the 1942 Atlantic Charter — enshrining principles of global peace and cooperation into a sort of constitution — alongside the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods System]], in order to internationalize the success of the New Deal in the post-war world.[2] With FDR's death in April 1945 that plan did not unfold, and the United States instead adopted the [[truman-doctrine|Truman Doctrine]], containment and proxy wars.[2]
