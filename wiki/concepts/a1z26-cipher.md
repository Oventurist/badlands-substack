# A1Z26 Cipher

The **A1Z26 cipher** is a simple substitution scheme in which each letter of the English alphabet is mapped to its ordinal position, so that A equals 1 and Z equals 26.[1] It is one of the most elementary cryptographic puzzles and is widely used as an introduction to the mechanics of encoding and decoding.[1]

## Use in the Q drops

In Badlands Media analysis, A1Z26 is treated as a decode methodology explicitly sanctioned from within the [[qanon|Q]] corpus itself.[1] Drop #35, posted on 1 November 2017 at 10:56:38 PM Eastern, was signed "4, 10, 20," the first appearance of that particular ciphertext.[1] Drop #40, posted on 2 November 2017 at 2:18:35 AM Eastern, supplied a clue by which the key could be discovered.[1] Applying A1Z26 to the signature yields the letters D, J, T — Donald John Trump.[1]

The article argues that Q highlighted this cipher deliberately, to confirm it as a valid decode method, and that other explicit instructions such as "timestamps have meaning," "misspellings matter," and "future proves past" were given so that Anons could discover further cryptographic keys such as deltas.[1] The general principle drawn is that valid decode methodologies are sourced from within the drops themselves.[1]

## Variants and popular examples

The famous Ovaltine decoder-ring scene in *A Christmas Story* is offered as an accessible illustration: it uses ciphertext numbers, a number-to-letter translator serving as the key, and a resulting plaintext message, in a scheme similar to A1Z26 but with a different mapping of the alphabet to numbers.[1]

## Application to Covfefe

The same cipher is the decode method applied to [[covfefe|Covfefe]], producing the number sequence 3, 15, 22, 6, 5, 6, 5 that underpins the wider Badlands thesis.[1]
