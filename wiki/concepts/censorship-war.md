# Censorship War

The "censorship war" is the term Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] uses for one of the many battlegrounds in a larger worldwide struggle between populism and globalism.[1] He dates its violent phase to roughly the eight years preceding February 2025, with wins and losses on both sides, and locates the fighting both in the court of public opinion and in literal courtrooms around the world.[1]

## Flashpoints

DeLarme describes a set of geographic flashpoints where governments stress-test how far they can go in censoring dissent.[1] [[brazil|Brazil]] is where the pro-censorship camp has enjoyed its most unprecedented success, chiefly through Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] and the [[supremo-tribunal-federal|Supremo Tribunal Federal]].[1] The European Union's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]], the United Kingdom's [[online-safety-act-2023|Online Safety Act]], and Canada's [[online-streaming-act|Online Streaming Act]] (Bill C-11) constitute the legislative fronts.[1] What these frameworks share, in his account, is passage over widespread opposition — a top-down imposition rather than democratic consensus, which undermines their legitimacy.[1]

He argues that the last four years saw the [[rules-based-international-order|International Rules-Based Order]] bolster its enforcement capacity with the stated goal of combating populist opposition globally and preventing the return of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to the Oval Office.[1] Earlier Badlands treatments of these initiatives appeared in the Fall 2023 piece "The Censorship Regime and the Road to 2024" and in DeLarme's own "The War on Dissent."[1]

## The counteroffensive

The February 2025 joint lawsuit by [[trump-media-and-technology-group|Trump Media and Technology Group]] and [[rumble|Rumble]] against Moraes marked, in DeLarme's military framing, a counterattack from a defensive position: a force absorbs an enemy assault from a fortified position and then strikes immediately as the attacker weakens or overextends.[1] See [[rumble-and-trump-media-v-moraes|Rumble and Trump Media v. Moraes]].[1]

He treats the American technology sector's turn toward [[donald-trump|Trump]] — exemplified by [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]]'s reversal and his request that Washington protect U.S. firms from foreign governments "going after American companies and pushing to censor more" — as part of the same shift.[1] Protection of this kind, he notes, is not meant for [[meta-platforms|Meta]] alone but for all American platforms that have collided with the domestic and international [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]], notably [[rumble|Rumble]] and [[x-corp|X]].[1]

DeLarme cautions that a win against Moraes would not be an endgame, since "censorship's hydra has too many heads," and that it remains to be seen how proponents such as the EU's [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] will respond.[1] The open question, in his formulation, is whether a resurgent push for free expression can outpace the machinery of control from Brasilia to Brussels.[1]
