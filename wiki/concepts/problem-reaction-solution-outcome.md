# Problem, Reaction, Solution, Outcome (PRSO)

**PRSO** — Problem, Reaction, Solution, Outcome — is the four-stage model used in the Badlands Media "Structures of Control" series to describe how manufactured or exploited crises are converted into permanent expansions of centralized authority.[1] Introduced in the series as a 3D system, it is said to gain a further dimension under [[4d-timeline-control|4D control]], where timelines and generational influence take center stage.[1]

## Mechanism

The article holds that the outcomes of each PRSO cycle feed a sophisticated feedback mechanism in which real-time data — public reactions, resistance levels, compliance metrics — is analyzed to refine the variables of the next iteration.[1] This is said to create self-reinforcing generational loops that compound over time, each cycle embedding unresolved societal trauma and conditioning populations to regard centralized authority as inevitable.[1] The framework is described as weaving dependency into collective consciousness and normalizing reliance on external systems for stability and security.[1]

## Worked examples

The series applies the model to four episodes:[1]

- **1929, the Great Depression.** Problem: a sudden and catastrophic financial collapse. Reaction: panic as breadlines form and families lose everything. Solution: centralized banking systems such as the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] emerging as saviors. Outcome: normalized generational dependency and eroded sovereignty. Feedback: the public's desperate acceptance of centralized banking became the template for later economic crises.[1]
- **9/11.** Problem: coordinated terror attacks inciting fear and outrage. Reaction: citizen demand for swift action. Solution: passage of the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]] and the launch of the [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]]. Outcome: future generations accepting surveillance and militarization as necessary. Feedback: resistance to mass surveillance was tracked, leading to covert integration of monitoring into social media and technology platforms.[1]
- **2008, the financial crisis.** Problem: "reckless practices" by elite institutions producing global collapse. Reaction: public anger at Wall Street amid fear of instability. Solution: government bailouts entrenching "too big to fail" institutions. Outcome: further shift of financial dependency toward centralized systems. Feedback: resistance to bailouts was studied, informing narratives casting central banks as stability providers.[1]
- **2020, the COVID-19 pandemic.** Problem: a global health crisis producing mass fear. Reaction: compliance with unprecedented lockdowns and mandates. Solution: normalization of vaccine passports and digital IDs. Outcome: deepened surveillance infrastructure and reliance on centralized public-health systems. Feedback: dissent, including protests and alternative media narratives, was tracked to refine future control strategies.[1]

## Reinforced trauma

The article argues that in 4D the tracking is psychological as well as tactical: unresolved fear from one crisis feeds the next, ensuring a public predisposed to compliance, while long-term variables drawn from historical patterns determine how each new crisis is framed to elicit predictable reactions.[1] Compounded trauma is said to lower collective consciousness and lock populations into survival-based thinking, which the series treats as the precondition for the vibrational manipulation it discusses elsewhere.[1]
