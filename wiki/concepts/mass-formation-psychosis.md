# Mass Formation Psychosis

**Mass formation psychosis** is a term used in alternative-media commentary to describe a condition in which a large population is conditioned, through sustained and coordinated messaging, to accept a false narrative as self-evident truth.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] applied the concept to the American public's reception of the [[russiagate-hoax|RussiaGate]] narrative.[1]

## Application to RussiaGate

In Cates's account, the public was subjected to a years-long psychological operation conducted through five channels: federal law enforcement agencies, intelligence agencies, congressional hearings and investigations, the mainstream media and major social platforms, and popular culture in the form of books, films and music.[1] The purpose was to sell the claim that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and those around him were Russian agents and traitors.[1]

Cates listed eleven media organizations he considered participants in poisoning the prospective jury pool for years before a U.S. Attorney was even appointed: the New York Times, the Washington Post, CNN, ABC, NBC, CBS, Fox News, Time, Newsweek, NPR, and Twitter/Facebook.[1] The campaign, he wrote, continued at full intensity for a further three years while [[john-durham|Durham]] conducted his investigation.[1]

## Consequences for prosecution

The practical implication drawn from the concept is procedural: [[durham-special-counsel-investigation|Durham's Special Counsel investigation]] had to be conducted "backwards," using minor charges against low-level participants as vehicles for surfacing suppressed evidence, because an unbroken conditioning campaign made a fair trial on major charges unattainable.[1] Cates argued that people who insisted the big indictments should have come years earlier were refusing to acknowledge the power of the psyop, and that a broader public awakening — including about the press and the [[russiagate-hoax|RussiaGate hoax]] itself — had to occur before major indictments could succeed.[1] He acknowledged that a large segment of the public remained influenced by the campaign even as many others had come to reject it.[1]
