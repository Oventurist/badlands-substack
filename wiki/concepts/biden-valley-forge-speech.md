# Biden Valley Forge Speech (January 2024)

The Valley Forge speech was President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s first major campaign event of 2024, delivered near Valley Forge, Pennsylvania on Friday, January 5, on the eve of the third anniversary of the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol.[1]

## Content

Biden argued that democracy and fundamental freedoms were under threat if former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] returned to the [[white-house|White House]], calling Trump and far-right extremists a threat and a danger to the freedoms on which the country was founded and echoing themes from his 2020 campaign, which he had called "a battle for the soul of the nation."[1] In remarks that seemed especially personal, he opened by saying, "The topic of my speech today is deadly serious. And I think it needs to be made at the outset of this campaign."[1]

He continued: "Today we are here to answer the most important of questions: Is democracy still America's 'sacred cause'?" — repeating a phrase used by George Washington — and added, "This is not rhetorical, academic, or hypothetical. Whether democracy's still America's 'sacred cause' is the most urgent question of our time. And it's what the 2024 election is all about."[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized the address as a "fiery, impassioned" speech that dealt not with Biden's plans for a second term but with the asserted danger posed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the hundreds of millions of Americans expected to vote for him.[2] In DeLarme's account, Biden portrayed himself and the Democratic Party as the exclusive protectors of democracy in the United States and compared Trump to [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] and his supporters to the Nazis, while making no mention of the economy or of escalating conflict in the Middle East.[2] DeLarme argued that describing the American electorate's chosen candidate and his voters as a Nazi-like threat worthy of scorn and social ostracization was itself a form of dehumanization, and that the United States is a constitutional republic rather than a pure democracy.[2]

A substantial portion of the speech was devoted to praising the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s prosecution of January 6 defendants. Biden stated that "more than 1,200 people have been charged with assault in the Capitol. Nearly 900 of them have been convicted or pled guilty. Collectively, to date, they have been sentenced to more than 840 years in prison," a line the audience greeted with approval, and he contrasted this with Trump calling the defendants patriots and promising to pardon them.[2] DeLarme responded that the great majority of those convicted in connection with January 6 were not alleged to have committed violence, and that the crowd was cheering the persecution of fellow citizens.[2]

Biden also recounted the testimony of a [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] officer who described the day as a "medieval battle" and said he had been more afraid inside the halls of Congress than while serving as a soldier in the Iraq War.[2] DeLarme disputed the framing, noting that no protester wielded a weapon inside the Capitol and that the only firearm discharged that day was fired by a Capitol Police officer, killing Trump supporter [[ashli-babbitt|Ashli Babbitt]].[2] He further criticized Biden's claim that Trump "echo[ed] the same exact language used in Nazi Germany" by posting the words "revenge," "power" and "dictatorship," pointing to the administration's own support for Ukrainian units with neo-Nazi associations such as the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] and to allegations of genocide in Gaza then before the International Court of Justice.[2]

## Reception and criticism

The New York Times praised the address and welcomed Biden's decision to make the democracy theme the focal point of his 2024 campaign.[2] Former [[barack-obama|Obama]] speechwriter [[jon-favreau|Jon Favreau]], host of [[pod-save-america|Pod Save America]], publicly gushed over the performance.[2] [[cnn|CNN]] reported that Biden's inner circle planned to ratchet up the intensity of attacks on Trump through the campaign year, with younger aides joking about when to "go full Hitler" — that is, when to move from saying Trump "parroted" the Nazi leader to a direct comparison.[2] CNN commentary separately asserted that Trump "poses the greatest threat to democracy that we've ever seen."[2]

DeLarme argued that the speech's democracy framing was undermined by the Democratic establishment's simultaneous efforts to jail its principal opponent and to remove him from state ballots, and by the party's refusal to hold primary debates or to place challengers such as [[dean-phillips|Dean Phillips]] and [[marianne-williamson|Marianne Williamson]] on the ballot.[2] He also pointed to [[missouri-v-biden|the federal court findings]] that the administration had committed one of the gravest First Amendment violations in years by using the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], [[fbi|FBI]], [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] and other agencies to pressure Big Tech into censoring dissent — a story broken by [[matt-taibbi|Matt Taibbi]] and colleagues in the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] reporting and, in his telling, buried by the corporate press.[2]

## Preparation

Aides told [[abc-news|ABC News]] chief White House correspondent Mary Bruce that the president was closely involved in writing the speech, which followed a meeting he held that week with historians and scholars at the White House.[1] The item was carried as a bonus entry in the [[badlands-media|Badlands News Brief]] of January 8, 2024, whose headline framed the address as Biden "barking at MAGA hats."[1]
