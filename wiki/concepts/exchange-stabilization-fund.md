# Exchange Stabilization Fund

The Exchange Stabilization Fund (ESF) is a US Treasury fund established in 1934 that is controlled by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] and the president and is exempt from Congressional approval.[1]

## Origins and function

The fund was originally set up as a means of intervening in currency markets to benefit the US economy.[1] Over time, according to the January 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "Master and Commander," it evolved into a loan program for other countries' central banks in distress — effectively a permanent bail-out option for global central banks.[1] The essay stresses that most people did not know the fund existed.[1]

## Role in the March 2020 Fed–Treasury arrangement

In March 2020 the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] financed a special purpose vehicle (SPV) for each of its emergency lending facilities, while the Treasury used the Exchange Stabilization Fund to make an equity investment in each SPV and take the "first loss" position.[1] In substance the Treasury, not the Fed, was buying the securities and backstopping the loans, with the Fed acting as banker and provider of financing, and [[blackrock|BlackRock]] hired to execute the purchases and administer the SPVs on the Treasury's behalf.[1] Commentary at the time described the scheme as essentially merging the Fed and the Treasury into one organization.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argued that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] used the fund not to bail out foreign central banks, as had become customary, but to bail out American companies struggling during the pandemic, forcing the Federal Reserve to finance the effort.[1] In his reading, this made the Fed and the Treasury servants of the American people rather than of the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]]-aligned central banking network, and marked the point at which the [[global-debt-system|global debt system]] lost its grip on the dollar.[1]
