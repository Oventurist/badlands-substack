# Pause Giant AI Experiments Open Letter

The open letter of March 22, 2023 called "on all AI labs to immediately pause for at least 6 months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4." Signatories cited in the July 2023 Badlands Media article "[[artificial-intelligence|Artificial Intelligence: Hysteria vs. Reason]]" include [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]], [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]] (rendered "Ava Raskin" in the source), [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]], and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].

## The article's objection

The Badlands article treats the letter as self-defeating. It measures the proposal against [[mo-gawdat|Mo Gawdat]]'s "three inevitables" — that AI is happening, that AI is becoming far smarter than humans, and that really bad things will happen — and concludes that if the first is accepted, a pause cannot work.

Former Microsoft CEO [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] is quoted from a May 2023 interview with ABC News making the same point: "If you just pause the good guys and you don't pause everyone else, you're probably hurting yourself." The author remarks that she "definitely didn't see myself agreeing with Bill Gates, but he is right: You're only hurting yourself by complying."

The argument extends to enforcement asymmetry. If bad actors such as drug cartels obtain AI capabilities, law enforcement must be able to counter them — a case for "AI policing" rather than abstention. And the capabilities in question are unprecedented in kind: "Nukes can't make more nukes. But AIs can create more AIs."

## Scope and carve-outs

The article notes that the letter itself "provides caveats for the research," so that the requested pause applies in practice only to the commercial side of AI and, more narrowly still, to public access. The systems visible to ordinary users — "the Chat GPTs of the world" — are described as "toys in comparison to those capabilities being developed by governments, tech R&D firms, and defense contractors," none of which would halt work without committing "competitive and national security suicide."

## Institutional context

To show that no pause materialized, the article assembles statements from the [[united-nations|United Nations]] (June 2020), the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] (June 2023), [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]] (June 2023), and [[pwc|PwC]] (June 2023). Its reading is that the UN issued a collaborative blueprint and that three years later "all the entities are full steam ahead on AI research" — permitted, in the author's view, so long as development proceeds "with the communist principles of intersectionality and equity" — while public access to [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] is cast as the threat to humanity. "See how that works?"
