# Five Stages of Grief

The five stages of grief — denial, anger, bargaining, depression and acceptance — are used in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as a framework for predicting how Americans who accept mainstream narratives would process the loss of that worldview following a [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] election victory.[1]

## Application to a Trump victory

In "The Aftermath of a Trump Victory" (24 October 2024), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that those willing and able to accept the truth would likely pass through the coping stages associated with grief and the loss of their false reality, while some would never accept it at all.[1]

**Denial.** Carlson predicted that some would deny the election result outright, insisting Trump could not have won a fair election and that it therefore must have been stolen — bypassing four years of conditioning that US elections are safe and secure.[1]

**Anger.** The next stage would be anger that, in their minds, so many stupid and evil people voted for Trump; they would refuse to call him their president, and would riot, protest and spit in people's faces.[1]

**Bargaining.** People would make deals with themselves to feel better, convincing themselves they would push back and expose the fascist and fraud they believed Trump to be.[1]

**Depression.** As the reality of the loss settled in, they would become depressed — fearing the world would end within a decade from climate change, that their rights would be stripped and that they would be jailed for dissent — losing hope and motivation, with some unable to function or work and some suicidal.[1]

**Acceptance.** Eventually most would realise the world was not ending, their rights were intact and the country was flourishing, and that they would not be imprisoned; they would recognise that none of the predicted catastrophes occurred, and would move on even while continuing to hate Trump and conservatives.[1]

## Outcomes after acceptance

Carlson divided the post-acceptance population into three groups: those who acknowledge they were wrong and humble themselves, those who understand they were wrong without saying so and move on regardless, and those permanently broken by the discovery that the world they thought was real was a media-created false reality.[1] He compared the aggregate effect to a large population of alcoholics going cold turkey simultaneously, with withdrawal symptoms from no longer being told what to believe, and warned of resulting suicides, depression and anxiety.[1] The framework underpins his argument that those already awake should act as a triage tent — helping the recoverable and declining to argue with the permanently lost.[1]
