# Money Trust

"The money trust" was the colloquial name given to the small circle of Wall Street financiers — centered on [[jp-morgan|J.P. Morgan]] and his cohorts — accused of exercising monopoly control over American credit, industry and banking in the early twentieth century.[1]

## The 1907 crash

It is widely believed, Badlands Media writes, that in 1907 Morgan and the money trust secretly crashed the stock market.[1] The group was aware that thousands of small banks were vastly overextended, some holding reserves of only 1 percent under the [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve]] principle, and bank runs soon became commonplace across the nation.[1]

## The Pujo findings

The [[pujo-committee|Pujo Committee]], a congressional subcommittee created after the Aldrich bill was identified as a bill to benefit the bankers, was tasked with investigating Morgan and several other Wall Street bankers.[1] Its report found that Morgan and others, including Rockefeller, [[paul-warburg|Warburg]] and [[jacob-schiff|Schiff]], had gained control of major manufacturing, transportation, mining, telecommunications and financial markets throughout the United States.[1]

## Political framing

Representative [[charles-a-lindbergh-sr|Charles A. Lindbergh Sr.]] argued that the real purpose of the monetary commission was to frame amendments to currency and banking laws "which would suit the Money Trust," and that "Wall Street speculation brought on the Panic of 1907" because depositors' funds had been loaned to gamblers and to anybody the Money Trust wanted to favor.[1] Many people at the time believed the panic had been premeditated and manufactured by Wall Street elites to gain ultimate control over banking and industry, but no action was ever taken against the moguls involved.[1]
