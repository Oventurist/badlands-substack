# United States Sovereign Wealth Fund

The United States Sovereign Wealth Fund is a state investment vehicle ordered into existence by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on February 3, 2025, when he signed an executive order directing the establishment of the first such fund in American history.[1] Trump described the move as fulfilment of a campaign promise, telling reporters in the Oval Office, "I think it's about time that this country had a sovereign wealth fund. I think in a short period of time, we'd have one of the biggest funds."[1] He also suggested the fund could be used to keep [[tiktok|TikTok]] operating in the United States, without offering details.[1]

## Design and funding

Sovereign wealth funds are investment vehicles owned by national governments, used to stabilize an economy through diversified holdings and to generate wealth for future generations; they are most common in non-Western, oil-rich states including Abu Dhabi, China, Norway, Singapore and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[1] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said the administration would "stand this thing up within the next 12 months," but did not specify where the capital would originate.[1]

Trump indicated the fund would invest in national development projects such as highways, airports, manufacturing hubs and medical research, help pay down the national debt, and be financed with income collected from new tariffs.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] welcomed the announcement and highlighted the tariff-funding mechanism.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Trump's name-check of Saudi Arabia as deliberate signal, noting that the Saudi [[saudi-public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]] was capitalized in part with assets seized during the 2017 anti-corruption purge — "What's [theirs] is ours."[1] Burning Bright also placed the fund within a wider sequence of 2025 moves alongside the tariff campaign and the dismantling of [[usaid|USAID]], describing it as a possible "#Drainage" operation in which seized system assets are rehypothecated back to the people from whom they were originally taken.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] compared the initiative to the Saudi PIF, which is funded by proceeds from [[saudi-aramco|Aramco]] and underwrites much of the Saudi state, and argued that the United States had lacked such a fund because its government had long operated as an extractive criminal syndicate; he speculated that assets seized by executive order might be folded into the new fund.[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] read the announcement as a "#Drainage" communication as well.[1]
