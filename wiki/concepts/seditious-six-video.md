# "Seditious Six" Video

The "Seditious Six" video is the name applied by critics to a video message released on November 18, 2025 in which six Democratic members of Congress — all of them military veterans or former intelligence officers — urged U.S. service members and intelligence personnel to refuse illegal orders from the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1] The participants were Senators [[elissa-slotkin|Elissa Slotkin]] of Michigan and [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]] of Arizona, and Representatives [[chris-deluzio|Chris Deluzio]] of Pennsylvania, [[maggie-goodlander|Maggie Goodlander]] of New Hampshire, [[chrissy-houlahan|Chrissy Houlahan]] of Pennsylvania and [[jason-crow|Jason Crow]] of Colorado.[1]

## Administration response

Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] characterized the video as a "politically-motivated influence operation" that injected doubt into the armed forces, writing on X that while the message "may seem harmless to civilians," it "carries a different weight inside the military."[1] Hegseth argued that the video never identified any specific illegal order, that it "created ambiguity rather than clarity," that it employed "carefully scripted, legal-sounding language," and that it "subtly reframed military obedience around partisan distrust instead of established legal processes."[1]

The release drew broad pushback from Republican lawmakers and administration officials. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the six lawmakers "traitors" and accused them of "SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR," the phrasing from which the episode's nickname derives.[1] White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] subsequently said the president wanted the six Democrats to face consequences but did not want members of Congress executed.[1] The lawmakers featured in the video said they had received a variety of threats following Trump's post.[1]

## Commentary and related developments

Badlands commentary framed the episode through the lens of the [[higgins-memo|Higgins memo]], a May 2017 document by Rich Higgins describing "withering information campaigns" aimed at delegitimizing and removing the president; [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] cited the memo on X in response to the video, arguing that the situation reflects "political warfare" and an ongoing color revolution rather than ordinary partisan conflict.[1] Jon Herold noted that the memo has been recommended reading in the Devolution series for years.[1]

Herold also observed that on November 18, 2025 — the same day the video was published — the Department of War issued a new instruction, [[dodi-8025-01|DoDI 8025.01, "DoD Internet Content Blocking"]], whose details were not publicly available; he suggested the timing implied a connection to the video.[1]
