# 2025 Off-Year Elections

The 2025 off-year elections, held on Tuesday, November 4, 2025, were the first major electoral tests of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second presidency and produced a sweep of victories for the Democratic Party.[1]

## Results

Democrats won major contests across multiple states, building momentum into the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]].[1] CNN projected that [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] would win New York City's mayoral race, while [[abigail-spanberger|Abigail Spanberger]] and Rep. [[mikie-sherrill|Mikie Sherrill]] won the gubernatorial races in Virginia and New Jersey respectively.[1] Californians also voted to approve the Democrats' redistricting push.[1] [[jay-jones|Jay Jones]] was projected to unseat Virginia Attorney General [[jason-miyares|Jason Miyares]] despite a scandal over violent 2022 texts about then-Speaker [[todd-gilbert|Todd Gilbert]].[1]

Several historic firsts accompanied the results: Mamdani would be New York City's first Muslim mayor and the first South Asian to hold the office, Spanberger would become Virginia's first female governor, Sherrill would become New Jersey's first female Democratic governor, and [[ghazala-hashmi|Ghazala Hashmi]], projected as Virginia's lieutenant governor, would be the nation's first Muslim woman elected statewide.[1]

In a social media post that night, Trump blamed GOP losses on the government shutdown and on his own absence from the ballot.[1]

## Election-day disruptions

As New Jersey voters went to the polls, bomb threats forced voters out of multiple polling places; the threats were found to be non-credible but still disrupted voting.[1] Mamdani called the threats "incredibly concerning" and "an illustration of the attacks we're seeing on our democracy," linking them to what he described as the Trump administration's approach of intimidating voters with baseless allegations of voter fraud.[1] White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] called Mamdani's remarks "completely irresponsible" and not based in fact.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] joked that Republicans "would have won these elections if [[scott-presler|Scott Pressler]] registered voters harder."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump and common sense hold the elections to be fraudulent while "Systemites, NeoCons, Globalists and NGOs" deny it, urging readers to "believe your eyes."[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the best possible outcome of the "fake elections" was that more people would come to understand that elections are fake and that taking them seriously at all assists the enemy.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] pointed to remarks he had made on August 15, 2025 at GART 9 in Deadwood, South Dakota, saying elections are fake, that the winner "was installed," and that Glenn Youngkin had been "a pacification project."[1]

## Republican recriminations

Republicans were left reeling the day after the vote as the scale of their defeats set in, prompting fears that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his low approval ratings would again drag down the party's candidates in the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterm elections]].[2] Party figures blamed their candidates, the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|government shutdown]], a misguided focus on demonizing transgender issues, and a weak economic message.[2] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] used a news conference to cast [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]], the mayor-elect of New York City and a self-described democratic socialist, as the new leader of the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]]; in emails and group chats Republican officials called their Virginia gubernatorial nominee fatally flawed and chided donors for insufficient giving; and on Fox News others argued Democrats had prolonged the shutdown for their own advantage.[2] As [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] observed, the one person no Republican dared to blame was Trump himself.[2]

## Democratic embrace of disruption

[[politico|Politico]] framed the Democratic sweep as a forceful signal that backlash was building against the Trump administration's policies, and also as a repudiation of "Bidenism."[2] After a presidency that had promised a return to normalcy, Democrats and many independents embraced political disruption rather than a restoration of conventions.[2] In New York City voters elevated Mamdani, a 34-year-old activist, to one of the nation's most demanding executive jobs; in California radical politics found expression in procedure, as Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and his party responded to a Republican push to gerrymander red states by discarding California's nonpartisan redistricting process and asking voters to approve a new map eliminating GOP seats — the [[2025-california-redistricting-counterpunch|California redistricting counterpunch]].[2] "The rules of the game have changed. Now, we have to rewrite the new rules," Newsom said, adding that while there was a desire to return to some semblance of normalcy, "you have to deal with the crisis at hand."[2]

## Additional Badlands commentary

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] called it comical to watch commentators dissect every possible explanation for the results while never mentioning what he termed the fraudulent election system, asking whether the complaint about "weak candidates" in fact reflected structural barriers preventing America First candidates from reaching the ballot.[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] criticized self-described patriots upset that Trump, Q, and the military had not saved them while preserving the system, arguing that conferring power on corrupt actors through knowingly fake elections guarantees repetition and that the commitment to legitimizing the system's authority is itself the problem.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the narrative shift toward disruption — "if we can't run the institutions, we'll burn them down" — and argued that the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|DSA]]-driven platform now shaping the Democratic Party is not popular with the public, closing with "elections are fake and tyrants never voluntarily return power to the people."[2]

## Down-ballot sweep

Reporting after the November 2025 elections found that Democratic gains extended far beyond the marquee contests, in what The Washington Post characterized as a referendum on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[3] In Pennsylvania's Bucks County, voters elected a Democratic district attorney for the first time since the 1800s as part of a Democratic sweep of every county office, including controller and recorder of deeds.[3] In Georgia, Democrats ousted two Republicans on the Public Service Commission, the party's first capture of a nonfederal statewide office in the state since 2006; in Connecticut, Democrats took control of 28 towns from the GOP; and in New Jersey, Democrats won their biggest majority in the General Assembly since the Watergate era.[3]

While attention focused on the Virginia and New Jersey governor's races and the New York mayoral contest, the party also won hundreds of lower-profile state and local races, often swamping Republican incumbents with overwhelming turnout.[3] Pennsylvania Democratic strategist Dan McCormick said voters "are not just mad — they're really mad, and they are willing to do something about it."[3] The results were also cited as the backdrop to Trump's announcement of a [[trump-tariff-dividend-proposal|$2,000 tariff dividend]], since Democratic campaigns had centered on affordability and cost of living.[3]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] declared that "elections are fake" and that laws were being passed in the people's name through the same fake electoral process.[3] He quoted [[liz-harrington|Liz Harrington]], who wrote on X that votes were being skimmed from MAGA voters in order to "pass" abortion referendums and that "the computers being used to manipulate our elections must be banned."[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a different reading, arguing that amid a rapid and open communist re-brand for mainstream liberalism, Trump was aiming his rhetoric not solely at the Left but at Republicans, pushing them to seize the moment and actualize his mandate — with the "uniparty most affected."[3]
