# Central Banking

Central banking is the institutional arrangement by which power and authority over a currency and the wider financial system are concentrated in a central organization — the sense in which, as Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] observes, the institutions holding power over the financial system are called "central" banks.[1]

## Critique of centralization

Lange argues that the entire financial system has become centralized and controlled from top to bottom, with a cabal of elite bankers owning and controlling the currency, owning and controlling governments, and using client politicians to pass laws that benefit them.[1] He quotes Bob Moriarty's dictum that "you cannot have an honest economy without an honest financial system and honest money."[1]

A parallel trend is consolidation within commercial banking: the number of banks across the country has been declining for decades, increasing centralization in the industry, and with fewer banks there are fewer opportunities and choices for small businesses, farmers and first-time home buyers.[1]

## Trust as the system's vulnerability

Lange identifies trust — reliance on the integrity, strength, ability and surety of a person or thing — as the one thing a global financial system requires and simultaneously the banks' greatest vulnerability, since a lack of trust is what makes banks susceptible to bank runs and possible failure.[1]

## The decentralizing answer

The remedy proposed is to decentralize the financial system and create competition, giving people the power to choose.[1] Digital banks and [[financial-technology|financial technology]] services are described as disrupting the control central banks have held for many generations.[1] Lange concludes the article by promising a further installment on what the coming financial collapse will look like and why he does not fear [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currencies]] at all.[1]

## The first American central bank

Badlands Media traces central banking in the United States to December 1791, when, in [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]]' account, international bankers who had used Britain and other nations as proxies for commercial conquest schemes installed the [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|First Bank of the United States]] as an offshore central bank.[2] Within six months the [[panic-of-1792|Panic of 1792]] followed, worsened by the new bank restricting credit — a sequence Deschamps called a carbon copy of the 2023 [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] episode.[2] The section is framed by Thomas Jefferson's warning that banking institutions are more dangerous to liberty than standing armies and that the issuing power should be restored to the people.[2]
