# Transgender Athletes

**Transgender athletes** in competitive sport are treated in Badlands Media commentary as both a genuine fairness question and, more importantly, as an instrument of manufactured cultural conflict.

## The fairness argument

The observation offered is asymmetry: no biological women appear to be choosing to compete against men. Advocates claim there is no advantage to being born male, yet women who transition to male do not seem to compete against other males; the flow runs the other way, and when biological males compete against females they tend to dominate them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-we-being-distracted) The inference drawn is that the pattern is too consistent to be incidental — almost as if the arrangement is purposely designed to upset people, since if transgender competitors were finishing in the middle of the pack no one would care.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-we-being-distracted)

## The instrumentalization argument

The stronger claim is that transgender athletes are not the beneficiaries of the institutions promoting them but their raw material. The [[international-olympic-committee|Olympic Committee]] is said to have effectively barred from the Games anyone who did not fully transition by age twelve, on unfair-advantage grounds — which, in this reading, demonstrates that the committee does not actually care about transgender people or their rights.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-we-being-distracted) Instead they are described as being used by the [[cabal|Cabal]] to upset and distract the world; the athletes themselves are said not to understand that they are being used, believing they are being appreciated, while spectators do not understand that they are being purposely manipulated by the show.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-we-being-distracted)

The theme was crystallized in the [[paris-olympics-2024-opening-ceremony|Paris 2024 opening ceremony]] and its Last Supper tableau, which [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cited as the clearest example of using transgender imagery to generate maximal offense.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-we-being-distracted) He was careful to add that recognizing manipulation does not require endorsement: people should not support anything they find morally repulsive, but should vote with their dollars and their conscience rather than surrender their attention to engineered anger.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-we-being-distracted)

## Mid Vermont Christian School forfeiture (2023)

In February 2023 the [[mid-vermont-christian-school|Mid Vermont Christian School]] girls basketball team withdrew from the Vermont Division IV state tournament rather than play an opponent with a transgender student-athlete, forfeiting its first-round game against Long Trail.[2] Head of school [[vicky-fogg|Vicky Fogg]] said that "playing against an opponent with a biological male jeopardizes the fairness of the game and the safety of our players," and that allowing biological males into women's sports "sets a bad precedent for the future of women's sports in general."[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] praised the forfeiture as a model of principled parental and institutional resistance, arguing the long-term stand outweighed a lost championship.[2]

## West Virginia middle school protest (2024)

In April 2024 five West Virginia middle schoolers who protested a transgender athlete's participation in a track and field competition were barred from future meets, prompting the state attorney general to ask the U.S. Supreme Court to weigh in on transgender student-athlete bans for a second time.[3] The five girls from Lincoln Middle School stepped up to the circle for their turn at the April 18 shot put event and refused to throw; the event was won by [[becky-pepper-jackson|Becky Pepper-Jackson]], a 13-year-old who takes puberty-blocking medication and estrogen hormone therapy.[3] West Virginia Attorney General [[patrick-morrisey|Patrick Morrisey]] filed a lawsuit against the [[harrison-county-board-of-education|Harrison County Board of Education]] on the dissenting students' behalf.[3] While West Virginia law bans transgender girls from playing on girls' sports teams, a federal appeals court ruled that the law could not lawfully be applied to the eighth-grader.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode inverted the inclusion rationale: for years the public had been told that males assigned at birth could not be prevented from participating in female sports because doing so was mean and exclusive, yet it was treated as acceptable to prevent females from competing in female sports in order to protect the feelings of male competitors.[3] She credited the West Virginia attorney general for backing the girls and said the actual girls without Y chromosomes are the ones who will suffer from their school district's embrace of the latest political fad.[3]

## California defiance of the Trump administration (2025)

In July 2025 California announced it would not ban transgender athletes from competing in K-12 school sports or alter its anti-discrimination policies to exclude them, becoming the second state after Maine to defy the Trump administration on the issue.[4] US Education Secretary [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]] had given the state 10 days from June 27 to rescind any sports prizes awarded to trans athletes and to bar them, after the agency's Office of Civil Rights found that California had violated federal anti-discrimination law by allowing transgender girls to compete against cisgender girls.[4] The state Department of Education's top attorney said the state would not comply with a federal agreement requiring it to rescind trans-inclusionary guidelines and to send personalized apologies to cisgender female athletes who had lost to a transgender opponent.[4] The office of Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] called McMahon's proposed agreement a "political document" that carried no weight and that would force the state to illegally violate its own anti-discrimination laws.[4] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] remarked that California was pressing both states' rights and cities' rights arguments on the same day, linking the dispute to the concurrent [[macarthur-park-immigration-operation|MacArthur Park immigration operation]] confrontation in Los Angeles.[4]
