# Nuclear Proliferation

Nuclear proliferation — the spread of nuclear weapons technology to additional states — is treated in Badlands Media analysis not as a failure of American policy but as one of its covert instruments. Contributor Joe Lange argues that "the CIA was the biggest offender of nuclear proliferation in the world," breaking the very non-proliferation laws it publicly championed.[1]

## Pakistan as the seed

According to the account of former CIA WMD analyst [[rich-barlow|Rich Barlow]], senior U.S. officials in the late 1980s broke U.S. and international non-proliferation protocols to shelter [[pakistan|Pakistan's]] ambitions and even sold it banned WMD technology, while the Pentagon prepared to sell Pakistan jet fighters capable of delivering a bomb.[1] The strategic rationale was Pakistan's value as a springboard into [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] and a route for passing weapons and cash to the [[mujahideen|mujahideen]].[1] Lange concludes that Washington effectively "gave" Pakistan the bomb.[1]

## Onward transfer to sanctioned states

Once nuclear-armed, Pakistan was positioned to share the technology with nations banned from obtaining it — a mechanism by which what Lange calls the [[cia|CIA]]/[[shadow-government|shadow government]] circumvented non-proliferation law.[1] British and American intelligence acknowledge that the weapons programs of Iran, Libya and [[north-korea|North Korea]] were all secretly enabled by Pakistan.[1] All three were simultaneously designated state sponsors of terrorism, which Lange says was no coincidence: painting them as enemies sustained public fear and a perpetual state of war, keeping the taxpayer money spigot flowing.[1]

## Nuclear diplomacy as cover

The article treats the Clinton-era agreement with North Korea and [[barack-obama|Obama's]] 2015 Iran deal as the same playbook run twice: nominally freezing weapons development while in practice freeing the recipient's own cash to buy technology, since sanctions bar such purchases through the banking system and the CIA prefers cash in illegal operations.[1] Trump's answer, in this telling, was to install [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] at the CIA to expose the off-the-books operations, and then to dismantle the North Korean program outright — producing the collapse and closure of the Punggye-ri test site and the historic Trump–[[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] summit.[1] Lange frames the result as "a future no longer threatened by nuclear proliferation and rogue states."[1]
