# 2025 Harvard Federal Funding Freeze

On the night of Monday, April 14, 2025, the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration announced that it was freezing $2.2 billion in federal grants to [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] over concerns about antisemitism on campus.[1]

## Background

The freeze was announced hours after Harvard flatly rejected a set of demands from the administration, placing nearly $9 billion in total federal funding for the university at risk.[1] The proposed conditions for continued funding included eliminating so-called DEI initiatives, screening international students for support of terrorism and antisemitism, reporting foreign students for misconduct, and monitoring ideological diversity in admissions and hiring.[1]

Harvard, led by President [[alan-garber|Alan Garber]], claimed the directive violated the university's constitutional rights and academic freedom, arguing that no government may dictate what private universities teach or whom they admit and hire.[1] The [[general-services-administration|General Services Administration]] and the Department of Education responded that Harvard's statement "reinforces the troubling entitlement mindset that is endemic in our nation's most prestigious universities and colleges — that federal investment does not come with the responsibility to uphold civil rights laws."[1]

The White House had also targeted Columbia, Cornell, and Northwestern universities with threats to their federal funding.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary argued that the administration was not dictating curriculum or hiring but merely setting conditions on the receipt of billions in taxpayer funds, and compared Harvard's posture to the brinkmanship seen in the concurrent tariff negotiations.[1] The commentary characterized elite universities as captured institutions and questioned the propriety of federal subsidies to them.[1]
