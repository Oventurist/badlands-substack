# Useless Eaters

"Useless eaters" is the phrase Badlands Media reporting uses for the population that [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] theorists claim technological growth renders economically superfluous.[1] [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] is presented as the leading contemporary promoter of the view that technological growth itself produces such a class.[1]

## The argument

Harari holds that humans have only two basic abilities, physical and cognitive: when machines replaced human physical abilities people moved to cognitive work, and "if AI becomes better than us in that, there is no third field humans can move to".[1] He describes an emergent "high caste" dominating the new technology that "won't exploit the poor. They just won't need them. And it's much more difficult to rebel against irrelevance than against exploitation."[1]

Asked what should be done with these people, Harari has said the biggest question in economics and politics in coming decades will be "what to do with all these useless people?", that the problem is boredom and the absence of meaning, and that his "best guess at present, is a combination of drugs and computer games".[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] notes that this follows the path of [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]]'s 1962 "Ultimate Revolution" lecture at Berkeley.[1]

## The critique

Ehret argues the thesis holds only if consideration is restricted to information technologies; once productive technologies such as higher-efficiency energy sources are included — as in the writings of [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] — the claim that human irrelevance grows in proportion to technological improvement collapses.[1] He also observes that Harari always excludes himself from the category of hackable, purposeless humans.[1] The anti-Malthusian tradition of [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]] and [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] is offered as the antidote.[1]

## As zombie language

In "The Zombie Apocalypse vs. The Great Awakening" (June 21, 2023), [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] reads the labels "consumers" and "useless eaters" as variations on the [[zombie-apocalypse|zombie]] theme, carrying the implication that ordinary people serve no function other than consumption.[2] Drawing on [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]]'s definition of a zombie as a human being without a will of his or her own — neither dead nor alive, possessing rudimentary motor function and perpetually hungry — he argues this is precisely how the world's death cults regard humanity.[2] He ties the vocabulary to the secularist-materialist denial of transcendent significance to the human spirit, contending that the cults want people to believe their souls do not matter because such people are more likely to give them up.[2]
