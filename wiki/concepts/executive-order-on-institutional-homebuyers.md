# Executive Order on Institutional Homebuyers

In January 2026, [[donald-trump|President Donald J. Trump]] signed an executive order aimed at preventing large institutional investors from purchasing single-family homes, prioritizing individual American families and first-time buyers over Wall Street firms in the housing market.[1]

## Provisions

The order directs federal agencies to issue guidance prohibiting government programs from approving, insuring, guaranteeing, or facilitating sales of single-family homes to institutional investors.[1] It promotes policies giving individual buyers priority access to foreclosed properties, imposes disclosure requirements, and includes anti-circumvention measures.[1]

The Treasury Secretary — [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] — is tasked with reviewing rules on institutional ownership of single-family homes, while the Attorney General and the Chairman of the Federal Trade Commission are to scrutinize acquisitions for anti-competitive effects, with heightened enforcement in the single-family rental sector.[1] The [[department-of-housing-and-urban-development|Department of Housing and Urban Development]] must identify institutional investors participating in federal housing programs through ownership disclosures, and the administration is to prepare legislative proposals permanently codifying restrictions on institutional purchases.[1]

To further reduce borrowing costs, [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] are directed to purchase $200 billion in mortgage-backed securities in order to lower mortgage rates.[1]

## Rationale

The White House framed the actions as essential to restoring the American Dream of homeownership, which it claims was undermined by high inflation and interest rates under the previous administration.[1] According to the accompanying fact sheet, institutional investors have been outbidding families, reducing available supply, driving up prices, and converting neighborhoods into corporate rental portfolios.[1] By reserving single-family homes for owner-occupants, the policies are intended to increase affordability, build generational wealth through homeownership, and foster stable communities.[1]
