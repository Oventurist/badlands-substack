# Outer Space Treaty

The Outer Space Treaty of 1967 is the foundational instrument of international space law, signed by 109 United Nations members.[1] It was drafted and passed as UN resolution 1962 under the leadership of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], who had also called for ending the Cold War by introducing a higher principle of U.S.–Russian cooperation on space science, with the aim of landing both Russian and American astronauts on the Moon.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the treaty, for all its symbolic weight, "has never evolved beyond the vaguest of generalities," and that the [[artemis-accords|Artemis Accords]] as first unveiled in 2019 were an attempt to "put meat onto the bones" of that framework by specifying ten operational principles for lunar activity.[1] Ehret groups the treaty with the 1969 Moon landing and the 1975 Apollo–Soyuz mission as milestone achievements of an era in which the call of a new age of cooperation still resonated despite Cold War geopolitics.[1]
