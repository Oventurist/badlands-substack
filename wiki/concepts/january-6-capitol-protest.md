# January 6 Capitol Protest

The events of January 6, 2021 in Washington, D.C. are treated in Badlands Media commentary as a paradigm case of a public event whose appearance was engineered to differ sharply from its substance.[1]

## Badlands account of the day

Writing in November 2023, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] described roughly a million people arriving in Washington to show support for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and to peacefully protest what they believed to be a stolen election.[1] He wrote that it was quickly discovered that anti-Trump activists were present disguised as Trump supporters — including members of [[antifa|Antifa]] and [[black-lives-matter|BLM]] wearing MAGA hats and T-shirts "fresh out of their plastic bags, creases and all" — causing and encouraging violence, vandalism and entry into the Capitol building.[1] He further alleged that the [[fbi|FBI]] had agents and assets doing the same, and that [[us-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] were opening doors and encouraging protesters to come inside.[1]

## Claimed narrative function

Carlson argued that despite the implausibility of the scene, the general public accepted the framing that Trump supporters had attempted to overthrow the government.[1] He asserted that a majority of Americans now regard January 6 as a political stunt by Trump's enemies coordinated by the FBI and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], but that the operation had already served its purpose: it diverted public attention from claims of a stolen election.[1] Like any big lie, he wrote, the January 6 lie eventually unravelled — and a majority of Americans now also believe there was election interference in 2020 — yet the damage was already done.[1] These characterisations reflect the author's position and the [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud claims]] rather than adjudicated findings.[1]

Carlson used the episode as the precedent for his suspicion that other filmed confrontations, such as pro-Hamas and pro-Palestinian street protests, might include paid or planted participants holding inflammatory signs in order to discredit the wider crowd.[1]

## The 2023 "staged show" reading

The Badlands essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI," published in March 2023, argues that the release of the J6 videos on [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s program produced evidence of what most of its readers already believed — that government agencies possibly coordinated the whole thing.[2] It characterises the day as a staged show intended to make [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his supporters seem like violent nut jobs, a narrative easily absorbed by the Left because it is what they had been conditioned to believe and wanted to believe.[2]

The footage showing [[jacob-chansley|Jacob "Jake Angeli" Chansley]], the QAnon Shaman, being escorted through the Capitol with a police escort is described as impossible to ignore or spin, and the [[january-6-committee|J6 committee]] as able only to lie and claim it had not watched the videos — which the author says makes its members look worse.[2] [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]] should, in the essay's view, be exposed as a CIA/FBI stooge.[2]

The article also recalls early videos and pictures of Azov soldiers — described as Ukrainian Nazis — taking part in the demonstration, as well as members of the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]], and asks what the odds are that [[antifa|Antifa]], [[black-lives-matter|BLM]], [[azov-battalion|Azov]] and the CCP, all accused of being foot soldiers of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] cabal, happened to be present, and why there is no longer any talk of Azov and the CCP at J6.[2]

On Q and Trump's foreknowledge, the essay argues it makes sense that the Q Team stopped posting exactly four weeks before January 6 because they probably knew the cabal's plan, and states that Trump knew what was going to happen — which is why he asked for the [[national-guard|National Guard]] to be brought in and delayed his speech to keep more people away from the Capitol.[2] After three years of teaching Anons how to fish, the Q Team judged itself too big a target and distraction, and stepped back.[2] The essay concludes that the cabal specifically tried to make Anons appear violent and crazy, which tells the author that Anons are a huge threat to their plan, their power and their existence.[2] See also [[intelligence-patsy|intelligence patsy]].[2]

## Anticipation in the TIP war games

The [[transition-integrity-project|Transition Integrity Project]]'s 2020 election exercises predicted that, in the event of a Trump victory followed by Biden-supporter protests, the resulting violence would actually be caused by Trump supporters disguised as Biden supporters.[3] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] observes that this closely resembles what occurred on January 6, though in his reading the roles were reversed: Biden's people disguised as Trump supporters.[3]

## Sund's account and the fake insurrection framing (2023)

In an August 2023 essay, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s reporting had led 65% of likely US voters — roughly two-thirds, per [[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] — to believe the feds provoked the Capitol riot.[4] Drawing on Carlson's interview with former Capitol Police Chief [[steven-sund|Steven Sund]], she described Sund as having been set up by members of the [[uniparty|uniparty]] establishment to take the fall for the "intelligence failure" that allowed the Capitol to be overrun.[4] Sund said multiple agencies withheld intelligence in the days beforehand, that the briefing and sharing processes differed from those used for the MAGA rallies of November and December 2020 and from every other major First Amendment protest in his thirty years of DC law enforcement, and that although [[government-accountability-office|GAO]] and Senate reports described high threat levels he never received that intelligence.[4] On the day itself the chain of command denied him support for 71 minutes while the Capitol and his officers were overwhelmed.[4]

Ashe contrasted Sund's full disclosure of phone records with [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s production of only an edited documentary by her daughter [[alexandra-pelosi|Alexandra Pelosi]], and cited Sund's claim that [[bennie-thompson|Bennie Thompson]] declared Pelosi and her records "off limits" to the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]].[4] She concluded that January 6 was not an insurrection by Trump supporters but the final step of traitors in government to solidify their November 3 coup.[4]
