# French Revolution

The French Revolution is the late-eighteenth-century upheaval that the conspiracy literature surveyed by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] repeatedly links to the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]].[1] Among the sensational claims attached to the Order is that it managed both the French and the [[american-revolution|American]] revolutions behind the scenes.[1]

The most dramatic origin story of the Order's discovery is tied to the Revolution: an Illuminati courier named Johann Jakob Lanz, a former Catholic priest, was said to have been killed by lightning in 1785 while riding through Regensburg, whereupon police examining his saddle bag discovered the existence of the Order and detailed plans for the coming French Revolution — a story the Masonic lodges have refuted.[1]

[[john-robison|John Robison]] devoted the final chapter of *Proofs of a Conspiracy* to the Order's role in the Revolution, noting that cosmo-political and skeptical opinions and sentiments found full expression in the political occurrences in France, and delving into the intrigues of [[count-mirabeau|Count Honoré Gabriel Riqueti Mirabeau]].[1] The [[jacobins|Jacobins]], who executed the Terror, are described as having an undisputed relationship with the Illuminati.[1]

## Described as a beta test

In Badlands Media's account of engineered uprisings, the French Revolution is characterized as "a successful beta test" of the [[color-revolution|color revolution]] technique developed by European power brokers and planned by the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]] to overthrow the established order.[2] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] credited the research of [[marvin-s-antelman|Marvin S. Antelman]] with documenting the correspondence behind the claim, and used the episode to argue that populations who regain power through such campaigns tend to lose it again to elites in short order.[2]
