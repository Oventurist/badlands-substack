# Israeli Troop Withdrawal from Gaza (January 2024)

On Monday, January 1, 2024, the Israeli military announced it would begin withdrawing several thousand troops from the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]], at least temporarily — the most significant publicly announced reduction since the war with [[hamas|Hamas]] began.[1]

## Rationale

The military cited a growing toll on the Israeli economy after nearly three months of wartime mobilization with little end in sight to the fighting.[1] [[israel|Israel]] had been considering scaling back its operations, and the United States had been prodding it to do so more quickly as the death toll and privation in Gaza rose.[1]

## Humanitarian situation

More than 20,000 people had been killed in Gaza since the beginning of the war, most of them civilians, according to local health authorities, primarily in Israeli bombing.[1] With aid delivery restricted and aid workers unable to move safely within the territory, half of Gaza's roughly 2.2 million people were at risk of starvation according to the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[1]

## Israeli framing

Rear Admiral [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]], the Israeli military spokesman, emphasized that demobilizing some soldiers did not suggest any compromise of Israel's intention to continue fighting until it destroys Hamas, and that fighting across Gaza remained intense.[1] Hagari, who had said he expected "warfare throughout this year," indicated some troops would be recalled to service during 2024.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the drawdown as part of a "strange and even contradictory pattern" in the Middle East, in which Israel signalled reduced kinetic deployments in Gaza while maintaining a rock-solid public stance of war throughout 2024 and beyond, even as escalation proceeded in the Red Sea.[1] He argued the various moves — including [[iran|Iran]]'s warship deployment and the rotation of the [[uss-gerald-r-ford|USS Ford]] carrier home — might be explained by logistics and strategy, but that observers should track what the players do rather than only what they say, adding that he was "not convinced these conflicts are long for this world."[1]
