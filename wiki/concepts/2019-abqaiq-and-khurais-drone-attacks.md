# 2019 Abqaiq and Khurais Drone Attacks

On September 14, 2019, the war in [[yemen|Yemen]] reached into [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] with drone strikes on two major Saudi oil refineries and a claim of responsibility from Yemen's Houthi rebel faction.[1]

A Houthi spokesman, Brigadier General Yahya Sare'e, said in a statement broadcast by Al-Masirah, the faction's news organisation, that the group's forces "carried out a massive offensive operation of ten drones targeting Abqaiq and Khurais refineries."[1] Sare'e described it as one of the largest operations the [[houthis|Houthis]] had mounted in Saudi Arabia, and it reached deeper than before into the kingdom, some 500 miles from Yemeni territory.[1]

## Context in the Badlands account

Badlands Media presents the refinery attack as the first of two major events that would trigger a massive change in the Middle East, the second being the December 31, 2019 attack on the United States embassy in Baghdad and the subsequent killing of [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]].[1] The article notes that the attack was carried out by Iran-backed Houthi rebels and situates it within a proxy war between Saudi Arabia under Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and [[iran|Iran]], in which Yemeni rebels were supplied by Iran and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1]

The article links this pattern to an earlier episode: within hours of Lebanese Prime Minister [[saad-hariri|Saad al-Hariri]]'s resignation in November 2017, Saudi Arabia said it had intercepted a ballistic missile fired from Yemen, destroyed over Riyadh with fragments landing near King Khalid International Airport.[1] Badlands argues the missile capability came from Hezbollah and Iran rather than Yemen itself, framing the Houthis as puppets and Tehran as the puppet master.[1] The eventual China-brokered truce between Saudi Arabia and Iran in March 2023 included a commitment by Iran to stop further attacks on Saudi Arabia and curtail support for militant groups targeting the kingdom.[1]
