# Humiliation Ritual

"Humiliation ritual" is a term used in independent and dissident media to describe a public act or policy understood as deliberately degrading to a population, imposed by governing or globalist institutions in order to demonstrate dominance and induce submission.[1]

## Application to Britain

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] applied the term to the United Kingdom in July 2026, writing that for the British people "the humiliation ritual has reached a fever pitch."[1] The judgment was rendered amid the ongoing revelation of [[grooming-gangs|grooming gangs]] and what the author called the government-facilitated destruction of a long and storied national history.[1]

The proximate trigger was outgoing Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]]'s Pride remarks at Downing Street, in which he said he was "really proud" that the United Kingdom has "the gayest parliament… anywhere in the world," and pledged continued defence of LGBT rights alongside a proposed ban on conversion practices.[1] The Brief's headline framed the episode as "a globalist humiliation ritual," pairing it with the socialist insurgency inside the American Democratic Party.[1]

## Memetic dimension

The commentary held that such episodes are potent precisely because they are highly [[memetics|memetic]]: the internet amplifies them, securing the leader's legacy in caricature, and the resulting meme of Britain being intentionally made into a "fake and gay mecca" communicates a core truth its audience recognises regardless of literal accuracy.[1]

## Head shaving as humiliation ritual (January 2026)

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] extended the concept to a domestic protest phenomenon in January 2026, in which women participating in the anti-ICE protest wave publicly shaved their heads, filmed themselves doing so, and framed the act as female empowerment.[2] She argued that head shaving is inherently a humiliation ritual — as practiced in fraternities, boot camps, and locker rooms — and therefore an incoherent vehicle for empowerment claims.[2]

Ashe explicitly distinguished intent from outcome, noting that the point of such a ritual is "to subjugate and mock and scorn and humiliate," and citing the Crown of Thorns as a case in which the humiliating intention failed to achieve its intended outcome.[2] She invoked the shearing of Aslan in *The Chronicles of Narnia* as a fictional parallel, and treated the exception as proving the rule that the ritual's purpose is subjugation.[2]
