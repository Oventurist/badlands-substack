# White Fragility

*White Fragility* is a book by [[robin-diangelo|Robin DiAngelo]] that became a central text of American corporate and institutional antiracism programming following the death of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] in 2020.[1] Its author remained a relatively obscure professor until Floyd's death propelled her and the book to national prominence.[1]

## Institutional adoption

Reporting cited in the Badlands News Brief described DiAngelo as having become "a staple of teacher trainings, corporate affinity groups, fundraisers, and antiracist book clubs."[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] corroborated this from personal experience, recounting that in the summer of 2020 "Racial Justice Task Force" affinity groups spread through her firm and her clients' companies and that *White Fragility* was made required reading; declining to read it was itself treated as proof of fragility and an unwillingness to confront one's own whiteness.[1]

## Plagiarism controversy

In August 2024 a complaint filed with the University of Washington alleged 20 instances of plagiarism in DiAngelo's 2004 doctoral thesis, *Whiteness in Racial Dialogue: A Discourse Analysis*, including material allegedly taken from minority scholars such as Thomas Nakayama and Stacey Lee.[1] The allegations were read in Badlands commentary as undercutting the moral authority of a work that had positioned itself as an instrument of racial justice, with Ashe describing DiAngelo as "one of the key architects of weaponized empathy as a means for social change."[1]
