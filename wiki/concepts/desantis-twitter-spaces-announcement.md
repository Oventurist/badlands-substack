# DeSantis Twitter Spaces Presidential Announcement

The DeSantis Twitter Spaces presidential announcement was the May 2023 launch event at which Florida governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] declared his candidacy for President of the United States in a live audio session on [[twitter|Twitter]] Spaces alongside [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1] The event became better known for its technical failures than for its content.[1]

## Reception

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on May 30, 2023, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] described the announcement as "a bit of a flop," judging that "technology wise, it was a mess, content wise, it was boring."[1] Carlson noted that a clever and funny satirical video mocking the spectacle circulated widely afterwards, featuring voices in the likeness of Musk, [[george-soros|George Soros]], [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], Cheney, Hitler, Satan, the [[fbi|FBI]], DeSantis himself and even [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## The foreknowledge question

Carlson's principal observation about the event was chronological. A video of that production caliber, he reasoned, would require a fair amount of planning, yet it "just popped up right after the Twitter Spaces debacle."[1] He therefore wondered aloud whether the video had been made before the event occurred, and whether it was known ahead of time that the launch would fail — which, he added, could only be the case if Twitter were "in on it."[1] Carlson was explicit that he was not claiming to know the answer and was "simply asking the question."[1]

## Interpretation as a political trap

In the same essay Carlson treated the announcement as the opening move of what he called a sting: because no conservative figure could remain neutral between DeSantis and Trump, the candidacy would force [[rino|RINOs]], [[neoconservatives|NeoCons]] and [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] to declare an allegiance and thereby reveal who funds and controls them.[1] He described the run as "a key component to setting the table for Trump's Presidency" and "a gift to Trump and the Patriots," rather than a threat to Trump or a sign that his support was waning.[1] Carlson left open whether the affair was [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] — a false feud — or a genuine betrayal, arguing that the practical outcome would be the same either way.[1]
