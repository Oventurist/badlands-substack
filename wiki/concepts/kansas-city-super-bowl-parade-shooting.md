# Kansas City Super Bowl Parade Shooting

The **Kansas City Super Bowl parade shooting** was a mass shooting that occurred on Wednesday, February 14, 2024, at the end of the parade celebrating the Kansas City Chiefs' Super Bowl victory.[1]

## The shooting

At least 22 people were hit by gunfire, including eight children, as the celebration concluded, sending terrified fans running for cover in what became yet another high-profile public event marred by gun violence.[1] One of the victims — a mother of two identified by her radio station as a DJ — was killed.[1]

## Investigation

Kansas City Police Chief [[stacey-graves|Stacey Graves]] said three people had been detained as part of the investigation.[1] She said she had heard that fans may have been involved in tackling a suspect but could not immediately confirm that.[1] Police did not immediately release details about the people detained or about a possible motive.[1] Graves said firearms had been recovered but did not specify what kind.[1]

"I'm angry at what happened today. The people who came to this celebration should expect a safe environment," Graves said.[1]

The incident was carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of February 15, 2024, without accompanying editorial commentary.[1]

## False flag interpretation

Badlands' "[[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]]" Chapter 6, published the following day, treated the parade shooting as an example of the pattern Q described in the [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] drops: that a mass shooting followed by an immediate gun-control push tends to occur whenever damaging news breaks against Democrats.[2] The author listed the preceding week's events as the trigger — the report of Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]] highlighting [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s mental decline, the House vote to impeach DHS Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]], and Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s rejection of the Ukraine spending bill — calling them a combination blow to the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[2]

The chapter argued the choice of venue was itself suspicious given what it called the propaganda apparatus built around the relationship between [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]] and Chiefs player [[travis-kelce|Travis Kelce]], noting that Kelce had posted in favor of gun control three days before the shooting.[2] It also observed that the shooting fell on the anniversary of the Parkland school shooting and was followed by the same immediate calls for gun control.[2]
