# Tax-Free Tips Proposal

The tax-free tips proposal refers to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s June 10, 2024 public pledge that, as president, he would work to exempt gratuities from federal taxation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) In Badlands Media commentary the proposal is read not as retail campaign pandering but as the opening move of a broader campaign against the American taxation and monetary system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The escalation thesis

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that making tips tax-free "could be viewed as a first step towards abolishing income tax," and that after income tax the money supply system itself would be the next target.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Because the [[deep-state-cabal|Deep State Cabal]] depends on private bankers controlling the money supply, Carlson contends that a credible move in this direction represents an existential threat to it — and therefore a plausible trigger for the desperation displayed in the summer of 2024.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He characterizes the pledge as "a shot across the bow—a warning to the Deep State that their days are numbered."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Argument against taxing tips

Carlson holds that taxing tips is conceptually incoherent: a tip is not income but "a way people are shown appreciation for meeting or exceeding customer's expectations."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He notes that some industries have converted tips into de facto wages, with employers paying employees little or nothing and shifting the burden of compensation onto customers, so that employees who are not tipped go substantially underpaid.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Implementation path

Carlson does not expect federal income tax to be revoked on day one of a second Trump term — January 20, 2025 — arguing that an abrupt repeal would panic the financial system and leave legitimate programs unfunded.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Instead he sketches a sequence: make tips tax-free first, then reduce overall rates, and eventually eliminate the federal income tax while funding government through tariffs and corporate taxes, with government jobs and programs reduced or eliminated over time.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The stated corollary is "less government, less government control."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## As a signal to the Deep State

In a follow-up article, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] restated the thesis in more explicit terms: he believes [[donald-trump|Trump]] hinted to the [[deep-state|Deep State]] that he intended to tear down the walls of the financial system to its foundation upon returning to the White House, and that he delivered this message precisely by starting to talk about tax-free tips — a signal so subtle, Carlson conceded, that it likely did not even pique the interest of many readers.[2]

The causal chain is compressed there into a single line: tax-free tips lead to a capped income tax, which leads to the abolishment of [[federal-income-tax|income tax]], which leads to the destruction of the financial system as we know it.[2] On this reading the turbulence of mid-2024 had nothing to do with [[joe-biden|Biden]] and the Democrats and everything to do with Trump informing the Deep State that its control of the American financial system was nearly at an end.[2] Carlson adds that Trump left the financial system intact during his first presidency because the timing was not yet right for what he plans to do starting in 2025.[2]

## 2024 campaign convergence and expert criticism

By August 2024 the proposal had become the hottest tax topic of the presidential cycle, with Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] embracing the elimination of taxes on tips for service and hospitality workers over a weekend in August, roughly two months after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] first proposed it in June.[3] Both campaigns pitched the idea with an eye toward Nevada, a key state teeming with tipped workers, especially in Las Vegas.[3] The idea caught fire with those workers and the union representing many of them.[3]

Economists on both the left and right warned of unintended consequences: depending on structure, the cut could add to an already unsustainable federal deficit, reduce revenue for Social Security and Medicare, and open a loophole allowing high-end earners such as financiers to recategorize income and shield it from taxation.[3] Howard Gleckman, a senior fellow at the nonpartisan Tax Policy Center, told Politico it was "one bad idea layered on top of another," adding that "there are not a lot of tax upsides."[3]
