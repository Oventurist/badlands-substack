# The Crisis of Democracy

*The Crisis of Democracy* is a 1975 report issued by the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] as part of its early series of Task Force Reports, collectively known as "The Triangle Papers."[1]

## Argument

The report was written by [[samuel-p-huntington|Samuel P. Huntington]], who claimed that what America really needed was "a greater degree of moderation in democracy."[1] Huntington argued that democratic institutions were incapable of taking prompt action against crises, using the Three Mile Island nuclear accident as an illustration, and the paper suggested that leaders with "expertise, seniority, experience, and special talents" were needed to "override the claims of democracy."[1]

## Interpretation and legacy

Badlands Media reads the paper as an effort to popularize the idea of globalists circumventing the Constitution, and treats it as the clearest textual evidence that the Trilateral Commission is more than a social club.[1] It notes that within five years the report's logic had produced concrete institutional results: Huntington went on to coordinate security planning for [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]]'s National Security Council, where he prepared Presidential Review Memorandum 32, the document that led to the 1979 executive order creating the [[fema|Federal Emergency Management Agency]].[1]
