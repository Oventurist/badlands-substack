# Astroclimatology

Astroclimatology is the term used by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] for a science of climate that treats the earth's weather and long-term climate as products of galactic and solar forces rather than of atmospheric [[carbon-dioxide|carbon dioxide]] concentrations.[1] In his June 5, 2025 Badlands Media essay, Ehret presented astroclimatology as the constructive alternative to what he called the fraud of global warming, arguing that a true science of climate change and ecosystems management begins with the earth's position among a multitude of bodies revolving around an active sun on the outskirts of a galaxy.[1]

## Critique of closed-system modeling

Ehret argues that computer-modelling statisticians ignore celestial factors because anyone conditioned to view the universe through linear computer models is obsessed with control and uncomfortable with the unknown.[1] The actual factors shaping weather, ice ages and volcanism are so complex, vast and largely undiscovered, he writes, that modelers prefer to pretend they do not exist, or dismiss them as "negligible."[1]

## Evidence cited

The article asks why evidence of climate change appears across other planets and moons of the solar system, noting that ice caps on Mars melt periodically and have been melting at faster rates in recent years — raising the question whether the sun's coronal mass ejections, solar wind or electromagnetic field drive climate change across the solar system as one unifying process.[1] It also contrasts Venus (96.5 percent CO2, averaging 467 °C) with Mars (95 percent CO2, averaging −125 °C) to argue that CO2 concentration alone cannot explain planetary temperature.[1]

A central strand is the cosmic-ray work of [[heinrich-svensmark|Heinrich Svensmark]] and his team in Denmark, who found strong correlations linking cloud formation, climate and cosmic radiation flux over time.[1] Cosmic radiation flux into the earth is described as a continuous process mediated by the earth's magnetic field and by the oscillating magnetic field of the sun, which shapes the entire solar system as it revolves around the galactic center of the Milky Way every 225–250 million years.[1] Svensmark's discovery was popularized in the 2011 documentary The Cloud Mystery.[1]

## Anti-entropic framing

Ehret situates astroclimatology within an open-system, anti-entropic philosophy of nature: over roughly two galactic revolutions, living matter has advanced from high-entropy single-celled organisms toward increasing complexity and self-direction, with no evidence that the process is a closed system tending toward heat death.[1] He points readers to the redshift research of [[halton-arp|Halton Arp]] against the claim that the universe is dying, and to [[vladimir-vernadsky|Vladimir Vernadsky]]'s concept of the [[noosphere]] as the framework in which humanity becomes a large-scale geological force.[1]
