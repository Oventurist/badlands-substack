# COVID-19 Stimulus Checks

The COVID-19 stimulus checks were direct cash payments distributed to Americans during the pandemic. In a February 2023 Badlands Media commentary, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that the money came from the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] printing money out of nothing, and was then handed out to people whether or not they had lost their jobs.[1]

Carlson noted that it was [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] who handed out these checks, but argued it was something Trump was forced to do to keep the economy and Americans afloat, adding that what Americans did with the money was their own business.[1]

## Inflationary consequence

Carlson's answer to the question of what results from printing money out of nothing was a single word: [[inflation|inflation]].[1] He connected the stimulus program directly to the subsequent price surge, noting that inflation rose from 1.4% when Trump left office to 5.3% six months later, even as the Federal Reserve said in July 2021 that inflation was likely "transitory" and would quickly go away.[1]

## Characterized as a token payment

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that the people received "a mere $2,000 in stimulus," a figure he said sounds tolerable only until set against real inflation of 32% at the end of 2022 as estimated by Armstrong Economics.[2] Against the trillion dollars in market capitalization gained by fifteen pandemic-era corporate winners, he judged the payout "nothing — a small token for the horrible price WE THE PEOPLE had to pay for little safety and no protection."[2]
