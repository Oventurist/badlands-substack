# M1 Abrams

The M1 Abrams is the United States Army's main battle tank, first deployed in 1980 and classified as a third-generation design.[1] Built by [[general-dynamics|General Dynamics]] at a unit cost of roughly $10 million, it saw extensive use in Iraq against Saddam Hussein's forces and against later insurgencies.[1]

## Transfer to Ukraine and battlefield performance

The United States sent 31 M1A1 Abrams to Ukraine in January 2023, a donation announced by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as evidence of "enduring and unflagging commitment to Ukraine."[1] In May 2024 CNN journalists became the first reporters to see the tanks in Ukraine, observing roughly six vehicles concealed in foliage at a location in the country's east.[1]

Ukrainian crews trained in Germany described serious shortcomings. One crew member with the callsign Joker said the armor "is not sufficient for this moment" and "doesn't protect the crew," adding that "today this is the war of drones," so tanks are targeted as soon as they roll out.[1] A colleague with the callsign Dnipro called the Abrams the "number one target," saying that "without defense, the crew doesn't survive at the battlefield."[1] Ukraine had announced in April 2024 that it was pulling its Abrams off the battlefield to avoid losing them.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the episode as a possible [[devolution|Devolution]] data point, arguing that the transfer amounted to disposing of surplus armor: in June 2022 the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had already commissioned General Dynamics to build the first new American tank in over forty years, later designated the [[m10-booker|M10 Booker]], and in September 2023 scrapped its Abrams upgrade plan in favor of an all-new prototype.[1]

He placed the Abrams in a longer history of armored warfare: the Soviet T-34 as the defining first-generation tank, credited with defeating the Germans at the Battle of Kursk — described as the largest tank battle in history with over 2 million combatants and 6,000 tanks; the German Tiger I as the most feared but over-engineered and produced in only about 1,300 units against more than 80,000 Soviet T-34s; and the American M4 Sherman as a vehicle GIs regarded as a "death trap."[1] He credited Heinz Guderian and Erwin Rommel with pioneering blitzkrieg maneuver doctrine while arguing that the Russians mastered combined-arms operations.[1] In his assessment, Ukrainian crews using the Abrams as stationary "pillboxes" nullified the tank's chief advantage — speed.[1]
