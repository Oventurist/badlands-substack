# Law of Armed Conflict

The Law of Armed Conflict (LOAC) is the body of international law governing the conduct of hostilities, commonly summarized in four basic principles.[1] The Badlands Media essay "Our 'Greatest Ally'" invokes LOAC to argue that the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government's military campaign in [[gaza|Gaza]] "completely and utterly violated" all four.[1]

## The four principles

**Distinction.** Additional Protocol I, Article 48 requires parties to a conflict to distinguish at all times between the civilian population and combatants, and between civilian objects and military objectives, directing operations only against military objectives.[1] The only legitimate object of attack is military personnel or property; civilians may be incidentally harmed but must not be the object of an attack.[1]

**Proportionality.** As stated in U.S. Army Field Manual FM 27-10, loss of life and damage to property incidental to attacks must not be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage expected.[1] The principle carries an obligation to consider all targeting options — verification of the target, timing, choice of weapons, and warnings or evacuations for civilians.[1]

**Military necessity.** Injury done to an enemy is excusable only so far as it is absolutely necessary; everything beyond that is criminal.[1] The principle prohibits torture to exact confessions, gratuitous wounding outside of combat, and any act of hostility that makes the return to peace unnecessarily difficult, as framed in the Liber Code.[1]

**Unnecessary suffering.** Additional Protocol I, Article 35.2 prohibits the employment of weapons, projectiles, materials and methods of warfare of a nature to cause superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering.[1]

The first two principles protect non-combatants; the latter two protect combatants.[1]

## Application in the article

DeLarme contrasts LOAC's requirements with reporting that Israel's stated right to protect itself has extended to rendering the Gaza Strip unlivable, arguing that razing Gaza with U.S. weaponry rather than conducting targeted operations against [[hamas|Hamas]] leadership fails the tests of distinction, proportionality and necessity.[1]
