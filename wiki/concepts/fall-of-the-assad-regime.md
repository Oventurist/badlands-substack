# Fall of the Assad Regime

The **fall of the Assad regime** refers to the collapse of the Syrian government of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in early December 2024, when opposition forces entered [[damascus|Damascus]] after a rapid offensive and declared the president deposed, ending more than fifty years of Assad family rule and terminating what commentators described as the last secular and Baathist state in the Middle East.[1]

## Collapse of the capital

Reporting compiled at the time described a situation that was fluid and unclear, with unverified accounts placing Assad and senior officials in Dubai, in Latakia on the Syrian coast, or in transit. What was not in dispute was that jihadist fighters had entered the heart of the capital.[1] Journalist Danny Makki, present in Damascus, observed that Syrian military and security personnel around the city simply fled, changed into civilian clothes and escaped, and that there was "no battle for Damascus" — the city was abandoned rather than contested.[1] [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]] verified footage showing soldiers leaving the city amid the sound of gunfire, taken moments before opposition forces claimed entry, and reported rebel sources saying government forces had withdrawn from the Defence Ministry headquarters. State television channels passed into the hands of anti-Assad factions.[1]

## Assad's asylum in Russia

Senior Russian diplomat [[mikhail-ulyanov|Mikhail Ulyanov]] said early on the Monday following the collapse that Assad and his family were in Moscow, appearing to confirm earlier media reports that the former Syrian president had been granted asylum.[1] Russian news agencies cited diplomatic sources describing the family's arrival and asylum "on humanitarian grounds."[1] The [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]] stated that Assad had agreed to step down after back-channel talks with unspecified armed groups, leaving the country while instructing officials to conduct "a peaceful transfer of power."[1] Ulyanov framed the episode as evidence that "Russia does not betray its friends in difficult situations… unlike the US."[1]

## Forces involved

Over the weekend the Syrian Army stood down as [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS) jihadists and US-armed [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]] militants advanced on and took the capital, after which the anti-Assad forces declared Assad deposed and claimed control of the government.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentators treated the official chronology skeptically. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the reporting was largely semantic: Assad had reportedly been in Moscow meeting [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] the previous week, and the notion that he returned to a collapsing capital in an active warzone only to immediately flee back to Moscow strained credulity — suggesting Assad had never left Russia and that the "flight" narrative was dramatic effect.[1]

Three motives were offered for the operation. The first was energy: around 2009 two competing pipeline proposals from the Persian Gulf would have supplied Europe with natural gas in direct challenge to Russia's Nord Stream, one originating in Iran and terminating in Lebanon for LNG shipment, the other running from Qatar through Turkey into Europe — both requiring transit through Syria, and both reportedly rejected by Assad.[1] The second was the [[five-seas-vision|Five Seas Vision]], Assad's 2005 proposal for a rail network linking the Mediterranean, Red, Black and Caspian seas and the Persian Gulf with Aleppo at its heart, a plan that Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and China had reportedly approached Assad about reviving under the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and [[saudi-vision-2030|Saudi Vision 2030]].[1] The third was [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]], with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] publicly boasting of ordering the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to annex land in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]], echoing Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s October claim that Damascus was part of Israel on scriptural grounds.[1]

Summarised, the argument was that the collapse was "about sticking it to Putin, stifling prosperity, and fulfilling Talmudic prophecy," and that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] would have to answer kinetically or forfeit the credibility of its multipolar project.[1]
