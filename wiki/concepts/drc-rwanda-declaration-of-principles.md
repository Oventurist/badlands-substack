# DRC–Rwanda Declaration of Principles

The DRC–Rwanda Declaration of Principles is an agreement signed on April 25, 2025 in Washington, D.C. between the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] and [[rwanda|Rwanda]], brokered by the United States to address decades of conflict in eastern DRC.[1]

## Signing and participants

Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] hosted the signing ceremony.[1] Foreign Ministers Thérèse Kayikwamba Wagner of the DRC and Olivier Nduhungirehe of Rwanda signed the document, committing their governments to peace, sovereignty, and economic cooperation.[1] Senior Adviser [[massad-boulos|Massad Boulos]] and Deputy Secretary Christopher Landau attended, underscoring the level of U.S. involvement.[1]

## Terms

The declaration mandated a draft peace deal by May 2, 2025 and pledged both nations to halt support for armed groups such as [[m23|M23]].[1] It placed emphasis on securing mineral supply chains critical to global technology industries, and aimed to facilitate the return of 7.3 million displaced people.[1]

## Context and commentary

The agreement was signed against a backdrop of escalating fighting in eastern DRC, where M23 had seized substantial territory including the city of Goma in January 2025.[1] Days before the ceremony, the Congolese government banned the party of former president [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]] and ordered his assets seized over alleged support for the rebels.[1]

Badlands commentary connected the declaration to a longer arc beginning with Executive Order 13818 of December 20, 2017, which froze the assets of [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]], and speculated that accountability might eventually extend to former Mossad director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]].[1]
