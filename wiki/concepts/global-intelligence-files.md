# Global Intelligence Files

The Global Intelligence Files are a cache of internal emails from the private intelligence firm Stratfor that [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] began publishing in 2012.[1] The release comprised thousands of messages dated from 2004 to 2011 and exposed the internal correspondence, sourcing practices and client relationships of a firm often described as a "shadow CIA."[1]

## Role in the Pizzagate controversy

The Global Intelligence Files later became source material for the [[pizzagate|Pizzagate]] controversy, and the establishment media subsequently used the Pizzagate label to dismiss a wide range of unrelated claims as "wild QANON Pizzagate conspiracy theories."[1] Commentators in the Truth Community have argued that despite what they characterise as an enormous, arguably billion-dollar narrative-management effort, many people continue to believe there is some truth to the material associated with the now-pejorative term.[1]

One frequently cited message from the cache is a May 14, 2009 email from Stratfor's Fred Burton to Don Kuykendall, copying Aaric Eisenstein, Darryl O'Connor and Susan Copeland, under the subject line "RE: Get ready for 'Chicago Hot Dog Friday'."[1] In it Burton wrote that he thought [[barack-obama|Obama]] "spent about $65,000 of the tax-payers money flying in pizza/dogs from Chicago for a private party at the White House not long ago," and asked whether the same "channels" were being used.[1] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] highlighted the email's odd specifics — the sum involved and the reference to "channels" — while acknowledging that nothing has been proven and that the matter retains "enough plausible deniability to keep the denial train rolling into the next century."[1]
