# Skid Row Voter Fraud Investigation

The Skid Row voter fraud investigation is a federal probe into alleged paid voter registration and ballot-related activity among homeless residents of the Skid Row district of downtown Los Angeles, connected to the city's [[2026-los-angeles-mayoral-election|2026 mayoral election]] and the concurrent gubernatorial primary races.[1]

## Federal operation

On a Thursday in mid-June 2026, federal agents conducted an operation on Skid Row as part of the investigation.[1] Roughly twenty agents, including personnel from Homeland Security Investigations, moved through the area shortly before noon, interviewing dozens of homeless residents and taking notes.[1] Witnesses described the agents as dressed in casual clothing, fanning out across the streets and speaking with locals who appeared to direct them toward specific areas and individuals.[1] [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] amplified the operation publicly and, in the first comment on his post, shared the Department of Justice press release from May 2026.[1]

## Allegations

The probe followed allegations that surfaced after Election Day, including videos in which Skid Row residents claimed they had been paid to participate in voting-related activities.[1] Some individuals alleged they received cash payments to sign registration forms, forge signatures, and complete voter information ahead of the mayoral race and primary elections.[1] In the recordings, residents said they were encouraged to support incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and Councilmember [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]]; one account stated that challenger [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] was not among the candidates promoted to voters.[1]

## Registration records tied to shelters

The investigation coincided with reporting that examined voter registration records linked to homeless shelters and social service organizations.[1] A cited review found more than 7,600 registered voters associated with such addresses, including approximately 1,160 registrations connected to the [[midnight-mission|Midnight Mission]] in Skid Row.[1] A separate homeless services drop-in center in Venice was reported to have 185 registered voters tied to its address; that facility had previously received a $600,000 taxpayer-funded grant while Raman chaired the Los Angeles City Council's Housing and Homelessness Committee.[1]

## Related enforcement and antecedents

The March 2026 undercover footage published by [[okeefe-media-group|O'Keefe Media Group]] documented an LA registration drive for homeless people involving money exchanged and fake names being registered.[1] On May 18, 2026, the Department of Justice announced the indictment of a California woman — described in the release as a longtime signature collector for ballot initiatives — for paying individuals, including homeless people in Skid Row, to register to vote; Badlands identified her as [[brenda-lee-brown-armstrong|Brenda Lee Brown Armstrong]] of Marina del Rey, 64, also known as "Anika."[1]

Federal officials separately issued a subpoena to [[america-votes|America Votes]], a major nonprofit voter mobilization group, as part of a widening investigation tied to the [[ohio-organizing-collaborative|Ohio Organizing Collaborative]]; a former director and board member described the scope as extensive and wide-ranging.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] laid out the timeline — the May 18 indictment, the June 2 primary, the June 7 announcement that Spencer Pratt had "lost" the jungle primary, and [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]]'s June 8 re-posting of his March video — and asked whether the Los Angeles mayoral election had itself been a sting operation and whether Armstrong had cooperated with investigators.[1] The commentary warned readers to note "fraud apologists" arguing that the scheme was "not enough to overturn the outcome," characterizing them as the same voices who previously denied fraud was occurring at all.[1]
