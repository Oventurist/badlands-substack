# Nassau County Mask Ban

The Nassau County mask ban is a 2024 local law in the suburban Long Island county of Nassau, New York, banning the wearing of masks in public spaces on the stated grounds that it would help fight crime during protests.[1]

## Provisions

The measure passed in the first week of August 2024 and was headed to Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman's desk for signature.[1] It makes wearing a mask and concealing one's identity a misdemeanor punishable by up to a year in jail and a $1,000 fine.[1] The law exempts people wearing face coverings for religious or health reasons, but states that masks worn otherwise are "often used as a predicate to harassing, menacing or criminal behavior."[1] Under the legislation, local law enforcement may demand that people remove their masks where there is a "reasonable suspicion of criminal activity or intention to partake in criminal activity," and during traffic stops.[1]

## Rationale and context

Proponents said the measure was necessary after protests connected with the Israel-Hamas war.[1] Legislator Howard Kopel said at a public meeting that the bill was brought forward in response to "antisemitic incidents, often perpetrated by those in masks" during those protests.[1]
