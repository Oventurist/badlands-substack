# There Are No Limits to Growth

*There Are No Limits to Growth* is a 1982 work by [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]], published as an early output of the Club of Life and written as a direct rebuttal to the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]] a decade earlier.[1]

## Argument on ecology

LaRouche wrote that it is not the growth of industry which destroys the world's forests; in most cases the cause is a lack of industrial output and a lack of good industrial management of the ecosphere.[1] The greatest single cause of ecological destruction over the previous fifteen years, he argued, had been the toleration of policies demanded by the self-described ecologists and neo-Malthusians of the Club of Rome, the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis (IIASA), the [[world-wildlife-fund|World Wildlife Fund]], the Aspen Institute, the Ford Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation and the U.S. [[sierra-club|Sierra Club]].[1] He held that not enough industrially produced energy — water management, chemicals and the like — was being put into farming the earth's biosphere, while biomass was being burned for fuel where nuclear-generated energy and modern industrially produced materials should be used in place of timber.[1]

## Critique of the Limits to Growth model

LaRouche identified two leading counts on which the MIT study was "most conspicuously fraudulent": first, that in attempting to prove industrial society was rapidly exhausting its natural resources, [[dennis-meadows|Meadows]] and [[jay-forrester|Forrester]] greatly understated the known quantities of those resources; and second, more importantly, that they projected consumption rates using systems of simultaneous linear equations, a choice that builds into the computer projections the assumption that absolutely no technological progress is occurring in society.[1] Since technological progress, including fundamental redefinitions of what "natural resources" means, had been the outstanding feature of European civilization for five hundred years, the study depended on the assumption that such progress had come to a sudden, absolute stop.[1]

## Anti-entropy

The work rests on LaRouche's discoveries in the science of physical economy, made between 1952 and 1956, premised on the concept that mankind's ability to leap from lower to higher forms of energy consumption — wood to coal to oil to nuclear fission to fusion — allows the upward transformation of humanity's physical economic potential without limits.[1] Creative leaps into the unknown drive discoveries of principle that raise humanity's potential relative population density along with standards of living, life expectancy and cognitive potential in ways no other animal can achieve, which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents as proof that mankind and the universe alike are anti-entropic in essence rather than tending toward "mathematical equilibrium" or "entropic heat death."[1]
