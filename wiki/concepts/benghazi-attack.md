# Benghazi Attack (September 11, 2012)

The **Benghazi attack** was the assault on the U.S. Special Mission Compound and the nearby CIA annex in Benghazi, Libya, on September 11, 2012, in which Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]], information management officer Sean Smith, and former Navy SEALs [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]] were killed. Badlands Media's account, published as "A Dereliction of Duty," argues that the attack and the absent U.S. military response were not the product of incompetence but of a deliberate political choice made to protect a covert weapons pipeline running from Libya to Syria.[1]

## Background

After the overthrow of [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], Benghazi is described as having become a safe haven for [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] training camps and the focal point for funding and supplying jihadists whom the United States was simultaneously fighting in Iraq and Afghanistan.[1] The newly formed Libyan government was, in this account, controlled by Al Qaeda elements such as [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhadj]], making Libya an ideal staging ground for arms transfers to the Syrian proxy force that became [[isis|ISIS]].[1] The operation is characterized as a secret undertaking of the [[state-department|State Department]] under [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] under [[david-petraeus|David Petraeus]], with the full approval of President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1]

## The attack and the response

Senior diplomats at the U.S. embassy in Tripoli learned of the attack roughly three minutes after the first wave began, and U.S. Africa Command learned of it within thirty minutes — ample time to respond to an assault that lasted more than seven hours.[1] The Diplomatic Command Center at the State Department in Washington received word immediately, yet that notice reportedly took about an hour to reach the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]; the article attributes the delay to Clinton not wanting a military response.[1] The first message out of the compound was a Diplomatic Security Agent's frantic warning, "If you guys don't get out here, we're all going to f—-ing die."[1]

By 5:00 p.m. Washington time the President, the Secretary of Defense, the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and the National Security Advisor had convened at the [[white-house|White House]], where Obama is on record directing that all available resources be used to save lives.[1] Secretary of Defense [[leon-panetta|Leon Panetta]] testified that by 6:00 p.m. he had identified "three distinct capabilities" and by 7:00 p.m. had issued the order to deploy them.[1] No assets ever arrived. A squadron at Aviano Air Base in Italy armed and readied aircraft and waited all night for a call that never came, with one participant saying, "I know everything was spun up and nothing was done. … At our level, we were doing everything we were supposed to be doing. At everybody else's level above us, it was political."[1] It was ultimately a Libyan militia made up of ex-Gaddafi soldiers that rescued the Americans trapped at the CIA annex, while the administration watched the attack on a live drone feed.[1]

## The deputies meeting

At 7:30 p.m., shortly after State learned the Pentagon was "spinning up as we speak," the White House convened a "Deputies Meeting" attended by Clinton and deputies from State, the Pentagon, and the White House; both the President and the Secretary of Defense were absent.[1] With one American dead and the Ambassador missing, Clinton's apparent first concern was the diplomatic impact of a rescue mission, and her department emphasized that any deployment of U.S. forces into Libya needed approval from the Government of Libya.[1] The article treats that condition as pretextual, noting that the Libyan government in question had been installed by Obama, Clinton, and [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and was staffed by the very Al Qaeda-linked figures Stevens had been dealing with.[1]

## "Dereliction of duty"

The House Select Committee report chaired by [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]], Part I, "Terrorist Attack on U.S. Facilities in Benghazi," contrasted the heroism of GRS personnel, Diplomatic Security Agents, and Team Tripoli with what it called the "total incompetence and dereliction of duty" of leaders in Washington who "chose to talk and spin while actually doing nothing," offering after-the-fact excuses such as the "tyranny of time and distance" for never attempting to rescue more than thirty Americans.[1] The article argues the failure was deliberate rather than incompetent and highlights the committee's "smoking gun" finding that "a direct order of the Commander in Chief was obstructed by Secretary of State Hillary Clinton" — obstruction for which she faced no consequence, which the author reads as evidence that Obama's order was itself a "cover your ass" political play.[1]

## Cover-up

Survivors and CIA contractors were subjected to repeated polygraphs as intimidation and were required to re-sign non-disclosure agreements, a step [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] defended before Congress as routine document updating when pressed by Rep. Devin Nunes.[1] Two officials who fought that night were presented with the new NDAs more than six months after the attack, at a May memorial service at CIA headquarters in Langley honoring Woods and Doherty.[1] The administration also focused public attention for weeks on an anti-Islam online video, which the article calls a "red herring" that kept the press from connecting the weapons deals to the attack.[1] See also [[stand-down-order|stand down orders]].
