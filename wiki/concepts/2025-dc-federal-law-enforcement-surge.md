# 2025 DC Federal Law Enforcement Surge

In August 2025, the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] deployed hundreds of federal police officers to target crime in Washington, D.C., as part of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s public safety push in the nation's capital.[1]

## Deployment

The [[white-house|White House]] said 450 officers from multiple federal agencies were deployed in high-traffic D.C. areas and other hotspots on August 9 and 10, 2025.[1] The officers came from 18 agencies, including the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], the [[drug-enforcement-administration|Drug Enforcement Administration]], and the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]].[1]

Trump ordered the increased law enforcement presence on August 8 after complaining about crime in the city, even as violent crime trended down.[1] The deployment preceded a further announcement by the president.[1]

## Initial seven-day patrol order

The [[white-house|White House]] announced on Thursday, August 7, 2025 that federal law enforcement officers would patrol the streets of Washington, D.C. for the next seven days, with the enhanced presence beginning at midnight.[2] The operation was led by the [[united-states-park-police|U.S. Park Police]] and included officers and agents from the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]], the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|ATF]], divisions of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and other agencies, all in marked units.[2]

The announcement came a day after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was considering taking control of the Washington, D.C. police department following a violent attack on a former [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] staffer.[2] Press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the capital had "been plagued by violent crime for far too long" and that starting that night "there will be no safe harbor for violent criminals in D.C."[2]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump's opponents were paralyzed by the pace of his actions, saying they did not know what to do and were reduced to defending themselves while Trump dictated "the pace and flow of the fight."[2] In his framing, Trump had "overloaded the System" — and had done so by design.[2]
