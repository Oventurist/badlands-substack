# Prediction Markets

Prediction markets are trading venues on which participants buy and sell contracts tied to the outcomes of real-world events, producing implied probabilities that are widely cited as market-based forecasts of political, financial and geopolitical developments.[1] [[polymarket|Polymarket]] is the most prominent such platform in US political commentary.[1]

## Regulatory scrutiny (2026)

By March 2026 the sector faced renewed scrutiny, with Democratic lawmakers reportedly working on legislation to rein prediction markets in following public outrage over bets tied to war and major geopolitical events.[1] Critics argue the markets create incentives to profit from conflict or to trade on insider information about events that participants may be able to influence.[1] Polymarket removed a market on the likelihood of a nuclear explosion after public backlash earlier that month.[1]

## Pressure on journalists

The most acute illustration of those concerns came when an Israeli journalist, [[emanuel-fabian|Emanuel Fabian]], said Polymarket users threatened him over a routine report about a missile strike near Jerusalem, because the reporting could determine the outcome of a wager with more than $23 million at stake.[1] Polymarket condemned the harassment, stating that "prediction markets depend on the integrity of independent reporting" and that attempts to pressure journalists to alter their reporting "undermine that integrity and undermine the markets themselves."[1]

## Cultural criticism

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], commenting on Polymarket's [[situation-room-bar|Situation Room]] bar in Washington, D.C., treated prediction markets as the endpoint of the gamification of public life, in which politics, culture, war and markets have been colonized by "the dopamine slot machine."[1] He also offered a second reading in which betting on the news reflects a rational adaptation by citizens who have concluded that real-time truth is unobtainable and that the central narrative is a shifting set of weaponized fictions.[1]
