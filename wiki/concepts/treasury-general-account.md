# Treasury General Account

The Treasury General Account (TGA) is the operating cash account maintained by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]] at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1] Because it sits on the Fed's balance sheet, movements into and out of the TGA directly affect the level of reserves held by commercial banks: when the Treasury sells bills, buyers pay from commercial bank accounts and that money moves into the TGA, reducing bank reserves — electronic liquidity rather than physical cash in the system.[1]

## July 2025 rebuild

In July 2025 the Treasury issued updated guidance on its short-term borrowing strategy, announcing that it would rebuild its cash balance to $500 billion by the end of the month through increased issuance of regular Treasury bills at 4-, 6-, and 8-week maturities.[1] The cash balance had fallen to $372 billion, according to a JPMorgan research note, well below the operational target range.[1] The department stated that it did not expect to issue additional cash management bills (CMBs) to rebuild reserves, signaling confidence in the T-bill market's capacity to absorb the increased issuance; the CMB series announced on June 24 had been completed.[1]

The rebuild followed passage of a sweeping $5 trillion tax and spending bill that included a significant debt ceiling hike.[1] At the May refunding the Treasury had targeted an $850 billion cash balance by the end of September, implying that further issuance would continue in subsequent months in line with internal cash balance policy.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued that the deliberate drain of bank reserves implied by the rebuild could mark a new phase in the transformation of the U.S. banking system, and linked it to the parallel growth of [[private-credit|private credit]] as a substitute for traditional bank lending.[1]
