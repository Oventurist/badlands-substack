# India-China border dispute

The India-China border dispute concerns the contested frontier between the two countries known as the Line of Actual Control, where deadly clashes between border guards in 2020 badly damaged bilateral relations and reshaped geopolitics across Asia.[1]

## 2024 patrolling agreement

In October 2024 a senior Indian official announced that India and China had reached an agreement on patrolling arrangements along the disputed border, described as a major thaw four years after the clashes.[1] Indian Foreign Secretary [[vikram-misri|Vikram Misri]] told reporters in New Delhi that "over the last several weeks, Indian and Chinese diplomatic and military negotiators have been in close contact with each other in a variety of forums, and as a result of these discussions, [an] agreement has been arrived at on patrolling arrangements along the Line of Actual Control," adding that it had produced "disengagement and a resolution of the issues that had arisen in these areas in 2020."[1] [[lin-jian|Lin Jian]], a spokesman for China's Foreign Ministry, confirmed that the two sides had "reached a solution" through diplomatic and military channels.[1]

## Timing and context

The Indian announcement came shortly before Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] were due to arrive in Russia for the [[2024-brics-kazan-summit|BRICS summit]] hosted by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Kazan, amid mounting speculation that the two leaders would discuss normalizing relations on the sidelines of the conference.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the deal as evidence that what he called the "Sovereign Alliance" — the [[brics|BRICS]] bloc — was positioning itself as the champion of peace and prosperity while the West declined, contrasting the constructive diplomacy in Kazan with what he described as a decaying post-American order.[1]
