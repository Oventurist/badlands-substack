# Gang of Eight Immigration Bill

The "Gang of Eight" immigration bill was a comprehensive immigration reform measure crafted in 2013 by a bipartisan group of eight United States senators and passed by the Senate before dying in the House of Representatives.[1] Badlands contributor coverage treats the bill as a central exhibit in the case that [[john-mccain|John McCain]] worked against the interests of the American people while presenting himself as a bipartisan statesman.[1]

## Sponsors

The eight sponsors were [[john-mccain|John McCain]] (R-Ariz.) and Senate Minority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] (D-N.Y.), together with Sens. [[jeff-flake|Jeff Flake]] (R-Ariz.), [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] (R-S.C.), [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] (R-Fla.), [[dick-durbin|Dick Durbin]] (D-Ill.), [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]] (D-Colo.) and [[robert-menendez|Robert Menendez]] (D-N.J.).[1]

## Contents and criticism

As reported by [[blaze-media|Blaze Media]] and cited in the article, the legislation would have granted amnesty to an estimated 11.5 million illegal immigrants, cost taxpayers trillions of dollars, and failed to reduce illegal immigration.[1] The bill contained no provision requiring construction of a border fence; instead it required the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to submit a "strategy" for fencing to [[us-congress|Congress]] — recommendations without physical construction, described in the cited commentary as "toothless."[1] The governing philosophy of the bill was summarized as "amnesty first, we'll worry about border security later," with the observation that in Washington, D.C., "later" means "never."[1]

The Badlands analysis argues that the bill's real function was money laundering dressed up as border security, and that legalizing 11.5 million people would have permanently altered the electorate and foreclosed future election victories for the bill's opponents.[1]

## Death in the House and attempted revival

The bill passed the Senate — McCain claimed it drew 68 votes — but was killed in the House of Representatives after conservative backlash, exemplified by the primary defeat of House Majority Leader Eric Cantor, demonstrated that the grassroots would not tolerate the open-borders consensus of the D.C. establishment.[1]

In an August 2017 interview with *The Arizona Republic*, McCain said that he and Schumer agreed on bringing the bipartisan amnesty legislation back, updated for "new challenges, like opioids," and predicted a similar vote count.[1] The revival attempt came while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was pressing for a border wall; the White House had already dismissed the Gang of Eight bill as "amnesty," with press secretary Sean Spicer confirming the president's opposition.[1] The article notes McCain continued "pleading for amnesty" even as Congress considered the [[raise-act|RAISE Act]], the immigration reform bill Trump supported.[1]

## McCain's position on the wall

McCain publicly cast doubt on the border wall approach, saying: "I'm not against a border wall, OK, but go to China and you'll see a border wall there. We need technology, we need drones, we need surveillance capabilities and we need rapid-reaction capabilities. ... But to think that a wall is going to stop illegal immigration or drugs is crazy."[1] The cited commentary conceded that most illegal immigrants enter legally and overstay visas, but maintained that border fences work to reduce illegal immigration.[1]
