# Vampire Facial HIV Outbreak

The vampire facial HIV outbreak was a cluster of HIV infections traced to cosmetic "vampire facial" procedures performed at VIP Beauty Salon and Spa in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and reported by the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] in April 2024.[1]

## The procedure

A vampire facial is a cosmetic procedure that uses a patient's own blood, reinjected into the face, with the aim of improving facial skin tone and texture.[1]

## The CDC investigation

Four women contracted HIV after receiving vampire facials at the New Mexico salon, according to a CDC report released in April 2024 — the first known instance of the virus being transmitted through cosmetic injection services.[1] The agency opened its investigation after multiple women tested positive for HIV and the infections were linked back to treatments received at the establishment.[1] Investigators found unwrapped needles and unlabeled syringes scattered throughout the spa, and concluded that patients were likely infected through shared needles.[1] Three of the women contracted HIV at the spa in 2018 and a fourth in 2019; the fourth infection was followed by a man contracting HIV after having sex with her.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the outbreak to criticise the cosmetic-vanity industry, writing that "vanity is a seductive bitch, and women will entertain the ridiculous in pursuit of her."[1] While allowing that the salon's clients plainly did not consent to being infected, she argued they were also unlikely to have checked the salon's references or vetted its procedural standards, noting that used syringes lying in the open should have registered as an obvious red flag.[1] She urged readers considering the procedure to interrogate the motive first, to insist on sterile needles if they proceed, and — in a claim contrary to mainstream medical guidance — advised against taking AZT in the event of infection.[1]
