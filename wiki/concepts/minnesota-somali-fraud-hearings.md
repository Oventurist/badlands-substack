# Minnesota Somali Fraud Hearings

The Minnesota Somali fraud hearings are congressional proceedings examining allegations that billions of taxpayer dollars were lost to fraud tied to federally backed social programs in Minnesota, with the alleged schemes associated in Badlands coverage with Somali diaspora networks in the Minneapolis area.[1]

## Coverage and commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America described the hearing statistics as "rough" and "sobering," and criticized the five-minute questioning format — noting while watching Representative Brandon Gill rush through his questions that "five minutes isn't enough" and can "only be for show," an unserious allotment for the People's representatives' pursuit of truth.[1] She closed with the slogans "Ban & Deport Somalis" and "Convict & Exile Complicit Americans…Exile them to Somalia."[1]

Contributor Burning Bright argued that the fraud revelations had "freshly indicted" the system and that the [[minneapolis-ice-shooting-of-renee-nicole-good|Minneapolis ICE shooting]] and ensuing protests functioned as a distraction injected into the zeitgeist days before a mass mobilization.[1]

## Federal response

Following claims that taxpayers lost billions in Minnesota, Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] announced the creation of a new [[assistant-attorney-general-for-fraud|assistant attorney general position focused on fraud investigations]], with initial efforts concentrated in Minnesota.[1] The administration also halted more than $10 billion in federal funding for social services programs in Minnesota, New York, California, Illinois, and Colorado, and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] froze federal child care funding for Minnesota.[1]

## January 2026 escalation

By January 2026 the scandal had escalated into a high-profile political firestorm, with Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] alleging a deliberate Democratic strategy to build voting blocs through fraudulent entitlements while the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] pushed aggressive measures including citizenship revocation.[2] Prosecutors and investigators estimated that up to half of $18–20 billion in federally funded welfare programs over recent years was lost to fraud, primarily in programs such as Medicaid — where 86% of Somalis in Minnesota reportedly receive free healthcare — food stamps, housing, childcare, and the $250 million pandemic-era child nutrition scheme known as [[feeding-our-future|Feeding Our Future]].[2]

House Oversight Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.), Senate Homeland Security Chairman [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] (R-Ky.), and House Majority Whip [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] (R-Minn.) led the investigations, describing the fraud as a "coordinated effort" and a "scam" to manipulate elections, congressional apportionment, and federal funding in blue states.[2] Whistleblowers, including Minnesota state workers, provided affidavits claiming they had warned Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Attorney General [[keith-ellison|Keith Ellison]] about the issues without action — allegations both officials deny.[2] Probes expanded to other Democratic states including California, New York and Illinois, with the administration freezing funds and targeting high-risk programs.[2]

In direct response, Senator [[eric-schmitt|Eric Schmitt]] (R-Mo.) introduced the White House-backed [[scam-act|SCAM Act]], and Deputy Chief of Staff [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] called the scandal "one of the greatest financial scandals in American history."[2] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] intensified the controversy in a January 19, 2026 Truth Social post targeting Representative [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] (D-MN), suggesting she be jailed or "sent back to Somalia" over the "$19 Billion Dollars in Minnesota Somalia Fraud."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reiterated her formula "deport the Somalis; prosecute the Americans," supporting the SCAM Act while insisting that everyone who enabled, covered up, or profited from the scheme be prosecuted, and calling to "end the NGOs" and restore the rule of law "equally, without fear or favor."[2]

## January 7, 2026 Oversight hearing

On January 7, 2026 the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] held a hearing on the multimillion-dollar pandemic fraud schemes in [[minnesota|Minnesota]], with state lawmakers called to testify in Washington.[3] The hearing coincided with the fatal shooting of [[renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]] by a federal immigration agent in Minneapolis, and Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that wall-to-wall coverage of the shooting buried the testimony, remarking on "what luck for Minnesota that this happened when it did."[3]
