# Essequibo Dispute

The Essequibo dispute is the long-running territorial contest between [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and [[guyana|Guyana]] over the Essequibo region, which comprises about two-thirds of Guyana's territory and includes oil-rich offshore waters where [[exxonmobil|ExxonMobil]] leads oil production.[1] In recent years Venezuela has intensified its claims to the region.[1]

## US position

Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said in March 2025 in Georgetown, standing beside Guyanese President [[irfaan-ali|Irfaan Ali]], that there would be "consequences" if Venezuela took forceful action to press its claims, opposing "regional threats based on illegitimate territorial claims by a narco-trafficking regime."[1] Rubio declined to specify what the Trump administration might do, but pointed to the power and size of the US Navy, implying that military action was possible.[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands commentary argued that Rubio was serving the interests of the City of London, characterising Guyana as a proxy state — the only country in mainland Central or South America under the British Commonwealth, which it joined in 1966 — and identifying seabed oil deposits as the resource being protected.[1] The commentary linked the dispute to high-profile arrests of oil executives worldwide in corruption probes involving Venezuela, probes launched by [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in March 2017 after he was handed a "laptop from hell" by the scorned ex-wife of one of his oil executives.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] speculated that Maduro and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might be working together to squeeze the players involved in Guyana and Venezuelan oil graft, noting that the companies involved are also connected to high-profile conflicts in Africa over other valuable natural resources.[1] Because the Guyanese seabed fields form part of a larger field belonging to Venezuela, the commentary argued, legal trouble follows for those exploiting Guyana; a 2018 report quoted IHS Global Insight analyst Diego Moya-Ocampos saying Venezuela's sovereignty claim would pose a "major complication" to recovering oil and minerals, since Caracas could bar companies operating in Guyana from working in Venezuela's own vast oil fields.[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] compared Rubio's warning to Joe Biden telling Vladimir Putin not to "invade" Ukraine more than a little bit.[1]
