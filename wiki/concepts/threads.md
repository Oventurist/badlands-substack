# Threads

Threads is a text-based social networking application launched by [[meta-platforms|Meta]] under chief executive [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], positioned in the press as a "Twitter killer" intended to displace [[twitter|Twitter]] as the dominant microblogging platform.[1]

## Launch and initial surge

Threads launched in the United States on July 5, 2023 and passed 100 million sign-ups within its first weekend, a pace that made it one of the fastest-adopted consumer applications on record.[1] Shortly afterward Zuckerberg posted, "Can't believe it's only been five days!"[1]

## Collapse in engagement

The hype collapsed almost immediately. Data from SensorTower and SimilarWeb cited in mid-July 2023 showed an exodus of users and a plunge in engagement, and subsequent SimilarWeb figures showed daily active users falling from 49 million to 23.6 million in a single week.[1] Usage in the United States peaked on July 7 at roughly 21 minutes of daily engagement per user; by the following Friday that figure had fallen to about six minutes.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed Threads as a product of establishment anxiety over freer speech on [[twitter|Twitter]] following [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s acquisition of that platform, writing that "anti-truth hysteria is a great clickbait strategy, but a terrible business strategy."[1] She argued that Musk undercut the Threads launch by announcing Twitter's creator monetization program with large payouts to prominent influencers — including a reported $25,000 to one of the Krassenstein brothers — which drew users back to Twitter.[1] In her assessment the app's target audience was "visionless and profit-motivated," so a "weak-featured platform with an echo chamber brand promise" was no match for direct cash incentives.[1]

## User attrition through August 2023

By late August 2023 the app's decline was documented in mainstream financial press. [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] had admitted in leaked audio the previous month that Threads was failing after its initial surge, which drew upwards of 100 million downloads in the five days following the July 5 launch.[2] At its peak the microblogging app counted roughly 50 million daily active users worldwide; by August, data from the analytics firm SimilarWeb cited by The Wall Street Journal put daily active users at fewer than ten million.[2]

## July 2023 engagement collapse

Within roughly a week of the launch, tracking firms reported a sharp reversal. Data from Sensor Tower and Similarweb cited by [[cnbc|CNBC]] showed that daily active users on the Tuesday and Wednesday after launch were down about 20 percent from the preceding Saturday, while average time spent per user fell by half, from twenty minutes to ten.[3] Anthony Bartolacci, managing director at the marketing intelligence firm Sensor Tower, said the launch "really did 'break the internet,' or at least the Sensor Tower models," adding that in more than ten years of estimating app installs the first 72 hours of Threads was "truly in a class by itself."[3] He cautioned that the early returns signalled "an uphill climb for Threads to carve out space in most users' social network routine," and that the backing of [[meta-platforms|Meta]] and integration with Instagram would not substitute for a value proposition beyond "Twitter, but without [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]."[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued the failure was predictable: [[twitter|Twitter]] was already synonymous with the format and had better infrastructure, and Threads' selling point — a speech platform with less functionality and less speech — was antithetical even to mainstream consumers.[3] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described Threads as "the Meta replacement for Twitter" and "a total flop," reading its collapse as part of a wider loss of globalist leverage over mass media following Musk's purchase of Twitter.[3]
