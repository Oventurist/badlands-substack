# FedNow

**FedNow** is an instant payment service built by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]. In the Badlands Media article "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] stresses that FedNow is a peer-to-peer payment system and *not* a digital currency, a distinction he says is widely confused in commentary about [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDCs]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom)

Lange nonetheless calls FedNow an important part of the broader plan, because the Fed has built a peer-to-peer platform that every financial service in the country can access and which eliminates fees charged by other intermediaries.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom) He contrasts it with the [[automated-clearing-house|ACH]] network, where settlement takes days and a third party retains the ability to cancel transactions.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom) Together with round-the-clock digital settlement, FedNow is presented as infrastructure that returns control and speed to individual users.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom)

## Launch and CBDC concerns (2023)

Badlands Media reported in May 2023 that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] had announced FedNow as "a new instant payment infrastructure developed by the Federal Reserve that allows financial institutions of every size across the US to provide safe and efficient instant payment services."[2] The service was expected to deploy in phases with an initial launch in July 2023, and that article characterized it as the first step in the United States toward a [[central-bank-digital-currency|Central Bank Digital Currency]].[2] The Fed's own position, quoted in the piece, was that it had "made no decisions on whether to pursue or implement a central bank digital currency" but had been exploring the potential benefits and risks through technological research and experimentation, focused on whether and how a CBDC could improve on an already safe and efficient US domestic payments system.[2] The Federal Reserve published an exploratory paper on the subject on January 20, 2022.[2]

## Announcement and the CBDC debate (March 2023)

On 10 March 2023 the United States [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] announced the new stage of its FedNow instant payments system, first announced in 2019, which will allow banks to instantly transfer payments across the financial system.[3] As banks and other financial institutions join the program, a growing network with clearing and settlement features will let businesses and individuals send and receive instant payments at any time of day.[3]

Fed governor [[michelle-bowman|Michelle Bowman]] said in 2022 that FedNow could offer some of the same benefits as a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]], thus weakening the case for adopting one — which, according to chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], remains years away in the United States.[3] Asked in March whether there was an advantage to FedNow over a CBDC or a stablecoin, Powell responded that "a CBDC is going to be years in evaluation," adding, "I think we can get this into the hands of the public very quickly, and we'll have real-time payments in this country very, very soon."[3]

Reactions were mixed. Lawrence Lepard, investment manager at Equity Management Associates, tweeted, "Right on schedule. Here is your CBDC launch."[3] Author Scott Santens disputed that depiction, arguing that FedNow has no smart contract ability and is not equivalent to a digital dollar: "FedNow implementation is one of the arguments against launching a CBDC. It's so not a CBDC that it actually reduces the odds of starting a CBDC."[3] Journalist [[jordan-schachtel|Jordan Schachtel]], who writes The Dossier, remained skeptical, warning that "FedNow appears to be a prototype CBDC" and that it "can quickly transform to a surveillance system," calling it "a giant red flag."[3] Badlands Media observed that these are the exact same fears associated with CBDCs.[3]
