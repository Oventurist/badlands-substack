# Lunar Soil Water Extraction

Lunar soil water extraction refers to a breakthrough technology reported in July 2025 by which scientists said the soil on the Moon could be used to produce water and other resources, potentially supporting human life on the lunar surface.[1]

## Rationale

Sustained human presence on the Moon — and the broader hope, held by a range of countries, that future missions could see humans stay there before heading further into the solar system — may only be possible if the necessary fuel, water and other resources can be manufactured on the Moon itself, since flying them from Earth could be impossible.[1] Reporting cited a cost of $83,000 to ship a single gallon of water to the Moon, with each astronaut requiring roughly four gallons per day.[1]

## Method

Using samples brought back from a Chinese mission to the Moon, researchers identified a new method allowing astronauts to obtain water from the lunar soil.[1] Previous attempts had used large amounts of energy and did not break down CO2; the new integrated system overcomes both problems.[1] "We never fully imagined the 'magic' that the lunar soil possessed," said Lu Wang of the Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, adding that "the biggest surprise for us was the tangible success of this integrated approach."[1]

## Commentary
