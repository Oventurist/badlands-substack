# Supremacy Clause

The Supremacy Clause of Article VI of the United States Constitution establishes that the Constitution, federal statutes and treaties are the supreme law of the land, binding state judges and displacing conflicting state or local law. A related doctrinal strand holds that states and their subdivisions may not directly regulate or burden the operations of the federal government or its officers acting within the scope of their duties.

That intergovernmental-immunity dimension is the basis of the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] complaint described in the [[badlands-brief-323|Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026]]. The United States asked a court to declare that the investigations, proceedings, adjudications and findings of [[district-of-columbia-bar|District of Columbia bar]] disciplinary authorities against former Assistant Attorney General [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] "violate the Supremacy Clause and Article II and are therefore void," and to enjoin any further disciplinary action based on his conduct as a federal government attorney. Officials argued that local bar regulation of executive branch deliberations improperly targets internal federal legal advice and risks deterring candid counsel — an application of the clause paired with Article II's vesting of executive power in the President.
