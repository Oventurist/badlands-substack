# Georgia Foreign Agents Law

The Georgia foreign agents law is legislation adopted by the parliament of the Republic of Georgia on Tuesday, May 14, 2024, that would impede the ability of foreign nongovernmental organizations (NGOs) to operate in the country.[1] Parliament passed the measure over large domestic protests and condemnation from the United States and other Western governments, and analysts described it as placing Georgia's [[european-union|European Union]] and [[nato|NATO]] ambitions in jeopardy.[1]

## Passage and veto fight

Georgia's president, [[salome-zourabichvili|Salome Zourabichvili]], vowed to veto the measure, but parliament approved it by an 84–30 margin — comfortably above the 76 votes needed to override a presidential veto.[1]

## U.S. reaction

After the law's passage, White House Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] told reporters that the administration was "deeply troubled by Georgia's Kremlin-style 'foreign agents' legislation," adding that the administration had "been outspoken about our concerns with the legislation, which runs counter to democratic values and would move Georgia further away from the values of the European Union and, let's not forget, also NATO."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the swift Western hostility as confirmation that NGOs function as instruments of CIA covert action, writing that the sequence — Georgia barring NGOs, NATO responding with hostility — was "just too perfect."[1] He linked the episode to [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] and the broader pattern of color revolutions in Europe.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Georgia, a former Soviet satellite that hosted its own color revolution in 2003 — the Rose Revolution — was effectively severing ties with NATO and the EU and curtailing attempts to draw it into the Western bloc "Ukraine-style."[1]
