# China–Brazil Currency Agreement

The **China–Brazil currency agreement** was a bilateral arrangement announced at the end of March 2023 under which [[china|China]] and [[brazil|Brazil]] agreed to settle trade and financial transactions directly in their own currencies, dropping the United States dollar as an intermediary.[1]

## Terms and scale

According to the Brazilian government, as reported by AFP and relayed by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], the deal allows the two countries to exchange yuan for reais and vice versa rather than routing payments through the dollar.[1] Bilateral trade between the two economies amounted to roughly $150 billion per year, making the arrangement one of the larger dollar-exempting settlement channels established to that point.[1]

## Context

The agreement extended China's network of bilateral, dollar-exempting currency arrangements — previously covering countries such as Russia, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia — to include the largest economy in Latin America.[1] ZeroHedge characterized the deal as "Beijing's latest salvo against the almighty greenback" and situated it within a broader acceleration of [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] following the weaponization of the dollar through sanctions after February 2022, noting that even [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had begun commenting on the trend.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that while domestic political and cultural controversies dominated the American media cycle, "REAL and lasting moves" were being made on currency, "the lifeblood of transactions."[1] He contended that the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] had retained its strength largely because nations feared intelligence-agency-assisted regime change, and that with a strong Russian–Chinese bloc emerging in the East, "the days of Petrodollar hegemony appear to be numbered."[1]
