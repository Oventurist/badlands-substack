# House Resolution on Disclosure of Congressional Sexual Misconduct Settlements

The House resolution on disclosure of congressional sexual misconduct settlements was a transparency measure passed by the United States [[house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] on Tuesday, June 30, 2026, requiring the public identification of members who used taxpayer funds to settle sexual misconduct allegations.[1]

## Passage

The resolution was offered by Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] and adopted by a near-unanimous vote of 420-0, with Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] voting "present."[1] It directs the [[house-ethics-committee|House Ethics Committee]] and the [[office-of-congressional-workplace-rights|Office of Congressional Workplace Rights]] to release, within 60 days, all records identifying which current or former House members used taxpayer funds to settle sexual misconduct allegations against them, including the amounts involved.[1]

## Background

The push followed high-profile resignations earlier in 2026 by Reps. [[tony-gonzales|Tony Gonzales]] and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] amid serious sexual misconduct allegations, which renewed scrutiny of how [[congress|Congress]] has handled such claims.[1] A prior House Oversight Committee subpoena had already revealed that the federal government paid more than $300,000 to settle claims against House lawmakers or their offices.[1] Congress ended the practice of using taxpayer dollars for these settlements in 2018, and the Ethics Committee has stated that it does not handle sexual harassment lawsuits or related settlements.[1]

## Reactions

Massie argued that disclosure was needed "to convince the people and assure the people that we are conducting the people's business with the utmost integrity."[1] Ethics Committee Chair [[michael-guest|Michael Guest]] supported making the information "readily available," while Mace described the timing as "political theater," noting that her earlier efforts had already compelled related materials through subpoena.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who had raised the subject days earlier at a "Taxation is Theft" panel at the Great American Restoration Tour, framed the episode as emblematic of a protected ruling class: members settle allegations with public money and the records are then sealed, with no arrests and no public shaming.[1] He argued that the system protecting them "must be destroyed" rather than reformed, invoking the American Revolution's aim of freeing the country from ruling elites.[1]
