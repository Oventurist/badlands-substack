# Pact of Diriyah

The Pact of Diriyah was the 1744 alliance between [[mohammed-bin-saud]], emir of the farming settlement of Diriyah, and the exiled preacher [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab]]. As described in [[arabian-nights]], it fused political and military authority with religious authority and became the foundation of the [[house-of-saud]] and of the [[emirate-of-diriyah]], the First State of Saud.

## Terms

Bin Saud offered the alliance after al-Wahhab's expulsion from Uyayna, framing it as a socio-religious reformation combining "might, reason, and faith." He attached two conditions: that al-Wahhab settle in Diriyah and serve as his counsellor, and that he not oppose the collection of taxation.

Al-Wahhab accepted the first and, citing his recent experience at Uyayna, rejected the second. He argued that taxation would create natural animus between bin Saud and his people, and that more wealth would come from the conquest of enemies than from bleeding one's own subjects. Bin Saud agreed. The source observes that, to this day, no tax is imposed or collected by the Saudi government.

## The oath

The agreement was confirmed with a *bay'ah*, a mutual oath of loyalty. The source records bin Saud's declaration — "This oasis is yours, do not fear your enemies. By the name of God, if all Nejd was summoned to throw you out, we will never agree to expel you" — and al-Wahhab's reply: "You are the settlement's chief and wise man. I want you to grant me an oath that you will perform jihad against the unbelievers. In return, you will be imam, leader of the Muslim community and I will be leader in religious matters."

The two men, described in the source as champions of reason and debate, embraced as brothers. The alliance was cemented dynastically by the marriage of bin Saud's son to al-Wahhab's daughter. A twentieth-century illustration of the 1744 agreement is reproduced in the article.
