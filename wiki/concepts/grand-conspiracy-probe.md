# Grand Conspiracy Probe

The "grand conspiracy" probe is a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] inquiry, run out of the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida, examining whether the prior federal investigations and prosecutions directed at President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] themselves amounted to a criminal conspiracy.[1]

## Scope

Prosecutors are reviewing prior federal probes into Trump, including the [[russiagate-hoax|Russian Collusion investigation]] tied to his 2016 campaign, the cases brought by former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], and the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 2022 search of Mar-a-Lago for classified documents.[1]

## FBI origins under Kash Patel (2025)

An earlier phase of the effort originated inside the [[fbi|FBI]] itself. *Just the News* reported in July 2025 that the Bureau had quietly launched an investigation into a decade of Democratic party and deep-state activity, from Russia collusion to [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], opening the door to the appointment of a special prosecutor to examine whether the well-documented episodes amounted to a criminal conspiracy to meddle in three U.S. elections to the benefit of Democrats and the detriment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The "grand conspiracy" case was opened several weeks earlier, after new FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] took over.[2]

Officials said the case could receive a significant boost if Trump were to declassify two secret tranches of evidence identifying a potential ignition point for the alleged conspiracy in the summer of 2016.[2] The first is a classified annex to a years-old inspector general probe of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s improper email server, sought by Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] and believed to show that credible information about possible wrongdoing was intentionally ignored by the FBI.[2] The second tranche was identified by former Russiagate Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] in his final report.[2] Both had remained sealed for nearly a decade and are highly classified because they reveal sensitive intelligence-gathering methods.[2]

## Personnel

The work is supervised by [[joe-digenova|Joe diGenova]], a Trump ally serving as counselor to acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and leading a civil rights unit in Miami, and is being developed by US Attorney [[jason-reding-quinones|Jason Reding Quinones]].[1] In June 2026 the Department confirmed that [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]] had begun work as a senior attorney in the office, reporting to Reding Quinones.[1] Olsen had previously been involved in efforts examining possible foreign interference in US elections and in the collection of voting machines and election materials in Puerto Rico, Georgia and Arizona to support the investigation.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued on Badlands Daily that no attorney in the nation better understands election fraud and malfeasance "on every level — fake voters, fake ballots, and fake counts," along with their evidentiary application across legal challenges, cyber expert analysis and legislative efforts, than Olsen.[1]

Writing in July 2025, Ashe in America connected the probe to Patel's book *Government Gangsters* and to his earlier role as lead Russiagate investigator for the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]], noting that Patel had said the committee's findings could not be discussed because they were classified rather than released — plot lines now being revived by former HPSCI chairman [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]].[2] She argued that Russiagate is "in fact, a grand conspiracy" whose record has been built for years, and that it supplies motive: "From before the escalator to [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] to the autopen, Russia Gate seems to get you everything *except why*."[2] The unanswered question, she wrote, is why whole-of-institution opposition to an outsider was required — a question she tied to the simultaneous re-emergence of 9/11 plea deals, Benghazi, and Iran in the news cycle, concluding: "She was never supposed to lose."[2]

## July 2025 origins and scope

Commentary in July 2025 argued that the FBI "grand conspiracy" case went far beyond former Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] and former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]].[3] The Daily Signal anchored that argument in a text message sent by former FBI lawyer [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] to her then-boyfriend, FBI Special Agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]], amid the 2016 campaign: "POTUS wants to know everything we are doing."[3] The piece stressed that Page was far more than "Strzok's girlfriend" — Comey's deputy was [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]], McCabe's right hand was Page, and Page served as the FBI's White House liaison during the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] investigations — meaning the president referenced was [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[3] When Fox News broke the story of a criminal investigation into Brennan and Comey, its sources used the word "conspiracy."[3]

## Trump's embrace of a special prosecutor

On Wednesday, July 16, 2025, President Trump embraced the FBI's decision to open a conspiracy probe into a decade of alleged intelligence abuses and weaponized law enforcement, suggesting it could be led by a special prosecutor and even delve into "credible evidence" in the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] case in order to give Americans greater transparency and accountability.[3] He also vowed to declassify two highly sensitive pieces of intelligence to further the prosecutor's efforts.[3] "It was a disgrace what happened, what happened in 2016 and what happened in 2020," he said. "And our voting has to be straightened out. I always say if you don't have borders, if you don't have fair and free voting, you don't have a country."[3] Unprompted, Trump volunteered that a special prosecutor appointed by Justice to examine [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization]] could also look at "anything credible" on Epstein and his files.[3]

Germination. Harvest."[3] He observed that Trump refocused public attention on the Russiagate narrative during the same week the media went all-in on Epstein, concluding that "the enemy doesn't fear 'the list'" and that "it all started with TREASON. That's where it ends."[3]
