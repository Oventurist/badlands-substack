# Hydroxychloroquine

Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) is an anti-malarial drug that became one of the most politically contested therapeutics of the COVID-19 pandemic, alongside the antiparasitic [[ivermectin|ivermectin]].[1]

## FOIA litigation over suppression

In December 2023, [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] (AFL) sued the [[food-and-drug-administration|U.S. Food and Drug Administration]] and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] for failing to turn over documents concerning the government's suppression of HCQ and ivermectin as COVID-19 treatments.[1] According to the complaint, AFL had filed a [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]] request in August 2022 with both agencies seeking records about HCQ covering the period from March 1 to September 1, 2020, and received confirmation and tracking numbers from each agency; a parallel request concerning ivermectin followed in September 2022.[1] Neither agency complied with the requests.[1]

AFL, a nonprofit describing its mission as promoting the rule of law, preventing executive overreach and ensuring due process and equal protection, said it sought the records to determine when and why government officials — including [[anthony-fauci|Dr. Anthony Fauci]], then director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases — discouraged and suppressed the use of the two drugs.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that the public had been "witness to the ugly and downright totalitarian tactics used to suppress any information or treatment guidelines" relating to HCQ and ivermectin, arguing that thousands of doctors and hundreds of peer-reviewed studies had supported their efficacy while the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]], [[department-of-health-and-human-services|HHS]] and other agencies obscured, obfuscated and withheld information that could have saved lives.[1] He framed the lawsuit as evidence that "the cracks in the dam are growing beyond repair" for those who promoted and defended the official COVID-19 narrative.[1]
