# CIA Re-entry into Venezuela (2026)

Following the capture of former Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in January 2026, the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] moved to establish a permanent ground presence in [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[1] The agency had supported the operation against Maduro, including a covert team deployed in August of the previous year to track his movements and an insider source within the Venezuelan government.[1]

## Ratcliffe visit to Caracas

CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] became the first senior Trump administration official to visit Caracas, meeting interim President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] — whom the United States backed over opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] on the basis of classified CIA assessments — as well as military leaders, whom he warned that Venezuela could no longer serve as a safe haven for US adversaries such as [[china|China]], Russia and [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Structure of the re-entry

Sources familiar with the planning described the CIA leading initial re-entry efforts because of ongoing instability, prioritizing the establishment of an intelligence annex to run informal liaison channels with Venezuelan officials, factions and opposition figures before full diplomatic operations resume.[1] The [[state-department|State Department]] was handling long-term diplomacy and assessments for reopening the embassy, with a small team already in Caracas evaluating the US facility that had been shuttered since 2019.[1]

## Trump statements and open questions

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] stated that the United States had been "asked" to reopen the embassy, claimed the administration would effectively "run" the country during the transition, and expressed an intent for American oil companies to resume operations and assist in rebuilding.[1] Long-term objectives remained unclear as the White House continued to define the broader mission.[1]
