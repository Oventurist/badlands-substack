# Tax Cuts and Jobs Act

The **Tax Cuts and Jobs Act** is the 2017 tax law approved by the Republican-controlled [[congress|Congress]] and signed into law by then-President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Section 965 and litigation

Among its provisions was the [[mandatory-repatriation-tax|Mandatory Repatriation Tax]], also known as the Section 965 transition tax.[1] That provision came before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in [[moore-v-united-states|Moore v. United States]], argued December 5, 2023, in a dispute over whether the federal government may tax "unrealized" income consistent with the [[sixteenth-amendment|16th Amendment]].[1] The outcome was expected to bear on whether Congress could enact a wealth tax of the kind proposed by Sen. [[ron-wyden|Ron Wyden]].[1]

## Renewal push (February 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] gathered House Republican leaders at the [[white-house|White House]] on Thursday, February 6, 2025 to relay his tax priorities, telling Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], Majority Leader [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]], Majority Whip [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] and others that he wanted to fulfill his campaign promise to stop taxing tips.[2] Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said Trump also wanted "no tax on seniors, Social Security, no tax on overtime pay," and sought to renew his 2017 tax cuts under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, whose provisions were set to begin expiring later that year.[2]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that settling for incremental breaks rather than Trump's stated constitutional ideals would reduce his leverage, asking whether anyone believed Trump wanted the public "groveling for crumbs."[2]
