# FD-1023 Confidential Human Source Form

The FD-1023 is the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] form used to record information provided by a confidential human source. A specific FD-1023 dated June 2020 became the focus of congressional scrutiny in June 2023 because it documented a source's allegations of a "criminal bribery scheme" involving [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and a Ukrainian oligarch.[1]

## Grassley disclosures

On Monday, June 12, 2023, Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] of Iowa read from a largely unredacted version of the form on the floor of the [[united-states-senate|United States Senate]].[1] According to Grassley, the document stated that "the foreign national who allegedly bribed Joe and Hunter Biden allegedly has audio recordings of his conversations with them — 17 such recordings."[1] He specified the breakdown: fifteen recordings of phone calls between the foreign national and Hunter Biden, and two recordings of calls between the foreign national and then-Vice President Joe Biden.[1]

Grassley said the recordings "were allegedly kept as a sort of insurance policy for the foreign national in case he got into a tight spot."[1] He added that the FBI record "also indicates that then-Vice President Joe Biden may have been involved in Burisma employing Hunter Biden."[1]

The foreign national described in the form was identified as [[mykola-zlochevsky|Mykola Zlochevsky]], the Ukrainian owner of [[burisma|Burisma]], who according to sources familiar with the record referred to Joe Biden as "the big guy" in a conversation several years before the document's June 2020 date.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the FBI's redaction of material transmitted to Congress rested on a dubious basis, since redactions are normally justified by national security interests, and suggested the more likely motive was protecting entrenched intelligence interests.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the disclosure alongside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federal indictment announcement the same week, arguing the investigations were converging and would ultimately "boomerang" on those pursuing Trump.[1]
