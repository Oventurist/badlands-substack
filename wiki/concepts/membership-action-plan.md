# Membership Action Plan (MAP)

A Membership Action Plan (MAP) is the formal [[nato|NATO]] mechanism by which a candidate state advances toward accession, and it functions in "A Bright Red Line" as the marker of how far Western commitments to Ukraine have travelled.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article notes a deliberate change in official language: where officials once spoke of being "in discussions" or of "negotiating the possibility of a MAP for Ukraine," the current formulation — exemplified by [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]]'s statement that "Ukraine WILL become a member of NATO" — treats membership as decided and inevitable.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The 2008 cable of [[william-burns|William Burns]] addressed the consequences of a premature MAP offer, especially to Ukraine, directly: it would be seen "not as a technical step along a long road toward membership, but as throwing down the strategic gauntlet."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Burns predicted Russian-Ukrainian relations would enter a deep freeze, that Moscow would weigh economic measures from an immediate increase in gas prices to world market levels through to a clampdown on Ukrainian workers in Russia, and that the move would create fertile soil for Russian meddling in Crimea and eastern Ukraine.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Waiver for Ukraine (2023)

In July 2023, AFP reported that [[nato|NATO]] was set to drop the Membership Action Plan requirement in Ukraine's case — a hurdle that all members joining between 1999 and 2020 had been required to endure.[2] Secretary-General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] paired the waiver with a promise of a fast-tracked process once the alliance was ready to consider Ukraine's application, though President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] still criticized the absence of a firm timeline.[2]
