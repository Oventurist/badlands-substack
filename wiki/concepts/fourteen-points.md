# Fourteen Points

The Fourteen Points were the statement of war aims presented by President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] to Congress in January 1918.[1]

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 1" reports that the points were helped into being by [[the-inquiry|the Inquiry]], the body of roughly 100 prominent Americans assembled by Wilson's confidential advisor [[edward-mandell-house|Edward Mandell House]] to plan the postwar world.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues the points "could be seen as a globalist wish list," pointing to their calls for the removal of "all economic barriers" between nations, "equality of trade conditions," and the formation of "a general association of nations."[1]

That last provision became the [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]], the covenant for which the U.S. Senate ultimately refused to ratify, prompting House and his colleagues to pursue their aims through the privately organized institutes that became [[chatham-house|Chatham House]] and the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]].[1]
