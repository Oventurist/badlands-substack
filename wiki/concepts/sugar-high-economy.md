# Sugar High Economy

A **"sugar high" economy** is the term used for the artificially stimulated propping up of an economy through sustained low interest rates and continual expansion of the [[federal-reserve-balance-sheet|Federal Reserve balance sheet]], rather than through underlying productive growth.[1]

## Description

Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] applied the term to the entire eight-year duration of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s presidency, during which the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] kept interest rates extremely low and never judged the economy healthy enough to normalize rates or begin reducing its holdings.[1] He argued the American economy was not genuinely in "intensive care" for that whole span, and that policymakers instead chose to keep the economy propped up so that the administration's policies would appear beneficial.[1]

Carlson likened the stimulus dynamic to taking a double espresso after three hours of sleep: effective in the immediate term but impossible to sustain, since one cannot live on six Red Bulls a day and three hours of sleep indefinitely.[1] Eventually, he wrote, rates must be returned to a normal and sustainable level while the balance sheet is drawn down, ideally in a slow and methodical manner that stays within the economy's pain threshold rather than triggering collapse.[1]

## Later usage

Reviewing the fifteen years to April 2023, Carlson concluded that the only periods of genuine progress toward stabilizing and lowering the balance sheet were [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s pre-COVID presidency and the year of interest rate hikes under [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]]; the rest of the time, in his phrase, "the economy has lived on pixie sticks and Red Bull."[1] He characterized Powell's tightening as the equivalent of a parent taking a credit card away from a spoiled child who has no sense of what it means to earn money.[1]
