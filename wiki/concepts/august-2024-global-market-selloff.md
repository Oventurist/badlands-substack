# August 2024 Global Market Sell-Off

The **August 2024 global market sell-off** was a sharp, synchronized decline in equity markets that peaked on Monday, August 5, 2024, when the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1,033.99 points, or 2.6%, to close at 38,703.27.[1] The Nasdaq Composite lost 3.43% to finish at 16,200.08 and the S&P 500 slid 3% to end at 5,186.33, with the Dow and S&P 500 both registering their largest single-day losses since September 2022.[1] Japan's stock market posted its worst drop since Wall Street's Black Monday of 1987, amplifying fears of broader global turmoil.[1]

## Causes

Contemporary reporting identified fears of a United States recession as the principal driver, triggered by a disappointing July jobs report released the previous Friday that showed weaker-than-expected job growth and a rising unemployment rate of 4.3%.[1] Investors also expressed concern that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] had fallen behind in cutting interest rates to cushion a slowing economy, the central bank having voted the previous week to hold rates at their highest level in two decades.[1] United States futures mirrored losses across Europe and Asia as the rout extended through Monday.[1]

## The "fighting a ghost" critique

Investor [[cole-smead|Cole Smead]], chief executive of Smead Capital Management, told CNBC that the Fed had little room to manoeuvre because it was still contending with the after-effects of extraordinary fiscal stimulus, which had distorted readings of the real economy.[1] "The Fed is trying to fight a ghost in some respects. The ghost is a massive amount of federal spending on deficit, 7% of U.S. GDP, and it's very hard to counteract a problem like that, that you didn't create," Smead said, adding that [[jerome-powell|Jay Powell]] was doing all he could but that the underlying difficulty was fiscal rather than monetary.[1]

## Political framing

Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] labelled the decline "the Kamala Crash," linking it to the ascension of Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] as the presumptive Democratic presidential nominee.[1] "Of course there is a massive market downturn. Kamala is even worse than Crooked Joe," Trump wrote on social media, adding that markets would "NEVER accept the Radical Left Lunatic that DESTROYED San Francisco and California" and predicting "THE GREAT DEPRESSION OF 2024!"[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media commentary argued that the United States had in substance been in recession for a considerable period and that the official designation had been avoided by redefining the metric, a pattern the outlet describes as the regime gaming statistics for narrative benefit.[1] The commentary directed most criticism at the Federal Reserve for delaying rate cuts, while accepting that Trump's "Kamala Crash" branding had merit given Harris's roles as AI czar and border czar.[1] It also noted [[jim-cramer|Jim Cramer]]'s Friday advice not to panic — recalling his infamous defence of Bear Stearns — and his suggestion that investors prefer gold to cryptocurrency.[1]

A second commentary, by Burning Bright, framed the episode in civilizational terms, describing a financial order built on leverage and usury as one of the "great lies of the False Reality," and arguing that the accelerating cycle of wars and monetary resets had been halted by Trump and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]. The piece concluded that decentralization and the sovereign individual offered the only exit and that the dying system's "death throes" should not be mourned.[1]
