# New York Ban on Cameras in Courtrooms

New York State maintains among the most restrictive laws in the United States barring cameras and broadcasts inside courtrooms in most proceedings, a statutory regime dating back to the 1930s that the state Legislature has barely modified since.[1]

## Scope and comparisons

According to a 2022 report by The Fund for Modern Courts, a nonpartisan nonprofit, only Washington, D.C. is more stringent than New York on media coverage inside the courtroom.[1]

## Trump arraignment and reform push

The rule drew renewed attention with the April 2023 arraignment of former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Manhattan Criminal Court, a proceeding that attracted more cameras to Manhattan than perhaps any case in state history — though not inside the courtroom itself.[1] Amid the high-profile appearance, some state lawmakers mounted a renewed push to change the law.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that while cameras are barred from federal courtrooms, they are permitted in most state courtrooms, and suggested that dismissing the indictment against Trump might not be the optimal outcome if a televised state trial could instead be secured.[1]
