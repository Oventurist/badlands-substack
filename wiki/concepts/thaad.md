# THAAD (Terminal High Altitude Area Defense)

THAAD is the advanced United States terminal-phase ballistic missile defense system whose interceptor inventory became a focus of controversy after the Iran war.[1] According to reporting cited in May 2026, the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] assessed that the United States fired more than 200 THAAD interceptors during the conflict — roughly half the American inventory — along with large numbers of SM-3 and SM-6 naval missiles.[1]

Analysts argued the war exposed a major vulnerability in Western missile-defense production capacity, since systems such as THAAD and Patriot are expensive and slow to replace.[1] Critics contended that the expenditure strained US readiness for other potential conflicts, particularly in the Indo-Pacific, while supporters maintained that the joint missile-defense effort prevented far greater regional escalation and civilian casualties.[1] [[israel|Israel]] was reported to have conserved more of its own interceptor stockpile during the same period, a claim Israeli officials denied while saying operations were coordinated closely with Washington throughout.[1]

## Saudi co-production, 2024

In February 2024 [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] signed agreements enabling Saudi Arabian companies to manufacture parts of the THAAD system, announced on the sidelines of the World Defense Show in Riyadh.[2] Lockheed said the sub-contracts would enhance manufacturing capabilities in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and transfer expertise to strengthen the kingdom's defense industry, while state-owned [[saudi-arabian-military-industries|SAMI]] signed eleven agreements at the same event and [[qatar|Qatar]] was also reported to be manufacturing parts.[2] Badlands commentary treated the localization as evidence that unipolar hegemony had ended, observing that the Biden administration had removed the THAAD systems [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] deployed to Saudi Arabia in 2019.[2]

## October 2024 deployment to Israel

On October 13, 2024, the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] confirmed that Washington would send an advanced THAAD battery to Israel to protect the country in the event of an Iranian response to an expected Israeli reprisal attack, along with roughly 100 American soldiers to operate it.[3] Pentagon spokesman Maj. Gen. [[pat-ryder|Pat Ryder]] said Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] authorized the deployment at the direction of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and that the system would bolster Israel's air defenses following Iranian missile attacks in April and October 2024.[3] Ryder framed the move as underscoring "the United States' ironclad commitment to the defense of Israel."[3] Tehran appeared to threaten the U.S. troops to be stationed in the region.[3]

Badlands commentary played down the deployment's significance, noting that the United States had been sending THAAD batteries to Israel and Arab states since 2019, including one dispatched with troops in October 2023.[3] Contemporaneous reporting described THAAD as a complementary system to the Patriot that defends a wider area, capable of hitting targets at ranges of 150–200 kilometers (93–124 miles), with each battery consisting of six truck-mounted launchers, 48 interceptors, radio and radar equipment, and requiring 95 soldiers to operate.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that no serious analyst would read 95 soldiers as preparation to invade Lebanon, observed that the roughly 40,000 other U.S. personnel in the region were mostly shipboard and on routine deployments, and said he found no evidence of any U.S. troops mobilized for offensive action on fronts bordering Israel — calling the narrative a "nothing burger" aimed at villainizing [[us-central-command|CENTCOM]] and General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]].[3]
