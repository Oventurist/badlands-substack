# THAAD

THAAD (Terminal High Altitude Area Defense) is a United States Army missile-defense system designed to intercept short-, medium-, and intermediate-range ballistic missiles during their terminal phase. In the Badlands corpus it appears as the centerpiece of the May 2026 reporting on American interceptor expenditure during the Iran war.

## Expenditure during the Iran war

According to reporting cited in the May 22, 2026 Badlands Brief and attributed to [[the-washington-post|The Washington Post]], the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] assessed that the US fired more than 200 THAAD interceptors — roughly half the American inventory — along with large numbers of SM-3 and SM-6 naval missiles while carrying much of the missile-defense burden for [[israel|Israel]] during the conflict with [[iran|Iran]].

Analysts said the conflict exposed a major vulnerability in Western missile-defense production capacity, since systems like THAAD and Patriot are expensive and slow to replace. Critics argued the war strained US readiness for other potential conflicts, especially in the Indo-Pacific, while supporters said the joint missile-defense effort prevented far greater regional escalation and civilian casualties. The episode also frames the criticism of President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s proposed [[golden-dome|Golden Dome]] system in the May 2026 Russia-China declaration.
