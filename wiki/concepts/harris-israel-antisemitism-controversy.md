# Harris Israel Antisemitism Controversy

The Harris Israel antisemitism controversy was a July 2024 political dispute in which Republicans accused Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] of embracing antisemitic protesters while progressives urged her to challenge Israel's conduct in its war with [[hamas|Hamas]].[1] The dichotomy made Israel an early and volatile test of Harris's ability to navigate a foreign policy issue that had stumped U.S. leaders for decades, arriving within days of her becoming the presumptive Democratic nominee.[1]

## The dispute

On the Republican side, Rupert Murdoch's New York Post lambasted Harris over her positioning in screaming headlines and a string of negative stories following her decision to skip Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s speech before the U.S. Congress on Wednesday, July 24.[1] "ISRAEL LEFT BEHIND," the tabloid's cover blared that morning above a photo of Harris.[1]

At the same time, left-flank Democrats strategized over how best to use their leverage on an issue resonant with coveted voting blocs.[1] Hatem Abudayyeh, chair of the U.S. Palestinian Community Network and spokesperson for March on the DNC, told POLITICO: "Every time we've talked in the past six months about the genocide and who's responsible and who's complicit and who has blood on their hands, we say 'Genocide Joe' and 'Killer Kamala.'"[1]

A person of color who had voiced measured support for pro-Palestinian demonstrators, Harris was described as particularly vulnerable to Republican charges of antisemitism.[1] She sought a compromising tone, delivering remarks that Thursday affirming her defense of Israel while condemning the treatment of civilians in Gaza — but, as POLITICO put it, "the middleground is a gamble."[1]

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] pressed the attack at a campaign rally in North Carolina, telling a booing crowd: "She's running away from Israel. Even if you're against Israel or you're against the Jewish people, show up and listen to the concept. But she's totally against the Jewish people."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], treated the episode as an encapsulation of Trump as the "Infinite Player" operating on a gameboard of his own making.[1] He argued that the middleground was a gamble precisely because Trump had made it one: by campaigning hard against Radical Islam in 2016, Trump drove a Democratic base already sympathetic to Middle Eastern populations, and traditionally anti-war, further into what he termed the "pro-Muslim" camp.[1] Trump then "juked them out of their cleats" by traveling to Saudi Arabia and taking part in the sword dance.[1]

In this reading, the true 2016 campaign issue was ISIS, the Syrian Civil War, and migrant displacement rather than Israel and Palestine, but Trump hyper-polarized the paradigm through cheeky rhetoric and then pivoted the axis onto the one subject enjoying near-unanimous congressional support, as demonstrated by the standing ovations for Netanyahu.[1] "Like Atlas moving the heavens," he wrote, Trump had upended the balance of power in Washington, leaving the Uniparty establishment unsure how to handle the hot potato while the Democrats faced the task with what he called the dumbest presidential candidate they had ever elevated.[1] His closing word was "Accelerate."[1]
