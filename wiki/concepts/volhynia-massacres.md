# Volhynia Massacres

The Volhynia massacres were a World War II-era campaign of mass killing in which up to 120,000 Poles, Jews, Roma and Armenians — including women and children — were killed by the nationalist Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA).[1] The killings are officially regarded in Poland as a genocide.[1]

## Contemporary political significance

The massacres remain a live issue in Polish–Ukrainian relations. In January 2025, [[law-and-justice|Law and Justice]] presidential candidate [[karol-nawrocki|Karol Nawrocki]] argued that Kyiv could have no future in the European Union or NATO until Ukraine takes responsibility for the Volhynia killings.[1] Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], visiting Poland, replied that if Ukraine were excluded from the EU and NATO and left without security guarantees, Nawrocki "should start training, because it may turn out that he will have to take up arms."[1] The exchange produced outrage in Poland and accusations from PiS politicians that Zelensky was interfering in the Polish presidential election.[1]
