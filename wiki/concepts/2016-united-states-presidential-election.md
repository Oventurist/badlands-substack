# 2016 United States Presidential Election

The **2016 United States presidential election**, won by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], is treated in [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]]'s [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series as a decisive turning point in the contest between the [[deep-state|Deep State]] and what the author calls a positive counter-elite.[1]

## The author's account

The author writes that one of his bigger regrets in life is that he did not vote in 2016, having then believed that all politics were akin to pre-scripted wrestling and that the animosity toward Trump was part of a bizarre New World Order theater.[1] He went to bed before the night was over; on waking, his landlord appeared mildly shocked while telling him Trump had won, and his first thought was that the polls had given Hillary Clinton a 90-plus percent chance of victory.[1] At a café that morning he found everyone shaken to the core, having believed in a version of reality that Trump's victory had destroyed.[1]

## Trauma thesis

The author, raised in an extremely left-leaning culture, argues the 2016 result was one of the most traumatic experiences left-leaning Americans have undergone, far exceeding the pain caused by the Bush victories of 2000 and 2004.[1] His explanation is that Bush's victory was subconsciously considered within the realm of possibility, whereas Trump's was presumed fundamentally impossible; the result therefore defied his acquaintances' sense of reality itself.[1] What drove the mass trauma response, he contends, was not that "their" candidate lost but that their sense of reality had been torn asunder.[1] A friend reported that television anchors openly said on air, "this has never happened before."[1]

## Black swan for the Deep State

Trump's victory is characterized as a black swan event for Deep Staters, demonstrating that he really was an outsider.[1] Though the author did not vote for Trump, he became a strong supporter in the weeks immediately after the election, having realized that mainstream animosity toward Trump was genuine rather than artificial.[1] He argues that the world saw the Deep State caught off guard perhaps for the first time in anyone's lifetime, and that the looks of horror on the faces of figures such as [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]] and [[stephen-colbert|Stephen Colbert]] hypnotically suggested to their audiences that Trump really was the monster they believed him to be — noting that facial expressions are subconsciously suggestive and that humans readily detect genuine fear in others.[1]

The author concludes that this contagious terror, spread to millions of mainstream-media viewers, indicates Trump represents an existential threat to the establishment's entire system: one does not lose face and unload all one's ammunition unless backed against a wall.[1] The mainstream reaction is therefore treated as the primary evidence that Trump poses a very real threat to the Deep State, and that a positive counter-force gained the upper hand in the ongoing [[fifth-generation-warfare|5G battle]].[1]
