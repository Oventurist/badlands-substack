# Kerry Committee Report

The **Kerry Committee Report** was the 1989 product of a two-year investigation by the Senate Foreign Relations Subcommittee on Terrorism, Narcotics, and International Operations, chaired by [[john-kerry|John Kerry]], into links between the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and drug trafficking during the 1980s.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes Kerry as a Yale alumnus and member of the Washington elite.[1]

## Findings

The investigation focused primarily on Central America but also touched on matters relating to [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[1] Its central conclusion was that U.S. officials frequently overlooked drug activities by allies when doing so served strategic interests — a pattern DeLarme argues applies equally to Afghanistan.[1] The report notes that "elements of the U.S. government were aware of the involvement of some of their allies in drug trafficking," yet took no significant action, prioritising anti-communist objectives instead.[1]

On the Central American front, the committee found that supporters, suppliers, and pilots of the [[nicaraguan-contras|Contras]] were deeply entangled in cocaine trafficking, often with the knowledge of U.S. officials who failed to act "for fear of jeopardizing the war efforts against Nicaragua."[1] It documented payments from the [[state-department|State Department]] to companies owned by known traffickers, ostensibly for humanitarian aid, even after some had been indicted — including roughly $806,000 directed to narcotics-linked firms for Contra supplies.[1]

The committee's findings were published under the title *Drugs, Law Enforcement, and Foreign Policy*, listed among the article's principal sources.[1]
