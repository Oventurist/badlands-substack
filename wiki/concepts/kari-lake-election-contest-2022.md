# Kari Lake Election Contest (2022)

The Kari Lake election contest was a 70-page lawsuit filed on the afternoon of Friday, December 9, 2022 by Republican gubernatorial candidate [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]], alleging that fraud and maladministration in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] invalidated the result of the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 Arizona general election]] for Governor.[1] The complaint was lodged with the Clerk of Court in [[maricopa-county-superior-court|Maricopa County Superior Court]].[1]

## Procedural setting

The Arizona general election was officially certified on Monday, December 5, 2022 by the sitting Secretary of State, [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] — who, as the Democratic candidate for governor, thereby certified her own election. [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] characterized this as "a total clown show over here in the wild, wild West."[1] Under Arizona law candidates had five days after certification in which to contest the results, making Friday, December 9 the deadline; several campaigns filed that afternoon.[1]

## Parties

Lake was the sole contestant and plaintiff. The defendants were Katie Hobbs, both personally as contestee and in her official capacity as Secretary of State; [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]] in his official capacity as Maricopa County Recorder; [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]], Clint Hickman, Jack Sellers, Thomas Galvin and Steve Gallardo in their official capacities as members of the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]]; [[scott-jarrett|Scott Jarrett]] in his official capacity as Maricopa County Director of Elections; and the Board of Supervisors itself.[1]

## Allegations

### Scale and margin

The complaint asserted that the number of illegal votes cast in Arizona's November 8, 2022 general election far exceeded the 17,117-vote margin, and that witnesses showed hundreds of thousands of illegal ballots had "infected" the election in Maricopa County.[1] Its framing was that the case was about restoring trust in an election process that county officials and Hobbs had shattered.[1]

### Machine and tabulator failures

The suit alleged that 59 percent of Maricopa County's 223 vote centers experienced printer and tabulator failures, disproportionately affecting Election Day voters, who broke for Lake by a ratio of three to one.[1] A cyber expert who had performed security testing for nine years for the laboratory that certified the county's machines was quoted as concluding that "machine failures Arizona voters experienced on Election Day could not have occurred absent intentional misconduct," and that "an unintentional widespread failure of this magnitude occurring could not arise absent intentional misconduct."[1] On a conservative estimate the suit put the resulting suppression of Lake's Election Day vote at between 15,603 and 29,257 votes.[1]

Contemporaneous texts from technicians hired by the county were cited — "I'm having a 911," "Tabulators aren't reading ballots. It's maybe 50/50," and "… line around building all day."[1] Against the Board of Supervisors' claim that the tabulation issues were fixed by mid-afternoon, the complaint said no fewer than 34 vote centers had tabulator breakdowns after 3 p.m. and throughout the day, and that the Board had tried to conceal how many vote centers had lines longer than ninety minutes.[1]

### Disenfranchisement

A poll worker testified that roughly 175 voters at his vote center gave up because of long lines and did not vote; a 68-year-old woman was described as unable to vote for the first time since 1981 because she could not stand in a two-to-three-hour line.[1] The account @Peoples_Pundit was cited for a finding that Republicans encountered difficulty casting a ballot at a rate of 58.6 percent against 15.5 percent for Democrats.[1]

### Signature verification

The complaint called signature matching "a joke." A 2020 review of 230,339 envelopes was said to have found 18,000 "egregious" mismatches — more than the entire 10,457-vote margin — and 19,631 that failed standards; in 2022, 4,328 of the same names from the egregious mismatch set "voted" again, as did 5,289 of the bad-standards names.[1] Whistleblower testimony described level-two managers "reversing and approving signatures" that had been rejected, with most of their work not subject to observer accountability, and rejected ballots re-run through processing because managers wanted them approved.[1] One witness said there was no way to know who placed "verified" stickers on ballots and that the system was "wide open to abuse"; the third-party contractor [[star-center|Star Center]] was said to have cured ballots off site with no observers present and without sight of the actual signature.[1]

### Chain of custody

Whistleblowers alleged that over 300,000 mail-in ballots had no chain of custody, leaving no way to determine whether they were legal ballots, and that "the required chain of custody for these ballots does not exist."[1] A whistleblower at [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] put the figure at 298,942 early ballots and said Maricopa County did not know how many ballots Runbeck received on election night — conduct the suit described as against the law.[1] Runbeck employees were said to have been unlawfully allowed to add family members' ballots into batches with zero chain of custody, such that "there is no way to tell the number of ballots illegally injected into 2022 election," and the vendor was described as able to print duplicate ballots at will without traceability.[1]

An observer described ballots inside containers going uncounted, ballots delivered without required documentation, and temporary employees moving unsecured metal carts full of ballots without security or monitoring, concluding that "the entire ballot transfer process provides opportunities for legal ballots to be lost or illegal ballots to be added."[1] The complaint further alleged that uncounted "[[door-3-ballots|Door 3]]" ballots were co-mingled with already-tabulated ballots and that "there is good reason to believe that the number of Door 3 ballots is far greater than the 16,724 that Maricopa County claims," and that the county added 25,000 ballots to its totals after Election Day.[1]

### Censorship and conflict of interest

The suit alleged that Hobbs and Richer colluded with the federal government to censor Americans and infringe their [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] rights, with Hobbs complaining even about a "private" Facebook post stating that Trump had won.[1] It emphasized that Hobbs oversaw an election in which she was a candidate down ten points, administered by a County Recorder who had founded a PAC whose sole mission was to stop Lake and [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]].[1]

### Prayer

The complaint concluded that "the maladministration and illegal votes in Maricopa County caused the State of Arizona to wrongfully name Hobbs as the candidate who received the most votes in the election of Governor of Arizona" — summarized by Gillespie as "KARI WON."[1]

## Reception and companion suits

[[michael-patrick-leahy|Michael Patrick Leahy]] of [[star-news-digital-media|Star News Digital Media]] discussed the suit and its assigned judge on Steve Bannon's *War Room*.[1] The Lake filing was one of three: [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]] refiled his own contest after an earlier suit with the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] was dismissed as premature, and Finchem filed jointly with [[jeff-zink|Jeff Zink]] against [[adrian-fontes|Adrian Fontes]], [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]] and Hobbs.[1] [[blake-masters|Blake Masters]], notably, filed nothing.[1]

Gillespie predicted that the Arizona courts would reject the case at every level until it reached the Supreme Court, where she believed a precedent might be set to overturn results from 2020 and 2022 up and down the ballot.[1]
