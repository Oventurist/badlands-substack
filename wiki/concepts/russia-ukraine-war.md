# Russia-Ukraine War

The Russia-Ukraine war is the armed conflict between Russia and [[ukraine|Ukraine]] that escalated into full-scale war in 2022 and dominated Western foreign policy through the 2024 United States election cycle.[1]

## NATO expansion as cause

Badlands Media holds that expansion of [[nato|NATO]] toward Russia's borders is "widely accepted as the reason" for the war, which makes President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s repeated boasts during his July 2024 interview with [[george-stephanopoulos|George Stephanopoulos]] — that he personally "expanded NATO" — an effective admission of responsibility for starting it.[1] Biden also claimed in the same interview that he had "shut down [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]," a statement the commentary called fundamentally untrue and jarring in light of the expansion admissions.[1] On this reading, the administration provoked Russia and its recklessness indirectly caused the deaths of hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians.[1]

## Battlefield situation, mid-2024

Contrary to media framing that Ukraine was winning or could win, Russian forces were making significant territorial gains by mid-2024.[1] An Associated Press headline on July 4, 2024 reported: "Ukraine's army retreats from positions as Russia gets closer to seizing strategically important town," referring to fighting around Chasiv Yar.[1]

## Manpower and foreign fighters

Facing a dwindling military, the Ukrainian legislature passed a bill permitting the conscription of prisoners.[1] Ukraine also drew on foreign volunteers and mercenaries, recruited in part through viral video campaigns by units such as the [[third-separate-assault-brigade|3rd Separate Assault Brigade]], an offshoot of the [[azov-battalion|Azov Brigade]].[1] A [[new-york-times|New York Times]] investigation into [[chosen-company|Chosen Company]], an American-led international unit, documented allegations that members executed wounded and surrendering Russian soldiers.[1]

## Political dimension

Membership in NATO remained Ukraine's central war aim, but at the [[2024-nato-washington-summit|2024 NATO Washington summit]] Kyiv was again refused both membership and a timeline, on grounds of insufficient anti-corruption reform.[1] Under [[article-5|Article 5]], admitting Ukraine during active hostilities would place the alliance automatically at war with Russia.[1] The conflict also weighed on European politics: the 2024 French legislative elections that produced a hung parliament were expected to have far-ranging implications for the war.[1]

## December 2023 ceasefire signals

In December 2023 the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] reported that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had been quietly signalling openness to a ceasefire, even as he publicly insisted Russia's war goals were unchanged.[2] Buoyed by Ukraine's failed counteroffensive and flagging Western support, Putin told his generals that Russia's invading troops were doing "what we want," pledging "We won't give up what's ours" and adding dismissively, "If they want to negotiate, let them negotiate."[2] Yet through back-channel diplomacy he had been signalling since at least September, via intermediaries, that he would accept a ceasefire freezing the fighting along current lines — far short of his ambitions to dominate Ukraine — according to two former senior Russian officials close to the Kremlin and American and international officials who received the message from his envoys.[2] American officials said Putin had also floated a ceasefire a year earlier, in the autumn of 2022 after Ukraine routed Russia's army in the northeast, indicating satisfaction with captured territory and readiness for an armistice.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] characterised the coverage as "the end-stage of Overton's Goalpost" (see [[overton-window|Overton window]]), arguing that what the mainstream media framed as negotiation was nothing of the sort: Russia sought recognition of the de facto annexed Donbas territories already under its military control as sovereign Russian Federation territory.[2] He noted the reporting followed the same media apparatus conceding that Russia had clearly won its fight with the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]], a claim citizen journalists had advanced from observable reality for over a year.[2] In his framing, what Putin was said to be negotiating for was simply Western acknowledgement of an already-actualized victory.[2]

## Second year of the war (February 2023)

On Monday, February 20, 2023, U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] made a surprise visit to Kyiv, walking the streets of the Ukrainian capital and promising to stand with Ukraine "as long as it takes."[3] Biden, in his trademark aviator sunglasses, walked side by side with President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], in green battle fatigues, to a gold-domed cathedral on a bright winter morning pierced by the sound of air raid sirens.[3] From Kyiv the president flew on to Poland, where he was scheduled to deliver a speech on how the United States had rallied the world in support of Ukraine and to stress American backing for [[nato|NATO]]'s eastern flank.[3]

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was due to deliver a major address the following day setting out his aims for the second year of the invasion, making the two speeches a matched pair of statements of war objectives at the conflict's first anniversary.[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] treated the Kyiv trip as the single most "boneheaded, tone deaf, ill-conceived, infuriating, and embarrassing" thing Biden could have done on Presidents' Day, noting that it took place "to the din of unnecessary air raid sirens" while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] travelled to Ohio in the aftermath of the East Palestine derailment.[3] The juxtaposition, he argued, made the contrast of "America Last versus America First" as stark as possible.[3]

## Trump–Putin call of May 19, 2025

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] spoke by telephone for more than two hours on Monday, May 19, 2025, primarily about the Ukraine conflict.[4] Trump said the conversation "went very well" and that talks between Moscow and Kiev would begin "immediately," while Putin described the call as "very useful."[4] In a post on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] shortly afterward, Trump wrote that the tone and spirit of the conversation were "excellent," adding, "If it wasn't, I would say so now, rather than later."[4]

"Russia and Ukraine will immediately start negotiations toward a Ceasefire and, more importantly, an END to the War," Trump stated. "The conditions for that will be negotiated between the two parties, as it can only be, because they know details of a negotiation that nobody else would be aware of."[4] Badlands commentary noted that the [[vatican|Vatican]], as represented by the Pope, had said it would be very interested in hosting the negotiations, with contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writing that Trump had chosen the Vatican as the stage for a meeting with Putin.[4]
