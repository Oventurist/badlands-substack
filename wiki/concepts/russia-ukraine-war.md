# Russia–Ukraine War

The **Russia–Ukraine War** began when [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] initiated what Russia termed a "special military operation" in Ukraine on 24 February 2022.[1] From the outset of the conflict the United States made it immediately clear that it would take primary responsibility for funding and arming Ukraine, a posture that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued has never been adequately justified to the American taxpayer.[1]

## The unanswered questions

DeLarme framed the war around two persistently unanswered questions: why the American taxpayer should care who owns what land in Eastern Europe, and why Putin decided to invade in the first place.[1] He argued that U.S. funding of the war unquestionably harms Americans economically, while the manner in which it serves the public interest remains unclear.[1] As with Iraq and Afghanistan, he contended, the public is offered only the explanation that best suits the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] and the U.S. Security State.[1]

The article rejected the premise that Putin — described by [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and every subsequent president as rational — suddenly became a psychopath overnight and attacked Ukraine for no reason, with plans to march across Western Europe attacking [[nato|NATO]] countries next.[1] Propagandized real-life supervillains, DeLarme wrote, do not spring up out of nowhere; there are always motivating factors, and those factors are almost always suppressed before they reach Western audiences.[1]

## The suppressed-context argument

As an analogy, the article cited the [[osama-bin-laden|Bin Laden]] letter, which circulated on TikTok until the U.S. government pressured the platform to remove it.[1] The letter conveyed a side of the story rarely allowed to see daylight; one need not agree with its position to recognize that it ran counter to the narratives of the U.S. Security State.[1] Muslim extremist cells across the Middle East did not simply "hate us for our freedoms," as George W. Bush claimed, but acted in response to decades of U.S.-sponsored killing, regime change and destabilization in the region.[1]

DeLarme credited [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], [[oliver-stone|Oliver Stone]], [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]], [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] and the late [[gonzalo-lira|Gonzalo Lira]], among others, with making an omitted perspective visible.[1] That alternative picture shows a nation that watched U.S.-sponsored regime change in neighboring countries despite promises to the contrary, followed by arms shipments to those neighbors and insistence that NATO would expand to include Ukraine — a country occupying Russia's most sensitive border, one that played a critical role in how Russia was attacked in both World Wars.[1]

## Attrition, manpower and the draft

The article held that it was well known to anyone not relying on major networks that this was a war Ukraine could not hope to win, coming down largely to the number of soldiers on each side and the vast gap between the two nations' artillery production capabilities.[1] For that reason Ukraine began pleading with Western supporters to enlist and fight rather than merely virtue signal with Ukrainian flags in profile pictures.[1] Remarkably few Americans heeded the call, which DeLarme attributed to the reasonable position that most people do not care who controls what land in Eastern Europe given domestic troubles at home.[1]

Desperation for manpower led [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], already working with a vanishingly small conscripted army, to sign legislation lowering the draft age to 25 in April 2024.[1] Even so, the article argued, a lowered draft age would not sustain the war effort much longer nor beat back the Russians; if Ukraine joins NATO — which it "pretty much has to do" to keep the one-sided war of attrition going — it could be the United States military shipping off to the front lines.[1]

DeLarme observed a sad irony in that many of the same people who plastered social media with Ukrainian flags are now young people serving in the military, and noted the global consensus that the U.S. military is "quickly becoming the softest fighting force on earth," juxtaposed against the training of Russian Spetsnaz forces.[1] With recruitment at an all-time low, he suggested the U.S. military in its current form could become the cannon fodder Zelensky requires.[1]

## Interpretive frames

The article closed by offering two lenses on the wider conflict. The first treats all current and brewing conflicts as fragments of one great war — readable as BRICS and BRICS-aligned nations against Western hegemony, though DeLarme called that a surface-level interpretation, preferring a framing of "country-first" representatives fighting what Winston Churchill called the "High Cabal."[1] The second holds that the events are theatre of war designed for mass population awakening.[1] Whether one is in the black-pilled doomer camp or the "patriots are in control" camp, he argued, the conflict is worth dissecting, because each earth-shaking event in the war of stories jars more people awake — much as COVID-19 did.[1]
