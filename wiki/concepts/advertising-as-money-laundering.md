# Advertising as Money Laundering

"Advertising as money laundering" is a claim advanced by Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in his September 17, 2024 essay "The Media is the Enemy of the People," holding that a substantial share of corporate advertising spending exists not to sell products but to move and legitimize illicit funds while buying influence over coverage.[1]

## The argument

Carlson's reasoning rests on the unmeasurability of advertising's effects.[1] Billions of dollars poured into advertising may or may not influence sales, and because no one can reliably quantify the return, the category becomes what he calls a perfect vehicle for laundering.[1] He applies the same reasoning to any market where value is subjective, citing modern art — which he says the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] created as a laundering instrument — and offering [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s paintings and a banana duct-taped to a wall as illustrations of prices detached from worth.[1]

The funds requiring laundering, in Carlson's account, come from two streams: money created out of nothing by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], and the proceeds of what he describes as the world's largest gun running, drug running and human trafficking operation, which he attributes to the CIA.[1] Laundering that money through media has the additional benefit of controlling the population, which he called a win-win.[1]

## Corporate and platform examples

Carlson states that 75% of broadcast television advertising revenue comes from [[big-pharma|Big Pharma]], and argues that an industry already holding the government and medical community "in their back pocket" does not need that exposure to sell drugs — it needs to control what is said about it, making it "the media's biggest sugar daddy."[1]

He extends the thesis to technology platforms, calling the notion that social media firms earn their revenue from advertising a farce and asserting that the bulk of the money comes from the CIA.[1] [[google|Google]] and [[facebook|Facebook]], he writes, exist because the agency built and funds their technology; advertising is cover, in the way that the car wash launders Walter White's methamphetamine income on the television series *Breaking Bad*.[1] The earnings of [[youtube|YouTube]] stars are given the same explanation.[1]

## The Twitter test case

Carlson treats [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s acquisition of [[twitter|Twitter]] as a natural experiment.[1] Musk immediately lost roughly 60% of the platform's advertising, and subsequent revelations showed how deeply the [[fbi|FBI]] and CIA had been involved in moderating, shadow banning and removing users.[1] Carlson asks whether advertisers withdrew on their own or whether the CIA simply stopped routing money through companies it controls once the agencies were expelled.[1]
