# 2023 United States Credit Rating Downgrade

The 2023 United States credit rating downgrade was the decision by ratings agency [[fitch-ratings|Fitch]] on Tuesday, August 1, 2023, to cut the US government's top credit rating from AAA to AA+.[1] Fitch cited an expected fiscal deterioration over the next three years together with a high and growing general government debt burden.[1] The dollar ticked lower following the announcement.[1]

## Background

The downgrade arrived two months after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and the Republican-controlled House reached a debt ceiling agreement following months of political brinkmanship, a deal that lifted the government's $31.4 trillion debt ceiling and suspended the debt limit until January 2025.[1] Fitch nonetheless judged that "there has been a steady deterioration in standards of governance over the last 20 years, including on fiscal and debt matters," notwithstanding that June bipartisan agreement.[1]

## Reaction

Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] said she disagreed with the downgrade, calling it "arbitrary and based on outdated data."[1] [[white-house|White House]] Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] said it "defies reality to downgrade the United States at a moment when President Biden has delivered the strongest recovery of any major economy in the world," and blamed Republican "extremism" — from cheerleading default to seeking deficit-busting tax giveaways — for threatening the economy.[1]

Investors use credit ratings to assess the risk profile of companies and governments when they raise financing in the debt capital markets, which made the downgrade a signal with practical market consequences rather than a purely symbolic rebuke.[1]
