# 2025 Israel-Syria Security Talks

The 2025 Israel-Syria security talks were negotiations between Israel and Syria's post-Assad government that could lead to a peace agreement between the two nations, but which by late June 2025 had struck a significant obstacle over Israeli military freedom of action inside Syrian territory.[1]

## The sticking point

Two sources familiar with the details told the Jerusalem Post that the administration of [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] had made clear that under any agreement Israel would not be permitted to operate or strike targets within Syrian territory.[1] Israel, for its part, insisted on retaining the ability to address security threats, particularly in scenarios where — as demanded by the Damascus administration — Israel withdraws from the buffer zones.[1]

## Israeli position

Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]], who in preceding months had described the Syrian president as a "terrorist in disguise" and warned against falling for his charm, said on June 30, 2025 that "Israel is interested in expanding the circle of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]]."[1] He named Syria and Lebanon as neighbours Israel seeks to include while safeguarding its security interests, and emphasized that "Israel applied its laws to the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] over 40 years ago, and in any peace agreement, the Golan will remain an inseparable part of the State of Israel."[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]] was never supposed to retreat so quickly and that al-Jolani was not supposed to become president overnight, with relative peace breaking out in Syria.[1] In his reading, a protracted conflict between Assad and al-Jolani would have drawn attention away from the IDF, which had faced sustained PR damage since the Israeli response to the [[october-7-attacks|October 7th attacks]], and chaotic warfare would have let [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] justify a large-scale IDF presence in Syria — potentially a cover for infrastructure enabling annexation of Syrian territory, as was done with the Golan Heights.[1]

He described discomfort at watching a former ISIS and Al Qaeda figure put on a suit and play politics while subordinates called for the implementation of Shariah law by local governments.[1] With [[donald-trump|Trump]] lifting [[syria-sanctions-relief|sanctions on Syria]] and Israel being asked to accommodate the former insurgents, he asked how neoconservative advocates could object, since regime change had been their objective.[1] He concluded that al-Jolani's demand for IDF withdrawal, combined with a record Saudi arms package and rumoured relief of weapons sanctions on Turkey, was eroding Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|Qualitative Military Edge]] and producing stronger, more cooperative neighbours rather than the regional empire Netanyahu envisioned.[1]
