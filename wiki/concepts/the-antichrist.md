# The Antichrist

**The Antichrist** is, in Christian theology as summarised by the Badlands Media essay "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?", a figure primarily associated with the New Testament and frequently conflated with [[the-beast|the Beast]] of Revelation 13.[1]

## Distinction from "anti-Christ"

The article distinguishes the Antichrist as a specific figure from the general condition of being "anti-Christ" described in 1 and 2 John, while noting the Antichrist would undoubtedly also be anti-Christ.[1]

## Pauline description

Paul describes the "son of lawlessness," interpreted as the Antichrist, as an individual who opposes God, exalts himself, and performs deceitful signs and wonders to deceive the people, but whose power is temporary.[1] Paul emphasises that believers must remain steadfast in faith, knowing the ultimate victory belongs to God and the Antichrist is defeated.[1]

## Old Testament readings

The concept is not explicitly mentioned in the Old Testament, but the article identifies passages some interpret as describing the figure.[1] Daniel's visions present a beast and oppressive ruler who defiles the sanctuary, replaces sacrifices with abomination, corrupts many by flattery, speaks blasphemies, worships a foreign god, and prospers until the appointed time of wrath, before the Ancient of Days removes his dominion and grants the everlasting kingdom to the saints (Daniel 7:19–27, 9:24–27, 11:31–45).[1] Ezekiel addresses the prince of Tyre, a mere man who exalted himself as a god and whose pride, wealth, violence, sin, and corruption bring about his destruction at the hands of foreign nations (Ezekiel 28:1–19 NKJV).[1]

## Interpretive stance

Both New and Old Testament descriptions reference believers present in the world as the events unfold, a point the article uses against pre-[[the-tribulation|Tribulation]] departure readings of [[the-rapture|the rapture]].[1] The author declines to identify the Antichrist with any particular person, holding that scripture's personifications are likely attempts to describe the spiritual nature of Satan and that there are many human faces.[1]
