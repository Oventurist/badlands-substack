# Judiciary Act of 1789

The Judiciary Act of 1789 is the federal statute by which [[congress|Congress]] established the federal district courts and their judgeships, creating the lower federal judiciary that the Constitution left to legislative discretion.[1]

## Constitutional basis and the abolition argument

The Act became a focal point of Badlands commentary in March 2025 amid conflict over Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]]'s order blocking deportations under the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]].[1] Because Article III, Section 1 of the Constitution gives Congress the power to "ordain and establish" inferior courts, the argument advanced was that Congress retains authority to modify or even abolish them through legislation: the Constitution mandates only the Supreme Court, leaving the structure and existence of lower courts to the legislature.[1] On that reading, Congress could reorganize the federal judiciary, reduce the number of district courts, or eliminate them entirely and shift their jurisdiction elsewhere — to state courts or a restructured federal system — without violating the Constitution, since the power to create such courts implies the power to alter or undo them.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] paired this argument with the observation that judicial impeachment had occurred roughly fifteen times in American history — "not nearly enough, but not without precedent" — and concluded by asking whether, beyond impeaching bad judges, the country should also dissolve bad courts.[1]
