# EU Code of Practice on Disinformation

The Code of Practice on Disinformation is a voluntary European Union agreement under which online platforms commit to measures aimed at stamping out disinformation.[1]

## Twitter's withdrawal

In late May 2023, [[twitter|Twitter]] withdrew from the code, a move announced by EU Internal Market Commissioner [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]].[1] Breton wrote that "Twitter leaves EU voluntary Code of Practice against disinformation. But obligations remain," adding: "You can run but you can't hide. Beyond voluntary commitments, fighting disinformation will be legal obligation under #DSA as of August 25. Our teams will be ready for enforcement," a reference to the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[1] Twitter had not confirmed the withdrawal at the time of reporting, which characterized it as the latest move by owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to loosen the reins after his purchase of the company.[1]

## Commentary

[[johnny-q|Johnny Q]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], argued the exit was a significant step away from "Orwellian, 1984-like censorship," restoring Europeans' ability to express themselves and question a corrupted media, and enabling free debate.[1] He described the [[european-union|EU]]'s immediate threats of consequences as a display of fear and argued the bloc was underestimating both European citizens and Musk, concluding that while Twitter's overhaul remained incomplete, standing up to the EU gave "hope and credence to voices everywhere."[1]
