# The Influence of Sea Power Upon History

*The Influence of Sea Power Upon History, 1660–1783* is the 1890 book by [[alfred-thayer-mahan|Alfred Thayer Mahan]] that established naval supremacy as the organizing principle of modern geopolitics.[1] It was published in the same year as [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]]'s *[[cosmopolitan-railway|The Cosmopolitan Railway]]*, and [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats the two works as the manifestos of the opposing paradigms then contesting America's future.[1]

Ehret describes the book as a total break from the spirit of win-win cooperation that had defined American foreign policy to that point, arguing throughout its pages that America's destiny was to succeed the British Empire by rejecting rail development and adopting British colonial techniques.[1] Mahan took over the British imperial definition of "commerce," in which free trade functions as cover for the military domination of weaker nations, on the reasoning that open borders and the abandonment of protectionism make a people easier to rob.[1] From this he argued that the United States could no longer adhere to the "outdated" [[monroe-doctrine|Monroe Doctrine]], because a new order of world empires demanded another guiding philosophy if America was to avoid extinction.[1]

The historian Francis P. Sempa wrote that the book soon "became the bible for many navies around the world," with the Kaiser of Germany demanding that all of his officers read it — a development that followed the 1890 ouster of [[otto-von-bismarck|Otto von Bismarck]] as German Chancellor.[1] [[theodore-roosevelt|Theodore Roosevelt]] subsequently ordered copies for every member of Congress.[1]
