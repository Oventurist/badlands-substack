# Denaturalization

Denaturalization is the legal process by which the United States government strips a naturalized citizen of American citizenship. In June 2025 the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] issued a memo directing that denaturalization be prioritized in cases where naturalized citizens are charged with crimes.[1]

## The 2025 DOJ memo

The memo directed department attorneys to pursue "civil denaturalization" in cases involving "war crimes," "extrajudicial killings," "human rights abuses," and for those "convicted of crimes who pose an ongoing threat to the United States," as well as "terrorists."[1] It instructed that "the Civil Division shall prioritize and maximally pursue denaturalization proceedings in all cases permitted by law and supported by the evidence."[1]

## Scope of the affected population

Of the 46.2 million immigrants living in the United States in 2022, 24.5 million — 53 percent — were naturalized citizens, according to a Migration Policy Institute analysis of government data, indicating the potential breadth of the policy's reach.[1]

## Background and commentary

Badlands commentators noted that a U.S. citizen may lose citizenship in specific cases, including committing an act of treason against the United States as a naturalized citizen facing denaturalization for certain crimes.[1] They observed that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had created a dedicated DOJ section focused on denaturalizing criminals and enemies of the country in February 2020, and framed the 2025 memo as a continuation of that effort under an "America First" rationale — that a person cannot obtain citizenship and then work to destroy the country from within.[1] Another Badlands contributor raised the question of whether the American citizenship of dual citizens could likewise be revoked.[1]
