# Ultra-Millionaire Tax Act

The Ultra-Millionaire Tax Act is proposed federal legislation, reintroduced by Senator [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]] (D-MA) on Tuesday, March 19, 2024, that would impose an annual tax on the net worth of the wealthiest American households.[1]

## Provisions

Under the bill, the federal government would collect 2 percent annually from households with a net worth between $50 million and $1 billion, and 3 percent annually from households worth more than $1 billion.[1] A companion bill was introduced in the House of Representatives by Representatives [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]] (D-WA) and [[brendan-boyle|Brendan Boyle]] (D-PA).[1]

## Scope and debate

Warren has said the measure would apply to roughly the wealthiest 100,000 households in the country, approximately 0.05 percent of the population, and framed the levy as asking successful Americans to "pitch in two cents" on each dollar above the $50 million threshold.[1] She invoked President Biden's argument that tax loopholes allow Jeff Bezos to pay a lower effective rate than a public school teacher.[1]

Opponents, including commentary published by [[zerohedge|Zero Hedge]] and republished in the Badlands News Brief, characterized the bill as an attempt to normalize the confiscation of privately held assets, warning that once such a tax is accepted at the top brackets, its extension to lower wealth thresholds becomes politically feasible.[1]
