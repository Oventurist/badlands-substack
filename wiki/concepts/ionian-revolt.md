# Ionian Revolt

The Ionian Revolt was the rebellion of the Ionian city states of Anatolia — referred to collectively as Phrygia — against the [[persian-empire|Persian Empire]] between 499 and 493 BC.[1] In [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s November 2025 essay "Don't Be Symbol-Minded," the revolt is presented as the origin point of the [[phrygian-cap|Phrygian cap]] as a symbol of freedom and honor, before the cap was later appropriated by the mystery cults.[1]

Ehret placed the revolt in a wider republican moment that included Solon's defeat of the oligarchy of Athens, the Battle of Marathon in 490 BC, and the flowering associated with Aeschylus, Sophocles, Socrates and Plato — the period he credits with producing the best of what is sometimes called western civilization.[1] He described the revolt as having been sabotaged, after which Anatolia was reabsorbed under the control of the Persian Empire, characterized as the "Marcher Lord of Babylon" managing the global [[mystery-cults|mystery cults]].[1] Only then, he argued, were the deities Mithras and Cybele-Attis, newly outfitted with the Phrygian cap, infused into the governance of the defeated Ionian population.[1]

In the essay's conclusion Ehret held that the principles animating the Ionian rebellion of 499 BC, the leading intelligentsia around [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] in 1776, and the fight led by [[china|China]] and its allies today are identical in principle, though not in form.[1]
