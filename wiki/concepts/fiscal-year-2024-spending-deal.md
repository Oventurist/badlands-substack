# Fiscal Year 2024 Spending Deal

The fiscal year 2024 spending deal was a topline funding agreement announced by House and Senate leaders on Sunday, January 7, 2024, described as the first step toward averting a government shutdown later that month even as the threat still loomed.[1]

## Terms

The topline numbers agreed to by House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] and Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] provided $1.59 trillion for fiscal year 2024, comprising $886 billion for defense spending and $704 billion in non-defense spending.[1] The leaders also agreed to a $69 billion side deal in adjustments directed toward non-defense domestic spending, which a Democratic source told [[cnn|CNN]] brought the non-defense figure to nearly $773 billion and overall spending to close to $1.66 trillion.[1]

## Remaining obstacles

While agreement on funding levels reduced the chances of a shutdown, [[congress|Congress]] still faced major hurdles ahead of two funding deadlines, January 19 and February 2.[1] Among them was how to handle demands by conservatives to use the funding bill as leverage to impose more stringent immigration and border security requirements.[1]
