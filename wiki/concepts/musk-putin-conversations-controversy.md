# Musk–Putin Conversations Controversy

The Musk–Putin conversations controversy arose in late October 2024 after the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]], citing several current and former US, European, and Russian officials, reported that [[spacex|SpaceX]] chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had been in "regular contact" with Russian president [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] since 2022, discussing personal topics, business, and geopolitical tensions.[1]

## National security dimension

The reporting raised national security questions that highlighted the US government's dependence on SpaceX.[1] SpaceX holds contracts with the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]], which relies on the company to place its satellites in orbit, and multiple contracts with [[nasa|NASA]], which depends on its rocket launches to carry astronauts to space and resupply the International Space Station.[1] [[axios|Axios]] framed the dilemma as a choice between continuing a relationship with Musk despite the liability he might pose, or severing ties and losing access to services the government relies on.[1] Musk had acknowledged while campaigning for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at a Pennsylvania town hall earlier that month that he holds a "top-secret" security clearance.[1]

## Calls for investigation

NASA administrator [[bill-nelson|Bill Nelson]] told Semafor on October 25, 2024, that such calls "would be concerning" if true, particularly for NASA and the Defense Department, and said the report should be investigated without specifying by which agency.[1] The same day, Rep. [[adam-smith|Adam Smith]], the top Democrat on the House Armed Services Committee, told Politico the Defense Department should investigate whether Musk's alleged actions ran "to the detriment" of national security.[1] A growing number of Democratic lawmakers joined the push within a day of the Journal's story.[1]

## Response

Musk did not respond to requests for comment from Business Insider.[1] The official SpaceX account on X, in a statement Musk reposted, called the Journal's report an "incredibly misleading story" built on "completely unsubstantiated claims from unnamed sources."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the episode as another storyline in what Badlands calls [[trumpamania|TrumpaMania]], following Musk's earlier public spat with Nicolás Maduro, and used it as an occasion to attack NASA's leadership and institutional credibility while praising the engineers at SpaceX and Tesla.[1]
