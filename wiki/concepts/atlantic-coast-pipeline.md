# Atlantic Coast Pipeline

The Atlantic Coast Pipeline was a proposed natural gas pipeline intended to carry West Virginia fracked gas all the way to Virginia Beach, serving as the sister project to the [[mountain-valley-pipeline|Mountain Valley Pipeline]].[1] Planning began during the Obama administration, and the project was spearheaded by [[dominion-energy|Dominion Energy]].[1]

## Cancellation

The pipeline faced sustained legal attacks that Badlands commentary attributed to [[george-soros|George Soros]] and allied environmental litigants, and it was officially cancelled in the summer of 2020.[1] The Trump administration's Energy Secretary, Dan Brouillette, lamented that the obstructionist environmentalist lobby had used lawfare to double the project's cost to $8 billion, effectively killing it.[1]

In the same reporting, Dominion Energy announced it would sell all of its natural gas operations to [[berkshire-hathaway|Berkshire Hathaway]], Warren Buffett's company, which Badlands commentary said ensured the pipeline project was never revived — prompting speculation about what role Buffett may have played behind the scenes in the original obstruction.[1]

## Aftermath

Two years later the New York Times, which Badlands characterized as having cheerled the obstruction, reported with apparent incredulity that natural gas prices in Virginia had doubled and that electricity prices were rising sharply as utilities such as Dominion Energy shifted investment from natural gas toward more costly electric grid buildout.[1] The land later leased for the [[commonwealth-fusion-systems|Commonwealth Fusion Systems]] plant is held by Dominion Energy, connecting the pipeline saga to Virginia's next energy chapter.[1]
