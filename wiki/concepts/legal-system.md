# Legal System

In the Badlands Media framework advanced by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], the legal system is sharply distinguished from the law itself.[1] The law, in this account, is the set of rules that govern all of reality: natural laws such as gravity and electromagnetism, and moral laws governing fairness, ethics, contracts, trust, duty and stewardship.[1] These laws are said to have existed before mankind and to persist after it, so that man lives "beneath" the law and cannot invent it.[1] The legal system, by contrast, is created by man to bring order and stability to human civilization.[1]

## Analogies used

Deschamps offered a series of analogies: the law is the game of baseball while the legal system is little league; the law is the idea of a perfect hamburger while the legal system is one man's attempt at making one; the law is Elvis Presley while the legal system is an Elvis impersonator.[1] The legal system is thus described as "a piece of non-material technology" designed to coordinate social systems.[1]

## Rules, order and harmony

The essay illustrates ordering power with a vibrating plate of sand: without vibration the particles are disordered, but under vibration they form coherent shapes, just as law brings order to whatever is subject to it.[1] Rules govern games, games coordinate human behavior, and ten people on a basketball court without rules produce only awkwardness until they agree on rules and form teams.[1] By this reasoning the legal system produces harmony — though, the article stresses, possibly a bad harmony, depending on who designed it.[1]

## Capture by an elite

The central claim is that the legal system was designed by cheaters, fraudsters and manipulators who rigged it to benefit themselves at everyone else's expense: a giant game whose rules are drawn from real laws of the universe but arranged to serve an occulted elite.[1] Because it is man-made, the legal system varies by jurisdiction in the way house rules differ between a father's home and an uncle's — variation the article attributes to the freedom God gave mankind to govern itself.[1] Trouble arises when most people neither understand the law nor participate in making it, leaving the system to advantage those who know how to use it.[1] Federal tax administration is offered as the example: the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] produces extremely complex tax codes that only insiders can navigate to minimize what they pay, a rigged design *ab initio*.[1]

The article situates the theological grounding of law alongside this critique, arguing that for most of human history the prohibition on murder rested on a right to life granted by a god-like being, and that despite the efforts of secular humanists every legal system on earth still rests on the belief that law comes from God while the justice system is man's attempt to carry out God's will.[1] The founding fathers, in recognizing this, are credited with creating one of the best nations on earth.[1]

## Relation to the rule of law

The legal system as it exists today is explicitly said not to be a [[rule-of-law|rule of law]] system.[1] Deschamps announced that a following installment would show it to be a [[color-of-law|color of law]] system instead, and would argue that the ultimate weapon against the cabal is for the people to reclaim knowledge of the law and build a true rule-of-law society — a project he claimed [[donald-trump|Trump]] and the patriots were preparing the world for.[1]
