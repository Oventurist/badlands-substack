# Softwar (Jason Lowery)

*Softwar: A Novel Theory on Power Projection and the National Strategic Significance of Bitcoin* is a book by United States Space Force Major [[entities/jason-lowery|Jason Lowery]] that treats [[concepts/bitcoin|Bitcoin]] proof-of-work as a new, non-kinetic domain of strategic competition rather than merely a financial technology.[1]

## Core argument

Lowery's thesis, as quoted in Badlands Media commentary, asks readers to imagine that instead of trying to replace physical power with abstract power, people accept that there is no substitute for physical power and instead seek to replace kinetic power with electric power.[1] If a non-kinetic and therefore non-lethal form of warfare could still enable global-scale strategic power competitions to settle disputes, manage resources, and establish a pecking order, Lowery argued, humanity "might be able to break the cycle."[1]

## Reception in Badlands commentary

A Badlands Brief essay marking fifteen years of the Bitcoin blockchain used Lowery's framing to argue that the transition to this new war-fighting domain is already underway and that physical retribution — arrests, military tribunals, and executions at [[entities/guantanamo-bay|Guantanamo Bay]] — would be meaningless if the underlying monetary system could simply be reconstituted a few generations later.[1] The essay paired Lowery's argument with a 2014 remark by [[entities/julian-assange|Julian Assange]] that the cryptography underlying Bitcoin can "create situations that can defend itself or the people who use it, against even the full might of the superpower."[1]

## Softwar versus Hardwar

An April 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] described Softwar as "a new form of war doctrine" released through the thesis work of United States Space Force Major Jason P. Lowery, and distinguished it from its predecessor, "Hardwar," on the grounds that it is fought primarily on the digital battlefield rather than the physical one, while retaining physical elements and considerations within the system.[2] Force projection under Softwar therefore occurs in a non-kinetic manner.[2]

The essay's author, an aerospace system safety engineer by profession, treated the thesis as strengthening the case for building on top of [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] regardless of other considerations, and emphasised the significance of a serving [[united-states-space-force|United States Space Force]] major laying out an accessible argument that Bitcoin is a national strategic priority — a "weapon of peace" against all enemies, foreign and domestic, with [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] counted among them.[2]

## Application to AI-generated data

The same essay extended Lowery's framework to the hazard of AI-generated text, audio and video overwhelming humanity's ability to distinguish truth from fabrication, arguing that the key mitigation is possession of something the AI does not have: a mechanism capable of imposing "severe physical costs" on AI bot farms.[2] Bitcoin, in this reading, associates bits of data with an auditable energy cost through micro-transactions, producing an immutable record of which data has "paid the cost" — trivial for humans, prohibitive for AI at scale.[2] The author noted that using Bitcoin to defeat bot farms is neither new nor exclusive to Softwar, citing [[michael-saylor|Michael Saylor]], [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] as having described comparable scenarios.[2]
