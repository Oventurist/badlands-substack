# 2025 US–EU Trade Deal

The 2025 US–EU trade deal was a trade agreement struck on July 27, 2025, between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]], which led the [[european-union|European Union]] to suspend planned countermeasures against U.S. tariffs.[1]

## Suspension of EU counter-tariffs

EU officials announced on Monday, August 4, 2025, that the bloc would suspend its planned countermeasures against U.S. tariffs for six months.[1] The retaliatory tariffs had been set to take effect that week and were placed on hold while both sides worked to finalize a joint statement formalizing the agreement.[1] A spokesperson for the EU Commission said the deal aimed to restore "stability and predictability for citizens and businesses on both sides of the Atlantic," and confirmed that the suspension of countermeasures was expected to be in place by Tuesday.[1]

## Terms

The delay marked a notable concession from one of the United States' largest trading partners after weeks of negotiations.[1] The broad trade agreement Trump announced the previous month includes 15% tariffs on most European imports to the United States, including automobiles.[1] In exchange, the White House said the EU agreed to eliminate all tariffs on U.S. industrial exports to Europe, commit to $750 billion in U.S. energy purchases, and increase investment in the United States by $600 billion beyond existing levels.[1]
