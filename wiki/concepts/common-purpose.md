# Common Purpose

Common purpose, in the framework of the [[nature-of-civilizations|Nature of Civilizations]] series by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], is the shared belief, creed or motto that allows the social layers of a civilization to act in a coordinated way.[1] The February 24, 2023 essay "The Steering Wheel of Civilization" argues that historically the civilizations that thrived were those with a clear common purpose, producing reliable participation in the population.[1]

## Definition and function

Common purpose does not mean perfectly uniform opinions shared by all people.[1] It means only that enough shared ground exists to produce coordinated action. The essay illustrates this with voters for [[george-w-bush|George Bush]] in 2000, who did not perfectly agree with one another but shared enough common ground on the question of whom to vote for that they acted as one, and with participants in [[world-war-i|the Great War of WWI]], who joined for unique yet similar enough reasons to produce coordinated mass action.[1]

Reliable behavior generated by common purpose is described as essential to large-scale initiatives — farming and food storage for an entire country, war efforts, city building and irrigation systems.[1] Every recognizable feature of civilization, including roads, large buildings, supply chains and shipping schemes, is presented as a product of reliable participation and common purpose.[1]

## Group identity and social inclusion psychology

The family is offered as the universally recognizable model: members believe they are kin, share a surname, look similar and live together, all of which activate what the essay calls social inclusion psychology — the sense of belonging that members of a properly formed social group feel.[1] This sense of belonging is described as so important that most people are driven to preserve it whether or not they are aware of doing so, which makes it usable by good and bad actors alike and explains why [[identity-politics|identity politics]] is among the more reliable methods for maintaining in-group preference.[1]

## Decline of organic community

The essay argues that roughly 100 years ago, traditional methods of controlling self-governing groups were supplanted by a mass media propaganda machine that replaced close-knit in-groups with disintegrated social groups.[1] Organic groups are naturally self-directed, like children playing hide and seek, but mass media destabilized organic communities and made the steering mechanism of family units increasingly dependent on media.[1] Social media is said to have escalated this further by breaking up families, with a future metaverse virtual reality system expected to deepen the separation again.[1]

Where people were once grouped by familial association, religious affiliation, race and creed, knew one another and kept business within the community, the modern tribe is based on social media and internet clicks, offering little benefit beyond a feeling of inclusion — a condition the essay argues makes populations far easier to control by those with influence over online tribal associations.[1]

## Manufactured and restorative common purpose

The essay notes that the goals of the [[parasitic-elite|parasitic elite]] — described as the so-called Illuminati, cabal, globalists and [[new-world-order|New World Order]] types — include ends most people would never willingly support, which creates the need for mass mind control techniques developed by the [[shadow-men|Shadow Men]].[1] [[color-revolution|Color revolution]]-style propaganda tactics are identified as an effective means of generating common identities in social groups, usually deployed insidiously for elite benefit but, the author contends, potentially usable for good when a population is lost in self-deception.[1]

Historical and contemporary examples are given. [[roman-empire|Rome]], both before and after the empire, maintained a common purpose across a melting pot of cultures, classes and socio-religious groups by grafting a Roman identity onto all of them, sustained by [[mystery-schools|mystery schools]] and metropolitan life, and enforced through conquest, violence and slavery.[1] In the present day the essay describes arguably positive groups working with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] under the banner of a [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] or America First ideological foundation as seeking to restore a benevolent political identity to the United States and the world, with Trump and increasingly [[qanon|Q]] credited with reigniting American patriotism.[1]
