# Indictment of John Bolton

The **indictment of John Bolton** is the 18-count federal criminal case brought against former national security adviser [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] in October 2025, in which the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] alleged that he systematically shared classified information with people who were not authorized to read it while writing a tell-all book.[1] The charges were filed the day before the October 20, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] discussed them, and Bolton pleaded not guilty in court the following morning.[1]

## The Atlantic's assessment

Writing in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] under the headline "The Bolton Case Is Not Like the Others," the magazine urged readers not to add Bolton's indictment to what it called the growing pile of specious prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s enemies without first reading the government's allegations.[1] The piece described the indictment as detailed and precise, compiled by experienced prosecutors rather than "political lackeys," and as relying on Bolton's own words to implicate him.[1]

The Atlantic conceded that readers should question whether the charges would have been brought if Trump were not president, noting that officials in [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s administration had passed on the chance to charge Bolton and that Bolton had ample basis to argue he was singled out as one of Trump's most voluble and persistent critics.[1] It nevertheless argued that political animus does not by itself make the charges baseless, and drew a sharp distinction between the Bolton case and the prosecutions of former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] and New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] — cases the magazine described as so weak that a U.S. attorney resigned rather than present them to a grand jury, and which career prosecutors reportedly told his replacement the government would probably lose at trial.[1] People knowledgeable about the internal deliberations over whether to charge Bolton with mishandling classified information told the magazine that the decision had not been an easy call, but that the resulting case was "righteous."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that while the indictment would have actual effects on the battlespace, its biggest benefit to Trump's justice campaign was psychological: because Bolton is a "former Trump ally," the move functioned simultaneously as [[narrative-shielding|narrative shielding]] and as a warning shot signalling that nobody is safe — "Friends close. Enemies closer."[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] connected the indictment to remarks Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] made to [[andrew-napolitano|Judge Napolitano]] four days before the charges were filed, in which Macgregor said it was [[sheldon-adelson|Sheldon Adelson]] who convinced Trump to bring Bolton into the first administration.[1] He read this not only as signal but as proof of "drafting" — Trump using the inertia of the NeoCon and Zionist agenda to advance the white hat plan.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] inverted The Atlantic's framing, asking "what if the others are just like the Bolton case?"[1] She characterized the Comey and James charges as "starter charges" and predicted superseding indictments that could add both new charges and new defendants based on new evidence, arguing that the administration should take note of any government employee who argues to abandon public abuses rather than prosecute them.[1] She also asked who the U.S. attorney was who resigned rather than prosecute Comey — a reference to [[erik-siebert|Erik Siebert]] — and questioned why The Atlantic appeared to be rewriting the Siebert story relative to its original headline.[1]
