# French Connection

The French Connection was a heroin smuggling scheme through which the drug was moved from Indochina through Turkey to France and then to the United States and Canada.[1] The operation started in the 1930s, reached its peak in the 1960s and was dismantled in the 1970s; at its height it was responsible for the vast majority of the heroin used in the United States.[1]

## CIA involvement alleged

Badlands Media writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argued that since the end of the Second World War the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] had been profiting from drug trafficking, and that in the French Connection era the agency was protecting the Corsican mafia in Marseille, France, which controlled the global heroin trade at the time.[1]

## Supply from the Golden Triangle

The Corsican mafia and the CIA obtained their opium supplies from the [[golden-triangle|Golden Triangle]].[1] When France lost the Indochina war, the French Connection lost control of that supply, and in the Badlands account the CIA needed the U.S. military to step in to protect it — the underlying motive for American entry into the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam War]] and for the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war in Laos]].[1]

## Early CIA involvement

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] places the French Connection at the start of the newly created [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s dealings with organised crime.[2] Founded in 1947, the agency wasted no time cutting deals with the Corsican mob in Marseille to keep the docks free of law enforcement; heroin began flowing into the United States, and in DeLarme's assessment the agency's fingerprints were all over it.[2] He treats the arrangement as a direct continuation of [[operation-underworld|Operation Underworld]], the wartime bargain struck by the [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]], and as an early chapter in the pattern that would culminate in the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]].[2]
