# Continuity Amendment

The "continuity amendment" is a proposed constitutional amendment, floated by a bipartisan group of House members in 2024, that would allow members of [[congress|Congress]] to be replaced quickly in the event of a mass-casualty event.[1]

## Proposal

As reported in a September 19, 2024 *[[washington-post|Washington Post]]* article quoted at length by Badlands Media, four House members — two Republicans and two Democrats — argued that Congress must ensure it can continue to govern after mass violence.[1] The article asked readers to imagine "a mass shooting of members of Congress that leaves a large swath of the country unrepresented and shifts the balance of political power in Washington," and cited as motivating events the 2017 attempted assassination of members at a congressional baseball practice, the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol, a sharp rise in threats against members, and a possible second assassination attempt against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Representative Kilmer was quoted saying the amendment would also deny a would-be assassin the power to flip control of the House from one party to another, with any interim appointee serving only until a special election could be held.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing in October 2024, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] rejected the stated rationale, asking whether anyone really believes these politicians are worried about the mass murder of congressmen.[1] In his reading, what they actually fear is the [[mass-arrests|mass arrests]] of politicians and the question of how government would function afterward.[1] Carlson argued that the members understood Trump would be behind such arrests and that the overwhelming majority of those arrested would be [[deep-state|Deep State]] never-Trumpers, so that rapid replacement would ensure Deep State appointees remained in place to fight Trump's agenda.[1] Without such replacements, he wrote, the situation would resemble "a Patrick Mahomes-led offense going up against a defense with no players."[1]
