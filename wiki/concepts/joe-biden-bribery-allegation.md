# Joe Biden Bribery Allegation

The Joe Biden bribery allegation refers to congressional Republicans' claim that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was personally involved in a bribery scheme connected to his family's foreign business dealings, a claim that House investigators said in mid-2023 they expected to be able to prove.[1]

## Comer and Jordan on Hannity

During an appearance on Fox News Channel's "Hannity" on Monday, July 31, 2023, Rep. [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-KY), chairman of the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]], and Rep. [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-OH), chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], indicated they would be able to "prove" the allegations.[1] Host [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] asked both men whether this was now officially "the Joe Biden bribery allegation" and whether they could prove it; Comer answered, "I sure hope so. And I do believe that there's a lot of smoke. And where there's smoke, there's fire. We just heard testimony today that Joe Biden had lied to the American people."[1]

Asked whether the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] had been helping to cover it up, Jordan replied, "Well, it sure looks that way," and pointed to two dinners at the Cafe Milano restaurant in Washington, D.C., which Biden attended in full on both occasions.[1] One of those dinners, Jordan said, included Elena Baturina, "this Russian — the wealthiest woman in Russia, worth over $1 billion," a fact he characterized as "a different story than what he's been telling us all along."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the stage appeared set for Biden's departure, and raised whether he would leave sooner or later, whether he would stay in the race long enough to earn an impeachment, and whether a Newsom/Whitmer ticket would be pushed in line with [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel's]] then-recent prediction — naming [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] as the replacement pairing.[1] His central claim was that "Biden is finished."[1]

DeLarme argued that the more important question was [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], holding that a significant portion of Americans on both sides of the aisle suspected Joe had been a proxy for Obama, and that removing Biden would not address "the root problem."[1] He suggested the "brand" [[hunter-biden|Hunter]] was selling was not his father's, and that Biden was unlikely to have had the standing within "the democrat mafia" to threaten to withhold a billion dollars from Ukraine on his own — but that Obama did.[1] Noting Obama's return to the headlines, he hoped for more scrutiny of the Obamas in coming months, since "sleepy Joe is not the big fish we're looking for," and closed with Obama's own reported line: "Never underestimate Joe's ability to #$%& things up."[1]

## CelticCapri Corp and the 2017 tax returns

In June 2023, Republican lawmakers demanded transparency from President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] regarding his 2017 tax returns, in which his entity [[celticcapri-corp|CelticCapri Corp]] listed nearly $10 million without specifying revenue line items — raising the question of who paid the entity and for what, in the wake of Biden's alleged link to a $5 million Ukrainian "bribery" scheme.[2] Before the 2020 election, USA Today had published a fact-check supporting claims that the Bidens earned roughly $15.6 million from speaking fees and book deals between 2017 and 2019, with more than $10 million attributed to Biden's memoir *Promise Me, Dad* and $3 million to Jill Biden's book.[2]

[[the-federalist|The Federalist]] argued that the sourcing for the $10 million figure led only to Biden's campaign website with disclosure links to individual returns and no S-corporation returns, leaving readers "a smokescreen" — the perceived opacity that prompted lawmakers to press for details on the passthrough entity.[2]

## Badlands commentary (June 2023)

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] contrasted the coverage of the bribery allegations with that of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s indictment, writing that the mainstream media had put in "zero effort" reporting on Biden while devoting extensive screen time to Trump's [[trump-classified-documents-case|classified documents case]].[2] He argued that Trump's conduct was shielded by the [[presidential-records-act|Presidential Records Act]] while Biden stood accused not only of pay-to-play and bribery schemes but of using the [[fbi|FBI]] to cover them up.[2]

DeLarme further asserted that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] had admitted in his own words to giving half of his salary to his father for the previous three decades, and that at least 459 documented crimes — including money laundering and tax fraud — appeared on the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]], which the FBI had held since December 2019 and, in his view, knowingly covered up.[2]
