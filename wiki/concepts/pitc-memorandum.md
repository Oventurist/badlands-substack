# PITC Memorandum

The PITC memorandum is a March 2015 presidential memorandum issued by President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] that established a White House information technology director and an Executive Committee for Presidential Information Technology (PITC).[1] The directive was created by executive action following a fall 2014 foreign cyberattack that breached an unclassified network serving the Executive Office of the President, amid reports of two separate hackings believed to be tied to the Russian government in late 2014 and spring 2015.[1]

## Content

The memorandum established the president's "exclusive control" over information resources provided to the president, the vice president and the Executive Office of the President.[1] It further made clear that any records sent to EOP systems, or records stemming from those systems, are controlled by the president.[1]

## Relevance to the Trump documents case

In January 2024 [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]], a conservative nonprofit headed by former Trump adviser [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]], shared with the *Washington Examiner* a six-page Freedom of Information Act request seeking records about the "secretive" committee, arguing the little-known directive "may be relevant" to former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s liability in his criminal classified documents case.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the episode showed Trump using "the weapons of the enemy against them," with an Obama-era policy turned around to define and codify presidential authority over classification, declassification, and information access relating to foreign interference and national security threats.[1] He described the pattern as "National Essential Function-y," consistent with the [[devolution|Devolution]] thesis, and noted the timing of such disclosures amid the [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] prosecution as evidence of long-range planning.[1]
