# Invisible Enemy

**The Invisible Enemy** is a term used within the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] corpus to describe the adversary that the outlet's writers hold responsible for the wars, financial crises, and information operations they cover — an adversary defined precisely by its refusal to appear as a named state, army, or political party. In [[badlands-blitz-episode-11|Badlands Blitz Episode 11]], subtitled "Iran, NATO and the Invisible Enemy," hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] use "the spiraling narrative of the Iran War to map the strategic pincer that appears to be in the offing by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] against the true Invisible Enemy."

## Meaning in the corpus

The qualifier "true" is doing significant work in that formulation. The argument implied by the episode is that the publicly designated enemy of a given conflict — in this case [[iran|Iran]], or whichever state the Western alliance and [[nato|NATO]] have currently framed as the aggressor — is a substitute target, and that the genuine antagonist is a transnational network that operates above and across the nation-states nominally at war. The enemy is "invisible" both because it is unincorporated, holding no flag or seat, and because it works through instruments — media narrative, finance, intelligence services, supranational institutions — that present themselves as neutral machinery rather than as a belligerent.

## Relation to fifth-generation warfare

The concept is inseparable from the corpus's treatment of [[5th-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], in which the decisive battlefield is perception and the combatants are frequently unidentifiable. Episode 11 pairs the Invisible Enemy framing with a warning about "the dangers of playing white hat-black hat games with the characters that orbit" [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on what the hosts call the fifth-generation game board. The two points are complementary: if the enemy is invisible by design, then confident public identification of who serves it and who opposes it is itself a form of exposure to manipulation.
