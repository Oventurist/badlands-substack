# Canadian Parliament Nazi Veteran Tribute

The Canadian Parliament Nazi veteran tribute was a September 2023 scandal in which members of Canada's House of Commons gave a standing ovation to a man who had fought in a Nazi division during the Second World War, during Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]]'s visit to Ottawa.[1]

## The incident and apology

Several days after the revelations, Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] said he was offering "Parliament's unreserved apologies" to the world.[1] "This is a mistake that deeply embarrassed Parliament and Canada," Trudeau said in a televised address ahead of his apology in the House of Commons.[1] He added that "all of us who were in this House on Friday regret deeply having stood and clapped, even though we did so unaware of the context."[1]

Trudeau characterized the ovation as "a horrendous violation of the memory of the millions of people who died in the Holocaust," saying it was deeply painful for Jewish people and also hurt Polish people, Roma people, LGBTQI+ people, disabled people, racialized people and the many millions targeted by the Nazi genocide.[1] He did not take questions from reporters and pointed blame at the House of Commons speaker.[1]

## Diplomatic fallout

Trudeau acknowledged that the incident, occurring during Zelenskyy's visit, struck a blow to Ukraine's public relations efforts as it sought to rally support against Russia, and that Moscow was using the debacle to its advantage in propaganda.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the affair as a moving "Overton's Goalpost" applied to what he called the Canadian Nazi Party: an initial denial that the House had praised an avowed Nazi; a diminishment that he was not one of the "really bad" Nazis; a qualification conceding he was SS and had committed well-documented atrocities against Russians and Poles; and a distraction stage arguing that raising the issue aids "the more evil side," the Russians.[1] Burning Bright listed a final "Admit" stage as still pending.[1]

## Immediate coverage and the Rota apology

Contemporaneous Badlands coverage recorded that Speaker [[anthony-rota|Anthony Rota]] apologized on Sunday, September 24, 2023, two days after introducing 98-year-old [[yaroslav-hunka|Yaroslav Hunka]] as a war hero of the [[first-ukrainian-division|First Ukrainian Division]] in the [[house-of-commons-of-canada|House of Commons]].[2] Rota stated that he had "subsequently become aware of more information which causes me to regret my decision" and stressed that neither his fellow parliamentarians nor the Ukrainian delegation had known of his plan in advance; Hunka was a constituent of his own district.[2] The apology was reported by [[associated-press|AP News]].[2]

[[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that the ovation from [[justin-trudeau|Trudeau]] and [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] was unsurprising given Hunka's service in a Third Reich unit accused of war crimes, and argued that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] "no longer cares about hiding what they truly are, and who they truly emulate."[2] Burning Bright called it "perhaps the most memetic event of the weekend," noting that the standing ovation for an avowed Nazi occurred "in front of the world" while Trudeau sent Zelensky home with hundreds of millions in fresh proxy-war funding amid an economic depression at home.[2]
