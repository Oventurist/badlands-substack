# Chester County Poll Book Failure

The Chester County poll book failure was an election administration breakdown during the November 2025 general election in Chester County, Pennsylvania, in which the poll books used to verify voters at polling places listed only registered Republicans and Democrats, omitting roughly 75,000 independent and unaffiliated voters.[1] County election officials responded by extending voting hours, keeping polls open until 10 p.m. after what was described as a disastrous start to election day.[1]

## Background

Under Pennsylvania procedure, a voter appearing at a new polling place for the first time must present identification and sign the poll book; in subsequent years the voter signs adjacent to the signature already on file.[1] Because independent and unaffiliated voters may not participate in Pennsylvania's spring primary — which is restricted to registered Republicans and Democrats — one plausible explanation advanced in reporting was that the county was operating from a primary poll book during a general election, although that explanation was never confirmed.[1] Chester County election officials promised to return a call to [[the-federalist|The Federalist]] on election day but never did.[1]

## Consequences

Voters whose names were absent could not be verified as registered and were required to cast provisional ballots.[1] Handling was inconsistent across precincts: some voters were turned away and told to come back later, while many others cast provisionals.[1] Chester County GOP Chairman Raffi Terzian told The Federalist that it was unknown whether voters who had been turned away all returned to vote, and that precincts ran out of provisional ballot supplies including secrecy envelopes and the stickers used to seal them.[1]

## Federal response

Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] confirmed in an interview with [[catherine-herridge|Catherine Herridge]] that 75,000 independent voters in Chester County had been omitted from the poll book, calling it "a massive fail" and saying that people could "expect that there will be follow-up action…on the failure to administer elections properly in multiple jurisdictions."[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode read as a setup in which Republicans would blame Democrats for the error and use it as a catalyst to introduce electronic poll books, naming the vendor [[knowink|KnowInk]].[1] She compared the dynamic to the aftermath of the 2000 hanging-chad controversy, noting that Republicans had also delivered universal mail-in balloting, motor voter registration, and electronic voting machines, and calling it "notable and unnerving" that the same drama was again being used to transform elections.[1] She grouped the story with contemporaneous reports on fake ballots in Fulton County and fake counts associated with Jasmine Crockett as the three main buckets of election fraud, concluding that if the episode represented a Republican "HAVA 2.0" plan rather than a genuine fix before the midterms, the appropriate response was to "fight, fight, fight."[1]
