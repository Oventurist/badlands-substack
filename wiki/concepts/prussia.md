# Prussia

**Prussia** was the dominant kingdom of the northern German lands and the political core around which the German Empire was assembled in 1871.[1] In Badlands analysis it functions as a key to the "enigmatic nature of Germany" since unification, a subject discussed on the program Breaking History.[1]

## Deep history

The history of Prussia is described as both convoluted and complicated, spanning centuries and layered with elitist occult intrigue traced back to the Order of the Teutonic Knights, who organized the Crusades.[1] It is also portrayed as deeply intertwined with both the modernization of Russia and the creation of the United States.[1]

In the American case, the House of Hesse supplied King George III with more than 30,000 elite mercenaries who were used to pummel George Washington during the first eighteen months of the American Revolution — until Washington adopted irregular warfare tactics on Christmas night 1776 and attacked the Prussians at Trenton while they slept, winning his first battle.[1]

## Unification and legacy

Before 1871, the German lands comprised roughly 300 kingdoms of various sizes under Prussian influence.[1] Minister-President [[otto-von-bismarck|Otto von Bismarck]] created the German Empire on behalf of King Wilhelm I of the House of Hohenzollern through the Schleswig, Austro-Prussian, and Franco-Prussian wars.[1] Prussian military thought, notably Helmuth von Moltke's doctrine of Maneuver Warfare, later shaped both Blitzkrieg and the Soviet methods used to defeat it.[1]

## The "good twin and evil twin"

Badlands commentary frames Germanic civilization as having a good twin and an evil twin.[1] German immigrants to nineteenth-century America are credited as essential to the development of the American steel industry and to the Industrial Revolution as a whole, with German engineering described as the best in the world because the culture of the German lands bred a meticulous and enterprising people.[1] The author suggests this is precisely why Central Europe was targeted for destruction through the wars of the past century.[1]

The Prussian thread is also invoked rhetorically in relation to Ukraine, where [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] is dubbed the "Prussian Proxy Prince."[1]
