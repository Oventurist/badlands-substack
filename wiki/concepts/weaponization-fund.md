# Weaponization Fund

The "weaponization" fund is the internal name for a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] initiative created under the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration to support investigations and legal actions tied to alleged political abuse by federal agencies during prior administrations.[1] The program was associated with inquiries into alleged misconduct involving federal law enforcement, intelligence agencies, censorship coordination, and politically motivated prosecutions.[1] It is understood to deal in part with restitution to targets of such abuse.[1]

## Reported shutdown

In June 2026, [[axios|Axios]] reported that the Trump administration was preparing to eliminate the fund, citing two anonymous sources.[1] According to that reporting, administration officials had come to view the effort as politically burdensome and legally difficult to sustain as attention shifted toward broader institutional reforms and budget priorities.[1] Critics inside and outside the administration reportedly argued that the initiative had created expectations of sweeping criminal accountability that proved difficult to deliver in court.[1] Supporters viewed the program as a symbolic commitment to confronting entrenched bureaucratic and prosecutorial abuse.[1]

The reported move came amid wider restructuring at the Justice Department and renewed debate within Trump-aligned circles over whether systemic reform or high-profile prosecutorial action should be the administration's primary strategy going forward.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the reporting outright, characterizing the claim that the fund was too hard to manage as "statist wishful thinking" resting on two anonymous sources.[1] She noted that leftist politicians around the country — including Senator [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]] and Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] — were discussing taxing weaponization disbursements at 100 percent, a measure she framed as an attempt to ensure that recipients such as [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] could not benefit.[1] Her expectation was that such maneuvers would push Trump to double the fund rather than scrap it, and she insisted that "there must be accountability."[1]
