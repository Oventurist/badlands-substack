# 2024 Nevada Republican Caucuses

The **2024 Nevada Republican caucuses** were held on Thursday, February 8, 2024, and were projected by CBS News as a win for former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who easily defeated challenger Ryan Binkley.[1]

## Split primary and caucus

Nevada held both Republican and Democratic state-run primaries on Tuesday, February 6, but Trump did not appear on the GOP primary ballot because the [[nevada-republican-party|Nevada Republican Party]] opted to hold its own caucuses, making the caucuses the only contest that would allocate the state's 26 delegates.[1] The dual-track arrangement gave Nevada Republicans a second chance to vote for their preferred candidate, this time with Trump on the ballot.[1]

Former United Nations ambassador [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] participated in Tuesday's primary rather than the caucuses and finished second behind the ballot line "none of these candidates," an embarrassing outcome for a candidate presenting herself as a serious challenger to Trump.[1]

## Turnout analysis

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the real signal in Nevada would be turnout rather than the foregone caucus result, since Trump faced no real challenger and was swiftly declared the winner.[1] Nevada had roughly 1.9 million active voters, of whom 559,743 were registered Republicans according to the secretary of state's office; only 42,702 voters participated in the primary Haley contested — 7.6 percent of registered Republicans and 2.2 percent of total voters — with Haley herself receiving 14,328 votes.[1] Ashe read those figures as evidence that most Republican voters intended to take the caucus route, and directed readers to The Brief podcast for the comparison once caucus numbers were in.[1]
