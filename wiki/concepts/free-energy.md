# Free Energy

**Free energy** is the term used in alternative-technology circles for devices or processes said to generate usable power without a conventional fuel input, and, by extension, for the broader claim that such technologies exist but have been suppressed. In a January 24, 2024 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] titled "Who Says Free Energy Isn't Real?", the [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] author argued that mainstream narratives asserting the impossibility of free energy do not survive logical scrutiny, and that the question belongs squarely within the [[digital-soldiers|Digital Soldier]] research program rather than outside it.[1]

## Argument from cosmology

The essay's central argument proceeds from cosmology rather than from engineering. The author asked how the laws of physics could "spring into existence" all at once in the [[big-bang-theory|Big Bang]], according to what prior conditions such an explosion could occur, and how matter and the logic governing it could emerge from genuine randomness in which nothing existed.[1] He contended that mainstream science cannot be entirely correct about the Big Bang and that a more fundamental origin than the singularity must be sought — including the question of where the singularity itself came from, assuming it exists at all.[1] If the universe genuinely "came from nothing," he reasoned, then the universe taken as a whole may be described as its own free energy source.[1]

The author suspected that this line of inquiry is rarely pursued precisely because answering it would reveal how free energy works, and that academic science will never produce a genuine model of how the universe emerged from the singularity so long as its benefactors do not want humanity empowered with accurate information about the nature of existence.[1]

## Argument from self-creation

A second argument, which the author described as a logical "checkmate" of academia, rests on reality theory. If multiple realities exist, he argued, then an ultimate reality comprising all sub-realities must also exist.[1] Reality cannot reach outside of itself to fetch the raw materials required to create itself; any "place" from which it drew such materials would itself be part of the universe, since separate realities are by definition inaccessible and undetectable, and anything capable of affecting our reality necessarily belongs to it.[1] The universe must therefore be one hundred percent self-created, and so must contain the potential for self-generation — which, in the author's formulation, is the same thing as free energy.[1]

From this he concluded bluntly that self-created energy *is* free energy, and that anything capable of generating matter from nothing qualifies as a free energy device; an accurate model of the "creation-from-nothing" process would put an investigator well on the way to building one, with everything else reducing to details.[1]

## Critique of establishment science

The essay anticipated that most working scientists and academics would reject the argument, and answered in advance that appeal to authority is not a real argument and appeal to consensus is a red herring, with only experimental or logical demonstration carrying weight.[1] The author distinguished real science from "establishment science," arguing that while there are many intelligent people in science and technology, the functionaries who run the field have no interest in new or innovative ideas even when they are viable on their face.[1] Real science, in his account, has no room for subjective opinion — one cannot decide that 2 + 2 equals anything but 4, and the rules of logic can only be discovered, not edited.[1]

He attributed the resistance to two causes: scientists are beholden to careers and funding sources, so that inconvenient truths are ignored, misrepresented and attacked; and human ego makes people unwilling to face the existential dread of updating a worldview, though the real danger lies in refusing to do so.[1] He further argued that the burden of proof falls on mainstream science to show why free energy is *not* possible, since science that refuses to entertain and systematically test possibilities is "nothing more than mere pageantry," and offered condolences to honest scientists prevented from pursuing genuine lines of inquiry.[1]

## Relation to the wider information war

The author framed the topic as continuous with, not a divergence from, the usual Badlands subject matter of Trump, [[devolution]], Q and [[5g-war|5G warfare]].[1] He described the conflict as a full-spectrum battle spanning the spiritual, cultural, political, economic and physical domains, in which control of energy and resources is a key component, and asked whether human energy needs could be met without the existing grid.[1] Invoking the Q instruction to "expand your thinking," he argued that Digital Soldiers must take everything into account, including the nature of existence itself.[1]

He stated his personal belief, based on years of research, that many solutions to vital problems — including cancer healing methods and energy technologies that would render the grid obsolete — have been deliberately hidden, misrepresented and slandered, while noting that he could not make definitive claims and was speaking as a matter of opinion and First Amendment right.[1] Politically, he observed that nothing could be more damaging to the establishment than information proving it to be lying while simultaneously undermining artificial dependence on the current system.[1] He expected either the promised [[the-storm|Storm]] and a massive cleanup of the country, or total infrastructure failure driven by hiring practices that devalue merit in favor of "equity" and by the collapse of useful education — in either case, self-sufficiency in energy and food would be desirable.[1] Spreading such truth, he concluded, hastens the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]], since "knowledge IS power" and no scenario exists in which it is preferable to be powerless.[1]

The essay directed readers to the book *[[a-practical-guide-to-free-energy-devices|A Practical Guide to Free Energy Devices]]* as a starting resource.[1]
