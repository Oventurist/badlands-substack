# 2026 Politico Stolen Election Poll

The **2026 Politico stolen election poll** was a survey highlighted by [[politico|Politico]] in May 2026 that found widespread distrust of the American election system across party lines, with large numbers of voters expressing concern that future elections could be "stolen."[1]

## Findings

According to reporting on the survey, only about 39% of respondents said without reservation that the 2020 election was not stolen, while roughly 40% said the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]] were unlikely to be stolen.[1]

The poll also found sharp partisan differences over what constitutes a stolen election.[1] Republicans were more likely to cite concerns about voter fraud, mail ballots, and election administration, while Democrats pointed to voter suppression, redistricting, court rulings, and access to voting.[1] Analysts argued the findings reflect a growing bipartisan erosion of trust in US electoral systems even as Americans fundamentally disagree on the source of the perceived threat.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the "source of the perceived threat" is irrelevant to the overall finding that a majority of Americans do not trust US elections, a situation she said could be called a crisis.[1] She recalled that after the 2024 election, when Democratic-aligned commentators raised their own stolen-election claims, Badlands supported investigating those allegations on the principle that inquiry into elections is always a good thing.[1] Her position was that elections cannot be real until there is honesty about how fake they have been, that the reasons Americans distrust elections vary but the net effect is the same, and that centralized and complex US elections rest on "a pinky promise of trust with people that have proven themselves wholly untrustworthy."[1] She added that the president knows this, and asked whether the public would figure it out anytime soon.[1] Related themes appear at [[election-integrity-army|the Election Integrity Army]].[1]
