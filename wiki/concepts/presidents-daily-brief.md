# President's Daily Brief

The President's Daily Brief (PDB) is the top-secret intelligence product prepared for the President of the United States and, by custom, made available to former presidents.[1]

## Revocation of Biden's access (March 2025)

In March 2025, Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] posted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had officially revoked former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s access to the President's Daily Brief.[1] Biden — who had barred Trump from receiving classified briefings after the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol by a pro-Trump mob — would no longer receive the top-secret intelligence, under a directive Trump first ordered begun in February.[1]

ABC News framed the move as the latest in Trump's retribution campaign against what he claims was "weaponization" of the justice and intelligence segments of the federal government under Biden and Democrats.[1]

## Related clearance revocations

Trump also ordered revoked the security clearances of former Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], former national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], former Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]], New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]], Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg, and attorneys Mark Zaid, Norman Eisen and Andrew Weissman.[1]

## CIA capture of the brief (1976)

Badlands Media argues that the PDB became an instrument of intelligence-community control over the presidency under [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]].[2] A CIA.gov biography quoted in the August 2023 article "Clowns in America" records that on becoming Director of Central Intelligence in 1976 Bush insisted upon direct access to President [[gerald-ford|Gerald Ford]], usually editing the President's Daily Brief himself or briefing at National Security Council meetings in order to convey the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s analysis directly to the president.[2] He brought Agency officers to brief Ford in person, a tactic that impressed the president and lifted morale at Langley.[2] Bush read the PDB avidly as [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s vice president and, as the 41st president, made a CIA briefing his first order of daily business, remarking that agency analysts "tell it like you see it" with "no policy axe to grind."[2]

Writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] called this "a huge deal": an agency whose code was "admit nothing, deny everything" had become a fixture of the president's most important daily briefing, elevating the CIA within the executive branch, giving it influence over every administration since, and accelerating the formation of a [[shadow-government|shadow government]].[2]

## The Obama PDB trap

In the same article, Badlands claimed the PDB was turned back against those who had weaponised it — "the perfect boomerang."[2] Drawing on QAnon drop 1991, Lange asserted that FBI analyst [[tonya-ugoretz|Tonya Ugoretz]] inserted evidence into President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s daily brief indicating that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s private server had given China access to national secrets, creating a "legal paper trail" in the official archives and answering the recurring [[qanon|Q]] question, "How do you introduce evidence legally?"[2] Because Obama and members of his administration were using the same server under pseudonyms — a fact later confirmed by a Department of Justice inspector general report cited by The Federalist — acting on the brief would have implicated them, so nothing was done.[2] The design of the trap was attributed to General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]].[2]

A related claim concerns FBI text messages: PJ Media reported that [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] and [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] discussed "apparent drama" involving one of Obama's presidential daily briefings on the same day in April 2014 that Obama fired Flynn as head of the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]], a coincidence Lange rejected.[2]
