# Honest Services Fraud

Honest services fraud is a federal anticorruption theory under which prosecutors charge public officials and their associates with depriving the public of the "honest services" owed to it. The doctrine was narrowed by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in a pair of rulings reported in May 2023 that discarded corruption convictions arising from a sweeping New York probe.[1]

## The 2023 rulings

In the case of [[joseph-percoco|Joseph Percoco]], a former aide to New York Governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]], the Court unanimously held that a $35,000 payment accepted from a real estate developer while Percoco was managing Cuomo's 2014 re-election campaign fell outside the statute, because he was not employed by the government at the time and therefore owed no honest-services duty.[1] NBC News described the decisions as a "double blow to federal prosecutors" that made corruption cases harder to bring.[1]

## Interpretation

The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s theory in these prosecutions treated the deprivation of "potentially valuable economic information" as actionable fraud, a standard the 9-0 Court rejected in favor of requiring a loss of money or property, stating that "the government cannot use vague fraud statutes to advance novel and sweeping theories in prosecutions of political actors."[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the outcome as a rare judicial limit on what she called the authoritarian executive branch and its "wholly corrupted justice department," while noting additional corruption cases still awaited the high court.[1]
