# Iowa Noncitizen Voter Challenge Ruling

The Iowa noncitizen voter challenge ruling was a federal court decision issued on Sunday, November 3, 2024 — days before the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 general election]] — permitting Iowa officials to challenge the ballots of 2,176 registered voters whom the state believed might not be U.S. citizens.[1]

## Background

Iowa Secretary of State [[paul-pate|Paul Pate]] said he was working to ensure the election's integrity when, on October 22 and after early voting had already begun, he instructed county auditors to act on a list derived from Iowa Department of Transportation records.[1] The [[american-civil-liberties-union|ACLU]] joined a group of naturalized citizens in suing Pate, a Republican, arguing that his decision wrongly prevented "numerous naturalized U.S. citizens" from voting with a regular ballot and that the twelve-year-old transportation list rested on outdated information likely to burden naturalized citizens and deter voting.[1]

## Decision

U.S. District Judge [[stephen-locher|Stephen Locher]], a Biden appointee, declined to grant the requested relief.[1] He noted that the Supreme Court had allowed Virginia to continue automatic purging of voters while state officials appealed a lower court decision temporarily blocking that program, and said the Supreme Court's guidance was for courts to "act with great caution before awarding last-minute injunctive relief into how Iowa officials handle election issues."[1] The passage Badlands identified as decisive read: "Whatever concerns plaintiffs might have about the nature and timing of Secretary Pate's letter, it would not be appropriate for the court to respond by granting injunctive relief that effectively forces local election officials to allow ineligible voters to vote."[1]

## Context and commentary

Republicans including [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] have claimed that significant numbers of noncitizen immigrants vote in U.S. elections; Axios reported there is no evidence such voting has ever occurred on any significant scale.[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] took the opposite view, accusing the ACLU, [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]] and the Biden–Harris [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] of working to keep ineligible voters on the rolls, and noted the timing alongside a widely criticized Iowa poll showing [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] leading in the state.[1] She predicted that the left would respond with claims of voter suppression.[1]
