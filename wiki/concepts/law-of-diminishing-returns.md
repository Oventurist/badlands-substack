# Law of Diminishing Returns

The "law of diminishing returns" is described in Badlands Media reporting as a pseudo-scientific doctrine advanced by the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]] economists [[john-stuart-mill|J. S. Mill]] and [[david-ricardo|David Ricardo]], and refuted by [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s economic advisor [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]].[1]

The supposed law presumed a deterministic devaluation of land over time as rents increased under a "law of exploitation" of the unfit by the "more fit".[1] In Carey's account, [[thomas-malthus|Malthus]] first assumed that cultivation always began on the richest soils, so that population growth forced recourse to inferior soils with steadily diminishing returns to labor; Ricardo then assumed that diminishing production must be accompanied by an ever larger landlord's share of a shrinking product; and Mill concluded that this was "the most important proposition in political economy".[1] The result was to present the growing inequality of the people of England as the working of "a great law established by the Creator for government of the human race", and a tendency toward the subjection of the masses — toward slavery — as a necessary result of divine institutions.[1]

Carey answered in *Unity of Law* (1872) that technological progress caused unproductive lands to become more productive over time, and that the power to support life increased rather than diminished, with increased returns to all parties in a non-zero-sum system of mutual cooperation.[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats this closed-system economics as the ancestor of Marxist class-struggle theory and of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s 1968 [[limits-to-growth|limits-to-growth]] revival, which entered the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] with the 1973 [[davos-manifesto|Davos Manifesto]] and now underwrites [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] talk of "useless eaters".[1]
