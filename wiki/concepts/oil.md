# Oil

Oil is treated in Badlands Media commentary as one of the two instruments — alongside money — by which global power is exercised.[1] Joe Lange's November 2022 essay "Money and Oil" argues that there are two things that control the world and can be used as tools or weapons, that the world is dependent on both, and that whoever controls them controls it.[1]

## Strategic value

Lange quotes [[john-mccain|John McCain]] — whom he describes in hostile terms while conceding the remark is "right on target" — to the effect that "whoever controls oil controls much more than oil."[1] Every economy in the world is described as dependent on petroleum, including all manufacturing, all transportation, all supply chains and all militaries.[1] The essay also cites promotional language from Tiger General, a supplier of equipment to the oil and gas industry, to the same effect.[1]

## Oil and twentieth-century war

The commodity is said to have risen to prominence during [[world-war-i|the First World War]] and to have become as valuable as gold, with countries willingly trading gold for it.[1] [[world-war-ii|The Second World War]] is characterised outright as a war about oil, with both Germany and [[japan|Japan]] defeated in large part because they could not supply their militaries and industry with fuel.[1] Lange extends the pattern to the [[iraq-war|Iraq war]], asking rhetorically whether anyone believes it was not about oil.[1] The general conclusion is that the "cabal puppet masters" have always recognised oil's value in determining the power structures of the world.[1]

## Oil and the dollar

The abandonment of the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] is attributed in the essay to the same central banks that control the world's money supply, which then created the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] by requiring that all payments for oil be made in U.S. dollars while the dollar served as the world's reserve currency — producing what Lange calls a fiat and energy feedback loop.[1] The ability to dictate to entire governments follows, in his account, from controlling their economies from the ground up, "from the fiat to the energy."[1]

## Oil prices and the economy

With oil around $100 a barrel, Lange writes, energy costs drive up prices across the board.[1] He argues that the 2008 housing bubble burst in part because oil rose from the $50–60 range to roughly $100, forcing millions of Americans in unaffordable mortgages to choose between paying the mortgage and filling the gas tank to get to work — with many ceasing to pay their mortgages.[1] He sees the same dynamic operating on the U.S. economy in late 2022, with [[joe-biden|Biden]] draining the strategic reserves to lower gasoline prices temporarily before the midterms without effect, and appealing to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to increase output.[1]

## Loss of cabal control

Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] is described as doing the opposite of what Washington asked, cutting production by two million barrels per day, whereas under [[donald-trump|Trump]] he had reset the oil price by massively increasing production, helping grow the American economy.[1] Lange argues that this practice, together with increased output by American producers, helped the global economy survive what he calls the cabal's plan to use the "Plandemic" as a catalyst for a global reset.[1] With Saudi Arabia now aligned with [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]] and a possible future member of [[brics|BRICS]], he concludes that the cabal no longer controls the oil price and that inflationary oil prices will push the [[global-debt-system|global debt system]] over the edge.[1]
