# JN.1 "Heart Failure Pandemic" Claim

The JN.1 "heart failure pandemic" claim refers to warnings issued in late December 2023 that a new COVID-19 strain would cause a global wave of cardiac disease — a framing that met with widespread public skepticism.[1]

## The claim

Scientists warned that the new strain, known as JN.1, would cause many people to suffer "reduced cardiac function."[1] Japan's top research institute, Riken, issued a report stating that the ACE2 receptors to which the coronavirus clings within human cells are "very common" in the heart.[1] The report conceded that conclusive clinical evidence linking persistent SARS-CoV-2 infection to declined cardiac function had not been reported, describing its own work as a proof-of-concept study whose findings about persistent cardiac infection and the "opportunistic progression of heart failure" should be validated by a three-dimensional human cardiac tissue model, which it called an "alarm bell for a global healthcare risk."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that almost no one was buying the narrative that an explosion of heart problems was caused by a new variant, except those seeking to leverage a global pandemic during a US presidential election year.[1] She contended that the "died suddenly" phenomenon began shortly after mass vaccination and has recurred in waves, and that the common denominator among young people collapsing was receipt of the experimental mRNA injections — with the regime first blaming climate change and, when that proved unpersuasive, turning to the "new variant" explanation as cover for the damage of its coercive multi-year policies.[1]

The commentary developed an extended analogy to Paula Hawkins's novel *The Girl on the Train*, in which an alcoholic protagonist is gaslit into blaming herself for injuries inflicted by her husband, likening this to a public conditioned to accept blame for harms caused by government policy.[1] The piece concluded that the surge in cardiac problems among the young is neither their fault nor that of their carbon footprint, and that justice in the novel arrived only when the victim reclaimed her power.[1] Related themes are treated in [[covid-19-narrative|the COVID-19 narrative]] and [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]].[1]
