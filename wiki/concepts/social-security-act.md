# Social Security Act

The Social Security Act is the 1930s federal statute that established the United States' national old-age insurance system.[1] According to the National Archives, prior to the Act's passage the responsibility for caring for the elderly fell to families, local groups and the states, apart from pensions paid to veterans; the widespread suffering of the Great Depression elicited congressional support for numerous proposals for a national old-age insurance system.[1]

## 90th anniversary and the 2025 eligibility purge

In August 2025, marking the Act's 90th anniversary, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said at a press conference that his administration had "already kicked nearly 275,000 illegal aliens off the social security system," adding that many of those removed had already left the country while continuing to receive checks, and that the figure had since grown larger.[1] He argued the removals were strengthening the program, contrasted his approach with that of the Biden administration — which he said "never kicked anybody off" — and said historic deportations would remove many more people he described as committing Social Security fraud.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded that a population of that size was large enough to swing a national election, adding a sardonic remark about the same individuals presumably not appearing in voter registries.[1]
