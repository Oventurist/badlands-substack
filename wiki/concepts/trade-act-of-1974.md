# Trade Act of 1974

The Trade Act of 1974 is the United States statute that formalized a new system of congressional delegation in trade policy through "fast track authority," known formally as Trade Promotion Authority (TPA).[1]

## Fast track authority and Section 301

Fast track allowed presidents to negotiate comprehensive trade agreements subject to an up-or-down congressional vote without amendments.[1] The Act simultaneously armed the executive with [[section-301-of-the-trade-act-of-1974|Section 301]] powers to retaliate against unfair foreign trade practices.[1] Periodic renewals of TPA have sustained executive primacy in trade negotiations since, and presidents of both parties have invoked Section 301 and the Section 232 authority created by the [[trade-expansion-act-of-1962|Trade Expansion Act of 1962]] to impose tariffs unilaterally.[1]

## Significance in the tariff debate

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] traced this chain of delegation — from the [[reciprocal-trade-agreements-act-of-1934|RTAA]] through the 1962 and 1974 acts — in arguing that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariffs rest on well-established executive authority and would survive review at the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]
