# Sulfur Dioxide (E220)

Sulfur dioxide, designated E220 in additive numbering, is a sulfur-based preservative that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described as undeniably toxic, in contrast to additives whose risks can be obscured by clever wording or by biased studies that alter parameters to reach a desired result.[1]

## Regulation

In the United States, the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] has completely prohibited the use of sulfur additives on fruit and vegetables.[1] The International Labour Organization of the United Nations advises avoiding E220 for anyone suffering from conjunctivitis, bronchitis, emphysema, bronchial asthma, or cardiovascular disease.[1]

## Health effects and exposure

Adverse reactions attributed to sulfur dioxide include bronchial problems — particularly among those prone to asthma — hypotension, flushing, tingling sensations, and anaphylactic shock.[1] Sulfur additives also destroy vitamins B1 and E, an effect the article describes as particularly damaging to developing children.[1] E220 is found in beer, soft drinks, dried fruits, juices, cordials, wines, vinegar, and potato products.[1] It belongs to the broader family of [[sulfites-in-food|sulfites used in food]].[1]
