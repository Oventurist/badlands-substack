# Executive Order 13912

Executive Order 13912 is a presidential directive signed in March 2020 authorizing the activation of the National Guard under 10 U.S. Code §12302, the statutory provision governing the call-up of the Ready Reserve during a declared national emergency.[1] Issued during the onset of the COVID-19 emergency, the order is described in Badlands Media commentary as far more consequential than its public health framing suggested.[1]

## Continuity of Government trigger

In the reading advanced by the article, EO 13912 functioned as the legal trigger for [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government (COG)]] operations, and was never subsequently deactivated.[1] Because the order remains quietly in effect, the argument runs, the emergency authorities it unlocked continued to operate in the background across the intervening administrations, providing a persistent legal substrate for actions taken during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term.[1]

Title 10 National Guard orders issued under the authority of EO 13912, layered with Presidential Emergency Action Documents (PEADs), are said to have enabled sealed indictments to be managed through military channels, emergency oversight of the misuse of classified material, and military intelligence operating above traditional federal investigative agencies such as the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the FBI.[1]

## Place in the wider legal architecture

The article groups EO 13912 with [[executive-order-13818|EO 13818]], [[executive-order-13848|EO 13848]], and [[executive-order-14151|EO 14151]] as the four orders that together dismantled the legal scaffolding of the entrenched bureaucracy and transferred jurisdiction back to what the author calls constitutional actors.[1] In this framing the orders are not ordinary policy instruments but a deliberate reengineering of the legal landscape designed to survive hostile successor administrations.[1]

## Criticism

Legal scholars associated with the [[brennan-center-for-justice|Brennan Center]] and the [[american-civil-liberties-union|ACLU]] have argued that COG powers of the sort activated by EO 13912 are being used to circumvent constitutional checks and balances.[1] Their objections centre on the classified and non-transparent nature of COG mechanisms, the potential violation of the Posse Comitatus Act through the use of military intelligence in domestic investigations, and the risk that the judiciary comes to be viewed as compliant rather than independent.[1]
