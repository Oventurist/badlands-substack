# Operation Wake Up America

**Operation Wake Up America** is the name given by Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] to a purported covert political-awakening campaign that she argued was being run first by [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] at the national level and subsequently by [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] in Arizona.[1] Introduced in a December 2, 2022 Badlands Media essay subtitled "All the World's a Stage," the framework treats both figures as deliberately cast performers executing a scripted plan to convert politically disengaged Americans — described in the piece as "normies" — into supporters of the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]].[1]

## Premise

Gillespie's argument begins from the claim that since Trump descended the escalator to announce his 2016 candidacy he has "never made a promise he didn't keep" and never speculated on something that failed to come true, a record she treats as anomalous for any human being.[1] She raises, without endorsing, popular explanations for this apparent omniscience — game theory, "Project Looking Glass," particle acceleration, and time travel — and explicitly leaves the question to "bigger brains."[1] The asserted foresight functions in the essay as the foundation of the operation rather than as a claim to be proved.[1]

The essay reconstructs Trump's public biography as deliberate preparation: the New York real-estate developer's swagger, the bestseller *The Art of the Deal*, the casino and gambling businesses, his long registration as a Democrat, and donations to politicians of both parties are read as evidence that he "knew how to play the game."[1] [[the-apprentice|The Apprentice]] is described as a ratings juggernaut that served the strategic purpose of introducing Trump — and his children [[donald-trump-jr|Don Jr.]], [[ivanka-trump|Ivanka]] and [[eric-trump|Eric]] — to a national and global audience, building the persona later deployed in politics.[1] Gillespie writes that Trump's "character, reputation and persona have been carefully crafted over the years" and that he was chosen for "the role of a lifetime" by parties who will likely never be identified.[1]

## Operation Wake Up America 1.0 (national)

The first phase is dated to roughly the time of the 2020 election and summarized as a four-point national playbook: execute a [[devolution|devolution]] plan to ensure continuity of government after the loss of what the author calls a rigged election; dismantle the [[deep-state|Deep State]] piecemeal through lawfare, clandestine operations and election flips; awaken as many Americans as possible with messaging aimed at normies; and manipulate or capture the mainstream media so that the narrative reaches normies rather than the already-awakened.[1]

In this telling, the aftermath of the 2020 election saw the beginning of a "Trump Shadow Presidency," with an installed Biden administration held to have no true access to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], no true access to Air Force One, and no nuclear football.[1] The corresponding post-White House checklist has Trump awakening normies through crafted messaging, spotlighting the failures of the Biden administration, holding rallies that normalize America First sentiment, allowing the installed regime to generate chaos — inflation, gas prices, pandemic issues, Ukraine, China — while the movement grows, and returning constantly to immigration and the border wall under the slogan "If you don't have a border, you don't have a country."[1]

## Operation Wake Up America 2.0 (Arizona)

Phase 2 transposes the same template onto Arizona and [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]].[1] Gillespie dates her own realization to the Turning Point USA "Rally to Protect Our Elections" in Phoenix in July 2021, where Trump recognized figures involved in the Arizona audit — including State Senator Wendy Rogers and Arizona GOP chair [[kelli-ward|Kelli Ward]] — and saved Lake for last, appearing not to know her and remarking, "Wow! This could be a big night for you!"[1] Because Lake had interviewed Trump repeatedly as a Phoenix news anchor, Gillespie concluded he was acting, and that "to Donald Trump, all the world's a stage."[1]

Lake is presented as a parallel constructed figure: a 27-year top-rated anchor at Phoenix's Fox affiliate, a skilled communicator, a "27-year veteran of the 'enemy of the people' that is the Mainstream Media," and at her core a show-woman chosen for a role she plays "to perfection."[1] Her June 2, 2021 gubernatorial announcement is quoted at length, including her warning that Democrats would turn Arizona into "California or worse" and her invocation of the Founding Fathers' expectation that in times of crisis "patriots would rise up from the people to lead us forward."[1]

The 2022 Arizona playbook predicts that Lake will contest the election but that a corrupt judiciary will allow [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] — described as a communist and a puppet — to be "crowned" governor.[1] Lake would then run a shadow governorship with America First figures including [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]], [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] and Blake, use America First legislators to build her own continuity-of-government arrangements and disrupt the Hobbs administration, dismantle the Deep State through lawfare and election flips, awaken Arizonans while other states watch, shame the mainstream media into resuming journalism, and keep border "invasion," drug and sex trafficking, and fentanyl stories visible.[1] Gillespie predicted Hamadeh would win his attorney-general recount, giving the movement "a highly-placed and powerful operative deep inside the machine."[1]

## Reception and framing

The piece cites a [[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] survey finding that 71% of likely U.S. voters — including 40% saying "very likely" — believed problems with the election in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] affected the outcome of Arizona's Senate race.[1] It closes by asserting that the names Trump and Lake are being written into American history in real time, and with the exhortation "let the Arizona games begin! GIDDY UP!"[1] Badlands Media appends its standard disclaimer that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors rather than the outlet itself.[1]
