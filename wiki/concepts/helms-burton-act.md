# Helms-Burton Act

The Helms-Burton Act is the United States statute codifying and strengthening the economic embargo against [[cuba|Cuba]].[1] Its Title III, which permits litigation over property confiscated by the Cuban government, is subject to periodic presidential waiver and is a central object of Havana's sanctions-relief requests.[1]

## 2026 Negotiations

In talks with the Trump administration led on the American side by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], Cuba sought relief from sanctions including eased embargo restrictions, a waiver of Title III of the Act, and removal from the state sponsors of terrorism list.[1] The largely undisclosed negotiations were confirmed in mid-March 2026 by Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]], and Cuba's senior diplomat in Washington, [[lianys-torres-rivera|Lianys Torres Rivera]], publicly invited expanded economic cooperation.[1] US officials said any deal would require major political and economic changes in Cuba, including privatization and greater financial independence for citizens.[1]

## Supreme Court ruling of June 2026

On June 23, 2026, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] decided [[exxon-mobil|Exxon Mobil Corp.]] v. Corporación Cimex, S.A. (Cuba) (No. 24-699), holding in a divided decision that the Helms-Burton Act itself abrogates the sovereign immunity of Cuban agencies and instrumentalities for private suits alleging trafficking in confiscated United States property.[2] The ruling removed a longstanding jurisdictional obstacle to claims brought under the Act by American property owners whose assets were seized after the Cuban revolution.[2] It was issued alongside other opinions that Badlands commentary described as emphasizing statutory text, historical practice, and limits on judicial creation of new liability.[2]
