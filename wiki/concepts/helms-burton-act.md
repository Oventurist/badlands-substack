# Helms-Burton Act

The Helms-Burton Act — formally the Cuban Liberty and Democratic Solidarity (LIBERTAD) Act of 1996 — is the U.S. law codifying and tightening the embargo against [[cuba|Cuba]], including its Title III provision allowing U.S. nationals to sue foreign entities trafficking in property confiscated after the 1959 revolution. In the Badlands corpus it appears as one of the sanctions instruments Cuba is seeking relief from in its 2026 economic talks with the Trump administration.

## Title III waiver and Cuban demands

The Badlands Brief of April 2, 2026 reports that Cuba's senior diplomat to the United States, [[lianys-torres-rivera|Lianys Torres Rivera]], proposed expanded economic cooperation with the Trump administration and identified a package of relief Cuba is seeking: easing embargo restrictions, waiving Title III of the Helms-Burton Act, and removal from the U.S. state sponsors of terrorism list. The largely undisclosed talks are led on the American side by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] following a U.S. oil blockade imposed roughly two months earlier, and were confirmed in mid-March by Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]].

## Context

U.S. officials cited in the coverage said any deal would require major political and economic changes in Cuba, including privatization and greater financial independence for citizens. The Helms-Burton waiver demand is presented as part of a broader Cuban opening that also includes proposed cooperation on Alzheimer's research and drug trafficking enforcement.
