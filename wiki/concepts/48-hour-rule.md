# 48-Hour Rule

The **48-hour rule** is an information-hygiene heuristic promoted within [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary, holding that a reader or commentator should refrain from forming and broadcasting a firm conclusion about a breaking news event until roughly two days have passed.[1]

## Rationale

In his February 28, 2023 essay "The Road Without Struggle," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] presented the rule as a defensive practice against [[poisoning-the-well|poisoning the well]] — the technique by which, in his account, [[deep-state|Deep State]] operators respond to truths they cannot suppress by seeding exaggerated or absurd versions of them.[1] Carlson wrote that when something big breaks he tries not to react immediately, telling himself to be patient and process the information first, because some stories are straightforward while others are bait.[1]

The underlying concern is that a premature public claim gives adversaries something falsifiable to attack. Carlson argued that the operators "want to trick us into overplaying our hand" and "want us to make statements that they can later poke holes in," citing the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream]] bombing and viral video of [[joe-biden|Biden]] falling down stairs or appearing in Ukraine as examples of material engineered to provoke instant overreach.[1] Waiting allows the initial injected distortions to be separated from the durable core of a story.[1]

## Relationship to other Badlands practices

The rule functions as the temporal component of a broader discernment discipline Carlson developed across several essays, alongside source-interrogation questions and awareness of one's own confirmation bias.[1] It complements rather than replaces analysis: the waiting period is intended to be spent processing information from multiple angles rather than simply deferring judgment.[1]

## Application to the Epstein list (January 2024)

Anticipating the release of the so-called "Epstein list," Badlands writer [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote in January 2024 that readers would encounter a mix of long-known material, genuinely new names, and deliberate disinformation.[2] Because some of the information would be fake, he argued, the trustworthiness of all of it comes into question — and therefore the 48-hour rule would be in effect.[2]
