# 2023 BBC Presenter Child Sex Abuse Allegations

The 2023 BBC presenter child sex abuse allegations concerned a high-profile [[bbc|BBC]] on-air personality taken off the air in July 2023 while the broadcaster investigated claims that the presenter had paid a teenager more than £35,000 for pornographic images since 2020, when the young person was seventeen years old.[1]

## The allegations

The controversy was reported as the latest in a series of child sex abuse scandals involving BBC on-air talent.[1] While the corporation removed the individual from its broadcasts, it did not name the person at the center of the allegations.[1] Commentary aggregated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] from the writer BlueApples characterized this as a reversal of the BBC's usual practice, in which it routinely relies on unnamed sources and anonymous witnesses to advance its own unsubstantiated accusations — a tactic for which the online influencer [[andrew-tate|Andrew Tate]] had castigated the network during an interview about a then-pending criminal investigation against him.[1] In this instance, the account argued, the BBC wielded "that sword of anonymity" to shield one of its own.[1]

## Context: the BBC's reputation

The same commentary noted that the BBC has taken pride in being the world's first national television outlet since it first broadcast in November 1936, tracing its history further back to its 1922 founding as a radio station.[1] It argued that what was once "a beacon of broadcast media" had become "a mouthpiece of Britain's propaganda ministry," with embarrassing interviews — from [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to Andrew Tate — repeatedly exposing the hubris of its punditry and production.[1] Those public missteps were described as merely the tip of the iceberg relative to the deeper issue of abuse the organization had kept out of public view.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the public is "constantly told that powerful people trading in child sex is a conspiracy theory," even as arrests for child sex trafficking and child pornography occur daily, with the implication that such crimes belong only to the underworld rather than to "the polished faces of the beautiful people."[1]

She described the BBC as "the CNN of Britain" and a major megaphone of international news, and wrote that this status explained how a television personality remained on air for months after his child sex crimes were shown to the network's leadership — with the victim's family watching him continue to appear after they had presented images of him abusing their underage daughter.[1] Her conclusion was that "the establishment protects itself."[1]

The commentary tied the story to the film [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]], which critics had dismissed as sensationalized QAnon conspiracy propaganda despite being based on the true story of real trafficked children.[1] Quoting [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]]'s repeated line that "God's children are not for sale," Ashe in America argued that in 2023 they in fact are, because a market of buyers exists — and that according to reporting, many of those buyers work in mainstream news.[1]
