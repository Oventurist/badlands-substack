# Battle of Quebec

The Battle of Quebec, fought on the Plains of Abraham in 1759 during the Seven Years War, was the engagement in which the French lost their hold on North America and in which the young English general James Wolfe was killed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) It is the subject of [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]]'s 1770 painting *The Death of General Wolfe*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Strategic consequences

Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes the battle as the decisive moment at which the French empire lost its stranglehold in North America, giving [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]]'s networks the ability to organise what would become the American Revolution.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Those networks had already been planning the conditions for independence for decades, as the historian [[graham-lowry|Graham Lowry]] argued in *How the Nation Was Won: Volume 1 (1630–1757)*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

It was understood that once Quebec fell to the British it would side with the republican cause: the French Canadians almost unanimously regarded the British as tyrants while getting along well with the colonists of the thirteen colonies, and Quebec was expected to become the fourteenth signatory of the Declaration of Independence.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Franklin travelled to Quebec in 1774 to organise elites and population toward that end and had set up Canada's first postal service in 1759 to establish lines of communication.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Certain games and bribes ensured this did not happen.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Native participation

[[william-johnson|William Johnson]], depicted by West in native stockings and moccasins, was instrumental in securing Mohawk and Iroquois support for the English rather than the [[jesuits|Jesuit]]-run French, and the article states that when they chose to fight alongside the English the scales of battle were tipped entirely.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) West placed a Mohawk warrior at the centre of his composition carrying almost more weight than the dying Wolfe, contrasting native strength with the weakness of the British aristocratic general and, by implication, the dying British Empire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) In reality Wolfe was shot and died behind a bush with only two people in attendance; West's crowded composition was a deliberate departure.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
