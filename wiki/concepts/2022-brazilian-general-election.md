# 2022 Brazilian General Election

The 2022 Brazilian general election was the presidential contest in which incumbent [[jair-bolsonaro|Jair Bolsonaro]] was declared the loser to [[luiz-inacio-lula-da-silva|Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva]], a result that Badlands Media treated as a potential turning point in a global struggle over election integrity.[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on November 1, 2022, the author of the essay "Brazil Breaking" framed the contest as possibly "the first elections domino" in a worldwide counter-assault on election-rigging systems.[1]

## Course of the election

The first round of voting took place in early October 2022, and according to the Badlands account Bolsonaro's intra-party competition was rooted out in that round.[1] Despite record turnouts recorded for months leading up to the vote, Bolsonaro found himself on what the article pointedly called the "losing" end of the national election against Lula, described as a globalist and European Union sympathizer.[1] The article declined to litigate polling data, turnout margins or national sentiment in detail, deferring that analysis to others, but asserted that its readership would not accept the official narrative of the upset.[1]

## Comparison with the 2020 United States election

The piece placed the Brazilian result alongside the 2020 United States presidential election, arguing that the Brazilian contest was already rivaling that election in terms of controversy.[1] It further situated the dispute on the doorstep of what it called the most contentious and anticipated midterm election cycle in American history, suggesting that events in Brazil would produce ripple effects felt worldwide and particularly in the United States.[1] The author connected the Brazilian situation to theories he and his peers had advanced regarding a potential sting operation carried out during and following the 2020 election.[1]

## Military involvement and audit expectations

Central to the article's reading of the election was the role of the [[brazilian-armed-forces|Brazilian Armed Forces]], which Bolsonaro said had been invited to join in electoral transparency.[1] Bolsonaro reported that the armed forces had told him it was impossible to grant a seal of credibility or reliability to the voting system given the many vulnerabilities the system still featured.[1] The article added that swirling statements and reports suggested Brazil's military apparatus could be considering full-scale audits of the nation's recent elections and underlying election systems, in the wake of widespread protests and tensions resulting from the disputed result.[1]

## Interpretation as a template

The essay proposed that whatever action followed from Bolsonaro and his loyalists within the military might be used as a legal and narrative template for operations to come in the United States.[1] It cited [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], who had noted that Bolsonaro's alliance with the military on the eve of the election recalled draft executive orders prepared but not actioned by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Read through the lens of [[fifth-generation-warfare|Fifth-Generation Warfare]], the article argued, the disconcerting initial results should not be dismissed nor treated as final before all the fallout had settled.[1]
