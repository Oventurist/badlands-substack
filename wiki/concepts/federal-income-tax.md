# Federal Income Tax

The federal income tax is presented in Badlands Media commentary as an unnecessary instrument of control rather than a genuine funding mechanism for the United States government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Constitutional objection

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that the founding fathers viewed income tax as unconstitutional, and that politicians had to amend the Constitution in 1913 in order to tax Americans' incomes.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Even setting the constitutional question aside, he asks how the same dollar can justifiably be taxed multiple times: income tax, property tax, sales tax and capital gains tax amount to double, triple and quadruple taxation "certainly without representation."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He extends the point, arguing all taxes are taxation without representation because the government does not represent or work for the people.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The analogy offered is paying sales tax on clothing, then a tax to put it in the trunk, another to take it out, and a final tax to hang it in the closet — an absurdity nobody would accept, yet, he writes, "we live in fear of the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], which is why it was created."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The 1913 convergence

Income tax was imposed on Americans in the same year that big bankers gained the ability to print money, set interest rates and control the money supply through what Carlson calls the unconstitutional creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] around 1913.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Income tax, the forming of the Federal Reserve and the formation of the IRS all occurred in that single year — a convergence he insists is not a coincidence.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The funding illusion

Carlson argues that taxes are not actually needed to run the country and do not in fact pay for government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Their function is to create the illusion that Americans pay for their government, which in turn supports the illusion that the government works for them.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Common justifications — roads and the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]] — collapse, he says, when one notices Americans also pay gas tax, car tabs and postage; he cites Trump's demand that Amazon deliver its own packages rather than be subsidized through the USPS, noting the volume of Amazon trucks on the road today and the company's continued success.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The pre-1913 record

Before the federal income tax existed, Carlson notes, the railroad system was built, power lines were installed across America, and much of the highway and freeway system was laid, alongside a military, schools, hospitals and sidewalks.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Between the American Civil War and the founding of the Federal Reserve in 1913, America became an economic giant with no federal income tax at all, which he says makes the case for needing one now difficult to sustain: "When was the last time you saw a new freeway built? Where is the money really going?"1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Function today

In Carlson's account the Fed prints money to fund itself and the financial system, with banks and corporations receiving that money and passing it among corporations, governments and politicians; the government therefore represents banks and corporations, not the people.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) If Americans stopped paying income tax, they would begin asking who funds everything and might wake up to the scheme — "like a drug dealer who lives a lavish lifestyle with no reported income."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Income tax, he concludes, expands the size and control of government and is used to control Americans, but is not necessary to operate the government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Layered taxation and the debt-slave thesis

In an April 2024 essay Carlson described the income tax as only one layer of a stacked system: income is taxed federally and, in many states, again by the state, while purchases generate sales tax, property is taxed, and investments and savings are taxed — so the same dollar is taxed when it is earned, when it is spent and when it is saved.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He added [[inflation|inflation]] as "the biggest tax of all," produced by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] printing money out of nothing.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

Working through an illustrative calculation, he assumed roughly 30% in income and payroll taxes, 10% inflation, 10% property tax and 10% sales tax, leaving 40 cents of a dollar; a further 5% for miscellaneous levies such as car and gas taxes left 35 cents, and saving 10% for retirement left 25 cents to cover food, shelter, insurance, clothing, utilities and everything else — before interest on mortgages, student loans, car loans or business loans.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He conceded the arithmetic was rough but insisted the point stands: the majority of what Americans earn is taken from them, and the system is designed to make them debt slaves whose servitude keeps them from pushing back.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

Carlson maintained that because government can print money at will it does not need the revenue, so taxation exists to sustain control rather than to fund operations.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He acknowledged filing and paying his own taxes despite believing taxes are theft, being unwilling to risk jail and leave his family destitute — "I'm afraid to leave the plantation" — and asked who would stay in line without the threat of the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], concluding that "if not for the funding of the system, there would be no system."2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

## Trump's 2024 proposal to replace it with tariffs

In a private meeting with Republican lawmakers at the Capitol Hill Club on June 13, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] floated an "all tariff policy" that would ultimately allow the United States to abolish the federal income tax, with tariffs on imports supplying federal revenue and serving as negotiating leverage against bad actors abroad.[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that such a campaign would force opposing candidates and their media allies to make affirmative pro-tax arguments, which would in turn expose the legality and constitutionality of the income tax to public scrutiny and confront ordinary Americans with the question of what it means to have been taxed their entire lives.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] added that the framing neutralizes the standard "muh roads" objection to abolishing taxation, since revenue would still flow from tariffs on voluntary foreign trade.[3] See [[trump-all-tariff-policy-proposal]].
