# Farmer Bridge Assistance Program

The Farmer Bridge Assistance Program is a [[department-of-agriculture|U.S. Department of Agriculture]] initiative providing one-time direct payments to eligible agricultural producers to help them manage market disruptions and ongoing economic pressures.[1]

## Payment rates

On December 31, 2025, USDA released per-acre payment rates by commodity under the program.[1] The program forms part of a $12 billion farmer support package, of which $11 billion is designated for direct commodity payments.[1] The payments are intended to provide short-term financial relief and support operational stability for U.S. agricultural producers.[1]

## Accompanying anti-fraud measures

The same announcement cycle included USDA steps to eliminate fraud in grant and other farmer support programs, with Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke L. Rollins]] directing all USDA agencies and staff offices to immediately adopt the first-ever set of USDA General Terms and Conditions for all future awards.[1] Rollins said the Trump administration had been working since "Day One" to promote government efficiency, streamline regulations, and eliminate waste, fraud, and abuse, and that updating over 100 sets of terms and conditions — some lacking termination clauses — each time a new policy was announced had proven a "herculean task."[1] She said the action reduces bureaucracy, eases access to USDA programs, and strengthens the department's ability to act swiftly when recipients, cooperators, subaward recipients, and subcontractors are not compliant with federal law and applicable executive orders.[1]

## Criticism

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that farmer "bailouts" often function as a money-laundering mechanism: global corporations set prices for both inputs and outputs, remove price floors, erase margins, and force small and medium farms into heavy debt, after which bailout funds service that debt and pass through farmers to corporations, transferring wealth from Americans to corporate creditors.[1] In this account producers end up where they began, having perhaps bought time while the predatory dynamic remains in play.[1] She pointed to regenerative farming as the structural solution favored by farmers she has spoken with, noting that on the same day Rollins announced regenerative farming as a top-five research priority for USDA, and expressing hope — though not confidence — that the shift would heal soil and prioritize American producers.[1]
