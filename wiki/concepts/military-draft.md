# Military Draft

The military draft — compulsory conscription into United States military service — re-entered American public consciousness in late 2023 amid fears that widening conflict in the Middle East could draw the United States into a multi-front war.[1]

## 2023 draft anxiety

Google searches for "Will I get drafted into war..." hit their highest level since the 2007 Iraq war surge, an indicator that public worry was rising sharply.[1] Commentary published by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and excerpted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] argued that if the United States were dragged into a multi-front conflict in the Middle East, a new draft would be "almost assured."[1]

The same analysis contended that the generational cohort most alarmed by draft prospects was the one most inclined to support [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and most vocal in backing war against [[russia|Russia]] in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], including the argument that expending Ukrainian conscripts in a proxy conflict was "cheaper" and more efficient than deploying American troops.[1] It further asserted that wars are won with soldiers rather than technology and money, and that without a steady supply of troops any defensive or offensive posture degrades.[1]

## Recruitment shortfall

Military recruitment numbers had been dismal for several years, with a cited [[pentagon|Pentagon]] study indicating that up to 77% of Gen Z did not meet preliminary physical and mental qualifications or could not pass criminal background checks.[1] In some cases the Department of Defense was reported to have built pre-boot facilities — colloquially "fat camps" — to bring recruits into good enough physical condition to survive normal boot camp.[1]

## Counter-commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disputed the disdainful framing of young Americans, comparing it to the way Boomers condescended to Gen X and noting that "there is truly nothing new under the sun."[1] She argued that Gen Z is the most educated generation in American history with unprecedented access to information, and that this access led them to reject the establishment faster than older cohorts.[1] In her account, young people are not refusing to vote for Biden so much as refusing to vote at all because they see elections as theater; they decline military service because they reject the premise of "the banker wars"; and they avoid the news because they know it is fake.[1] She concluded that "the kids are alright" and that older generations should stop mocking them and instead teach them "the way out of the war of stories."[1]

## October 2023 draft-search surge

Interest in conscription spiked again in October 2023. Google search data showed that queries for "Will I get drafted to war?" jumped above the February 2022 high recorded when Russia invaded Ukraine and hit the highest level since President George Bush announced the troop surge in Iraq in early 2007.[2] The surge followed the [[october-7-attacks|Hamas invasion of Israel]], which opened a second major geopolitical flashpoint alongside the war in Ukraine, then 1.7 years old, and coincided with warnings from cycle analyst [[charles-nenner|Charles Nenner]] that the world was entering a multi-year war cycle.[2] Speaking in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told a crowd the United States was "closer to World War III than we've ever been," warning of "weaponry the likes of which this world has never seen before."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the issue was personal, having three sons — one of draft age — and described as terrifying the prospect that a government she considers corrupt and illegitimate could send them to fight foreign wars, tying the risk to unresolved questions about stolen elections.[2] She argued that the greater danger was conflict on American soil, pointing to an open border through which unvetted military-aged males from around the world were moving while institutional energy directed Americans' attention abroad.[2] Crediting Trump and the [[america-first-movement|America First]] wing with calling out "the emotional blackmail, war drums, and lack of American interests in the resource prioritization math," she said the search data was newsworthy precisely because young Americans and their mothers recognized conscription as a live possibility.[2]

## 2024 fight over draft registration for women

In June 2024, Senate Democrats added language to the annual defense authorization bill requiring women to register for the draft, prompting a backlash from Republicans and social conservatives and complicating the chances of moving the bill on the Senate floor before Election Day.[3] Conservatives led by Senator [[roger-wicker|Roger Wicker]] (R-Miss.) were certain to attempt to remove the provision, a vote that reporting described as politically difficult for Democrats in tight reelection races, including [[jon-tester|Jon Tester]] of Montana and [[jacky-rosen|Jacky Rosen]] of Nevada.[3]

Republican Senate candidate [[sam-brown|Sam Brown]], an Army veteran severely burned by an improvised explosive device, made the issue central to the Nevada race, posting a video on X in which he pointed to the scarring on his face as evidence of the dangers women would face in combat theaters.[3] "Look at my face. This is the high cost of war," he wrote, adding that "forcing America's daughters to register for the draft is UNACCEPTABLE."[3]
