# H.Res. 500 (Congressional Staff Background Checks)

H.Res. 500 is a House resolution introduced by [[kat-cammack|Kat Cammack]] that would amend House rules to require criminal background checks for all House employees.[1] In July 2026 Cammack called for an immediate floor vote on the measure after repeated stalls.[1]

## Provisions

The resolution would require, in addition to criminal background checks, public disclosure filed with the Clerk of the House of any payments, contracts, or agreements with foreign governments in the three years prior to employment, and of any foreign citizenship or nationality held by the employee.[1] The requirements would apply to current staff as well as new hires.[1] Cammack's stated rationale is that congressional staff routinely handle sensitive personal information yet, unlike executive-branch employees, face no mandatory vetting.[1]

## Context and companion rule change

The push coincided with Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s endorsement of expanding the House Code of Official Conduct to bar lawmakers from sexual relationships with any congressional staff, not merely subordinates; the existing rule, adopted in 2018, prohibits intimate relationships with subordinates in personal offices or on committees, with an exception for married staffers.[1]

Badlands commentary treated both moves as self-indicting. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]]'s amplification of the bill backfired because the public was stunned such vetting did not already exist after [[eric-swalwell|Swalwell]] and Fang Fang, Dianne Feinstein's driver, the Senate room sex tape, and what she called the secret sex-crime slush fund.[1] Her conclusion was that [[congress|Congress]] is itself the problem and "problems don't get to design their own solutions."[1]
