# Iranian Hacking Operations Against Trump Associates

A series of reported foreign hacking operations targeted members of [[donald-trump]]'s inner circle during 2024, attributed in most public reporting to Iran.[1]

## Kash Patel

In early December 2024, CNN reported that [[kash-patel]], Trump's pick to run the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], had recently been informed by the bureau that he was targeted as part of an Iranian hack, with hackers believed to have accessed at least some of his communications.[1] Trump transition spokesman [[alex-pfeiffer]] declined to comment specifically on the hack, saying instead that Patel "was a key part of the first Trump administration's efforts against the terrorist Iranian regime and will implement President Trump's policies to protect America from adversaries as the FBI Director."[1]

## Other targets

The Patel disclosure followed a pattern of similar notifications. The FBI informed [[todd-blanche]], one of Trump's lead attorneys and his pick for deputy attorney general, that his cellphone had been tapped by Chinese hackers, an allegation the Chinese government denied.[1] Blanche was the second Trump attorney believed to have been targeted by foreign hackers; attorney [[lindsey-halligan]] was reported in August 2024 to have been targeted in a separate Iranian hacking effort.[1] [[donald-trump-jr|Donald Trump Jr.]] said he too had been notified by the FBI that he was "one of the top targets" of Iran.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media described the reporting as "a story template" leading somewhere significant.[1] The commentary recalled that earlier in 2024 Iranian hackers were said to have targeted the Trump campaign and possibly obtained internal research on figures including eventual vice-presidential pick [[jd-vance]].[1] What stood out to the writer was that Trump's team knew of the hack well before it was widely reported, and that when it was reported [[politico]] and other outlets adopted a defensive tone, claiming they had received the material but chose not to publish it; the Kamala Harris campaign subsequently said it had been targeted by a similar effort.[1] The commentary speculated that the Trump team was signaling to the establishment that it knew who was truly behind the hacking attempts, and closed by inviting readers to consider connections between known US intelligence figures and Iran and to ask who "Iran" really is.[1]
