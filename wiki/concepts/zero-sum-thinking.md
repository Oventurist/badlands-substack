# Zero-Sum Thinking

Zero-sum thinking is the framework Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies as the common root of the two dominant, and in his view inadequate, responses to the North American water crisis.[1]

## Definition

The doctrine is defined as the belief that ecosystems and human economies are closed systems, maintained by finite energy, such that any new energy expended in one part of the system requires an equivalent amount of energy to be removed from another part.[1] Both prevailing water policies are said to be trapped inside this assumption.[1]

## The two zero-sum solutions

The first approach proposes to divert water from one place to another in piecemeal fashion, but in drought-stricken regions there is little abundance to begin with, so moving water from a scarce place to a scarcer one merely creates new tension and postpones the crisis by months or years.[1] The second and more cynical position holds that it was a mistake to have used science and technology to populate the west coast of the United States over the past 120 years; its adherents regard the great dams of Franklin Roosevelt's New Deal, which made the Imperial Valley possible, as an abomination of nature.[1] Figures such as former California Governor [[jerry-brown|Jerry Brown]] are cited as concluding that humanity should adapt to the deserts nature created and depopulate places like California, whose population exceeds that of the whole of Canada by over seven million people.[1]

## The non-zero-sum alternative

Ehret argues that abandoning the premise that North American water systems must be treated as regionally closed systems, and adopting an open-system, top-down perspective in which ecosystems and economies are "more than the sum of their parts," changes both the parameters of the problem and the pathways available to solve it.[1] He points to China's [[south-north-water-transfer-project|Move South Water North Project]] as a working demonstration of the alternative paradigm, and to a revived [[north-american-water-and-power-alliance|North American Water and Power Alliance]] as its continental application.[1] He frames the choice as between the anti-human [[great-reset|Great Reset]] agenda and a forgotten paradigm more worthy of human civilization and natural law.[1]
