# Horseshoe Theory

Horseshoe Theory is the proposition that the political far Left and far Right, rather than sitting at opposite ends of a straight line, curve toward one another like the ends of a horseshoe and increasingly share grievances, methods and enemies.[1] The concept has been developed at length in Badlands Media writing, including a multi-part series published on the Badlands Substack.[1]

In the August 2024 *Badlands News Brief*, contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked Horseshoe Theory while analyzing the destruction of progressive members of [[congress]] by pro-Israel spending.[1] He argued that the "earnest Left and the earnest Right now have common ground for earnest dialogue," and that Horseshoe Theory combined with what he called "Trump's Gambit" offered a path to "save both America and Israel from certain doom."[1]

The framing is used to distinguish sincere dissidents on both flanks from what the author describes as controlled-opposition actors — figures such as [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] and [[cori-bush|Cori Bush]] on the left and censorship-friendly elements of MAGA on the right — who, in his account, mirror one another's demands for suppression of speech.[1] He held that hate-speech measures advanced in Republican-controlled state legislatures were aimed less at protecting a religion than at insulating a foreign government from criticism, a precedent he feared would eventually be turned against criticism of the U.S. government itself.[1]

## Application to the French protests (2024)

In April 2024, Badlands contributor
[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] applied Horseshoe Theory
to France, where the 2018 [[yellow-vest-movement|Yellow Vest]] protests by
city-dwellers against the elitist green agenda had been joined by farmer
protests across western Europe against plans he described as requisitioning
privately-owned farmland and halting food production.[2] He characterized the
resulting alignment of conservative rural farmers and liberal-leaning
city-dwellers against the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] agenda as "a
beautiful moment of unity" and a textbook case of the theory.[2]

## Origins in Badlands writing

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] first set out the framework in an article titled "Horseshoe Theory" published on the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] Substack roughly a year before May 2024, in which he collected historical anecdotes of warring parties uniting at pivotal moments when their existential demise was at hand and finding common ground in a shared enemy.[3] He defined the resulting coalitions as temporary political alliances formed to achieve specific goals, noting that they did not resolve the underlying differences between the parties but allowed them to be set aside in order to secure their mutual survival.[3] Only "the strongest minds of the highest caliber" are capable of such coalitions, he argued: "Any moron can employ violence; It takes a real leader to employ peace."[3]

## The Logical Mind and the Bleeding Heart

Central to his version of the theory are two cognitive "muscles" — the Logical Mind and the Bleeding Heart — which he holds manifest in the media-manufactured political spectrum as "Right" and "Left" respectively.[3] Each has virtues and shortcomings, and when one muscle is too weak the other compensates in ways that are not necessarily ideal; he offers religion and Middle East warmongering as an example of the Right falling victim to its Bleeding Heart.[3] In his formulation, the Logical Mind without the Bleeding Heart becomes a "Neo-Con," while the Bleeding Heart without the Logical Mind becomes an "Emotional Woketard."[3] The cognitive bridge connecting the two is Inspiration, which he linked to the [[4th-psychological-operations-group|4th Psychological Operations Group]]'s presentation that the Psywar is won through the power of words and ideas and by winning hearts and minds.[3]

## May 2024 campus protests as evidence

He cited a May 2024 episode in which pro-Israel and pro-Palestine demonstrators at a university chanted "F**k Joe Biden" in solidarity while Democrats were reported to be in "panic mode" as live confirmation of the thesis, writing that although "we still have a ways to go, and we may not win over everybody, Horseshoe Theory is real, and it is happening."[3]

## Vindication in the 2024 consensus reporting

In October 2024, an Axios "Behind the Curtain" analysis argued that at the most polarized time in living memory the two American parties had never been more unified on ideas, and that [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], for all their differing worldviews, disagreed far more about style than substance if taken at their word.[4] The piece attributed this to shifting demographics and political coalitions producing a loose American consensus on many major policy topics, and to the general-election habit of sprinting to the center, while conceding massive disagreements over executive power, use of the Justice Department, abortion access, environmental regulations, taxes and America's place in the world including support for Ukraine.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], author of the original "Horseshoe Theory" series, treated the Axios framing as belated confirmation of a thesis for which he had been called crazy, writing that America is much closer to consensus than it realizes and that the differences are marginal in the grand scheme.[4] He attributed the perception of irreconcilable division to intense psychological warfare operations waged by "bloodsucking traitors in the [[state-department|State Department]] and orbiting agencies," arguing that most people are simply getting bad information.[4] He predicted America would unite and that, when it did, bad actors in government would seek to hurt as many Americans as possible, "because they are the real terrorists."[4]

## Origins of the series (July 2023)

The theory received its fullest early exposition in "Horseshoe Theory - Part 1: Uniting the Tribes Against Our Common Enemy," published on the Badlands Substack on 14 July 2023 by [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|Ghost of Patrick Henry]].[5] The essay proposed that readers could find common ground with fellow citizens on the other side of the political spectrum, while acknowledging that a few actors are "simply beyond saving."[5] The author framed the project in personal terms, describing older members of the Badlands community whose relationships with their adult children had been severed by politics, and invoking the veterans' code that nobody gets left behind.[5]

He drew a sharp distinction between bad-faith actors who knowingly abused public trust — with whom no reconciliation is sought — and the majority, whom he characterized as victims of "the most sophisticated psychological warfare operation in human history" and who should be welcomed back if they express genuine remorse.[5] He credited a discussion between [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] on the 4 July 2023 episode of [[badlands-story-hour|Badlands Story Hour]] with articulating that distinction.[5]

## Critique of the binary spectrum

The article argued that the political spectrum as presented to the public — Right versus Left, Conservative versus Liberal, Republican versus Democrat, Traditional versus Progressive — is a deception in all of its framings, not merely in the party-political one.[5] Citing [[just-human|Just Human]] on the appeal of binary paradigms to the human mind, the author held that such simplification erases the shades of gray where most people actually land and inherently promotes tribalism, which is how the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] enemy consolidates power through divide-and-conquer.[5] He urged readers to consume the literature and rhetoric of their opponents, both to test their own convictions and to avoid becoming the monster they seek to destroy.[5]

## Logical Mind and Bleeding Heart in Part 1

Part 1 introduced the paired cognitive frameworks later reused across the series.[5] Those in the [[truth-movement|Truth Movement]] — described by [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] as "heterodox thinkers" — engage news and information through logic and reason, making Research and Reasoning the defining actions of the movement; in the author's metaphor, "if Logic is our weapon of choice, then Discernment is our body armor," and the conflict is a war of attrition within the finite game described by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]].[5]

The Left, by contrast, engages primarily through emotion, with the Logical Mind relegated to a passive background function — a configuration the author named the Bleeding Heart, and which the Left regards not as indiscipline but as an evolution of morality.[5] He explicitly rejected the view that this is purely a weakness, arguing that emotional response is what distinguishes human beings from artificial intelligence.[5] As illustrations he offered [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]] speaking through tears about [[adrenochrome|adrenochrome]] harvesting and the making of [[sound-of-freedom|The Sound of Freedom]], and the decorated Marine and undercover law-enforcement veteran [[alfredo-luna|Alfredo Luna]] (AlphaWarrior) describing a midnight raid on his home in which weapons were pointed at his wife and son and his infant daughter was left alone in her crib for hours.[5] The undisciplined Bleeding Heart, he wrote, would burn down cities in response, while the Logical Mind knows the real battle is fought elsewhere.[5]

## Common Ground and Evil

The essay located the shared foundation of Right and Left in a common existential threat: Evil, understood as the drive to dominate others, whether highly disciplined and coordinated (Machiavellian politicking and [[fifth-generation-warfare|5GW]] psyops) or undisciplined and solitary (a psychotic killer).[5] Because the overwhelming majority of human beings are, in his account, good souls seeking a better world, that shared danger constitutes the Common Ground — and this, he wrote, "is the essence of Horseshoe Theory."[5] He also argued that the Uniparty has learned to weaponize emotional outrage in undisciplined minds, and that in the era of outrage mobs and [[cancel-culture|cancel culture]] what is lacking is adequate outrage from the morally righteous Logical Mind.[5]

## Historical analogies

Two historical alliances of former enemies were offered as precedents.[5] The first was Charles Martel — the illegitimate son of the Frankish ruler Pepin II, later the inspiration for the character Jon Snow — who after a lifetime of civil war approached his sworn enemies and united them to defeat the Umayyad Caliphate at the Battle of Tours in 732, an event the article credits with saving Western civilization; Martel's grandson Charlemagne would be anointed the first Holy Roman Emperor, launching the Carolingian dynasty, while Charles himself never held a title.[5] The second was the alliance formed around 965 under Sviatoslav I, Grand Prince of Kiev, in which the proto-Russians fought alongside the Byzantines and the Turks — all former enemies who had each failed alone — to destroy the [[khazaria|Khazarian Empire]], depicted in the article as having rejected the Abrahamic religions in favor of Babylonian blood magic and worship of Ba'al.[5] The author also likened the desired coalition to the Irish and Scots uniting against England in *Braveheart*.[5]

## Inspiration as the bridge

Part 1 concluded that warring factions cannot be united by equations, logic and facts alone, and that winning hearts and minds — particularly of those who reason through the Bleeding Heart — "will require inspiration, not persuasion," making Inspiration the cognitive bridge between Logic and Emotion.[5] To support this the author turned to the Greek Muses, who presided not only over literature, science and the arts but also over history, and to Herodotus, whose *The Histories* the librarians at Alexandria divided into nine books named for the nine Muses.[5] Quoting Richard C. Jebb's *The Genius of Sophocles* on the Persian wars giving Herodotus "the first genuinely historical inspiration felt by a Greek," the author characterized Herodotus as the first to deploy narrative seeding — a war of stories conducted with benevolent intent and grounded in truth rather than deception — and drew an explicit parallel between this series and his [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] *Patriot Chronicles*.[5] Specific case studies of common ground were deferred to Part 2.[5]
