# Horseshoe Theory

Horseshoe Theory is the proposition that the political far Left and far Right, rather than sitting at opposite ends of a straight line, curve toward one another like the ends of a horseshoe and increasingly share grievances, methods and enemies.[1] The concept has been developed at length in Badlands Media writing, including a multi-part series published on the Badlands Substack.[1]

In the August 2024 *Badlands News Brief*, contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked Horseshoe Theory while analyzing the destruction of progressive members of [[congress]] by pro-Israel spending.[1] He argued that the "earnest Left and the earnest Right now have common ground for earnest dialogue," and that Horseshoe Theory combined with what he called "Trump's Gambit" offered a path to "save both America and Israel from certain doom."[1]

The framing is used to distinguish sincere dissidents on both flanks from what the author describes as controlled-opposition actors — figures such as [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] and [[cori-bush|Cori Bush]] on the left and censorship-friendly elements of MAGA on the right — who, in his account, mirror one another's demands for suppression of speech.[1] He held that hate-speech measures advanced in Republican-controlled state legislatures were aimed less at protecting a religion than at insulating a foreign government from criticism, a precedent he feared would eventually be turned against criticism of the U.S. government itself.[1]

## Application to the French protests (2024)

In April 2024, Badlands contributor
[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] applied Horseshoe Theory
to France, where the 2018 [[yellow-vest-movement|Yellow Vest]] protests by
city-dwellers against the elitist green agenda had been joined by farmer
protests across western Europe against plans he described as requisitioning
privately-owned farmland and halting food production.[2] He characterized the
resulting alignment of conservative rural farmers and liberal-leaning
city-dwellers against the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] agenda as "a
beautiful moment of unity" and a textbook case of the theory.[2]

## Origins in Badlands writing

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] first set out the framework in an article titled "Horseshoe Theory" published on the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] Substack roughly a year before May 2024, in which he collected historical anecdotes of warring parties uniting at pivotal moments when their existential demise was at hand and finding common ground in a shared enemy.[3] He defined the resulting coalitions as temporary political alliances formed to achieve specific goals, noting that they did not resolve the underlying differences between the parties but allowed them to be set aside in order to secure their mutual survival.[3] Only "the strongest minds of the highest caliber" are capable of such coalitions, he argued: "Any moron can employ violence; It takes a real leader to employ peace."[3]

## The Logical Mind and the Bleeding Heart

Central to his version of the theory are two cognitive "muscles" — the Logical Mind and the Bleeding Heart — which he holds manifest in the media-manufactured political spectrum as "Right" and "Left" respectively.[3] Each has virtues and shortcomings, and when one muscle is too weak the other compensates in ways that are not necessarily ideal; he offers religion and Middle East warmongering as an example of the Right falling victim to its Bleeding Heart.[3] In his formulation, the Logical Mind without the Bleeding Heart becomes a "Neo-Con," while the Bleeding Heart without the Logical Mind becomes an "Emotional Woketard."[3] The cognitive bridge connecting the two is Inspiration, which he linked to the [[4th-psychological-operations-group|4th Psychological Operations Group]]'s presentation that the Psywar is won through the power of words and ideas and by winning hearts and minds.[3]

## May 2024 campus protests as evidence

He cited a May 2024 episode in which pro-Israel and pro-Palestine demonstrators at a university chanted "F**k Joe Biden" in solidarity while Democrats were reported to be in "panic mode" as live confirmation of the thesis, writing that although "we still have a ways to go, and we may not win over everybody, Horseshoe Theory is real, and it is happening."[3]

## Vindication in the 2024 consensus reporting

In October 2024, an Axios "Behind the Curtain" analysis argued that at the most polarized time in living memory the two American parties had never been more unified on ideas, and that [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], for all their differing worldviews, disagreed far more about style than substance if taken at their word.[4] The piece attributed this to shifting demographics and political coalitions producing a loose American consensus on many major policy topics, and to the general-election habit of sprinting to the center, while conceding massive disagreements over executive power, use of the Justice Department, abortion access, environmental regulations, taxes and America's place in the world including support for Ukraine.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], author of the original "Horseshoe Theory" series, treated the Axios framing as belated confirmation of a thesis for which he had been called crazy, writing that America is much closer to consensus than it realizes and that the differences are marginal in the grand scheme.[4] He attributed the perception of irreconcilable division to intense psychological warfare operations waged by "bloodsucking traitors in the [[state-department|State Department]] and orbiting agencies," arguing that most people are simply getting bad information.[4] He predicted America would unite and that, when it did, bad actors in government would seek to hurt as many Americans as possible, "because they are the real terrorists."[4]
