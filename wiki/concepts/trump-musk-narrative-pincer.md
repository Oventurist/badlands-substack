# Trump-Musk Narrative Pincer

The "Trump-Musk Narrative Pincer" is a framework advanced by Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] since 2022, holding that the public convergence of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] constitutes a two-pronged information-warfare maneuver against the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1]

## The August 2024 X conversation

The framework received its most visible test when Trump officially returned to [[x-corp|X]] and held a roughly two-hour live conversation with Musk on X Spaces that generated more than a billion views.[1] The stream had to overcome significant technical problems caused by a sustained DDoS attack on the platform shortly before it began.[1] The attack followed sustained complaints from legacy media that Musk would allow Trump to say "whatever he wants to."[1]

## Argument

Burning Bright wrote that he could not get enough of "the public actualization of the Trump-Musk Narrative Pincer" he had been projecting since 2022.[1] He noted, as [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] had pointed out in a Fox appearance and as the Power Hour had discussed for the better part of two years, that Musk was the top contractor for Trump's [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] — suggesting the relationship neither began nor ended there.[1]

Because both men had become "more symbols and cyphers than people in the War of Stories," and because they rode opposite sides of the sociopolitical zeitgeist into mass psychological prominence — Musk representing the techno-optimist emergent Left, Trump the nationalist, conservative and disaffected-liberal flank that became MAGA — their union was framed as simultaneously catastrophic for the Deep State and unifying for the Collective Mind.[1] The author argued that leaders must be seen "not as destroyers or conquerors, but as builders and unifiers," and that both men qualified as builders now beginning their public unification.[1] He observed the irony that self-branded collectivists appeared most disturbed by the trend, citing a montage of media headlines calling the conversation a threat.[1]
