# Saudi-Pakistan Defense Pact

The Saudi-Pakistan defense pact is a bilateral security arrangement between [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[pakistan|Pakistan]] that emerged as a nucleus for a wider regional security architecture in the aftermath of the US-Israeli conflict with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Proposed expansion

Following the conflict, Saudi Arabia was reported to be strengthening coordination with Pakistan, [[turkey|Turkey]] and Egypt.[1] Pakistan's defense minister said Islamabad wants [[qatar|Qatar]] and Turkey added to the pact in order to build a broader regional economic and military alliance aimed at reducing dependence on outside powers.[1]

The expansion effort was reported alongside Riyadh's proposal for a Middle East non-aggression pact with Iran modeled on the [[helsinki-accords|Helsinki Accords]].[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the countries [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] has drawn together are not historic allies, and that their partnership rests instead on a shared vision of future economic prosperity — the security scaffolding required to make the kingdom's development plans achievable.[1]

## Signing in Riyadh (September 2025)

Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Pakistani Prime Minister [[shehbaz-sharif|Shehbaz Sharif]] signed a joint strategic defense agreement in Riyadh on Wednesday, September 17, 2025, according to the Saudi Press Agency.[2] The agreement was framed as part of the two countries' efforts to enhance their security and to achieve security and peace in the region and the world.[2]

The pact aims to develop aspects of defense cooperation and enhance joint deterrence against any aggression, and stipulates that any aggression against either country constitutes aggression against both.[2] Sharif arrived in Riyadh earlier that day for a state visit accompanied by a high-level delegation; an official reception was held in his honor and the two leaders held a session of talks, with Sharif asking that his greetings be conveyed to [[king-salman|King Salman]].[2]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the mutual-defense clause as a rebuke to the [[deep-state|Deep State]], arguing that the region used in the 1970s as a beachhead to radicalize Muslims into jihadis — creating the Mujahideen, later known as [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] — was "officially out of business."[2] He further contended that recent fighting in Jammu-Kashmir between Pakistan and India had been designed to draw both into a conflict that would destabilize the region and perpetuate an ancient rivalry, and framed the pact as a "Sovereign Alliance" helping Asia form a defensive phalanx.[2]

A separate Badlands take by Burning Bright cited Lt. Gen. [[steve-kwast|Steve Kwast]] on nuclear policy, quoting him that "the energy side of that conversation and the space side ... will make nuclear weapons something that we can get rid of safely," and characterizing this as the reality underlying what he called Trump's re-nuclearization agenda disguised as de-nuclearization.[2]
