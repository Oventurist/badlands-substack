# Killing of Hassan Nasrallah

The killing of [[hassan-nasrallah|Hassan Nasrallah]], the longtime leader of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], occurred on the evening of Friday, September 27, 2024, when the Israeli air force struck an underground complex hidden in the southern Beirut suburb of Dahieh.[1]

## The strike

Roughly one hundred munitions — including, it was believed, U.S.-made 2,000-pound bombs — were used in the overwhelming air raid.[1] Nasrallah, who was careful to the point of paranoia about his security arrangements and only rarely appeared in public, would have given little notice of his plan to undertake the trip to the meeting.[1] Israeli intelligence penetration of Hezbollah was nonetheless so deep that Israel knew Nasrallah and other surviving members of the group's already decimated leadership would be gathered at the supposedly secret location, and that an order to bomb them could be given.[1]

[[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was required to give permission for the attack from New York, where the Israeli prime minister had delivered a bellicose speech at the United Nations General Assembly; there was, presumably, felt to be little time to wait.[1]

## The alleged mole

According to an unsubstantiated report in the French newspaper Le Parisien, the mole who informed the Israelis that Nasrallah was en route to the bunker was Iranian.[1] If true, the detail would be eye-catching, given that [[iran|Iran]] is Hezbollah's principal backer.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry pointed to a chart circulated by Ezra Cohen-Watnick illustrating how many Hezbollah leaders [[israel|Israel]] had killed in the preceding weeks, and argued that Israel had "crossed the threshold of no return," making war with Iran appear almost inevitable.[1] In his reading, the real objective had always been to draw the United States into a war with Iran, which made purported anecdotes by Colonel Lawrence Wilkerson about CENTCOM commander General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]] significant: if Wilkerson was accurate, such provocation could not be rewarded with U.S. troops, and Netanyahu had effectively called Kurilla's bluff.[1] He expected the public narrative to remain that the United States was helping Netanyahu, and argued that the best optics for the [[brics|BRICS]] alliance would be for a military from outside the region to engage the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] and pursue Netanyahu to force his surrender.[1]
