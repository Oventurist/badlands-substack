# Corpus Hermeticum

The Corpus Hermeticum is a collection of seventeen Greek Hermetic treatises written between roughly 100 and 300 CE and the most popular body of the "Religio-philosophical Hermetica," the subgroup of writings that gives [[hermeticism|Hermeticism]] its central content.[1] It is attributed to [[hermes-trismegistus|Hermes Trismegistus]] and was surveyed for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] in his February 2023 article "Exploring Hermeticism."[1]

## Content

The Corpus Hermeticum, together with the companion treatise [[asclepius|Asclepius]], dwells on the oneness and goodness of God, urges purification of the soul, and expands on the relationship between mind and spirit.[1] It formed the primary focus of DeLarme's survey of the Hermetic literature.[1]

## Dating and transmission

Modern scholars and translators have dated the Corpus Hermeticum to the second century CE or earlier.[1] The teachings attributed to Hermes as a whole span roughly 300 BCE to 1200 CE.[1] The Corpus was reintroduced to the mass consciousness during the Renaissance after having been seemingly lost to the ages — or, in DeLarme's framing, intentionally obscured.[1] On the standard account it emerged in parallel with early Christianity, [[gnosticism|Gnosticism]] and [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]], with believed overlap with the Chaldaean Oracles and late Orphic and Pythagorean literature.[1]
