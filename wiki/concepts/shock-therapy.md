# Shock Therapy

"Shock Therapy" is the name given to the program of rapid economic liberalization imposed on post-Soviet Russia during the 1990s.[1] In the account given by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], despite the many promises of East–West cooperation made at the end of the Cold War, the 1990s instead saw "a bloodied Russia swimming with sharks."[1]

Figures such as [[strobe-talbott|Strobe Talbott]] and [[jeffrey-sachs|Jeffrey Sachs]] were assigned the task of breaking the Russian government and its people economically, psychologically and morally under the Shock Therapy program, which the article describes as overseen by the worst elements of the [[imf|IMF]], the [[city-of-london|City of London]] and Washington utopians.[1]

## Context

Shock Therapy is contrasted with the development-oriented alternative promoted in the late 1980s by [[alfred-herrhausen|Alfred Herrhausen]] and allied American statesmen, who called for billions of dollars in development aid to help the Soviet Union transition through large-scale infrastructure and industrial modernization.[1] Alongside the economic program, basic security guarantees were abandoned: the promise made by then-Secretary of State [[james-baker|James Baker]] not to move [[nato|NATO]] "one inch beyond its 1992 configuration" was increasingly disregarded as the alliance transformed from a Cold War defensive structure into an aspiring global offensive one.[1]

Behind the illusion of America's victory over communism, the article argues, a rot was growing, as post-industrial policies of the 1970s and 1980s transformed America's industrial base into a services economy without sovereign capacity to produce for itself or maintain basic infrastructure.[1]
