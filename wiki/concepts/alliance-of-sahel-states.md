# Alliance of Sahel States

The Alliance of Sahel States is the confederation of West African military-led governments — centred on [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], [[mali|Mali]] and [[niger|Niger]] — that the Badlands corpus treats as one of the principal theatres of the contest between sovereign national governments and the [[global-cartel|globalist]] order.

## Designation as "Conflict A"

In the January 30, 2026 Badlands Brief, contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] labels the Alliance of Sahel States "Conflict A" among the three major African conflicts he tracks, and identifies [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] as the external power involved — in contrast to "Conflict B" in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], where President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is involved, and "Conflict C," where "the Arabs are running point."

## Burkina Faso as case study

The commentary anticipates that "the 'muh democracy' folks are about to go apoplectic" over Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]]'s dissolution of all political parties by decree, noting that Burkina Faso had over 100 registered political parties of which only 15 held parliamentary seats. Alongside the political consolidation, the Brief records [[russia|Russia]] preparing to build a nuclear plant and the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] completing a thermal power plant in the country. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicts the situation "will become a case study to show how quickly a third world nation where only 20% of the population has electricity can be brought into the Golden Age at an accelerated rate," tying the alliance to the corpus's [[global-golden-age|Global Golden Age]] and [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] themes.

## Rebellion against ECOWAS and the Ukraine accusation (May 2026)

The May 18, 2026 Badlands Brief develops the alliance's origins and its place in the joint U.S.-Nigerian operation that killed [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]]. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry describes the AES as having been "in rebellion against the UN-affiliated [[ecowas|Economic Community of West African States]] (ECOWAS) since 2022," when Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] led a military coup against his own commander, who had overthrown the government of [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] just eight months earlier. A map in the Brief shows the former ECOWAS member states of Burkina Faso, [[mali|Mali]] and [[niger|Niger]] now constituting the AES.

The commentary argues that organizations like ECOWAS "are created to control and suppress nations around the world," that the region's terrorist groups are funded by the [[state-department|State Department]], [[cia|CIA]], [[united-nations|UN]], [[nato|NATO]] and NGOs under those umbrellas, and that Russia has been helping Traoré and his allies "reclaim their sovereignty from these ISIS-linked groups." Russia and the AES have repeatedly accused [[ukraine|Ukraine]] of funding, arming and training the groups; the commentary claims the money Congress approved for Ukraine "was always intended to fund these groups, so that the Deep State could overthrow the governments of the countries they have lost control over" — countries providing critical resources like gold (Burkina Faso) and uranium ([[niger|Niger]]). By killing Al-Manuki, the commentator concludes, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] did "a great service to Captain Traore and his Sahelian allies," though the mainstream media will frame it as Trump siding with ECOWAS against the AES.
