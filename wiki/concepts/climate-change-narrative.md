# Climate Change Narrative

The **climate change narrative** is treated in "AI Can Help Us Expose the NWO" as a framing device deployed to reinforce collectivism, [[neo-marxism|neo-Marxism]], the loss of individual rights and the loss of national sovereignty.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] added it explicitly to his prompt sequence, instructing ChatGPT to implement it within the [[global-harmony-consortium|Global Harmony Consortium]]'s plan and to interweave it with the [[satanic-inversion|principle of inversion]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

In the model's synthesis the narrative performs two distinct jobs. First, it is used to emphasise overpopulation as a global threat, subtly linking that threat to traditional family values and urging a reduction in reproduction — with [[esg|ESG]] scores rewarding companies that promote contraception and population control.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Second, it is leveraged to promote collectivism and global governance and to erode national sovereignty, with the Consortium influencing international agreements to push for centralised control over environmental policies.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

The environmental component of ESG scoring is identified as the narrative's enforcement mechanism: environmental scores are used to push climate change policies, reinforcing the narrative in corporate and governmental behaviour, while social and governance scores handle the parallel ideological work of [[queer-theory|Queer Theory]] and [[critical-race-theory|CRT]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Like the other vectors, the narrative is described as being integrated into education systems slowly, as part of psychological operations aimed at shaping the perspectives of successive generations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## Wildfire policy dispute (2026)

The narrative was invoked in Badlands commentary on the [[canadian-wildfire-smoke-crisis-2026|2026 Canadian wildfire smoke crisis]], in which [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that climate advocates attribute the fires to a warming, drying earth yet campaign against the forest management that would prevent them.[2] The commentary framed the movement as needing fires to validate its predictions and described President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s sanctions threat against Canada as "taking on the climate cult."[2]

## Pregnancy heat-risk framing (May 2025)

A May 2025 CBS News report highlighted by Badlands linked early-season heat waves in the central and southern United States to health dangers during pregnancy, citing an analysis by the nonprofit Climate Central that found climate change nearly doubled the average number of "pregnancy heat-risk days" — extremely hot days linked to increased risk of preterm birth — in the U.S. from 2020 to 2024.[3] Dr. Céline Gounder, a CBS News medical contributor and editor-at-large for public health at KFF Health News, said such days raise risks of heat stroke, heat exhaustion and preterm birth, because pregnancy stresses the heart, increases blood volume and makes dehydration more likely.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the story was political rather than medical, pointing to Gounder's complaint that surveillance work by NOAA and the CDC was being cut by the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], and to an accompanying study claiming homicide and suicide kill more pregnant people nationwide than any medical cause — a study she said was pushing gun control in the name of pregnant women.[3] She concluded that the broader narrative aims to make women afraid of pregnancy, and advised staying hydrated and using a fan, adding that this was not medical advice.[3]
