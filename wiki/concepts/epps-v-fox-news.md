# Epps v. Fox News

Epps v. Fox News is the defamation lawsuit filed in July 2023 by [[ray-epps]] against [[fox-news]], accusing former host [[tucker-carlson]] of defaming him by portraying him as a government plant in the January 6, 2021 events at the United States Capitol.[1]

## Background

Epps was seen on video goading Trump supporters to enter the Capitol on January 6, 2021, and had long been suspected within alternative media of being a federal agent or asset.[1] In a text message to his nephew he wrote: "I was in the front with a few others. I also orchestrated it."[1] In March 2023 Epps retained attorney [[michael-teter|Michael Teter]], formerly of [[perkins-coie]], who sent Carlson a letter demanding retraction of "false and defamatory statements" characterizing Epps as a January 6 government plant.[1]

## The filing and disclosure of impending charges

The Wednesday, July 12, 2023 lawsuit disclosed that Epps would be criminally charged over January 6.[1] The filing stated that "in May 2023, the Department of Justice notified Epps that it would seek to charge him criminally for events on January 6, 2021—two-and-a-half years later," and argued that "the relentless attacks by Fox and Mr. Carlson and the resulting political pressure likely resulted in the criminal charges."[1] Epps, then 62, was described in coverage aggregated by [[zerohedge]] as a key instigator of the riot.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], argued that the premise of Epps's case — that Fox was responsible for public belief that he was a federal confidential human source — is undercut by the fact that crowds chanted "Fed! Fed! Fed!" at him on January 5, 2021 after he declared, alongside [[john-sullivan|John "JaydenX" Sullivan]], "Tomorrow, we're gonna go into the Capitol!"[1] The commentary held that of all January 6 defendants the evidence against Epps was the most damning, that he was nonetheless removed from the FBI watch list and treated favorably by the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]], and that disgraced former Rep. [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]] coached him through his committee testimony.[1]

The timing was also emphasized: Rep. [[troy-nehls|Troy Nehls]] had questioned [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] about the failure to charge Epps, playing video of Epps at the Capitol, and Wray declined to comment except to object to the suggestion the FBI would incite an insurrection; hours later news broke that prosecutors planned to charge Epps.[1] Ashe in America read the sequence as a pacification message — "Trust the system" — and concluded that any charges from the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] came far too late to alter public belief, particularly given the long imprisonment of other January 6 defendants for lesser conduct.[1]
