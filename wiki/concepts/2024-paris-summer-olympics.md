# 2024 Paris Summer Olympics

The 2024 Summer Olympic Games were held in Paris, France, opening in late July 2024 with a ceremony staged along the Seine rather than in a stadium.[1]

## Stated themes and criticism

The stated tenets of the 2024 Games were Community, Diversity and The Collective — a framing that ZeroHedge characterized bluntly as meaning "the theme of this year's Olympic Games is woke."[1] Earlier signals had drawn similar criticism: in May 2024 it was revealed that drag queens and transgender activists would carry the Olympic Torch, despite the convention that torchbearers are selected from people who have made significant contributions to their communities.[1]

## Opening ceremony

The opening ceremony was planned by "queer artistic director" Thomas Jolly, who said he wanted "everyone to feel represented."[1] It featured performances by a large cast of drag queens, sexualized dancing, and a tableau widely described as an LGBT recreation of The Last Supper.[1] Viewership in the United States was projected to hit record lows — below even the 2022 Beijing Winter Games — according to Gallup polling cited by ZeroHedge.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Luciferian and Illuminati symbolism in Olympic opening ceremonies was not new, dating back at least to London 2012, but that audiences were now noticing — and being censored for saying so.[1] She criticized the ceremony's "pornographic runway" and the segment featuring a blue-painted performer she read as a mockery of God, while praising other portions: the metal band performing at the castle while Marie Antoinette held her head, the parade of athletes on boats down the Seine, and the Eiffel Tower light show.[1] She argued that the conduct of the ceremony's organizers should not cause Americans to abandon Team USA athletes, recalling watching Michael Johnson become the first-ever double gold medalist in the 200m and 400m at the 1996 Games.[1]

## Competition notes

Surfing, which debuted as an Olympic event at Tokyo 2020, was held for the Paris Games in Tahiti, French Polynesia — roughly 10,000 miles from the host city.[1] The Badlands commentary noted that the US gymnastics team was off to a strong start and that cyclist Haley Batten took silver in women's mountain biking, the best-ever American finish in that event.[1]
