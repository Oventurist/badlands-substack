# Flywheel of Freedom

The **Flywheel of Freedom** is a rhetorical and organizing metaphor advanced by Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] in her December 9, 2022 essay "We Are 'The Plan'," in which she urges rank-and-file political activists to understand their individual, seemingly minor efforts as cumulative pushes on a massive rotating disk that eventually reaches unstoppable momentum.[1]

## Origin of the metaphor

The image is borrowed directly from the business writer Jim Collins and his book *Good to Great: Why Some Companies Make the Leap … and Others Don't*, which Gillespie quotes at length.[1] Collins asks the reader to picture a metal flywheel roughly thirty feet in diameter, two feet thick, and weighing about five thousand pounds, mounted horizontally on an axle. The first pushes produce almost imperceptible movement; only after hours of persistent effort does the wheel complete a single rotation. With consistent pushing in the same direction the wheel gathers speed — three turns, ten, a hundred — until a breakthrough occurs and the wheel's own weight carries it forward.[1]

Collins's key point, which Gillespie adopts as her thesis, is that no single push can be identified as the decisive one: the outcome is "all of them added together in an overall accumulation of effort applied in a consistent direction," and any individual heave, however large, represents only a small fraction of the total effect.[1]

## Political application

Gillespie recasts the flywheel as a model of civic participation for the populist and [[qanon|Q]]-adjacent movement. Rather than waiting passively for institutional actors to deliver justice, she argues, ordinary citizens supply the repeated small pushes that generate political momentum.[1] The essay explicitly ties this to the slogan "We are the plan" — the claim that the awaited [[trust-the-plan|plan]] is not an external operation to be observed but a collective effort in which every participant has a role.[1]

Concrete "pushes" listed in the article include meeting and educating neighbors, joining a church community or community garden, volunteering with charities (delivering meals to the homebound, repairing cars for single mothers, recording books for the blind, knitting blankets for seniors, making sandwiches for the homeless), sharing Badlands Media videos and Substack articles, getting involved in election integrity work, listing a business on the values-based marketplace [[publicsq|PublicSq]], and praying without ceasing.[1]

## Related arguments

Gillespie links the flywheel image to the [[tea-party-movement|Tea Party movement]] that emerged spontaneously in 2009, which she cites as proof that a small group of dedicated people working in a consistent direction can alter the composition of the legislative branch.[1] She also credits [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s [[war-room|Warroom]] program with generating real momentum by directing tens or even hundreds of thousands of listeners into election work ahead of the [[2022-midterm-elections|2022 midterms]], describing each such activist as another push on the flywheel.[1]
