# Drain the Swamp

"Drain the swamp" is the slogan adopted by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during his 2016 campaign to describe the removal of entrenched, self-dealing interests from Washington. In [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] commentary the phrase has acquired a broader meaning: not merely the expulsion of corrupt actors but the *exposure* of them, on the theory that the network could not be dismantled until it was visible.

Writing on the [[2023-trump-cpac-speech|2023 Trump CPAC speech]], [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] used the swamp metaphor to argue that Trump's principal first-term achievement was informational. For decades, he wrote, the political "chess board" had "lay sunken at the bottom of a murky swamp (by Design)," making it difficult to glimpse through the dark waters; Trump "drained the swamp and now we know the good pieces, the bad pieces and have been studying their moves."[1] Getz described the resulting transparency as "open viewing for the citizens of the world," with no further need to soften descriptions of "evil deeds" as merely "bad deals" or "not being smart."[1]

Getz cited Trump's CPAC boast that a second term would be more effective because of this reconnaissance: "now I am experienced and I know the people of Washington. I didn't know them. I was from New York. I only came here 17 times," and "I now know the good ones, the bad ones, the weak ones, the strong ones—I know em all!"[1] Getz added that the wider community had also "picked apart the Washington Establishment brick by brick," assembling an organized view of "who does what, how it works and where the money flows," while Trump had spent four years studying the same actors "right in the belly of the beast."[1] The speech ended with Trump's pledge, "I will totally obliterate the [[deep-state|Deep State]]."[1]
