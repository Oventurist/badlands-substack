# Diamond Alternative Energy v. Environmental Protection Agency

*Diamond Alternative Energy v. Environmental Protection Agency* is a case argued before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] that could determine the fate of California's [[clean-air-act|Clean Air Act]] waiver — the mechanism behind what has been widely described as a de facto electric vehicle (EV) mandate in more than a dozen states.[1]

## Oral argument

The Court heard oral arguments on Wednesday, April 23, 2025.[1] While the case does not directly center on the legality of the waiver itself, the Court's ruling on whether the petitioners have standing could set a precedent that energy experts called "massive," creating an opportunity for the administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to "balance" emissions standards set by what those experts termed "extreme climate policy."[1] Elimination of the waiver, the policy experts told the Daily Caller News Foundation, could lead to greater consumer freedom and cheaper vehicle prices in California, the District of Columbia, and the 17 other states that follow aspects of California's emissions standards.[1]

Jeffrey Wall, counsel for Diamond Alternative Energy, argued that "the [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] waiver here allows California to limit the number of vehicles that run on liquid fuel. Petitioners make and sell liquid fuel, so vacating the waiver would redress their injuries," and invoked Justice Brett Kavanaugh's reasoning in *Energy Future Coalition* that "part of the injury in a case like this one is the denial even to compete in the marketplace."[1] Pressed on specificity by Justice Clarence Thomas, Wall said he was "talking about a market restriction that directly tilts or forecloses the playing field. It says you can't sell your product, your good, your service into a particular market."[1]

Myron Ebell, former senior fellow and director for the Center for Energy and Environment at the Competitive Enterprise Institute, told the Daily Caller News Foundation that "the entire future of the auto industry, and what kinds of automobiles people are allowed to buy, what choices they have … All of that is at stake in this ruling," adding, "now we finally are at the point where we can win this debate."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the core question as whether California can rig energy markets, identifying the liquid fuel market as the specific market at issue.[1] She noted that California is the fifth largest economy in the world — behind the United States, China, Germany, and Japan — and the second-largest total energy consumer among the states after Texas, though its per capita energy consumption is the fourth-lowest in the nation, citing the California State Energy Profile from the U.S. Energy Information Administration as of April 17, 2025.[1] Removing California's ability to manipulate the liquid and alternative fuel markets, she argued, is notable given persistent claims that the country stands at the precipice of a new energy future.[1]
