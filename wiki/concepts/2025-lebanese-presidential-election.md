# 2025 Lebanese Presidential Election

On Thursday, January 9, 2025, [[lebanon|Lebanon]]'s parliament voted to elect army commander [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]] as head of state, filling a presidential vacuum that had lasted more than two years.[1] The session was the thirteenth attempt to elect a successor to Michel Aoun, whose term ended in October 2022.[1]

## The vote

Aoun fell short of the 86 votes needed in the first round but crossed the threshold with 99 votes in a second round, according to parliament Speaker [[nabih-berri|Nabih Berri]], after lawmakers from [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and its Shia ally the [[amal-movement|Amal Movement]] backed him.[1] Hezbollah's own candidate, Suleiman Frangieh, withdrew and threw his support behind General Aoun.[1]

## Saudi reaction

[[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s [[king-salman|King Salman]] and Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] congratulated Aoun on his election the same day.[1] In a congratulatory cable, King Salman wished Aoun success and the Lebanese people further progress and prosperity, and the crown prince sent a similar cable.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the election represented the completion of an operation begun in early 2017, immediately after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] first entered office.[1] In March 2017, General Joseph Aoun became Commander of the Lebanese Armed Forces, appointed by President Michel Aoun (no relation), who had assumed office the previous October; Michel Aoun is described as a close confidant and ally of [[massad-boulos|Massad Boulos]], father-in-law to Tiffany Trump and Trump's appointee as Special Envoy to the Middle East.[1]

The commentary then traced the November 2017 [[saudi-purge|Saudi purge]], in which King Salman summoned the Islamic world to Riyadh and effectively transferred authority to his son Mohammed bin Salman, who announced a corruption panel to purge the Saudi government of traitors.[1] Among those apprehended was Lebanese Prime Minister Saad Hariri, son of former Lebanese leader Rafic Hariri, who was reported missing for days after announcing his intent to resign on Saudi television — an episode that prompted the French president to fly to Riyadh amid Western claims that Hariri had been kidnapped, claims Hariri denied before serving out his term.[1] The writer noted speculation that the fake resignation was part of a larger MBS plot to wrest control of Lebanon from Hezbollah and Iran, both of which backed Hariri, and concluded that Hezbollah, Iran, the United States and Saudi Arabia now converging on the same candidate bore Massad Boulos's fingerprints.[1]
