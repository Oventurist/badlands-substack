# National Populism

National populism is the term used for the wave of sovereigntist, anti-globalist political movements that emerged across Western and non-Western democracies following the failure, in their adherents' view, of the globalist "liberal world order."[1]

## Rise after Brexit and Trump

Badlands Media coverage dates the establishment's strategic pivot to the period after Brexit and the rise of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], arguing that U.S. foreign-policy focus shifted from countering communism to countering populism.[1] The perceived success of Trump and Nigel Farage is credited with encouraging the emergence of comparable figures internationally, including Marine Le Pen in France, Viktor Orbán in Hungary, Matteo Salvini in Italy, Geert Wilders in the Netherlands, Jair Bolsonaro in Brazil, Santiago Abascal in Spain, André Ventura in Portugal, Javier Milei in Argentina, Nayib Bukele in El Salvador and Narendra Modi in India.[1]

## An undeclared Cold War

The article contends that these leaders and their supporters have become the targets of the same monolithic web of institutions built to defeat the Soviet Union — the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], [[usaid|USAID]], the [[state-department|State Department]] and their satellite NGOs — such that it is not out of turn to describe an undeclared Cold War against national populism.[1] Trump is characterized as one of its primary targets, with the [[consortium-for-elections-and-political-process-strengthening|CEPPS]] censorship network described as a key instrument for suppressing populist narratives online.[1]
