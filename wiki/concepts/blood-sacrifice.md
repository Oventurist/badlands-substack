# Blood Sacrifice

**Blood sacrifice** is examined at length in the June 21, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Zombie Apocalypse vs. The Great Awakening" by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], which asks what the underlying purpose of the practice is and answers that it belongs to a system of control rather than to any genuine divine order.[1]

## Sacrifice-demanding deities

The essay observes that a great number of mankind's ancient gods are famous for demanding sacrifice from their followers, and that many of these deities conspicuously appear to view creation the way an investor views a balance sheet, demanding payment for a wide variety of "transactions" in blood.[1] [[baal|Ba'al]]/[[molech|Moloch]] is given as a well-known example of a god demanding constant sacrifice in perpetuity; certain [[aztecs|Aztec]] gods are said to have borne the same demands; and the phenomenon is described as known worldwide under many names.[1]

Such "deities" are characterised as running a certain type of economy — one designed to satisfy their own wants and desires.[1] The essay concludes that the true purpose of blood sacrifice comes down to the preferences and tastes of those who wish to usurp God's creation and zombify the masses for their own purposes, and that the claim of these false rulers to operate under a divine mandate is a total lie.[1]

## Sacrifice as an inversion of life

Sacrificial blood ritual is said to have nothing to do with the Will of God.[1] The essay notes that the word *evil* is an inversion of the word *live*, taking this to imply that living and doing evil are in some fundamental sense opposite actions working in opposite directions, since God's Will is for life to live, grow and fulfil its potential.[1] It cites researchers — notably [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]] — for the view that at least one purpose of blood-ritual sacrifice is to "give the middle finger" to God.[1]

## Etymology, consideration and stakes

The essay observes that the root of the word "sacrifice" is "sacred," which on one level of interpretation connects to the ideas of *effort* and *investment*, and asks whether language itself has been deliberately engineered to blend sacred and blasphemous concepts within the same words.[1] It argues that in a real sense every decision or action is a sacrifice, since choosing one thing means not choosing another and every investment carries opportunity costs.[1]

A further link is drawn to the legal concept of *consideration* — something substantial offered up to satisfy a contract or pact — and to the common question of what is at *stake* in a deal, which the author reads as more sacrifice language and symbolism.[1] This blending of dark-occult, religious, legal and financial concepts within a single word is presented as telling a great deal about how the world really works.[1]

## Insane pseudo-gods

The essay holds that, regardless of what "polite society" says, there is nothing unreasonable or irrational in supposing that humanity is dealing with insane, death-worshipping pseudo-gods and their elite proxies.[1] It allows the possibility that these elites are aided by an off-world intelligence or by something demonic while declining to speculate deeply, and stresses instead that they appear heavily invested in remaining undiscovered — asking why they would go to such lengths if they were as powerful as they wish to appear.[1] Framing the relationship as one of parasite and host, the author argues the cults need humanity while humanity has no need of them, and that evil need not be feared but only recognised and refused.[1]
