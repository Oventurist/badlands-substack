# 2024 Newspaper Non-Endorsements

The **2024 newspaper non-endorsements** refers to the decisions by two major American legacy newspapers — the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] and [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] — to decline to endorse a candidate in the 2024 presidential race, and the internal revolts, resignations and subscription cancellations that followed.[1] Badlands Media covered the episode in its October 28, 2024 News Brief as a marker of the collapsing authority of the institutional press during the [[information-war|Information War]].[1]

## Sequence of events

The controversy began in the week before October 28, 2024, when the Los Angeles Times, described by Badlands as "a rabidly leftist paper," declined to endorse a candidate in the 2024 presidential race.[1] At least three members of the paper's editorial board resigned in protest, and public outrage was directed at the paper's owner rather than at the editorial process itself.[1]

The following Friday, The Washington Post also announced that it would make no presidential endorsement, a decision attributed to owner [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]].[1] Again, editorial board members resigned, and the backlash was substantially larger than in the Los Angeles case.[1] Editor-at-large [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]], husband of [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]], quit over the decision.[1]

## Newsroom reaction

Reporting aggregated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] described a staff "meltdown," liberal readers cancelling subscriptions, and internal discussions of mass resignation.[1] One Post editorial board member told *Semafor* that "people are shocked, furious, surprised," adding, "If you don't have the balls to own a newspaper, don't."[1] The paper's chief technology officer was reported to have directed engineers to block reader questions about the non-endorsement on an internal system.[1] Another board member told [[brian-stelter|Brian Stelter]] that the decision was "an outrageous abdication of responsibility," asserting that "Democracy doesn't die in darkness, it dies when people anticipatorily consent to a fascist's whims" — an inversion of the Post's own masthead slogan.[1]

Veteran Watergate reporters [[bob-woodward|Bob Woodward]] and Carl Bernstein publicly demanded that the free press act out of a duty not merely to engage in politics but to endorse the Democratic nominee, [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the affair as legacy journalists condemning newspapers for failing to endorse the journalists' own preferred candidate, making the press itself the story.[1] She contrasted the episode with 2009, when Washington bureaus jointly rebuked the Obama administration over its treatment of Fox News on institutional-standards grounds — a norm she argued no longer exists.[1] She offered competing explanations for the owners' choices: that proprietors had decided to stop letting "the inmates run the asylum," or that non-endorsement was simply the correct business decision amid declining readership and a credibility crisis, noting Bezos's record of subordinating ideological positioning to commercial interest.[1]

Ashe concluded that many ordinary readers were responding not by cancelling subscriptions but by concluding that their trusted journalists had been political propagandists all along, writing that "the trash is taking itself out."[1] The episode was discussed further on the Badlands program *Culture of Change* episode 79.[1]
