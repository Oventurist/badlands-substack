# Civil Rights Movement

The Civil Rights Movement of the 1960s is characterized in a December 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] as one of several decade-defining campaigns that he argues were engineered rather than organic.[1] Carlson writes that the Civil Rights Movement "benefitted Blacks the least," paralleling his claim that the [[womens-movement|Women's Movement]] benefited women least, and that the true beneficiaries of both were the banking, real estate, retail, and service industries.[1]

He asserts that both movements were heavily orchestrated by the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[cia|CIA]], stating that "there was nothing organic about either of them," while explicitly adding that he is not arguing against equal treatment for every human being — only that these two movements in particular were, in his framing, [[deep-state|Cabal]]-run psyops.[1]

The essay places the Civil Rights Movement within a broader argument that the entire decade of the 1960s functioned as a psychological operation against the [[baby-boomers|Baby Boomer]] generation, alongside the hippie counterculture, drug culture, and the Women's Movement, all set against the backdrop of the Vietnam War.[1] Carlson contrasts this with the cultural output of the [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] years, citing *The Cosby Show* replacing *Good Times* as an example of Black Americans no longer being portrayed as poor and struggling.[1]
