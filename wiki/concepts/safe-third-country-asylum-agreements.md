# Safe Third Country Asylum Agreements

Safe third country agreements are bilateral arrangements under which the United States transfers asylum seekers to another nation to pursue protection claims there rather than in the US.[1] By late 2025 they had become the central instrument of the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's effort to close what it described as "a huge loophole" in legal immigration — the asylum request.[1]

## Implementation

Over the final two months of 2025 it became apparent how the administration intended to close that loophole.[1] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] began asking courts to summarily dismiss asylum claims without a hearing and to send migrants to a third country where they could pursue relief, even when the migrant had no connection whatsoever to that place.[1] The federal government relied on safe third country agreements that Trump officials had reached with a number of nations, including Uganda, Honduras and Ecuador — countries with reputations for destabilizing gang violence or histories of human-rights abuses.[1]

The effort represented the latest attempt by the administration to curtail immigration to the United States and to meet its stated annual deportation goal of one million people.[1]
