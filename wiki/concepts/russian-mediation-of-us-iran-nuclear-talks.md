# Russian Mediation of US–Iran Nuclear Talks

In early March 2025 it was widely reported that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had agreed to help the Trump White House broker talks with Iran on curtailing the country's nuclear program — a development described at the time as very unexpected and unlikely.[1]

## Origins of the request

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] reportedly relayed the request for Putin to play a direct role in new negotiations with Iran during their February 2025 phone call.[1] The topic was further broached and additional details discussed during the US–Russia talks in Riyadh that followed, according to Bloomberg reporting on Tuesday, March 4, 2025.[1]

## Official responses

Neither the Iranian nor the US governments publicly commented on the Bloomberg report, which was based on anonymous sourcing.[1] Russian state media quickly acknowledged that Moscow stood ready to help, with TASS reporting that Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] told Bloomberg that "Russia believes that the United States and Iran should resolve all problems through negotiations," adding that Moscow "is ready to do everything in its power to achieve this."[1]

## Badlands analysis

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted the timing alongside Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s same-day flight to the United States to meet the White House, arguing that Smotrich would be seeking Trump's support for an Israeli conquest of Palestine and needed it because Iran and the Arab states — backed by China and Russia — would move against Israel if it proceeded.[1] He predicted the negotiations would result in the US blessing an expanded Iranian nuclear energy program as well as a new nuclear energy program in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], something Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman had sought for years, observing that the United States built Iran's first nuclear reactor in 1975 and that Russia has helped maintain it.[1] Such developments, he argued, would trigger outrage in Tel Aviv, which is why reconciliation between Tehran and Washington was so timely.[1]
