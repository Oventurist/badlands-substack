# 2026 United States Midterm Elections

The 2026 United States midterm elections are the congressional elections scheduled for November 2026, which will determine control of the House and Senate for the final two years of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidency. They are the subject of the second item in the January 19, 2026 Badlands Brief.

## Trump's remarks

In a January 2026 Reuters interview, Trump suggested that the United States "shouldn't even have an election" that year because of what he described as the success of his administration. [[white-house|White House]] Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the president was speaking facetiously and was referring to his view that the country was "on the right track." In the same interview Trump acknowledged concerns within his party about potential losses, noting that the party of an incumbent president historically loses seats in [[congress|Congress]]. He also told House Republicans in early January that the party must win the midterms or risk impeachment proceedings if Democrats retook control.

## Polling and policy context

A national polling average aggregated by RealClearPolitics showed Democrats holding a narrow lead on the generic congressional ballot, with Democratic candidates at roughly 46 percent and Republican candidates at about 41 percent. Concurrent surveys placed Trump's job approval below 50 percent, with disapproval higher in several recent polls. Ahead of the elections Trump emphasised policy initiatives including a $50 billion rural health plan aimed at the difficulties of rural healthcare systems.

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responds by noting that critics worried about Trump cancelling the midterms include supporters of Ukraine, "which cancelled elections years ago 'for democracy' and never looked back." She summarises the argument advanced by CNN, drawing on Congressional Research Service work, as holding that "cancelling fake elections is worse than elections being fake," which she rejects as silly.

Her position is that American elections are fraudulent by design: "Fake elections are treason. They aren't fake by accident. They're fake on purpose, and a lot of people are in on it," she writes, arguing that the participants act "to serve the interests of globalism" and that accountability must fall on Americans rather than on foreign nations or a rewritten account of the role of U.S. intelligence. She concludes that any postponement by Trump and Congress would occur only because elections are fake and "in service of accountability and due process," adding that "Trump will only cancel elections *for democracy*. And that's a good thing."
