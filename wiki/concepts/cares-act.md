# CARES Act

The **CARES Act** (Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act) was a $2.2 trillion emergency spending bill signed into law by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2020 in response to the COVID-19 pandemic.[1] Beyond its economic provisions, the Act authorized the placement of federal inmates into home confinement during the public health emergency, a mechanism that later became the legal foundation for a major clemency action.[1]

## Role in the 2024 clemency action

The nearly 1,500 individuals whose sentences were commuted in the [[2024-biden-mass-clemency-action|2024 Biden mass clemency action]] had been serving their sentences at home for at least one year under the COVID-era CARES Act, according to the [[white-house|White House]] fact sheet accompanying the December 12, 2024 announcement.[1] Commentary at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted the irony that a bill signed by Trump had become "a key part of Biden's strategy."[1]

## Fiscal scale and criticism

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the CARES Act as "inflation super-spreading," pointing out that the 2020 stimulus was roughly $1.4 trillion larger than the $831 billion stimulus passed in 2009 to combat the Great Recession — a comparison she raised against the refusal of contemporary economists to acknowledge a recession.[1]
