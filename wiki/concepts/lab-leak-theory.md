# Lab-Leak Theory

The lab-leak theory holds that COVID-19 originated from a laboratory in Wuhan, China, rather than from natural zoonotic spillover.[1] Its proponents, including figures in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], pushed a series of legal and administrative cases in 2026 as accountability for scientists they claim hid evidence of a possible laboratory origin.[1]

## 2026 accountability wave

Beginning in April 2026, actions against U.S. scientists affiliated with the [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases]] included debarments, indictments, arrests, removals from leadership, and congressional subpoenas, with enforcement authority shifting toward the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[1] Cases involved [[ralph-baric|Ralph Baric]], [[david-morens|David Morens]], [[jeffrey-taubenberger|Jeffrey Taubenberger]], and [[vincent-munster|Vincent Munster]], and culminated on July 7, 2026 with Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] announcing a subpoena for [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] to testify before the Senate.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] characterized the cluster of cases as "lab-leak payback," noting that all five centered on the small NIAID-affiliated scientific community.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the magazine's lament reflected its own complicity, recalling its October 2022 call for a pandemic "amnesty."[1]

Badlands commentary rejects the lab-leak framing itself, arguing that COVID "didn't leak from a lab" but was instead a set of physiological and psychological contagions deployed on the American people — a "portfolio of crimes against humanity" connected to fake elections, the autopen controversy, and RussiaGate as parts of a single "grand treasonous conspiracy."[1] The stated conclusion is that there can be no "Golden Age without a Justice Phase."[1]

## CIA whistleblower testimony (2026)

In May 2026, [[cia|CIA]] whistleblower and current agency employee [[james-erdman-iii|James Erdman III]] told the [[senate-homeland-security-and-governmental-affairs-committee|Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee]] that [[anthony-fauci|Dr. Anthony Fauci]] had influenced how the agency handled its assessment of a potential lab leak.[2] Erdman testified that the CIA was "considering calling this a lab leak" on August 12, 2021, after a 90-day review, but that its position changed on August 17, 2021, with no documentation provided to explain the reversal.[2]

He alleged that Fauci's role in what he called a cover-up was "intentional," carried out in part by steering the intelligence community toward "curated" lists of subject matter experts and scientists in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia, and that conflict-of-interest concerns tied to Wuhan-linked research went unraised internally.[2]

## The 2023 House select subcommittee hearing

The first hearing of the House select subcommittee investigating COVID-19 origins, held March 8, 2023, produced two central pieces of testimony for proponents of the theory. Former [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] Director [[robert-redfield]] said he had been sidelined from internal origins debates and that [[anthony-fauci]] did not appreciate his support for a laboratory origin.[3]

[[jim-jordan]] focused on virologists [[kristian-andersen]] and [[robert-garry]], who had written to Fauci on January 31, 2020 that the virus displayed "unusual features" that "(potentially) look engineered" and that colleagues found the genome "inconsistent with expectations from evolutionary theory," only to reverse their public position three days later following a conference call with Fauci and [[francis-collins]].[3] Jordan tied the reversal to roughly $9 million in subsequent grant funding.[3] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] presented the sequence as evidence that the early scientific consensus against a lab origin was manufactured through funding leverage rather than reached through evidence.[3]

### CIA whistleblower allegations (September 2023)

Whistleblower testimony provided to Congress in September 2023 alleged that the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] had offered to pay six of the seven analysts on its COVID Discovery Team to abandon their finding that SARS-CoV-2 most likely originated in a Wuhan laboratory and instead assess that the virus had jumped from animals to humans.[4] The panel chairmen wrote that "six of the seven members of the Team believed the intelligence and science were sufficient to make a low confidence assessment that COVID-19 originated from a laboratory in Wuhan, China."[4] [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] and [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] demanded the relevant documents and pay information from CIA Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] by 26 September 2023.[4] Badlands Media commentary noted the reversal of fortune for a hypothesis whose mention had once been banned from public discourse, and pushed further, asking whether the event was "not a leak, but a launch" and when mainstream discussion would critically examine Event 201.[4]

## COVID.gov redirect, April 2025

In April 2025, the [[white-house|White House]] redirected COVID.gov to a new landing page titled "Lab Leak: True Origins of COVID-19," which makes a five-point argument for the theory that the virus originated from a mistaken laboratory leak in Wuhan, China.[5] The new site appeared to draw on the final report of the Republican-led Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic, released in December 2024, though there has never been a consensus or a "smoking gun" explaining the pandemic's start.[5] As recently as the previous week, the COVID.gov page had listed resources for testing, treatment, and vaccination, along with information about Long COVID.[5]

The five pieces of evidence advanced by the White House were that the "virus possesses a biological characteristic that is not found in nature"; that data show all cases "stem from a single introduction into humans"; that "Wuhan is home to China's foremost SARS research lab"; that researchers at that lab "were sick with COVID-like symptoms in the fall of 2019"; and that "if there was evidence of a natural origin it would have already surfaced."[5]

Badlands commentator [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] pushed past the origins debate to question whether there was a pandemic at all, arguing that a genuine pandemic would not have circulated for four to five months with no mitigation while concerts, sporting events, bars, restaurants, and small businesses stayed open in a booming economy.[5] He wrote that the deaths were largely confined to hospitals following what he called a deadly medical protocol, recorded for profit, and diagnosed with tests that did not work or on symptoms previously called the flu.[5] Absent the experts, television personalities, death counters, and public shame campaigns, he argued, no one would have suspected anything was happening, and society had been "Hoodwinked."[5]

## 2023 Slack message disclosures

In July 2023, ZeroHedge — drawing on reporting from Racket and Public — published newly released Slack messages in which authors of the [[proximal-origin-paper|Proximal Origin]] paper privately described a Wuhan lab leak as "so friggin' likely," even as the published paper and subsequent media coverage dismissed the hypothesis.[6] Independent researcher Yuri Deigin, long derided for raising the possibility, said the messages "confirm what we long suspected."[6] Reporting stressed that the documents do not establish that the virus escaped the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] or that American scientists were implicated, but that they show scientists and journalists prioritizing a "single message" over open inquiry.[6] Josh Rogin of the Washington Post was cited as one of the few mainstream journalists who continued pursuing the story.[6]

## Trump op-ed and the House "Proximal Origin" memo (March 2023)

In a March 2023 Daily Mail op-ed, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] wrote that three years earlier he had declared COVID-19 almost certainly came from the Chinese Wuhan lab and that the world was finally admitting the truth, calling the cover-up of the virus's origins one of the greatest scandals in history and putting the cost of the outbreak and the lying about it in excess of $50 trillion by some estimates.[7]

Days later, the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] alleged that [[anthony-fauci|Dr. Anthony Fauci]] had "prompted" the creation of the [[proximal-origin-paper|"Proximal Origin of SARS-CoV-2"]] paper specifically to rule out the laboratory hypothesis, after a February 1, 2020 call on which several scientists had warned the virus may have leaked from a Wuhan lab and may have been intentionally genetically manipulated.[7]


## Congressional declassification mandate (2023)

Congressional pressure on the lab-leak question produced the [[covid-19-origin-act-of-2023|COVID-19 Origin Act of 2023]], passed unanimously by both chambers and signed on March 20, 2023, which required the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] to declassify within 90 days all information on possible links between the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] and the origins of Covid-19.[9] The push followed the Energy Department's conclusion, held with "low confidence," that the virus was likely the result of an accidental laboratory leak in China.[9] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] warned that the resulting release could serve as a pretext for escalating tensions with China by attributing the outbreak to the Chinese state rather than to rogue elements within Wuhan.[9]

## Chinese rejection and counter-accusation (April 2025)

On April 30, 2025 the Chinese government issued [[2025-china-covid-origins-white-paper|a white paper]] insisting that the lab leak theory was "extremely unlikely" and accusing the United States of being the true origin point of COVID-19.[10] Beijing said "substantial evidence" suggested the virus might have been present in the United States earlier than the outbreak in China, and accused Washington of "shamelessly politicizing SARS-CoV-2 origins tracing" to divert attention from its own pandemic failures.[10] The counter-accusation followed the White House's revamping of its COVID-19 information earlier that month to assert that the virus had leaked from the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]], a position President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had publicly declared.[10]

Badlands commentary predicted that the Chinese narrative would incrementally lead back to Ukraine and the biological research facilities that [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]] acknowledged the United States had funded there, potentially implicating [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], [[metabiota|Metabiota]] and [[rosemont-seneca|Rosemont Seneca]].[10]
