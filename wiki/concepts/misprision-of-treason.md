# Misprision of Treason

Misprision of treason is the federal offense, codified in Title 18 of the [[united-states-code]], committed by a person owing allegiance to the United States who has knowledge of the commission of treason and conceals it rather than disclosing it to the President, a judge, or a state governor. It is a crime of omission: the offender need not participate in the underlying act, only know of it and stay silent.

## Use in Badlands commentary

The Badlands Brief of July 17, 2026 carried the standing title "Illegal Psychological Warfare Campaigns & Misprision of Treason," applying the concept to officials in the Intelligence Community who, according to materials released through the [[white-house-election-integrity-portal]], possessed knowledge of foreign threats to American election infrastructure and of [[china]]'s activities against the [[donald-trump]] campaign, but withheld that intelligence from the President and the public.

The argument advanced in the Brief is that withholding foreign-threat intelligence from the commander in chief during what the President described as a crisis worse than Pearl Harbor and 9/11 — and one involving that same foreign power — is not a bureaucratic failure but a concealment offense with statutory consequences. Paired in the same issue with the [[marco-rubio]]–[[stephen-miller]]–[[scott-bessent]] initiative against transnational political violence, the framing casts election subversion and ideological infiltration as "twin fronts of the same existential conflict," with prosecution rather than disclosure as the intended endpoint: "The time for empty talk is over. Now arrives the hour of action."
