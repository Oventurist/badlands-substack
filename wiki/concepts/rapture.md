# Rapture

The rapture is the belief that both living and dead believers will ascend into heaven to meet Jesus Christ at the Second Coming. It is subjected to sustained reinterpretation in the Badlands Media feature [[are-we-entering-the-millennial-reign|"Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?"]].

## Terminology

The article notes that the term "rapture" appears nowhere in the New Testament. The concept derives from the Apostle Paul's use of the phrase "be caught up" — *harpazō* in Greek, *rapio* in Latin — in 1 Thessalonians 4:17. The term is used in scripture to mean "to seize, carry off by force," "to seize on, claim for one's self eagerly," or "to snatch out or away," appearing also in Matthew 11:12 and 13:19, John 6:15, 10:12 and 10:28–29, Acts 8:39 and 23:10, 2 Corinthians 12:2 and 12:4, Jude 1:23, and Revelation 12:5. Further support is commonly drawn from 1 Corinthians 15:51–52, Matthew 24:40–41 and Luke 17:34–36.

## The article's reinterpretation

Patriots in Progress accepts that Paul described a real coming event but doubts it is synonymous with the disappearance of the church, both because the church is still present and because of what he calls unique data suggesting the world is already passing through [[great-tribulation|the Tribulation]]. Reading eschatological scripture, he finds believers pictured in the world at various points receiving warnings and encouragement, including the blessing on "the dead who die in the Lord from now on," which falls after the seal and trumpet judgments and before [[the-beast|the Beast]].

Examining Matthew 24:36–44, he observes that the passage does not specify *who* is taken — believer or non-believer — while stating clearly that the end times will resemble the days of Noah, when the flood removed the evil from the world. He therefore proposes that the rapture may instead be the process or event by which believers successfully endure the Tribulation, resist the [[mark-of-the-beast|Mark of the Beast]] and enter the [[millennial-reign|Millennial Reign]], summarized as: "the rapture will remove the evil." The dispute over a pre-Tribulation rapture is presented as the original motivation for the article's whole hypothesis.
