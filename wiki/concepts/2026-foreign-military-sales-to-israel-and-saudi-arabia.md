# 2026 Foreign Military Sales to Israel and Saudi Arabia

In late January and early February 2026, the US [[state-department|State Department]] approved Foreign Military Sales notifications totaling approximately $15.7 billion to [[israel|Israel]] and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], announced alongside a broader set of coordinated trade, energy, and arms moves by the administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Composition of the package

Roughly $6.67 billion was allocated to Israel, featuring 30 AH-64E Apache attack helicopters with associated weapons and equipment, plus 3,250 Joint Light Tactical Vehicles.[1] Approximately $9 billion was allocated to Saudi Arabia, primarily for Patriot Advanced Capability-3 (PAC-3) missile systems and roughly 730 interceptors with related support equipment.[1] The approvals were transmitted to [[congress|Congress]] amid ongoing regional tensions involving [[iran|Iran]] and broader Middle East security concerns.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the apparent contradiction of simultaneously arming Israel and re-arming Riyadh dissolves when viewed through the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] lens.[1] In that reading, Saudi Arabia is the premier "bridge player" in the Middle East theater, primed to fill a regional void as peacemaker and stabilizer while maintaining warm, functional ties with both Russia and China and anchoring the expansion of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]].[1] The package, on this account, balances Israel's security needs against broader regional calm — strengthening both parties while weakening the prospect of war — even as the mainstream narrative reads it as fuel for a NeoCon forever-war script.[1]

The same commentary extended the argument to the "Iranian knot": the administration is portrayed as beating war drums against Tehran while simultaneously cutting energy and investment deals with Iran's closest regional partners and defenders, isolating holdouts through sovereign encirclement and economic disentanglement rather than kinetic escalation.[1]
