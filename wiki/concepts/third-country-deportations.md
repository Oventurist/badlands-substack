# Third-Country Deportations

Third-country deportations are removals in which the United States deports a migrant to a country other than his or her nation of origin. The practice became a major legal flashpoint in 2025 when the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] allowed the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration to resume such removals with limited notice to the deportee.[1]

## Supreme Court ruling

On Monday, June 23, 2025, the Supreme Court ruled that the Trump administration could resume deporting migrants to countries other than their home nation with limited notice.[1] In a 6–3 decision that broke down along ideological lines, the justices stayed an April ruling by a Boston federal judge that had stymied the rapid deportation effort.[1] The conservative majority did not provide a rationale for the ruling.[1]

Justice [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] wrote a 19-page dissent joined by Justices [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]] and [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]].[1] "In matters of life and death, it is best to proceed with caution," Sotomayor wrote. "In this case, the Government took the opposite approach."[1] She added: "This Court now intervenes to grant the Government emergency relief from an order it has repeatedly defied. I cannot join so gross an abuse of the Court's equitable discretion."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the decision as part of a run of Supreme Court precedent clarifying the powers of the presidency and "neutering the lawfare strategy" standing between the people and what she called the Golden Age.[1] She characterized the dissenting liberal justices as opposing [[agenda-47|Agenda 47]] and wanting to retain "criminal invaders," concluding that "nothing can stop what is coming."[1] The removals connect to the broader enforcement push involving [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and proposals such as [[alligator-alcatraz|Alligator Alcatraz]].[1]
