# Oklahoma City Bombing

The Oklahoma City bombing was the 1995 attack on the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City, carried out using ammonium nitrate, a chemical used both as fertilizer and as an ingredient in explosives.[1] The explosion killed 168 people and injured approximately 850.[1]

## Relevance to the 2023 railcar disappearance

The bombing was invoked repeatedly in coverage of the April 2023 disappearance of roughly 30 tons — about 61,000 pounds — of ammonium nitrate shipped by [[dyno-nobel|Dyno Nobel]] from Cheyenne, Wyoming, to a rail stop in the Mojave Desert in California.[1] Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that ammonium nitrate "was a key ingredient in the 1995 Oklahoma City bombing" while questioning the shipper's theory that the entire load had leaked out of a sealed railcar in transit.[1]

## The attack in detail

On April 19, 1995, a Ryder rental truck pulled up to the [[alfred-p-murrah-federal-building|Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building]] in Oklahoma City, and at 9:02 a.m. it exploded, causing a third of the building to disintegrate from the force of the blast.[2] At least 168 people were killed and more than 680 injured; the explosion destroyed or damaged 324 other buildings across sixteen blocks, destroyed 86 cars, and caused over $652 million in damage.[2]

In less than ninety minutes the prime suspect, [[timothy-mcveigh|Timothy McVeigh]], was apprehended while driving without a license plate and carrying illegal weapons.[2] Within days, authorities used forensic evidence to link McVeigh and his accomplice [[terry-nichols|Terry Nichols]] to the explosion.[2]

## False flag interpretation

The Badlands Media essay "False Flags and the Occult" argues that both the media and the authorities ignored eyewitness accounts of more than one bomb found in the debris, and that — as with the September 11th attacks on the World Trade Center six years later — the debris was carted off and destroyed before a thorough examination could be completed, leaving an official narrative that ran counter to reports on the ground as the account of record.[2] The article states that many researchers regard the event as a government-sponsored PSYOP, a [[false-flag-operation|false flag]], and notes that it fell within the forty-day [[season-of-sacrifice|season of sacrifice]] described by [[mark-passio|Mark Passio]], alongside the Waco massacre of April 19, 1993 and the Columbine shootings of April 20, 1999.[2]
