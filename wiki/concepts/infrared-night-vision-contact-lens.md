# Infrared Night Vision Contact Lens

The infrared night vision contact lens is a wearable optical device developed by scientists to let wearers see in the dark, described in a study published in the journal Cell in May 2025.[1]

Unlike traditional night vision goggles, the lenses require no power source and allow wearers to perceive infrared and visible light simultaneously.[1] The lens uses tiny nanoparticles that absorb infrared light and convert it into wavelengths visible to mammalian eyes, specifically enabling detection of near-infrared light in the 800–1600 nanometre range.[1] Study senior author Tian Xue of the University of Science and Technology of China said the research "opens up the potential for non-invasive wearable devices to give people super-vision," with possible applications in emergency and rescue operations.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that bulky consumer night vision goggles had only recently been considered a major advance, asked what other technologies might be delivered through contact lenses, and suggested the announcement was a sign that "disclosure is rapidly accelerating."[1]
