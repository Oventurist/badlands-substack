# 15-Minute Cities

**15-minute cities** are an urban planning concept treated in Badlands Media commentary as an instrument of population control and social atomization.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Argument

In *America Has a CIA-Run Economy*, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that part of the technology push of the past twenty years is intended to minimize the human interactions people have daily: the less people interact, the less they connect, the less they care or empathize with one another, and ultimately the less they procreate.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

He describes the end goal as a condition in which people rarely leave their apartments in 15-minute cities — working from home, having food and goods delivered, and spending a Saturday evening at home with an AI robot partner. Carlson concedes this sounds implausible while insisting that considerable progress toward it has already been made, and dismisses the convenience justification.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) The reconfiguration of [[starbucks|Starbucks]] stores toward drive-throughs, pre-ordering and reduced seating is offered as concrete evidence of the trend.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Connection to WEF urban mobility targets

Badlands commentary has linked the 15-minute city concept to the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s May 2023 Urban Mobility Scorecard Tool, published with Visa under founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], which was reported as setting a goal of limiting 76% of private cars by 2050.[2] In that reading the mobility target and the 15-minute city are complementary: reducing the private vehicle fleet presupposes an urban form in which everything a resident needs is provided within a fifteen-minute distance of their front door.[2] Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized the pairing sarcastically as a future in which people "own nothing" and eat bugs, argued that whether the instrument is 15-minute cities or vaccine passports the Davos globalists keep devising bleaker visions of the future, and observed that even committed leftists in his experience showed little enthusiasm for the ruling class's dystopian plans — a gap he expected propaganda to close.[2]

## Oxford protests (February 2023)

On Saturday, February 18, 2023, thousands of protesters marched in Oxford, England, against the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]-backed concept of 15-minute cities, after the local council announced it would trial such a scheme.[3] The Oxfordshire County and City Councils had proposed a "traffic filter system" using licence-plate-reading cameras to fine residents who drive to other parts of the city without an exemption or resident permit during certain hours, with a trial phase due to begin the following year as part of the wider 15-minute city movement.[3]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the concept as "a totalitarian nightmare" rooted in a modern socialist agenda, arguing it strengthened the case for leaving major cities in favour of self-reliant rural communities. He contended that the Davos elite assume ordinary people are dependent enough on modern conveniences to accept any edict, dismissed the climate justification as an insult to public intelligence given the absence of comparable restrictions on private jets and yachts, and linked the scheme to United Nations-aligned programmes such as [[agenda-2030|Agenda 2030]], America 2050, and the Wildlands Project.[3]

## Historical framing by Justin Deschamps

In *Trump's 2025 Vision*, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] placed the 15-minute city at the end of a long arc of deliberate settlement engineering, arguing that planners had worked since before the first industrial revolution to drain rural, church-centered communities into large cities functioning as neo-feudal estates, later extending the pattern to centrally dependent suburbs.[4] Under the [[great-reset|Great Reset]], he wrote, all of these forms would be compacted into the 15-minute city model.[4]

Deschamps contrasted this trajectory with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s proposal for ten new [[freedom-cities|Freedom Cities]] and accompanying baby bonuses, which he argued cut directly against the 15-minute megacity agenda and its supporting narrative of manmade climate change driven by overpopulation.[4] He treated the existence of a visibly superior alternative as the decisive threat to the model, invoking the [[asch-conformity-experiments|Asch conformity experiments]] to argue that conformity to an unattractive vision requires the removal of all contradictory examples.[4]
