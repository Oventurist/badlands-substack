# 15-Minute Cities

**15-minute cities** are an urban planning concept treated in Badlands Media commentary as an instrument of population control and social atomization.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Argument

In *America Has a CIA-Run Economy*, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that part of the technology push of the past twenty years is intended to minimize the human interactions people have daily: the less people interact, the less they connect, the less they care or empathize with one another, and ultimately the less they procreate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

He describes the end goal as a condition in which people rarely leave their apartments in 15-minute cities — working from home, having food and goods delivered, and spending a Saturday evening at home with an AI robot partner. Carlson concedes this sounds implausible while insisting that considerable progress toward it has already been made, and dismisses the convenience justification.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) The reconfiguration of [[starbucks|Starbucks]] stores toward drive-throughs, pre-ordering and reduced seating is offered as concrete evidence of the trend.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Connection to WEF urban mobility targets

Badlands commentary has linked the 15-minute city concept to the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s May 2023 Urban Mobility Scorecard Tool, published with Visa under founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], which was reported as setting a goal of limiting 76% of private cars by 2050.[2] In that reading the mobility target and the 15-minute city are complementary: reducing the private vehicle fleet presupposes an urban form in which everything a resident needs is provided within a fifteen-minute distance of their front door.[2] Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized the pairing sarcastically as a future in which people "own nothing" and eat bugs, argued that whether the instrument is 15-minute cities or vaccine passports the Davos globalists keep devising bleaker visions of the future, and observed that even committed leftists in his experience showed little enthusiasm for the ruling class's dystopian plans — a gap he expected propaganda to close.[2]
