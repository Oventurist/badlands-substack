# Whole-of-Society Approach

The "whole-of-society" approach is a governance term describing the coordination of government agencies, private industry, media, academia and civil society organizations toward a common policy objective. Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] uses it as the organizing concept for how the establishment he calls the [[deep-state|deep state]] actually operates.[1]

## DeLarme's reading

In "The Language of Control," DeLarme wrote that in attempting to understand how the so-called deep state works he concluded it "is not really that *deep* at all; it's staring you in the face everywhere you look."[1] The whole-of-society approach, in his account, means that assets are embedded in every aspect of society; while some elements are genuinely hidden, the lion's share is hidden in plain sight, obscured by vague jargon and terms carrying dual meanings.[1]

He described the curated environment produced by this arrangement as a "Truman show-esque materialist world" shaped by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], the [[cia|CIA]], [[usaid|USAID]], the NGO and GONGO network, Wall Street and the private sector, including the "Big Six" media conglomerates said to own 90 percent of media output.[1] He argued that the interplay between large corporations, bureaucracy, intelligence agencies and civil society institutions has perfected the herding of the masses through external stimuli — media, social media and predictive programming — in ways that ensure the continuation of the ruling class.[1]

## The judiciary as a key sector

DeLarme identified the judicial system as one of the most important sectors within the whole-of-society approach.[1] Legal personnel tasked with using the courts to suppress populist upheaval invoke the phrase [[rule-of-law|"the rule of law"]], while operatives create legal frameworks, run "rule of law programs" and organize [[lawfare|lawfare]] campaigns through vehicles such as USAID, the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|U.S. Institute of Peace]].[1] He concluded that every institution, from the media to the judiciary, plays a role in maintaining the status quo, and that the remedy lies in awareness, transparency and decoding the vocabulary itself.[1]
