# 2023 Wray House Judiciary Testimony

The **2023 Wray House Judiciary testimony** was the appearance of [[christopher-wray|FBI Director Christopher Wray]] before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on Wednesday, July 12, 2023, beginning at 10 a.m. ET.[1] It was Wray's first appearance before the committee since Republicans won control of the House and Rep. [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] became its chairman.[1]

## Expected scope

Wray was expected to face questions over the alleged weaponization of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] against former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the perception that the bureau and the Justice Department gave [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] "the kid glove treatment" by comparison, and how the FBI influenced Twitter to censor conservatives — particularly those amplifying the Hunter Biden laptop story.[1] Jordan said the committee would "examine the politicization" of the FBI under Wray and Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]].[1]

Wray was also expected to be pressed on the bureau's handling of the claim that the owner of [[burisma|Burisma]] had kept secret recordings of conversations with the Bidens as an "insurance policy," a matter on which the agency had stonewalled the production of supporting documents.[1] Sen. [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] said the recordings "were allegedly kept as a sort of insurance policy for the foreign national in case he got into a tight spot," and that the FD-1023 form also indicated then-Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] may have been involved in Burisma's employment of Hunter Biden.[1] Grassley added that the FBI made Congress review a redacted *unclassified* document inside a classified facility, which he said showed "the disrespect the FBI has for Congress."[1]

## Course of the hearing

The hearing ran roughly five hours, with legislators questioning Wray about the weaponization of government, asymmetrical standards of justice based on political ideology, and the bureau's failures to operate within the confines of the law.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that House Democrats treated Wray warmly and congratulated him on the FBI's successes, while Rep. [[cori-bush|Cori Bush]] demanded the bureau do better to avoid harassing black and brown communities and acknowledged participating in the "Ferguson Uprising."[1] In this account every Democrat on the panel maintained that claims of weaponization, asymmetry, and failure were conspiracy theories.[1]

Republicans pressed the Director on internal corruption, on FBI employees using intelligence resources for personal purposes such as looking up former romantic partners, on [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]], on the persecution of parents, and on the bureau's alleged inattention to 459 documented crimes attributed to the "Biden Crime Family."[1] Rep. [[victoria-spartz|Victoria Spartz]] questioned Wray on the bureau's intelligence sharing with compromised foreign agencies and alleged that the existing process involves actioning compromised intelligence against American citizens.[1]

## Perjury allegations

Badlands' commentary asserted that few of the fifteen questions posed in advance by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] were asked and none were answered truthfully, and that Wray perjured himself at least twice.[1] Wray claimed unequivocally that the FBI did not reallocate resources from sex-crimes investigations to surveilling parents at school board meetings, a claim disputed by multiple whistleblowers including former agent [[steve-friend|Steve Friend]], who said agents working sex-crime cases were reassigned to tracking parents' license plates outside school board meetings.[1] Wray also stated that the FBI was not engaged in censorship or content-suppression activities, which the commentary called provably false in light of the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] and the judge's preliminary findings in [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri v. Biden]].[1] The commentary concluded that Wray's comfort in committing perjury before Congress suggested he believed he would never be held accountable.[1]

## Scheduling and anticipation (June 2023)

On June 27, 2023 — roughly two weeks before the hearing — a source familiar with the arrangements told [[new-york-post|The New York Post]] that [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] would appear before the panel headed by Rep. [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] on July 12, after lawmakers returned from the July 4 congressional recess.[2] The appearance was set against whistleblower accusations that the bureau had mishandled the criminal investigation into [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], and followed a multi-week subpoena battle between Wray and Congress over an informant document outlining bribery allegations against President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], then 80 years old.[2] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and Wray's [[fbi|FBI]] had become a lightning rod for Republican lawmakers who accused the bureau of being weaponized against conservatives and unwilling to investigate corruption allegations against the president.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] previewed the hearing as must-see television and set out the agenda she hoped members would pursue: Biden's bribery scandals, Wray's attempts to obstruct access to the FD-1023, and the continuing emergence of new bribery allegations, including [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]]'s claim that Hunter Biden had negotiated bribes with his father and Iran as recently as eighteen months earlier.[2] She also wanted the weaponization of law enforcement addressed more broadly, citing the bureau's targeting of pro-life and other America First activists and specifically Catholics, and singled out Byrne's memoir claim that a 2008 law let the Director of Central Intelligence take over the FBI and that [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] had invoked it on matters relating to Russia.[2]
