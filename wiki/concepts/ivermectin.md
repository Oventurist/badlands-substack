# Ivermectin

Ivermectin is an antiparasitic medication that became a focal point of the COVID-19 information war after the [[food-and-drug-administration|U.S. Food and Drug Administration]] publicly discouraged its use as a treatment for the disease.[1]

## FDA messaging

The FDA published an article titled "Why You Should Not Use Ivermectin to Treat or Prevent COVID-19" and promoted it on social media, including a widely circulated post reading "You are not a horse. You are not a cow. Seriously, y'all. Stop it."[1] Critics argued the material omitted the fact that physicians retained the legal authority to prescribe ivermectin off-label notwithstanding the agency's discouragement.[1]

## Litigation and removal of FDA posts

Three doctors sued the FDA over the campaign in 2022. The case was dismissed at first, then revived by the 5th Circuit Court of Appeals and remanded; on review the lower court found that only Dr. [[mary-bowden|Mary Bowden]] had standing.[1] In March 2024 the FDA agreed to take down the posts and online materials, and was given a three-week deadline to remove them from various platforms.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "the regime screeched 'horse paste!' at the top of their lungs" to discourage Americans from a medication that would likely have cured them if taken at the appropriate time and as part of an appropriate regimen.[1] She argued that thousands of doctors who publicized effective treatments were banned from the public square, with many losing medical licenses and at least one going to prison, and that they were ultimately proven correct.[1]

Her central claim was that the availability of an effective treatment would have precluded the emergency use authorization for the COVID-19 vaccines, which she described as bioweapons — giving the FDA, the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] and the global health regime a motive to demonize treatments they knew to be effective.[1] She noted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had repeatedly told Americans about ivermectin and other treatments, and predicted that the revival of the false claim that Trump told people to "inject bleach" — pushed in a Joe Biden campaign ad — would soon be followed by a reappraisal of hydroxychloroquine.[1]

## Pharmacology and safety profile

Ivermectin was first used in the 1970s as an antiparasitic primarily for animals, but its use in humans became widespread in later decades — particularly as a malaria prophylaxis and treatment — and it is considered very safe.[2] Despite this, when COVID-19 peaked in 2020 and 2021 the drug emerged as a fringe remedy and was instantly denounced as a "horse dewormer," including in a [[cnn|CNN]] segment mocking podcaster [[joe-rogan|Joe Rogan]] for saying he had taken it successfully.[2]

## Chris Cuomo's reversal (May 2024)

In May 2024, former CNN anchor [[chris-cuomo|Chris Cuomo]] — who had once said anyone promoting ivermectin should be "shamed" — announced on the PBD Podcast hosted by [[patrick-bet-david|Patrick Bet-David]] that he was taking a regular dose of the drug to treat inflammation and brain fog associated with long COVID.[2] "Ivermectin was a boogeyman during COVID. That was wrong. We were given bad information about Ivermectin. The real question is, why?" he said, adding that the entire medical community knew the drug could not hurt patients.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that independent researchers had been consulting the same clinicians in real time, and that patients turned to veterinary "horse paste" only because doctors would not prescribe the drug and pharmacists would not fill prescriptions under government pressure and propaganda.[2] She argued many lives could have been saved with ivermectin, hydroxychloroquine and Vitamin D, and that thousands died after being denied these treatments and instead given remdesivir and placed on ventilators.[2]

## America First Legal FOIA lawsuit (December 2023)

In December 2023 [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] sued the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] for failing to release documents about the government's suppression of ivermectin and [[hydroxychloroquine|hydroxychloroquine]] as COVID-19 treatments.[3] AFL filed [[freedom-of-information-act|FOIA]] requests concerning ivermectin — described in coverage as a popular antiparasitic medication — in September 2022 and received tracking numbers, but neither agency complied.[3] The organization said it sought to determine when and why officials including [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] discouraged and suppressed its use.[3] Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued that thousands of doctors and hundreds of peer-reviewed studies had supported the drug's efficacy against COVID-19 while agencies withheld information from the public.[3]
