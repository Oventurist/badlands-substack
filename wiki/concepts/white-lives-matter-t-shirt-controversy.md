# White Lives Matter T-Shirt Controversy

The White Lives Matter T-shirt controversy refers to the reaction that followed the Yeezy Season 9 fashion show in Paris on October 3, 2022, at which [[kanye-west|Ye (Kanye West)]] and [[candace-owens|Candace Owens]] posed for photographers wearing T-shirts reading "White Lives Matter."[1]

## Background and reaction

Critics and mainstream outlets characterized "White Lives Matter" and "All Lives Matter" as slogans used by white supremacist and far-right organizations since 2013.[1] Badlands Media disputes that framing, arguing the statement is self-evident to anyone thinking independently and that the shirt was widely understood — including by Ye and Owens — as a joke, two Black Americans stating the obvious.[1] The article notes that the display appeared to offend white liberals most of all, and asserts that without media amplification the episode would have faded within days.[1] Ye's father, [[ray-west|Ray West]], a former Black Panther, reportedly called the shirt funny — "just a Black man stating the obvious."[1]

On October 6, 2022, Ye discussed the controversy on Fox's [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] show, bringing it to a mass audience.[1] The article observes that this appearance fell 366 days before the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]] on Israel.[1]

## Escalation and cancellation

On October 5, 2022, [[sean-combs|Diddy]] publicly said he supported Ye as a freethinker but did not "rock with" the shirt.[1] Diddy sought a face-to-face meeting, which the article interprets as an effort to bring Ye back on narrative; on October 7, 2022, Ye responded publicly that he would use Diddy "as an example to show the Jewish people that told you to call me that no one can threaten or influence me," a post that got him banned from [[instagram|Instagram]], owned by [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].[1]

An October 8, 2022 late-night tweet in which Ye said he was going "death con 3 On JEWISH PEOPLE" triggered a ban from pre-Musk [[twitter|Twitter]]; the article argues "death con 3" referenced DEFCON 3 alert status, meaning he intended to begin exposing rather than harming anyone.[1] After [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] purchased Twitter on October 27, 2022, Ye was welcomed back on November 20, then removed again on December 2, 2022 for posting an image of a swastika inside a Star of David.[1]

By early December 2022 Ye had been comprehensively cancelled, with businesses and individuals severing ties and few defending his speech rights.[1] The article states the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] spearheaded the drive to cancel Ye as an example to others considering stepping out of line, and frames the episode as a precursor to the campaign later waged against Musk.[1]
