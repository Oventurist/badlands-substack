# Chatham House Rule

The Chatham House Rule is a protocol governing the confidentiality of discussions at private meetings.[1] It gives participants the discretion to use information gleaned from the talks but forbids the identification of speakers or participants, thereby ensuring anonymity.[1]

## Use at the Bilderberg Meeting

The rule is the mechanism by which the details of the annual [[bilderberg|Bilderberg Meeting]] remain obscured from the public.[1] A Bilderberg Meetings press release stated that "thanks to the private nature of the Meeting, the participants take part as individuals rather than in any official capacity, and hence are not bound by the conventions of their office or by pre-agreed positions."[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] criticized this framing, writing that the Bilderbergs "arrive as individuals, hence they need not make their discussions public, but they act as one," and that permitting elected officials and other custodians of public rights to engage in such behavior amounts to open conspiracy tolerated by supposedly free countries.[1]
