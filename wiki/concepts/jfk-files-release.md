# JFK Files Release

The JFK files release refers to the declassification and publication of federal government records concerning the assassination of President John F. Kennedy, ordered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and announced for March 18, 2025.[1]

## Announcement

Speaking to reporters while touring the Kennedy Center in Washington, D.C., Trump said 80,000 pages of unredacted files on the Kennedy assassination would be released the following day, fulfilling a campaign-trail promise to declassify the documents.[1] "So, people have been waiting for decades for this, and I've instructed my people … lots of different people, [Director of National Intelligence] [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], that they must be released tomorrow," he said.[1]

Trump emphasized that the material would not be filtered: "You got a lot of reading. I don't believe we're going to redact anything. I said, 'just don't redact, you can't redact.'"[1] Asked whether he had reviewed the contents, he said he had "heard about them," and declined to provide official interpretation: "I'm not doing summaries, you'll write your own summary."[1]

## Executive order

The release followed an executive order Trump signed in January directing the disclosure of federal records related to the assassinations of Kennedy, former Attorney General Robert F. Kennedy, and Martin Luther King Jr.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers noted that the announced release date of March 18 corresponded to a "Delta" for Q-Drop 956, quoting the drop's line "Panic mode. Enjoy the show."[1]

## Contents of the March 2025 tranche

The Trump administration's unveiling on Tuesday, March 18, 2025, of more than 2,000 documents related to the assassination set off a scramble for any scraps of revelatory information within the files.[2] As The Washington Post continued to review the more than 60,000 pages released, nothing initially upended the notion that Lee Harvey Oswald was the lone gunman in the 1963 killing, but the newly unredacted files revealed new details about [[cia|CIA]] agents and operations that the agency had kept secret for decades.[2]

Larry Schnapf, an attorney who had been pushing the government to release the records since 2017, said those details had previously been kept secret by national security officials and argued that the fresh disclosures suggest the government habitually labels too many documents as classified: "You can see how that term has been abused."[2]

## Reception and aftermath

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the release as the opening stage of "a timeline unification playing out in order to maximize a multi-generational American awakening," arguing that the mirrors between the Kennedy and Trump eras were being distilled and that "the key to the future lies in the past, because we're in a War of Stories."[2]

Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he never had high hopes for this initial deployment of the files but did not consider the disclosure story finished, expecting either more files or "more truth in some other form."[2] Citing [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] on Devolution Power Hour, he noted the released material demonstrated that the CIA had infiltrated every major corporation, NGO and public institution and had overthrown numerous governments using tactics including crop burning and bio-warfare.[2] The release was immediately followed by the [[cia-headquarters-armed-man-incident-2025|arrest of an armed man outside CIA headquarters]], which Badlands writers read as a possible false flag intended to generate sympathy for the agency.[2]

## Coverage of the March 18, 2025 tranche

The US government began releasing the trove on the evening of Tuesday, March 18, 2025, following the January executive order requiring unredacted files in the case to be made public.[3] Trump estimated that 80,000 pages of documents would be unsealed, telling reporters the day before, "You got a lot of reading... I don't believe we're going to redact anything."[3] Historians combing the records did not expect many ground-breaking revelations, and it remained unclear how much of the material released by the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] was genuinely new, since many documents had previously appeared in partially redacted form.[3]

The files bore on the killing of President John F. Kennedy, shot during a visit to Dallas, Texas, on 22 November 1963, and on the enduring belief among many Americans that the gunman [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] did not act alone.[3] Jon Herold, working through the new files publicly, highlighted documentation in which intelligence assets were reminded that the notion of the government killing JFK was merely a "political murder conspiracy," remarking that it was "not the best look."[3]

## Trump's remarks on Oswald (March 2025)

Shortly after the release of thousands of assassination-related files, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told Outkick radio host Clay Travis that he believed [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] personally killed President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] but did not act alone, saying "I do, and I always felt that... Of course he was … helped."[4] The Department of Justice has long maintained that Oswald acted alone, though decades of conspiracy theories have questioned whether one or more additional people were involved; Oswald was charged with the assassination, denied responsibility, and was killed two days after the 1963 shooting.[4] Trump's suggestion of an accomplice was not confirmed by the released files, and he described their substance as "somewhat unspectacular," which he said might be "a good thing."[4]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the remarks as Trump leaning fully into what the author called the obvious analogues between his administration and Kennedy's, arguing that on both the domestic and international level the two are nearly identical and describing the moment as a "timeline unification."[4]

## Continued cover-up claims (March 2025)

Despite the declassification of reams of government files on the killing, Rep. [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]] continued in late March 2025 to search for a cover-up, asserting without evidence that an allegedly previously unreleased NBC video could reveal new details of President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]]'s death and purportedly show [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] near Kennedy's vehicle at the time of the shooting.[5] Her [[task-force-on-the-declassification-of-federal-secrets|Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets]] was scheduled to hold a hearing on the JFK files on April 1, 2025, a date [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted with irony.[5]
