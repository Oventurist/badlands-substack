# Cuban Embargo

The Cuban embargo is the long-standing set of United States economic sanctions against [[cuba|Cuba]], and a central point of dispute in the Badlands Media essay "Glimpses into Hell — Part 1."[1]

## The debate

[[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] issued a statement condemning "the U.S. federal government's inhumane treatment of Cubans" and urging Washington to immediately lift the embargo, calling the policy the heart of Cuba's crisis and accusing it of undermining Cubans' right to choose their own government.[1] The essay replies that the statement is "exactly backward," since the United States sanctions Cuba precisely because its communist rulers deny the Cuban people that right.[1]

The rhetoric that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s trade embargo is the sole cause of Cuba's troubles is dismissed in the article as "simple propaganda."[1] Citing [[antonio-garcia-martinez|Antonio Garcia-Martinez]], the essay notes that virtually none of the protesters on the ground mentioned the embargo, that Cuba can trade freely with the rest of the world, and that the lack of US trade — apart from a short opening during the Obama era — has been the norm for decades.[1]

## Political posturing

DeLarme accuses partisans in Washington of virtue signaling and "standing in solidarity" with Cubans by working to remove an embargo that was imposed specifically to hinder the very communist party those Cubans are protesting.[1] Cuban Americans, he writes, argue that changing the embargo would do little for the people while greatly benefiting the state; genuine solidarity would instead mean raising awareness of human-rights violations.[1]
