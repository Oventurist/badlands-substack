# Preventive War

**Preventive war** is the doctrine of striking a perceived adversary before that adversary reaches its full potential and acquires the capacity to strike first. It was adopted in the latter 1700s by [[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]] of [[prussia|Prussia]], who paired it with the practice of launching multiple simultaneous attacks intended to disorient and overwhelm an opponent, preventing them from mounting a defense.[1]

## Theory and reversal

In its original conception the object was to deliver a stunning blow — to "bloody their nose" — that would force a negotiated surrender, rather than to annihilate the enemy through a prolonged campaign.[1] Frederick abandoned the doctrine in his old age, famously remarking that "preventive war is like suicide for fear of death," and concluding that statecraft and virtuous leaders avoiding brash military actions were the true crux of peace and prosperity.[1]

## Legacy

The doctrine was later fused with [[maneuver-warfare|maneuver warfare]] by the Nazis to produce [[blitzkrieg]], which twisted the "bloody their nose" objective into outright annihilation.[1] Analysts have compared President George W. Bush's invasion of Iraq to the younger Frederick's preventive-war doctrine.[1] Badlands commentary invoked the doctrine in June 2024 in connection with Israeli planning for a preemptive land and air incursion into Lebanon against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1]
