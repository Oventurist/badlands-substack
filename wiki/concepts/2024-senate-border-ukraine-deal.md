# 2024 Senate Border-Ukraine Deal

The 2024 Senate border-Ukraine deal was a bipartisan legislative package negotiated in the U.S. Senate in January 2024 that would have paired border enforcement measures with military and economic aid to Ukraine. It became one of the defining domestic political fights of the 2024 election year before collapsing under Republican opposition.[1]

## Origins

Senate Republicans had initially insisted that border policy changes be included in President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s $110 billion emergency request for funding covering Ukraine, Israel, immigration enforcement and other national security needs.[1] The resulting negotiation produced a compromise on border and immigration policy attached to the supplemental funding.[1]

## Biden's Push and Republican Resistance

On Friday, January 26, 2024, Biden pressed [[congress|Congress]] to embrace the deal, saying in a late-evening statement that the proposed policies would "be the toughest and fairest set of reforms to secure the border we've ever had in our country," and pledging to use a new emergency authority to "shut down the border" as soon as he could sign it into law.[1]

House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] suggested the compromise could be "dead on arrival" in his chamber, writing that "rather than accept accountability, President Biden is now trying to blame Congress for what HE himself intentionally created."[1] The deal also came under fire from Republicans including [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the likely presidential nominee, who eviscerated it as a political "gift" to Democrats.[1] Observers noted that Biden's embrace of the deal alongside Republican resistance could mark an election-year shift in the politics of immigration, while the diminishing prospects for passage carried far-reaching consequences for U.S. allies abroad, especially Ukraine.[1]

## Badlands Commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that no legislation was necessary because "the full authority to solve the problem already exists," contending that the bill's actual purpose was "to increase the surveillance state while making the process of illegal alien entry more efficient," financed by $110 billion for what he called fake foreign proxy wars.[1] He framed the persistence of the crisis as deliberate: "The problem exists because both sides of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] want it to."[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded with a first-principles argument that "sovereignty radiates from the ground up," calling the belief that sovereignty is bestowed top-down "the greatest PsyOp in human history."[1] In his account sovereignty begins within each man and expands outward through family, town, city, state and nation; just as a man needs no permission to defend his family, a state needs no permission to defend its borders, and a nation needs no authority beyond that transferred from its collected sovereigns.[1]

## Terms of the February 2024 agreement

By the first weekend of February 2024 the Senate had produced a $118 billion bipartisan agreement, negotiated by Sens. [[james-lankford|James Lankford]] of Oklahoma, [[chris-murphy|Chris Murphy]] of Connecticut, and [[kyrsten-sinema|Kyrsten Sinema]] of Arizona, while the House had gone, in one critic's phrase, "full 'Israel or Bust.'"[2] Opponents charged that the text would allow 1.5 million illegal entrants into the United States every year, allocate $2.3 billion to non-governmental organizations and other groups involved in transporting them, provide $14.1 billion in security assistance to Israel, and direct $60 billion in support to Ukraine, while locking in green card giveaways through 2030.[2] Particular attention fell on a $2.33 billion carve-out for "Refugee and Entrant Assistance," under which amounts "may be used for grants or contracts with qualified organizations, including nonprofit entities, to provide culturally and linguistically appropriate services."[2]

Lankford's own state legislature censured him for negotiating what critics called a poor border deal with Democrats.[2] Commentators renewed the argument that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] could close the border by executive action immediately but was declining to do so until Ukraine and Israel money materialized, characterizing the posture as a quid-pro-quo arrangement.[2]

## Biden's "show some spine" speech (February 6, 2024)

As the deal faltered, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took the border fight directly to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Tuesday, February 6, 2024, blaming his predecessor and putative challenger for torpedoing the bipartisan immigration agreement out of crass politics at the expense of national security.[3] Weighing in forcefully after months of largely staying out of the fray, Biden called on congressional Republicans to "show some spine" and stand up to Trump, while effectively acknowledging that the deal negotiated over several months was doomed and vowing to make it a campaign issue.[3]

"All indications are this bill won't even move forward to the Senate floor," Biden said in a speech televised from the White House. "Why? A simple reason. Donald Trump. Because Donald Trump thinks this is bad for him politically."[3] He said Trump would "rather weaponize this issue than actually solve it" and had leaned on Republicans to block it, adding: "It looks like they're caving. Frankly, they owe it to the American people to show some spine and do what they know to be right."[3]
