# White House Ballroom

The White House Ballroom is a privately funded construction project at the [[white-house|White House]] undertaken during the second administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Litigation

A lawsuit brought by preservationists and other opponents argued that the construction violated historic preservation standards.[1] On 26 February 2026 a federal judge declined to issue a preliminary injunction halting the work, allowing construction to continue while the litigation proceeds.[1]

## Trump's response

Trump celebrated the ruling in a [[truth-social|Truth Social]] post, writing that the judge had "thrown out, and completely erased, the effort to stop its construction," and emphasizing that "not one dollar of Taxpayer money is being spent," with funding instead provided by what he called "Patriot Donors and Contributors."[1] He said the ballroom was anticipated to host future inaugurations and large state visits, was ahead of schedule and under budget, and would "stand long into the future as a symbol to the Greatness of America."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that "the big beautiful ballroom is very much alive," pairing the ruling with the [[executive-order-14251|Executive Order 14251]] decision as setbacks for opponents of the president's agenda that week.[1]

## National security stay motion (February 2026)

On February 2, 2026, the [[department-of-justice|U.S. Department of Justice]] asked a federal judge to stay any injunction against the White House East Wing Modernization and State Ballroom Project before one could be issued, citing "the exigencies involved in suspending a major ongoing construction project with national security implications" and a desire "to avoid an emergency situation or at least to facilitate expedited proceedings on appeal."[2] The filing argued that "halting construction would imperil the President and others who live and work in the [[white-house|White House]]," that the open construction site was itself "a hazard" that "complicates Secret Service operations," and that a supplemental classified declaration set out further reasons why a halt "will endanger national security and therefore impair the public interest."[2] The underlying lawsuit seeking to block the project was filed by the [[national-trust-for-historic-preservation|National Trust for Historic Preservation]].[2]

## DOJ ultimatum to the National Trust (April 2026)

In April 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] escalated further, urging the [[national-trust-for-historic-preservation|National Trust for Historic Preservation]] to withdraw its lawsuit outright and warning that if the case was not withdrawn by Monday morning the department would move to dissolve the existing injunction blocking construction and seek dismissal in court.[3] Assistant Attorney General [[brett-shumate|Brett Shumate]] wrote in a sharply worded letter that the lawsuit places "the lives of the president, his family, and his staff at grave risk," and called on the group to immediately dismiss the "frivolous lawsuit," citing heightened security concerns following the recent assassination attempt on [[donald-trump|President Trump]].[3]

Shumate argued that recent security incidents reinforce the administration's position that the ballroom is necessary to reduce risks associated with presidential travel to external venues.[3] The dispute stems from the Trust's December lawsuit challenging the demolition of the [[white-house|White House]] East Wing to make way for a 90,000-square-foot ballroom, which argued the project required congressional approval; the White House has maintained the renovation falls within presidential authority and is privately funded, requiring neither congressional funding nor authorization.[3]

## NCPC approval (April 2026)

The [[national-capital-planning-commission|National Capital Planning Commission]] voted 8-1 on a Thursday in early April 2026 to approve President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s plan to build the new ballroom.[4] Commission chair [[will-scharf|Will Scharf]] said many of the public comments submitted to the panel fell outside its authority — including objections over funding, demolition, and general political criticism — remarking, “We are not some sort of free-ranging ballroom justice commission.”[4] Following the vote Trump thanked the commission on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], saying the project had been approved “overwhelmingly, 8-1,” and noting that even Sen. [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] had voted in favor.[4] He said presidents for more than 150 years had envisioned a White House ballroom, calling it a long-needed addition for hosting state visits, grand events, and inaugurations.[4]

## Underground military complex (March 2026)

Speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One on a Sunday night in late March 2026, Trump said the US military is constructing a "massive complex" beneath the new ballroom, and that the details became public only after a "stupid lawsuit" forced elements of the project into the open.[5] He said construction was already underway, that the military was "doing very well" with the work, and that the structure includes bulletproof glass and drone-resistant roofing and ceilings reflecting the realities of modern threats.[5] Trump added that the national security component had been intended to remain classified, that the military "wanted it more than anybody," and that its disclosure was "unpatriotic"; the episode is covered at [[white-house-underground-complex|White House underground complex]].[5]

## Construction during the October 2025 shutdown

Construction of the 90,000-square-foot White House ballroom continued despite the government shutdown that began at 12:01 a.m. ET on October 1, 2025, according to ABC News reporting.[6] Hours after the lapse took effect, a White House official said, "There will not be a stoppage of ballroom work when the shutdown occurs."[6] According to a White House [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] memo, the ballroom's construction was funded by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and other private donors, meaning it was unaffected by the federal budget negotiations that had spurred the shutdown; the official said, "Work will continue to be performed as the funds currently supporting are not tied to a FY26 enacted appropriation."[6]

Badlands contributor CannCon argued the project was effectively "funded by all the defamation and unconstitutional acts taken against him over the last eight years. Not the taxpayer," adding that YouTube "alone should have added two zeros to their settlement."[6]

## Funding from the YouTube settlement

Under [[youtube|YouTube]]'s $24.5 million settlement of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s lawsuit, filed in court papers on 29 September 2025, $22 million was directed to a trust for the National Mall — the entity handling construction of the $200 million ballroom at the White House.[7] The remainder was earmarked for other plaintiffs in the case, including the American Conservative Union and Naomi Wolf.[7]

## Demolition of the East Wing (October 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] confirmed on Wednesday, October 22, 2025 that the entire East Wing of the [[white-house|White House]] was being demolished to make way for the planned ballroom.[8] "In order to do it properly we had to take down the existing structure," Trump told reporters in the Oval Office, flanked by renderings of the ballroom.[8] The acknowledgement followed videos posted on social media earlier that week showing a crane being used to begin tearing down the structure.[8]
