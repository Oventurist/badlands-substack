# Beef Packer Antitrust Investigation (2026)

In May 2026 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and [[department-of-agriculture|Department of Agriculture]] announced an intensified antitrust investigation into the "Big Four" beef packers — [[jbs|JBS]], [[cargill|Cargill]], [[tyson-foods|Tyson Foods]], and [[national-beef|National Beef]] — citing price manipulation, foreign ownership risks, and supply chain issues.[1]

## Scope

Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] revealed reviews of more than three million documents and hundreds of rancher interviews.[1] Agriculture Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke Rollins]] called foreign-owned packers such as JBS and National Beef a national security threat amid reports of slave labor ties, stating that "half of these meatpacking giants, including the largest meat packer in the world, are either foreign-owned or have significant foreign ownership and control, making them a threat not just to our cattle producers, but a threat to America itself."[1]

The probe follows [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s November 2025 directive and ties into a coming settlement on poultry and meat prices, arriving as US beef herds hit 1950s lows due to droughts and declining ranch numbers.[1]

## Forthcoming settlement

Blanche said the Department had executed on the President's executive order to stop anti-competitive behavior in the broader food supply market and would announce later that week a historic settlement directly affecting the prices of proteins such as chicken, pork, and turkey.[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America summarized the developments as an immediate settlement promising lower US meat prices, ongoing investigations seeking whistleblower help against the impacts of globalism and collusion on US agriculture, and Big Ag being named a threat to America itself.[1]
