# 2026 Denaturalization Campaign

The 2026 denaturalization campaign is an effort announced by the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] on June 8, 2026, to revoke the citizenship of 17 naturalized U.S. citizens accused of immigration fraud or other crimes. The administration described it as the largest denaturalization campaign ever undertaken by the federal government, with cases filed in federal courts across the country.[1]

## Legal basis

Federal law permits the government to pursue denaturalization when officials allege that citizenship was obtained through fraud, concealment of material facts, or other disqualifying conduct.[1] According to CBS News, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] expanded its denaturalization priorities in 2025 as part of a broader immigration enforcement initiative.[1]

## Allegations

Court filings cited in reporting allege that some targeted individuals concealed criminal activity during naturalization, used false identities, or otherwise failed to satisfy statutory requirements for citizenship, including the requirement to demonstrate good moral character.[1] The group includes individuals accused or convicted of offenses ranging from child sexual abuse and fraud-related crimes to visa fraud and money laundering; the allegations remain subject to judicial review.[1]

## Consequences

Justice Department and administration officials said the proceedings are intended to address cases involving alleged abuse of the naturalization process, and that those named have the opportunity to challenge the government's claims in court.[1] If a court orders denaturalization, the person generally reverts to a prior immigration status and loses the rights and protections associated with U.S. citizenship, including protection from deportation.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that engaging in immigration fraud is one of the only ways naturalized citizens can lose citizenship, and noted that prior Brief discussions of the topic had most often centered on Minnesota congresswoman [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]], who allegedly married her brother to help him obtain lawful status.[1] Omar is not among the 17 named, which Ashe took as evidence that "obviously there's more to do."[1] She endorsed the campaign and argued it should be far broader: "It must be way more than 17. The job isn't over until every last fake American is stripped of their status and returned to their homeland."[1]
