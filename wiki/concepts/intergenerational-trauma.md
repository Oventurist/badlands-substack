# Intergenerational Trauma

**Intergenerational trauma** is the phenomenon by which unresolved trauma persists across generations, shaping the behavior and beliefs of descendants who did not experience the originating event.[1] It is one of the established concepts a February 11, 2025 Badlands Media essay draws on in proposing [[generational-dependence-syndrome|Generational Dependence Syndrome]].[1]

Citing [[peter-levine|Peter Levine]]'s *Waking the Tiger*, the essay argues that unresolved trauma does not dissipate but lingers and cycles through families and communities, silently shaping behaviors and creating a pervasive baseline of anxiety and hyper-vigilance that conditions individuals to equate submission with safety.[1] The author contends that generational trauma is less about historical events themselves than about rules that continue to be followed without question, an idea illustrated through the [[five-monkeys-experiment|Five Monkeys Experiment]].[1]

The essay further invokes epigenetic research, asserting that trauma alters gene expression in ways that are passed down and act as a biological echo of past crises influencing emotional resilience in descendants, and quotes [[joe-dispenza|Joe Dispenza]] to the effect that the environment signals the gene to reprogram the cell.[1] Inherited fear is said to manifest as hoarding of resources, resistance to adaptation, and fear of change, locking lineages into survival-oriented thinking.[1] A related dimension is drawn from [[carl-jung|Carl Jung]]'s notion of the collective "shadow," in which repressed fears remain unexamined beneath societal norms.[1]
