# Rome Statute

The Rome Statute is the treaty that establishes the jurisdiction of the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]]. Its significance in July 2026 U.S. debate is that the United States is not a party to it.[1]

Reuters reported that because the United States has not joined the Rome Statute, U.S. law provides no domestic mechanism requiring state or local officials to enforce ICC arrest warrants — the legal basis for the conclusion that New York City could not execute the ICC's 2024 warrant for Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] despite Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]]'s stated intent.[1] The same non-party status underpins Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s argument that the court improperly targets American military personnel and officials, and the [[state-department|State Department]]'s campaign urging other countries to withdraw from the court, as [[venezuela|Venezuela]] did.[1]
