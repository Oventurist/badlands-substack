# Neighborhood Checks (Naturalization)

**Neighborhood checks** are a long-unused element of the United States naturalization process that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] moved to revive in August 2025 as part of a broader tightening of how immigrants applying for U.S. citizenship are scrutinized.[1]

## The DHS memo

A [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] memo first obtained by CBS News, and later confirmed by the department to Newsweek, showed that neighborhood checks would be brought back into use by [[uscis|U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services]].[1] USCIS Director Joseph B. Edlow said in a statement to Newsweek that "the Immigration and Nationality Act directs USCIS to conduct personal investigations and incorporating neighborhood investigations will help enhance these statutorily required investigations to ensure that we are meeting congressional intent."[1]

## Context

Newsweek framed the change as part of a series of adjustments to the immigration system made since January by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Secretary of Homeland Security [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], who state that the measures are necessary to ensure national security is maintained and illegal immigration is halted.[1] Immigration advocates have warned that some of the stricter measures put up unnecessary barriers to legal status for people who have been living within U.S. law for decades.[1]
