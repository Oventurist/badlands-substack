# National Strategy to Counter Islamophobia

The National Strategy to Counter Islamophobia is a first-of-its-kind U.S. federal initiative announced on Wednesday, November 1, 2023 by Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] on behalf of the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

## Announcement and scope

In a video address, Harris said the strategy "will be a comprehensive and detailed plan to protect Muslims and those perceived to be Muslim from hate, bigotry, and violence," and would "address the concern that some government policies may discriminate against Muslims."[1] [[white-house|White House]] press secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] said the effort would be led by the Domestic Policy Council and the National Security Council, working with community leaders, advocates and members of Congress.[1]

Jean-Pierre stated that "for too long, Muslims in America, and those perceived to be Muslim, such as Arabs and Sikhs, have endured a disproportionate number of hate-fueled attacks and other discriminatory incidents."[1] She cited the killing of six-year-old [[wadea-al-fayoume|Wadea Al-Fayoume]], stabbed to death outside Chicago on October 14, 2023 in an attack that also injured his mother; the Department of Justice investigated the case as a hate crime and suspect [[joseph-czuba|Joseph Czuba]] pleaded not guilty.[1]

The strategy was described as part of a directive Biden issued in 2022 establishing an interagency group to coordinate government efforts against Islamophobia, antisemitism and related forms of bias.[1] Coverage of the announcement noted that it came as reported antisemitic incidents were soaring in the wake of the October 7 attacks by [[hamas|Hamas]] and the ensuing war in Gaza.[1]
