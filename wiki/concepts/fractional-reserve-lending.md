# Fractional Reserve Lending

**Fractional reserve lending** is the banking practice in which institutions lend out assets they hold on deposit while retaining only a fraction as reserves.[1]

Badlands Media traces its emergence to the early American banking system: banks began as a convenience for storing US-minted coins, then allowed their bank notes to be openly traded, creating their own economy over the official [[hard-money|hard money]] economy. Bankers understood that over time they could loan these assets out and charge interest on them, and the practice shifted from coins to paper.[1]

The article states that the only way this scheme could be stopped is if the people demanded their gold back en masse, which is why abandoning the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] became a banker priority.[1]
