# Judicial Lawfare Against the Trump Agenda

Judicial lawfare against the Trump agenda refers to the wave of federal court rulings that blocked or delayed executive actions during the opening months of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term, and to the coordinated litigation campaign alleged to lie behind them.[1]

## Scale of the rulings

Reporting by Joe Hoft in June 2025 held that activist judges had halted Trump's agenda at least 181 times in a four-month span.[1] "That is a huge number," the account read. "To give you that in percentage terms, in over 50% of Trump administration agenda has been halted at least temporarily."[1] The piece argued that judicial lawfare in the United States was out of control and that the judges could not act without the underlying cases being filed for them to rule on.[1]

## Alleged coordination by Norm Eisen

The same reporting asserted that [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]] had recently boasted of being behind 152 of the efforts to derail or slow the Trump agenda.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the phenomenon should be regarded as "one big [[color-revolution|Color Revolution]]," detached from what he terms the Reality Prime level.[1] On that reading it is pointless to treat the decisions as real in any meaningful way, because "the abstraction does and will fall in the face of actual power"; he described the system as totally compromised and anti-constitutional, concluding that "the solution is not within the system and never was."[1]

Paul further tied Eisen to the [[january-6-committee|January 6 hearings]] and to what he called the fake Trump indictments, describing them as fictions within a Color Revolution playbook in which media stories are prosecuted in hopes the public will reject Trump — a publicity campaign he judged to have failed.[1]
