# War of Stories

The **War of Stories** is an analytical refrain used by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], published as an essay on the Badlands Substack and invoked repeatedly in his commentary on geopolitics.[1] It treats world events as contests between competing narratives — stories told to and about populations — rather than as a straightforward sequence of material causes.[1]

## Application to China and Taiwan

In April 2023, Burning Bright applied the War of Stories lens to escalating tensions between China and [[taiwan|Taiwan]], describing a conversation with Just Human on the program DEFECTED in which he set out a personal theory of what form "escalation" between the two would take.[1] He forecast a series of escalating stand-offs in the Taiwan Strait, the "buzzing" of aircraft, and eventual involvement of the Japanese, Australian and United States navies.[1] He argued that this reading mapped onto [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "WW3 and Peacemaker" narrative seeding, and onto Just Human's Regions of Responsibility concept, in which a rebalancing multi-polar world order takes shape as the United States withdraws from its role as the world's watchdog and enforcer.[1]

He further warned that such a cascade could begin with a new [[gulf-of-tonkin-incident|Gulf of Tonkin]]-style mass psychological, near-kinetic event — an incident whose narrative effect exceeds its kinetic content.[1] The framework is presented alongside Just Human's maxim that "Understanding > Reacting," positioning narrative analysis as an alternative to reflexive response to headlines.[1]

## The 2023 Israel–Palestine front

"War Pigs" describes the Israel–Palestine conflict that began in October 2023 as "the latest narrative in the war of stories," serving multiple strategic purposes at once: fuelling the war economy, dividing America, and providing a pretext to bolster the Western censorship regime.[6]

The article's distinctive contribution to the framework is the argument that the narrative war does not require the audience to adopt any particular position — only that they adopt one.[6] Manufactured binaries produce mobilised constituencies on both flanks, each serving the interests of the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]], which is why propagandists can profitably cultivate support for both sides of a war the United States funds on only one side.[6]

## Application to BRICS and the multipolar order

In his August 2023 essay "BRIC by BRIC," [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] restated the War of Stories thesis in its strongest form: that "every Kinetic or Actual War in human history has been the result of a lost Information War, and Information Wars are really Wars of Stories."[2] He argued that human beings are innately oriented toward stories, characters and symbols, and that the globalist establishment exploits this through the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] of problem, reaction and solution to steer populations "along greased psychological rails toward predetermined solutions."[2]

Under the right framing, he wrote, this same susceptibility is emancipatory: affirming, sovereignty-minded stories shape moral and ethical frameworks, while subverted ones lead the public over "the proverbial lemmings' cliff."[2] He held that the trend was being reversed against its own architects — a process he calls the [[good-reset|'Good' Reset]] — because "we have better storytellers," and that the only thing capable of freeing a chained mind is the truth used to paper over it.[2] What holds for the individual mind, he added, holds for the Collective Mind, "the ultimate prize on the Game Theory Game Board."[2]

## "Right the Ship" and the observable indicator of who is winning

In "Right the Ship" (January 12, 2023), [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that in recent weeks at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] he had asserted that "stories run much of the world — rather, world events" and that the ongoing War of Stories was one he contended the sovereign side was winning.[3] He grounded the claim in [[game-theory|Game Theory]]: a combatant need not win every political, administrative, rhetorical or kinetic engagement to win the war, only control more of the Game Board than the adversary.[3] Because the true extent of that board is unknowable — though vast, encompassing virtually every political, military and media faction in the world — he offered a proxy indicator: one can guess who is winning the [[information-war|Information War]] by observing which sides, factions and public players appear to be reacting to Narrative Deployments and which appear to be seeding them.[3]

He also argued that Potential Events — those previously confined to the hypothetical, rhetorical or "media" realms — can provoke Actual Events on the board and retain independent power through their effect on the directional thinking, and therefore the directional momentum, of the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]].[3] Applying that Actual–Potential dichotomy to unfolding events was, in his framing, the corrective needed to keep readers centered amid information overload.[3]

## Applied to the Kirk assassination aftermath (2025)

In "The Exploitation of Charlie Kirk," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] borrowed the "war of stories" framing from Badlands co-host Burning Bright to offer an alternative reading of the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's speech-enforcement measures after the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[4] Viewed through that lens, DeLarme suggested, the measures might be intended to draw attention to something and to give the left's voters — not its players — a taste of their own medicine, so that in future they would be slower to embrace censoring opponents and better able to see the hypocrisy in it.[4]

## "The War of Stories" (December 2022)

Burning Bright devoted an entire December 30, 2022 Badlands Media essay to the term, subtitled "The News is Fake. The War is Real."[5] Building on an earlier exploration of the [[overton-window|Overton Window]]'s effect on the American and worldwide zeitgeist, he argued that public figures on the world stage had over recent decades increasingly taken on the likeness of archetypal characters rather than sovereign actors pursuing their own sociopolitical agendas.[5] On that basis he contended that what the community calls the [[information-war|Information War]] is more accurately a War of Stories, in which [[narrative-deployment|Narrative Deployments]] by the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] — used to program and misguide societies toward self-defeating ends in the service of unseen globalist masters — are now countered by an increasingly engaged, awakened and decentralized citizen intelligence network.[5]

Participation in the War of Stories, he wrote, requires first understanding the narrative techniques in effect, the patterns on display and the characters on the stage, and also accepting that both "our side" and "theirs" employ many of the same psychological and informational tactics in this [[fifth-generation-warfare|Fifth-Generation War]].[5] Although events from Potential to Actual are obfuscated by all sides on the [[game-theory-game-board|Game Theory Game Board]], he judged that the presence of more talented showmen and narrative crafters on the sovereign side lent credence to the idea that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] — represented by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and other figures pushing America First rhetoric or exposure of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] [[system-of-systems|System of Systems]] — was pushing the Overton Window back toward freedom "one deployment at a time."[5] Recontextualizing outsized public personalities as vehicles for paradigm-shifting narratives, he argued, reframes apparently bombastic scandals and controversies as attention-grabbing strategic exposures and disclosures.[5]

The essay closed by naming the contest a war for control of the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]] — "the ultimate prize in this war to end all wars" — and urging readers to guard their own minds as part of its cognitive keystone.[5]
