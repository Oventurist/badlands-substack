# DOJ Requests for State Voter Lists

In 2025 the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] began seeking the voter registration lists of several states — representing data on millions of Americans — along with other election information ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]], raising concerns about how the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration planned to use the information.[1]

## Scope

At least nine states received requests for information over a three-month period, according to letters from the DOJ obtained by Stateline.[1] Some states also received emails from a DOJ official asking for meetings to discuss information-sharing agreements.[1] The most sweeping publicly known request was directed at [[colorado|Colorado]], where the department demanded all records related to the 2024 election — a massive trove of documents that could include ballots and even voting equipment.[1] The scale of the Colorado inquiry underscored the extent of the administration's attention to state election activities.[1]

## Origins

The department's focus on elections followed a March directive in which President Trump instructed Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to seek information about suspected election crimes from state election officials, and empowered her to potentially withhold grants and other funds from uncooperative states.[1]

## Commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] summarized the response of the outlet's commentariat bluntly: "The whole thing is a scam," adding that "Our elections are fake."[1]
