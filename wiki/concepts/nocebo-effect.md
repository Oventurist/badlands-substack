# Nocebo Effect

The **nocebo effect** is the negative counterpart of the placebo effect: harm or symptoms produced in a person by negative expectation rather than by any physical cause. Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] invoked it as the medically documented bridge between mass-media messaging and physical and psychological harm to the public.[1]

## Use in Badlands analysis

Deschamps opened his January 20, 2023 essay on [[mass-psychosis]] with the assertion that, as of February 2021, it is a medical fact that fear-inducing information repetitively spread through mass media can and does adversely affect the general public's mental health, in the form of nocebo effects and mass hysteria.[1] The claim served as the empirical anchor for the essay's larger argument that fear messaging is a usable weapon rather than an incidental byproduct of news coverage.[1]

Because the nocebo mechanism operates through expectation and belief, it fits the essay's account of psychogenic causation: a flood of negative emotion — chiefly fear and anxiety — impairs the mind's ability to process reality logically, and in group settings that emotion is shared and magnified.[1] In this framing, repetitive negative media coverage does not merely mislead an audience; it can measurably injure it, which is what allows media saturation to function as an instrument of [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generational warfare]].[1]

## Relationship to mass hysteria

The essay paired the nocebo effect with mass hysteria as the two documented population-level harms of repetitive fear messaging.[1] It cited research by Philipp Bagus, José Antonio Peña-Ramos and Antonio Sánchez-Bayón arguing that mass and digital media in connection with the state may have had adverse consequences during the COVID-19 crisis, with the resulting collective hysteria contributing to government policy errors.[1] The [[world-health-organization|WHO]] and the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] were described as insisting on guidance that the corporate press amplified, despite both being, in the author's characterization, privately funded institutions rather than governing entities.[1]
