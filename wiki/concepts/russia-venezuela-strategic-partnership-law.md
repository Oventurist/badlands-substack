# Russia–Venezuela Strategic Partnership Law

On Monday, October 27, 2025, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] signed legislation formalizing a strategic partnership with Venezuela, strengthening cooperation across energy, mining, finance, and defense.[1]

## Origins

The law enshrines an agreement first signed on May 7 during a Moscow meeting between Putin and Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1] It underscores the growing alignment of Moscow and Caracas as both face US sanctions, and signals Russia's broader push to promote a multipolar global order.[1]

## Provisions

Under the pact, the two nations will expand collaboration in the economy, energy, mining, disarmament, and non-proliferation.[1] The agreement also commits them to countering neo-colonial narratives, condemning racism and genocide, and opposing the "distortion of colonial history."[1] Key provisions reject unilateral coercive measures, including extraterritorial sanctions, as violations of international law and the UN Charter.[1] Both sides pledged to develop a joint financial infrastructure insulating bilateral trade from Western influence, and to advance cooperation on arms control and equal security for all nations.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands hosts welcomed the move, framing Putin as stepping up to defend Maduro and warning that any effort to remove him would now have to contend with Russia.[1]

## May 2025 Moscow strategic partnership agreement

An earlier strategic partnership agreement between the two countries was signed in Moscow on Wednesday, May 7, 2025, after talks between President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and his Venezuelan counterpart [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], who had travelled to the Russian capital for the May 9 Victory Day celebrations marking the 80th anniversary of the Soviet defeat of Nazi Germany.[2] Under the agreement Moscow and Caracas pledged to oppose unilateral sanctions, violations of the UN Charter, and what they described as manifestations of neo-colonialism.[2] The document published on the Kremlin's website outlined cooperation on global and regional security, counterterrorism, counter-extremism, and efforts to combat the falsification of history and the glorification of Nazism, and set a long-term course for deeper ties in the economy, energy, investment, mineral extraction, transport, telecommunications, healthcare and culture.[2]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry used the signing to revisit the question of whether Maduro belongs to the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], recalling Maduro's "laptop from hell" — material handed to him in 2017 by the scorned ex-wife of one of his oil officials, documenting corruption among members of his own government as well as dozens of asset-management executives from firms such as Glencore.[2] He argued that the forty lawsuits Maduro filed against those executives in U.S. court in 2018, though ultimately dismissed, supplied material later used in subsequent prosecutions and civil actions, making Maduro an essential source in the prosecution of corrupt executives controlling the world's natural resources.[2]
