# Post-War Gaza Comprehensive Plan

The **comprehensive plan for post-war Gaza** is the framework the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] was reported in August 2025 to be assembling for the management of the Gaza Strip after the end of hostilities.[1]

## Announcement

Special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] announced that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would chair a "large meeting" at the [[white-house|White House]] on Wednesday, August 27, 2025 to discuss the plan.[1] Witkoff's remarks appeared to be the first time he had revealed the existence of a US plan for the so-called "day after," as Washington had largely deferred on the issue to its Arab allies in the region since Trump's February remarks on his vision to take over Gaza and permanently relocate its residents.[1] While Israel welcomed that idea, it was roundly rejected by US partners in the region whom Trump had hoped would be willing to take in Palestinian refugees.[1] "Many people are going to see how robust it is and how well meaning it is, and it reflects President Trump's humanitarian motives," Witkoff said in a Fox News interview, without elaborating further.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the meeting as Trump taking control of the situation and exerting himself to drive resolution, having given Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] the opportunity to resolve the Gaza conflict on his own terms only for "Bibi" to flounder while rushing from political crisis to political crisis to keep his coalition from collapsing.[1]

The commentary drew a contrast between Trump's first and second terms with respect to Netanyahu and Israel. In the first term there were "cat-and-mouse games," in which Trump's team would organize a massive operation such as the Soleimani strike or negotiate a major deal such as the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], only for Netanyahu to call the night before finalization and back out.[1] Netanyahu had far more political capital and allies then, but was publicly charged with three felonies related to public corruption in 2019, remained in office only thanks to emergency COVID measures and the willingness of his rivals to cooperate "for the greater good," lost an election in 2021 and was driven from power by Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid, then returned eighteen months later with new allies among the Ultra-Orthodox radicals, [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]].[1] He has since sought to overhaul the judiciary so that as Prime Minister he could stack the Supreme Court with his own judges, likely to assure that a forthcoming conviction on the felony charges would be overturned on appeal.[1]

In this account Trump gave Netanyahu "all the proverbial rope he needs to hang himself, politically," and Bibi obliged, taking down Israel with him.[1] Whether the White House meeting represented a turning point in that relationship remained to be seen; the author speculated it might be another strategic deployment designed to bait Netanyahu into further isolating Israel geopolitically while "the sycophants in [[congress|Congress]] (namely, the [[republican-party|Republican Party]]) continue to expose themselves for the traitors that they are, totally beholden to a foreign entity, and not the American People."[1]
