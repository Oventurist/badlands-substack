# Apollo Program

The Apollo program was the American crewed lunar effort that placed humanity on another celestial body for the first time at the close of the 1960s.[1] In Badlands Media's reading of 1968, Apollo represented one of two opposing futures then competing for dominance in America and the world: a space-based economic system that inspired great hopes across the entire planet.[1]

The rival future — race riots at home, an insane and escalating war in [[vietnam-war|Vietnam]] that ultimately killed over 50,000 young Americans and millions of Vietnamese, and endless sums absorbed into the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]] — is the one Badlands says the citizens of 1968 chose.[1] The consequence was that America's space program was atrophied, with Apollo's moon program killed off in 1973, at the same time that [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]]'s civil rights movement was being replaced by FBI-funded radicals under [[cointelpro|COINTELPRO]].[1]

Badlands frames the present moment as a repetition of that fork, with humanity once again standing on the precipice of a new age of cooperation, space exploration and international development set against a dystopian world order.[1]

## Dissolution as an anomaly

In "Time to Revive the Spirit of JFK," [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] asks why the Apollo Program — which had generated revolutionary technologies in every branch of economics and medicine — found itself dissolved by 1973, with advanced Saturn rockets retired into museums.[2] He groups this with a cluster of related anomalies: the dropping of [[john-f-kennedy|Kennedy]]'s cooperation offers to the Soviet Union after his death, despite [[nikita-khrushchev|Khrushchev]] having written of his desire that they proceed; the peaking of [[nasa|NASA]]'s budget in 1964 at 4% of GDP and its diversion into the war in Vietnam; the 1972 dismantling of the [[nerva-program|NERVA]] nuclear rocket programs; and the failure of the [[apollo-soyuz-test-project|Apollo–Soyuz]] mission to become the foundation of a new era of space diplomacy.[2] His explanation is the rise of [[neo-malthusianism|neo-Malthusian]] statecraft across Western governments.[2] The protection of heritage sites such as the Apollo 11 landing site later became the seventh of the ten [[artemis-accords|Artemis Accords]] principles.[2]
