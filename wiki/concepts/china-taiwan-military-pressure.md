# China-Taiwan Military Pressure

China-Taiwan military pressure refers to the sustained campaign of naval and air deployments by the People's Republic of [[china|China]] around [[taiwan|Taiwan]], intensifying in early 2024.[1]

## Warship deployments

China was reported in January 2024 to be maintaining a constant military presence around Taiwan, with four warships strategically deployed in various directions.[1] The deployments were reported by the Japanese newspaper Yomiuri Shimbun citing official sources, drawing attention to a security situation around Taiwan that remains in flux.[1]

## Air and naval incursions

Taiwan's Ministry of Defense said that 33 [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]] aircraft and six PLA Navy vessels entered Taiwan's Air Defense Identification Zone, of which 13 aircraft crossed the median line.[1] The median line is the artificial line separating Taiwan from the Chinese mainland; Beijing's aircraft had increasingly crossed it over the preceding two years.[1]

## U.S. naval transits

On January 24, 2024, the USS John Finn transited the Taiwan Strait, which the U.S. Navy described as demonstrating its "commitment to upholding freedom of navigation for all nations."[1]

## April 2023 naval stand-off and PLA drills

In April 2023, roughly 20 military ships — about half from China and half from [[taiwan|Taiwan]] — were involved in a stand-off near the Taiwan Strait's sensitive median line, according to Reuters reporting relayed by Sky News.[2] The confrontation coincided with a second day of Chinese drills in which Beijing said its military had carried out simulated precision strikes on "key targets on the island and the surrounding sea areas," while Taiwan reported multiple air force sorties by Chinese aircraft.[2] A security source told Reuters that the mock air and sea attacks on Sunday targeted "foreign military targets" in waters off Taiwan's southwest coast, adding that "Taiwan is not their only target" and calling the exercise "very provocative."[2]
