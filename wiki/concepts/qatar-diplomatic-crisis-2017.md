# 2017 Qatar Diplomatic Crisis

The 2017 Qatar diplomatic crisis was the rupture in which [[qatar|Qatar]] was blockaded by Saudi Arabia and other Arab states. The article, which says it addressed the episode in the series' first instalment, presents it as a deliberate strike against terrorist financing carried out with the approval of President [[donald-trump|Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

Qatar is described as one of the biggest funding sources for [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]], and the blockade as the means by which that funding was cut off.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) In June 2017, following the crisis, [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] was placed on a terrorist watchlist on suspicion of terrorism-related activities conducted with Qatari support — a designation backed by Egypt, the UAE, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia and Libya's Tobruk government, which issued a warrant for his arrest.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article ties the sequence forward to January 2019, when a British parliamentary report implicated Belhaj in migrant smuggling and Libyan public prosecutor Siddiq Al Sour issued a warrant for "abuses and war crimes," driving Belhaj to relocate to Istanbul — and asks pointedly who was president at the time.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)


## Badlands account in "Trump Takes Out the King"

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s article "Trump Takes Out the King" treats the Qatar crisis as the first test of Saudi commitments made at the [[arab-islamic-american-summit|Arab Islamic American Summit]] of May 21, 2017.[2] Two days after the summit, statements endorsing [[iran|Iran]] as "an Islamic power in the region that cannot be ignored" and [[hamas|Hamas]] as "the legitimate representative of the Palestinian people" were widely attributed to Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad al-Thani]] — remarks the [[new-yorker|New Yorker]] noted would have been unremarkable from anyone but a Gulf royal, for whom open endorsement of Iran is explosive.[2]

On June 5, 2017 the Saudi government accused Qatar of "dividing internal Saudi ranks, instigating against the state, infringing on its sovereignty, adopting various terrorist and sectarian groups aimed at destabilizing the region," and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain announced a blockade and the severing of diplomatic relations — moves the New Yorker called effectively acts of war.[2] [[donald-trump|President Trump]] tweeted his support afterward, writing that during his recent trip he had stated there could no longer be funding of radical ideology and that "Leaders pointed to Qatar—look!"[2]

Lange emphasizes that the move blindsided the American national security bureaucracy: the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[cia|CIA]] were not informed in advance, and when a U.S. official complained of receiving no notice, Emirati ambassador Yousef Al Otaiba replied that he had already informed the White House.[2] A former American intelligence official told the New Yorker it was inconceivable that the Saudis or Emiratis would have acted without U.S. approval and that "it's pretty well understood that the White House gave the green light," a claim a senior administration official denied.[2] American diplomats, the magazine reported, knew almost nothing about what was happening between the White House and the Gulf monarchies.[2]
