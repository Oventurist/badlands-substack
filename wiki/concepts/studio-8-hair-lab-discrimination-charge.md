# Studio 8 Hair Lab Discrimination Charge

The **Studio 8 Hair Lab discrimination charge** is a civil rights enforcement action brought in November 2023 by the [[michigan-department-of-civil-rights|Michigan Department of Civil Rights]] against salon owner [[christine-geiger|Christine Geiger]] and her business, Studio 8 Hair Lab, after she publicly stated that she would not serve people who identify as anything other than a man or a woman.[1]

## The charge

Michigan officials charged Geiger and the salon with discrimination on November 15, 2023, following an investigation into complaints filed over her comments.[1] John Johnson Jr., the department's executive director, told reporters in a briefing that "the truth is, based on a thorough investigation, that Studio 8 and its owner Christine Geiger, openly and repeatedly violated the [[elliott-larsen-civil-rights-act|Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act]]."[1]

## The underlying statements

In July 2023, Geiger posted on Facebook: "If a human identifies as anything other than a man/woman please seek services at a local pet groomer. You are not welcome at this salon. Period."[1] She added that salon workers might refer to customers as "hey you" if they requested a particular pronoun.[1] In another post she said that "LGB are more than welcome" but that transgender people were not.[1]

## Legal framework

The Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act prohibits discrimination on the basis of certain characteristics, including religion.[1] Implemented in 1977, it was expanded in 2023 by the state legislature and Democratic Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] to cover gender identity, enshrining a 2022 Michigan Supreme Court interpretation.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that Geiger had simply made clear her salon "is not a good fit for people that need a safe space," and that if the goal were customer comfort, her candor was itself a service to potential clients.[1] The real objective, she contended, was "to make sure that she's not allowed to not play the pronoun game," because "every space has to be a safe space, or the rainbow cult will ruin your life."[1] She pointed to Colorado baker [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]], who had spent more than eleven years in court over his refusal to bake a gay wedding cake, won at the Supreme Court, and then faced the process all over again over a transgender-themed cake — calling that pattern [[lawfare|lawfare]].[1] In her framing, lawfare has two aims: punishing opponents, and chiseling away at the contours of the law, with the process itself serving as the punishment while the regime fundraises and saturates the narrative and the defendant struggles to find counsel.[1] She concluded that the practice "must be abolished," that there must be harsher penalties for weaponizing the law "out of frivolity and hurt feelings," and that otherwise it becomes normal and "the communist overthrow of America will be complete."[1]
