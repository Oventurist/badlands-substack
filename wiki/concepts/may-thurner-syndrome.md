# May-Thurner Syndrome

May-Thurner Syndrome is a relatively rare vascular condition that can predispose patients to blood clots in the leg.[1] According to the Cleveland Clinic, the syndrome in and of itself is not particularly dangerous; the principal risk arises if a clot in the leg breaks off and travels to the lungs, producing a pulmonary embolism, which is life-threatening.[1]

## Boebert diagnosis

The condition entered American political news in April 2024 when Colorado Representative [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] was taken to a Colorado hospital after experiencing severe swelling in one of her legs, and doctors found a blood clot and diagnosed her with May-Thurner Syndrome.[1] Boebert underwent surgery to remove the clot and insert a stent; her campaign said the surgery was successful, and doctors at UCHealth Medical Center in Loveland said she was expected to make a full recovery with no long-term health effects.[1] Boebert publicly thanked Dr. Rebecca Bade and the UCHealth team for their care and for providing insight on the diagnosis.[1]

## Commentary on illness and representation

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a constituent of Colorado's fourth congressional district, used the diagnosis to raise a broader question about serving in office while ill, noting that she had previously questioned why Representative [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] was absent for votes before learning he was fighting cancer.[1] She argued that being able to show up is a non-negotiable part of the job, calculating that Scalise represents 775,766 Louisianans and Boebert 731,516 Coloradans in CD3, rising to 748,891 in CD4, so that roughly 1.5 million Americans were effectively without representation while their representatives fought illness.[1] While wishing both members healing and recovery, she said she wanted more information on how Boebert's diagnosis, treatment and recovery would affect her ability to appear for critical votes and craft legislation.[1]
