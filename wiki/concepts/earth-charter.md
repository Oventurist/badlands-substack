# Earth Charter

The Earth Charter is a declaration of environmental and developmental principles drafted between 1996 and 2000, described in Badlands Media commentary as the formalization of the doctrine established at the 1992 [[rio-earth-summit|Rio Earth Summit]].[1]

## Authorship

The charter was co-authored by former Soviet Premier [[mikhail-gorbachev|Mikhail Gorbachev]] and Canadian businessman and UN organizer [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], and the International Earth Charter drafting committee was chaired by [[steven-rockefeller|Steven Rockefeller]].[1] It is presented in the source as a companion instrument to Agenda 21, later rebranded [[agenda-2030|Agenda 2030]].[1]

## Interpretation

In this account, the Earth Charter belongs to a lineage of documents through which the Rio process consolidated NGOs and corporations under a "green" agenda, giving supranational institutions authority over the development choices of sovereign nations.[1] The article contrasts this western definition of "sustainability" — read as scarcity management and de-industrialization — with the Eurasian definition, which it characterizes as the expansion of energy, water and industrial capacity through projects such as the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and China's [[south-north-water-transfer-project|South-North Water Transfer Project]].[1]

Strong's own stated ambitions are used as interpretive context: in a 1990 interview he described a hypothetical group of world leaders concluding that "the only hope for the planet" was the collapse of industrialized civilization, and asking whether it was "our responsibility to bring that about."[1]
