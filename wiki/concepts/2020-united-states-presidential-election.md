# 2020 United States Presidential Election

The 2020 United States presidential election, which resulted in the certification of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as president over the incumbent [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], is treated throughout the Badlands Media corpus as a contested and, in the view of its contributors, stolen election. The September 2023 essay [[are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in|Are Jack Smith and Fani Willis in on the Plan?]] approaches the election specifically through the question of whether allegations of fraud have ever received a genuine judicial hearing.

## The litigation record

The article states that 55 cases were filed in connection with the 2020 election and that 50 of them were thrown out on [[legal-standing|standing]] rather than on the merits of the evidence presented. From this the author draws the conclusion that "a LOT of evidence that never got the chance to be heard," and argues that commentators who claim there is no evidence because the courts dismissed the cases misunderstand basic procedure: standing must be established before a court can rule on evidence at all. The essay speculates that many of these dismissals reflected "some form of corruption or compromise operating in the background."

## Centrality to the Trump prosecutions

In the source's analysis, the 2020 election is the substantive core of both the federal indictment brought by [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] and the Fulton County racketeering indictment brought by [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]]. Both, it argues, rest on the allegation that Trump lied about the election and knew his claims to be false; that alleged lie is described as the predicate "crime" giving rise to all the other charged offenses. The article maintains that Trump "has never contradicted himself on the 2020 election" and has been "100% consistent since the get-go."

Because Trump is now a defendant, the essay argues, he possesses powers of discovery and a due-process entitlement to demonstrate the truth of his claims — making the criminal cases, paradoxically, the first realistic forum in which evidence of election fraud might be heard. Should such evidence reach the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], the author suggests, it "could open a massive can of worms for the Biden administration."
