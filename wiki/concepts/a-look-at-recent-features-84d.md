# A Look At Recent Features

"A Look At Recent Features," subtitled "Catch Up on the Latest from Badlands Contributors!", is an edition of the Badlands News Brief published on November 7, 2025 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on its Substack. The post recorded 41 likes at the time of archiving. Rather than presenting original reporting, the edition functions as a curated roundup of recent long-form feature articles by Badlands contributors, published while the Badlands team was attending GART that week. The brief notes that regular publication would resume the following week and directs readers to still-available GART virtual tickets.

## Format and framing

The article opens with an explanation that, with the team away at GART, "today's Badlands News Brief is a look back at our recent feature articles from Badlands contributors." It includes a sponsored placement for My Shroom Vibe, a microdosing blend described as being made from organically grown psilocybin mushrooms with third-party testing for purity; the copy claims the product eases depression and anxiety while boosting creativity, focus, and memory through enhanced neuroplasticity, and offers readers 10% off with the promo code "BADLANDS."

Each entry in the roundup carries the feature's title, author, publication date, cover image, and an excerpt, followed by a "Read full story" link to the full piece on the Badlands Substack.

## Featured articles

The edition highlights seven features published between October 10 and November 1, 2025.

"Combating the Transhumanist Religion" by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] (November 1, 2025) is described as a continuation of a series; the excerpt states that in part one readers "were introduced to the 'above ground' and 'below ground' movements that have been carefully sculpted to quite literally, induce as many humans as possible to participate in the willful self-destruction of industrial civilization."

"The Venezuelan Squeeze" by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] (October 30, 2025) carries an excerpt clarifying that "the information presented here is not necessarily intended as an attack on the Trump administration specifically, but on the broader Western foreign policy establishment—including politicians, corporations, and tax-exempt organizations that profit from and perpetuate NATO-style globalism."

"Disinformation is Necessary" by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] (October 28, 2025) is introduced with the single-line question, "What would be the purpose of creating confusion?"

"On Ted Kaczynski and False Prophets" by Matthew Ehret (October 25, 2025) refers back to an earlier Badlands piece in which readers "were introduced to the figure of Theodor Kaczynski (aka: The Unabomber,) and the common axioms he shared with the leading oligarchists running the World Economic Forum and [[great-reset|Great Reset]] today." (See Ted Kaczynski.)

"A More Perfect (Labor) Union" by Ryan DeLarme (October 23, 2025) opens by observing that "in the great American mythos, labor unions are typically depicted as bulwarks of the working class—gruff, principled warriors wrestling with corporate titans and shielding families from the cold winds of corporate greed," a framing the feature goes on to complicate.

"The Rising Scourge of Eco-Terrorism" by Matthew Ehret (October 21, 2025) begins from the 2022 Hollywood film *How to Blow Up a Pipeline*, in which "several young, anxiety ridden eco-activists conspire together to literally blow up pipelines in Texas to save civilization."

"Shadows, Sovereigns and Civil Wars - Part 2" by Matthew Ehret (October 10, 2025) is identified as the second feature in the "Shadows, Sovereigns and Civil Wars" series.

## Editorial note

The brief closes with Badlands Media's standard disclaimer that "the opinions expressed in the Badlands News Brief are those of the authors and do not necessarily reflect the attitudes or positions of Badlands Media," together with an appeal for readers to share the piece, comment, and support the outlet through paid subscriptions so that "our collective of citizen journalists" can "take back the narrative." It ends with the outlet's recurring slogan, "We are the news now."
