# De-Dollarization

**De-dollarization** refers to the process by which countries reduce their reliance on the United States dollar for international trade, commodity pricing, and central bank reserves, thereby eroding the dollar's status as the global reserve currency.[1] By early 2023 the trend had become a recurring theme in alternative financial commentary, with a growing number of countries conducting cross-border trade in currencies other than the dollar.[1]

## The 2023 acceleration

Coverage compiled by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] in April 2023 catalogued a rapid succession of developments described as evidence that de-dollarization had "shifted into overdrive," including the Malaysian prime minister's statement that there was "no reason" for Malaysia to rely on the US dollar as the influence of the Chinese yuan grew.[1] The same reporting framed de-dollarization as having moved from a theoretical concern to a measurable shift in global settlement practices.[1]

## Weaponization of the dollar

A central argument advanced in the April 2023 commentary is that the acceleration began when the Biden administration began "wielding the dollar as a political weapon," most conspicuously by freezing dollar reserves held by Russia's central bank in response to the war in Ukraine.[1] Writing in the *Daily Caller*, economists E.J. Antoni and Peter St. Onge argued that a reserve currency's second critical feature, after stability, is its apolitical nature, and that this feature was being "gutted."[1] They noted that the frozen assets were not American property but dollars owned by the Russian central bank and the Russian people, and that the seizure was intended to trigger bank runs and collapse Russia's credit system — an outcome that did not occur.[1] Instead, they argued, the episode exposed a willingness to violate trillions of dollars of foreign-owned assets, setting a precedent whose danger was "difficult to overstate."[1]

[[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], then a host at [[fox-news|Fox News]], amplified this analysis, warning that a war against Russia would be "the last war we can afford to fight" and criticizing the administration for antagonizing much of the world.[1]

## Historical framing

Commentators linking de-dollarization to US foreign policy have pointed to earlier leaders — notably Saddam Hussein and Muammar Gaddafi — who threatened to conduct oil trade outside the dollar system before their governments were destroyed, a pattern cited as evidence that challenges to dollar hegemony carry existential risk for the challenger.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of April 7, 2023, the commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed de-dollarization within a broader sequence of anticipated events: economic contagion and the demolition of the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] system, creating both the practical and psychological conditions for a change in currency valuation; the threat of kinetic war with a Russia–China alliance that would cast NATO and the "New West" as a new Axis; and the resulting opening for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to return as a peacemaker.[1] Burning Bright characterized the overall situation as a "Crisis Cascade" afflicting the establishment rather than the public.[1]

## Zambia's criminalization of the dollar (2024)

In July 2024 the [[bank-of-zambia|Bank of Zambia]] drafted rules requiring the [[zambian-kwacha|kwacha]] and its subunit the ngwee to be used for all domestic public and private transactions, with up to ten years in prison or substantial fines for those transacting in foreign currency, principally the US dollar.[2] The central bank said increasing dollar use in the local economy undermined its monetary policy tools and pressured the exchange rate.[2]

Badlands commentary treated the move as a landmark in African de-dollarization, noting that the kwacha had been reported in February as Africa's best-performing currency against the dollar and arguing that flooding [[zambia|Zambia]] with a rapidly inflating currency could be a tactic to destabilize a growing economy.[2] The Brief observed that criminalizing the dollar "would have been inconceivable even just a year or two ago."[2]

## Russia–Iran currency agreement (2024)

On 25 November 2024 the governor of the [[central-bank-of-iran|Central Bank of Iran]], [[mohammad-reza-farzin|Mohammad-Reza Farzin]], told the 11th Conference on Modern Banking and Payment Systems in Tehran that [[russia|Russia]] and [[iran|Iran]] had completed their de-dollarization of bilateral trade, describing the shift to national currencies as part of a plan to counter "unjust sanctions."[3] "We have entered into a currency agreement with Russia and abandoned the dollar. Now we only trade in rubles and rials," he said, adding that the two countries' financial authorities had also agreed the exchange rate to be used in foreign trade transactions.[3] The two sanctioned states had first announced their intention to settle mutual trade in rubles and rials in July 2022.[3] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] paired the announcement with NATO Admiral [[rob-bauer|Rob Bauer]]'s warning that businesses should prepare for a "wartime scenario" and with Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen's abrupt departure, presenting the cluster as evidence of accelerating monetary realignment.[3]

## March 2023 milestones

Late March 2023 produced a cluster of events that Truth Community commentators read as an inflection point. [[china|China]] completed its first yuan-settled liquefied natural gas trade when the state oil company [[cnooc|CNOOC]] and France's [[totalenergies|TotalEnergies]] transacted roughly 65,000 tonnes of UAE-sourced LNG through the [[shanghai-petroleum-and-natural-gas-exchange|Shanghai Petroleum and Natural Gas Exchange]].[4] Beijing had for years emphasised settling oil and gas trades in yuan in order to internationalise the currency and weaken the dollar's grip on world trade, an effort amplified by [[russia|Russia's]] embrace of the yuan under Western sanctions.[4]

In the same week [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] approved partial membership in the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]], prompting [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] to ask what would happen to the dollar if the kingdom followed by joining [[brics|BRICS]].[4] Separately, [[indonesia|Indonesia]] moved to drop [[visa|Visa]] and [[mastercard|Mastercard]] in favour of a domestic payment platform, with [[bank-of-indonesia|Bank Indonesia]] preparing the transition and President [[joko-widodo|Joko Widodo]] citing the sanctions imposed on Russia as justification.[4]

Financial analyst [[kirk-elliott|Dr. Kirk Elliott]] argued that generations of American financial network effect were unwinding because the United States had pushed Russia out of the system, and that the exit of a 278-million-person economy made dollar-denominated savings measurably riskier; he pointed to central-bank accumulation of precious metals as the counterweight.[4]

## Putin's "irreversible" declaration (August 2023)

Addressing the [[2023-brics-johannesburg-summit|2023 BRICS summit in Johannesburg]] remotely, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said de-dollarization was "gaining momentum" and characterized the dollar's receding global centrality as an "objective and irreversible" process.[5] He argued that the five [[brics|BRICS]] members were becoming the new world economic leaders, with 26% of global GDP and, on a purchasing-power-parity basis, 31% of the global economy against 30% for the [[g7|G7]], and attacked "illegitimate sanctions" and the "unlawful freezing of sovereign states' assets."[5] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the summit's announcements, including rumors of a gold-backed BRICS currency, as an attack on the U.S. [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] whose "Final Fiat Countdown" would begin with Saudi accession to the bloc.[5]

## The China–Brazil settlement deal (March 2023)

At the end of March 2023 the Brazilian government announced that [[china|China]] and [[brazil|Brazil]] had reached a deal to trade in their own currencies, dropping the US dollar as an intermediary entirely.[6] The arrangement covers roughly $150 billion in annual bilateral trade as well as financial transactions, with yuan exchanged directly for reais and vice versa, and extends China's network of dollar-exempting bilateral currency arrangements — previously including Russia, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia — to the largest economy in Latin America.[6] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] called the deal "Beijing's latest salvo against the almighty greenback" and noted that de-dollarization news was by then "dropping fast and furious," with even [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] commenting on the trend and the dollar "fully weaponized after February 2022 for the entire world to see and fear."[6] See [[china-brazil-currency-agreement|China–Brazil currency agreement]].[6]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that while political and cultural distraction events dominated the US media cycle, real and lasting moves were being made on currency, and that with a strong Russian–Chinese bloc emerging, "the days of Petrodollar hegemony appear to be numbered."[6]

## Lula's call to end dollar dominance (April 2023)

In April 2023 Brazilian president [[luiz-inacio-lula-da-silva|Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva]] urged [[brics|BRICS]] countries to develop a new currency and move away from the dollar, coverage of which was carried in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of April 17, 2023.[7] The appeal came as [[brazil|Brazil]] and China strengthened ties, having recently agreed to use the yuan in cross-border trade, although analysts quoted in the reporting saw de-dollarization as unlikely given the lack of good replacements.[7]

Badlands Media sponsor and financial analyst [[kirk-elliott|Dr. Kirk Elliott]] observed that news of shifts from the US dollar to alternative trade arrangements had accelerated over the preceding two years from a monthly to a daily occurrence.[7] He pointed to Mexico, one of the United States' largest trading partners, reportedly considering joining BRICS, and advised readers to anticipate the direction of the trend in their personal careers, finances, and businesses.[7]
