# China Special Maritime Operation off Taiwan (2026)

The 2026 "special maritime law enforcement operation" was an action launched by China east of [[taiwan|Taiwan]], reported as a bonus item in the June 9, 2026 *Badlands Brief*.[1]

According to China's Ministry of Transport and the state news agency Xinhua, the operation began on a Saturday and was intended to exercise maritime administrative law-enforcement jurisdiction and to protect national rights and interests.[1]

## Trigger

The reported action followed a May 28 summit in Tokyo at which Japanese Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]] and Philippine President [[ferdinand-marcos-jr|Ferdinand Marcos Jr.]] announced plans to begin maritime delimitation talks concerning their exclusive economic zones and continental shelves.[1] Chinese Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[mao-ning|Mao Ning]] said the waters under discussion lie east of Taiwan and that any delimitation negotiations there must involve China.[1]

## Reactions

Taiwan objected to the planned talks as well, stating that the area overlaps with waters in which it claims rights and interests and urging Japan and the Philippines to consult Taipei.[1] Japanese Chief Cabinet Secretary [[minoru-kihara|Minoru Kihara]] said any agreement between Tokyo and Manila would not be legally binding on third parties.[1]

Taiwan's coast guard said it deployed at least five vessels in response and monitored four Chinese government vessels departing from Xiamen.[1] The developments came amid ongoing disputes involving China, Taiwan, Japan, and the Philippines over maritime claims and jurisdiction in waters surrounding Taiwan.[1]
