# Apple Vision Pro

The **Apple Vision Pro** is a mixed-reality headset produced by [[apple|Apple]], priced at nearly $4,000, which reached retail store shelves on Friday, February 2, 2024.[1] Within days of release, early adopters were photographed and filmed using the device in public settings, generating widespread commentary about the social consequences of wearable spatial computing.[1]

## Launch reception

Coverage aggregated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] under the headline "Society One Step Closer To Dystopia" documented early adopters attempting to integrate the headset into ordinary daily activity, including numerous videos circulating on X of users wearing the device while driving vehicles.[1] Commentators noted that the animated series The Simpsons had depicted comparable headset-wearing scenes years earlier, treating the coincidence as another instance of the show anticipating cultural developments.[1] Black Mirror creator Charlie Brooker had previously said that Apple's Vision Pro presentation struck him as unsettlingly similar to his own dystopian anthology series, remarking, "It's weird, it's really weird."[1]

## Transhumanism critique

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] framed the device within a broader argument about incremental cultural conditioning, comparing the process to the multi-generational ideological subversion described by Soviet defector [[yuri-bezmenov|Yuri Bezmenov]].[1] Esler argued that the Vision Pro represents one more step along a path toward unchecked [[transhumanism|transhumanism]], and that this technological trajectory is set to collide with the effects of identity politics and [[queer-theory|queer theory]].[1]

The unifying pattern he identified was "daily disembodiment": identity politics conditioning individuals to analyze themselves through categories of race, gender, sex and disability; queer theory conditioning them to deconstruct cultural norms; and consumer technology conditioning them, upgrade by upgrade, away from the presence of the body and toward avatars.[1] Esler distinguished deliberate engineering by bodies such as the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and the [[united-nations|United Nations]] from disembodiment that he considered a natural byproduct of industrial-era human ingenuity directing awareness outward.[1]

Extending the argument, Esler asked whether ideological and technological disembodiment would eventually converge — producing "trans-meta" people who identify chiefly as their online avatars, and social pressure to address them accordingly — and whether neurological changes in generations raised inside virtual and augmented reality would be reframed as a novel form of neurodivergence challenging supposed bigotry.[1] His proposed remedy was to raise children skilled at cultivating embodiment and committed to empiricism, treating the body rather than a mind "floating in cyberspace" as the ultimate truth-seeking instrument, and allowing technology to serve as an assistant balanced against a life of physical presence.[1]
