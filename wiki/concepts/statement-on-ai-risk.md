# Statement on AI Risk (2023)

The **Statement on AI Risk** was a one-sentence public declaration published on May 30, 2023 by the [[center-for-ai-safety|Center for AI Safety]], signed by dozens of artificial intelligence industry leaders, academics and public figures, warning that the threat of an AI-driven extinction event should be treated as a top global priority.[1]

## Content

The statement read in full: "Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war."[1] Its brevity was deliberate, designed to allow signatories with widely differing views on timelines and mechanisms to endorse a single shared premise.[1]

## Signatories

Signatories included [[openai|OpenAI]] chief executive [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]]; Geoffrey Hinton, often described as a "godfather" of AI; senior executives and researchers from Google DeepMind and [[anthropic|Anthropic]]; Kevin Scott, chief technology officer of [[microsoft|Microsoft]]; internet security and cryptography pioneer Bruce Schneier; climate advocate Bill McKibben; and the musician [[grimes|Grimes]], among others.[1]

## Context and coverage

[[cnn|CNN]] reported that the statement highlighted wide-ranging concerns about unchecked [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]], while noting that AI experts consider society still far from developing the kind of [[artificial-general-intelligence|artificial general intelligence]] depicted in science fiction, since contemporary chatbots largely reproduce patterns from their training data rather than thinking independently.[1] CNN also argued that ChatGPT had "helped heighten an arms race in the tech industry over artificial intelligence."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she was initially glad to see business leaders belatedly discussing safety and ethics in emerging technology, but that on second reading the signatories appeared to be "attempting to put the genie back in the lamp."[1] She noted that CAIS director Dan Hendrycks compared the statement to atomic scientists issuing warnings about the very technologies they had created, and observed that emerging technologies had enjoyed little oversight, leaving the public dependent on what industry chooses to disclose about actual capabilities.[1] She argued that the established regulatory order cannot secure basic freedoms regarding the internet or data privacy because it relies on lobbyist experts with a vested interest in minimal oversight.[1] Drawing out Hendrycks' analogy, she pointed out that the atomic scientists' warning came in 1939 and their creation was deployed over Hiroshima and Nagasaki six years later, and asked whether the CAIS warning would prove equally prophetic.[1]
