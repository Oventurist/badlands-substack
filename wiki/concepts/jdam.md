# Joint Direct Attack Munition (JDAM)

The Joint Direct Attack Munition is a guidance kit that converts unguided ("dumb") bombs into GPS-guided precision weapons. The extended-range variant, the JDAM-ER, can strike targets more than 50 miles away.[1]

## Transfer to Ukraine

A Biden administration defense aid program involved sending JDAM-ER kits to Ukraine to give Ukrainian forces standoff precision strike capability.[1] A leaked classified document referenced the approval of "1,000 arming lanyards" for Ukrainian forces, suggesting that more than 1,000 of the smart bomb kits were to be transferred.[1]

## Reported battlefield failures

A highly classified [[pentagon|Pentagon]] document connected to alleged leaker [[jack-teixeira|Jack Teixeira]] indicated that American smart bombs were failing in Ukraine as a result of effective Russian electronic warfare.[1] The document reviewed Russian countermeasures rendering the munitions ineffective and also noted that in some cases technical problems caused the weapons to fail to detonate.[1]

According to reporting cited in the leak coverage, Russia was using GPS jamming to interfere with the weapons' targeting process, causing JDAMs — and at times other American systems such as guided rockets — to miss their targets.[1] Former Pentagon official and retired CIA officer [[mick-mulroy|Mick Mulroy]] said jamming of the guidance signal would explain why the munitions were not performing as expected compared with their performance in other war zones.[1]

## Interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the disclosure as evidence undercutting the narrative that Ukraine was winning the war, and further speculated that if the U.S. government were in a state of [[devolution|Devolution]], with the U.S. military quietly coordinating with Russia at senior levels to avoid direct great-power conflict, then apparently "simple" jamming successes would carry added significance.[1]
