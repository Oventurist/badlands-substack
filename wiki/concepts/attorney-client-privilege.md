# Attorney-Client Privilege

Attorney-client privilege is the legal doctrine protecting confidential communications between a lawyer and client from compelled disclosure. It became a central public controversy in April 2018 when the FBI raided the New York office and residence of [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]], the personal attorney of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## The Cohen raid

Trump responded to the raid by tweeting "Attorney-client privilege is dead!"[1] CNBC noted that the circumstances were not as simple as the president suggested: investigators must clear a high legal standard to secure a search warrant for a lawyer's office under Justice Department guidelines.[1] A further key exception applies when a lawyer and client collude on potential crime or fraud; as attorney Danny Cevallos wrote, "the purpose of this exception is to assure that the secrecy between lawyer and client does not extend to obtaining advice in furtherance of contemplated or ongoing criminal or fraudulent conduct."[1]

## The precedent argument

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that Trump's amplification of the raid was deliberate. By drawing the entire country's attention to the phrase "attorney-client privilege," Trump established precedent: no future president can invoke the privilege to hide crimes, and defense lawyers who participate in covering up a client's crimes have no protection either.[1] Lange names [[barack-obama|Obama]], Bush, and Clinton as the figures whose future defense strategy was thereby foreclosed, and describes Trump as having made himself "bait" so the enemy would walk into the trap.[1]

Q drop 1287 (April 27, 2018) framed the raid in these terms, asking "How do you introduce evidence into an investigation (legally)?… Admissible in the court of law? Insert Rudy," linking the raid to [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]]'s arrival on Trump's legal team.[1] In this reading the destruction of the privilege defense, and the subsequent hand-off of the Cohen matter from [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] to [[geoffrey-berman|Geoffrey Berman]] at the [[southern-district-of-new-york|SDNY]], were both mechanisms for legally injecting an "insurance file" of evidence into ongoing criminal investigations.[1] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than settled legal analysis.
