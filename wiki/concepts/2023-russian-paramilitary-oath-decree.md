# 2023 Russian Paramilitary Oath Decree

The 2023 Russian paramilitary oath decree was an order signed by President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] on Friday, August 25, 2023 — two days after the presumed death of mercenary chief [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]] — requiring Russian paramilitary fighters to swear an oath of loyalty to the Russian flag.[1]

## Provisions

Published on the Kremlin's website, the decree stated its purpose as "forming the spiritual and moral foundations for the defense of the Russian Federation" and applied to members of "volunteer formations," a term commonly used in Russian official language to describe mercenary groups.[1] Its scope also extended to groups "contributing to the execution of tasks given to the armed forces" and to territorial defense units.[1] Fighters covered by the measure must pledge "their loyalty to the Russian Federation... strictly follow their commanders and superiors' orders, and conscientiously fulfill their obligations."[1]

## Context and Badlands commentary

The decree followed the plane crash northwest of Moscow that is believed to have killed Prigozhin and his senior [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] commanders, and came against the backdrop of competing narratives about that event.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] characterized the situation as one of "dueling Narratives": in the West, the establishment line held that Putin and the Russian chain of command had ordered the killing in order to sow discord and chaos, while in Russia the prevailing account was that Prigozhin had been assassinated in an act of terrorism.[1]

Burning Bright argued that, whether Prigozhin was alive or dead, the oath requirement had "the look of a continuance of June's Russian loyalty test," referring to the abortive Wagner march on Moscow earlier that summer.[1] He noted two related developments: [[alexander-lukashenko|Alexander Lukashenko]] had announced that Wagner troops would be permitted to remain in Belarus, conveniently reinforcing Belarusian military strength in continuation of the bloodless and strategic "March on Moscow"; and Pentagon spokesman Gen. [[pat-ryder|Pat Ryder]] had stated that Wagner's presence on the ground in Ukraine was "no longer a significant factor."[1] Combining these with Western media's turn against the Ukrainian counteroffensive, Burning Bright posed the questions of whether Russia had effectively "won" the war and whether Wagner was being retired, vetted and reallocated.[1]
