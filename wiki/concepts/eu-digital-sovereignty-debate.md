# EU Digital Sovereignty Debate

The EU digital sovereignty debate concerns how far the [[european-union|European Union]] should go in reducing its dependence on American technology companies, and it exposed a rift between France and Germany at a Brussels summit in October 2025.[1]

## The Franco-German split

When EU leaders backed a "sovereign digital transition" at a summit in Brussels on Thursday, October 23, 2025, their words masked a disagreement between the bloc's two largest founding members over how to handle America's overwhelming dominance in technology.[1] In Paris, sovereignty is understood as backing local champions and breaking reliance on US Big Tech; in Berlin, the focus is on staying open and protecting Europe without severing ties with a major German trading partner.[1]

The leaders' statement was described as a typical fudge, citing the need for Europe to "reinforce its sovereignty" while maintaining "close collaboration with trusted partner countries," according to a near-final draft obtained by [[politico|Politico]] ahead of the gathering.[1] Commentators argued that such ambiguity plays into the hands of incumbent US interests.[1]

## Amazon outage

The bloc's reliance on American technology was brought into sharp focus again on Monday, October 20, 2025, when an outage at Amazon cloud servers in Northern Virginia disrupted the morning routines of millions of Europeans.[1]
