# r/K Selection Theory

**r/K selection theory** (abbreviated RKST in the source material) is a framework drawn from population biology that a Badlands Media contributor writing as [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] presents as "the master key to politics" — the single best available explanation for the bifurcation of humanity into "conservative" and "left/liberal" camps.[1]

## Core Formulation

In the author's summary, r/K selection theory is a theory of how particular biological and psychological types emerge from particular environments, depending on the relative availability of resources in those environments, which in turn determines the degree of *parental investment* those types confer upon their offspring.[1]

"r-selected" organisms are said to emerge from environments where resources are plentiful, while "K-selected" organisms emerge from environments in which scarcity and harsh conditions had to be overcome in order to survive.[1] K-selected types reproduce a smaller number of highly adaptable offspring within a more tribal setting — the author's illustrative example is a wolf pack — whereas r-selected types reproduce more indiscriminately and form far looser in-groups, illustrated by rabbits.[1]

In the human world, the K-selected instinct is said to manifest as the competitive nuclear family, with high investment in offspring; the r-selected pattern is associated by the author with indiscriminate sex and the championing of single motherhood.[1]

## Environmental Logic

The r-selected strategy, the article argues, "makes perfect sense in an environment with infinite (or quasi-infinite) resources": where there is no pressure to compete for limited goods, advantage accrues to whoever consumes and reproduces most, not to whoever practices restraint or saves resources for a rainy day.[1] In scarcer environments the advantages invert — those who breed indiscriminately without regard for the fitness of their offspring lose out to those who breed selectively and conserve.[1]

The author stresses that the model is a simplification in practice: many humans exhibit both r- and K-selected strategies to some degree, and it would be a misnomer to think that a person or a people could not display traits of both. A sex addict (described as an r-selected trait) may nonetheless exhibit loyalty to their nation (described as K-selected behavior).[1] On the whole, however, the traits are said to cluster together, and the two survival-and-replication strategies are described as *mutually exclusive* at the level of strategy — engaging in one is always at some expense to the other, in the same way that one cannot be simultaneously polygamous and monogamous.[1] The article attributes a great deal of human misery to individuals chasing contradictory r and K directives at the same time.[1]

Humanity, the author holds, has been generally more K-selected throughout its history, because the conditions of material abundance needed to produce leftism are themselves difficult to bring about.[1]

## Application to Ideology

The author argues that the notion that the Western world should abolish all ethnic and national distinctions and become a source of welfare for the entire world is a "distinctly r-selected viewpoint" — only an organism whose instincts were formed in times of plenty could entertain it.[1] The "consume as much as possible and reproduce with anyone who's available" ethos of the r-selected organism is described as indistinguishable from the ethos of modern consumerist society, and as unsustainable.[1]

A caveat is added regarding in-group preference. Although r-selected people are described in trait tables as "relatively indifferent to in-group," the author notes that leftists are conspicuously collectivist. The distinction he draws is that leftist in-group preference is not rooted in genuine kinship, loyalty, or camaraderie, but in *loyalty to ideologies that make them feel safe* or that spike dopamine; groups, parties, and companies inspire loyalty in proportion to how strongly they signal adherence to those ideologies.[1]

## Historical Cycles

Applying the theory to history, the article reformulates the widely circulated four-part cycle — strong men create good times; good times create weak men; weak men create hard times; hard times create strong men — in r/K terms: K-selected men create conditions of material success and abundance; abundance creates r-selected men; r-selected men squander their civilizational inheritance; hard times create K-selected men.[1] The author places America's entry into the third phase in the 1960s, when the baby-boom generation enjoyed unprecedented economic abundance and, in large numbers, eschewed tradition in favor of "sex, drugs, and rock and roll," while arguing that the country is now entering phase four.[1] He explicitly disclaims a blanket judgment of all boomers, describing the analysis as a comment on broad trends.[1]

The theory is presented as the reason historians have long observed societies drifting leftward as they become materially prosperous: leftism, on this account, is simply the survival and replication strategy that emerges in r-selected environments — that is, during times of plenty.[1]

## Sources and Reception

The author credits the book [[the-evolutionary-psychology-behind-politics|The Evolutionary Psychology Behind Politics]] as the work that demonstrated the thesis conclusively in his view, and describes his own article as the simplest breakdown he could produce of that material.[1] The exposition is presented as part one of a two-part series, laying the groundwork for the claim that [[leftism]] consists of "predators and prey."[1]
