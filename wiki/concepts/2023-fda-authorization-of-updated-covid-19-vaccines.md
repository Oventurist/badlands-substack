# 2023 FDA Authorization of Updated COVID-19 Vaccines

On September 11, 2023, U.S. drug regulators authorized new [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] in an effort to counter the poor effectiveness of the existing slate of shots.[1]

## Scope of the authorization

The U.S. [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] cleared products from [[moderna|Moderna]] and [[pfizer|Pfizer]] that would be available later that month to Americans as young as six months of age.[1] The agency granted full approval for people aged 12 and older, and emergency authorization for children from six months to 11 years of age.[1] No mention was made of [[novavax|Novavax]], whose vaccine was also available in the United States.[1]

The updated shots targeted XBB.1.5, a subvariant of the Omicron variant.[1] According to the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]], that subvariant had already largely been displaced by newer strains including EG.5 by the time of the authorization.[1]

[[peter-marks|Peter Marks]], a top FDA official, said in a statement that "vaccination remains critical to public health and continued protection against serious consequences of COVID-19, including hospitalization and death," and encouraged those eligible to consider getting vaccinated.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that the FDA had approved the new shots for everyone as young as six months old with no clinical trial data available, calling the decision a "genocidal rerun" that was "beyond parody."[1] She contended the approval came despite what she described as overwhelming evidence that the virus is not deadly for the vast majority of people, that the vaccines neither work nor prevent transmission, and that fibrous clots and unprecedented cardiovascular failure are deadly side effects.[1] She framed the decision as evidence that global population reduction is the explicit goal of established institutions, and predicted that [[covid-mandates|mandates]] would follow.[1]
