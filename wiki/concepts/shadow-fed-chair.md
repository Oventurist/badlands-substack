# Shadow Fed Chair

The **"shadow" Fed chair** is the informal name for a proposed maneuver in which a sitting U.S. president names his intended successor to the chairmanship of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] long before the incumbent chair's term expires, creating a parallel voice on monetary policy that markets might begin to follow instead of the actual chair.[1]

## Origins

The concept was first floated publicly by Scott Bessent before he became [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Treasury secretary, and re-entered mainstream coverage in June 2025 when Trump said he would announce his pick to succeed Federal Reserve Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] "very soon" — despite Powell having eleven months left in his term.[1] CNN reported that announcing a nominee that far in advance would be an unprecedented development in the central bank's 111-year history.[1]

## Rationale and criticism

Trump's stated frustration with the Fed centred on its refusal to lower interest rates, and he attacked Powell relentlessly for months over the stance.[1] Former Fed officials and academics warned that installing a shadow chair could undermine the sitting chief and intensify the economic uncertainty that had persisted since Trump took office.[1]
