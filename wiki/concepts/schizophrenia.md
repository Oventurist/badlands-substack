# Schizophrenia

**Schizophrenia** is reinterpreted in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — What is Clown World?" as evidence for the reality of non-physical hostile entities rather than as a purely internal disorder.[1]

The essay revisits the work of [[jerry-marzinsky|Dr. Jerry Marzinsky]], who spent decades working with schizophrenic patients in maximum-security prisons and published *An Amazing Journey into the Psychotic Mind*.[1] Its summary of his conclusion is blunt: the voices schizophrenics hear are not hallucinations but the voices of hostile, non-physical entities — demons — and Marzinsky found that reciting Psalm 23 is an effective way to fight back against them.[1]

A discovery singled out as especially striking is that the voices hate being called out as parasites: calling them evil, diabolical or psychopathic provokes no reaction, but calling them a parasite agitates them greatly.[1] The author's explanation is that the first step in solving any problem is being able to describe it accurately.[1] He compares the medical system's teaching that the voices originate in the patient's own mind to claiming that a tick attached to the body is a legitimate part of it, arguing that so long as this goes unchallenged the entities enjoy near-perfect cover.[1] In any parasitic relationship, he writes, it is most advantageous to the parasite if the host regards it as part of itself — ideally as indispensable, even as a source of guidance or entertainment.[1]

The same reasoning is applied to psychedelic experience: many people describe the entities they meet as emanations of their own psyche, but the author sees no reason to prefer that interpretation over the simpler hypothesis of an [[astral-plane|astral realm]] accessible through altered states of consciousness.[1]
