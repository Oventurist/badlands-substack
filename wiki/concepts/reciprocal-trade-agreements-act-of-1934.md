# Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act of 1934

The Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act of 1934 (RTAA) is the United States statute by which [[us-congress|Congress]] first delegated to the president the authority to negotiate bilateral tariff reductions — a power Congress had previously guarded closely under Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution, which gives the legislative branch sole authority to "lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises."[1]

## Legacy

The delegation created by the RTAA was renewed repeatedly through the mid-twentieth century and became the foundation of executive primacy in American trade policy.[1] It was expanded by the [[trade-expansion-act-of-1962|Trade Expansion Act of 1962]] and further formalized by the [[trade-act-of-1974|Trade Act of 1974]].[1] Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited this statutory lineage in arguing that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariffs are lawful and would be upheld by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]
