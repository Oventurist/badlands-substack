# November 2023 Continuing Resolution

The **November 2023 continuing resolution** was a short-term federal funding measure passed by the [[us-house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] on Tuesday, November 14, 2023, to avert a government shutdown at the end of that week.[1] The bill was authored by Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] and cleared the chamber on a bipartisan vote of 336 to 95.[1]

## Vote composition

Of the votes in favor, 209 came from Democrats, meaning a majority of the supporting coalition was drawn from the minority party rather than the Speaker's own conference.[1] The measure was opposed by 93 Republicans and two Democrats.[1] Following House passage, the [[us-senate|Senate]] was required to approve the measure before Friday for it to take effect.[1]

## Structure of the "laddered" plan

The measure adopted an unusual two-step or "laddered" structure. Funding for a first tranche of priorities — military construction, Veterans Affairs, transportation, housing and the Energy Department — was extended until January 19, 2024.[1] The remainder of the federal government, covering anything not addressed in the first step, was funded until February 2, 2024.[1] The proposal contained no additional aid for Israel or Ukraine, omissions that were a central point of contention in the surrounding appropriations fight.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the outcome surprised no one, noting that more Democrats than Republicans voted for the funding and that the Republican defections were "likely political" and indicative of which members faced difficult challenges in the coming election rather than a matter of principle.[1] She characterized Republican leadership's framing of the vote as a "win" as hollow, observing that the arrangement guaranteed a repeat of the same standoff in January and February, and concluded that the episode further demonstrated that [[congress|Congress]] was "irreparably dysfunctional and useless."[1]
