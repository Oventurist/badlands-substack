# Biden Prostate Cancer Diagnosis

In May 2025 former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was reported to have been diagnosed with an aggressive form of prostate cancer, disclosed days after the release of audio from his interviews with special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]].[1]

## Clinical picture

The disease was characterized by a Gleason score of 9 and Grade Group 5 with bone metastasis, a presentation with a five-year survival rate of between 30% and 40%.[1] Fox News senior medical analyst Marc Siegel described the cancer as "pretty far advanced" and said, "It's the most aggressive type. That means it has the highest risk of spread — which obviously he has had."[1] Siegel noted that Biden was also presenting with urinary symptoms, another indicator of advanced disease, and that prostate cancer is often asymptomatic in its early stages.[1]

## Political reaction

David Axelrod suggested that conversations about Biden's "mental acuity" would be more muted following the announcement.[1] Badlands commentary observed that House Republicans said nothing about the diagnosis on X while Senate Republicans issued thoughtful and prayerful statements, and asked where the demands for committee hearings had gone.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s allies at Badlands declined to celebrate. Jon Herold wrote that he "wouldn't wish [prostate cancer] on anybody" and was "praying he makes a full recovery so he can watch all his friends go to prison."[1] Ashe in America said she would neither gloat nor "pretend this is a brand new diagnosis," pointing out that Biden had said he had cancer in 2022 and that the media had dismissed the remark as a gaffe; she argued the diagnosis made the officials operating "the cancer-riddled dementia patient and his autopen" more culpable, not less, and called for [[jill-biden|Jill Biden]], the medical staff, the chief of staff, the cabinet, [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] and [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] to testify under oath.[1]

Chris Paul wrote that he had projected such an event more than four years earlier when describing Biden as "the Fall Guy," a scapegoat to be blamed for the regime's program and then written out of the story "as sympathetically as possible."[1] He predicted the character would be killed off at a moment when a distraction was needed, suggesting the week before the nation's 250th birthday celebration and military parade, and argued that the announcement would make further discussion of Biden's dementia and illegitimacy socially forbidden.[1] The episode is closely bound up with the [[autopen-scandal|autopen scandal]] and with the release of the [[hur-report|Hur]] interview recordings.[1]

## Screening history controversy

On Tuesday, May 20, 2025, a spokesperson for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s office said the former president's last known screening for prostate cancer had been in 2014, when he was 71 and serving as vice president.[2] Physicians and experts quoted by The Washington Post noted that it is not unusual for men over 70 to stop having regular prostate-specific antigen (PSA) tests, the standard screening used to assess prostate cancer risk.[2] Biden's office said he received the diagnosis on the Friday before the announcement, and had previously stated that it emerged while he was being examined "after experiencing increasing urinary symptoms."[2] Leaders in both parties were reported to be startled when the diagnosis was announced that Sunday, described as aggressive but manageable through hormone therapy, with the office saying "the President and his family are reviewing treatment options with his physicians."[2]

## Additional Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] said the reporting was laying out the "Joe Biden as Fall Guy" scenario he had projected four and a half years earlier, and suggested that Biden's "cancer moonshot" would ultimately recast him as a tragic hero.[2]

[[canncon|CannCon]] called for a full investigation into the diagnosis, situating it in a sequence that included the preceding week's chatter about Biden's cognitive decline and the release of the [[hur-report|Hur]] audio, which he said decimated Mockingbird Media narratives, followed by the revelation of Gleason-9 prostate cancer metastasized to the bones — timing that in his view provided cover against attacks over a five-year coverup.[2] He noted that [[scott-adams|Scott Adams]] had announced the same diagnosis and described the disease as "100% not curable" once in the bones and "100% curable" while isolated in the prostate.[2] From those premises he posed three questions: whether White House physicians knew and when, and whether they would be held accountable for missing it; whether, if they knew, Biden himself knew and willingly forwent treatment in order to run and serve; and whether the physicians informed not Biden but whoever was handling the presidency, who then kept him in the dark.[2] He judged it likely Biden knew and tried to push through to the end of his term, arguing that hair loss and "chemo-brain" would have been glaringly obvious.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the ten-and-a-half-year screening gap "preposterous" for a man who held the two most powerful offices in the world during six of those years, and argued that "we didn't catch this aggressive and advanced cancer because we didn't look" is a very silly thing to believe.[2] Recounting a conversation with her mother-in-law, a retired geriatric care nurse who had buried three children including a daughter lost to brain cancer in the 1980s, she relayed the woman's adamant conviction that Biden has brain cancer rather than dementia — "if the cancer is in his bones, it's in his brain" — calling it a plausible theory.[2] Ashe concluded that manipulating people's emotions over their personal losses from cancer in order to avoid accountability for treason is "sociopathic."[2]
