# Illinois and Chicago National Guard Lawsuit

The Illinois and Chicago National Guard lawsuit is a legal challenge filed on Monday, October 6, 2025, by the State of Illinois and the City of Chicago seeking to block President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s move to federalize and deploy the [[national-guard|National Guard]].[1]

## Claims

Lawyers for the state and city argued that the foundational principle separating the military from domestic affairs was "in peril" as the administration pushed to send the Guard into cities nationwide.[1] They asked a federal judge to block the "relocation, federalization, or deployment" of any state's National Guard to Illinois, contending that the state was being singled out because of its political leadership.[1]

Attorneys for the plaintiffs further argued that Trump had not provided a lawful basis for taking control of the Guard, noting that federal law permits such action only in cases of rebellion or invasion, and that the move violated the [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus Act]], which bars the use of the military for domestic law enforcement.[1]

## Context

The filing came days after US District Judge [[karin-immergut|Karin Immergut]] temporarily enjoined a parallel deployment of out-of-state Guard troops to Portland, Oregon — a ruling White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] called "untethered in reality and in the law."[1] Illinois Governor [[jb-pritzker|JB Pritzker]] had separately accused [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] of racially targeting enforcement subjects, a claim rejected by White House policy deputy [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]].[1]
