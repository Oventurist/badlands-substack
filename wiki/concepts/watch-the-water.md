# Watch the Water

"Watch the water" is a recurring instruction attributed to Q and treated within the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series as an open-ended directive to monitor maritime and water-related events for significance.[1]

In Chapter 20 of the series, published October 5, 2024, the author invokes the phrase while discussing the short-lived East and Gulf Coast port strike, which had been suspended until January 15.[1] Having catalogued a series of numerological coincidences surrounding [[harold-daggett|Harold Daggett]], the president of the [[international-longshoremens-association|International Longshoremen's Association]] — the $1.7 million valuation of his home, its 7,163 square feet (digits summing to 17), his 76-foot yacht read as a reference to 1776, his reference to 17 deaths at ports within three months, and the reported 45,000 workers on strike — the author concludes that the episode was "laden with far too many comms" to ignore, and that there was more to the strike than publicly understood.[1] The closing remark, "we were told to 'watch the water', after all," presents the phrase as the interpretive key linking a labour dispute at American ports to the wider Q narrative.[1]

The passage also notes that [[donald-trump|President Trump]] had a relationship with Daggett and had met him at Mar-a-Lago some months earlier, a fact the author treats as further evidence that the strike was not a random event.[1]

## Use as a name-based cue

The phrase has also been read as a pun rather than a literal instruction. In her November 11, 2022 Badlands Media article titled "Watch the Water," [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] applied it to [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]], the Republican candidate in the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 Arizona general election]], noting that "our friend Q said 'watch the water' three times" and concluding simply: "Her name is Kari LAKE."[2] In that reading the directive functions as advance notice that Lake would be a decisive figure, and Gillespie paired it with a second Q-adjacent phrase, "It's time to return the diamonds."[2]

Gillespie used the cue to frame her central claim that Lake's "unspoken top priority" — beyond the border and election-reform pledges she had made publicly — would be to reopen the question of the 2020 election in Arizona, making the state "the first of MANY dominoes to fall."[2]
