# Badlands Brief: Crypto Smears, Professional Plotlines, & Meat Probes

"Crypto Smears, Professional Plotlines, & Meat Probes" is an installment of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily news-commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated May 5, 2026. In keeping with the format of the series, each segment pairs a summary of mainstream or independent reporting with commentary from a named Badlands contributor. The May 5 edition covers a newly disclosed witness in the Russiagate accountability inquiry, European fuel supply strain, a new United States naval operation in the Strait of Hormuz, NATO's outreach to the entertainment industry, an antitrust probe into the major beef packers, and a defamation suit brought by a Trump-linked cryptocurrency venture.

## New witness tied to Russian interference assessments

The lead item reports that a newly disclosed witness involved in United States intelligence assessments of Russian election interference in 2016 and 2020 has come forward to congressional investigators. According to reporting from Just the News cited by the Brief, the individual has knowledge of intelligence community processes tied to Russia-related findings and has provided information to lawmakers examining how those assessments were developed and communicated.

The Brief situates the disclosure within the longer arc of the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 2016 counterintelligence investigation known as [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] and the subsequent review by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]], whose final report in 2023 criticized aspects of the FBI's handling of intelligence but did not establish a criminal conspiracy involving senior officials. It notes that United States intelligence agencies had concluded that [[russia|Russia]] interfered in the 2016 election principally through cyber operations and influence campaigns aimed at undermining confidence in the electoral process and harming Democratic candidate [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], findings embodied in the [[2017-intelligence-community-assessment|January 2017 Intelligence Community Assessment]].

The witness is identified as [[gavin-wilde|Gavin Wilde]]. Just the News reported: "Gavin Wilde, who now works at think tanks in the nation's capital, co-authored the flawed January 2017 intelligence community assessment on Russian meddling in the 2016 election prior to serving on the Trump National Security Council in 2018 and 2019. And he has also said that he worked on U.S. intelligence assessments on alleged Russian influence efforts ahead of the 2020 election as well." Wilde was previously referred to as "Witness 2" in the [[first-trump-impeachment|Ukraine impeachment]] documents.

Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] calls the development significant, describing Wilde as the co-author of the 2017 assessment and now reportedly a government witness in what she terms the "treasonous grand conspiracy" against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after the 2016 election, adding: "She was never supposed to lose." She questions whether Wilde is "newly disclosed" rather than "newly flipped," suggesting that a participant who was "hands on keyboard in multiple overt acts" has likely been cooperating for some time given the pace of disclosures. She quotes the reporting that "the 2019 claims by Witness 2 were critical in helping Intelligence Community Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]] push Ukraine whistleblower [[eric-ciaramella|Eric Ciaramella]]'s complaint forward, and Wilde's 'Russiagate'-linked biases were concealed from House investigators during the impeachment process," noting that [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] discussed that obstruction in his book *Government Gangsters*. Asking who directed Wilde between 2017 and 2019, she cites the observation that the Intelligence Community Assessment "was written at the direction of then-President [[barack-obama|Obama]] and largely overseen by then-FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] and former Director of National Intelligence [[james-clapper|James Clapper]]," closing with the single word "Accelerate."

## Europe warns of tight fuel supplies

The second segment reports that European officials and energy analysts are warning of tightening fuel supplies across parts of the continent, attributing the strain to market disruptions, refining constraints, and geopolitical tensions affecting global oil flows. Inventories of refined fuels including diesel are described as under pressure, with constrained refining capacity, shipping bottlenecks, and instability in key transit routes named as contributing factors. Policymakers and industry voices acknowledge gaps in visibility over real-time fuel inventories across fragmented national storage systems, complicating coordinated responses; while [[european-union|Europe]] maintains strategic petroleum reserves, tracking accessible refined fuel is more complex than tracking crude stockpiles alone.

The Brief notes that the debate is complicated by political divisions over energy policy, with some officials, including allies of Italian leadership, calling for reconsidering restrictions on Russian energy imports, while others argue Europe has sufficient resources but faces policy-driven limits on production and use. Analysts emphasize that the problem is not an absence of oil but a combination of logistics, refining capacity, and policy choices that could produce localized shortages or price spikes. The item is summarized on this wiki under [[european-energy-crisis|the European energy crisis]].

[[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] recalls that two weeks earlier International Energy Agency executive director Fatih Birol said Europe had roughly six weeks of jet fuel supply before shortages would begin. He argues Europe "is now staring down a bleak abyss," refusing both to develop its own oil and gas industries and to buy from Russia. He recalls that President Trump warned German Chancellor Angela Merkel and other Europeans about exactly this predicament on June 9, 2018, at the Group of Seven summit in Canada — the occasion of a widely circulated meme image — pointing out the absurdity of asking the United States taxpayer to fund the military defense of a continent whose members depend on their supposed adversary for energy. He characterizes the sequence as self-inflicted: ending domestic energy programs, becoming dependent on a neighbor, provoking a kinetic conflict with that neighbor, and expecting a foreign country to fund the resulting defense.

## Project Freedom and the Strait of Hormuz

The Brief reports that President Trump announced a new military operation called Project Freedom, aimed at reopening the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and escorting commercial vessels through the shipping lane as clashes with [[iran|Iranian]] forces escalated on Monday. United States naval and air assets engaged Iranian drones and small boats near the strait, destroying multiple vessels while attempting to secure safe passage for commercial shipping and move stranded ships through the waterway, which handles a significant share of global oil traffic. Iranian state-linked sources claimed their forces struck a United States warship; the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] rejected the assertion, stating that no American vessels were hit and that United States forces repelled the attacks. Reports from the region indicated some commercial ships were damaged or targeted while others transited under United States protection, with the situation described as fluid.

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argues that the deployment is "kinetic-adjacent in presentation, yet primarily narrative in function," forcing every other actor to orient around a new baseline authored by Trump. He observes that the media apparatus has grown candid that the "Actuals" on either side cannot be independently confirmed at the speed of the story cycle, and that even official confirmations arrive shaped by the propaganda requirements of [[5th-generation-warfare|fifth-generational conflict]]. Whether or not specific strikes, interceptions, or transits occurred as described, he writes, "they are not occurring for the reasons most immediately offered," and these quiet admissions are cumulative: "the war we are shown is not the war that is being fought, and the reasons we are given are rarely the operative ones."

He proposes that psychological orientation should shift from "What is happening?" to the question of projected end states, offering three candidates for Project Freedom: a tactical restoration of commercial movement that relieves pressure on global energy flows while preserving room for negotiated de-escalation; a broader demonstration that traditional chokepoints can no longer be held by any single actor without unsustainable narrative and material costs; or a public signal, legible to domestic and multipolar audiences alike, that the United States under Trump will not be drawn into the perpetual conflict trap the previous paradigm required. Each end state, he argues, shapes the counter-narratives from media and institutional voices, which typically emphasize escalation risk, recklessness, or the inevitability of wider entanglement — all defending the legitimacy of the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based order]] as its enforcement mechanisms fray. Applying the same lens to Iran, he reads Tehran's warnings, claimed strikes, and ceasefire-violation charges as projecting an end state in which external powers must treat Iranian red lines as operational realities rather than rhetorical posturing. Either way, he concludes, Trump has chosen "to raise the temperature on the Global Battlespace rather than continue the slower boil."

## NATO, the film industry, and European troop drawdowns

The fourth segment reports that [[nato|NATO]] officials held discussions with film and television industry representatives as part of efforts to shape public understanding of the alliance's mission, even as tensions grow over potential United States troop reductions in Europe. NATO sources describe the engagement as strategic communications and public outreach, a practice common among governments and defense alliances; critics characterize it as an attempt to influence narratives through entertainment media. The reporting cited is from [[rt|RT]].

At the same time, Trump has signaled he is considering reducing or repositioning United States forces stationed in parts of Europe, including Spain and Italy, amid disputes over defense spending and burden-sharing. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said she would oppose any unilateral United States withdrawal from Italy, underscoring reliance on the American security presence, and other European officials warned a drawdown could weaken deterrence. Reports also indicate internal disagreements within NATO over strategy and posture, including a reported decision not to proceed with a plan to place Tomahawk missiles in [[germany|Germany]].

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicts "at least a few movies over the next few years that will glorify NATO while vilifying Russia," and notes the timing of Trump's posturing to leave NATO as Europe faces a jet fuel shortage and general energy crisis. He argues Trump is simultaneously motivating European countries to redevelop their oil sectors and to establish their own national defenses, since United States scale-back will make those defenses necessary. Europeans could buy oil and gas from Russia, he writes, but NATO complicates that because "the organization only exists to combat Russia" — adding that they could still do it by abandoning [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and letting the Russians deal with [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]].

## Justice Department antitrust probe into the beef packers

The Brief reports that the Trump administration's [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and Department of Agriculture announced an intensified antitrust investigation into JBS, Cargill, Tyson Foods, and National Beef, citing price manipulation, foreign ownership risks, and supply chain problems. Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] revealed reviews of more than three million documents and hundreds of rancher interviews, while Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins called foreign-owned packers such as JBS and National Beef a national security threat amid reports of slave labor ties.

Rollins said on Monday: "Half of these meatpacking giants, including the largest meat packer in the world, are either foreign-owned or have significant foreign ownership and control, making them a threat not just to our cattle producers, but a threat to America itself." The probe follows a Trump directive from November 2025 and ties into a coming settlement on poultry and meat prices, at a time when United States beef herds have fallen to 1950s lows because of droughts and declining ranch numbers.

Ashe in America quotes the department's statement that it "has executed on [the President's] Executive Order to stop anti-competitive behavior in the broader food supply market. Later this week, we'll be announcing a historic settlement that will directly affect the prices of proteins like chicken, pork and turkey." She recaps three simultaneous developments: an imminent settlement promising lower meat prices, ongoing investigations in which the department seeks whistleblower help against the effects of globalism and collusion on United States agriculture, and the naming of Big Ag as "a threat to America itself." She adds that she cannot wait for "the Blanche doomer spin on this one."

## Trump-linked crypto firm sues Justin Sun

A bonus item reports that World Liberty Financial, a cryptocurrency venture co-founded by President Trump and his sons, filed a defamation lawsuit on Monday against crypto billionaire Justin Sun, accusing him of a coordinated effort to damage the company's reputation. The complaint, filed in Miami-Dade County, Florida, seeks unspecified damages and a public retraction over statements Sun made on social media.

World Liberty alleges that Sun bet against its $WLFI token and used third parties to conceal purchases, and that the company moved to freeze assets tied to one of his firms. The filing claims Sun responded with a "scorched earth" campaign, including threats of litigation and public attacks, after the company refused his demands. The suit further accuses Sun of spreading false claims about the company's operations and amplifying them through influencers and bot accounts, generating millions of views and significant reputational harm. Sun, founder of Tron, had not publicly responded to the allegations.
