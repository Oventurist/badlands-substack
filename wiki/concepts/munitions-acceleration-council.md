# Munitions Acceleration Council

The Munitions Acceleration Council is the informal name for a United States Department of Defense effort to sharply expand American missile production in anticipation of a possible conflict with [[china|China]].[1] Reported by the *Wall Street Journal* on 29 September 2025, the initiative involves the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] pressing defense contractors to double or quadruple output amid mounting concern over insufficient weapons stockpiles.[1]

## Origins and structure

The drive was launched in June 2025, when the Department of Defense invited top missile makers to a Pentagon roundtable.[1] The meeting was led by Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff General [[dan-caine|Dan Caine]], and drew major arms contractors, startups such as [[anduril|Anduril Industries]], and crucial component suppliers.[1]

Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg is described as playing an "unusually" hands-on role in the effort, personally telephoning some executives on a weekly basis to track their progress.[1] Pentagon spokesman [[sean-parnell|Sean Parnell]] told the *Wall Street Journal* that President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Secretary Hegseth were "exploring extraordinary avenues to expand our military might and accelerate the production of munitions," characterising the work as a collaboration between defense industry leaders and senior Pentagon officials.[1] The council is focused on twelve specific weapons the Pentagon wants on hand for a potential China conflict.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the report as the opening of a hype cycle for a long-anticipated conflict over Taiwan, while questioning how the defense industry working alongside top Pentagon officials to rebuild the military differed in any meaningful way from how the system has always operated.[1]
