# START II Treaty

The START II Treaty was a strategic arms reduction agreement between the United States and Russia that would have banned the use of multiple warheads on intercontinental ballistic missiles and vastly reduced the total number of warheads held by each side.[1]

Russia announced its withdrawal from START II a day after the United States officially left the [[anti-ballistic-missile-treaty|Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty]], the abrogation of which had been announced by President [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] on December 13, 2001.[1] The collapse of both treaties in quick succession is presented as the launching point of a renewed arms race between offensive and defensive systems, and as part of the wider pattern of subverted opportunities for US–Russian cooperation described in the article.[1]
