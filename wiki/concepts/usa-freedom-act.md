# USA Freedom Act

The USA Freedom Act is a United States surveillance-reform statute enacted in 2015 that ended the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]]'s bulk collection of Americans' telephone records.[1]

## Origins in the Snowden disclosures

Badlands Media presented the law as the principal domestic legislative consequence of the [[snowden-disclosures|Snowden disclosures]], arguing that the leaks by [[edward-snowden|Edward Snowden]] prompted its passage in the 114th Congress.[1] The publication cited the Act alongside international responses — including the European Court of Justice's 2015 ruling against the U.S.–EU Safe Harbor Agreement and the wave of commercial encryption adoption known as the "Snowden effect" — as proof that the disclosures produced tangible reforms rather than mere damage.[1]

## Relevance to the Gabbard hearing

The article invoked the Act during its defense of [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] against senators who demanded she label Snowden a traitor, noting that Snowden was never charged with treason and that his leaks demonstrated the government itself had been violating the Constitution by spying on citizens.[1] Badlands Media contrasted the reforms the Act achieved with the repeated renewal of [[section-702|Section 702]] without reform, which it treated as evidence that Congress's appetite for surveillance reform was short-lived.[1]
