# Denver Executive Order 152

Denver Executive Order 152 is a municipal executive order signed by Mayor [[mike-johnston|Mike Johnston]] on Thursday, February 26, 2026, restricting federal immigration enforcement activity in the city of Denver, Colorado.[1] It was issued in response to stated concerns over federal immigration enforcement actions, including mass deportations and operations conducted by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]].[1]

## Provisions

The order bans ICE and other federal immigration agents from using city-owned or city-controlled property for immigration enforcement operations without a judicial warrant, subpoena, or court order.[1] It also bars city agencies from sharing databases or technology with the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] or its immigration partners absent legal compulsion such as a warrant or court order.[1]

Operationally, the order directs the Denver Police and other local law enforcement to use de-escalation protocols to protect peaceful protesters during civil immigration enforcement operations; to identify ICE agents with body cameras when tactically safe; to provide life-saving aid to anyone harmed; and to intervene in cases of excessive force, including potentially detaining federal agents.[1] Johnston confirmed the city would continue cooperating with ICE on violent criminals where a warrant exists.[1]

## Legal status and reaction

DHS responded the following Friday by calling the order "legally illiterate."[1] Badlands commentary agreed with that assessment, noting that local governments cannot legally prevent federal agents from enforcing immigration law in public spaces or on non-city property, and that federal supremacy generally prevails in immigration enforcement.[1] The order was characterized as largely symbolic in practice but an "appropriate triggering event" for a broader federal–municipal confrontation, and — because its legal infirmity is widely understood yet the policy was adopted anyway — as arguably an act of insurrection in the Civil War sense.[1]
