# Razor Blade Throat COVID-19 Resurgence

"Razor blade throat" is a term used in China to describe severe throat pain, likened to swallowing shattered glass or razor blades, which became the signature symptom of a COVID-19 resurgence reported in the country in the spring of 2025.[1]

Pulmonologist and epidemiologist [[zhong-nanshan|Zhong Nanshan]] said in a May 19, 2025 interview with China-based media that the symptom was more pronounced in this wave and was accompanied by more frequent coughing.[1] Dr. Li Tongzeng, director of the Infectious Diseases Department at Beijing You'an Hospital, told Chinese media that the wave began in March and was expected to peak in late May.[1] Zhong identified the dominant strain as the Omicron XDV variant — highly transmissible but relatively less virulent — with early symptoms of fever, headaches, fatigue, a burning throat, and severe coughing, and assessed the epidemic to be in a "climbing phase" lasting six to eight weeks before subsiding by the end of June.[1]

Chinese netizens described their experiences on Weibo, the social platform closely monitored by the Chinese regime, with one account recounting a colleague coughing "so wildly I thought she choked on food," her main symptom being "razor blade throat."[1] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] remarked that the phrase sounded "like the name of a metal band or something."[1]
