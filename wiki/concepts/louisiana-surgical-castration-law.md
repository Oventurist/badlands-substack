# Louisiana Surgical Castration Law

The Louisiana surgical castration law is a 2024 statute, Senate Bill 371, authorizing state judges to sentence certain convicted sex offenders to undergo surgical castration.[1]

## Enactment and provisions

Governor [[jeff-landry|Jeff Landry]], a Republican, signed the bill into law on June 18, 2024; it was sponsored by state Senator Regina Ashford Barrow and was expected to take effect on August 1, 2024.[1] The law allows Louisiana judges to punish offenders aged 17 and older with surgical castration if they are convicted of aggravated sex crimes against a victim under the age of 13.[1]

The Department of Public Safety and Corrections oversees the procedure, which is not to be performed if it is "not medically appropriate."[1] A court-appointed medical expert must determine whether the offender is "an appropriate candidate" for the procedure within 60 days of sentencing.[1] For offenders sentenced to incarceration or institutional confinement, the legislation directs that the procedure "shall be performed not later than one week prior to the offender's release from the institution."[1] An offender who "fails to appear" as required by court order for surgical castration may be charged and sentenced to three to five years in prison without the benefit of probation, parole, or suspension of sentence.[1]

The measure was reported by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] and carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief covering news from June 24, 2024.[1]
