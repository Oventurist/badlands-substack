# AI-Powered Drone Swarms

AI-powered drone swarms are groups of unmanned aerial vehicles that use artificial intelligence to coordinate with one another during an attack, deciding among themselves how and when to strike.[1] Military experts describe swarm technology as the next frontier in drone warfare because of its potential to deploy tens or even thousands of drones at once to overwhelm the defenses of a target, whether a city or an individual military asset.[1]

## Battlefield use in Ukraine

In a reported example, a trio of [[ukraine|Ukrainian]] drones flew under the cover of darkness to a Russian position and decided among themselves exactly when to strike.[1] Ukraine has conducted swarm attacks on the battlefield for much of the preceding year, and analysts said they were not aware of the technology being used regularly in combat until hearing of the Ukrainian operations.[1]

## Proliferation and ethics

The United States, China, France, Russia and South Korea are among the countries pursuing swarm technology.[1] The U.S. has been exploring it since at least 2016, when it launched more than 100 small drones from three jet fighters; the Defense Department said at the time that "the micro-drones demonstrated advanced swarm behaviors such as collective decision-making, adaptive formation flying, and self-healing."[1] The use of AI on the battlefield has raised ethical concerns that machines could be left to decide the fate of combatants and civilians.[1]

Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] tied the story to the defense-software sector, asking whether there is a [[palantir|Palantir]]-like company operating in Ukraine as there is in Israel, "or is it just Palantir in all three places?"[1] He questioned what Palantir CEO Alex Karp means when he says he is defending "the West" rather than America, comparing the framing to that used by David Frum, Douglas Murray and other neoconservative figures in prominent positions.[1]
