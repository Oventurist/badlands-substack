# Judicial Watch v. Oregon Voter Roll Maintenance Lawsuit

The Judicial Watch lawsuit over Oregon's voter roll maintenance is a federal case brought by [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] against Oregon's secretary of state alleging failure to clean the state's voter registration rolls as required by federal law.[1]

## Filing and claims

Filed in October 2024, the suit argues Oregon violated Section 8 of the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] (NVRA) by failing to make a "reasonable effort" to remove ineligible voters from its registration rolls.[1] Judicial Watch alleges that 29 of Oregon's 36 counties removed few or no registrations, that 35 counties reported registration rates over 100%, and that Oregon has the nation's highest known rate of inactive voter registrations.[1]

## DOJ statement of interest

In June 2025 Judicial Watch announced that the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] had filed a statement of interest supporting the lawsuit.[1] U.S. District Court Judge [[michael-j-mcshane|Michael J. McShane]] scheduled a hearing in the case for June 18, 2025, in Eugene.[1]
