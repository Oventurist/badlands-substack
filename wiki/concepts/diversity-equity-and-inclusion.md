# Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion

Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) refers to the set of institutional programs, boards, hiring frameworks, and contracting preferences adopted across American government bodies and corporations to address demographic disparities.[1] By 2023 DEI had become a central target of conservative state governments, which characterized the practices as discrimination based on immutable characteristics.[1]

## The Florida case

On August 1, 2023, the [[central-florida-tourism-oversight-district|Central Florida Tourism Oversight District]] announced the abolition of all DEI programs within its jurisdiction, including its DEI board, following an internal investigation.[1] The district said its predecessor, the [[reedy-creek-improvement-district|Reedy Creek Improvement District]], had run hiring and contracting programs that discriminated against Americans on the basis of gender and race and cost taxpayers millions of dollars.[1]

## Debate over reach

Commentary at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] endorsed the principle that discrimination based on immutable characteristics is wrong while cautioning that the announcement's practical effect was limited: it originated with the oversight board created by [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] rather than with [[disney]] itself, and so applied only to Florida-based operations within the district.[1] Pro-DEI analysts argued the prohibition governed only government workers.[1] Michael McAuliffe, a former federal prosecutor and elected state attorney in Florida, told [[newsweek|Newsweek]] that the pronouncement "appears to be a mixture of political posturing and legal implementation," and that Disney, like many American companies, was likely revamping its DEI initiatives to promote a productive workforce "without running afoul of the newly announced prohibition on race as an explicit remedial factor in its business decisions."[1]

## FAA hiring lawsuit and the 2025 aviation disaster

The [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]]'s diversity hiring practices became a flashpoint in early 2025 when details resurfaced of a 2015 class-action lawsuit alleging the agency had denied roughly 1,000 would-be air traffic controllers jobs because of diversity hiring targets.[2] Lead plaintiff [[andrew-brigida|Andrew Brigida]] suggested the agency's obsession with diversity hiring and inclusion had made an accident likely, and the litigation drew renewed attention after an [[american-airlines|American Airlines]] jet collided with a Black Hawk helicopter over Washington, DC, killing 67 people amid controller staffing levels described as "not normal."[2]

Badlands commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the height of DEI policy application had become "affirmative action on steroids" and that the burden of justifying such policies and funding priorities had shifted onto those seeking to continue them — a burden they could not meet.[2] The commentary predicted DEI would die because the public was watching planes fall out of the sky, and framed the moment as building support for a return to valuing competence before compassion in staffing critical capabilities.[2]

## University of Florida terminations, 2024

In a memo circulated on Friday, March 1, 2024, the [[university-of-florida|University of Florida]] announced it was firing all employees in DEI positions and administrative appointments in order to comply with [[florida-board-of-governors|Florida Board of Governors]] regulation 9.016 on prohibited expenditures.[3] The university closed its Office of the Chief Diversity Officer, eliminated DEI positions and administrative appointments, and halted DEI-focused contracts with outside vendors.[3] Affected employees were to receive the university's standard twelve weeks of pay and were encouraged to apply, through April 19, for expedited consideration for other posted positions, with UF Human Resources fast-tracking interviews and answering all applications within the twelve-week window.[3] The memo added that the Office of the Chief Financial Officer would reallocate the roughly $5 million previously reported to Tallahassee as DEI expenditure into a faculty recruitment fund.[3] Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], who had made halting DEI a cornerstone of his overhaul of education in the state, wrote on X: "Florida is where DEI goes to die."[3]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] commented that he did not know how much DeSantis personally had to do with DEI initiatives "running into an America First, logic-based brick wall in Florida," noting that the Florida state legislature had been working to eliminate what he called Marxist and communist programs for the better part of three years, and that the terminations amounted to "well-timed Actuals injected into the news cycle."[3] He asked whether a "cascade" was coming and argued that "normies like winners, and the communists are NOT that."[3]

## Vance's "dustbin of history" declaration (December 2025)

At [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]]'s AmericaFest 2025 in Phoenix, Arizona, Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] declared that gutting federal DEI laws — which he said discriminated against white and Asian Americans — was one of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s big wins since returning to office.[4] Vance framed DEI as antithetical to American values and to a meritocratic society, telling the crowd, "We don't treat anybody different because of their race or their sex. So we have relegated DEI to the dustbin of history, which is exactly where it belongs."[4]

## DEIB and persistence at the state level

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] countered that DEI was neither dead nor static, reporting that the head of "diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging" at the [[colorado-department-of-corrections|Colorado Department of Corrections]] had denied her request to see [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] and doubled down after she objected to her treatment as a member of the media.[4] She argued that DEI was "not only alive and well in Colorado, it's mutating again" into DEIB, and questioned why the concept of "belonging" was being promoted within a prison system.[4]
