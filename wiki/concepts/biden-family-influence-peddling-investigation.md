# Biden Family Influence Peddling Investigation

The Biden family influence peddling investigation was the House Oversight Committee inquiry, chaired by Kentucky Republican [[james-comer|James Comer]], into cash transfers among members of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s family. In November 2023 the probe escalated with subpoenas issued to the president's son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and his brother [[james-biden|James Biden]].[1]

## Subpoenas and interviews

House Republicans issued the subpoenas following weeks of evidence showing cash transfers between Biden family members, characterized in the reporting as either laundered payments from foreign governments or, at best, years' worth of unreported income disguised as loans.[1] Other family members and associates were asked to appear for transcribed interviews, including Sara Biden, Hallie Biden, Elizabeth Secundy, Melissa Cohen, and associate-turned-whistleblower [[tony-bobulinski|Tony Bobulinski]].[1]

## Comer's statements

Comer said the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] had "followed the money and built a record of evidence revealing how Joe Biden knew, was involved, and benefited from his family's influence peddling schemes," and that the committee would now question family members and associates on that record.[1] He added: "Unlike the many lies President Biden told the American people about his family's business schemes, bank records don't lie. These records reveal how the Bidens sold Joe Biden around to the world to benefit the Biden family, including Joe Biden himself, to the detriment of U.S. interests."[1] He said the Oversight Committee, along with the Judiciary and Ways and Means committees, would continue to follow the facts.[1]

Before issuing the subpoenas, Comer told Just the News that his investigation would begin with family members and that the president himself would be the final person interviewed: "We're still a long way from talking to Joe Biden, but we're, we're ready to talk to the family members now."[1] He warned that anyone defying the subpoenas would be held in contempt of Congress.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America dismissed the subpoena signing as "a silly made-for-TV moment," arguing that photo ops in Washington are a dime a dozen and that the Republican priority since obtaining the gavel had been political posturing for party fundraising.[1] The commentary held that the show hearings prove the federal legislative branch is "largely ceremonial and meaningless," asked what the cost, return on investment and carbon footprint of such theater might be, and concluded that the bloated federal establishment no longer serves the American people, invoking Benjamin Franklin's "a republic, if you can keep it."[1]
