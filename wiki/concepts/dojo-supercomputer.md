# Dojo Supercomputer

Dojo is the name [[tesla|Tesla]] gave to a supercomputer project assembled to run the artificial intelligence software behind the self-driving capabilities in the company's vehicles.[1] Reporting in October 2023 disclosed that Tesla was building a "bunkerlike" structure in Texas to house part of the machine, a step described as evidence that the company was thinking past electric vehicles.[1]

## Purpose and design

The project was framed as "an audacious plan by [[elon-musk|Musk]] to take greater control over the technology it needs to run the AI software at the heart of its products."[1] Beyond internal use, the compute capacity could eventually be sold as cloud services to other companies.[1] Dojo is built around an in-house Tesla-designed chip known as the D1, with Musk committing to invest "well over" $1 billion in the effort.[1]

The strategic driver was supply dependency. Tesla's vehicles were powered by [[nvidia|Nvidia]] AI chips, and the D1 program was intended to reduce reliance on that single supplier — a motive shared by other technology giants including [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]] and Microsoft.[1] Musk conceded Tesla would have stayed with Nvidia if the chipmaker could meet Tesla's growing demands; the forward plan was to run both Nvidia and D1 silicon inside Dojo.[1] Glenn O'Donnell, a semiconductor researcher at Forrester, described the general scarcity: "Everybody who is trying to do this is complaining that they can't get enough of these chips."[1] Tesla's shift toward a "robotaxi" product model was cited as the reason far more compute was required.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] read the Dojo buildout as a possible long-term effort to keep semiconductor and computer-chip production corridors — described as the most-traded commodity in the modern world — outside the control of a globalist oligarchy tied to ESG and DEI initiatives.[1]
