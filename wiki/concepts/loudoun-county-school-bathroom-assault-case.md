# Loudoun County School Bathroom Assault Case

The Loudoun County school bathroom assault case concerns the sexual assault of a Virginia high school student and the subsequent prosecution of her father, Scott Smith, whose confrontation with school officials became a national flashpoint over school district transparency.[1]

## The incident

Smith said in 2021 that his 15-year-old freshman daughter at Stone Bridge High School was raped by a 14-year-old transgender boy in the girls' bathroom on May 28 of that year.[1] He told the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]] that school officials telephoned him and reported only that there had been a "physical altercation."[1] After arriving at the school he learned that his daughter had in fact been sexually assaulted, and school staff said they wanted to handle the situation "internally" without calling police.[1]

## Confrontation and conviction

Smith became enraged, and officials called Loudoun County Sheriff's deputies.[1] "I went nuts. I called the principal a pussy. Six cop cars showed up like a fucking SWAT team," Smith said, adding that he "exploded with rage."[1] Video of the confrontation circulated widely.[1] He was charged with obstruction of justice and disorderly conduct in June 2021 and convicted in August 2021.[1]

## Pardon

After several investigations, Virginia Governor [[glenn-youngkin|Glenn Youngkin]] pardoned Smith, with the governor's office announcing the decision on a Sunday in September 2023.[1] The pardon was reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## Treatment in "What Is a Woman?"

The Loudoun County events feature in the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]] documentary [[what-is-a-woman|"What Is a Woman?"]], which Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reviewed in June 2023.[2] She summarised the case as one in which an alleged transgender female sexually assaulted two different girls in female spaces at two different schools, and in which the school board and education system covered up the assaults, ostensibly to protect the narrative and the abuser.[2]

Ashe criticised the film's handling of the episode: rather than centering the rights and roles of parents in their children's education, the retelling foregrounded [[matt-walsh|Matt Walsh]]'s publicity stunt of renting a basement in order to become a resident and thereby qualify to speak at a school board meeting, presenting Walsh as the hero of Loudoun County.[2] She linked the case to her own experience of withdrawing her children from school after a similar bathroom incident, and to what she described as an intensifying trend of protecting criminals at the expense of their victims.[2]
