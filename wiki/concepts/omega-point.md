# Omega Point

The Omega Point is the terminal phase of the evolutionary cosmology devised by the Jesuit [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]], a future state at which humanity would bifurcate into a new evolutionary organism.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that Chardin's Darwinian Christianity professed to gloss over the problematic randomness inherent in Darwin's directionless system by inserting a form of directionality — but not one encumbered by morality, purpose or the categories of "better" and "worse."[1] Instead, directionality was tied to the Omega Point, a concept Ehret likens to [[ray-kurzweil|Ray Kurzweil]]'s notion of the [[technological-singularity|Singularity]].[1]

In Chardin's system the Omega Point teleologically drives an increasing rate of complexity across time, with the universe divided into four phases: the big bang creation of the universe (cosmogenesis), the emergence of life (biogenesis), the emergence of cognition (homogenesis) and the spiritual convergence of humanity (Christogenesis).[1] The third phase Chardin dubbed the age of the [[noosphere|Noosphere]]; the fourth phase is the Omega Point itself.[1]

In *The Future of Man*, Chardin wrote that the Omega Point "represents our passage, by translation or dematerialization, to another sphere of the Universe: not an ending of the Ultra-Human, but its accession to some sort of Trans-Human at the ultimate heart of things."[1] Ehret contends that once the masquerade of "Christ consciousness" and "global love" is stripped away, the Omega Point reveals the misanthropic eugenical fanaticism of a high priest of a dystopian scientific dictatorship, in which bloodshed, war and evil are merely necessary events along the path toward a transhuman species.[1]
