# Badlands Brief: Canadian Separatists & Guerilla Campaigns

"Canadian Separatists & Guerilla Campaigns" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published on Substack by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dated May 26, 2026. The Brief describes itself as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war," pairing excerpted news items with commentary from named Badlands contributors. This edition carried five main segments and a bonus item, with commentary from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. The post recorded 132 likes. As with every edition, the Brief carries a disclaimer that the opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## Trump and the expansion of the Abraham Accords

The lead item reproduced a lengthy [[truth-social|Truth Social]] statement from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on negotiations with [[iran|Iran]]. Trump wrote that "Negotiations with the Islamic Republic of Iran are proceeding nicely! It will only be a Great Deal for all or, no Deal at all — Back to the Battlefront and shooting, but bigger and stronger than ever before — And nobody wants that!"

Trump recounted discussions held on the preceding Saturday with a roster of regional leaders: President [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud]] of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]; [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan]] of the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]]; Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani]], Prime Minister Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim bin Jaber Al Thani and Minister Ali al-Thawadi of [[qatar|Qatar]]; Field Marshal [[asim-munir|Syed Asim Munir Ahmed Shah]] of [[pakistan|Pakistan]]; President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] of [[turkey|Türkiye]]; President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah El-Sisi]] of [[egypt|Egypt]]; [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]] of [[jordan|Jordan]]; and King [[hamad-bin-isa-al-khalifa|Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa]] of [[bahrain|Bahrain]].

Trump stated that, after the work done by the United States "to try and pull this very complex puzzle together, it should be mandatory that all of these Countries, at a minimum, simultaneously, sign onto the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]]." He allowed that "one or two" might have a reason not to sign, which "will be accepted," but held that most should be "ready, willing, and able to make this Settlement with Iran a far more Historic Event than it would, otherwise, be." He characterised the Accords as having been "a Financial, Economic, and Social BOOM" for existing members — the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, [[morocco|Morocco]], [[sudan|Sudan]] and [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] — none of which, he said, had ever suggested leaving or pausing. He predicted the arrangement would "bring true Power, Strength, and Peace to the Middle East for the first time in 5,000 years."

Burning Bright's commentary reprinted a passage he had written in November 2023, one month after the October 7 events, which he describes as a global inflection point and "the core 'split' that fully separated the wheat from the chaff in the MAGA movement, and even in the so-called Truth Community." The 2023 passage argued that "Peace, at the end of the day, should not be extreme, but foundational," that Trump is "primed to be the ultimate problem-solver," and that solutions were already in place "from an impending economic collapse and transition to a potential two-state treaty in the Middle East, regions of responsibility forming around the world while the Warmongers and their ilk are chased from the shadows, and then purged in the light, first Narratively, and eventually … quite Actually." He framed current events as a "War of Stories" provoking a public mandate, adding that "if you have to break a few Bidens, the Uniparty Establishment and the largest decentralized criminal cabal in human history on the way to doing so … all the better," and closed on the maxim that "we are playing an Infinite Game, and that Peace is the Infinite Player on all timelines."

Returning to the present, Burning Bright wrote that he had argued in 2023 that Trump's "Impossible Task" was peace itself, and that his "Kobayashi Maru" would be the "Forever War Gordian Knot" of the Middle East with the [[israel|Israel]]–Iran conflict at its heart. He judged that a deal has already been agreed by the appropriate parties and is nearing public disclosure, that Trump is calling for a "Macro Region of Responsibility" in the Middle East which includes Iran itself, that the MAGA movement has been separated from "Congress, Zionists, NATO Warmongers and Faux-MAGA Grifters," and that "the public appetite for war has been exhausted." He concluded: "The Peacemakers are taking the board."

## Carney's Brexit warning to Alberta

The second segment reported that Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] is warning [[alberta|Alberta]] separatists not to repeat what he calls the "dangerous bluff" of [[brexit|Brexit]], invoking his experience as governor of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] during the United Kingdom's 2016 referendum on leaving the [[european-union|European Union]]. Carney said Alberta voters should be wary of arguments that separation is merely a negotiating tactic with Ottawa, drawing a direct comparison to Brexit: many Britons, he argued, were told they could vote for a "soft" exit and negotiate later, only to face years of political and economic fallout.

The warning followed [[danielle-smith|Danielle Smith]]'s announcement that Alberta will hold an October referendum asking whether the province should begin the legal process toward a future binding independence vote — the [[alberta-independence-referendum|Alberta independence referendum]] process. The Brief notes that critics immediately identified the irony: Carney was among the most prominent establishment voices warning Britain against Brexit in 2016, when pro-Brexit conservatives accused him of running "Project Fear." Alberta conservatives now make the same argument, that federal elites are trying to frighten voters away from asserting provincial leverage and sovereignty.

Ashe in America's commentary — headed "Oh, Canada!" — supported the effort, writing that her "biggest issue with [[canada|Canada]] is that they swear allegiance to Britain and, therefore, exist as a global regime proxy on America's northern border." She called Carney "the crown's man," vehemently anti-Brexit in his day and still "white knuckling the status quo in the midst of awakening." She wrote that she supported the truckers, supports the Alberta separatists, and supports "Canexit," adding that "a Canadian Declaration of Independence in America's 250th year seems like some sort of proof," while conceding she is "not optimistic it will happen."

## Trump on destroying Iran's "nuclear dust"

The third item covered Trump's proposal that Iran's enriched uranium — which he has repeatedly called "nuclear dust" — be either surrendered or destroyed as part of an emerging framework tied to ceasefire and [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] negotiations. In a Truth Social post on Monday, Trump wrote: "The Enriched Uranium (Nuclear Dust!) will either be immediately turned over to the United States to be brought home and destroyed or, preferably, in conjunction and coordination with the Islamic Republic of Iran, destroyed in place or, at another acceptable location, with the Atomic Energy Commission, or its equivalent, being witness to this process and event."

The Brief reported that US negotiators are exploring several disposal scenarios for Iran's stockpile, including transfer to third countries such as Russia, Turkey, China or Pakistan, or supervised dilution and destruction inside Iran. Iran continues publicly to insist on its right to civilian nuclear technology, even as officials signal a willingness to reassure the international community that Tehran is not pursuing a weapon.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that Trump is "bucking all the demands of Israel and her diaspora," which had insisted on American boots on the ground to extract the material, and is instead proposing to rely on Iran destroying it — what he calls "the Venezuela Model" — followed by a restoration of relations. Such a resolution, he wrote, "would be highly subversive of the Zionist regime," to the point of raising the question of whether the United States and Iran were ever actually at war. He noted that Israel has made clear it will proceed with its wars against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]/Lebanon and Iran regardless of US policy. In his reading, Trump's "exposure operation against these subversive elements within our government" will have been a success: "The Arabs and the Persians will be able to make peace, joined by the Pakistanis and the Turks, forming a coalition that will be capable of challenging Israel and keeping its aspiring hegemony in check." He concluded that "the real enemy is not abroad, it is inside our own government," and that Trump had achieved this "by playing the heel."

## Spencer Pratt's guerrilla campaign in Los Angeles

The fourth segment covered [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]], the former reality-television figure and 2026 [[los-angeles|Los Angeles]] mayoral candidate, who has been using power washers and stencils to blast clean slogans into filthy city sidewalks: "IMAGINE IF THE STREETS WERE THIS CLEAN" and "SPENCER PRATT FOR MAYOR." [[modernity-news|Modernity News]] framed the tactic as a guerrilla campaign advertisement against incumbent mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] — the dirtier the street, the stronger the contrast.

The Brief observed that the stunt works because Pratt's campaign already runs on homelessness, street disorder, anger over fire response, and anti-bureaucracy messaging, and that polling and fundraising momentum have placed him in serious contention for a runoff against Bass in the [[2026-los-angeles-mayoral-election|2026 Los Angeles mayoral election]]. The reaction from the city's elite has been correspondingly hostile: [[drew-carey|Drew Carey]] called Pratt a "serial scammer," while other critics have attacked his residency, hotel spending and MAGA-adjacent support. The Brief's judgment is that "the more they sneer, the more Pratt's outsider campaign benefits from the core visual argument: if the city were clean, the ad wouldn't work."

Ashe in America described the tactic as "just a smart use of resources," contrasting Bass's presumed reliance on traditional, expensive campaign media with Pratt's viral AI videos and low-budget stunts. She noted that Newsweek had reported over the weekend that Pratt is dominating in fundraising, and that the most recent polling cited by the New York Times places him within three points of Bass — while cautioning that the poll came from a private research firm with no sponsor cited, work she and her Colorado circle call "inbred polling." She closed with a bleak note on "this fake election cycle," calling the spectacle "a bit depressing in its reflection of the American people and their collective mind" while conceding it is entertaining, "and isn't that all we can really ask of our fake elections?"

## Israel–Ukraine fallout over honours for a Nazi collaborator

The fifth segment reported flaring tensions between Israel and [[ukraine|Ukraine]] after the Ukrainian government held a state reburial ceremony honouring [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]], a leader of the wartime [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN) who collaborated with Nazi Germany during the Second World War. According to multiple reports, President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] and senior officials attended the ceremony in Kyiv, where Melnyk was described as a national hero and his remains, repatriated from Luxembourg, were interred. Israel's Foreign Ministry condemned the event, saying there is "no place for ignoring historical truth and the memory of the victims murdered by the Nazis and their collaborators." [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]] also criticised the honours, warning that celebrating leaders tied to movements that collaborated with Nazi Germany undermines [[holocaust|Holocaust]] remembrance.

The Brief situates the controversy on a longstanding fault line: figures such as Melnyk and [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] are viewed by many Ukrainians as anti-Soviet independence fighters, while critics including Israeli and Polish officials point to documented collaboration between OUN factions and Nazi Germany, including involvement in atrocities against Jews and Poles. Israel and Poland jointly condemned state-sponsored glorification of Bandera and Melnyk as early as 2020, and Ukrainian officials at the time responded that it was an internal Ukrainian matter.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry supplied an extended historical commentary. He wrote that the history of Russia "is perhaps the most tragic and complicated in all of world history," and that Ukraine — "which means 'borderlands' in Russian" — is a large part of that complexity. He noted that modern Ukraine is the largest country in Europe, while historical Ukraine proper was far smaller and localised around Kiev: the eastern and southern regions belonged to the Russian Empire, the western regions to the Austro-Hungarian (Hapsburg) Empire and the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.

He described these territories as among the most ethnically and culturally diverse anywhere, situated on the Steppe — "the largest open plain in the world" — between the Carpathian Mountains and the Ural Mountains at the Russia–Kazakhstan border, conquered by "every great empire, kingdom, khanganate, and caliphate to ever ride on horseback across Eurasia," and the region where all horses on earth originate. Every major religion, he wrote, including Judaism and countless forms of paganism, has gained a foothold there, and cultures from Vikings to Greeks to Mongols to Muslims and Cossacks have ruled in turn; unlike the Middle East, multiple empires could coexist simultaneously because Ukraine is "the breadbasket of Europe."

From this he drew the conclusion that modern Ukraine "will never be the homogenous ethno-state that Andrey Melnik and Stepan Bandera aspired to create when they formed the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN) in 1929." He noted that both men were born Austro-Hungarian citizens of German/Cossack descent in the western regions of modern Ukraine, and that the OUN's predecessor, the [[ukrainian-military-organization|Ukrainian Military Organization]] (UVO), was founded in 1920 and operated in secret out of Prague, engaging in terrorism to advance its political aims. Their counterpart, he argued, were the Jewish militants who emerged from the eastern and southern regions of Ukraine — the Russian Pale of Jewish Settlement — who became known as the Bolsheviks (see [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik Revolution]]).

Though the OUN "immediately allied with the Nazis in the 1930's and began running pogroms for them against their Jewish brethren in the East," he wrote, the Bolsheviks and the UVO/OUN shared common ground: both were "hyper-violent terrorist organizations," and both "harbored a historic hatred for Russians, blaming Russia for their respective cultures' subjugation." This, he suggested, may explain how "the Banderite culture of violent insurgency became adopted by Jewish-Ukrainian militants," and he added the aside that he believes there were "thousands of Jewish Nazis in the Third Reich, as well. All of which were Zionists." His conclusion was that the present moment shows "the hyper-radicalization of two cultures (Israel/Ukraine) with a shared ancestry and history — both fueled by their historical connection to the lands they currently inhabit," one more secular and one more esoteric, but both "possessed by a conviction that they are fulfilling some kind of prophecy or destiny."

He was arrested in Turkey on May 15 and extradited to the United States, where federal charges accuse him of coordinating attacks tied to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East.
