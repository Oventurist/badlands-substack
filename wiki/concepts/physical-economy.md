# Physical Economy

**Physical Economy** is the science of economic value first developed, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, by the American statesman [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]], and distinguished from all monetarist-statistical economic theories.[1] LaRouche's system does not assume that all monetary claims within a system are equally valuable, as Ehret says the monetarist does who does not care whether such claims are tied to nation-destroying hedge fund gambling, whore houses or productive steel mills.[1]

Real economics, on this account, is based neither on "monetary values" nor on "carbon footprints" but on the successful reproduction of society into the deepest future possible.[1] Only in this pursuit of true progress can increases in prosperity for every nation and every individual occur; such increases are measured in terms of increasing rates of the productive powers of labour per capita and per square kilometre, requiring ever greater densities of scientific creative breakthroughs in time.[1]

The associated metric is **increasing energy flux density**, which Ehret argues must be understood as the standard of value under any new fixed exchange rate system, whether or not gold serves as a reserve.[1] He describes this capacity for progress as a prerequisite for survival that empires have yearned to crush since ancient Rome, because such a society would not tolerate the fixed system of zero change demanded by empire — expressed most venomously, in his view, in [[prince-philip|Prince Philip]]'s green agenda for depopulation and war.[1] He argues that only the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait]] project offers the preconditions for war avoidance, the defeat of "green suicide" and nation building, with the effect of increasing the energy flux density of society as a whole.[1]

## Restatement in the Alaska summit essay

In "Trump, Putin, Alaska and the Future of Multipolarity," [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] restates that the science of physical economy was first developed by the American statesman [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] and is distinct from all monetarist-statistical economic theories, because LaRouche's system does not assume that all monetary claims within a system are equally valuable — unlike the monetarist, who does not care whether claims are tied to hedge fund gambling, whore houses or productive steel mills.[2] Real economics, on this account, is based neither on monetary values nor on carbon footprints but on the successful reproduction of society into the deepest future possible, measured in increasing rates of the productive powers of labour per capita and per square kilometre, requiring ever greater densities of scientific creative breakthroughs in time.[2] Ehret pairs this metric of increasing energy flux density with the restoration of [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall]], a genuine national banking system and a [[new-bretton-woods|New Bretton Woods]] fixed exchange rate system, noting that the US derivatives exposure stands at roughly $1.2 quadrillion in nominal assets.[2]
