# 2024 RFK Jr. Endorsement of Donald Trump

The 2024 RFK Jr. endorsement of Donald Trump refers to the announcement on Friday, August 23, 2024, in which [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] suspended his independent campaign for the White House and endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a late-stage shakeup of the presidential race that observers expected could give the former president a modest boost from Kennedy's supporters.[1] Hours after the announcement, Kennedy joined Trump onstage at an Arizona rally, where the crowd burst into chants of "Bobby!"[1]

## Kennedy's stated reasoning

Kennedy said his internal polling had shown that his continued presence in the race would hurt Trump and help Democratic nominee [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], though recent public polling did not provide a clear indication that he was having an outsize impact on support for either major-party candidate.[1] He cited free speech, the war in Ukraine, and "a war on our children" among the reasons he would seek to remove his name from the ballot in battleground states.[1] Speaking at his event in Phoenix, Kennedy said these were "the principal causes that persuaded me to leave the Democratic Party and run as an independent, and now to throw my support to President Trump."[1]

## Ballot mechanics

Kennedy made clear that he was not formally ending his bid, saying supporters could continue to back him in the majority of states where they were unlikely to sway the outcome.[1] He took steps to withdraw his candidacy in at least two states late that week — Arizona and Pennsylvania — but election officials in the battlegrounds of Michigan, Nevada and Wisconsin said it would be too late to remove his name from the ballot even if he wished to do so.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writers framed the endorsement as a long-anticipated convergence rather than a conventional political transaction. One contributor recalled having written about the possibility more than a year earlier in a Badlands Substack article titled "Horseshoe Theory," and described the moment as historic — speculating that future generations might identify it as the point at which "the MAGA movement transcended politics and transformed into the Great American Revolution."[1]

The accompanying commentary argued that the movement should not be measured by conversion rates to a single ideology, holding that the imposition of a homogenous ethos through coercion, manipulation, or fear is the method of the adversary rather than of the movement itself.[1] The writer contended that the remarkable element is former enemies from different walks of life setting aside differences voluntarily to oppose a mutually recognized evil, and closed with an appeal to faith in divine seeding of good men across the cultural spectrum.[1]

A second Badlands take acknowledged that Kennedy's background, connections and positions on past and present issues concerned some MAGA voters, but argued that disaffected liberals and independents were never going to swing toward Trump via a mirror-image candidate.[1] In this reading, Trump did not need those factions to win in 2016 or 2020 and did not need them in 2024 either; the endorsement was instead about unifying the American people through what the writer called "the most complex and effective PsyOp of all time," designed to undo generations of mass psychological damage attributed to the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1] Within that "War of Stories" framing, characters function as cognitive cyphers: Trump is the symbol of anti-establishment sentiment on the right and Kennedy the equivalent on the left, with the sequence summarized as "Convergence > Unification > Awakening."[1]
