# 2023 Jeddah Peace Summit

The 2023 Jeddah peace summit was the second international conference convened to discuss terms for ending the war in Ukraine, held in early August 2023 in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia.[1]

## Participants

The summit was led by Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and its notable attendees included Ukrainian president [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and United States national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], who brought an American delegation.[1] Fewer than fifty nations sent delegates and only a handful of heads of state attended; the two-day conference drew what journalist [[seymour-hersh|Seymour Hersh]] described as little international attention.[1]

Conspicuously absent was any representative of Russia, which was not invited — a fact Hersh treated as fatal to the summit's stated purpose of brokering peace.[1]

## Context and criticism

Hersh situated the summit within a broader critique of the Biden administration's foreign policy team, which he characterized as a trio of war hawks comprising Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], Sullivan, and Under Secretary [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]].[1] He noted that Mohammed bin Salman had earlier that year announced a merger between his state-backed golf tour and the PGA, and that four years prior he had been accused of ordering the assassination and dismemberment of journalist [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]] at the Saudi consulate in Istanbul.[1] Hersh concluded that the White House's "wishful approach" to the war would continue but that "the end is nearing," describing the administration's public assessments as being "out of a comic strip."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the summit to a cluster of concurrent signals: a senior NATO defense official floating a return to negotiations that NATO itself had pressured Kyiv to abandon in March 2022, and mainstream outlets conceding that the [[2023-ukrainian-counteroffensive|Ukrainian counteroffensive]] had failed.[1] He framed Hersh's intervention as the end of a chapter rather than of the story, and tied it to Hersh's earlier reporting on the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream]] and Crimean Bridge bombings.[1]
