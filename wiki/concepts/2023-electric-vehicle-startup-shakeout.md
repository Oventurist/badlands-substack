# 2023 Electric Vehicle Startup Shakeout

The 2023 electric vehicle startup shakeout refers to the collapse in valuations and cash reserves among electric-vehicle and battery companies that went public during the preceding boom years, documented in a December 2023 Wall Street Journal analysis.[1]

## Scope of the collapse

According to the Journal's review of the companies' most recent filings, at least 18 EV and battery startups that had gone public in recent years were at risk of running out of cash by the end of 2024.[1] The list included [[nikola|Nikola]] and [[fisker|Fisker]], firms that had attracted investors with bold promises to transform the industry and fight climate change with electric trucks and SUVs before stumbling amid rising costs and manufacturing problems.[1]

Three companies — [[lordstown-motors|Lordstown Motors]], Proterra and Electric Last Mile Solutions — had already filed for bankruptcy.[1] Battery maker Romeo Power and charging firm Volta were sold at a fraction of their valuations at the time they went public.[1] The median stock among the companies evaluated was down more than 80% from its market debut and further still from its peak, wiping out tens of billions of dollars in market value in only a couple of years.[1] Gavin Baker, chief investment officer at Atreides Management, called it "by far the most insane bubble I have ever seen."[1] Several surviving firms said they were working to reduce costs and had since raised capital.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the shakeout through the "rug-pull" concept common in the Truth Community, arguing that the pattern applies to narratives as well as assets.[1] He described reversing his own earlier reading of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[tesla|Tesla]]: where he once viewed Tesla as a Trojan horse for a Green New Deal agenda advanced by globalist interests, he came to see Tesla's market dominance as the force triggering a financial-crisis cascade across the green-energy space, leaving Tesla to absorb the excess.[1] He cited Musk's Department of Defense connections under the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and Tesla's status as a leading domestic producer and continual innovator in concluding that the company functions as a thorn in the side of the globalist agenda.[1]
