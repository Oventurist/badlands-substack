# Canadian Human Rights Act

The Canadian Human Rights Act is the federal statute prohibiting discrimination on enumerated grounds. A February 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] traces how the Act — together with the Criminal Code — was amended in 2016 to include gender identity and expression, converting [[gender-ideology|gender ideology]] into a national legal framework for [[canada|Canada]].[1]

## Evolution of protected grounds

The essay describes decades of legal change across Canada to safeguard same-sex couples' rights, followed by a turn in 2002 when the Northwest Territories became the first Canadian jurisdiction to specifically add "gender identity" as a forbidden basis of discrimination in its human rights legislation.[1] The author treats this as the point at which Canadian law moved beyond shielding same-sex couples from prejudice and instead entrenched the notion that sexual orientation is unimportant to the fabric of society.[1] The federal amendment followed in 2016 under [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]; see [[bill-c-16|Bill C-16]].[1]

## Definition of gender identity

The [[ontario-human-rights-commission|Ontario Human Rights Commission]] definition adopted in the surrounding policy framework describes gender identity as "each person's internal and individual experience of gender... their sense of being a woman, a man, both, neither, or anywhere along the gender spectrum," which "may be the same as or different from their birth-assigned sex" and is "fundamentally different from a person's sexual orientation."[1]

## Contested application

Under the resulting regime, the article argues, Canadians face laws that require them to identify others by their claimed gender identity or risk being accused of hate crimes — a position it says most people do not adhere to.[1] The essay also cites the case of the [[vancouver-rape-relief|Vancouver Rape Relief & Women's Shelter Society]], which was found not guilty of discrimination on freedom-of-association grounds yet lost municipal funding for failing trans equity and inclusion requirements and subsequently closed.[1]
