# Spot Bitcoin ETF Approval

The spot Bitcoin exchange-traded fund (ETF) approval refers to the United States [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]]'s authorization of exchange-traded funds holding [[bitcoin|bitcoin]] directly, a decision described in contemporaneous coverage as one of the most seismic financial developments in decades.[1]

## The premature announcement and claimed hack

On January 9, 2024, an announcement proclaiming the approval of spot Bitcoin ETFs was posted from SEC Chair [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]]'s account on [[x-corp|X]], shortly after Gensler had issued a pre-emptive warning about the risks of crypto assets.[1] Minutes later, a further post stated that the announcement was false and that the account had been compromised.[1]

X's Safety team subsequently published the results of its probe, confirming that the breach did not involve any of X's own systems and that the compromised account "did not have two-factor authentication enabled at the time the account was compromised."[1] Commentators noted that although the mainstream establishment might have preferred to blame [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] or the platform, the episode instead showed that the world's leading securities regulator had failed to enable basic two-factor authentication on an account capable of moving markets.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the incident as a demonstration of narrative control, arguing that "either the ETF approval is being rugged, or the announcement is," and that either way the affair placed maximum attention on decentralized currencies and on the administrative bodies acting as custodians of free exchange and value discovery.[1] The item was tied to the premise of [[rugpull-radio|Rugpull Radio]], hosted by GMONEY and Patriots In Progress, that most fronts of the [[information-war|Information War]] exist to shield the fiat financial system at the core of the "System of Systems."[1]

## Formal approval on January 10, 2024

Following the previous day's false start, the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] officially approved a spot Bitcoin ETF on January 10, 2024 — and bungled the release again.[2] The agency posted an "Order Granting Accelerated Approval" at a public link and then pulled the link, producing a second false start before the decision was confirmed.[2] Bloomberg ETF analyst James Seyffart observed that "it certainly looks like the #Bitcoin ETF Approval order had hit the SEC website," while his colleague Eric Balchunas summed up the saga with "It's over, thank god!"[2] Coverage also noted Democratic dissent over the approval and that Chair [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]] distanced himself from crypto he associated with terrorist financing.[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-media|Badlands]] News Brief, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] said cryptocurrencies remained one of the most controversial topics in the Truth Community because most were "money-laundering rug-pull schemes," but argued [[bitcoin|BTC]] was different as the only crypto that authorities treat as an asset.[2] He read the ETF episode chiefly as an exposure of the administrative state, noting that the SEC had not set up two-factor authentication on its X account — producing the prior day's leak — and that the Wednesday announcement was likewise premature, calling it "almost like we're watching a War of Stories."[2]
