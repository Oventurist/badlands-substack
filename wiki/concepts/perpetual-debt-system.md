# Perpetual Debt System

The **perpetual debt system** is the term used by Erik Carlson in Badlands Media for the financial arrangement he attributes to the 12th-century [[venetian-bankers|Venetian Bankers]]: a debt system in which debt never goes away, deliberately designed so that it can never be paid off.[1]

Carlson explains the mechanism through analogy. Imagine a mortgage or car loan that would never be retired: the car could be scrapped and sold for parts, and the loan would continue regardless.[1] If a borrower could not service the debt, the obligation would simply be passed on to someone else. He characterizes the arrangement as parasitic — "this debt persists like a parasite that leaves one host only to infest another. The hosts eventually die, but the parasite lives forever."[1]

## Relation to the modern financial order

In the article's framework, the perpetual debt system is the foundational technology of the control structure later exercised from the [[city-of-london|City of London]] and, in the United States, through the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1] Carlson acknowledges that many people believe the founding of the Federal Reserve in 1913 is when the financial system of control was created, and agrees it was a monumental shift bringing America and much of the world fully under a debt slavery system — the point at which US inflation was essentially birthed and currency backed by nothing began to be printed out of thin air — but insists this is not where it all started.[1]

The concept underpins the article's argument that walking away from the financial system requires a prepared alternative, since an individual or nation cut off without one faces confiscated or worthless assets, lost employment and eventual destitution.[1]
