# War on Terror

The War on Terror is the name given to the sustained American counterterrorism campaign launched after the September 11, 2001 attacks. In Badlands commentary it is treated less as a foreign military project than as the period in which domestic surveillance and informant-driven policing were normalized inside the United States.[1]

## Informant networks

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that during the War on Terror the [[fbi|FBI]] built a network of more than 15,000 informants whose primary purpose was to infiltrate Muslim communities and to create and facilitate phony terrorist plots, so that the Bureau could then claim it was winning the war on terror.[1] He described this as an escalation of tactics first developed under [[cointelpro|COINTELPRO]], in which federal institutions planted agents or acquired informants inside groups such as the Ku Klux Klan and the Black Panthers in order to steer them in ways that stoked division and supported the narratives of the day.[1]

## Continuity with the January 6 prosecutions

In the same commentary, DeLarme argued that the pattern recurred on January 6, 2021, with the FBI inserting informants and agent provocateurs into the crowd, infiltrating and manipulating groups such as the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] and the [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]], and — in his framing — creating groups like Patriot Front and Blood Tribe out of whole cloth, with the [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] kidnapping case offered as a further example.[1] The broader claim is that the security state's central message — that the greatest threat to America is one's own neighbor rather than a foreign adversary or a corrupt official — is a product of this institutional method.[1]

## Indirect death toll estimates

A May 2023 report by the [[costs-of-war-project|Costs of War Project]] at [[brown-university|Brown University]]'s Watson Institute for International and Public Affairs estimated that the post-9/11 War on Terror may have caused at least 4.5 million deaths across around half a dozen countries.[2] The report examined people killed indirectly by the wars in Afghanistan, Iraq, Libya, Pakistan, Somalia, Syria, and Yemen, arguing that "death outlives war."[2] Author and project co-director [[stephanie-savell|Stephanie Savell]] said that "in a place like Afghanistan, the pressing question is whether any death can today be considered unrelated to war," and that wars often kill far more people indirectly than in direct combat, particularly young children.[2]

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the figure to argue that the Truth Community risks losing the forest for the trees by focusing on narrative rather than actual consequences, citing the frequency with which the [[cia|CIA]] and U.S. intelligence agencies have dominated the media cycle — from alleged international sabotage operations and drip-fed disclosures about JFK and 9/11 to their alleged role in the [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate hoax]] targeting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the suppression of the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] story.[2] He concluded that the net effects of these clandestine moves against American sovereignty have "touched every man, woman and child on this earth," and that "justice is coming."[2]
