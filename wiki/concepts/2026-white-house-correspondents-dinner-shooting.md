# 2026 White House Correspondents' Dinner Shooting

The 2026 White House Correspondents' Dinner shooting was an armed attack at the Washington Hilton on April 25, 2026, during the [[white-house-correspondents-association-dinner|White House Correspondents' Association Dinner]], in which shots were fired and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was evacuated by the [[united-states-secret-service|United States Secret Service]].[1] Trump was unharmed, according to Associated Press reporting.[1]

## Course of events

The suspect, identified by AP as [[cole-thomas-allen|Cole Thomas Allen]], was reported by Reuters to have carried a shotgun, a handgun and knives, to have fired at a Secret Service agent, and to have been subdued and arrested.[1] CBS News reported, citing two law enforcement sources, that an agent was struck by a round while wearing a bulletproof vest and was expected to be all right.[1]

Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] said the suspect appeared to be targeting Trump administration officials, potentially including Trump himself.[1] Officials cited by CBS News said the suspect left writings describing a plan to target administration officials "prioritized from highest-ranking to lowest."[1]

## Response

Trump posted on Truth Social that the Secret Service and law enforcement had done a "fantastic job," and announced the dinner would be rescheduled within 30 days.[1] He also posted a photograph of the shirtless suspect.[1] The following night Trump appeared on 60 Minutes, where Badlands commentary said he "humiliated" anchor Norah O'Donnell and the network she works for.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing for the Badlands Brief, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry called the night a bizarre turn of events, said there were "many quirks and oddities" still to examine, and noted that the attacker's approach to infiltrating the event was chaotic rather than professional.[1] He highlighted that ABC had run a story about Allen in 2017 when Allen was a student, and placed that detail inside a broader Badlands thesis: that the media and the intelligence community appear to have prior familiarity with each of Trump's would-be assassins, a pattern the Brief describes as dark and disturbing.[1] The event also had a narrative-displacement effect: [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the shooting made people momentarily forget the newly announced federal indictment of the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]].[1]
