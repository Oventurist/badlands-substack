# Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?

"Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", subtitled "Trump's Safe Haven Plan," is an article published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on June 2, 2023 and written by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]. It forms part of the author's continuing series on the global financial system, following earlier installments referenced in the text as "Master and Commander" and "The Midas Touch." The piece advances a contrarian reading of [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currencies]] (CBDCs): where much of conservative and alternative media treats CBDCs as the instrument of total financial control by a globalist [[cabal]], the article argues that they are instead a defensive reaction by that cabal to technologies and geopolitical shifts it does not control, and ultimately a tool in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s plan to return the world to honest, gold-backed money.

## Premise: the end of the debt system

The article opens with the assertion that "the global financial debt slavery system is coming to an end," collapsing under a weight of debt that was never going to be repaid. The world, it says, is indebted by hundreds of trillions of dollars and has reached a point at which countries can no longer service the interest, making some form of [[great-reset|"Great Reset"]] inevitable.

Restating a thesis from earlier articles in the series, Lange describes the U.S. dollar as the cabal's "Achilles heel" and its "Keystone." The cabal established the dollar as the world's reserve currency and then struck a deal with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], the world's largest oil producer, to accept only dollars for oil — the arrangement described elsewhere in the corpus as the [[petrodollar]] system. Because central banks are compelled to hold large dollar reserves for trade and currency exchange, the dollar became tied to nearly every financial transaction on earth, allowing its controllers to dictate foreign policy and to punish non-compliant states by weaponizing the currency. The article reproduces an early [[q|Q]] post on the concept of the "Keystone," glossing the nation that dominates all others as the United States and the keystone of cabal world domination as the dollar.

Trump, the article contends, took control of the most dominant nation in the world "not just militarily, but financially." Referring to his previous article, Lange restates the claim that Trump controls the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] and the dollar and is using them to bankrupt the global debt system, on the logic that "you have to collapse the system if you want to replace it." The framing question of the piece is therefore not whether a Great Reset will occur but whose reset will prevail: Trump's or the cabal's, financial freedom or total control.

## Part one: the global drivers of CBDCs

The article's first section asks what is actually driving the worldwide push toward digital currency. The conventional fear narrative holds that central banks are building digital money as part of a grand plan to monitor and control every transaction. Lange counters that digital currency rests on blockchain technology, a distributed and decentralized ledger — "the opposite of what the cabal wants, and clearly not their plan."

To define the underlying technology the article quotes Investopedia at length on distributed ledgers: a database "consensually shared and synchronized across multiple sites, institutions, or geographies," allowing transactions to have public witnesses, with each node able to hold an identical copy and all changes propagated within seconds or minutes. This is contrasted with the centralized ledger used by most companies, which is described as more prone to cyber attack and fraud because it has a single point of failure. Investopedia also notes that blockchain is a type of distributed ledger, the type used by bitcoin.

From this the article poses a rhetorical question: what came first, Bitcoin or CBDC? Bitcoin, it argues, forced central banks to react and to compete with innovations arising from cryptocurrencies and fintech companies; the rush to build CBDCs is a product of competition, not of a long-laid scheme. Lange states he does not believe the timing of Bitcoin and blockchain was coincidental, describing them as part of the plan to bring down the cabal.

The accelerant, according to the article, is [[brics|BRICS+]]. The BRICS nations are said to have already built a separate, parallel global financial system independent of the dollar, transacting with one another in their own currencies. Quoting The Cradle, the article reports that Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Algeria, Egypt, Bahrain, and Iran had formally asked to join the bloc ahead of its annual summit in South Africa, with nineteen nations in total expressing interest, according to Anil Sooklal, South Africa's ambassador to the group. Citing Bloomberg analysis, the same passage notes that while the G7 and BRICS contributed equally to global growth in 2020, the Western-led bloc's performance had declined, and that by 2028 the G7 is expected to constitute 27.8 percent of the global economy against 35 percent for BRICS. It further records that Alexander Babakov, Deputy Chairman of Russia's State Duma, had revealed BRICS was developing a "new currency" to be presented at the summit — a summit scheduled for that August, at which, the article says, the bloc planned to ditch the dollar in favor of a currency backed by gold and other commodities.

[[china|China]] is credited with starting the movement by purchasing oil in yuan convertible immediately into gold, through the Shanghai Petroleum and Natural Gas Exchange opened in March 2018 and the Shanghai Gold Exchange. The BRICS, Lange writes, "have ripped control of the world's oil supply away from the cabal."

[[russia|Russia]] is described as leading the move to a gold-backed currency. Quoting Ledger Insights, the article reports that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], speaking at a Sberbank conference on artificial intelligence, pushed back against international sanctions by proposing a digital currency payment system for cross-border transactions independent of banks and similar to hawala. Putin is quoted saying that "in today's illegitimate restrictions, one of the lines of attack is settlements," that the international payment system is expensive and its correspondent accounts and regulation "controlled by a narrow club of states and financial groups… In fact, they are the masters of life," and that "based on digital currency technologies and distributed registries, it is possible to create a new system of international payments, and much more convenient. But at the same time completely safe for participants and completely independent of banks and third-party interference." Lange treats this as Putin spelling out what is coming, and as evidence that the cabal is reacting rather than leading.

## Part two: the advantages of digital currency

The second section addresses what the article calls fear-mongering about CBDCs, in particular the assumption that cash will be abolished — an outcome Lange rejects. Drawing on his previous article about the return to the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] and states making gold and silver coins legal tender, he argues that paper and digital money alike will be gold-backed and "redeemable," meaning cash survives.

Quoting Forbes, the article lists the benefits of digital currency: faster payments compared with ACH or wire transfers, which can take days to confirm; cheaper international transfers, since cross-border transactions and currency conversions currently carry high fees; 24/7 access, unconstrained by bank weekends and holidays; support for the unbanked and underbanked, with the FDIC's 2019 survey cited for the figure of more than seven million American households without a bank account who pay costly fees to cash checks and send money orders; and more efficient government payments, allowing tax refunds, child benefits, and food stamps to be delivered instantly rather than by mailed check or prepaid debit card.

Lange then draws a distinction between electronic money and digital money. Under the Automated Clearing House (ACH) network, transfers pass through a third party he describes as cabal-controlled, take several days to become official, and can be cancelled before settlement; the same apparatus, he writes, has the power to freeze accounts and credit cards. Digital currency on a blockchain is peer-to-peer and near-instant, eliminating that third-party control and, in his framing, expanding individual freedom. He identifies FedNow as an important component of the plan — not a digital currency but a peer-to-peer payment platform built by the Federal Reserve, accessible to every financial service in the country and eliminating intermediary fees. Digital dollars, he adds, would trade around the clock, remove bank and credit card fees, and reduce the fraudulent government payments he says the cabal deliberately built into the system.

## Part three: CBDC as the path to freedom

The final section sets out why the author considers CBDCs essential to averting catastrophe. Financial commentators he follows, he writes, uniformly expect a crash larger than the 2008 credit crisis and worse than the Depression, because global debt is far greater than in the 1930s and the world financial system has been centralized around the dollar, so the crash will be global rather than regional.

Three developments, he says, have clarified how the transition can be survived: the BRICS construction of a parallel financial system outside cabal control; the worldwide preparation, including in the United States, for a return to a gold standard and honest money; and CBDC as "the path to freedom." Blockchain settlement in minutes rather than days or weeks is presented as the technology needed to move populations into a new gold-backed system quickly enough to limit the duration of suffering. The article notes that Trump has never attacked CBDCs, in contrast to right-wing media, governors, and members of [[congress]], and treats this silence as significant. It restates the claim that Trump retains control of the [[treasury-department|Treasury]] and the Federal Reserve through [[devolution]] and [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] plans, and refers to an earlier article on rolling the Federal Reserve into the Treasury.

The article then identifies the figures it says are behind the American CBDC push. The first is jared kushner, Trump's son-in-law and senior adviser. Citing CNBC, it reports that a 250-page release of Treasury Secretary Steven Mnuchin's crypto-related email correspondence — obtained by CoinDesk through a Freedom of Information Act request — shows that in 2019, during a multi-year crypto bear market, Kushner was quietly advocating a U.S. digital dollar issued by the Fed. The article quotes Kushner's email: "Steven – Would you be open to me bringing a small group of people to have a brainstorm about this topic?" and "My sense is it could make sense, and also be something that could ultimately change the way we pay out entitlements as well saving us a ton in waste fraud and also in transaction costs…" CNBC judged Kushner's position prescient, noting that whereas few countries were seriously exploring national digital currencies in mid-2019, at least 87 countries representing over 90 percent of global GDP were doing so by the time of writing, according to research from the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]]'s CBDC tracker.

The second figure is J. Christopher Giancarlo. The same CNBC report describes Giancarlo, as former chairman of the CFTC, among the "crypto progressive voices" in Mnuchin's ear, with a July 2018 email exchange showing him pressing through his executive assistant for an in-person meeting with the Treasury Secretary. Giancarlo told CNBC he could not recall the specific request but said that throughout 2018 he "routinely apprised Secretary Mnuchin of ongoing operation and supervision of the Bitcoin Futures market launched under CFTC oversight in December 2017." Quoting Wikipedia, the article records that Giancarlo served as acting CFTC chair from Trump's inauguration on January 20, 2017, was nominated as full-time chair on March 14, 2017, and was confirmed by the Senate on August 3, 2017; that he is a blockchain advocate; and that he founded the Digital Dollar Project and formerly directed BlockFi. His congressional testimony earned him the nickname "Crypto Dad," also the title of his book.

A Forbes interview is quoted at length. Asked what he learned researching the book, Giancarlo answered: "money is too important to be left to central bankers," invoking Georges Clemenceau's remark at the end of World War I that "war is too important to be left to the generals." He argued that a free society has much to gain from the modernization of finance that crypto could bring but much to lose if it has no say in the values encoded into the future of money, whether non-sovereign money such as Diem or sovereign money in the form of a digital dollar — values of "free enterprise, free economic activity, individual privacy, free of censorship by governments, for legal activities." Lange presents these values as what Trump is fighting for behind the scenes.

## The constitutional strategy

The article maintains that digital currency cannot be stopped, since the whole world is digitizing data of every kind and laggards will be left behind; the only real question is what values the digital dollar embodies. Asked whether a CBDC is merely a digital [[fiat-currency|fiat]] dollar, Lange answers yes — unless it is backed by gold, which he says the Constitution requires.

He treats the state-level bans on CBDCs and congressional opposition not as setbacks but as deliberate elements of the plan, engineering a court fight between the Federal Reserve and the states over both the definition of money and the constitutionality of the central bank itself. Trump, he argues, prioritized flipping the courts precisely to obtain precedent-setting decisions from the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], which would revisit the Legal Tender Cases and the Gold Clause decisions and ultimately rule on whether the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve Act of 1913]] and an all-powerful central bank are constitutional. The goal is described as a digital dollar backed by gold, redeemable, and protective of constitutional rights.

## Conclusion

Lange calls the overall plan to free the world from what he terms the Rothschilds-controlled debt system "brilliant." The fiat system is being bankrupted; the BRICS have built a replacement; most countries have repatriated gold and are buying more to back their currencies; and nearly a hundred countries are developing CBDCs. Blockchain, he concludes, will allow a much faster transformation from a fiat system into an honest money system while forcing governments and banks into honest, easily audited bookkeeping on a decentralized ledger. The article ends on the note that this represents "financial freedom for the first time in our lives," followed by the standard Badlands Media disclaimer that contributors' articles represent the opinions of their authors, and a pointer to more of Lange's work on his own Substack.
