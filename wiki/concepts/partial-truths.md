# Partial Truths

"Partial truths" (or half-truths) is the central epistemological concept of Erik Carlson's essay *A Journey of Ever-changing Truths*, which distinguishes between truths, lies, and statements that are technically accurate but stripped of the context required to understand them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Argument

Carlson argues that while people sometimes mistake outright lies for truth, they more often mistake partial truths for complete truth. Once a partial truth appears to answer the question a person was asking, the search for further information stops — making the dissemination of partial truths an effective method of keeping curious minds from reaching the full picture.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

He illustrates the role of missing context with a hypothetical: the statement that a man shot and killed another man in his home reads as cold-blooded murder, but the added fact that the deceased had broken in and was holding a knife to the shooter's wife's throat transforms the killer into a hero and the deceased into someone who invited his own death. The underlying physical facts are unchanged; only the context differs.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Audience segmentation

The essay claims the media makes a living pushing half-truths, and that different audiences receive different products: roughly 80% of Americans, in Carlson's estimate, will accept whatever they are told by a well-groomed news anchor or by a person in a white lab coat with a stethoscope, while the remaining 20% who question what they are told are served purpose-built half-truths — statements that may be literally true but lack the details and context that would give the full story.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## The onion metaphor

Finding the truth is likened to peeling an onion, where the dry outer layer differs greatly from the layers within. Carlson describes his own research as a sequence of revisions: some inquiries led directly to full truths, some rabbit holes ended at half-truths he mistook for complete answers, and in other cases continued investigation revealed that what he had accepted as complete was itself partial.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) The essay applies this method to the [[rodney-king|Rodney King]] beating, organized crime, and the structure of the [[deep-state-cabal|Deep State Cabal]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)
