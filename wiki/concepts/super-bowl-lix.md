# Super Bowl LIX

Super Bowl LIX was the National Football League championship game played in New Orleans, Louisiana on Sunday, February 9, 2025, before an estimated 73,000 attendees.[1]

## Security posture

Authorities implemented what was described as an unprecedented and comprehensive security strategy for the game, driven in large part by the attendance of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — the first time a sitting U.S. president attended a Super Bowl in person.[1] Previous presidents, such as Ronald Reagan, had performed the pre-game coin toss remotely from the White House, but none had attended.[1] Secret Service spokesman Anthony Guglielmi said security measures had been "further enhanced this year" specifically because of the presidential visit.[1]

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] noted a wave of "see something, say something" messaging in the week before the game from Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and others, and pointed to an ESPN article he described as "weirdly placed and oddly worded" about how safe the game would be.[1]

## The Sum of All Fears pattern

Fleuret drew a series of parallels to the film *The Sum of All Fears*, in which a nuclear device detonates at a Super Bowl attended by the president.[1] He observed that the film's game is set in Baltimore but depicted in a domed stadium, which Baltimore lacks and New Orleans has; that a Baltimore Ravens game three weeks earlier had been briefly stopped when a drone flew over the stadium bowl; and that the previous Super Bowl held in New Orleans featured a mid-game power outage.[1] He also cited "ISIS" stories planted the day after the New Orleans truck-ramming attack, and Trump's use of "Nessun Dorma" — the aria played at the end of the film — during the 2024 Republican National Convention.[1] His conclusion was that a scare event was possible but the day would probably end up being just a football game.[1]

Fleuret additionally remarked on the selection of [[kendrick-lamar|Kendrick Lamar]] as halftime performer, speculating about whether he would play "Not Like Us," a song whose later verses attack child rapists.[1]

## Officiating controversy

The officiating crew was described as a "third team" under scrutiny in Super Bowl 2025.[2] After a postseason in which the perception grew that the NFL wanted officials to call games in favor of the Kansas City Chiefs, the issue did not dominate the game itself; the refs made some questionable calls, but they went both ways and the lopsided score rendered them inconsequential in the 40–22 Philadelphia Eagles win.[2] Chiefs wide receiver [[deandre-hopkins|DeAndre Hopkins]] pushed back on the media narrative afterward, asking, "I saw a lot of things in the media about the refs, but what are you all going to say now about the refs and us? There was a lot of touchy calls. Are you going to report that? Are you going to talk about the refs now?"[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued the sequence was a legitimacy operation, comparing it to what he called the Regime's handling of the 2024 election: run national media stories for weeks alleging the [[nfl|NFL]] had rigged games for Kansas City, have Kansas City lose, then run follow-up stories like the Hopkins piece.[2]
