# Mexican Drug Cartels

Mexico's drug-smuggling cartels are transnational criminal organizations whose scale, according to research published in late 2024, rivals that of the country's largest corporate employers.[1]

## Scale as an employer

A study led by researcher Rafael Prieto-Curiel estimated that some 175,000 people actively work for Mexico's smuggling cartels, a figure that would make them the country's fifth-largest private employer.[1] Prieto-Curiel described recruiting as "the secret of the success of a cartel," calling the cartels Mexico's top recruiter at more than 350 new people each week — a rate necessary to offset heavy losses from arrests, killings and dropouts.[1]

"Cartels, they need to have roughly 175,000 members," he said. "They cannot be much smaller because they would have collapsed. They cannot be much bigger because they would have grown so fast."[1] He and his fellow researchers used computer models to study the notoriously secretive organizations, running millions of permutations across roughly 150 different cartels and evaluating their recruitment against attrition.[1]

## Relationship to US immigration enforcement

The cartel workforce estimate circulated alongside coverage of American debates over [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary cities]] and anticipated mass deportations under the incoming Trump administration.[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied cartel-linked criminality to conditions in Denver and Aurora, Colorado, citing home invasions, jewelry-store and gun-shop thefts, stolen cars, apartment-complex takeovers, kidnappings and torture — including an Aurora resident whose fingernails were pulled out by Tren de Aragua kidnappers.[1]
