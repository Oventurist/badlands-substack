# Zone of Proximal Development

The **zone of proximal development** is, in the account given by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in "Parenting For Freedom" (May 12, 2023), the term for the ideal balance between a child's learned skills and their unlearned skills.[1] Deschamps argues that a child's success later in life — which he treats as centered above all on good learning — is directly dependent on how well this zone is managed.[1]

## Real learning versus memorization

In this framing, real learning is distinguished from what Deschamps calls ineffectual memorization. Genuine learning requires personal investment, authentic curiosity, a willingness to explore all information about a subject, and a drive to act on what was learned in order to entrench it into oneself.[1] The zone of proximal development is greatly assisted by parents and other child-helpers who encourage the child to step out of their comfort zone to learn something new and try it out.[1]

## Consequences of mismanagement

Where the parental figure and community support is absent, toxic, or simply not challenging enough, the child never develops the ability to seek out new knowledge, grasp it once encountered, and apply it effectively to improve their life.[1] Such children, Deschamps writes, go on to become incompetent, emotionally disturbed adults who are easily led by manipulative minds and easily swayed by groupthink into following the herd — in his phrasing, "the perfect person to act as a cog in the societal machines of tyranny."[1]

## Relationship to hormesis

Deschamps presents the zone of proximal development as the psychological equivalent of the hormetic effect described under [[hormesis]].[1] He compares it to a weight trainer who "damages" their muscles through training so that they heal stronger; when a person learns just beyond their current abilities, the challenge strengthens them and activates potentials that would otherwise have remained dormant.[1]

The concept is embedded in the article's broader argument about the [[nuclear-family]], the necessity of a [[role-model|role model]], and the wider [[war-on-the-family|war on the family]].[1]
