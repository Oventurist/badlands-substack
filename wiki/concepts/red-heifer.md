# Red Heifer

The red heifer is a ritual sacrifice in Jewish tradition whose ashes are held to be necessary for the ritual purification that would allow a [[third-temple|third temple]] to be built in Jerusalem.[1] The animal must be perfectly red and blemish-free, without a stray white or black hair.[1]

## The 2024 ritual practice

In August 2024 a group of religious Israelis were pictured practising the ritual of the red heifer, which is meant to herald the building of a new Jewish temple on the site of the [[al-aqsa-mosque|Al-Aqsa Mosque]].[1] Radical Jewish groups hold that the temple must be constructed on the raised plateau in Jerusalem's Old City known as the Temple Mount, where Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock shrine stand today, and some believe the event will herald the arrival of the messiah and possibly even the end of the world.[1] Journalist Yinon Magal posted that "Temple worshipers are now practising the mitzvah [religious duty] of a red cow in front of the Temple Mount, which will enable the return of purity and the observance of all the temple mitzvahs," alongside a picture of activists from the [[temple-institute|Temple Institute]].[1]

## The Texas heifers

In 2022 five red heifers arrived in Israel from a Texas ranch and were kept in an archaeological park next to Shilo, an Israeli settlement near the Palestinian city of Nablus.[1] The Temple Institute imported the animals after years of searching for suitable cows, intending them for eventual use in the purification ritual.[1] Their slaughter on the Mount of Olives would, according to advocates, allow Jewish people to be purified so they could perform rites and worship on the site of Al-Aqsa Mosque.[1]

## Kabbalistic significance

In Badlands commentary, the red heifer is bound up with [[kabbalah|Practical Kabbalah]]: the 16th-century Rabbi Isaac Luria taught that pursuing Practical Kabbalah without the Third Temple constructed, or without the ashes of burned red heifers, could result in something highly detrimental such as the unwitting summoning of demons, which led to the practice being prohibited among commoners.[1]
