# Biden Pseudonym Emails

The Biden pseudonym email controversy concerns records showing that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] used at least three pseudonymous email accounts during his tenure as vice president.[1]

The [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] disclosed that it holds approximately 5,400 records containing such pseudonymous email addresses.[1] The figure emerged in a letter from the Archives to the [[southeastern-legal-foundation|Southeastern Legal Foundation]], which had filed a Freedom of Information Act request in June 2022 for documents referencing three accounts: robinware456@gmail.com, JRBWare@gmail.com, and Robert.L.Peters@pci.gov.[1] NARA stated it had identified roughly 5,138 email messages, 25 electronic files, and 200 pages of potentially responsive records requiring processing before the request could be answered.[1] The disclosure was reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and aggregated as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## Congressional demand for unredacted records

In August 2023, House Republican investigators asked the National Archives to hand over any unredacted records in which the then-Vice President used a pseudonym.[2] Emails previously retrieved from [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s abandoned laptop had revealed Biden's use of the Robert.L.Peters@pci.gov address while serving as Vice President of the United States.[2]

The records further showed that Biden aide John Flynn copied Hunter on ten emails containing Joe Biden's daily schedule between May 19 and June 15, 2016.[2] One of those emails detailed plans for a telephone call with Ukraine's then-president [[petro-poroshenko|Petro Poroshenko]].[2] Flynn copied Hunter at his address at [[rosemont-seneca-partners|Rosemont Seneca Partners]] while Hunter was serving on the board of the Ukrainian energy company [[burisma|Burisma]], an entity deemed corrupt by the Obama-Biden [[state-department|State Department]].[2]

[[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] wrote that "The Committee's need for these Vice-Presidential records is specific and well-documented," and that the Committee sought "to craft legislative solutions aimed at deficiencies it has identified in the current legal framework regarding ethics laws and disclosure of financial interests related to the immediate family members of Vice Presidents and Presidents — deficiencies that may place American national security and interests at risk."[2]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the real story was that a president who insisted he had never spoken with his son about overseas business dealings was copying that son on official United States foreign policy communications, and that journalists should instead have asked how often Hunter was engaged in Joe Biden's official vice-presidential role.[2] She also noted that the revelation was not new: [[marco-polo|Marco Polo]] had published Joe Biden's alias on page 159 of its Biden Laptop report released in October 2022, a document circulated to every House Republican multiple times.[2]
