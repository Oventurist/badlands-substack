# Gaetz Syria Troop Withdrawal Resolution (2023)

The Gaetz Syria troop withdrawal resolution was a March 2023 war powers measure that would have compelled the removal of U.S. forces from Syria within six months. It failed in the House of Representatives on Wednesday, March 8, 2023, by a vote of 103–321.[1]

## Sponsorship and vote

The resolution was sponsored by Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] (R-Fla.) and emphatically backed by several more conservative lawmakers, including Rep. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] (R-Ga.) and Rep. [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] (R-Ariz.).[1] The coalition supporting it was cross-partisan but small: 56 Democrats and 47 Republicans voted yes, while 150 Democrats and 171 Republicans voted no.[1]

## Underlying deployment

Roughly 900 U.S. troops remained in Syria at the time, conducting operations to counter [[isis|ISIS]].[1] Although the U.S.-designated terrorist group had lost much of its territory, it retained a presence in Syria and maintained sleeper cells.[1]

On the House floor, Gaetz argued that American troops in Syria were trapped in a "hellscape" of war and meddling by various foreign nations, and that American counter-terrorism operations in the country had no end in sight.[1] He further argued that ISIS forces in Syria did not represent a serious threat to the United States and that the soldiers should therefore be withdrawn.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that Gaetz had "once again exposed those in our Congress who are beholden to the establishment, specifically the war machine," and urged readers to record the names of members who voted against withdrawal.[1] He characterized the deployment as an "endless war cash grab" that lawmakers of both parties could agree on, contrasting it with unmet domestic priorities such as housing homeless veterans and repairing a defective rail system.[1] DeLarme speculated that keeping the soldiers deployed might be less about Syria itself than about positioning for "bigger cash-generating conflicts down the line," citing [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s remark that "American sons and daughters will have to join the fight soon," and argued that troops should instead be brought home to patrol the border and American streets.[1]
