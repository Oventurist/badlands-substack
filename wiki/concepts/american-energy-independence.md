# American Energy Independence

**American energy independence** refers to the condition in which the United States is not reliant on foreign suppliers for its energy needs. It is the baseline against which Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] measures subsequent policy in his May 2023 essay on [[energy-consolidation|energy consolidation]].[1]

## Claimed achievement under Trump

Carlson argues that when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] left office the country could arguably be called energy independent: the United States might still have imported energy from other countries, but it was not dependent on them.[1] He describes this as a substantial achievement whose historical precedent, if any, he is unsure of.[1] Two supporting details are offered: gasoline cost $2.38 a gallon the day Trump left office, and the depleted national reserves had been refilled "to the tippy top" without consumers suffering at the pump to accomplish it.[1]

## Reversal under Biden

According to Carlson, one of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s first acts — on his literal first day in office — was to suspend oil and gas leasing in order to curb carbon emissions, making the country energy dependent again.[1] The stated justification was fighting climate change, with, in his account, no apparent concern for the good jobs lost.[1] He contends the real motive was financial benefit to the Biden family, its allies, and what he calls their puppet masters.[1] Gasoline prices, he writes, immediately and continuously rose, and a year later Biden attributed the increases to Vladimir Putin's invasion of Ukraine even though the invasion post-dated the price rises by a year.[1]

Carlson highlights a further irony: after the policy shift, a substantial portion of US energy imports came from Russia, while Biden simultaneously presented himself as tough on Russia and Trump as Putin's puppet.[1] In Carlson's framing, "apparently not buying gas or oil from Russia makes you their puppet, but buying from them makes you 'tough on them'."[1]

## Relation to consolidation

Energy independence matters to the essay's central thesis because dependence and consolidation both reduce optionality.[1] Carlson's argument is that having multiple energy sources — domestic and diverse — is what prevents a single actor from controlling access, and that policies presented as climate measures systematically remove that redundancy.[1]
