# The New Climate Denial Report

"The New Climate Denial" is a study released on January 16, 2024, by the [[center-for-countering-digital-hate|Center for Countering Digital Hate]] (CCDH) documenting rising skepticism toward climate alarmism among young audiences and calling on technology platforms to censor content that contradicts what the group calls the authoritative scientific consensus.[1]

## Findings

The study reported a significant increase between 2018 and 2023 in YouTube content expressing one of three viewpoints: that "climate solutions won't work," that "climate science and the climate movement are unreliable," or that "the impacts of global warming are beneficial or harmless."[1]

Researchers also found that roughly one-third of teenagers — the predominant YouTube audience — hold views such as that "climate policies cause more harm than good" or that climate change is "a hoax to control and oppress people." CCDH described the findings as alarming and "startling," reflecting a growing rejection of narratives emphasizing imminent global catastrophe.[1]

## Censorship recommendations

CCDH characterized the trend as troubling and urged large technology platforms such as YouTube to censor content contradicting the scientific consensus on climate change. The organization is a left-leaning group that was sued by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] for allegedly engaging in a "scare campaign" to drive advertisers away from his platform.[1]

## Reception and commentary

Skeptics of climate alarmism, and those who view climate activism as a vehicle for political control or as a secular religion sometimes labeled the "climate cult," were expected to treat the findings as welcome news rather than a crisis.[1]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that hysteria about young people is overblown, contending that in 2024 "sticking it to the man" means appealing to reason and logic over emotion, which ultimately leads to a rejection of global communism. She noted that this generation has greater access to information than any preceding it.[1]

Ashe argued the calls for censorship would backfire, since the targets are not fringe voices but "Nobel laureates, theoretical physicists, meteorologists, professors, and environmental scientists from across the world." Her stated concern was not that young people would become communists but that they would be "blackpilled" — recognizing that the system is overtly corrupted and that authorities lied to them in their formative years, and directing their frustration at the masses who play along. Her prescription was to give them something to hope in: "America First."[1]
