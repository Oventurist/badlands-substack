# 2020 George Floyd Riots

The riots that spread across the United States in 2020 following the death of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] caused, over the course of eight weeks, some $2 billion in damage and 29 deaths.[1]

Badlands Media attributes the destruction to Soros-funded radicals and reads the episode as a deliberate replay of the race riots that nearly tore the nation apart after the murder of [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] in 1968.[1] The comparison is the article's organising device: 1968 and 2020 are treated as the same operation run twice, with a nonviolent civil rights tradition displaced by funded and directed street violence.[1]

The 2020 unrest is then set against 2025, when the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]] again awoke the spectre of social chaos, this time amidst a more advanced economic collapse of the trans-Atlantic bubble economy.[1] Race riots had not yet begun in that later period, but Badlands argues the fissures between the radical left and radical right had never been greater.[1]

## Geographic distribution and mass hysteria (January 2023 analysis)

A January 20, 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used the riots as a case study in [[mass-psychosis]], arguing that the death of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] in May 2020 and the resulting media-fueled public rage did not produce much harm in smaller towns and rural areas.[2] Damage instead concentrated in large cities such as New York, Chicago, Atlanta and Los Angeles, which the essay characterized as the areas with the least respect for individual rights and the most socialist ideology; in small towns where people know their neighbors, there was significantly less property damage because social conditions made harming others more costly.[2]

The essay attributed the outrage to self-reinforcing negative information from a presumably authoritative source: the belief that Floyd died because of systemic racism, reinforced by agreeable social media and echoed uniformly by CNN, NBC and the New York Times.[2] Authoritative sourcing, in this account, acts as though permission for violence has been granted, normalizing and encouraging participation in the hysteria.[2] Deschamps further argued that the collective rage toward "the system" and police felt by believers in the [[black-lives-matter|BLM]] account was "relieved" by protests and rioting, a response promoted in the media and by social media influencers, and that like the COVID hysteria it was fostered by authorities encouraging group participation in a harmful social trend.[2]
