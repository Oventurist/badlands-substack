# Narrative Warfare

**Narrative Warfare** is one of the core analytical frames used by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators, referring to conflict waged through competing stories and information operations rather than — or alongside — kinetic force.[1] It was named as one of three central subjects of the twelfth episode of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]], "The Fake and Gay, but Also Real and Serious War," published 28 March 2026.[1]

## Treatment in The Blitz Episode 12

In that episode, hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the contradictory public accounts of the Iranian Ayatollah — characterised in the episode's framing as "fake and gay, but also real and possibly not gay," and, per [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], "actually probably gay, according to what he's been told" — as a case study.[1] The episode's premise is that a story can be simultaneously a manufactured psychological operation and a real, serious event with real consequences, and that distinguishing the layers is the practical work of narrative warfare analysis.[1]

The hosts presented the discussion as a corrective for a world, and specifically a [[truth-community|Truth Community]], that they argue has "quite forgotten" narrative warfare along with [[sovereign-disentanglement|sovereign disentanglement]] and [[first-principles|first principles]].[1]

## The June 2025 ceasefire F-bomb

Badlands contributors treated President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s profane outburst about [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]] on June 24, 2025 as a textbook narrative-warfare manoeuvre rather than a lapse.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], who calls Trump the "Master of Narrative Warfare," argued that for those who believe Trump engages more on the narrative battlespace than the kinetic one, "the actuals are often second-order effects on the back of the narratives the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] wants to tell and sell."[2] In his reading, Trump reset the battlespace by forcing all parties to negotiate in public, "dropped the mask" by dropping the F-bomb, and "didn't destroy nuclear weaponry — he destroyed a nuclear narrative," leaving hawks to either capitulate or escalate in full view of what he termed the Collective Mind.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] made the mechanical point that the F-bomb at the end of the clip guaranteed virality.[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] framed the episode as evidence that "the fake kinetic war is the fully-visible cover story for the very real information war."[2]

## Definition and mechanics (Deschamps, January 2024)

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] defined narrative warfare as the use of storytelling and information to shape perceptions, influence opinions, and gain advantage in conflict — "a battle of narratives, where different sides try to tell their version of the story to sway public opinion or achieve strategic goals."[3] In an interview on the Mel K show, General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] said that ongoing attempts to assassinate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] are being conducted not by killing him as with JFK but by means of narrative warfare, because killing a person's character using the tools of [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] is far simpler and more efficient than a real assassination.[3]

Deschamps traced a continuous chain of narratives constructed since 2016 to undermine Trump's reputation, from the Russia probe to claims that he labeled all Mexicans rapists and killers to assertions that he would destroy America and democracy itself.[3] He argued the campaign has failed: after seven or eight years of relentless effort by the largest news media organizations in conjunction with big tech, support for Trump increased — including among the minority groups he was accused of despising — while audiences for the outlets attacking him declined.[3] He cited the [[twitter-files|Twitter files]] and the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] suppression as evidence of a real plot to use big tech to rig the election between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Trump, and earlier [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]] leaks in which [[facebook|Facebook]] whistleblowers said staff had been told it was acceptable to target straight white men and to treat Make America Great Again supporters like white supremacists and Nazis, and that left-wing users could threaten Trump supporters with death.[3]

## Function within asymmetric operations

In Deschamps's framework, narrative warfare is the concealing layer of [[asymmetric-warfare|asymmetric warfare]]: it matters less that anti-Trump messaging grew his following, because the operation only requires that a small percentage of the population remain radicalized and that their voices be amplified by media and big-tech echo chambers.[3] He wrote that asymmetric operations "rely on narrative warfare to hide the asymmetry," and that after the 2020 election the media had to continue its psyops to keep extremist voices in the forefront in order to sustain narratives refuting [[2020-election-fraud-claims|electoral fraud claims]].[3]

Applied to the culture war, he argued that the story of the LGBTQ community targeting children radicalizes conservatives against progressives while erasing groups such as [[gays-against-groomers|Gays Against Groomers]], [[lesbians-united|Lesbians United]] and the [[lgb-alliance|LGB Alliance]], and that the mirror narrative of conservatives committing "trans genocide" performs the same function against the right.[3] He described the [[deep-state|Deep State]] hybridization of narrative and asymmetric warfare as exploiting emotional states on both sides and the cognitive capacity each loses under intense emotional stimuli.[3] Rejecting narrative warfare, which appeals to emotion at the expense of critical thinking, was presented as the only route back to authentic political identity and genuine debate.[3]

## Canadian case study

Deschamps presented [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]'s government as a model of narrative warfare fused with asymmetric operations, funding the [[canadian-anti-hate-network|Canadian Anti-Hate Network]] to produce "Anti-Hate Kits" while government-financed outlets including the [[canadian-broadcasting-corporation|CBC]], Global and CTV position the network as an authority that justifies harsh policy.[3] Because he is not speaking to the majority, Deschamps argued, Trudeau can make blatantly false claims and ignore hostile international press while keeping his base radicalized and framing majority opposition as a "fringe minority."[3]
