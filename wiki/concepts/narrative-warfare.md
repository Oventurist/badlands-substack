# Narrative Warfare

**Narrative Warfare** is one of the core analytical frames used across [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary. It denotes conflict waged primarily through the construction, promotion and collapse of stories about events, rather than through the events themselves. In this framing, the decisive battlefield of a modern conflict is the interpretation the public accepts, and combatants therefore invest in shaping perception with at least as much effort as they invest in kinetic operations.

## Treatment in the Badlands corpus

The concept is closely bound to the outlet's broader model of [[5th-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], in which attribution is deliberately obscured and the audience is itself a target. In Badlands Blitz Episode 12, hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] present narrative warfare as the first of three subjects — alongside [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] and [[first-principles|First Principles]] — that they argue the wider world and the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] alike have "quite forgotten."

The episode's illustration of the principle is the set of mutually contradictory claims circulating about the Iranian Supreme Leader, [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]], during the conflict involving [[iran|Iran]]: the story is described as simultaneously "fake and gay" — the community's shorthand for a fabricated or staged event — and "real and serious," with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] cited as relaying secondhand accounts. The point drawn from the example is that a narrative can be manufactured in its presentation while the underlying war remains materially consequential, and that treating the two as a single question is precisely the error narrative warfare exploits.
