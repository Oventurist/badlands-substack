# Medieval Warm Period

The medieval warming period is the interval from roughly 1000 to 1350 A.D. during which, according to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s June 5, 2025 Badlands Media essay, global mean temperatures were significantly warmer than anything currently being experienced.[1] Ehret presents it as one of several anomalies in the temperature record that undercut the claim that [[carbon-dioxide|carbon dioxide]] drives climate.[1]

The article's central illustration is that the Vikings in Greenland "had no coal plants or SUVs," and yet mean temperatures were still warmer than today by a long shot — a fact that demands explanation from any theory attributing warming to industrial emissions.[1] Ehret charges that [[michael-mann|Michael Mann]], with the assistance of East Anglia's [[phil-jones|Phil Jones]], attempted to erase this warm period from the record via the "hockey stick" model.[1]

Alongside the medieval warm period, the essay lists the absence of warming during the intensive industrial growth of 1940–1977 — when many scientists warned instead of an impending ice age — and the [[global-warming-pause|global warming pause]] of 1998–2012 as further anomalies.[1]
