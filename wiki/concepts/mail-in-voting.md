# Mail-In Voting

Mail-in voting is the practice of casting ballots by post rather than in person, a persistent focus of election-integrity disputes in American politics and the core subject of the 2026 litigation over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order on election administration.[1]

## 2026 executive order litigation

Trump's order sought to impose new federal election requirements, including proof-of-citizenship measures and restrictions affecting mail voting.[1] Among its provisions, it instructed the [[united-states-postal-service|US Postal Service]] to establish new standards for mail-in ballots, directed the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to compile lists of confirmed US citizens eligible to vote and provide them to states, and directed the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to prioritize investigations into state and local election officials who issue federal ballots to people deemed ineligible.[1] A federal appeals court blocked key portions of the order in 23 Democrat-led states in July 2026, keeping Judge [[indira-talwani|Talwani]]'s injunction intact pending litigation and setting up a probable [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] fight whose resolution could land close to the midterm elections.[1]
