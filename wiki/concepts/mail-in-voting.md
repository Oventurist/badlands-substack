# Mail-In Voting

Mail-in voting is the practice of casting a ballot by post rather than in person at a polling place. It ranges from traditional absentee balloting on request to universal systems in which election officials mail ballots to every registered voter without a request. In the Badlands Media corpus, mail-in voting is the central contested mechanism in the broader argument that American elections are systemically compromised.[1]

## Trump's position

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has argued for eliminating the practice outright, asking, "Why would you want mail-in ballots if you know it's corrupt? It's a corrupt system."[1] A February 2026 Politico report summarized by the Badlands Brief described Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] as ignoring the President's calls for reform, with several members suggesting his position went too far.[1]

## The Republican spectrum

The positions catalogued by the Brief formed a spectrum. [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] (R-NY) said he supported mail-in voting while objecting to unrequested universal mailing.[1] [[cory-mills|Cory Mills]] (R-FL) urged caution but located the fix in voter registration.[1] [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]] (R-FL) argued for treating ballots as evidentiary material under strict custody, citing Florida practice.[1] [[derrick-van-orden|Derrick Van Orden]] (R-WI) reframed Trump's objection as directed only at noncitizens and improperly registered voters, not at the sick or at military personnel.[1]

## Legislative prospects

The Brief reported that election-related legislative reforms were passing the House but were expected to stall in the [[senate|Senate]].[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that attempting to change election rules during an election year is "a horrifying prospect" for the beneficiaries of the existing system, and questioned why the coordinated cabinet-level push for reform began only in 2026, well into the midterm campaign season.[1] She offered possible sequencing explanations — that DOGE data and tooling, immigration enforcement experience, and voter-roll litigation discovery all had to come first — while concluding that "the most logical outcome is that we're getting another fake election."[1] She also connected the fight to attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]]'s proposals for new election laws, and cited [[john-solomon|John Solomon]]'s claim that the President would ultimately win over congressional Republicans.[1]
