# Mail-In Voting

Mail-in voting is the practice of casting ballots by mail rather than in person at a polling place. It became the central flashpoint of the 2026 [[election-reform-campaign|election reform campaign]] pressed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Trump's position

Trump has argued that mail-in balloting is inherently vulnerable to fraud: "Why would you want mail-in ballots if you know it's corrupt?" he said. "It's a corrupt system."[1]

## Republican resistance

A February 2026 Politico report found that Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] were largely ignoring the President's calls for reform.[1] Rep. [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] said he supported the use of mail-in voting while objecting only to states mailing ballots without request; Rep. [[cory-mills|Cory Mills]] urged caution but said fixing voter registration "will fix a lot of this"; Rep. [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]] pointed to Florida's practice of treating ballots "like they're evidence in a trial"; and Rep. [[derrick-van-orden|Derrick Van Orden]] framed Trump's objection as targeting ineligible voters rather than the method itself.[1] Election-related legislative reforms were passing the House but were expected to stall in the [[united-states-senate|Senate]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Republicans were "playing politics on election reforms," noting that changing election rules during an election year is "a horrifying prospect" for the beneficiaries of the existing system.[1] She questioned why the coordinated cabinet-wide push began only in the midterm campaign season, suggesting possible explanations including the need for DOGE data and tooling, immigration enforcement experience, and the evidentiary record built through voter roll and election lawsuits.[1] Her stated expectation was that meaningful reform is unlikely in an election year, and that "the most logical outcome is that we're getting another fake election" — though she also noted [[john-solomon|John Solomon]]'s report that the President intends to win over members of Congress directly.[1]

## The 2024 Heartland study

In February 2024 the [[heartland-institute|Heartland Institute]] published an analysis of the likely impact of fraudulent mail-in ballots on the 2020 presidential election, concluding that the outcome would "almost certainly" have been different without the massive expansion of voting by mail.[2] The study rested on a December Heartland/[[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] survey finding that roughly one in five mail-in voters admitted to potentially fraudulent actions in that election.[2]

The researchers concluded that mail-in ballot fraud "significantly" impacted the result and that, absent the pandemic-era expansion — often implemented without legislative approval — President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would most likely have won: "Had the 2020 election been conducted like every national election has been over the past two centuries, wherein the vast majority of voters cast ballots in-person rather than by mail, Donald Trump would have almost certainly been re-elected."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the study did not go far enough, holding that framing the outcome as individual voter fraud rather than [[2020-election-fraud-claims|systemic election fraud]] "is a distracting lie that ultimately serves the regime," and faulting its recommendations for keeping too much authority centralized at the state level rather than with counties and precincts.[2]

## AfD call to end mail-in ballots (2024)

After the [[2024-saxony-state-election|Saxony state election]] of September 2024, the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] called for an end to mail-in votes, citing security concerns and shady practices, while simultaneously investigating an alleged software error that cost it a parliamentary seat.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that "mail-in balloting and software issues are a global concern" and endorsed a "public trust prenup" — independent audits of every election regardless of outcome — rather than government-run risk-limiting audits conducted without outside oversight.[3]

## Millbourne, Pennsylvania indictment (March 2025)

In March 2025, The Federalist argued that an indictment of Pennsylvania Democrats demonstrated exactly how mail ballot fraud happens, contrasting the case with years of press assurances that such fraud is rare.[4] The outlet cited Associated Press reporter Ali Swenson's October claim that "yes, voter fraud happens … but it's rare and election offices have safeguards to catch it," a subsequent AP video titled "Why voter fraud isn't really a problem in U.S. elections," and a CNN video fact-checking [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s claims about voter fraud in Pennsylvania whose subhead nonetheless acknowledged "incidents of suspected voter registration fraud."[4]

According to the account, Pennsylvania's vulnerable online voter registration system allowed three Democrats to attempt to steal the 2021 general election for mayor of Millbourne after they gamed the online voter portal to fraudulently register nearly three dozen non-residents, with none of the touted safeguards catching the scheme.[4] Badlands contributor Ashe in America linked the case to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s concurrent review of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]'s conviction, arguing that election system vulnerabilities exist at every level from voters to ballots to counts.[4]

## Trump's push to eliminate mail-in voting (August 2025)

In August 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would launch a movement to end mail-in voting and phase out electronic voting machines, pledging to take executive action before the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[5] In a Truth Social post he called mail-in ballots and voting machines "highly inaccurate, very expensive, and seriously controversial," contrasting them with cheaper, more secure watermarked paper ballots that he argued would deliver immediate and indisputable results.[5]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected counter-arguments from [[andrew-yang|Andrew Yang]] that voting had become too difficult, writing that two decades of the existing system had produced "fake voters, fake ballots, fake counts, and the lowest levels of institutional trust in US history," and characterising Yang as a globalist promoter of taxpayer-funded universal basic income.[5]
