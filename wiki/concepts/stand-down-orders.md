# Stand Down Orders

"Stand down orders" is the organizing concept of the article, which uses the phrase both literally — orders given to rescuers at Benghazi — and figuratively, for policies that shielded America's declared enemies.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Benghazi

Former Special Forces officer [[kris-paronto|Kris Paronto]], one of the CIA contractors who fought on the night of the attack, told Politico: "There is no sensationalism in that: We were told to 'stand down.' Those words were used verbatim — 100 percent."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) He described hearing gunfire, being told by the base chief to wait outside the compound villa with two vehicles readied, and hearing radio calls for help from [[state-department|State Department]] diplomats with "an urgency in their voice that I can't portray."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) When his colleague John "Tig" Tiegen told the base chief the team was "losing the initiative; we need to go now," Paronto heard the words "stand down" before the team bucked leadership and left anyway.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## The figurative stand down

The article argues that President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s requirement of [[white-house|White House]] approval for every battlefield action was "essentially a stand down order" protecting [[isis|ISIS]] and Al Qaeda.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) It similarly casts [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]]'s 1998 Memorandum of Notification, which barred killing [[osama-bin-laden|Osama Bin Laden]], as a stand down order, and connects the two through Q drop 1887.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) In this reading, stand down orders are the mechanism by which a shadow government protects terrorists it supplies and funds, sustaining never-ending wars, taxpayer theft and money laundering.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)
