# Younger Abstention Doctrine

The **Younger abstention doctrine** is a federal-courts rule requiring federal judges to refrain from intervening in ongoing state criminal proceedings, leaving the litigant to raise federal constitutional claims in state court first.[1] The doctrine became central to the December 2025 federal habeas and bond litigation brought by former Mesa County clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1]

## Application in the Peters case

On Monday, December 8, 2025, a federal court dismissed Peters' petition for bond without prejudice, finding that it could not intervene because her matter remained pending in Colorado state court.[1] The order stated that "without question, Ms. Peters raises important constitutional questions concerning whether the trial court improperly punished her more severely because of her protected First Amendment speech," but that because the question remained pending before Colorado courts, the federal court "must abstain from answering that question until after the Colorado courts have decided the issue."[1] The application was accordingly dismissed without prejudice under Younger.[1]

Judge Varholak did not reach the merits of the First Amendment appeal and acknowledged the importance of the claims, but held that because they were pending rather than exhausted in state court, abstention was mandatory.[1]

## Exceptions to Younger

Younger admits of exceptions — for example, where prosecution is undertaken in bad faith or as harassment.[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued that Peters should be released because what is happening to her is the product of weaponized government and asymmetrical justice, a claim that would legally be framed as an "exception to Younger."[1] The court noted, however, that "at no point does Ms. Peters argue that this Court should find an exception to Younger, as opposed to arguing that the three Younger requirements were not satisfied" — meaning her counsel never advanced the exception argument despite months of briefing on the doctrine.[1]
