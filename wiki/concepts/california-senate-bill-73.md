# California Senate Bill 73

California Senate Bill 73 is a state law signed by Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] in May 2026 that sharply restricts law enforcement access to ballots and election records. It is discussed in the [[badlands-brief-257|Badlands Brief of May 29, 2026]] as a pre-primary measure by the state government.

## Provisions

The law limits peace officers from accessing ballots, voter lists, rosters, and certified voting technology. Officers are generally barred from interfering with election administration except in cases involving urgent public health or safety threats. A court order is required before law enforcement may take possession of key election materials. Removing packages containing voted ballots from election officials is classified as a crime, with civil penalties of up to $50,000 for violations involving ballot custody, and violations can carry criminal penalties, fines, and up to three years in jail.

Newsom stated that the measure was intended to "clarify the rules of engagement."

## Commentary

Writing in the Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because ballot chain of custody is "essentially non-existent" in practice, the statute's custody provisions may have the effect of criminalizing ordinary, status quo election administration. The Badlands treatment situates the bill within a broader pattern of state-level resistance to federal election-integrity initiatives, contrasting it with Judge [[carl-nichols|Carl Nichols]]'s ruling allowing the federal voter citizenship executive order to proceed.
