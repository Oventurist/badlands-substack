# COP30

COP30 was the [[united-nations|United Nations]] climate conference held in Belem, [[brazil|Brazil]] in November 2025.[1]

## Pavilion fire

A fire broke out in a pavilion inside the conference venue on Thursday, November 20, 2025, forcing an evacuation as panicked delegates ran for the exits.[1] UN and security crews rushed with extinguishers as smoke engulfed the corridor and people shouted "fire!"[1] Firefighters arrived as smoke billowed inside and outside the conference building; Brazil's tourism minister told reporters at the venue that the fire was under control and no one was hurt.[1]

The fire occurred while ministers from around the world were deep in negotiations aimed at breaking a deadlock over fossil fuels, climate finance and trade measures, with one day left in the two-week conference.[1]

## Assessment and aftermath

Coverage after the summit's conclusion described a fractured process. The United States snubbed the talks, petro-states and fossil-fuel-hungry emerging economies obtained most of what they wanted, and European delegations struggled to demonstrate that they were prepared to lead efforts against global warming.[2] Two weeks of negotiations ended with a deal that even critical delegates said showed a divided, leaderless assembly of nearly 200 nations could make some progress, though the hardest decisions — accelerating the transition away from fossil fuels and reducing trade barriers to clean energy technologies — were deferred to future summits.[2] The final days included a fire that interrupted discussions.[2]

Zero Hedge argued that institutional climate discussion had shifted over five years from net-zero emissions goals toward a more mercenary debate over carbon taxation, and that COP30 in Brazil was largely a squabble over which countries would gain the most access to carbon taxes and donations collected by global intermediaries.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that COP conferences had always been boondoggles but that the failure was now being acknowledged even by The Guardian and [[politico|Politico]], adding that when the United States declines to attend, "the globalist grifters just embarrass themselves."[2]
