# Big Tech

"Big Tech" is the collective term for the dominant American technology and platform companies — principally Microsoft, Apple, Alphabet (Google), Amazon, Meta (Facebook), Nvidia and Tesla — whose market power, censorship decisions and political alignments have been a recurring subject of Badlands Media commentary.[1] In an August 22, 2024 essay titled "Big Tech Climbing Aboard the Trump Train," contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated the sector as a single political actor undergoing a visible realignment toward [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the run-up to the 2024 election.[1]

## Origins in state funding

Carlson argued that the sector's rise cannot be separated from US national-security funding, writing that without [[darpa|DARPA]] and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] the country probably would not have Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Netflix, or perhaps even Tesla.[1] He described the founders as figures who accepted intelligence-community backing to expand their vision and only later understood they had effectively become "a kept mistress" at the CIA's beck and call, and said he did not begrudge them because some may have believed they were doing something noble for the country.[1]

He further contended that the [[obama-administration|Obama administration]] grew the [[faang-stocks|FAANG]] companies into mega-caps by granting preferential treatment over competitors, ensuring they could monopolize their markets, and that under the [[biden-administration|Biden administration]] the [[magnificent-seven-stocks|Magnificent Seven]] continued to soar.[1] By his account these firms are now effectively "invaluable, irreplaceable."[1]

## The 2024 realignment

Carlson catalogued the public shift: [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] apologizing to Trump after the failed assassination attempt and saying he would not back a Democrat; [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] funding Trump's campaign at a reported $45 million a month; and investors and founders including [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]], [[david-sacks|David Sacks]], [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]] and [[ben-horowitz|Ben Horowitz]] publicly climbing aboard what he called the Trump Train.[1] Not all of [[silicon-valley|Silicon Valley]] followed: Carlson singled out Google as an example of firms doubling down to keep Trump out of the White House, remaining loyal to the entities that created their technology, funded their businesses, and manipulated the system on their behalf.[1]

His explanation for the shift was that the tech titans had concluded the [[deep-state-cabal|Deep State Cabal]] now intends to destroy America, and that if the country falls, Silicon Valley falls with it.[1] Walking the streets of San Francisco and seeing "the human carnage," he wrote, they can see the plan being implemented in the Bay Area itself.[1] He posed the rhetorical questions of who cares how much money one has if the high-end stores and restaurants are closed, or about driving a Lamborghini that cannot be parked without being vandalized.[1]

## Forgiveness argument

The essay's central appeal is that the movement should "begrudgingly learn to forgive and forget" with respect to Big Tech, excluding crimes against humanity, human trafficking and child abuse from any amnesty while allowing that lesser corruption may need to go unpunished.[1] Carlson cited George Washington's earlier service alongside the British Army and the Apostle Paul's persecution of Christians as precedents for accepting a genuine change of heart.[1] He also speculated that the underground fortresses reportedly built by tech billionaires may have been intended as protection from their Cabal overlords rather than from angry citizens.[1]

## Open questions raised

Carlson ended by listing unresolved threads he saw as connected: the Silicon Valley Bank bailout of early spring 2023, the attack on Nancy Pelosi's husband in the fall of 2022, the push to replace Joe Biden with [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], and the [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] [[ftx|FTX]] collapse — asking whether the whole sequence amounted to a "massive white hat coup" in which Trump and his allies took control of Big Tech and cryptocurrency.[1]
