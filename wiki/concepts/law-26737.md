# Law 26.737

Law 26.737 is Argentina's 2011 rural land statute, which set caps on foreign ownership of rural land and restricted acquisitions of strategic parcels.[1]

## Repeal effort under Milei

The government of President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]] is pursuing reforms that would significantly alter Argentina's land-ownership regime.[1] A legislative proposal developed by a government-aligned council would effectively remove the longstanding limits on foreign ownership of rural land, overturning key provisions of Law 26.737.[1] A separate plan presented by the "Consejo de Mayo" envisages a broader liberalization of land purchases by private foreign investors, drawing criticism from analysts who argue it could erode protections on national territory.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reports that on December 9 the Argentine government announced by executive order that the prohibition on foreign entities owning tracts of rural land had been repealed, together with a related 2020 law barring the rezoning of rural land affected by forest fire for 30 to 60 years.[1]

## Legal pushback

These reform efforts have faced legal resistance: a court ruling recently suspended the repeal of the land law, temporarily blocking the provisions that would have eliminated restrictions on land purchases by foreigners following a legal challenge.[1] The timing of the repeal effort alongside the [[patagonia-wildfires-2026|Patagonia wildfires of 2026]] and Argentina's substantial rare earth and critical mineral resources has fueled suspicion among commentators.[1]
