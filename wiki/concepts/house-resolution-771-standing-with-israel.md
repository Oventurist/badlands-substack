# House Resolution 771 (Standing with Israel)

House Resolution 771 was a resolution expressing strong support for [[israel]] that the [[us-house-of-representatives|U.S. House of Representatives]] approved on Wednesday, October 25, 2023.[1] It was the first bill brought to the floor after Rep. [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-LA) was elected Speaker of the chamber, and thus functioned as the new Speaker's first legislative move.[1]

## Content and vote

The resolution stated that the United States "stands" with Israel in the wake of the October 7 [[hamas]] attack and amid Israel's bombing campaign in [[gaza]].[1] It reaffirmed the U.S. commitment to providing military aid to Israel and declared that [[congress]] would work to pass more, stating that the House "stands ready to assist Israel with emergency resupply and other security, diplomatic, and intelligence support."[1] The measure also called for continued U.S. enforcement of sanctions on [[iran]] and condemned the Hamas attack.[1]

The resolution passed 412–10.[1] Nine Democrats voted against it: Reps. [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] (NY), André Carson (IN), [[cori-bush|Cori Bush]] (MO), [[al-green|Al Green]] (TX), Summer Lee (PA), [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]] (NY), [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] (MN), Delia Ramirez (IL), and [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] (MI).[1] The sole Republican to vote no was Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] (KY).[1]

## Massie's objection

Explaining his opposition on X, Massie pointed to the sanctions provisions and the promises of military support and aid, saying the resolution "contains an open-ended promise of military support that is so broad that it could be interpreted to commit US soldiers to the conflict," and adding that "US troops should not be engaged in this conflict."[1]
