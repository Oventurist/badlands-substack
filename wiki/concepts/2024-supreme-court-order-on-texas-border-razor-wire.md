# 2024 Supreme Court Order on Texas Border Razor Wire

On January 22, 2024, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] voted 5–4 to allow [[united-states-border-patrol|U.S. Border Patrol]] agents to remove razor wire that had been installed along the U.S.–Mexico border by Texas Governor [[greg-abbott]], while the underlying legal challenge continued.[1]

## The order

In a brief order, the high court vacated a ruling issued in mid-December 2023 by the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-fifth-circuit|U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit]].[1] Justices Clarence Thomas, Samuel Alito, Neil Gorsuch, and Brett Kavanaugh voted to deny the application to vacate the lower-court injunction, which would have prevented Border Patrol agents from removing the barrier.[1] Chief Justice John Roberts, along with Justices Amy Coney Barrett, Ketanji Brown Jackson, Elena Kagan, and Sonia Sotomayor, sided with the Biden administration.[1] No justice provided an explanation for their vote.[1]

## Background and arguments

The order represented a win for President [[joe-biden]]'s administration, which had struggled to curb illegal immigration since taking office in 2021 amid an ongoing battle with Abbott, a Republican, over the border.[1] The administration filed an emergency request arguing that Texas was blocking federal agents from carrying out their duties, and that the barrier prevented agents from reaching illegal immigrants who had already entered the United States.[1] Lawyers for Texas countered that Washington had failed to secure the border and that the razor wire fencing had been erected under the [[operation-lone-star]] plan; the federal government argued the wire blocked agents from reaching "the very border they are charged with patrolling and the individuals they are charged with apprehending and inspecting."[1] The reporting was carried by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]].[1]

## Procedural history

Texas installed its own border measures in 2023, after which the Biden administration began removing them.[1] In October 2023 Texas went to the Fifth Circuit, arguing that Border Patrol agents were violating Texas law, and the appeals court granted a stay — a temporary pause — of the removal activity.[1] The Biden administration then applied to the Supreme Court, arguing the stay prevented agents from doing their job because Texas had placed barriers so as to block access to roughly 2.5 miles of border.[1] Texas responded that the stay served the public interest, that the border was not secure, that federal agents were not enforcing federal law, and that the application was unnecessary because the case was already docketed for argument on February 7.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[just-human]] urged readers toward "understanding over reacting," noting that the four conservative justices favoured leaving the stay in place while the other five did not, and stressing that the federal government has the authority and power to enforce its own laws — something he said the Court had just reaffirmed, such that neither Texas nor any other state can prevent it from doing so.[1] He declined to endorse the decision as correct, but called it understandable, and predicted Texas would next press the question of what happens where the federal government fails to enforce its own laws to the grave detriment of state citizens — an issue he expected the Court to view differently.[1]
