# Right to Commerce

The right to commerce — also called the inalienable right of free association, or the right to contract — is described in the Badlands Media essay "Cracks in the Curtain" as the right to engage in trade with other citizens as one lawfully chooses: to work, to buy, and to sell.[1]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argues that this right is "actually at the heart of what WE THE PEOPLE can do to take back our freedoms from government overreach and the insidious agenda of the globalist international bankers," while deferring the full discussion to a future post in the [[nature-of-the-control-system|Nature of the Control System]] series.[1]

The right is introduced in the context of emergency powers. The essay contends that a government cannot lawfully decide unilaterally to change monetary policy — for example by ending the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] — unless the citizens have given it that power, and that in the United States the government has instead used various "emergencies" to claim a power it never had: near-total control over the way citizens engage in business and commerce.[1] Whatever the merits of the debate over the lawful basis for emergency powers, the essay argues, citizens almost always have to rein in the government to get their rights back, and rarely have the time, energy, and cohesive support among themselves to do it — one reason it gives for cabal consolidation of power over the past 400 years proceeding almost completely unchecked.[1]
