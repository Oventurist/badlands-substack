# Bankman-Fried Second Trial Dismissal

In late December 2023, federal prosecutors abandoned plans to try [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] a second time on additional charges after the [[ftx|FTX]] co-founder was convicted of a massive fraud the previous month.[1]

## The decision

The government told US District Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] in a letter that, in the interest of expediency, it would drop plans to try Bankman-Fried for conspiracy to bribe foreign officials, bank fraud, and operating an unlicensed money transmitting business, among other charges.[1] Bankman-Fried, then 31, had been found guilty of seven counts of fraud and conspiracy and faced the possibility of decades in prison.[1]

Prosecutors explained that the Bahamas, which had extradited Bankman-Fried to face the original US charges, had not consented to the additional charges.[1] Such consent is required under treaty obligations, and Bankman-Fried's earlier legal challenge in the Bahamas had prompted the US to split the case in two.[1] The government added that much of the evidence for a second trial had already been introduced at the original trial and could be weighed by the judge at sentencing, set for 28 March.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writer [[just-human|Just Human]] emphasised that the dismissal changed little practically: Bankman-Fried was already convicted, already in federal prison, and would remain there for years, and the dropped counts rested on almost all the same evidence.[1] Just Human argued the operative question is why the Bahamian government would not consent, pointing toward people who were never in the Bahamas and never extradited but who remain in the United States and could be charged with similar crimes — suggesting Bankman-Fried's parents were most likely the brains behind the campaign finance scheme.[1]
