# 2023 Face Mask Carbon Dioxide Study

The **2023 face mask carbon dioxide study** was a review by German researchers, published in the journal *Heliyon*, concluding that face masks can cause carbon dioxide poisoning even when worn for short periods and may have contributed significantly to stillbirths when worn by pregnant women, as well as to testicular dysfunction and cognitive decline in children.[1] The work comprised a review of 43 previously published studies on CO2 exposure, mask-wearing and pregnancy, and was reported by the *Daily Mail* and by [[summit-news|Summit News]].[1]

## Findings

The study noted that short-term exposure to CO2 concentrations as low as 0.3 per cent caused brain damage, increased anxiety and impaired memory in pregnant rats and young mice.[1] In another cited experiment, male mice exposed to 2.5 per cent CO2 for four hours suffered destruction of testicular cells and sperm, an exposure the authors equated to 0.5 per cent CO2 in humans over the same period.[1] A further experiment found stillbirth and birth defects in pregnant rats exposed to 3 per cent CO2, equivalent to roughly 0.8 per cent for humans.[1] The review also pointed to research finding that just five minutes of mask wearing raised CO2 levels to between 1.4 and 3.2 per cent, and alluded to the surge in stillbirths recorded during the pandemic.[1] The researchers stressed that the review provided circumstantial evidence only.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that "the truth always makes more sense than the socially enforced lie," maintaining that masks were never considered effective at slowing viral spread by most reasonable professionals because viruses are too small to be stopped — likening a virus to a golf ball and a mask to a soccer goal.[1] He contended that masking was never about disease transmission but about social compliance, "making people do something they know to be bogus as an act of submission to an authority," and about the physiological effects of compromised cognition, memory and reproduction identified in the study.[1]

Deschamps pointed to China, where mask wearing is normative, as a place where birthrates have declined and compliance with totalitarianism is likewise the norm.[1] He acknowledged that the study's data were correlative and that vaccines, increased stress and social isolation were plausible contributing factors, while calling for "a return to merit-based social policy" and "peaceful noncompliance of any policy that on its face lacks merit."[1] His conclusion was that "going along with something you know to be untrue or harmful in the name of not ruffling feathers almost always comes at a terrible price."[1]
