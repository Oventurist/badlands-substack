# Ruby Ridge

Ruby Ridge is the northern Idaho site of the 1992 standoff between federal agents and the family of Randy Weaver, described in the Badlands Media essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI" as one of two massacres — the other being Waco — through which its author first learned how federal agencies manufacture extremism.[1]

## The informant allegation

The essay's specific claim about Ruby Ridge concerns entrapment rather than the siege itself. [[fbi|FBI]] informants, it states, went to church picnics pushing race hate, and Randy Weaver responded with incredulity.[1] The detail is offered as a concrete illustration of the article's general model: the agencies approach a target community with extremist rhetoric supplied from outside, hoping to elicit or fabricate the radicalism they will later claim to have discovered.[1]

## Place in the wider pattern

Author [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] generalises the episode into a standing method. When a group of people who are anti-government, or for limited government, or who merely want the government held accountable begins to form, the [[cia|CIA]] and FBI infiltrate it and try to turn it into a hate group, or at least make it appear to be one.[1] In some cases the agencies do not infiltrate but instead start such groups themselves.[1] The purpose, in the article's telling, is to make the public equate anti-government sentiment with hate groups.[1]

Ruby Ridge thus functions in the essay as the historical precedent for its reading of [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6]], where the same techniques are alleged to have been applied to supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The article insists there is "nothing new under the sun" — that what has been happening lately has been going on since the FBI and CIA were formed.[1]
