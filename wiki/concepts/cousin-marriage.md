# Cousin Marriage

Cousin marriage — specifically marriage between first cousins — became a flashpoint in British public health and immigration debate in September 2025 after guidance from the [[nhs|National Health Service]] appeared to promote its advantages.[1]

## NHS guidance controversy

Britain's socialised health service came under fire for guidance issued by [[nhs-england|NHS England]]'s Genomics Education Programme claiming that first-cousin marriages offer "stronger extended family support systems and economic advantages."[1] The practice is highly prevalent among Africans, Pakistanis, and other ethnic minority groups in Britain, and carries well-documented risks of birth defects.[1]

Critics including Conservative MP [[richard-holden|Richard Holden]] condemned the NHS for endorsing what they regard as a harmful practice, with Holden saying the service "should stop taking the knee to damaging and oppressive cultural practices."[1] Holden also attacked the Labour government for ignoring calls to end cousin marriage as a way of deterring chain migration, although the Conservatives likewise declined to end it across their fourteen years in office from 2010 to mid-2024.[1]
