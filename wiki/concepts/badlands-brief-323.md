# Badlands Brief: We're Winning, plus Cuban Dominoes & Weaponized Ethics

An edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published May 15, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], carrying 133 likes at the time of archiving. The Brief describes itself as a daily digest of Badlands commentary on "the narratives dominating the info war," pairing summarized news items with signed analysis from the outlet's contributors. This edition covered five main items — a federal election-integrity initiative, a proposed Saudi–Iranian non-aggression pact, the Taiwan question in Trump–Xi diplomacy, deteriorating conditions in Cuba, and a Justice Department lawsuit against District of Columbia bar authorities — plus a bonus item on Democratic redistricting rhetoric.

## Federal expansion of election-integrity enforcement

The Brief opens with a report that a [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration effort to expand federal involvement in voter verification and mail-in ballot rules is advancing through coordinated interagency discussions involving the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], and the [[united-states-postal-service|US Postal Service]]. According to the article, [[white-house|White House]] officials have held recent interagency talks on implementation involving DOJ [[civil-rights-division|Civil Rights Division]] head [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] and her deputies, USPS chief executive David Steiner, and DHS election-integrity official Heather Honey, described as a former aide and protégé of attorney Cleta Mitchell, who advised Trump during post-2020 election legal efforts.

The effort is traced to Trump's March executive order titled "Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections", which directs DHS to develop a "state citizenship list," instructs the DOJ to prioritize prosecution of state and local officials who distribute ballots to ineligible voters, and calls on USPS to draft rules requiring states to provide voter registration lists in order to process mail-in ballots. Internal disclosures cited by the source name additional participants: Deputy Homeland Security Advisor Anthony Salisbury, DOJ associate deputy attorney general Aakash Singh, principal associate deputy attorney general Trent McCotter, and counselor to the attorney general Henry Whitaker. USPS said it is reviewing the executive order and drafting regulations, while DHS and USCIS officials described internal deliberations as ongoing.

Commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] frames the moment as a vindication: "we need to pause and appreciate this moment... Kiddos, we made it." She notes with amusement that the first two words of the referenced mainstream article are "Clay Parikh" — a friend of Badlands, frequent guest on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]] and SitRep, and a multi-time attendee of the [[great-american-restoration-tour|Great American Restoration Tour]]. The quoted passage from *[[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]]* characterizes Parikh as "a cybersecurity expert from Alabama" who "spent years as a bit player in the world of election denial," not a star with his own platform "like the MyPillow guy," but who gained a modest following circulating conspiracy theories about Trump's 2020 defeat — including the claim that poll workers gave Trump supporters felt-tip markers that rendered ballots unreadable — and who more recently asserted that a group of federal lawmakers is covering up foreign election interference.

Ashe in America objects that these are "some of the least offensive things that Clay Parikh has asserted about the fake elections" and calls the characterization "a lazy assessment of the full range of the [[election-denial|election denialism]] at play," adding a parenthetical note that the author had been invited onto a Badlands show. She counters that Parikh is a certified ethical hacker and "the only public expert that has been hands-on-keys for penetration testing on the voting systems," certified as an expert in court proceedings nationwide and experienced at the highest levels of classification on critical infrastructure and elections. The segment closes: "Shame, *The Atlantic*. Also, they're right. The election deniers are winning. We're winning because our message is the true."

## Saudi Arabia's proposed non-aggression pact with Iran

The second item reports, citing *Financial Times*, that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] has floated a Middle East non-aggression pact with [[iran]] as part of postwar planning with allies once the US–[[israel|Israeli]] conflict with Tehran ends. Diplomats said Riyadh pointed to the 1970s Helsinki Accords, which eased Cold War tensions in Europe, as a possible framework for reducing regional conflict and expanding economic cooperation. Arab diplomats reportedly believe most Muslim and Arab nations would support the concept, though including Israel remains a major obstacle amid growing regional anger over Israeli military operations in [[gaza]], [[lebanon]], and [[syria]]. The report also describes Saudi Arabia strengthening coordination with [[pakistan]], [[turkey]], and [[egypt]]; Pakistan's defense minister said Islamabad wants [[qatar]] and Turkey added to the Saudi–Pakistan defense pact to build a broader regional economic and military alliance aimed at reducing dependence on outside powers.

The accompanying analysis by [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues that "for months, there has been relentless gaslighting that Saudi Arabia was secretly supporting and/or conducting warfare against Iran," and for years that the Saudis are subservient to Israel, that [[mohammed-bin-salman|MBS]] is a "Zionist puppet," and that the Kingdom had betrayed the Ummah in service of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]. In his account the reality is that Saudi Arabia began serving the Deep State in 1975 when King Faisal was assassinated, if not earlier, with many Saudi royals in bed with the [[cia|CIA]] — a relationship reaching its apex under the George W. Bush administration.

He dates the turn to the Obama years: in 2011 the lifelong governor of Riyadh, Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, was appointed Crown Prince by his brother King Abdullah. Salman became King in January 2015 and named his son Mohammed Deputy Crown Prince and Defense Minister; the son dismantled and rebuilt the government on paper within ten days. Mohammed was elevated to Crown Prince in June 2017, one month after Trump's first visit to Riyadh, and in November Salman named him acting sovereign, after which Mohammed arrested corrupt relatives said to be working with the CIA and, by extension, Mossad. The commentary concedes MBS harbors animus toward Iran but questions whether "The Reformer" — who expelled the radical clerics and effectively ended Wahhabism as a state institution, and who has spent eleven years implementing the ambitious Vision 2030 plan to transform the economy toward tourism, hospitality and high tech — would topple a rival at the cost of destroying the region. The conclusion is that the Deep State targets Saudi Arabia precisely because of its role uniting the Muslim world behind a prosperity-based rather than destructive vision, requiring a security architecture assembled from states that are "not historic allies."

## Taiwan, Trump and Xi

The third item reports that [[taiwan]] emerged as the clearest flashpoint in Trump's Beijing summit with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], even as both sides publicly emphasized stability, trade and cooperation. Xi warned that mishandling Taiwan could lead to "clashes and even conflicts," while US statements largely avoided the issue and said policy remains unchanged. The Brief's reading of "the signal in the noise" is that Beijing put Taiwan at the center of the meeting, Washington publicly conceded nothing, and Taipei reported "no surprises" while urging [[china]] to end military pressure — not a breakthrough, but a marker that Taiwan is moving higher on the board as the two leaders test whether economic détente can survive the hardest security question in US–China relations.

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] identifies the development as confirmation of the Multipolar Margin Call thesis he laid out in October during what he calls manufactured economic tensions between Trump and Xi. In his framing the US–China trade war "wasn't a bug of the dissolution of the rules-based international order; it was the core feature," forcing the rerouting of monetary flows, supply chains and manufacturing capacity back toward sovereign balance sheets on both sides of the Pacific. Layering in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] squeeze — with Xi offering to help reopen it, both sides agreeing it must stay open, and China signaling it will buy more American oil — he sees energy and capital being pulled out of "the globalist circulatory system" and redirected into "sovereign arteries," with "Regions of Responsibility" hardening and appeals to borderless governance "dashed on the rocks of pragmatic nationalism."

He describes an accelerating [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] cascade: [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] opened the sequence with [[ukraine]], the first major globalist proxy peeled off the board in the modern age; Trump continued it with [[venezuela]], Iran and a looming [[cuba|Cuba]] chapter; and Xi is positioned to complete the arc with Taiwan, "the last remaining globalist forward operating base of any real consequence." A clean sovereign resolution there would, in his telling, complete a trilogy of hard lessons: Iran demonstrated the folly of relying on distant nations for energy security, [[russia]] the folly of relying on distant nations and their proxies for national security, and China through an inevitable Taiwan crisis will demonstrate the folly of relying on distant nations for logistical and technological security — semiconductors, rare earths, advanced manufacturing. He argues the just-in-time global supply chain collapsed in the wake of "an engineered pandemic" meant to stop the Sovereign Alliance but which only accelerated its victory, and that on-shoring and reshoring will eventually "look less like protectionism and more like prophecy."

## Cuba under pressure

The fourth item, citing ZeroHedge, reports that Cuba faces worsening blackouts, fuel shortages and economic deterioration as the Trump administration increases pressure on Havana through tighter sanctions and regional isolation. The island's energy infrastructure is described as under severe strain, with dwindling fuel imports and recurring nationwide outages intensifying public frustration and pressuring the Communist government. Concurrently, per [[axios]], CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] reportedly met with Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] and senior regime officials, signaling that backchannel communication between Washington and Havana remains active. Analysts quoted view the combination of economic pressure and quiet diplomacy as an attempt to force concessions while preventing broader regional instability.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry comments that Trump appears to be "queuing up Cuba to be the next domino to fall, which will take all of the inertia out of the Middle East and the animus toward Iran," and asks whether the Castro family might return to power as a partner of Trump.

## DOJ suit against DC bar authorities

The final main item reports that the Justice Department filed a complaint against District of Columbia disciplinary authorities, alleging they are improperly using the attorney discipline process to regulate the official actions of federal government lawyers. The filing seeks to nullify what the department calls the DC Bar's unlawful prosecution of former Assistant Attorney General Jeff Clark, which centers on internal deliberations related to potential fraud in the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]] and remains tied to litigation nearly six years later. Officials argued the disciplinary process improperly targets executive branch deliberations and risks restricting federal attorneys from providing candid legal advice, and said the action advances Trump's executive order on ending the weaponization of the federal government and a related memorandum on preventing abuses of the legal system and federal courts.

Ashe in America notes that Clark drafted an internal memo representing his personal legal theory about election irregularities in Georgia, that all parties concede he believed its substance, and that the memo was never finalized or sent. She and [[canncon|CannCon]] covered what she calls a "sad and demoralizing hearing" at which DC bar authorities recommended disbarment over conduct they characterized as dishonest, tied to the draft letter. The Brief quotes the government's prayer for relief, which asks the court to declare that the defendants' investigations, proceedings, adjudications and findings against Clark violate the Supremacy Clause and Article II and are therefore void; to enjoin the defendants and their successors from any disciplinary action against Clark based on his conduct as a federal government attorney; to award costs and fees; and to grant other relief. Ashe in America endorses the argument that local bar regulators have no oversight of presidential legal decision-making through "ethics" reviews, hopes the precedent might eventually apply at all levels of government, and closes with "weaponized ethics is being adjudicated… Accelerate."

## Bonus item: Democratic redistricting rhetoric

A bonus item, sourced to [[politico]], reports that congressional Democrats are escalating rhetoric over the national redistricting battle ahead of the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]], with some lawmakers and strategists describing the fight as requiring a "war posture" after Republican-led map changes in states such as [[texas]] threatened Democratic [[house-of-representatives|House]] prospects. Democratic operatives argued the party must abandon "unilateral disarmament" and counter GOP efforts with every available legal and political tool. One strategist said the goal was to politically "crush" the "souls" of the [[maga|MAGA]] movement, reflecting an increasingly existential tone around control of the House amid a nationwide push for mid decade redistricting battles in what is expected to be one of the most aggressively litigated election cycles in modern US history.
