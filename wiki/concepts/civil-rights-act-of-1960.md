# Civil Rights Act of 1960

The Civil Rights Act of 1960 is a United States federal civil rights law enacted during the Eisenhower administration. In the Badlands corpus it appears as one of the three federal statutes — alongside the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] (NVRA) and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] (HAVA) — cited as the legal basis for the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s request for Georgia voter registration information.

## Role in the 2026 Georgia voter-roll dispute

The January 23, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the [[georgia-senate-ethics-committee|Georgia Senate Ethics Committee]] passed Resolution 563 on January 22, 2026, urging Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] to fully comply with the Justice Department's request for voter registration information "to meet obligations under the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA), Help America Vote Act (HAVA), and Civil Rights Act of 1960."

The Act's inclusion in the resolution ties the 1960 statute — best known for its provisions authorizing federal inspection of local voting records — to the post-[[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 election]] dispute over the integrity and accessibility of Georgia's voter rolls, in which Badlands commentators and state lawmakers pressed for full federal access to registration data following the allegations of vote-count manipulation presented to the [[georgia-state-election-board|State Election Board]] by investigator [[joe-rossi|Joe Rossi]].
