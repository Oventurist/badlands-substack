# 2024 Riyadh Arab-Islamic Summit

The 2024 Riyadh Arab-Islamic Summit was a gathering of Arab and Muslim leaders held in the Saudi capital on Monday, November 11, 2024, organized to press for the establishment of a Palestinian state. It came a year after the [[arab-league|Arab League]] and the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation|Organization of Islamic Cooperation]] held a first conference on the same subject, and days after American voters returned [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to the White House.[1]

## Mohammed bin Salman's remarks

Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], described by the Times of Israel as [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s de facto ruler, called on [[israel|Israel]] to respect [[iran|Iran]]'s sovereignty and refrain from attacking Iranian soil, telling the summit that the international community should oblige Israel "to respect the sovereignty of the sisterly Islamic Republic of Iran and not to violate its lands."[1] He also appeared to harden the kingdom's rhetoric against Israel, renewing "its condemnation and categorical rejection of the genocide committed by Israel against the brotherly Palestinian people, which has claimed the lives of 150,000 martyrs, wounded and missing, most of whom are women and children."[1] Reporting on the summit suggested Riyadh might be moving further away from U.S. efforts to broker a normalization deal with Jerusalem.[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the summit as a refutation of claims that the crown prince is a Zionist puppet beholden to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], and noted the shift in Israeli and Zionist media coverage from calling him an "Arab Partner" to "Saudi Arabia's de facto ruler."[1] He recalled that after the October 7 attack Mohammed bin Salman summoned the 57-nation Muslim world to Riyadh, told the assembled leaders to stand down, and took the lead in navigating the crisis — while ensuring Israel remained provisioned as Houthi rebels effectively shut down shipping to Haifa by targeting cargo vessels.[1]

He argued that the Arabs demonstrated restraint and humanity while Netanyahu unleashed his fury on a civilian population, and that with Netanyahu turning toward Iran the crown prince had again convened the 57-nation body, uniting rival tribes of Islam that had been at odds for 1,300 years into "a coalition (not a caliphate)" championing peace and prosperity.[1] After a decade of rivalry with Iran, Mohammed bin Salman was described as stepping into the crossfire to defend his former adversary and to protect the fragile Sunni–Shia peace he and his father had cultivated.[1] The commentary closed by placing the next move with Israel — whether its people would abide by the settler movement and the pursuit of Greater Israel or demand new leadership — and asserting that Trump would not let Netanyahu's Kahanist allies threaten his Arab friends.[1]
