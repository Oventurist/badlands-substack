# Conversion Therapy Bans

Conversion therapy bans are state laws prohibiting licensed mental health professionals from providing therapy intended to change a client's sexual orientation or gender identity, particularly for minors. The Colorado version of such a law, enacted in 2019, was struck down by the Supreme Court in [[chiles-v-colorado|Chiles v. Colorado]].[1]

## Structure of the Colorado law

The Colorado statute barred licensed counselors from providing talk therapy aimed at helping clients reduce unwanted same-sex attractions, change sexual behaviors, or align their gender identity with their biological sex.[1] At the same time, it expressly permitted counselors to offer "acceptance, support, and understanding" to clients exploring gender transition or identity.[1] It was this asymmetry that the Supreme Court identified as viewpoint discrimination under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]

## Speech versus conduct

A central question in the litigation was whether counseling speech may be regulated as professional conduct. The Court, in an opinion by Justice [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]], held that speech does not lose constitutional protection simply because the government labels it "treatment" or a "therapeutic modality."[1] In dissent, Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] warned the ruling could impair states' ability to regulate medical and mental health care and might permit "substandard medical care."[1]

The plaintiff in the case practiced exclusively talk therapy, without medications or coercive techniques, serving clients who sought faith-integrated counseling.[1]
