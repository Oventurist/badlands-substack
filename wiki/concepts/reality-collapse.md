# Reality Collapse

"Reality collapse" is the term Badlands Media uses for the principal near-term danger of [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]]: the destruction of a shared, verifiable basis for knowing what is true.[1] Asked what kind of power lies in the tool that AI's wielders now hold, the article answers in two words: reality collapse.[1]

## Mechanisms

The first mechanism is epistemic outsourcing. A machine can consume, comprehend, and analyze an entire book before a human can crack the cover; it can read all the books before a human reads one, then summarize and publish insights from them. Many humans will trust the machine and rely on those insights rather than read for themselves, because it is more efficient.[1]

The second is synthetic media. Three seconds of audio is all it takes for AI to simulate a person's voice and produce a [[deepfake|deep fake]] of them saying anything; a still photo or brief video clip is enough to fake the face delivering those words.[1]

The third is the reading of interior states. [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]] and [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]] hold that AI will soon be capable of dream mapping and dream interpretation because of rapidly increasing capability with functional MRI data — the machine reading brain activity and returning a visual or written representation of a person's dreams. Pulling that thread, the article says, leads quickly to *Minority Report*.[1]

## Political implications

George Orwell's *1984* supplies the template: "Every record has been destroyed or falsified, every book rewritten, every picture has been repainted... History has stopped. Nothing exists except an endless present in which the Party is always right."[1] [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] has predicted that 2024 would be the last human election and that reality collapse would be substantial enough that political leaders will be selected by machines; Badlands calls this a silly prediction on the grounds that leaders are already selected by machines, and treats the revelation of AI's election use cases as a further indictment of everyone who peddled the claim that 2020 was the safest and most secure election in history.[1]

The article's conclusion is that the implications of the technology are powerful, but the prospect of that power residing solely in the hands of despots is terrifying — and that this fear is precisely the cry of the globalists.[1] The *Westworld* line "Have you ever questioned the nature of your reality?" is invoked as the emblem of the condition, sharpened by the resemblance between [[mo-gawdat|Mo Gawdat]] and the show's chief engineer Arnold Weber.[1]
