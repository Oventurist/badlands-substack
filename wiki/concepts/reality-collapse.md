# Reality Collapse

"Reality collapse" is the term used in the July 2023 Badlands Media article "Artificial Intelligence: Hysteria vs. Reason" to name the central danger of artificial intelligence: not an autonomous machine turning on humanity, but the destruction of shared, verifiable reality by humans wielding the machine as a tool.

## Mediated comprehension

The article's first illustration is epistemic dependency. A machine "can consume, comprehend, and analyze an entire book before the human can even crack the cover. It can read all the books before the human reads one. It can summarize and publish insights from the book." Many people, the source predicts, will trust the machine's summary rather than read for themselves, because it is more efficient — surrendering the independent verification on which shared reality rests.

To sharpen the point the article quotes George Orwell's *1984*: "Every record has been destroyed or falsified, every book rewritten, every picture has been repainted, every statue and street building has been renamed, every date has been altered. And the process is continuing day by day and minute by minute. History has stopped. Nothing exists except an endless present in which the Party is always right."

## Synthetic media

The article reports that three seconds of audio is all an AI requires to simulate a person's voice and generate a [[deepfake|deep fake]] of them saying anything, and that a single still photo or brief video clip suffices to fake the corresponding face.

## Mind reading and prediction

Citing [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]] and [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]], the article states that AI will soon be capable of dream mapping and dream interpretation through rapidly improving analysis of functional MRI (fMRI) data — reading brain activity and producing a visual or written rendering of a subject's dreams. "Pull that thread," the source writes, "and you quickly find yourself in *Minority Report*."

## Political consequences

[[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] is quoted predicting that 2024 will be the last human election, with reality collapse becoming severe enough that machines select political leaders. The article dismisses the forecast as belated rather than wrong, holding that leaders "are already selected by machines," and treating AI's election use cases as an indictment of claims that 2020 produced "the safest and most secure elections in history."

The article's recurring literary touchstone is *Westworld*'s question, "Have you ever questioned the nature of your reality?" It concludes that the technology's implications are powerful but that the real terror lies in "this powerful technology solely in the hands of despots" — which, it adds, "is the cry of the globalists."
