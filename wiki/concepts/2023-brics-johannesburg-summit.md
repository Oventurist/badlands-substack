# 2023 BRICS Johannesburg Summit

The 2023 [[brics|BRICS]] summit opened in Johannesburg, South Africa, on August 22, 2023, and became a focal point for expectations that the bloc would formalize an alternative to the dollar-denominated financial order.[1]

## Putin's remote address

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] did not attend in person; Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] took his place in the inaugural "family photo," because the International Criminal Court arrest warrant and the Rome Statute would have obliged the South African government to seek Putin's arrest.[1] Addressing the summit remotely in Russian, Putin said [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] was "gaining momentum" and described the dollar's receding global centrality as an "objective and irreversible" process.[1]

Putin claimed the five BRICS members — Russia, China, India, Brazil and South Africa — were becoming the new world economic leaders, with a cumulative 26% share of global GDP, and that measured by purchasing power parity the bloc had already surpassed the [[g7|Group of Seven]], accounting for 31% of the global economy against 30% for the G7.[1] He said mutual investment among member states had increased sixfold over the preceding decade, their total investments in the world economy had doubled, and cumulative exports accounted for 20% of the global total.[1] He also attacked "illegitimate sanctions" that "seriously weigh on the international economic situation" and the "unlawful freezing of sovereign states' assets," blamed international food shortages on Western sanctions, and said Russia was consistently increasing fuel, food and fertilizer supplies to the Global South — a message calculated to appeal to aspiring BRICS applicants.[1] The summit coincided with Chinese leader [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] urging BRICS to become a geopolitical rival to the G7.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that rumors ahead of the summit included the official announcement of a gold-backed currency to wean BRICS+ nations off fiat, and argued that whether or not such a currency materialized, the announcements already made pointed to the continued strengthening and expansion of economic ties among these risen and rising powers.[1] What Western advocates of modern monetary theory treated as tangential was, in this reading, primary elsewhere: an attack on the U.S. [[petrodollar|petrodollar]], with Saudi Arabia's eventual accession marking the start of "the Final Fiat Countdown."[1]
