# Foreign Agents Bill (El Salvador)

The Foreign Agents Bill is legislation submitted to El Salvador's Legislative Assembly by President [[nayib-bukele|Nayib Bukele]] in May 2025, imposing a 30 percent tax on all donations received by non-governmental organizations operating in the country.[1]

## Background

Bukele announced the bill in response to a protest he characterized as manufactured. He wrote that "humble people were manipulated by self-proclaimed leftist groups and globalist NGOs, whose only real goal is to attack the government," citing the coordinated media presence, obvious crowding and professionally printed banners as evidence.[1] The underlying dispute concerned a case dating to 1998, brought to court in 2008, with a final ruling in favor of the creditor at every instance; Bukele noted that the person who won the case was later charged with fraud by the prosecutor but prevailed again in all criminal proceedings, and that the creditor was reportedly a member of a well-known ARENA party family.[1] He said protesters had been taken at night to demonstrate in front of a private residence unrelated to the case.[1]

## Provisions and rationale

Bukele said the government understood the difficult situation of the 300 affected families and was willing to find a real solution, but argued it would be unfair for Salvadorans who pay rent or housing fees to finance others' land through their taxes.[1] Revenue from the 30 percent donation tax would be used to pay off the housing cooperative's debt, producing what he described as an outcome where "everyone wins": families pay off their debt and keep their homes, the population does not pay for other people's claims, and NGOs finally fulfill "their supposed purpose of helping people."[1]

Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] cited the move approvingly as Bukele "going ham" on NGOs, drawing a parallel to French efforts to curb NGO influence over migration policy.[1]
