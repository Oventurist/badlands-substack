# The Cult of Morpheus

**"The Cult of Morpheus"** is a long-form essay by Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], published free on his Burning Bright Substack and excerpted at length in the February 16, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]].[1] The author described it as his latest and "potentially most controversial" long-form piece, and used it to criticize a segment of the truther community — particularly "Q Decoders" — for intellectual stagnation and for embracing legacy media outlets they once opposed.[1]

## Argument

The essay concedes that the [[qanon|Q drops]] genuinely prompted many of the investigations undertaken by the author and his peers, but argues that the durable learnings about the nature of the System and potential solutions to it came only through a self-propelled process of discovery afterward.[1] For those whose commentary remains limited to analyzing drops from a decade ago without pursuing the macro concepts those drops were attempting to elucidate, the author contends, measurable knowledge has remained static since Q first appeared.[1]

He describes a closed epistemic loop: such commentators frame everything they understand as part of "The Plan" — a plan they cannot fully describe — and everything they do not understand as also part of that same Plan, and therefore as requiring no description.[1] This self-reinforcing structure, he writes, relieves participants of responsibility for independent analysis.[1] He further argues that this "permanent present" reading of the operation is an extraordinarily convenient position for anyone pretending to support the Plan while actually working against it, since such actors need only echo a popular refrain from "the most widely read and disseminated 'secret' plan in history" to be granted presumptive alignment with it.[1]

## Application to Fox News

The practical implication the essay draws is that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] does not use outlets such as [[fox-news|Fox News]] as primary force multipliers because they are secretly packed with white hats delivering coded communications; rather, he is forced to engage with them because significant portions of the awakened audience still consume them — often unironically, or explicitly in the belief that those outlets are vehicles for secret comms.[1] Burning Bright calls this absurd on its face and a slap in the face to a community that once defined itself in direct opposition to the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]], rather than as cheerleaders for a new version of the same monolith, "replete with the same three-letter networks that marched the American Mind through the most recent forever war in the Middle East, and which would gladly do so again."[1]

Continuing to lend attention and energy to these institutions, he argues, perpetuates their relevance within the information ecosystem and compels those working to dismantle the broader System to allocate scarce strategic resources toward them.[1] He closes the excerpt by rejecting the idea of "unironically watching FOX News with people who happily cheered on a jingoist response to the inside job that was 9/11," and who then claim moral superiority over Democrats because "lifelong Democrat Donald Trump chose to put an 'R' next to his name" before embarking on a decades-long effort to free Americans not from the left but from the [[uniparty|Uniparty]].[1]

## Context in the Brief

The excerpt was published alongside commentary on Trump's Valentine's Day 2026 Truth Social post attacking [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]], which Burning Bright found "signal-soaked" chiefly because Trump assigned blame to Republicans for "using [Maher] to show how the Left is coming over our way," adding that "Our Base, the Greatest of All Time, laughs at your weakness when you do it!"[1] Burning Bright read that rebuke as carrying a lesson for the MAGA faithful and for decoders who had latched onto Fox News as full of secret white hats.[1]
