# Operation Prosperity Guardian

**Operation Prosperity Guardian** is the U.S.-led multinational naval coalition announced in December 2023 to safeguard commercial shipping in the Red Sea amid the [[red-sea-shipping-crisis|Red Sea shipping crisis]].[1]

## Announcement and membership

U.S. Secretary of Defense [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] announced the coalition on Tuesday, December 19, 2023, during a trip to the Middle East, naming Britain, Bahrain, Canada, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Seychelles and Spain as participants.[1] Two days later Austin said Greece and Australia had also joined, bringing the total to 20 countries — though he added that at least eight participants had declined to be publicly named.[1] The coalition was to operate as part of the U.S.-led Combined Maritime Forces.[1]

## Partner reservations

Several named allies qualified their involvement, giving the coalition only limited public backing.[1]

France's Defence Ministry said it supported efforts to secure freedom of navigation in the Red Sea and already operated in the region, but stated its ships would remain under French command and did not commit additional naval forces; France maintains a naval base in the United Arab Emirates and 1,500 troops in Djibouti, and its frigate Languedoc was then in the Red Sea.[1]

Italy's Defence Ministry said it would send the frigate Virginio Fasan to the Red Sea to protect national interests in response to specific requests from Italian shipowners, but stated this fell under its existing operations and was not part of Operation Prosperity Guardian.[1]

Spain's Defence Ministry said it would participate only in NATO-led missions or EU-coordinated operations, declaring: "We will not participate unilaterally in the Red Sea operation."[1]

Britain committed the destroyer HMS Diamond to the operation, with its defence ministry noting the coalition would function within the U.S.-led Combined Maritime Forces framework.[1]

## Formation reporting

The formation of the task force was first reported on December 18, 2023, by Politico's chief Pentagon correspondent Lara Seligman, who wrote that "The Pentagon is expected to announce tomorrow the formation of Operation Prosperity Guardian, a new task force to protect shipping from Houthi attacks in the Bab Al-Mandeb Strait and Red Sea, per DOD official."[3] Seligman reported that the operation would sit within the framework of the existing [[combined-maritime-forces|Combined Maritime Force]] 153, a partnership of 39 nations focused on counter-piracy and counter-terrorism in the Red Sea.[3] Austin confirmed shortly afterward that he was "announcing the establishment of Operation Prosperity Guardian, an important new multinational security initiative under the umbrella of the Combined Maritime Forces and the leadership of its Task Force 153."[3]

The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had been seeking to assemble the "broadest possible" coalition amid what had become daily Iran-backed Houthi attacks on commercial vessels transiting the Bab al-Mandab Strait, as well as Houthi rocket and drone launches into southern Israel that prompted US warship intercepts.[3] ZeroHedge framed the launch as the realization of Zoltan Pozsar's prediction that military protection of trade routes would take on a central-bank-like role in the emerging monetary order.[3]

## Collapse of the escort regime (December 2023)

The limits of the operation became apparent at the turn of 2024, when [[maersk|Maersk]] — which had attempted to restart Red Sea sailings under the Prosperity Guardian security umbrella just a day earlier — suspended all container ship transits of the Southern Red Sea after [[houthis|Houthi]] militants attacked the Maersk Hangzhou.[2] [[centcom|US Central Command]] reported that the ship was struck by a missile, that US destroyers shot down two anti-ship ballistic missiles, and that naval helicopters destroyed three Houthi boats attempting to board the vessel, in what CENTCOM counted as the 23rd illegal attack on commercial shipping since 19 November.[2] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] had already characterised Prosperity Guardian as an "utter failure" before its launch, and described the December events as the failure cascade of the Biden administration carrying over into 2024.[2]
