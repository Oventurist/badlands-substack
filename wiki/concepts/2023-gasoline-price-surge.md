# 2023 Gasoline Price Surge

The 2023 gasoline price surge was a run-up in U.S. fuel costs in September 2023 that pushed prices at the pump to a record high for that point in the calendar year.[1]

## Oil market drivers

Oil prices in the United States jumped back above $90 a barrel for the first time since November 2022, sending worrying signals to the Federal Reserve and the White House.[1] The surge in West Texas Intermediate dragged gasoline prices at the pump dramatically higher, and because of the lag in the supply chain, pump prices appeared set to rise further.[1]

## Record seasonal highs

Despite the end of the peak summer driving season, gas prices — already a factor in pushing up inflation in August — were expected to keep climbing through the following week because of the spike in oil prices.[1] Pump prices stood at a record high for that time of year, surpassing prior seasonal highs set in 2022, 2012, and 2008.[1] According to AAA, at least a dozen states had gas prices averaging $4 a gallon or higher, including Colorado, North Dakota, and California.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that gas prices hit record highs while [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] continued to tout [[bidenomics|Bidenomics]] and to insist to the electorate that the economy was good.[1] She singled out Biden's claim that he was "laser-focused on cutting energy costs, including by investing in clean energy to bolster our energy security," arguing that in practice the pain would continue and prices would climb as the administration pursued clean-energy solutions lacking current, scalable infrastructure.[1] She noted that the United States had been energy independent only two and a half years earlier and attributed the situation to "the stolen election."[1]
