# Leading Against White Supremacy Act of 2023

The Leading Against White Supremacy Act of 2023 was a bill introduced in the 118th Congress by Rep. [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson Lee]] (D-Texas) that would criminalize certain social media postings said to support "white supremacy" or "replacement theory."[1]

## Content and criticism

Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] described the measure as "an almost impenetrable word salad of convoluted provisions," writing that the only clear thing about it was that it would gut the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] and create effective thought crimes.[1] Turley judged that the bill was not going to pass, but said its anti-free-speech elements were deeply disturbing because they reflect successful speech-criminalization efforts in other countries, and echo elements of a bill he had testified against in the Senate the previous year.[1] He situated the proposal within a broader anti-free-speech movement growing among writers, journalists, academics and Democratic members of Congress, some of whom openly call for censorship and manipulation of what citizens see and read.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the totalitarian modus operandi requires censorship of truth, because freedom and fairness are hardwired into the human psyche and people would reject the authoritarian agenda if they saw it transparently.[1] He contended the bill would give censors a blank check to suppress anything deemed a threat to the globalist agenda, and that — as shown by the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] — the people pressing the censorship button are often low-grade thinkers untrained to carefully define white supremacy or apply legal standards.[1] He added that definitions of white supremacy and hate speech are never clear and are intentionally vague so they can be applied to almost any situation, which he offered as an easy tell for identifying a globalist policy.[1]
