# Public Banking Option

The "public banking option" is the concept of a publicly owned banking institution operating alongside private banks, holding citizens' deposits and lending in the public interest. Badlands Media treats it as one of the key structural safeguards capable of transferring power from private bankers back to the people.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Origins in the article's framing

The concept is traced to [[jose-figueres-ferrer|José "Pepe" Figueres]] of Costa Rica, who created a permanent public banking option that prevented private banks from controlling the government, alongside a citizens-only voter ID that permanently protected elections.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Publicly owned banks, in this account, protect the people's savings from being stolen or controlled by private bankers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## The Costa Rican model

Figueres nationalized the banking system after the [[costa-rican-civil-war|Costa Rican Civil War]], a move described as taking power over the government away from the cabal's puppet masters and one that made [[costa-rica|Costa Rica]] a target within the global financial system.[2] His answer was permanent public banking competition: banks owned and controlled by the people, forcing private banks to compete against institutions whose purpose is protecting depositors' money rather than generating profit.[2]

Citing the [[world-business-academy|World Business Academy]] and resident Scott Bidstrup, the article reports that of 29 licensed banks, mutual associations and credit unions in Costa Rica, four were established as national, publicly-owned banks in 1949 and have remained in public hands ever since, despite enormous pressure from the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] and the United States to privatize them.[2] Countless private institutions came and went over three decades, with depositors losing most of the value of their accounts, while the four state banks compete fiercely with one another, have suffered no failures in 31 years, and now hold fully 80% of all retail deposits.[2] The Academy material adds that this stability is a principal reason Costa Rica leads its region in attracting direct foreign investment, since most money is held in banks carrying the full faith and credit of the Republic and is therefore available for lending, while neighbours with fully privatized systems lurch from crisis to crisis.[2]

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]], the corrupt US government and the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]] hate this competition and have been unable to stop it; although the cabal damaged Costa Rica's economy when the world was forced onto the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] and the [[world-bank|World Bank]] imposed austerity, the people's savings remained protected in the publicly owned banks.[2] He treats the arrangement as proof that giving the people power over the banking system also ensures they retain power over their government.[2] In the same article he notes that the [[bank-of-north-dakota|Bank of North Dakota]] is the only public depository bank in the United States and that few people have private access to it, and forecasts that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] will create an American public banking option as one of the safeguards protecting the constitution after he leaves office.[2]

## American precedents

The United States has two principal precedents. The [[bank-of-north-dakota|Bank of North Dakota]], founded in 1919 and owned by the residents of the state, remains the only publicly owned bank in the continental United States and has survived the Great Depression, the 1980s Savings and Loan scandal and the 2008 credit crisis.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) It returns part of its profits to the state general fund — more than a billion dollars over its history — lowering taxes, funding infrastructure and offering low-cost student loans.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]], operating from 1911 to 1967, offered no-cost interest-bearing accounts at post offices nationwide and proved uniquely safe and reliable during the Great Depression.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Rationale

Rather than having public funds extracted from local communities to fuel Wall Street speculation, public banks can ensure those funds stabilize local economies and support local public priorities.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Ellen Brown, chair of the Public Banking Institute, argues that a public banking system can fund the goods, services and infrastructure needed by the people and the economy without unsustainable debt, taxation or environmental degradation.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The article lists conditions it says are common to both the Gilded Age that produced the BND and the present: corporations in bed with government, an economy strangled by massive debt, huge inequality between rich and poor, and a corrupt government in which nobody genuinely attempts reform.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

Public banking is also presented as a remedy for financial exclusion, eliminating reliance on predatory non-bank options such as payday lenders and check-cashing firms used by the 8.4 million unbanked and 24.2 million underbanked U.S. households.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Current movement

A movement to create a public bank has gained prominence in California since 2018, and efforts have been made in nearly two dozen other states, with New York, New Mexico, New Hampshire and Massachusetts introducing bills in a single year — none of which passed.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media argues that Trump could create a nationwide public option quickly by reviving postal banking and pairing it with [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]], and lists a public postal banking option and FedAccounts among four initiatives it attributes to him alongside a return to the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] and a behind-the-scenes push for a digital dollar.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)
