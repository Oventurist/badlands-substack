# 2024 Trump Return to Capitol Hill

On Thursday, June 13, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] returned to Capitol Hill to meet with congressional Republicans — his first appearance with lawmakers since the January 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol.[1] The Associated Press described it as a triumphant return, with energized House and Senate Republicans reinvigorated by his bid to retake the [[white-house|White House]].[1]

## The meetings

Trump arrived as the party's presumptive nominee despite pending federal charges for conspiring to overturn the 2020 election and a recent guilty verdict in an unrelated hush-money trial.[1] A packed room of House Republicans sang "Happy Birthday" to him at a private breakfast at GOP campaign headquarters across the street from the Capitol, presenting him with a baseball and bat from the annual congressional game; senators later produced an American flag cake with "45" candles, then "47," a reference to the next presidency.[1] Trump boasted that even his telephone rallies for lawmakers drew bigger crowds than pop star [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]], who had made no endorsement.[1] A separate meeting with GOP lawmakers at the Capitol Hill Club the same day produced his [[trump-all-tariff-policy-proposal|all-tariff policy]] remarks.[1]

## Coverage and Badlands critique

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the AP reporter appeared to be attempting snark but landed on "schizophrenic paranoia."[1] She noted that the lead sentence called the visit triumphant while in the same breath describing it as Trump's first since sending the mob to "fight like hell" ahead of the January 6 attack.[1] Several Republicans were quoted in support — Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]], and [[john-thune|John Thune]] — while only two Democrats appeared, of whom only [[pete-aguilar|Pete Aguilar]] was a legislator relevant to the story; the second was [[harry-dunn|Harry Dunn]], whom she characterized as a disgraced former police officer and Democrat political influencer, evidence that the piece was political theater rather than news.[1]

Ashe rejected the article's claim that Trump "has successfully purged the GOP of critics, silenced most skeptics and enticed once-critical lawmakers aboard his MAGA-fueled campaign."[1] Voters, not Trump, removed critics such as Liz Cheney and [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]], she argued, asking whether the AP believes voters should choose their representatives.[1] She called the "silenced most skeptics" claim unfounded, noting that outlets like the AP will not stop publishing warnings about what Trump would do if elected.[1] On "MAGA-fueled," she countered that a campaign fueled by making America great again is a winning campaign and that opposing it is a bad look.[1]

She tied the coverage to the [[2024-european-parliament-elections|European Parliament results]], arguing populism is rising because globalists overplayed their hand and abused their power, while the AP denies that the people's rebuke of their handlers is democracy in action.[1] Her chosen example of "gaslighting panic" was the article's line that as democracies come under threat from a far-rightward shift, experts warn the US system, once seemingly immune from authoritarian impulses, is at risk from populist and extremist forces of the kind Trump inspired to sack the Capitol — anonymous experts, she noted, shielding authoritarians who gave in to their worst impulses long ago.[1] She observed that the piece ran under "Politics" rather than "Opinion," a few paragraphs above the [[associated-press|Associated Press]] boast of being the most trusted source of information on election night with a history of accuracy dating to 1848.[1]
