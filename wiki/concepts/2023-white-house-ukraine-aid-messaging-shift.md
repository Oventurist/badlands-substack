# 2023 White House Ukraine Aid Messaging Shift

The 2023 White House Ukraine aid messaging shift refers to a change in how the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration publicly justified continued funding for [[ukraine]], reported by [[politico|POLITICO]] in October 2023.[1] Rather than framing the aid primarily as a national security imperative, the [[white-house|White House]] began urging lawmakers to sell the war effort abroad as a potential economic boom at home.[1]

## The talking points

Aides distributed talking points to Democrats and Republicans supportive of continued Ukraine funding, making the case that such spending was good for American jobs, according to five White House aides and lawmakers familiar with the effort who spoke on condition of anonymity.[1] The push was first previewed publicly in President Biden's Oval Office address the prior week and came ahead of the election of a new House Speaker, with the White House invoking patriotism to convince holdout Republicans to back not only Kyiv but a larger package including funds for [[israel]].[1]

The documents obtained by POLITICO stated: "As we replenish our stocks of weapons, we are partnering with the U.S. defense industry to increase our capacity and meet the needs of the U.S. and our allies both now and in the future."[1] They further claimed the supplemental request "invests over $50 billion in the American defense industrial base — ensuring our military continues to be the most ready, capable, and best equipped fighting force the world has ever seen — and expanding production lines, strengthening the American economy and creating new American jobs."[1] The talking points amounted to an implicit admission that the administration had work to do selling its $106 billion foreign aid supplemental request, and that framing it purely under national security had not worked.[1] See [[2023-supplemental-foreign-aid-request]].

## Commentary

[[badlands-media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the shift as a movement of the Overton window driven by the [[military-industrial-complex]], noting that even "MSM stooges" at POLITICO had caught on to the abrupt change in messaging as a bipartisan American public moved away from open-ended support for the Ukraine proxy war against the Russian Federation.[1] He sketched the progression as a sequence of rhetorical stages — deny ("we must defend Ukrainian democracy"), diminish ("we must stop Russian expansion... into lands populated by ethnic Russians"), qualify ("Ukraine isn't really a democracy, but it's the lesser of two evils"), distract ("look what they're doing to Israel — where's that new spending package?"), and finally admit ("Ukraine is corrupt from top to bottom, but the Military Industrial Complex could use some new contracts").[1] He concluded that [[donald-trump]] had stopped the "Forever War Machine" and that the narrative war was being lost by its promoters, so that "the mask is slipping, and the fangs are coming out."[1]
