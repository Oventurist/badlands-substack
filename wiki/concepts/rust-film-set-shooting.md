# Rust film set shooting

The Rust film set shooting was the fatal shooting of cinematographer [[halyna-hutchins|Halyna Hutchins]] on the New Mexico set of the Western film *Rust* on October 21, 2021, and the criminal proceedings that followed.[1]

## The shooting

[[alec-baldwin|Alec Baldwin]], the film's star and a co-producer, was pointing a revolver at Hutchins during a rehearsal in a small church on the set at Bonanza Creek Ranch when the gun discharged, killing her and wounding director Joel Souza. Baldwin has maintained that he pulled back the hammer but did not pull the trigger, and that the weapon fired on its own.[1]

It has never been officially determined who brought the live rounds that killed Hutchins onto the set.[1]

## Prosecutions

Armorer [[hannah-gutierrez-reed|Hannah Gutierrez-Reed]] was tried first; prosecutors alleged she was responsible for the live rounds. She was convicted of involuntary manslaughter and sentenced to 18 months in prison.[1]

Nearly three years after the shooting, Baldwin went on trial for felony involuntary manslaughter, facing the same 18-month maximum sentence if a jury convicted him unanimously. Coverage identified two dominant themes expected at trial: the chaotic atmosphere on the set, and the technical details of the Italian-made classic revolver Baldwin pointed at Hutchins.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] placed the televised Baldwin trial alongside the Karen Read trial in Massachusetts — which [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] had called the "info war for wine moms" — and the Young Thug trial as a genre of culturally polarizing court drama.[1] She rejected Baldwin's hammer-not-trigger account as inconsistent with how firearms function, noted that a witness in the case claimed to have seen him pull the trigger, and observed that the source of the live rounds remained unrevealed and might not emerge in court.[1]

Ashe predicted Baldwin would likely avoid accountability, noting he had assembled a legal team of mostly Harvard lawyers to face a local New Mexico prosecution, and argued the case could become "the biggest red pill of all recent cultural court dramas" if the 26-year-old armorer served a manslaughter sentence while the famous actor who held the gun went free.[1]
