# 2026 Iran Protests and Internet Blackout

In January 2026 protests and labor strikes spread across [[iran|Iran]], with demonstrations reported in Tehran and other cities amid economic grievances and political tensions.[1] Authorities responded by imposing widespread internet restrictions while deploying security forces, including riot police and plainclothes officers, to protest areas.[1]

## Casualties and official response

Non-governmental organizations reported dozens of people killed nationwide in clashes between protesters and security forces, though Iranian authorities released no independent casualty figures and did not confirm a death toll.[1] State media acknowledged the unrest and strikes but characterized the protests as limited and blamed foreign actors for inciting violence.[1] The demonstrations escalated in the wake of the anniversary of the killing of [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]], which officials cited as a reason for heightened security measures across the country.[1]

## The regime-change question

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry contrasted President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 campaign remarks on Iranian regime change — made on the Patrick Bet-David podcast — with his January 2026 appearance on the Hugh Hewitt Show, where Trump said he did not think [[reza-pahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]], son of the deposed Shah, would be an appropriate leader for Iran.[1] The commentary read this as throwing cold water on factions it labeled NeoCons and Zionists that had been promoting Pahlavi, noting his repeated trips to Israel and meetings with Benjamin Netanyahu.[1]

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicted that regime change in Iran was unlikely, while allowing for a scenario in which the religious elements of the government — the Ayatollah, the mullahs, and the IRGC — are expunged while the socially moderate political administration remains in power.[1] He further expected Republican members of [[congress|Congress]] to spend the episode shilling for regime change and forever war.[1]

## Subsidence and the color-revolution allegation

By mid-January 2026 the nationwide protests appeared to have been smothered following a security crackdown, according to Israeli media, while Iranian authorities accused the United States and Israel of encouraging the unrest.[2] President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] warned that foreign interference would be met with a response while calling for internal calm, and reporting indicated that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had conveyed through [[backchannel-diplomacy|backchannels]] that the US was not preparing to strike Iran.[2] Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] disputed the de-escalation narrative, claiming the regime was still killing protesters at an alarming rate.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that elements of the American and Israeli governments had attempted to launch a regime-change revolution, encouraged by Trump's rhetoric and by the raid that removed Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[2] In his account, Trump escalated his rhetoric theatrically once the protests took off and declared that the US military would likely intervene if anyone were killed — a promise that, he argued, needed only the appearance of atrocity to manufacture consensus for intervention, and whose very telegraphing signaled that Trump had no intention of following through.[2]

He further contended that the reported violence — decapitated police officers, hundreds of mosques and ambulances burned, molotov cocktails thrown at government buildings — resembled the work of [[isis|ISIS]] rather than of regime forces, and rejected the claim that ISIS is an Iranian proxy on the grounds that it was created by the [[cia|CIA]], [[mi6|MI6]], and [[mossad|Mossad]] to overthrow the Syrian government and adheres to Sunni rather than Shia Islam.[2] On this reading, Mossad/CIA/ISIS operatives were baited into deploying their [[color-revolution|color revolution]] playbook only for the expected support systems never to materialize, leaving provocateurs exposed.[2]

Ghost noted that Iranian leadership had begun disclosing Israeli and Western intelligence involvement while [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] advocated diplomacy, and cited Serbian President Aleksandar Vučić and Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] as foreign voices attributing the failing revolution to the CIA and Mossad.[2] He predicted a high likelihood that the Iranian government would recover hard evidence of that involvement, and asked what would follow when such evidence was presented to the world and to the American people.[2]

## Escalation, casualty figures, and the carrier gap (January 2026)

By mid-January 2026 protests that began over economic grievances, including the collapse of the rial in late December, had escalated into widespread unrest involving armed clashes, attacks on security forces, and vandalism in multiple cities.[3] Death toll reports varied significantly: one US-based rights group cited 646 total deaths, including over 500 protesters and 133 security personnel, while Iranian officials claimed at least 109 security forces killed by armed rioters, and unverified claims suggested higher protester casualties.[3]

The Iranian government imposed widespread internet and communications blackouts, throttled services, jammed signals, and confiscated [[starlink|Starlink]] equipment to restrict protest coordination and global visibility.[3] Tehran accused the United States and Israel of orchestrating the violence through trained agents, summoned European ambassadors from France, Germany, Italy, and Britain to view videos depicting armed rioters committing arson, gunfire, and attacks on officers, and demanded an end to foreign support for the protesters.[3]

[[donald-trump|President Trump]] publicly urged Iranians to continue protesting and to seize institutions, while threatening potential U.S. military intervention in response to crackdowns.[3] Israeli and Arab officials reportedly advised Washington to delay any strikes on [[iran|Iran]], arguing the regime was not yet sufficiently weakened for military action to achieve regime change, and recommending enhanced sanctions, cyberattacks, targeted killings, or support for bypassing communications restrictions instead.[3]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that after Trump ordered the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group out of the eastern Mediterranean on November 18 to the Caribbean for the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] operation, no naval presence remained within striking distance of Iran — an observation he said regime analysts were now echoing.[3] He argued Trump was encouraging paid provocateurs to escalate immediately even though Israeli media acknowledged no enthusiasm for military intervention inside Iran or around the Middle East.[3] He also observed that the Israelis had requested restoration of Iranian internet access so the population could receive hasbara propaganda, and questioned whether [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s offer of free Starlink to Iranians would allow interested parties, with the right AI tools, to track agitators in real time.[3]

## Escalation and the Moscow exit strategy (January 2026)

By early January 2026 nationwide protests continued across [[iran|Iran]], spanning multiple cities and provinces including major urban centers, driven by economic grievances such as high inflation, rising cost of living and currency depreciation.[4] The protests spread rapidly over the first weekend of the year, drawing in students, traders and pensioners, and chants turned political — including "Death to the dictator," a reference to Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]].[4] Sanam Vakil of [[chatham-house|Chatham House]] told NBC News that Iranians had united under external bombardment without abandoning their grievances, since the two feelings "are not mutually exclusive."[4]

The [[human-rights-activists-news-agency|Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA)]] reported that security forces used force including live ammunition in some locations, claiming at least 20 deaths — among them at least one member of the security forces — along with 51 citizen injuries and 990 arrests across 222 locations; Badlands cautioned that HRANA's figures, though relied upon across legacy media, differ from official accounts.[4] Khamenei condemned the demonstrators, saying "a number of agitated people, enemy mercenaries, had positioned themselves behind bazaar merchants," insisting "protest is legitimate, but protest is different from rioting," and that "rioters must be put in their place."[4] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on Truth Social that if Iran "shots and violently kills peaceful protesters… the United States of America will come to their rescue. We are locked and loaded and ready to go."[4] British media, citing unnamed Iranian intelligence sources, reported that Khamenei planned to flee to Moscow if he lost control of the country.[4]

Badlands read the events as a color revolution linked to the [[operation-absolute-resolve|Venezuela operation]]. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that days after [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] publicly tied the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] government to Iran, former CIA director [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] insinuated the protests were a [[mossad|Mossad]] operation, and highlighted a resurfaced 2009 documentary clip in which [[isaac-herzog|Isaac Herzog]] named Iran and [[venezuela|Venezuela]] as the two greatest threats to Israel before deferring further discussion off camera.[4] An Iranian police chief claimed arrested protest organizers had confessed to being paid, and Israeli journalist Hallel Rosen said on Israeli television that Venezuela was "the last rehearsal before Iran."[4] Badlands also recalled [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]]'s opposition to neoconservative regime-change wars and contrasted it with [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], who — appearing beside [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] aboard Air Force One — called for a complete overhaul of [[cuba|Cuba]] under American business leadership.[4]
