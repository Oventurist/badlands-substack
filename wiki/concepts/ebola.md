# Ebola

**Ebola** is a severe viral hemorrhagic disease caused by several distinct species of ebolavirus. In the Badlands Media corpus it appears both as the subject of a 2026 outbreak in Central Africa and as a recurring reference point in arguments about pandemic messaging.

## The 2026 outbreak

According to the Badlands Brief of May 27, 2026, an Ebola outbreak centered in Ituri province in northeastern the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]] was spreading faster than response efforts could contain it. The outbreak involves the [[bundibugyo-virus|Bundibugyo virus]] strain and had expanded into North Kivu and South Kivu provinces as well as into [[uganda|Uganda]], with confirmed cases in major population centers including Goma and Kampala. No vaccines or treatments exist for the Bundibugyo strain.

The [[international-rescue-committee|International Rescue Committee]] warned that "the warning signs are flashing red," and [[world-health-organization|WHO]] Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]] said delayed detection meant authorities were "playing catch-up with a very fast-moving epidemic." Response measures included intensified surveillance, patient isolation, contact monitoring and scaled-up operations by the WHO and the Congolese and Ugandan governments, with roughly $500 million pledged. Congolese officials described the affected provinces as "well contained."

## Other appearances

Ebola is also listed among the pathogen labels — alongside HIV, tuberculosis, malaria and hepatitis — reported by the [[select-committee-on-the-chinese-communist-party|Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party]] on vials recovered from the Reedley, California laboratory tied to [[jia-bei-zhu|Jia Bei Zhu]].

## Skeptical framing in the corpus

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responds to the outbreak coverage by questioning its timing, asking whether global think tanks that predicted increasingly frequent pandemics are "so good at predicting stuff … or they're planning stuff," and arguing that authorities appear to be "throwing all the public health crises at the wall to see what sticks." This is the source's contested commentary rather than a claim about the epidemiology.
