# Blitzkrieg

**Blitzkrieg** ("lightning war") is the German military doctrine of rapid, concentrated, combined-arms offensives intended to shatter an opponent's ability to mount a coherent defense. In Badlands analysis it is treated as a synthesis of two earlier Prussian strands: the [[preventive-war|preventive war]] doctrine associated with [[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]] and the [[maneuver-warfare|maneuver warfare]] concepts developed by [[helmuth-von-moltke|Helmuth von Moltke]].[1]

## Doctrinal origins

Frederick the Great, in the latter 1700s, adopted a preventive-war posture in which a perceived enemy is struck before reaching full strength, using multiple simultaneous attacks to disorient and overwhelm and thereby prevent an organized defense.[1] The theoretical aim was to deliver a stunning blow that forced a negotiated surrender rather than to annihilate the enemy through a prolonged campaign.[1] Moltke, a 19th-century Prussian field marshal, transformed doctrine by exploiting railways to deploy troops quickly and outflank enemies in a double envelopment, though he still valued prolonged siege as the decisive phase.[1]

The Nazis combined Moltke's maneuver concepts with Frederick's preventive-war logic to produce blitzkrieg, twisting the idea of "bloodying the nose" into an attempt at total annihilation through rapid brute force.[1] Frederick himself soured on preventive war in old age, remarking that "preventive war is like suicide for fear of death" — a caution the Nazi high command ignored to its own cost.[1]

## Critique and modern relevance

Badlands commentary argues that blitzkrieg fails against opponents who have developed elastic "shoulder defense" techniques, as the Russians did, absorbing and enveloping the penetrating thrust.[1] The concept resurfaced in June 2024 reporting on Israeli planning against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon, in which Israeli officials reportedly argued to the United States that they could pull off a "blitzkrieg," while US officials warned that a limited campaign could not be guaranteed.[1] Given Hezbollah's reported alignment with the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] and the Russian military, the Badlands take framed the prospect as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] adopting the most infamous and historically doomed Nazi field tactics against an adversary schooled in the countermeasure.[1] Analysts have also compared President George W. Bush's invasion of Iraq to the younger Frederick's preventive-war doctrine.[1]
