# Venezuelan Devolution

"Venezuelan Devolution" is a theory advanced by Badlands Media commentators holding that the removal of Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in January 2026 was not a regime collapse but a deliberate, negotiated [[continuity-of-government|continuity of government]] (COG) arrangement analogous to the [[devolution|Devolution]] theory applied to the United States.[1]

## Origin of the theory

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] presented the theory on Devolution Power Hour alongside [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], arguing that the Venezuelan government — with Maduro's blessing and constitutional authority as president — had entered a COG contingency plan.[1] Under this account the plan was negotiated through the Qataris, with Special Presidential Envoy for Special Missions [[richard-grenell|Ric Grenell]] acting as intermediary, and approved by [[donald-trump|President Trump]].[1] The Miami Herald reported on a related rumor in October.[1]

## Supporting indicators

Proponents point to several signals: Venezuela's Supreme Court ordering Vice President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] to assume the acting presidency "in the absence" of Maduro to ensure continuity of government; Rodríguez's swearing-in at the National Assembly on January 5, 2026 while Maduro appeared in a New York court on federal drug charges; [[nicolas-maduro-guerra|Nicolás Maduro Guerra]] publicly affirming that COG was in effect and holding the Bible at the swearing-in; and Rodríguez's immediate clarification that Maduro remained the true president — a claim Maduro echoed in court.[1]

## The sanctions rationale

Asked by Jon Herold why Maduro would go through the "song and dance" of removing himself, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry ties the answer to [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s announced imminent lifting of the sanctions Trump imposed during his first term.[1] Because [[executive-order-13850|Executive Order 13850]] paused rather than terminated the [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] fraud litigation, lifting sanctions would reopen discovery and restart monitored bank wires, generating new Suspicious Activity Reports and investigations.[1] In this reading, Maduro's ouster produced the optics that allow sanctions relief, opening "the floodgates for discovery via lawfare" to link banks and oil companies to the cartels.[1]

## The narrative-warfare reading

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] frames the episode as narrative engineering by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], arguing that the regime was "tweaked instead of changed" while the global regime was permanently blocked from executing its own regime-change operation.[1] In this account Maduro "fell on the story's sword" because he was the prime villain and narrative fulcrum on which the rebellion storyline rested, making it very difficult for the regime-change network to manufacture a public mandate against his deputies and install a WEF-aligned successor — though Burning Bright allows that Trump could still rug-pull the narrative and see [[maria-corina-machado|Machado]] or someone similar installed.[1] He also notes Russia's pre-emptive offer of asylum to Maduro following Trump's earlier promises of amnesty, and criticizes members of the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] for cheering the arrest using neoconservative framing.[1] Without such a "change event," he argues, there is no buy-in to an actual paradigm shift.[1]

## Oil and economic dimension

Trump told The New York Times the U.S. would be "using oil" and "taking oil" from Venezuela and that oversight of the oil sector would last much longer than a year.[1] The Wall Street Journal reported a plan to use Venezuelan reserves to push U.S. consumer prices toward roughly $50 a barrel by expanding output.[1] Bessent told Reuters additional sanctions could be lifted "as soon as next week" to facilitate oil sales and economic recovery, and said he planned to meet leaders of the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] and the [[world-bank|World Bank]] about re-engagement with Venezuela.[1]
