# US–Türkiye Memorandum of Understanding on Strategic Civil Nuclear Cooperation

The Memorandum of Understanding on Strategic Civil Nuclear Cooperation is an agreement signed by [[turkey|Türkiye]] and the United States on September 25, 2025, during President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]]'s visit to the [[white-house|White House]].[1]

## Signing

Turkish Energy Minister [[alparslan-bayraktar|Alparslan Bayraktar]] announced the deal on X, writing that the two countries had "started a new process that will further deepen the deep-rooted and multidimensional partnership between Türkiye and the United States in the field of nuclear energy," and that the memorandum was signed with U.S. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] in the presence of the leaders after their meeting.[1] The Turkish Energy Ministry did not release further details, but Turkish officials indicated that talks between the two countries covered the construction of large-scale nuclear power plants and small modular reactors.[1]

The nuclear memorandum followed another large-scale energy transaction concluded during the visit, under which the Turkish state pipeline operator BOTAS signed contracts for a combined 75.8 billion cubic meters of LNG imports with Swiss-based Mercuria and Australia's Woodside Energy.[1]

## Commentary and context

Badlands commentary framed the agreement as a "narrative deployment" landing 24 hours before Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was scheduled to address the United Nations.[1] Contributors noted that [[russia|Russia]] was already constructing a nuclear reactor for Turkey through [[rosatom|Rosatom]] — the same company said to be planning reactors in Kazakhstan, [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], Mali and other nations associated with the Sovereign Alliance thesis, and which EU authorities have accused of funding a populist Italian political party.[1] Erdogan was described as delivering on a 2023 campaign promise to make Turkey more energy independent, while also having voiced frustration that Turkey has not been permitted to develop a nuclear weapons program — a posture the commentary expected to alarm Netanyahu given Turkey's Muslim-majority population and hostility toward Israel.[1]
