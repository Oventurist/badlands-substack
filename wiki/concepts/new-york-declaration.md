# New York Declaration

The New York Declaration, also referred to as the Saudi-French Two-State Solution proposal, is the diplomatic framework identified by [[badlands-media|Badlands]] analysts as the explicit foundation of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s formal proposal to end the war in [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## Relationship to the Trump peace plan

When Trump unveiled his Gaza peace plan at a White House press conference alongside Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] on 29 September 2025, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the main takeaway was that the proposal was explicitly based on the New York Declaration.[1] The observation cuts against the framing offered by supporters of the Israeli position, since the declaration is a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]] instrument of the kind that Israel and what Badlands writers call the Western globalist hegemon had emphatically rejected.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] made the same point from a different angle, noting that Trump's 20-point peace plan looked "an AWFUL lot like a two-state solution" and that the episode therefore represented submission — but not by the side most observers expected to submit.[1]

## Tammy Bruce dismissal claim

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry has argued that the New York Declaration was the proximate cause of Tammy Bruce's removal from the [[state-department|State Department]], contending that she was fired after disrespecting the proposal in July 2025.[1] The claim is offered as evidence that the declaration carried far more weight inside the administration than public rhetoric from the US delegation suggested.[1]

## General Assembly endorsement (September 2025)

On Friday, September 12, 2025, the [[united-nations-general-assembly|United Nations General Assembly]] voted overwhelmingly to endorse the declaration, which outlines "tangible, timebound, and irreversible steps" toward a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]] between [[israel|Israel]] and the Palestinians ahead of a meeting of world leaders.[2] The seven-page text resulted from an international conference held at the UN in July, hosted by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[france|France]]; the United States and Israel boycotted that event.[2] The resolution endorsing the declaration received 142 votes in favor and 10 against, with 12 abstentions, ahead of a September 22 leaders' meeting on the sidelines of the high-level General Assembly at which Britain, France, Canada, Australia and Belgium were expected to formally recognize a Palestinian state.[2]

The declaration, endorsed by the 193-member body, condemns the attacks against Israel by [[hamas]] on October 7, 2023, which triggered the war in Gaza, and also condemns Israeli attacks against civilians and civilian infrastructure in Gaza, along with siege and starvation, "which have resulted in a devastating humanitarian catastrophe and protection crisis."[2]

### Terms and US reaction

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] recounted that the Saudis assembled the agreement in July with the French, the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], and the [[arab-league|Arab League]], demanding that Hamas surrender itself to the Palestinian Authority, turn over its weapons, release the hostages, and that its leadership leave Gaza forever.[2] He criticized US Representative to the UN [[morgan-ortagus|Morgan Ortagus]] for calling the declaration a "publicity stunt" and a "gift to Hamas" — reading [[tammy-bruce|Tammy Bruce]]'s July statement word-for-word — then stipulating as the only acceptable outcome precisely the terms the declaration already contained.[2] He concluded that the [[state-department|State Department]] is "a disgrace" and that its representatives should be replaced.[2]
