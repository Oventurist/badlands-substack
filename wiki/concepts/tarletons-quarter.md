# Tarleton's Quarter

"Tarleton's Quarter" was the phrase American colonists coined for the practice of refusing quarter — killing men who had surrendered — after [[banastre-tarleton|Banastre Tarleton]]'s dragoons cut down a surrendering force at the [[waxhaws-massacre|Waxhaws]] in 1780.[1]

## Origin and use

After the American commander raised the white flag, Tarleton drew his saber and led a charge through the ranks, leaving 113 killed, 150 severely wounded and about 200 prisoners within minutes.[1] The story became widespread throughout the colonies, and the bloodlust it represented was thereafter known as "Tarleton's Quarter."[1] The article stresses that Waxhaw was hardly the first or last occasion on which Tarleton demonstrated such reckless hatred toward the colonists.[1]

The phrase belongs to a broader escalation in which, the article argues, patriot militias responded with equal brutality and fewer and fewer prisoners were taken on either side of the South Carolina civil war.[1] Against this backdrop [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]]'s conspicuously good treatment of prisoners became a strategic asset, since Tory leaders who saw the war's outcome coming would surrender specifically to him, knowing he would not hang them.[1]
