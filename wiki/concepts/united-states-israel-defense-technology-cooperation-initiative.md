# United States-Israel Defense Technology Cooperation Initiative

The United States-Israel Defense Technology Cooperation Initiative is a program proposed as Section 224 of the [[house-armed-services-committee|House Armed Services Committee]]'s draft Fiscal Year 2027 [[national-defense-authorization-act|National Defense Authorization Act]].[1]

## Scope

The provision would authorize expanded collaboration between the United States and [[israel|Israel]] in defense research and development, weapons co-production, joint ventures, and licensing agreements, and would extend cooperation into emerging technology fields including artificial intelligence, quantum technologies, autonomous systems, directed energy, cyber capabilities, and biotechnology.[1] It also references "network integration" and "data fusion" between the two countries.[1] The two governments already cooperate on missile defense; the initiative would broaden that cooperation into additional defense technology sectors.[1]

## Analysis and controversy

A brief by [[steven-simon|Steven Simon]] for the [[quincy-institute-for-responsible-statecraft|Quincy Institute for Responsible Statecraft]] argued that deeper defense-industrial integration of this kind could shift the bilateral relationship away from annual military aid votes in [[congress|Congress]] and into defense acquisition processes that are less publicly visible.[1]

Critics characterized the draft as a quiet move to integrate the U.S. and Israeli militaries.[1] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] concluded the accusation was overblown after examining the document, and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] deferred to that reading while arguing that the political optics were disastrous — that introducing such a bill while Congress was already being accused of subservience to Israel was a self-inflicted narrative wound.[1]
