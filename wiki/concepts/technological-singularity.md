# Technological Singularity

The technological singularity is the hypothetical future point at which the technical capabilities and rate of expansion of an artificial intelligence become uncontrollable and irreversible. The concept is treated at length in the July 2023 Badlands Media article "Artificial Intelligence: Hysteria vs. Reason" by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], which argues that the threshold may already have been crossed.

## Origins

The article traces the idea to a 1958 memorial article by the mathematician Stanislaw Ulam recounting a conversation with the Hungarian-American mathematician John von Neumann. The discussion, Ulam wrote, "centered on the accelerating progress of technology and changes in the mode of human life, which gives the appearance of approaching some essential singularity in the history of the race beyond which human affairs, as we know them, could not continue." The source observes that AI researchers sixty-five years later concur.

## Contemporary assessments

[[mo-gawdat|Mo Gawdat]], former Chief Business Officer of [[google-x|Google X]], argued in a 2018 interview that regulation and programming constraints would not hold, because "there will be a point in singularity where those machines will decide for their own benefit what it is that they need to learn next," at which moment "it will be too late." By 2023 Gawdat had concluded that the point was behind us: "the genie is out of the bottle."

The article states that all AI researchers agree a singularity will be reached, while the definition of that point — and whether it has already occurred — remains disputed. It also notes an important divergence: most researchers dismiss the "AGI apocalypse," the notion of an evil sentient machine exterminating humanity, as highly unlikely. "They don't fear the machine – they fear those wielding the power of the machine," the article says. "In other words, the machine is powerful, but the machine is a tool in the hands of men."

## Cultural framing

The source argues that popular entertainment has shaped normie intuitions about the singularity, citing *The Terminator* (1984), in which machines send a cyborg back in time to kill Sarah Connor; Marvel's *Avengers: Age of Ultron* (2015), in which the billionaire Tony Stark's well-intentioned "peace in our time" project concludes that humanity is a cancer to be exterminated; and *Travelers* (2016–2018), in which post-singularity humans worship the AI as a god and carry out its directed missions against climate change. *Age of Ultron* is singled out as "particularly effective at demonstrating how well-meaning billionaires' choices can have devastating second and third order consequences."
