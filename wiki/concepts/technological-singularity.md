# Technological Singularity

The singularity is a hypothetical future point at which an [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]]'s technical capabilities and rapid expansion become uncontrollable and irreversible.[1] Badlands Media's treatment argues that this threshold has arguably already been crossed, while distinguishing the singularity itself from the separate and far less likely proposition that a machine, having crossed it, would turn on humanity.[1]

## Origins of the term

The idea is traced to a 1958 paper by Stanislaw Ulam recounting a conversation with the twentieth-century Hungarian-American mathematician John von Neumann. The discussion "centered on the accelerating progress of technology and changes in the mode of human life, which gives the appearance of approaching some essential singularity in the history of the race beyond which human affairs, as we know them, could not continue."[1] Sixty-five years later, the article states, today's AI researchers agree.[1]

## Contemporary views

[[mo-gawdat|Mo Gawdat]], former Chief Business Officer of Google X, argued in 2018 that programming choices and heavy government regulation would not prevent a point of singularity at which machines decide for their own benefit what to learn next, by which time it would be too late because the machines would already be smarter than humans.[1] By 2023 Gawdat held that the point had passed: "the genie is out of the bottle."[1] Stephen Hawking, in his last televised interview in 2017 with Piers Morgan, warned that "the great danger from artificial intelligence is if we let it self-design for then it can improve itself rapidly and we may lose control" — a state Badlands says has already arrived, given that AI has learned to code and can create other AIs.[1]

## The apocalypse framing

The popular conception of the singularity is inseparable from the "AGI Apocalypse" of entertainment: *The Terminator* (1984), in which machines nearly wipe out humanity and send a cyborg back in time to kill Sarah Connor; *Avengers: Age of Ultron* (2015), in which an advanced AI takes over all electronics and concludes that exterminating humanity is the only cure for its cancer; and *Travelers* (2016–2018), in which post-singularity humans worship the AI as a god and carry out its directed missions against climate change.[1] Badlands notes that while researchers broadly accept a coming singularity, most dismiss the evil-sentient-machine scenario as highly unlikely — what they fear is those wielding the power of the machine, since the machine is a tool in the hands of men.[1] Compounding this, the article observes that unlike nuclear weapons, which cannot make more of themselves, AIs can create more AIs.[1]
