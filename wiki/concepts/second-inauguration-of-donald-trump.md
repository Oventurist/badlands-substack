# Second Inauguration of Donald Trump

The **second inauguration of Donald Trump** took place on January 20, 2025, when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was sworn in as the 47th president of the United States on the west front of the Capitol building.[1]

## Scramble for invitations

By custom, foreign leaders do not attend inaugurations of US presidents, but Trump discarded that precedent by inviting Chinese president [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] to the ceremony, setting off a scramble among world leaders for invitations.[1] Xi declined the offer, with a top emissary expected to attend in his place.[1]

Registered foreign agents in Washington, D.C. told the New York Post that leaders were "burning up the phone lines" demanding access. "They're all going crazy. Chile, Peru, Nigeria, Mozambique," one agent said, adding that the leaders "were champing at the bit to attend."[1] The same agent said most would be refused, explaining, "If you're Costa Rica, there is just no way you get invited to this inauguration. What is the value add? You are not bringing business or major companies from your country."[1] El Salvador's [[nayib-bukele|Nayib Bukele]] and former Brazilian president [[jair-bolsonaro|Jair Bolsonaro]] were among those invited.[1]

Reports also indicated that Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] would not attend, despite having been invited and having attended the 2017 ceremony.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Trump, "the Master of Branding," had turned the event into an exclusive nightclub for geopolitics, arguing that attendance was not about access — Trump had been hosting foreign leaders at Mar-a-Lago for over a year — but about being seen at an event symbolizing the start of a new epoch.[1] He argued that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] was now widely recognized as real by the American public and resented by foreign leaders fighting for sovereignty against the feudalism of the US State Department.[1]

He named Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] as leaders he hoped would attend, floated Dennis Rodman as a possible ambassador to North Korea, and speculated about appearances by Stephen Baldwin and [[kanye-west|Kanye West]].[1] He predicted the day would not pass without surprises.[1]

## Venue change and television ratings

Trump moved the second inauguration indoors, citing the extreme cold in Washington, D.C., a decision that drastically reduced the size of the attending crowd; thousands of supporters nonetheless braved freezing temperatures outside the Capital One Arena in downtown Washington to watch the televised swearing-in, many waiting in long lines for a spot in the packed venue.[2] Crowd size had been a recurring subject of contention for Trump and a favored line of mockery from Democrats.[2]

Nielsen estimates showed 24.6 million viewers tuned in on Monday, January 20, 2025 to watch Trump sworn into office, a figure lower than the network viewership recorded for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s inauguration four years earlier.[2] Badlands commentator Brad Cologero Getz argued that the network number understated the true audience, pointing to very large concurrent viewership on X in addition to the 24 million on broadcast television and tens of millions more on YouTube and Rumble, and concluded that it was "the most viewed inauguration in American history."[2]

## First-week executive actions

Within barely twenty-four hours of taking office Trump pardoned more than 1,500 January 6 rioters, including some involved in violence; purported to end [[birthright-citizenship|birthright citizenship]]; exited the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]]; attempted to convert large portions of the civil service into patronage jobs; and issued an executive order defining gender as a binary.[2] Writing in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]], commentators noted that these steps met with a largely muted response from a "dazed left and press corps," a sharp contrast with 2017, when hundreds of thousands demonstrated in Washington and Americans flocked to airports at midnight to thwart the travel ban.[2]

## Day-one promises kept and the appointee purge

Breitbart reported that many of the promises [[donald-trump|Trump]] had vowed to deliver were fulfilled on his first day in office, thrilling Republicans; Trump had said in October that "your head will spin when you see what's going to happen" on Day One.[3] Among the pledges were launching the largest deportation in United States history, securing the border, ending [[birthright-citizenship|birthright citizenship]], pardoning January 6 defendants, unleashing American energy, terminating federal DEI programs, and beginning to drain the "deep state."[3] On Monday afternoon he began signing executive orders, rescinding 78 of former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s executive actions and taking action on numerous other issues.[3] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that humans innately seek to order reality according to logical frameworks — which is why the Hegelian Dialectic works against them — and that this was now being reversed on the Deep State, with the American Golden Age kicking off "mass systemic whiplash."[3]

Overnight, Trump began purging more than 1,000 appointees from the Biden administration, starting with celebrity chef [[jose-andres|Jose Andres]] and retired General [[mark-milley|Mark Milley]], issuing an "official notice of dismissal" to four appointees in a midnight Truth Social post.[3] "My Presidential Personnel Office is actively in the process of identifying and removing over a thousand Presidential Appointees from the previous Administration, who are not aligned with our vision to Make America Great Again," he wrote, adding that many more were coming.[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied the purge to the [[sword-dance|sword dance]] Trump performed during the inauguration festivities, recalling that his 2017 sword dance in Saudi Arabia preceded the [[saudi-purge|Saudi Purge]].[3]

## Immigration actions and the invasion declaration

The White House Spanish-language website disappeared, leaving behind a prominent "Go Home" button that struck a nerve with immigrant communities and sparked a social media firestorm, after a TikTok creator drew attention to it in a viral video.[3] Trump's border czar separately announced that deportation operations were under way.[3] Badlands writer [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] singled out the invasion declaration as the most interesting of Trump's day-one executive actions, quoting its finding that in joining the Union the States surrendered much of their sovereignty in exchange for the federal government's Article IV, Section 4 promise to "protect each of [the States] against Invasion," and that the federal government had failed in that obligation, so that "an invasion is ongoing at the southern border."[3]
