# 2025 Israeli Airstrikes on Southern Syria

The 2025 Israeli airstrikes on southern Syria were a series of attacks conducted on the night of Tuesday, February 25, 2025, on military targets near Damascus and in Deraa province, following Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s demand for the complete demilitarization of the region.[1]

## The strikes

Israeli warplanes struck several Syrian "command centers" and "sites containing weapons" in the town of Kiswah, south of Damascus, and in the southern province of Deraa.[1] The [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] argued that the presence of "military forces and assets in the southern part of Syria poses a threat to the citizens of Israel."[1]

A spokesperson for Israeli Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] confirmed that the Israeli Air Force was "attacking strongly in southern Syria as part of the new policy we have defined to pacify southern Syria."[1] Katz added: "Any attempt by the Syrian regime forces and the country's terrorist organizations to establish themselves in the security zone in southern Syria will be met with fire."[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry called the demilitarization rationale "a bunch of nonsense," arguing that [[syria|Syria]] had already been demilitarized in December, when the IDF requisitioned a number of Soviet T-55 tanks from the Syrian military — in his view likely a gift from Israel's "mercenary-turned-Syrian-president" [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Abu Mohammad al-Jolani]], who had rebranded himself as [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Hussein al-Sharaa]] in an apparent attempt to shed his Al-Qaeda and ISIS history.[1] He noted that earlier that month Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar had described the al-Jolani government as "polite jihadis" that was "gaining legitimacy from the world," and observed that the new authorities were reportedly reimplementing Sharia law.[1]

The commentary emphasized that Al Kiswah lies only about eight miles south of Damascus, the Syrian capital and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, and offered the aside that "Damascus" is a Greek name while modern Arabic is roughly three hundred years old.[1]

## Regional context

The piece recalled that in May 2023 Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] invited then-Syrian President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] to the Arab League summit in Riyadh, ending Syria's twelve-year exile from the organization after its 2011 expulsion, described as having occurred at the behest of Secretary of State Hillary Clinton.[1] Assad was greeted by MBS with hugs and kisses, an event that sparked worldwide intrigue, and during the summit MBS asserted that Damascus is part of Arabia and that he intended to see it return to the Arab sphere of influence and away from Iran and Turkey.[1] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicted that Netanyahu would take Damascus, and framed the open question as how Riyadh, Tehran, Cairo, and Moscow would respond.[1]
