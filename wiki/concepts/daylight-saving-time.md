# Daylight Saving Time

Daylight saving time is the practice of shifting clocks forward by one hour in spring and back by one hour in autumn, with the stated intent of moving an hour of sunlight from the early morning into the evening so that people can make more use of daylight.[1] Most U.S. states change their clocks in March and again in November.[1]

## Trump's December 2024 call to eliminate it

On Friday, December 13, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called daylight saving time "inconvenient, and very costly to our Nation" in a social media post and said the Republican Party would try to "eliminate" it.[1] Posting on X, he wrote that "the Republican Party will use its best efforts to eliminate Daylight Saving Time, which has a small but strong constituency, but shouldn't!"[1] Trump had previously voiced support for the idea, writing in March 2019 that "making Daylight Saving Time permanent is OK with me!"[1]

The post drew broad support online, with many commenters describing the twice-yearly time change as antiquated, while others noted that the likely outcome would not be the elimination of daylight saving time itself but rather making it permanent and thereby ending the clock changes.[1]

## Legislative history

Ending the clock change requires the approval of [[congress|Congress]].[1] Numerous bipartisan efforts have been attempted and all have failed: in March 2022 the Senate unanimously passed a bill to make daylight saving time permanent, but the measure died in the House, and a similar effort in 2023 also failed.[1]
