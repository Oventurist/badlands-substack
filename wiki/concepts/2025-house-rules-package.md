# 2025 House Rules Package

The 2025 House rules package was the governing rules resolution adopted by the narrowly divided United States House of Representatives at the opening of the 119th Congress, immediately after the reelection of Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[1]

## Adoption and motion-to-vacate threshold

The House adopted the package by a vote of 215-209.[1] Its most notable provision significantly raised the threshold for members to trigger an effort to oust the Speaker: under the section-by-section analysis, it would take nine members of the Republican conference to trigger a [[motion-to-vacate|motion to vacate]] the speakership, up from the single-member threshold that had allowed the GOP to oust then-Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] in the previous Congress.[1]

Johnson had been reelected on Friday in an extended first round of voting, with [[ralph-norman|Ralph Norman]] (R-SC) and Keith Self (R-TX) switching their votes to Johnson at the end and leaving [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] (R-KY) as the lone Republican dissenter.[1] The rules vote followed immediately, and the package specified that once adopted, members "shall consider a dozen bills listed at the end of the document."[1]

## The ICC sanctions provision

Adoption was not without Republican critics. Representatives Massie and [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] (R-Ga.) had objected to the inclusion of language queuing up a bill to impose sanctions over any [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] action against people such as Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1] Massie asked on social media: "How did a bill to protect Netanyahu make it into the House rules package to be voted on immediately after the speaker vote? Where are our priorities?!"[1] Per Common Dreams, the specific object of Massie's complaint was the eighth of the listed bills, which would impose sanctions over any ICC "effort to investigate, arrest, detain, or prosecute any protected person of the United States and its allies."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the sequencing "sneaky" and said Johnson's first act as Speaker in the new Congress looked much like his first term.[1] She pointed readers to the Culture of Change episode in which she and [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] read from Supreme Court Justice [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]]'s book *Over Ruled: The Human Toll of Too Much Law*, arguing that the law, the legislators, and the regulators are out of control and that Gorsuch effectively makes the judiciary's case for DOGE.[1]
