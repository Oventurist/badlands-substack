# ICE Non-Detained Criminal Docket Disclosure (2024)

The ICE non-detained criminal docket disclosure was a September 2024 data release, obtained by a House Republican, showing that the administration of President Joe Biden was allowing up to 650,000 criminal migrants and suspects to remain at large in American communities.[1]

## The data

The information came in a letter from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to Representative Tony Gonzales (R-TX), first reported by Fox News and amplified by Breitbart.[1] The letter stated that as of July 21, 2024, there were 662,566 noncitizens with criminal histories on ICE's national docket, including those detained by ICE and those on the agency's non-detained docket; of those, 435,719 were convicted criminals — including 13,099 convicted of homicide — and 226,847 had pending criminal charges.[1]

The data showed 13,099 migrants convicted of homicide who were not detained, plus an additional 222,141 undetained migrants facing criminal charges, among them 1,845 homicide charges, even though the agency was choosing not to use all of its 41,000 detention beds.[1] A further 15,000 migrant criminals were in jail, a population that included 277 migrant murderers and 51 migrants facing murder charges, and roughly 17,000 migrants had been found guilty of, or charged with, sexual offenses.[1] The term "noncitizens" in the data refers to illegal migrants, overstay migrants, and green card holders, all of whom can be deported if administration officials assign resources to the task.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Ashe in America framed the release as vindication of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s statement that "they're emptying their prisons into our country," for which, she wrote, he had been demonized, labeled racist and xenophobic, and targeted for removal from the ballot.[1] She recalled reporting in February 2021 on what she described as an instant change at the border on November 7, 2020, when Joe Biden was declared the winner of the election, citing active sources in CBP and ICE who told her that the shift affected both the volume of migrants arriving and their treatment by the government — a bureaucratic realignment that in her account preceded Trump's departure from office.[1]

Her central charge concerned unaccompanied children rather than adult criminality: children represented as part of a family unit to gain entry, later discovered not to belong to that unit, and then handed to NGOs subcontracted by the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], with the government barred from fingerprinting or photographing them and the NGOs performing that function instead.[1] She characterized the arrangement as "government sponsored child trafficking," larger in significance than stories about Venezuelans occupying apartment buildings or Haitians eating pets, and contrasted Trump's promise to end the practice and prosecute those responsible with denials from Kamala Harris and the establishment.[1]
