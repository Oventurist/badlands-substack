# Danger to Our Democracy

**"Danger to our democracy"** — and its companion formulation "protecting our democracy" — is a political slogan that [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treats as a piece of [[operation-mockingbird|Mockingbird media]] messaging rather than a sincere description of events.[1] In a January 1, 2024 essay titled "The 'Danger to Their Democracy'," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the possessive pronoun is the key to the phrase: the democracy in question "is not 'ours,' it's 'theirs'."[1]

## The argument

DeLarme wrote that in the weeks preceding the [[colorado-ballot-disqualification-case|Colorado ballot ruling]], the mainstream media ramped up its assurances that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is a tyrant, a crook and an insurrectionist whom the Democratic establishment must eliminate at all costs in order to "protect our democracy."[1] He posed the counter-question of which is the greater danger: a man who has never been charged with insurrection, or a deeply embedded political establishment willing to raze the Constitution to retain control of the executive branch.[1]

At first glance, he observed, "our democracy" reads like "our constitution" or "our rights" — language of unity and inclusivity.[1] But in usage, "our" refers not to all individuals but to a subset of them, namely the ruling class.[1] The phrasing is, in his account, an intentional attempt to quell discussion and debate by rendering dissenters as "other" and defining anyone who does not subscribe to a statist, elitist, technocratic and oligarchical version of democracy as a danger to the idea of democracy itself.[1] The endangered democracy, he wrote, is "merely a veneer employed to obscure the relentless growth of socialist bourgeois statism and the velvet fascism" infiltrating culture and society.[1]

## Institutional expressions

DeLarme pointed to several institutional vehicles for the phrase.[1] The **Protecting Our Democracy Act**, a bill in the 117th Congress pushed hard by progressive Democrats, sought to federalize elections.[1] [[microsoft|Microsoft]]'s "[[democracy-forward|Democracy Forward]]" initiative supplied a further example: at a conference on digital campaign security, a project-affiliated individual named Ethan Chumley described the movement's work as "supporting the institutions **we think** are fundamental to a healthy democracy" — an emphasis DeLarme added to highlight who decides.[1]

He also described [[defending-digital-campaigns|Defending Digital Campaigns]], a nominally non-aligned organization funded by Google, Facebook, Microsoft and others whose ostensible aim is campaign data security, but whose board comprises former Obama administration and then-current DHS officials alongside former Romney and Hillary Clinton campaign managers Matt Rhoades and [[robby-mook|Robby Mook]] and the chairman of DigiDems, an organization financed by the Democratic Party — as well as the law firm [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]] of "Russiagate" fame.[1] Democracy Forward, he added, partnered with [[newsguard|NewsGuard]], which he characterized as an organization that pretends to be a media fact-checker while rewarding outlets that pay lip service to establishment narratives and penalizing those that do not.[1]

## Who uses the phrase

[[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], [[msnbc|MSNBC]], [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], [[cnn|CNN]], [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] and innumerable others have parroted "protect our democracy," DeLarme wrote.[1] The kind of democracy they refer to, in his framing, is "a dishonest and rotted western oligarchy" that for the first time in recent history is poised to fall through peaceful, legal means — at the hands of patriots such as Trump, of foreign powers such as the BRICS nations, or both.[1]

His conclusion inverted the slogan: the real threat to establishment control is not merely Trump but ordinary readers and contributors of Badlands Media. "We are the danger to *their* democracy," he wrote, "and we should wear that designation as a badge of honor."[1]
