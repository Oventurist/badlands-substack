# 2025 Federal Workforce Buyout Offer

The 2025 federal workforce buyout offer was a government-wide severance proposal announced by the White House on Tuesday, January 28, 2025, offering to pay federal workers who did not wish to return to in-person work through September 30, provided they resigned by February 6.[1] It marked an acceleration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s already unprecedented effort to shrink the federal workforce.[1]

## Terms

The [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] described the offer as administrative leave with pay and benefits, and a spokeswoman confirmed that agencies would receive implementation details later that Tuesday and would be able to carve out exemptions.[1] A senior administration official told Axios that the government-wide email was intended "to make sure that all federal workers are on board with the new administration's plan to have federal employees in office and adhering to higher standards," adding: "We're five years past COVID and just 6 percent of federal employees work full-time in office. That is unacceptable."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary framed the offer as Trump "altering the terms of the deal between the people and the System," suggesting that employees who take the severance gain a head start in the private sector while those who decline are betting on a welfare state slated for gutting.[1]

## Second round of offers (April 2025)

In early April 2025 the Trump administration launched a second — and likely final — round of deferred resignation offers, giving thousands of federal workers another chance to walk away, with some employees exhausted by months of chaos seriously considering it.[2] At least seven agencies participated that week, including the departments of Defense, Agriculture, Energy, Housing and Urban Development and Transportation, as well as the Small Business Administration and the General Services Administration, all using the same Deferred Resignation Program that prompted more than 77,000 federal employees to voluntarily leave earlier in the year.[2]

Under the proposal, workers could resign immediately and be paid while keeping their benefits through at least September 30, according to the agency notices.[2] Unlike the first wave, which was coordinated through the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] and gave employees weeks to weigh their options, this round moved much faster, with agencies setting their own deadlines and workers given as little as one week — two at most — to decide.[2]

## Reaction among federal employees

Reporting at the end of January 2025 described a federal workforce of some two million people still reeling after receiving a mass email offering them the chance to preemptively resign ahead of additional and unspecified Trump administration efforts to shrink government.[3] With the terms of what was characterized as a stark but murky ultimatum unclear and likely subject to legal interpretation and challenge, upwards of hundreds of thousands of individual employees were struggling with what to do, increasingly uncertain about the stability of their jobs and their agencies.[3] One employee at the Department of Justice, granted anonymity to speak candidly without fear of retribution, summarized the mood as "chaos, mistrust, confusion."[3] The offer was described by one worker as "the last lifeboat in town."[3]
