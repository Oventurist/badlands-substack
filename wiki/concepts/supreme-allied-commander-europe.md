# Supreme Allied Commander Europe

Supreme Allied Commander Europe (SACEUR) is the senior operational command post of [[nato|NATO]], responsible for overseeing all alliance military operations in Europe. The role has traditionally been held by a four-star United States general, an arrangement that gives Washington direct command authority over allied military activity on the continent.[1]

## Reported 2025 review

In March 2025, NBC News reported that the Trump administration was considering giving up the U.S.-held SACEUR role.[1] The report surfaced alongside separate reporting that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was weighing consolidation of the combatant commands, including a merger of U.S. European Command with [[united-states-africa-command|U.S. Africa Command]].[1]

House Armed Services Committee chairman [[mike-rogers|Mike Rogers]] and Senate Armed Services Committee chairman [[roger-wicker|Roger Wicker]] issued a joint statement warning against unilateral changes of this magnitude made without coordination with the [[white-house|White House]] and [[congress|Congress]].[1] Defense experts and former NATO commanders quoted in the coverage warned that relinquishing SACEUR could weaken U.S. influence within the alliance — a consequence Badlands commentators argued was in fact the intended outcome of a deliberate American step back from permanent overseas warfighting.[1]
