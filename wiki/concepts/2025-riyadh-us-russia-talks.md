# 2025 Riyadh US-Russia Talks

The **2025 Riyadh US-Russia talks** were high-level negotiations held in the Saudi capital on February 18, 2025, at which Russian and American officials outlined their respective positions on ending the war in Ukraine. The session lasted roughly four and a half hours.[1]

## Background

The talks came as the conflict — described by observers as the gravest European security crisis since the Second World War — approached its third anniversary, and as the new administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] moved rapidly to formulate a settlement plan for the Russia-Ukraine war.[1] Reporting at the time noted that the administration's emerging approach had raised consternation in Kyiv and among European allies while Moscow appeared "elated," and that officials had made basic negotiating errors in rolling out the framework that, if uncorrected, could doom the effort.[1]

## Ukrainian exclusion and Trump's response

Kyiv objected publicly to being excluded from the Riyadh session. Trump expressed disappointment at the Ukrainian reaction, telling reporters after signing executive orders at Mar-a-Lago that "they're upset about not having a seat" and that "they've had a seat for three years, and a long time before that, this could have been settled very easily."[1] He added that "just a half-baked negotiator could have settled this years ago" without significant loss of land, life, or cities.[1] Badlands commentary read the remarks as Trump throwing [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and the Kyiv government "under the bus," and as an implicit challenge to Zelensky's legitimacy that undercut the Western case for backing Ukraine on democratic grounds.[1]

## Geopolitical interpretation

Former Defense Department senior security policy analyst [[michael-maloof|Michael Maloof]] characterized the Riyadh meeting as a tectonic shift in global geopolitics and predicted it could mark "the beginning of the end of [[nato|NATO]]," arguing that Europe would fragment into a series of regional defense alliances rather than remain one cohesive bloc of 32 countries.[1] Badlands commentary tied the meeting to the broader thesis of a [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] displacing globalist institutions, observing that the Middle East now appeared to be managing the affairs of Europe rather than the reverse.[1]
