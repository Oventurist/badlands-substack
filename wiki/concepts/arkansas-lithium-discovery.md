# Arkansas Lithium Discovery

The Arkansas lithium discovery refers to the October 2024 announcement of a potentially massive lithium reserve beneath southwestern Arkansas, identified in a study led by the [[united-states-geological-survey|U.S. Geological Survey]] (USGS).[1]

## The study

Published in *Science Advances*, the research estimated that between 5 and 19 million tons of lithium reserves are present in the region.[1] According to a USGS press release, if the resource proves commercially recoverable it could meet the projected 2030 world demand for lithium in car batteries nine times over — a figure far exceeding current worldwide demand.[1]

The work combined the USGS with the Arkansas Department of Energy and Environment's Office of the State Geologist, and employed a methodology using water testing and machine learning to estimate how much lithium is present in brines within the geological structure known as the Smackover Formation.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

Badlands writers read the announcement through the lens of the "Switch" theory that runs in parallel with [[devolution|Devolution]], which posits that beneath the noisy narratives dominating the American zeitgeist there are "Actuals" indicating an imminent American energy and manufacturing boom.[1] The lithium find was treated as the latest marker that the future of U.S. energy and production is brighter than commonly understood, and as validation of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s predictions on the subject.[1]

Commentary further emphasized that the deposit was reportedly discovered because of specific modeling mandates budgeted into the Energy Act of 2020, a Department of Energy directive framed as intended to kickstart a new energy era — described as a serendipitous timing ahead of Trump publicly resuming the office writers argue he never lost.[1]
