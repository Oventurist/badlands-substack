# Operation Trust

**Operation Trust** (often abbreviated OT) was a Bolshevik counterintelligence operation frequently cited in contemporary commentary as the archetype of a "hope-based" psychological operation.[1] As described in the July 16, 2024 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "In Defense of Q - We Were Never Told to Sit Still," Operation Trust told the Russian people that "the good guys are winning" while in fact creating an entire fake organization that opponents of the regime could join, thereby sequestering anti-communists.[1]

## Use as an analogy for Q

Operation Trust is regularly invoked by critics who contend that [[q-operation|Q]] is a modern rehash of the same technique, principally because Q told followers that "the patriots are in control," a message critics treat as functionally identical to Trust's reassurance that the good guys were winning.[1]

The [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] author rejected the comparison on two grounds.[1] First, he argued that "control" in Q's usage does not mean control of everything, but rather resembles a chess position in which a victor is discernible before all moves have been played; on his reading patriots hold a sufficient upper hand to be certain of victory, and what remains uncertain is only how much destruction the cabal inflicts on its way out.[1] Second, and in his view decisively, Operation Trust built a joinable fake organization, whereas Q created no organization and offered no membership of any kind, having done nothing beyond posting information on [[4chan|4chan]] and encouraging [[anons|anons]] to assemble the puzzle themselves.[1]

More broadly, the author granted that Q superficially resembles negative psyops and that the vast majority of hope-based psyops are probably false, but insisted that "appearances are just appearances": resemblance between two operations says nothing about what either was designed to accomplish.[1] He noted that he had never seen anyone substantiate the Trust comparison without relying on such superficial resemblance.[1]
