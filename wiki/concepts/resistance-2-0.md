# Resistance 2.0

**"Resistance 2.0"** is the label applied to the coalition of progressive organizations that convened in November 2024, immediately after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidential victory, to organize opposition to his second administration.[1] The name marks it as a sequel to the "Resistance" movement assembled after Trump's 2016 win.[1]

## Formation

Reporting cited by Badlands Media described more than 100 progressive groups gathering within days of the election.[1] Core sponsors included the [[working-families-party|Working Families Party]], [[moveon-org|MoveOn]], the [[indivisible|Indivisible Project]], Public Citizen and the [[american-civil-liberties-union|American Civil Liberties Union]], joined by Democratic Representative [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]] of Washington.[1] Organizers scheduled eight roughly weekly "Mass Calls" from 8 to 9 p.m. EST beginning November 7, 2024.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized the effort as "NGO world, Hollywood and possibly even the [[state-department|State Department]] running a domestic [[color-revolution|color revolution]] against MAGA."[1] He argued the network of tax-exempt organizations functions as the nervous system of the deep establishment, carrying out the intentions of a "think tank class" that includes the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]], the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[1]

DeLarme further contended that when pressed on what policy they oppose, the coalition can point only to [[project-2025|Project 2025]], a [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] initiative he says has nothing to do with Trump — leading him to conclude the groups are establishment attack dogs mobilized against populism rather than against any specific agenda.[1] Because street rioting had proven less effective than [[lawfare|lawfare]], he predicted the NGOs and their lawyers would be the primary vector of attack on Trump 2.0.[1]

## Related context

The article frames Resistance 2.0 as one of several post-election "moves and counter moves," alongside General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]]'s warnings about Trump's physical safety and the identification of [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] as the opposition's "tactical commander," and the fight over Senate leadership and cabinet confirmations.[1]
