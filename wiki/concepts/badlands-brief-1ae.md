# Badlands Brief: DC Exposes Itself as the Middle East Gets Gloriously Strange

"DC Exposes Itself as the Middle East Gets Gloriously Strange" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on July 22, 2026. Like other installments in the series, it pairs summarized news items with commentary from named Badlands writers — in this edition [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]. The brief carried 115 likes at the time of capture and closes with the standard disclaimer that the opinions expressed belong to the tagged authors rather than to Badlands Media as an institution.

The edition covers five subjects: a federal appellate ruling on the release of the Hur investigation recordings, contradictory [[politico|Politico]] headlines about the Iran war, a U.S.–Saudi civil nuclear agreement, two congressional self-governance measures, and an extended Iranian foreign-ministry interview, followed by a White House meeting on Lebanon.

## D.C. Circuit rejects Biden's emergency bid to block release of Hur audio

The brief reports that the [[dc-circuit-court-of-appeals|U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit]] denied an emergency request by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for an injunction blocking release of audio recordings and transcripts of his 2016–2017 private conversations with ghostwriter [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]]. The recordings were made at Biden's home while he worked on a memoir and were obtained by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] as part of Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s investigation into Biden's handling of classified documents.

According to the summary, the Justice Department initially withheld most of the records under FOIA exemptions but later agreed to release redacted versions to the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], [[mike-howell|Mike Howell]], and the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]]. Biden intervened in the litigation to block release; a lower court denied his request for a preliminary injunction, prompting the emergency appeal. The D.C. Circuit ruled that Biden had not shown a likelihood of success on the merits, citing a substantial public interest in allowing the public to assess whether the government had gone easy on a former vice president, while noting that redactions would protect sensitive personal details and that much of the content was already public through Hur's report and Biden's own memoir. The court extended an administrative stay until August 3 to allow time for further review.

Ashe in America quotes the panel's language directly: "When a highly visible investigation of a prominent public figure ends in a decision not to charge, the public has a strong interest in evaluating 'whether the government had the evidence but nevertheless pulled its punches.' Id. at 1093. The requested materials are germane to that interest." She frames this as the appeals court "surprisingly acknowledging the rights of the People" in understanding why Biden was not charged "when the government had him dead to rights and President Trump was charged for less," recalling Hur's conclusion that Biden was a "well-meaning elderly man with a poor memory." She argues it was "crazy he was allowed to continue being 'President' in that condition," given that he went on to sign executive orders and pardons including for his family — "Or did he?" The ruling, she writes, "feels like a step change in getting us to justice for the massively seditious and treasonous [[autopen-scandal|autopen scandal]]," in which "the dementia-ridden scrotus puppet is not the primary perp"; rather, "the people running him are." She concludes that "we can't have a golden age without a justice phase" and that the moment is closer than she thought possible: "Accelerate."

## Dueling Politico headlines on the Iran war

Burning Bright uses a pair of Politico headlines published the same week — one reporting that Democrats are desperate to halt [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s continuation of the Iran conflict, the other that the GOP is becoming "almost hysterical" in its anxiety over the same continuation — as evidence of what he calls [[uniparty|Uniparty]] unity accidentally revealed by the [[media-protectorate|media protectorate]].

He describes having spent "tens of thousands of words analyzing the Fake Iran War so far in 2026," and characterizes the conflict as "the war that is not a war," now in a perpetual holding pattern he believes is "part of Donald Trump's central plan against the central planners." He lists several "Actual Disentanglement operations" he has theorized through the year, including the on-shoring of U.S. energy production and the slow dissolution of what he calls the last great instance of the controlled-opposition dynamic playing out between the mirrored regimes of [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]]. Above these, he argues, the mass psychological impact of Trump's "perpetual engine" matters most to the Mind War.

The contradiction he identifies rests on the premise that "the Democratic party does not care about the human cost in the Middle East, and never has." If perpetuating the Iran war is genuinely damaging to Trump's mandate, and both left and right agree on that, he asks why the left would also work to stop it — and why the Uniparty contingent on the right would do the same. His conclusion is that "sometimes you can glimpse the real war operating beneath the fake ones," and that when the so-called left and right agree on something anathema to Trump's moves, "they're reminding us that the real enemy has been in the gates all along."

## The Saudi 123 Agreement

The brief reports that the Trump administration intends to submit to [[congress|Congress]] within days a [[123-agreement|123 Agreement]] with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] authorizing U.S. cooperation on civil nuclear technology. The document is expected to be signed by Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] and Saudi Energy Minister [[abdulaziz-bin-salman|Abdulaziz bin Salman]], following a preliminary agreement reached in Riyadh the previous year.

Crucially, the pact does not incorporate the so-called [[gold-standard|Gold Standard]] that would prohibit Saudi Arabia from enriching uranium or reprocessing spent fuel — both potential routes to weapons-grade material — and omits the Additional Protocol giving the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]] expanded rights to conduct snap inspections at undeclared sites. While the agreement creates a legal pathway for cooperation across the nuclear fuel cycle including enrichment, it does not require the United States to transfer related technology or capabilities. Once submitted, Congress has roughly 90 days of continuous session to review it; blocking the deal would require a joint resolution and a two-thirds majority to override a presidential veto. The summary notes that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] has previously said the kingdom would seek nuclear weapons if Iran obtained them, and that the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] accepted the Gold Standard when it signed its own 123 Agreement in 2009.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry notes that Badlands had covered the matter earlier in the week when it was still speculation. He explains that the document refers to Section 123 of the [[atomic-energy-act-of-1954|U.S. Atomic Energy Act of 1954]], which would allow U.S. contractors to work in Saudi Arabia developing reactors and infrastructure, and says he has tracked the issue since Trump returned to office. He emphasizes the absence of guardrails against weapons development, recalling that Mohammed bin Salman told Bret Baier in a Fox News interview years earlier that Saudi Arabia would build a bomb if Iran did.

He also observes that [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] "was vehemently against Saudi Arabia getting nuclear technology," and that the timing of the agreement "coming on the heels of his death" is "not lost on me." He expects other members of Congress to object because, in his view, "most of those clowns are owned by Israel, and Israel has made it clear that any Muslim nation receiving nuclear technology is a red line for them," but doubts Israel retains the political capital to stop the agreement outside of congressional procedural obstruction. His closing argument is that if American contractors are blocked, the kingdom will simply hire South Korean or Japanese firms: "One way or another, the Saudis are going to get a nuclear energy program. The question will be whether the US plays a role in the process."

## Congressional self-policing measures

The brief summarizes two developments. Congresswoman [[kat-cammack|Kat Cammack]] called for an immediate House vote on H.Res. 500, a resolution she had introduced to amend House rules to require criminal background checks for all House employees. It would also mandate public disclosure — filed with the Clerk and made available — of any payments, contracts, or agreements with foreign governments in the three years prior to employment, as well as any foreign citizenship or nationality, applying to both current staff and new hires. Cammack argued that congressional staff routinely handle sensitive personal information yet face no mandatory background checks of the kind required for executive-branch employees, and that efforts to advance the measure have repeatedly stalled.

Separately, House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] endorsed expanding the House Code of Official Conduct to ban lawmakers from sexual relationships with any congressional staff, not only subordinates. The current rule, adopted in 2018, prohibits intimate relationships with subordinates in personal offices or on committees, with an exception for married staffers; the proposed change would remove the subordinate limitation.

Ashe in America writes that [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]] "attempted to appear heroic" over the weekend by amplifying Cammack's bill, and that it "backfired spectacularly, as Americans everywhere reacted in shock that this wasn't already happening." She invokes [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] and [[fang-fang|Fang Fang]], [[dianne-feinstein|Feinstein]]'s driver, and other staffer scandals. Johnson's proposed sex-relationship rule likewise backfired, she argues, given "the sex tape in the Senate room," Fang Fang again, and "the secret sex crime slush fund." Her conclusion: "Congress continues to prove that they are the People's real problem — and problems don't get to design their own solutions," and until there are "real elections," the beneficiaries of the fake ones "should be prevented from doing anything that impacts the People. That includes receiving a paycheck."

## Araghchi's interview and the Gulf prince's warning

The final substantive item covers a lengthy interview given by Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]]. According to the summary, Araghchi said Tehran refused even temporary limits on uranium enrichment because doing so would sacrifice national "honor" and set a precedent of yielding to military pressure, and that Iranian leaders were certain the United States and Israel would attack regardless of negotiations — talks he described as largely intended to demonstrate that diplomacy had been attempted.

Araghchi claimed Iran closed the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] from the first day of fighting under a pre-agreed contingency plan triggered by any threat to the supreme leader, and that Iran later achieved strategic parity with the United States, forcing mutual language on respecting sovereignty into a memorandum of understanding. He identified internal penetration by spies as the regime's greatest vulnerability, noting that the [[supreme-national-security-council|Supreme National Security Council]] never convened in full during the war because the simultaneous loss of its members could collapse the system. He confirmed that meetings occurred in underground tunnels and said he has never met the new supreme leader, [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]], whose appointment he described as a signal of policy continuity. The interview drew sharp criticism inside Iran from hardline commentators and outlets affiliated with the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Revolutionary Guards]], who accused Araghchi of recklessly disclosing wartime contingency planning and leadership vulnerabilities.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry sources the item to [[iran-international|Iran International]] and quotes its report that Araghchi, speaking in the first part of a nearly 90-minute podcast interview with establishment podcaster Javad Mogoei, suggested foreign intelligence activity inside Iran may be more ambitious than intelligence gathering: "I believe this security breach is not limited merely to infiltration and obtaining information. Sometimes it also exists in influencing the decision-making process itself. And even more importantly, it extends to shaping our psychological environment and public perception." The remarks came in response to a question about the opening hours of the war, when coordinated strikes killed Iran's supreme leader along with senior military, security, and political figures. Iran analyst Omid Memarian of DAWN told the outlet that one manifestation of such influence would be officials advocating policies that prolong the war despite the damage to the Islamic Republic's own interests: "Some of the decisions they are making are exactly the things that Israelis might wish for."

The commentator reads this as an admission that [[mossad|Mossad]] may have infiltrated the Iranian chain of command to prolong the conflict — "such as breaking ceasefires and attacking Gulf Arab States" — and argues that "the notion of Iranian [[devolution|Devolution]] — that it was completely necessary — doesn't seem so crazy after all." He speculates about the intended role of General [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]]: whether he was meant to escalate conflict with Israel through his network of proxy militias and undermine earnest Iranian leadership to prevent peace, or whether he was so powerful that no benevolent actor could have taken office, asking whether [[masoud-pezeshkian|Pezeshkian]] and Araghchi would be in power had Soleimani never been killed.

The passage he finds most striking is Araghchi's account of a pre-war meeting with a Gulf prince, transcribed and translated by an AI because the Farsi clip lacked subtitles. In it the prince warns, "They will destroy your nuclear facilities, and they will seize your missile capabilities." Araghchi deflects — "Now let's not go into the details, because in the end we have dealings with them" — and describes the atmosphere: "their morale was all like this, they were all waiting for this event." He responds firmly: "If they strike our nuclear facilities, we will strike their nuclear facilities. Because our missiles do not reach American territory, but they do reach their bases in the region," and warns that "no one can threaten us. If someone threatens us, then all of Europe and all of America will stand against him." He then laughs and says, "Sir, God forbid, we do not want to strike you." The prince's closing remark, as recounted, is: "Endure for one more month at most, and this story will end forever."

Araghchi does not name Mohammed bin Salman, but the commentator argues his description is congruent with what MBS has said and done, and notes public reports of Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]]'s meeting with Iranian counterparts — a story he says the media twisted to look like Saudi threats. Revising the framing, he suggests Iran was being given a full warning by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], and that Araghchi now feels compelled to disclose developments related to regime-change operations. The mutual respect he perceives — Iran disclaiming any intent to destroy Gulf infrastructure, the Arabs warning of the coming attack — leads him to read the prince's closing line as a reference to [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]] and storytelling, "but most of all, they are talking about a [[5th-generation-warfare|5GW]] psyop being run against all parties involved, including the soldiers." He concludes that these disclosures come as "Israel's reputation is being destroyed," and finds it hard to believe some Americans still blindly support "the Zionist agenda."

## Trump, Aoun, and the Lebanon framework

The brief reports that Trump met on Tuesday at the [[white-house|White House]] with Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]] and pledged to help [[lebanon|Lebanon]] "a lot," describing the country as having been "very badly treated" and saying it would now receive the respect it deserves. The discussion focused on a June 26 U.S.-mediated framework agreement between Israel and Lebanon calling for the withdrawal of Israeli forces from southern Lebanon, the disarmament of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], and steps toward a possible formal peace agreement. Trump said Israeli forces were "in the process" of withdrawing and redeploying, acknowledged "a Hezbollah problem," indicated willingness to speak directly with the group if Aoun requested it, and raised the possibility of Syrian involvement against Hezbollah. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] reiterated U.S. support for full implementation, stating that Lebanon cannot achieve lasting peace while Hezbollah remains a powerful armed force and that a strong central government must become the sole legitimate military authority.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry situates the meeting weeks after Trump attended the [[nato|NATO]] summit, where he asserted that the IDF should withdraw from Lebanon and [[syria|Syria]] and that the Arabs should be left to deal with their neighbors. He notes that Aoun "has been widely panned as a Saudi loyalist" backed by the Turks and their antagonists, and draws an analogy to the Doha Agreement of 2020, praising Trump's patience in dealing with the [[taliban|Taliban]] and predicting "a replay": a historic deal with Hezbollah that also serves [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] "everything he ever wanted as it relates to the Arabs." He closes with a series of questions about what happens if Netanyahu refuses and demands more war, at what point the mainstream media accepts the framing that Trump's closest allies are Muslim countries angry at Israel, and what follows if Trump makes peace with the "terrorists" while Israel walks away from the table.
