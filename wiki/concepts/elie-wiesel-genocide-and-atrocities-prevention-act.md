# Elie Wiesel Genocide and Atrocities Prevention Act

The Elie Wiesel Genocide and Atrocities Prevention Act is a United States law signed by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] that was designed to increase the American government's capacity to detect, prevent, and respond to emerging genocidal situations abroad.[1] Badlands Media contributor AbsoluteTruth1776 cited the signing of the act as one of twenty government-related accomplishments of the Trump presidency in a March 2023 article.[1]

## Provisions

The stated purpose of the legislation was to allow the United States to treat genocide and mass atrocity as a national security matter rather than solely a humanitarian concern.[1] It established a Mass Atrocities Task Force and required that instances of atrocities be reported to [[congress|Congress]].[1] Supporters argued that the reporting requirement and interagency task force would allow Washington to identify and act against a potential genocidal regime before large-scale killing began.[1]

## Reception in Badlands commentary

Badlands Media framed the act as evidence that the Trump administration's foreign-policy record extended beyond trade and deterrence into humanitarian and atrocity-prevention law, contrary to critics who described the presidency as legislatively unproductive.[1]
