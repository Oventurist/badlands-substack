# Election Integrity Army

The **Election Integrity Army** is the term used by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for the nationwide network of Republican poll watchers, monitors, and legal volunteers that he says the party fielded during the 2024 presidential election and intends to field again, on a larger scale, for the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[1]

## Announcement

In a May 2026 Truth Social post, Trump announced that Republicans would repeat and expand the effort, writing that during his 2024 election — in which he said he won every swing state and carried both the Electoral College and the popular vote by wide margins — "the Republicans had an Election Integrity Army in every single State to preserve the sanctity of each legal vote," and that "we will be doing the same again in 2026, but it will be much bigger and stronger."[1] He closed the statement by declaring that all Americans should have their voices heard by casting a vote and assuring readers that "this Election will be fair."[1]

## Framing as a counter-effort

Trump framed the expanded operation as a direct response to a Democrat-led "Election Integrity Group" that he said Senate Minority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] was assembling, alleging it would attempt to suppress Republican voters and interfere in the elections.[1] He singled out former Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] — whom he associated with the Fast and Furious gunwalking operation under the Obama administration — and Democratic election lawyer [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], whom he tied to the Steele dossier and the 2016 Russia-collusion narrative.[1]

## Badlands criticism

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the premise of the announcement, arguing that "a 'fair' election using the same rigged systems is not a real election" and that "we cannot have real elections until we are honest about how fake they've been."[1] She contended that a presidential declaration that elections are now fair, issued without systemically addressing the reasons they are not, does not constitute the truth-and-reconciliation process required to deliver real elections.[1] Her critique reduced the problem to three elements — fake voters, fake ballots, and fake counts — and called for prosecution of what she described as a treasonous grand conspiracy surrounding the election machine, together with public disclosure of the truth about elections and accountability for co-conspirators.[1] She noted that she and fellow Badlands host [[canncon|CannCon]] had debated since the 2024 election whether the midterms might finally force a reckoning, and judged the country closer than ever to a critical mass of Americans recognizing the nature of the election system and "the cabal that runs it."[1]

Ashe also invoked a Trump quotation — "What truly matters is not which party controls our government, but whether our government is controlled by the people" — while arguing that the administration itself knows how fake past elections have been.[1]
