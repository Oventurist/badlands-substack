# Green Bonds

**Green bonds** are the class of "green financial instruments" that [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies as the principal mechanism by which the City of London and its allied central bankers intend to redirect savings into the green transition.[1]

Ehret writes that under the influence of [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] and [[king-charles-iii|King Charles]], the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] and some of the "dirtiest banks" in the Rothschild–City of London web of finance have promoted green financial instruments led by green bonds, in order to redirect pension plans and mutual funds towards green projects "that no one in their right minds would ever invest in willfully."[1]

He connects the instruments to the [[esg|Ecological, Social, Governance Index]] (ESGI), which he says has been set up across 51% of Germany's banks, including [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]].[1] Leading bankers supporting the index, such as Carney, have said that over 6.5 trillion euros could be mobilized under it, against a current base of roughly $160 billion.[1]

## Relationship to bail-ins

In Ehret's analysis the creation of green bonds runs hand-in-hand with the bail-in mechanisms implemented across the trans-Atlantic nations, which he says are designed to seize trillions of dollars from pension funds, RRSPs and mutual funds the next time a bailout is needed to prop up the "too-big-to-fails" — institutions sitting atop what he describes as a $1.2 trillion derivatives bubble waiting to blow.[1]

## Related vehicles

Carney endorsed the [[financial-stability-board|Financial Stability Board]]'s [[task-force-on-climate-related-financial-disclosures|Task Force on Climate Related Financial Disclosures]], created in 2015, which became the guideline for the UK government's July 2019 white paper *Green Finance Strategy: Transforming Finance for a Greener Future*.[1] The task force also spawned the Green Finance Initiative in 2016, described as a primary vehicle for diverting international capital flows into green tech.[1] [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]], Carney's former employer, created a "Green Index for virtuous investing" with the London-based Climate Disclosure Project, comprising the CDP Environment EW and CDP Eurozone EW sustainability indices.[1]
