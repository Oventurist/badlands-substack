# 2023 Pentagon Document Leaks

The 2023 Pentagon document leaks were one of the most remarkable disclosures of American secrets in a decade, exposing scores of pages of classified [[pentagon|Pentagon]] intelligence assessments about the war in Ukraine.[1] The New York Times reported on the leaks on April 15, 2023, and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried the story in its April 17 news brief.[1]

## Contents

In the leaked documents Ukraine's predicament appeared dire.[1] Missiles for its Soviet-era air defenses were projected to run out by May, its position in the key city of Bakhmut was described as "catastrophic," and its military had taken losses of more than 120,000 dead and wounded — fewer than Russia's estimated toll, but enormous for a country with less than one third of Russia's population.[1]

## Reaction in Kyiv

Despite the severity of the disclosures, there was little palpable alarm in the Ukrainian capital.[1] Some figures welcomed the leak, hoping it would underscore what President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had been saying for months: that Ukraine urgently needed more ammunition and weapons to expel Russian forces.[1] Oleksiy Honcharenko, a member of Parliament from the opposition European Solidarity party, said, "From many points of view, this leak is really useful, and good, even I can say good for Ukraine," warning that unless Western backers moved beyond what he called "incremental" support, "everything can go to waste, because so much is at stake today."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the leaks scandal as the latest episode illustrating that examining the net effects of political, legal, military, and economic deployments yields more reliable directional judgment than tracking claimed intentions amid what he called engineered chaos in the Information War.[1] He argued that both "patriots and globalists" use fifth-generation warfare obfuscation techniques continuously, and that "white hat-black hat games are stimulating, but of lesser importance than Net Effects."[1]

Burning Bright rejected the framing that the leaks were good news for the Ukrainian — and by extension [[nato|NATO]] — war effort, contending they could only deepen anti-war sentiment in the West and reduce financial and military backing for resistance to Russia's Special Military Operation.[1] He predicted that Ukraine would ultimately come to the table and negotiate peace with Russia, to the frustration of what he described as its globalist masters, and that the leaks formed part of the path driving the narrative in that direction.[1]

## Smart bomb failures disclosed in the leaks

One of the leaked documents connected to alleged leaker [[jack-teixeira|Jack Teixeira]] reported that American-made smart bombs were failing in Ukraine because of successful Russian electronic jamming measures.[2] The highly classified material reviewed effective Russian countermeasures that rendered the munitions ineffective and noted that in some cases technical problems caused the weapons to fail to detonate.[2] It also indicated that "1,000 arming lanyards" had been approved for Ukrainian forces, suggesting that more than 1,000 [[jdam|JDAM]] kits would be sent.[2]

Reporting on the slide said Russia was using GPS jamming to interfere with the weapons' targeting process, causing JDAMs and at times other American weapons such as guided rockets to miss their mark.[2] Former [[pentagon|Pentagon]] official and retired [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] officer [[mick-mulroy|Mick Mulroy]] said jamming of the guidance signal would explain the munitions' underperformance relative to other war zones.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] drew two conclusions: that the leaks undercut the narrative that Ukraine was winning the war, and that if the U.S. government were in a state of [[devolution|Devolution]] with the U.S. military coordinating with Russia at senior levels to avoid kinetic war between the two superstates, then "simple" jamming successes would take on added significance.[2]

## Arrest of Jack Teixeira

On April 13, 2023, 21-year-old National Guardsman [[jack-teixeira|Jack Teixeira]] was arrested in connection with the leak of hundreds of classified documents, per the [[washington-examiner|Washington Examiner]] and other outlets.[3] He had been identified by the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] ahead of the arrest at his home in Dighton, Massachusetts, and the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] reported that officials believed the documents came from an [[air-national-guard|Air National Guard]] member at Fort Bragg.[3] Local police blocked the road near the residence from around noon and declined to explain why, with at least one vehicle outside the blockade bearing U.S. government license plates.[3]
