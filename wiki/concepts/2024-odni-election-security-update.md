# 2024 ODNI Election Security Update

The 2024 ODNI Election Security Update was an assessment released on July 29, 2024 by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] alleging that Russia, China, and Iran were all recruiting Americans to spread propaganda advancing their interests ahead of the United States presidential election.[1]

## Findings

According to the update, some U.S. citizens had been knowingly helping foreign governments "seed, promote and add credibility to narratives that serve the foreign actors' interests," while others in the United States had been duped into aiding those actors.[1] Russian operatives in particular were said to have undertaken efforts to build and use networks of American and Western "personalities" to create and disseminate Russian-friendly narratives.[1] The intelligence office wrote that "these actors are seeking to back a presidential candidate in addition to influencing congressional electoral outcomes, undermine public confidence in the electoral process and exacerbate sociopolitical division."[1]

Intelligence officials briefed reporters largely on condition of anonymity and did not name the presidential candidates, but their comments made clear that they believed Russia was trying to help Republican nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] while Iran was seeking to undermine his candidacy.[1] The briefing noted that U.S. intelligence agencies had previously found that Russia sought to bolster Trump in 2016 and 2020.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], read the update as an implicit threat against independent American commentators rather than a neutral security warning.[1] Noting that he himself had authored a series titled "Righteous Russia" arguing that the Russians were comparatively the "good guys" relative to the Obama, Biden, and NATO establishments — and relative to the Azov Battalion, which he accused of nearly a decade of war crimes in the Donbas following the 2014 Maidan Revolution, which he characterized as a coup — he asked whether such writing would "make the cut" under the ODNI's framing.[1] He concluded that "reverse indicators abound in the Info War," and that an intelligence community following "generations of illegal psychological operations on American citizens" with this report could "kindly fu*k off."[1]
