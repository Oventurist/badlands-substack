# Australian Call for a Ban on Memes

In late April 2024, Australian Prime Minister [[anthony-albanese|Anthony Albanese]] appeared to call for a ban on memes on social media, in remarks reported as part of his government's continuing campaign against online speech.[1]

## The remarks

During a press appearance on Wednesday, April 24, 2024, Albanese seemed to lump memes mocking him together with the "misinformation" he argued should be purged from the internet.[1] "Social media platforms have a responsibility to make sure that misinformation isn't got out there," he said, adding that he had noticed that platforms "have removed various sites that were up containing fake images of myself superimposed on other people."[1] He continued: "That's the sort of thing that is going on, on social media. Social media has a responsibility to do the right thing here."[1] The reporting responded that superimposing a politician's face on an image is not illegal and constitutes freedom of expression and humour.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] predicted that Americans would soon be bombarded ad nauseam by foreign voices lecturing them on the dangers of free speech and critical thinking, and telling them that American values are incompatible with those of the lecturers.[1] He urged readers to relieve themselves of any concern for what such figures think, arguing that Americans have for too long allowed others to guilt them into surrendering their culture and heritage and to shame them over the triumphs of their forefathers.[1]

The commentary called for tapping back into what it described as sacred, esoteric knowledge inherent in good, moral people, and framed the choice as between wringing hands over the judgment of "dangerous hedonists" and simply ignoring them while restoring civilization through the power and strength that emanates when the human spirit is engaged in good works.[1] It warned that continued interference would require engagement, noting that the writer's side outnumbers its opponents "a million to one" and that a nonviolent resolution remains well within reach, and closed on the claim that the only thing to fear is one's own insecurity and that America's rebirth is inevitable if people remain true to themselves and one another.[1]
