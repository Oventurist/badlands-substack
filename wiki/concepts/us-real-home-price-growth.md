# US Real Home Price Growth

US real home price growth refers to the change in American residential property prices after adjusting for inflation, a measure that diverged sharply from headline nominal figures during 2022.[1]

## The 2022 divergence

US home prices grew significantly in 2022 even as interest rates climbed higher, but in inflation-adjusted terms the growth rate was far lower.[1] By the fourth quarter of 2022 real growth had fallen to roughly flat year-on-year, the slowest real growth seen in a decade.[1] A widely circulated Visual Capitalist graphic by Dorothy Neufeld compared nominal and real residential property price growth over fifty years using data from the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]], and was republished by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued in the Badlands News Brief that the perception of rising home values is largely an illusion produced by an inflationary banking system, and suggested that the global financial system in use today was designed for subversive purposes.[1] Because people believed home values were rising, he wrote, they refinanced their houses, took on credit card debt, and spent beyond their means.[1]

He extended the question to other distorted measures — prices, wages, resource costs, and monetary value generally — describing the modern financial system as a psychological operation designed to deceive everyone except its designers.[1] In his framing, participants "choose our own enslavement via debt," and the more people who enter that system, the harder it becomes for those outside it to function in areas of civilization where money exerts influence.[1] He closed by asking how the housing boom of the previous decade was fueled and whether the situation differed materially from conditions since fractional reserve banking became the norm.[1]
