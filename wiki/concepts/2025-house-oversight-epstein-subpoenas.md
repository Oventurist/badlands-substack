# 2025 House Oversight Epstein Subpoenas

The 2025 House Oversight Epstein subpoenas were a round of compulsory process issued by the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] on Tuesday, August 5, 2025, seeking testimony and records related to the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] case from a group of former high-ranking federal officials.[1]

## Scope

Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.) formally issued deposition subpoenas compelling testimony from eleven individuals.[1] The targets included former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and former Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]; former FBI Directors [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]; and a string of current and former U.S. Attorneys General — [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]], [[loretta-lynch|Loretta Lynch]], [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]], [[bill-barr|William Barr]], [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] and [[alberto-gonzales|Alberto Gonzales]].[1] The committee also subpoenaed the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for documents tied to Epstein and his network.[1]

## Bipartisan origins

The subpoenas followed action taken on July 23 by the Oversight Committee's Federal Law Enforcement Subcommittee.[1] In a voice vote, the panel approved a motion by Rep. [[scott-perry|Scott Perry]] (R-Pa.) authorizing subpoenas for the named individuals.[1] The subcommittee separately adopted, by an 8–2 vote, a motion from Rep. [[summer-lee|Summer Lee]] (D-Pa.) directing the release of DOJ records related to Epstein.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America called the prospect "must see TV" and pledged that Badlands would "cover every second."[1] She argued that if the former officials defied their subpoenas they should be imprisoned as Steve Bannon and Peter Navarro were, on the theory that those contempt prosecutions established the governing precedent, closing with the exhortation: "Accelerate."[1]
