# Fani Willis Disqualification Hearing

The Fani Willis disqualification hearing was an evidentiary proceeding held in Fulton County, Georgia, in February 2024, in which defense lawyers sought to remove District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] from the Georgia 2020 election interference prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his co-defendants on grounds of a conflict of interest arising from her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[1]

## Willis's testimony

On Thursday, February 15, 2024, Willis took the witness stand and forcefully pushed back against what she described as "lies" about the relationship, in what was characterized as an extraordinary hearing over misconduct allegations threatening to upend one of four criminal cases against Trump.[1] Willis had originally fought to stay off the stand but agreed to testify after a previous witness said the relationship with Wade had begun earlier than the pair had claimed.[1]

Her testimony grew heated under questioning from defense attorney [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]]. At one point Willis raised papers in front of her and shouted, "It's a lie!"[1] She told Merchant: "Do you think I'm on trial? These people are on trial for trying to steal an election in 2020. I'm not on trial no matter how hard you try to put me on trial," and at another point said, "Merchant's interests are contrary to democracy."[1] Willis was expected to return to the stand on Friday, with the hearing resuming at 9 a.m. ET.[1]

## Points of contention

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarized three threads from the first day of testimony.[1] First, Willis said she paid rent for an off-book condominium sometimes in cash but mostly by CashApp, in amounts that were not fixed; the condo was where she spent substantial time with Wade, which she attributed not to concealment of a relationship but to safety concerns at her own home stemming from her record on gang crime — a home where her elderly father and sometimes her daughters stayed.[1] Second, on financial records showing Wade paid for vacations, Willis testified that she reimbursed her share in cash, a habit she attributed to her father's teaching, with no records of the reimbursements existing.[1] Third, Willis said she had not disclosed the relationship to anyone in government because she is a private person and did not believe she had a duty to disclose it.[1]

Ashe in America argued that the county's top law enforcement official had ended up on the stand under oath explaining away evidence of an improper relationship, and noted that even CNN appeared to be distancing itself from the prosecutor.[1] Fellow Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the spectacle differently, saying he did not believe Willis's performance was scripted but did view it as part of a disclosure operation accelerating the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] by setting up conspicuously clownish figures to fail in public view.[1]
