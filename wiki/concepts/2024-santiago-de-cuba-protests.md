# 2024 Santiago de Cuba Protests

In March 2024 hundreds of residents of Santiago, [[cuba|Cuba]]'s second-largest city, staged a rare public protest amid prolonged blackouts and severe food shortages.[1] Protesters took to the streets on Sunday, March 17, chanting "power and food," as outages in some places extended for 18 hours or more per day, spoiling frozen food and heightening tensions across the island.[1]

## Background

Cuba has been mired in a near-unprecedented economic crisis since the COVID-19 pandemic, with vast shortages of food, fuel, and medicine driving a record-breaking exodus in which upwards of 400,000 people migrated to the United States.[1] Protests on the island remain exceedingly rare but have become more frequent in recent years as the economic crisis deepens.[1]

## Government and U.S. response

Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] confirmed the Santiago protest on X shortly after the rally concluded and called for dialogue in an "atmosphere of tranquility and peace."[1] He also asserted that "terrorists" from the United States were seeking to foment further uprisings, warning that "this context will be taken advantage of by the enemies of the Revolution, for destabilizing purposes."[1] The U.S. embassy in Havana said it was monitoring the protests in Santiago and elsewhere, urging the Cuban government "to respect the human rights of the protesters and address the legitimate needs of the Cuban people."[1] Cuban foreign minister Bruno Rodríguez blasted the embassy's comments, saying the U.S. government "must refrain from interfering in the country's internal affairs and inciting social disorder."[1]

## Commentary

[[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that the episode demonstrated that "communism doesn't work," pointing to soaring gas prices, food shortages, and rolling blackouts as the predictable outcome of communist governance.[1] He described Cuba as collapsing with no end in sight for its people, noted that the chaos followed the collapse of Haiti, and observed that the Caribbean situation was beginning to mirror the [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]] that began in 2010.[1] He interpreted the pattern as the deep state "turning all the screws in a last ditch effort to retain power."[1]
