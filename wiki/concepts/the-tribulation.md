# The Tribulation

**The Tribulation** is the period of divine judgement on a sinful and wicked world described in the [[book-of-revelation|Book of Revelation]], and the central question of the Badlands Media essay "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?" is whether the present era qualifies as that period.[1]

## Duration debate

The article notes an unresolved debate over whether the Tribulation is a literal seven-year span — derived from interpretations of the seventieth week of Daniel (Daniel 9) — or a symbolic period.[1] Revelation's three references to three-and-a-half-year periods (11:2–3, 12:6, 13:5) are used by many to support the seven-year reading, two of the periods running in series with one overlapping.[1]

## The twenty-one judgements

John uses seven seals, seven trumpets, and seven bowls to describe God's judgements during this time, twenty-one in total.[1] The article acknowledges debate over the degree of uniqueness, overlap, timing, duration, and literal or symbolic meaning of each.[1]

- The seven seals symbolise a progression of events leading to the final judgment and the establishment of God's Kingdom, representing conquest, conflict, bloodshed, warfare, violence, scarcity, economic hardship, famine, poverty, death, pestilence, beasts, suffering, persecution, martyrdom, and cosmic upheaval.[1]
- The seven trumpets signal both divine judgment and the culmination of God's plan for redemption, representing devastation of natural resources and beasts, contaminated and bitter water, reduced light, increased darkness, spiritual torment and anguish, war, and death.[1]
- The seven bowls represent the culmination of God's wrath, more intense and specific than the preceding judgements, poured out on those who rejected Him.[1]

## The threshold of proof

The author sets an explicit evidentiary bar: to conclude the present era qualifies, he would need credible data showing consequences on the scale described in the fourth seal and sixth trumpet — the death of one quarter and one third of humanity, respectively.[1] He observes the text is unclear whether these are the same event within a range (between 1/4 and 1/3) or sequential events totalling one half, and unclear whether the deaths are immediate or accumulate over time.[1] Examining the root words of the weapons granted to the rider, the four angels, and their army — sword, hunger, death, beasts, fire, smoke, brimstone — he concludes they encompass all types of destruction, warfare, famine, beasts, and death in very broad terms.[1]

## Candidate evidence

For historical scale, the only comparable event cited is the Black Death, which culminated around 1351 and killed by various records between one tenth and one quarter of the global population — yet was plainly not the end.[1] Against a present world population of roughly 8 billion, the author proposes counting not only the dead but everyone who would be alive but is not, owing to abortion, cancer, sterilisation, infertility, destruction of the nuclear family, starvation, thirst, overdose, suicide, murder, war, psychological operations, genocide, eugenics, slavery, and trafficking.[1] He notes that approximately 70 percent of all people received at least a first dose of the COVID-19 vaccine and, writing as a safety professional, says he has been horrified by subsequent death and injury data, asking how many will prove unable to reproduce or develop a lethal condition over time.[1] He cites [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s warnings about population collapse as a further data point.[1]

## Position on the timeline

Reading Revelation 14 — located roughly seven elevenths of the way through the book, after the seal and trumpet judgements and just prior to [[the-beast|the Beast]] and the [[mark-of-the-beast|Mark of the Beast]] — the author notes the blessing pronounced on "the dead who die in the Lord from now on" and takes it as evidence the church has not departed.[1] If the hypothesis holds merit, he suggests the present moment sits near that point: mostly over, much pain behind, some ahead, but God wins.[1]
