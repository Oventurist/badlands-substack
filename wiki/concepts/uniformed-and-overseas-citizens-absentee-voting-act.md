# Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act

The Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA) is the federal statute governing absentee registration and voting by members of the uniformed services and United States citizens residing outside the country.

## Colorado guidance and the 2026 RNC lawsuit

UOCAVA appears in the Badlands corpus through [[badlands-brief-169|"Condemnation, Calibration, & Constitutional Boxes"]], which reports that the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], together with Douglas County Clerk [[sheri-davis|Sheri Davis]] and Representative Jeff Crank, sued Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] in Denver District Court over state guidance interpreting the Act. Under that guidance, certain citizens living abroad may register and vote in Colorado even if they have never personally lived in the state, provided a parent, guardian, spouse or domestic partner previously resided there.

The suit contends that this interpretation violates Colorado's constitutional residency requirements, arguing that residency cannot be established derivatively through family ties and that a person who has never lived in the state does not qualify as a resident. It asks the court to block the policy and to remove the affected voters from the rolls. Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], while sympathetic to the underlying objection, argues that the filing's timing on the eve of the Colorado primary makes failure likely and raises the question of whether a suit brought knowingly to lose amounts to an abuse of legal process.
