# 2025 US Research Spending Cutbacks

The 2025 US research spending cutbacks were the reductions in federal science personnel and funding pursued by the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] in its first weeks, which the *Financial Times* argued carried a heavy long-term price for American innovation.[1]

## Scope of the cuts

Proposed cutbacks in government science personnel and spending were described as large and wide-ranging.[1] Although some were held in abeyance by the courts, there were substantial headcount reductions at the National Institutes of Health, the National Science Foundation, and the Centers for Disease Control, among other bodies.[1] External grants awarded for research at universities were also expected to be sharply curtailed, affecting institutions and scientists across the country.[1]

## Argument

The [[financial-times|Financial Times]] framed the issue as one investors should weigh even though such long-term policy consequences are difficult to price into markets.[1] It noted that the United States had led the world for several decades in its commitment to research and development, outspending all other countries, but now ranks eighth rather than first.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Burning Bright read the moment not as retreat but as evidence of a general capitulation pattern across multiple theaters, all folding in one direction, which he took to mean that not only [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] but the sovereign movement he represents holds the cards.[1] He extended the pattern to the corporate sphere, arguing that [[apple|Apple]], the largest company in the world by market capitalization, was being forced to abandon its globalist ambitions to satisfy Trump's on-shoring agenda, and that the "New Oligarchy" of Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon had long enjoyed both American protections and globalist exploitation — mutually anathema arrangements that made them "movable proxy states unto themselves."[1]
