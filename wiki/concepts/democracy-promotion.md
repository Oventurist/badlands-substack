# Democracy Promotion

"Democracy promotion" is described as a term used by NATO and friends of Anglo-American imperialism to justify warping foreign nations into "open societies."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article treats it as an example of the "language of control" it has referenced in previous posts: euphemistic vocabulary that conceals coercive practice.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

Decoded, the article defines democracy promotion as conforming a nation to the will of the international foreign policy establishment by installing NATO-approved leaders, flooding the country with American corporate franchises, disseminating establishment propaganda, altering a country's legal system via "rule of law programs," and wrangling a country's workforce into supporting Western-sponsored causes by manipulating the labor unions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

Historically the term is presented as a post-Cold War rebranding of anti-communism: U.S. agencies used unions to fracture international solidarity while prioritizing ideological alignment over workers' rights, and after 1983 the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] made democracy promotion its formal mandate — described in the article as code for regime destabilization.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]] is identified as the labor-sector instrument of this agenda, operating in Ukraine, Hong Kong, Myanmar and mineral-rich African states.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## "Democracy promotion" as dual-meaning language (DeLarme, 2025)

In "The Language of Control," Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated "democracy promotion" as one of the clearest examples of a term with a steganographic or dual meaning inside the political establishment's private vocabulary.[2] He noted the phrase is usually associated with Western foreign policy, particularly U.S. and European strategy since the Cold War, and quoted the "whitewashed, Wikipedia definition": a domestic policy to improve the quality of an existing democracy, or a strand of foreign policy by which governments and international organizations support the spread of democracy as a system of government, entailing the consolidation and building of democratic institutions.[2]

In practice, DeLarme argued, "democracy promotion" functions as a pretext for intervention — censorship, election interference and regime change carried out either militarily or through ostensibly nonviolent [[color-revolution|color revolutions]] — abroad and domestically alike.[2] When a target country, typically labelled a "closed society," refuses American demands, operations are staged there and described as democracy promotion; ironically, he wrote, this usually involves ousting a democratically elected leader in favor of one more sympathetic to the [[liberal-international-order|liberal international order]], thereby producing an "open society."[2]

The pattern he described continues after installation: Westernize the country, install a sympathetic leader, and hook it on large loans from institutions such as the [[world-bank|World Bank]], the [[imf|IMF]] or [[usaid|USAID]], leaving it trapped in unpayable debt and beholden to creditors.[2] The label, he wrote, softens a predatory act in the public mind.[2] Recent examples cited were the [[euromaidan|Euromaidan coup]] in Ukraine and the ousting of Evo Morales in Bolivia, both said to be largely funded and orchestrated by the [[state-department|U.S. State Department]], the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and USAID.[2] The brand of democracy being promoted, in his framing, is always their democracy rather than popular self-government.[2]

## Origins as a rebranding of covert influence

Badlands Media traces the term "democracy promotion" to the founding of the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] in 1983, whose stated aim was "to foster the infrastructure of democracy."[3] The outlet argues the phrase became a buzzword used to reframe the subtle but forced spread of Western hegemony after the [[cia|CIA]]'s covert political operations were curtailed by the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] era reforms.[3] After the Cold War, the foreign-policy establishment and military-industrial complex needed a new rationale, and the NED took on an expanded global role assisting the CIA, [[usaid|USAID]] and the [[state-department|State Department]] in democracy-promoting activities worldwide.[3] Following Brexit and the rise of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the article contends, the focus of that apparatus shifted from countering communism to countering [[national-populism|national populism]].[3]
