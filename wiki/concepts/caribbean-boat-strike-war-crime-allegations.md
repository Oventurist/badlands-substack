# Caribbean Boat Strike War Crime Allegations

The Caribbean boat strike war crime allegations refers to a controversy that erupted at the end of November 2025 over whether United States military officials committed a war crime during President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign of lethal strikes on vessels in the Caribbean suspected of smuggling narcotics.[1]

## The reported follow-up strike

The controversy was triggered by a *Washington Post* report published on Friday, 28 November 2025, which said that Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] had given a verbal order to kill everyone aboard boats suspected of smuggling drugs.[1] According to the report, that order led a military commander to carry out a second strike intended to kill those who had initially survived an attack in early September.[1] Killing shipwrecked survivors is generally regarded as a violation of the laws of armed conflict, which is what elevated the report from a policy dispute to an accusation of criminality.[1]

## Congressional reaction

Members of both parties in [[congress|Congress]] responded on Sunday, 30 November 2025.[1] Representative [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]], Republican of Ohio and a former chairman of the House Intelligence Committee, said on CBS's "Face the Nation" that "obviously if that occurred, that would be very serious, and I agree that that would be an illegal act."[1] Senator [[tim-kaine|Tim Kaine]], Democrat of Virginia, said on CBS that if the report was accurate the attack "rises to the level of a war crime."[1] Senator [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]], Democrat of Arizona, was asked on CNN whether a second strike to kill survivors constituted a war crime and answered, "It seems to."[1]

## Context: Venezuela diplomacy

The war crime allegations surfaced at the same moment that Trump confirmed he had spoken by telephone with Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], declining to characterize the substance of the call beyond saying, "I wouldn't say it went well or badly, it was a phone call."[1] The juxtaposition of bellicose rhetoric and lethal maritime operations with an apparent opening to direct diplomacy shaped Badlands commentary, which treated predictions of an imminent land war with [[venezuela|Venezuela]] — and of Maduro fleeing to Turkey — as overheated.[1]
