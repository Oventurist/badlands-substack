# Temporary Protected Status

Temporary Protected Status (TPS) is a US immigration designation that shields nationals of designated countries from removal and permits them to remain lawfully in the United States for a defined period.[1]

## Haitian TPS litigation (2026)

The second Trump administration moved to terminate TPS for Haitian nationals, a change blocked by Judge [[ana-reyes|Ana Reyes]] of the US District Court for the District of Columbia, who barred the termination from taking effect while litigation continues.[1] The ruling preserved protections that had been extended under the Biden administration and were otherwise set to expire.[1]

## Termination for Somalia (January 2026)

In January 2026 the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], under Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], ended TPS for Somali nationals, requiring those currently protected to depart the United States by March 17.[2] Noem stated that "temporary means temporary," citing improved country conditions in [[somalia|Somalia]] that no longer meet the statutory requirements for the designation, and asserting that continued protection is contrary to U.S. national interests under an "Americans first" approach.[2]

TPS for Somalia was originally designated in 1991 because of ongoing civil war and was most recently extended by the Biden administration in September 2024.[2] USCIS data indicated that 2,471 Somali nationals held TPS, with 1,383 applications pending and an estimated 600 beneficiaries residing in [[minnesota|Minnesota]].[2] The decision landed amid a broader confrontation over Somali-linked fraud networks in Minnesota and [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s Truth Social warning that "THE DAY OF RECKONING & RETRIBUTION IS COMING!"[2]

## Venezuelan TPS litigation (2025)

In May 2025, Solicitor General [[d-john-sauer|D. John Sauer]] asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to terminate the temporary protected status of more than 300,000 Venezuelan migrants, which would ease their deportation by the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[3] Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] had announced earlier that year that she would not renew the protection, which permits certain migrants to remain legally in the United States when they cannot return home due to natural disasters, armed conflicts, or other extraordinary events.[3]

## 2025 termination for Venezuelan migrants

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] moved in late January 2025 to end temporary protected status granted to Venezuelan migrants residing in the United States, with Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] announcing the revocation of the deportation protections on Tuesday, January 28, affecting more than 600,000 Venezuelan nationals.[4] Former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had extended TPS for Venezuelans for an additional 18 months shortly before Trump assumed office on January 20.[4] TPS is a designation allowing foreign citizens to remain in the U.S. when conditions in their home country, such as ongoing armed conflict or environmental disaster, prevent a safe return; its extension to Venezuelans was controversial in light of the rapid growth of the [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] gang in the United States.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that every DHS-related X account was alive with news of deportations, that thousands had been detained and removed, and that DHS, the Border Patrol, [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] and many U.S. military sites were publishing footage, concluding that the public supports the action.[4]

## October 2025 Supreme Court order on Venezuelans

On Friday, October 3, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] granted an emergency appeal from the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], allowing it to end temporary legal protections for more than 300,000 Venezuelan citizens in the United States.[5] The majority wrote in an unsigned order that "although the posture of the case has changed, the legal arguments and relative harms generally have not," so "the same result that we reached in May is appropriate here."[5] The Biden administration had twice used TPS to protect an estimated 600,000 Venezuelan migrants; Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] ended the 2023 designation covering 350,000 Venezuelans and sought to revoke two extensions that would have run through October 2026, an effort a San Francisco district judge blocked before the Supreme Court lifted the ruling in the [[2025-supreme-court-ruling-on-venezuelan-temporary-protected-status|2025 Supreme Court ruling on Venezuelan temporary protected status]].[5]
