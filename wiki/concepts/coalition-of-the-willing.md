# Coalition of the Willing (Ukraine)

The "coalition of the willing" is the informal name for a grouping of countries prepared to contribute peacekeeping troops to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] in the event of a negotiated peace deal with [[russia|Russia]].[1]

## Formation

A spokesperson for British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] said on Monday, March 17, 2025, that a "significant number" of countries were willing to provide peacekeeping troops in Ukraine should a peace agreement be reached.[1] Britain and France spearheaded the effort, developing a peacekeeping plan after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] opened talks pressing for a settlement with Russia.[1]

More than 30 countries were expected to be involved.[1] "The contribution capabilities will vary, but this will be a significant force, with a significant number of countries providing troops," Starmer's spokesperson told reporters.[1]

## Russian objection and open questions

Russia has repeatedly rejected the idea of soldiers from [[nato|NATO]] member states being stationed in Ukraine.[1] Asked whether the peacekeeping troops would be permitted to return fire if targeted, the spokesperson said military planning meetings were under way to work through such details.[1]

Badlands commentary framed the initiative as one half of "a tale of two governments," with the United States under Trump moving toward Russia and peace while the [[european-union|European Union]] moved toward Ukraine and war, and questioned whether European military leaders would lead their armies against battle-hardened Russian forces.[1]

## Troop pledges for Ukraine (April 2025)

After a meeting of defense ministers in Brussels on April 10, 2025, AFP reported that only six Western nations had pledged to send troops to Ukraine once hostilities between Kiev and Moscow end, with most of Ukraine's supporters reluctant to make promises.[2] The grouping of some 30 nations, predominantly EU and NATO member states, appeared split on a potential deployment, its members questioning the goals and mandate of the proposed mission.[2] The six identified nations included the United Kingdom, France and the Baltic states of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania; the sixth was not named by the agency.[2] Britain insisted the coalition had actual deployment plans and claimed the troops would contribute to a "lasting peace" between [[russia|Russia]] and [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the undisclosed sixth member and inferred that consternation, "perhaps even fear," was keeping the conflict from spreading across the continent, since no individual country besides Britain, France and the Baltic states appeared willing to treat Russia as an existential foe.[2]

## Trump's intervention on Russian oil (September 2025)

On Thursday, September 4, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] joined a call of the Coalition of the Willing countries, led by French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], who were meeting on ways to help Ukraine repel Russian forces.[3] According to a [[white-house|White House]] official, Trump told European leaders that Europe must stop purchasing Russian oil, which he said was helping Moscow fund its war against Ukraine.[3]

The official said Trump emphasized that Russia had received 1.1 billion euros in fuel sales from the EU in one year, and that European leaders must also place economic pressure on China for funding Russia's war effort.[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] read the episode as continued decoupling and dismissed the sanctions framing as "a worthless story layer."[3]
