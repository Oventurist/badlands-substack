# SALT I

SALT — the Strategic Arms Limitation Talks agreement of 1972 — limited the growth of U.S. nuclear warheads while operating on the presumption that Russia would do the same and that both powers would respect each other's spheres of influence.[1]

The treaty was an extension of the trust-building mechanisms won during the 1960s against the pressure of [[paul-nitze|Paul Nitze]]'s [[committee-on-the-present-danger|Committee on the Present Danger]], including the nuclear test ban treaty, the Open Skies Treaty and the Space Treaty.[1]

Whether U.S. foreign policy should presuppose an honest Soviet intention to adhere to détente and the 1972 SALT treaty, or should instead assume the Soviets were liars intent on imposing their own global world government, was the central question dividing the two warring factions of U.S. intelligence described by [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]] and [[elizabeth-gould|Elizabeth Gould]].[1] The hardline faction around Nitze, [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] and the rising neoconservatives held that this push toward trust and cooperation had to stop, and formed [[team-b|Team B]] in 1976 to reverse it.[1]
