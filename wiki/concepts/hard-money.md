# Hard Money

**Hard money** denotes a monetary system of tradable metal coins, as distinct from paper or fiat currency. Badlands Media describes the American economy of the 1800s as a battle between the Founding Fathers' hard money and the cabal's paper money systems.[1]

Before the bankers succeeded in their machinations, the founding fathers had established an economy of hard money — a system of tradable coins the article calls "the perfect economic system for the American people."[1] Banks began as a convenience market, a way for people to store US-minted coins, but they allowed their bank notes to be openly traded, creating a parallel economy over the official hard money economy and paving the way for [[fractional-reserve-lending|fractional reserve lending]].[1]

The article claims hard money led to a golden age of prosperity and freedom in which Americans owned their money, homes, land and freedom, and embodied an entrepreneurial spirit that barely exists in today's corporate society.[1] [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]] favored hard money over the bankers' paper money, and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is said to have sought to revive this older monetary posture.[1]
