# Hard Money

"Hard money" is the article's term for the system of tradable coin established by the founding fathers of the United States, contrasted throughout with the bankers' paper money.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Before the bankers succeeded in their machinations, the founding fathers had established an economy of hard money — a system of tradable coins described as the perfect economic system for the American people and a far cry from the system in place today.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Banks began as a convenience market, introduced as a way for people to store their U.S.-minted coins, before allowing their bank notes to be openly traded and thereby creating their own economy over the official hard-money economy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article credits hard money with a golden age of prosperity and freedom in which Americans owned their money, their homes, their land, and their freedom, and in which most Americans could embody an entrepreneurial spirit that it says barely exists today.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) By contrast, the modern corporate society is described as crushing small businesses and propelling the elite to stay in control.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

[[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]] favored hard money over the bankers' paper money and was the only U.S. President to completely pay off the national debt.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is described as trying to bring back this old monetary system in a posture reminiscent of Jackson.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)
