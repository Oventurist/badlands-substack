# Iran Suspension of US Talks (2026)

The Iranian suspension of US talks in June 2026 was Tehran's halting of indirect negotiations with Washington, conducted through mediators, in response to Israeli military operations in Gaza and [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[1]

## The suspension and Iranian conditions

[[tasnim-news-agency|Tasnim News Agency]], citing Iranian officials, reported that [[iran|Iran]] considered Lebanon to have been a key condition of a previous ceasefire arrangement and that the ceasefire had now been violated.[1] Iranian negotiators conditioned any future talks on an immediate end to Israeli military operations in Gaza and Lebanon and a complete Israeli withdrawal from areas of southern Lebanon.[1]

## Threatened escalation

Tasnim reported that Iran and allied resistance groups were prepared to respond to Israeli actions, with options including efforts to close the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and to activate pressure points along other strategic waterways such as the [[bab-el-mandeb-strait|Bab el-Mandeb Strait]].[1] Iran's armed forces and allied groups were described as committed to retaliatory measures unless their demands regarding Gaza and Lebanon were met.[1]

## Trump's response

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] subsequently announced a "very productive" call with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], saying Israeli troops would not enter Beirut and that forces already en route had been turned back, and that [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] had agreed through intermediaries to halt all shooting.[1] In a Truth Social post he asserted that "Iran really wants to make a deal, and it will be a good one for the USA and those that are with us," while complaining that "Dumocrats" and "seemingly unpatriotic Republicans" were making his negotiating job harder by "chirping" that he should move faster or slower, go to war or not go to war.[1] His advice to critics: "Just sit back and relax, it will all work out well in the end - It always does!"[1]
