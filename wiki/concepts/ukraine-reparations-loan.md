# Ukraine Reparations Loan

The Ukraine reparations loan is a [[european-commission|European Commission]] proposal to finance [[ukraine|Ukraine]] in 2026 and 2027 using the proceeds of immobilised Russian sovereign assets held in the [[european-union|European Union]] as loan guarantees.[1]

## Structure and legal path

Because decisions of the [[european-council|European Council]] require unanimity, opponents of the scheme were widely expected to be able to block it. Analysis published in October 2025 argued the opposite: that the reparations loan does not need unanimity and therefore could not be vetoed by Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] or Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]], but that it does require the approval of [[belgium|Belgium]] and the [[european-central-bank|European Central Bank]].[1] At Belgium's request — Belgium hosts the clearinghouse [[euroclear|Euroclear]], where the largest tranche of frozen funds sits — the Commission sought to have all 27 EU members share the financial risk.[1]

The Commission expected Orbán not to block the deal outright, while anticipating that initial reporting might nonetheless claim "Hungary blocked it," in which case it planned to point to specific legal justifications.[1] The Commission's working position was that changing the sanctions regime would not require unanimity.[1]

## Scale and context

Western countries blocked an estimated $300 billion of Moscow's funds after the escalation of the Ukraine conflict in February 2022, of which roughly €200 billion ($213 billion) is held by Euroclear.[1] Revenues generated by those assets have already been tapped to bankroll Kiev, and the Commission proposed using proceeds from around €210 billion in frozen assets to guarantee the new loans.[1] The plan advanced as US military aid declined and EU budgets tightened.[1] [[russia|Russia]] has described the steps taken so far as "theft" and vowed retaliation.[1]

## Opposition

Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] called on the EU to increase pressure on Moscow but cautioned the bloc to respect international law in any plan to use the frozen assets, making the remarks in an Italian Senate address ahead of the European Council summit in Brussels.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed Meloni's intervention as following the Trump administration's own stated opposition to seizure, and speculated that the EU charter itself might eventually be challenged by Russia over the bloc's conduct toward Ukraine.[1]
