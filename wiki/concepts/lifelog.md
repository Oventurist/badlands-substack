# LifeLog

**LifeLog** was a project of the Information Processing Techniques Office of [[darpa|DARPA]], within the U.S. Department of Defense.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Design

As quoted in the Badlands Media article *America Has a CIA-Run Economy*, LifeLog aimed to compile a massive electronic database of every activity and relationship a person engages in. The intended data included credit card purchases, websites visited, the content of telephone calls and emails, scans of faxes and postal mail, instant messages, books and magazines read, television and radio selections, physical location recorded via wearable GPS sensors, and biomedical data captured through wearable sensors. The stated high-level goal was to identify "preferences, plans, goals, and other markers of intentionality".[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

A second goal was predictive: to find meaningful patterns in the timeline, infer the user's routines, habits and relationships with other people, organizations, places and objects, and exploit those patterns.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Cancellation and the Facebook coincidence

The LifeLog program was cancelled on 4 February 2004 following criticism of its privacy implications.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] highlights that the same date was the launch day of Facebook, now [[meta-platforms|Meta]], and asserts that much of Facebook's early funding came from [[cia|CIA]] shell companies — presenting LifeLog's shutdown and Facebook's startup as two faces of the same surveillance objective.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## As a wolf in sheep's clothing

In a May 2024 essay, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used LifeLog as his primary illustration of the fable of the wolf in sheep's clothing: when the [[cia|CIA]] ran an information-collecting operation under that name, no one was going to hand over their personal information, but once it was transitioned into a social media site called [[facebook|Facebook]], people freely gave up everything needed to monitor and control them.[2] The reading is developed alongside his other [[big-bad-wolf-parables|big bad wolf parables]], in which he likewise treats *Little Red Riding Hood*'s disclosure to the wolf as the equivalent of willingly surrendering one's information to social media.[2]
