# 2024 Presidential Debate

The June 2024 presidential debate between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is treated in Badlands Media commentary less as a campaign event than as a staged pretext for removing a sitting president.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The "set-up" thesis

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that he does not believe Biden's performance caused the [[deep-state-cabal|Deep State]] to make its shift — what he calls "a changing of the batter."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) His objection is that everyone in the media had known of Biden's cognitive issues for years and had made excuses for him and protected him throughout, so the sudden, universal turn immediately after the debate demands explanation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) His answer is that the decision to replace Biden had already been made before the debate ever occurred, and the debate was chosen as the event that would justify it — which is why the press did not wait for its 4am talking points, "as their orders were already given to them."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

Carlson adds that viewers did not even see the worst of Biden that night, and raises the possibility that the drug regimen that renders Biden temporarily semi-coherent was deliberately withheld beforehand in order to expose his mental state.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He contrasts the debate with Biden's most recent State of the Union address, at which Biden appeared at his most coherent and many observers commented that drugs had been used to sustain him for an hour; the same preparation, he argues, plainly was not used for the debate, at which Biden was "out of it from the very beginning."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Aftermath

Carlson concedes that a reader could reasonably argue the ensuing chaos came simply from the public finally recognising the severity of Biden's condition, but maintains that the vast majority of people already knew Biden was unfit before the debate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Within weeks of the debate came the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]], Trump's selection of [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as running mate, the Republican National Convention, and Biden's withdrawal from the race in favour of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Immediate fallout

The debate, held on the Thursday before the June 29–30 weekend, was described by Axios as showcasing "two Joe Bidens": a veteran president rallying voters in a swing state, and an 81-year-old man struggling to string thoughts together onstage.[3] Biden often looked lost or slack-jawed and delivered meandering answers in a hoarse voice, and a Biden aide told Newsweek that the president was suffering from a cold.[3] Axios reported that the performance shattered the [[white-house|White House]]'s long effort to present Biden as capable of serving until 2029, when he would be 86, an effort that had involved stage-managing minutiae such as his sleep schedule, his orthopedic shoes, his walks to Marine One and his climb aboard Air Force One.[3] Eight current and former Biden officials told Axios that aides had internally seen flashes of an absent-minded Biden but typically brushed them off as ordinary lapses.[3] Aides also told Axios that the time of day determined which Biden appeared: he was dependably engaged between 10am and 4pm, and more prone to verbal miscues and fatigue outside those hours or while travelling abroad — reporting Badlands read as confirmation that Biden sundowns and that the condition is known, accepted and mitigated inside his White House.[3]

ZeroHedge described the debate as having mortally wounded Biden's political career, and the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] editorial board followed with a piece declaring that "the greatest public service Mr. Biden can now perform is to announce that he will not continue to run for re-election."[3] Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued that the Biden campaign's 7+ days of preparation at Camp David constituted an unforgivable miscalculation: the team prepped for the mainstream-media caricature of a sarcastic, attacking Trump so that Biden could play the sympathetic victim, and built post-debate talking points around Trump being a convicted felon, mean and nasty, and a liar.[3] Instead, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was restrained and respectful, followed rules that had been set at Biden's own demand, and allowed Biden to speak — with Biden the only candidate shouting while his microphone was off.[3]

## Scheduling

The debate was arranged on May 15, 2024, when both President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] accepted an invitation from [[cnn|CNN]] to meet on June 27 — a historically early showdown expected to set the tone for the final months of the 2024 campaign.[2] Biden announced his acceptance on X, writing, "I've received and accepted an invitation from @CNN for a debate on June 27th. Over to you, Donald. As you said: anywhere, any time, any place," and Trump told CNN's [[kaitlan-collins|Kaitlan Collins]], "The answer is yes, I will accept."[2] Later that morning both men accepted an [[abc-news|ABC]] invitation for a second debate on September 10.[2]

The agreement capped a rapidly escalating tit-for-tat: Biden's campaign had called for two debates hosted by news organizations and formally told the [[commission-on-presidential-debates|Commission on Presidential Debates]] the president would not participate in its previously scheduled fall debates, while Trump said he was on board with earlier debates and told radio host Hugh Hewitt he would accept any moderator.[2] Biden had only recently told Howard Stern, "I'm happy to debate him," before his campaign laid out proposed terms including June and September dates.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the matchup through what he called the Reverse [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] at the core of the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]]: where the Deep State had used thesis–antithesis–synthesis (problem–reaction–solution) to steer Americans away from their own interests, patriots had inverted it by presenting Trump as thesis, Biden as antithesis, and the [[america-first-movement|America First]] agenda as synthesis.[2]

## Biden's Camp David debate camp

On Thursday, June 20, 2024, one week before the debate, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] entered [[camp-david|Camp David]] in Maryland to begin a full week of preparation, using the secluded rural retreat for intensive coaching before traveling directly to Atlanta.[4] A campaign official said [[ron-klain|Ron Klain]], Biden's former chief of staff, was leading the debate camp preparations, with other senior aides and longtime advisers including campaign co-chair [[cedric-richmond|Cedric Richmond]] on hand.[4] Coverage of the encampment noted that Biden would be 86 at the end of a second term, the oldest president ever to serve.[4]

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], by contrast, was reported to be approaching the debate as just another campaign appearance, dismissing the idea that he required anything resembling Biden's all-hands-on-deck preparation.[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that no amount of preparation would fix Biden's performance and recalled that his team had cancelled a remaining 2020 debate while citing COVID-19.[4] She also highlighted the format: hand-picked moderators, no live audience, and rumors that the candidates would be seated.[4]

## Trump's call for "immediate debates" (February 2024)

Months before the debates were scheduled, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly demanded that he and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] debate as soon as possible, "for the good of the country."[5] Speaking on [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]]'s podcast in early February 2024, Trump urged that more Americans be made aware that Biden "can't do anything," saying: "He can't talk. He can't do anything. He's ruining our country and I don't think he's going to run," and speculating that donors or Biden's family might be behind a withdrawal.[5] "I'd like to call for immediate debates. I'd like to debate him now because we should debate. We should debate for the good of the country," Trump said, adding, "I am officially going to do that."[5] He also repeated an earlier offer to take Biden's Super Bowl interview slot, proclaiming "We'll get very good ratings."[5]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Biden actually accepting a live televised debate would be one of the most striking confirmations of the "Patriots in Control" refrain in the information war, since the attempt would be visibly futile from the establishment's perspective.[5] He noted that Trump had solidified his standing as champion of the people in his 2016 televised performances against Hillary Clinton and again ahead of 2020, and contended that allowing Biden to be savaged on air did not fit an establishment agenda that required persuading enough Americans that Biden legitimately won.[5]

## Aftermath: governors, family recriminations, and the Old Guard

By July 1, 2024, several Democratic state governors were reported to be quietly positioning themselves as potential late substitutes for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] while publicly professing loyalty, watching for slippage in his polling and a loss of donor support over the following two weeks; one governor told Politico that "the temperature is high" among party state leaders.[6] [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] defended the president immediately after the debate, saying he would "never turn my back" on him, but was widely read as ambiguous when he said he was ready "to step up."[6] Reporting noted that even in an open convention, [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] would be the clear front-runner — a point Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted while observing that Harris typically polled several points behind Biden.[6]

Separately, Politico reported that Biden's family privately trashed his top campaign advisers at a Camp David gathering that weekend and urged him to fire or demote members of his political high command, singling out [[anita-dunn|Anita Dunn]], her husband [[bob-bauer|Bob Bauer]] — who played [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in rehearsals — and former chief of staff [[ron-klain|Ron Klain]], who ran point on debate prep.[6] Complaints included insufficient preparation to go on the attack, over-emphasis on defending Biden's record instead of a second-term vision, and the president being overworked and under-rested; campaign spokesperson Kevin Munoz said Biden retained strong confidence in his aides and a senior aide denied frustration was aimed at the three.[6]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a competing reading, noting that despite public panic, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] set the narrative for the "Old Guard" by standing pat with Biden even as friendly media outlets turned on him.[6] Drawing on [[devolution]] framing and the research of [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], and on a discussion with [[just-human|Just Human]] on the program DEFECTED, he argued that the establishment's fear was not that it might have to replace Biden but that it might be unable to.[6] Ashe in America separately relayed [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s report that a reliable source said Obama was now backing an open convention, which if true would mean Democratic politicians were lying to their voters about their intentions.[6]

The debate's shockwaves reached abroad as well: a Haaretz op-ed argued that Trump's dominant performance and Biden's evident weakness signaled a likely Trump victory that would be bad for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], since Trump "feeds on weakness" and regards the Israeli prime minister — unable to destroy Hamas, mired in Gaza, facing economic deterioration and a probable International Criminal Court arrest warrant — as a loser.[6]
