# Biden Authorization of Long-Range Strikes Inside Russia (2024)

In mid-November 2024, senior U.S. officials confirmed that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had authorized Ukraine's military to use U.S.-provided long-range missiles against targets inside Russian territory, a reversal of a standing restriction that had governed American weapons transfers throughout the war.[1] The decision was first reported by The New York Times and subsequently confirmed to Fox News on a Sunday in November 2024.[1]

## Scope of the decision

According to a senior U.S. official, the authorized strikes would focus mostly on the [[kursk-incursion|Kursk]] region of western Russia.[1] A second official said it was unclear whether Biden intended to approve use of the missiles beyond the Kursk region.[1] Officials described the decision as having been spurred by Russia's move to bring some 10,000 North Korean soldiers into the fighting against Ukrainian forces in Kursk.[1]

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had previously stated that U.S. approval of such strikes would constitute an act of war by the United States against Russia; he had not publicly reacted at the time of the initial reporting.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media characterized the authorization as "more seditious treason," arguing that it came less than two weeks after American voters had delivered what the outlet called a strong mandate for a change in leadership, and that the outgoing party was responding by taking the single step Putin had repeatedly warned would provoke all-out nuclear war.[1]

The commentary connected the reported decision to earlier claims by Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]], who had asserted that certain officials within the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] intervened in September 2024 to block the transfer and use of long-range missiles, and that Biden had been visibly angry at being told he could not authorize them.[1] The piece raised the possibility that the November reporting was narrative escalation intended to provoke Moscow rather than an executed policy, and asked whether the same Pentagon officials would again intervene — invoking the notion of "ghosts in the machine."[1]
