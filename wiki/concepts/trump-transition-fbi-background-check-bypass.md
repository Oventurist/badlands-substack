# Trump Transition FBI Background Check Bypass

The Trump transition FBI background check bypass refers to the decision by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 transition team to forgo traditional [[fbi|FBI]] background investigations for at least some Cabinet selections, relying instead on private vetting firms to screen candidates for administration jobs.[1]

## Rationale

People close to the transition planning said Trump and his allies regarded the FBI's system as slow and beset by problems that could stall the president-elect's plan to begin implementing his agenda immediately.[1] Critics of the traditional process have long noted that intrusive background checks sometimes surface embarrassing material later used to inflict political damage.[1]

The discussions coincided with several contested selections, including [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] for attorney general and [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] for director of national intelligence.[1] Reporting emphasized that the president holds final authority over whom he nominates and with whom he shares intelligence, regardless of protocols established in the wake of the Second World War to ensure appointees lacked unknown foreign ties or other national security vulnerabilities.[1]

Circumventing the checks nonetheless represented a break with a long-established Washington norm and reflected Trump's mistrust of the national security establishment he characterizes as the [[deep-state|Deep State]]; sources said he had privately questioned the need for law enforcement background checks at all.[1] Dan Meyer, a national security attorney in Washington, told CNN that the incoming administration "doesn't want harmony" and that its officials "don't want the FBI to coordinate a norm; they want to hammer the norm."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media read the move through a historical analogy to the 1960s, when [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]] convened the informal advisory body known as [[executive-committee-of-the-national-security-council|ExComm]] to keep peacemaking efforts siloed from the [[joint-chiefs-of-staff|Joint Chiefs]] and the intelligence agencies.[1] The commentary noted that officials excluded from those deliberations derisively labeled the circle of Kennedys and Boston-area childhood friends — including [[kenneth-odonnell|Kenneth O'Donnell]] — the "Irish Mafia," and that the group used backchannels to Soviet leader [[nikita-khrushchev|Nikita Khrushchev]] to avert war.[1]

Applying the parallel to Trump's appointments, the outlet argued that a "new American Mafia" was forming around the president-elect, quoting the line "we went around them like nobody's gone around them before" and concluding that the Deep State was the party most affected.[1]
