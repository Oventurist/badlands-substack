# 1973 Chilean Coup

On September 11, 1973, Chilean President Salvador Allende was overthrown in a bloody military coup that ended a brief experiment in democratic socialism and took the lives of Allende and thousands of Chilean workers, students and political activists.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

Quoting Tim Shorrock's 2003 piece in The Nation, the article states that many trade unionists remain haunted by the knowledge that their own federation, the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]], played a key role in the U.S. campaign — led by the Nixon administration and the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] — to destabilize Chile in the years before the coup.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) From 1971 to 1973 the federation's [[american-institute-for-free-labor-development|American Institute for Free Labor Development]], one of four U.S.-government-funded labor institutes created during the Cold War, channeled millions of dollars to right-wing unions and political parties opposed to Allende's socialist agenda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

That aid helped finance the revolt by Chile's professional class and fanned the flames of social unrest that provided the pretext for General Augusto Pinochet's violent crackdown and the justification for his seventeen-year dictatorship.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)
