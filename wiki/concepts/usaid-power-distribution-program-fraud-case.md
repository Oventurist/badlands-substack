# USAID Power Distribution Program Fraud Case

The USAID Power Distribution Program fraud case was a federal prosecution arising from kickbacks taken on a [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]]-funded power distribution program (PDP) in Pakistan.[1]

## The prosecution

Stephen Paul Edmund Sutton, 53, a United Kingdom citizen, pleaded guilty and was sentenced in May 2025 for his participation in the scheme, which he perpetrated while employed by a contracting firm implementing the USAID-funded program.[1] The case was announced by U.S. Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Ferris Pirro]] and Acting Assistant Inspector General for Investigations Sean Bottary.[1]

Sutton pleaded guilty to one count of conspiring to commit theft concerning a program receiving federal funds, a felony.[1] In his role as a Logistics Operations Manager he took kickbacks of USAID funds used to pay for services rendered.[1] After fighting extradition for more than two years, he was extradited to the United States.[1] District Court Judge [[amit-mehta|Amit P. Mehta]] sentenced Sutton to time served and one day of supervised release, and ordered that he be turned over to immigration authorities.[1]

## Coverage

The May 21, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] carried the Justice Department announcement as a bonus item, consistent with the outlet's sustained attention to USAID as a vector of foreign-aid fraud and influence operations.[1]
