# People ex rel. Attorney General v. Naglee

*People ex rel. Attry. Gen. v. Naglee*, 1 Cal. 234 (1850), is an early California Supreme Court decision cited in Badlands Media commentary as authority for the proposition that the individual American states retain the full attributes of sovereign nations.[1]

## The cited passage

The article quotes the decision as holding that "[e]ach state possesses all the powers of an independent and sovereign nation, except so far as they have been ceded away by the constitution. The federal government is but the creature of the people of the states, and, like an agent appointed for definite and specific purposes, must show an express or necessarily implied authority in the charter of its appointment to give validity to its acts."[1]

## Role in the argument

The passage supplies the agency framing at the center of the article's thesis: the federal government is not a superior sovereign but a delegated agent whose acts are valid only insofar as they trace to enumerated powers.[1] Combined with the preamble's language about forming "a more perfect Union" rather than a new nation, the author, [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], concludes that Americans live in "a nation that has outsourced a small portion of its authority to the union."[1]

The case is presented in sequence with [[cherokee-nation-v-georgia|Cherokee Nation v. Georgia]], [[chisholm-v-georgia|Chisholm v. Georgia]], [[robinson-v-norato|Robinson v. Norato]], and dictionary definitions from [[blacks-law-dictionary|Black's Law Dictionary]] distinguishing a national government from a federal one.[1]
