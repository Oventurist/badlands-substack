# Mainstream Media

The **mainstream media** (MSM) is treated in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as the principal instrument through which the [[cabal|Cabal]] maintains narrative control over the American public.[1]

## Scope and definition

In a February 14, 2024 essay, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] describes explaining to critics of his [[donald-trump|Trump]] support that he does not get his information from the mainstream media, which he believes is compromised and paid to report disinformation.[1] He notes that interlocutors commonly protest that they also watch FOX, to which he replies that FOX is mainstream media; when they add the Washington Post and the New York Times, he replies that these are mainstream media as well.[1] By contrast he cites alternative media sources and people he trusts directly on [[x-corp|X]], [[truth-social|Truth Social]] and [[rumble|Rumble]], adding that he does not accept what he hears at face value but researches it independently.[1]

## The Gollum analogy

Carlson compares Americans who believe and defend the mainstream media to Gollum in *Lord of the Rings*, who guards a ring that is slowly destroying him yet cannot let it go.[1] On this reading the Cabal, acting through the media, is slowly killing the very people who continue to protect it.[1] He argues these people have been manipulated into believing they are in the right and are protecting lies as though they were a precious ring, because accepting that everything they know is false is too hard to bear — though many, he says, are reaching the point where they can no longer block out the truth.[1]

## Declining trust

Carlson reports that only one third of Americans now have any trust in the media and 40% have zero trust in it, and that 20,000 media jobs were lost in 2023 alone.[1] From this he draws a tactical conclusion: rather than attacking individual believers, critics should attack the media institution itself and continue encouraging people to let go of their trust in it, an approach he considers less personal and therefore more effective.[1] He adds that trying to win over strangers online is like Don Quixote battling windmills, since a stranger cannot deprogram someone unwilling to listen.[1]

## Role in division

Because media-driven division between Left and Right is what the Cabal requires to retain control — a dynamic Carlson likens to a Chinese finger trap — undermining media trust is presented as a precondition for the national unity summarised by [[wwg1wga|"Where we go one, we go all"]].[1] He distinguishes the sincere vocal Left, misled into protecting the system, from the Cabal elites who are the actual enemy.[1]

## Coverage of the Q movement

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] describes a four-stage media response to [[qanon|Q]]: total silence while the posts circulated only on the chan boards; mockery once they reached a wider audience, with followers painted as cult members; and, when ridicule failed to stem growth, infiltration of the movement by the [[deep-state|Deep State]] through paid shills and bought influencers.[2] He credits the resulting campaign with unusual effectiveness, arguing the Deep State has not been given enough credit for how well it discredited Q and that the character assassination worked thoroughly on what he calls a mind-controlled population.[2]

On his account the payoff was a reusable dismissal: any exposure of elite crime could be labelled "crazy QAnon conspiracy" and thereby ignored.[2] He applies this to public acceptance of Covid lockdowns and vaccines, confidence in the security of the 2020 election, and the conflation of [[january-6-capitol-protest|J6]] demonstrators with Q followers — in each case, he writes, dissent was reframed as QAnon rhetoric and audiences turned instead to whatever shiny object the media offered.[2] The social effect he identifies is that most people who heard of Q only after it had been ridiculed never accepted it, being unwilling to stand against an apparent public majority or to tolerate mockery.[2] Carlson's inference is that Q's silence weakened the device, since information emerging two years after the last post is harder to brush off as a QAnon theory.[2]
