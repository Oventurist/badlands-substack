# Mainstream Media

"Mainstream media" (MSM), also called **centralized media** in the Badlands corpus, refers to the news output of the small number of corporate conglomerates that have dominated broadcast, print, internet, radio and film. The term and its opposite, decentralized media, are defined at length in A Swan Song for the MSM.

## The Big Six

The article states that as of 2017 six conglomerates lorded over nearly all mainstream media: [[time-warner|Time Warner]], [[comcast|Comcast]], [[walt-disney-company|Disney]], [[sony|Sony]], National Amusements — now [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] — and [[rupert-murdoch|Rupert Murdoch]]'s [[news-corp|NewsCorp]]. Very little existed outside their umbrellas.

## Common ownership

The source argues that the apparent competition among these firms is illusory because they are mutually owned by the world's largest institutional investors: [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[vanguard-group|the Vanguard Group]], [[state-street|State Street]] and [[berkshire-hathaway|Berkshire Hathaway]]. Representatives of these asset managers are described as regular participants in elite policy forums such as the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]. The conclusion drawn is that "the 'big six' are just the propaganda arm of the big 1—the Western establishment, the system of systems."

## Function and critique

Historically, the article states, the primary mandate of a free press was to act as a counterbalance keeping establishment power in check. Its central charge is that this relationship has inverted: "the media is not only failing in its role of keeping the establishment in check, the media IS the establishment," operating as a propaganda arm of the state in tandem with the intelligence apparatus and the [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]]. The mechanism is described using [[bernard-cohen|Bernard C. Cohen]]'s agenda-setting observation that the press is "stunningly successful in telling its readers what to think about."

The essay also charges the sector with partisan mediocrity, arguing that [[cnn|CNN]] and [[msnbc|MSNBC]] on one side and [[fox-news|Fox News]] on the other are "two heads on the same wretched snake," each supplying establishment talking points while avoiding the topics covered by independent outlets such as [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]] and [[consortium-news|Consortium News]].

## Decline

The 2024 article documents an industry-wide contraction — layoffs at the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]], [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]], [[nbc-news|NBC News]], [[business-insider|Business Insider]], [[sports-illustrated|Sports Illustrated]] and [[national-geographic|National Geographic]], and the closure of [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed News]] — and predicts the sector's replacement by [[citizen-journalism|citizen journalism]].

## The "Media Industrial Complex"

The Badlands Media essay All Assets Deployed (November 16, 2022) refers to the establishment press as the "Media Industrial Complex," describing it as an institution that "has been tearing at" [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "for the better part of eight years" and that had already branded his supporters as election-denying extremists before his [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] announcement. The essay argues that dissenting voices within the [[maga|MAGA]] coalition risk advancing "the same divisive, defeating and directed rhetoric the Media Industrial Complex has been vomiting all along," making media narrative and intra-movement discord two faces of the same [[information-war|information war]].
