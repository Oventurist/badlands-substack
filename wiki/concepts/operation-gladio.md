# Operation Gladio

Operation Gladio is the name given to the NATO-linked "stay-behind" networks that, in Badlands Media's treatment, functioned as a long-running apparatus for covert political manipulation and proxy warfare rather than as a purely defensive Cold War contingency.[1]

## Turkish hub

In its December 2024 coverage of Turkish policy toward Syria, the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] noted that Turkey has been a key component of [[nato|NATO]] since 1952 because of its geography relative to Russia and the Black Sea.[1] Citing analysis by Colonel Towner-Watkins, the Brief stated that Turkey has served as a major hub for Operation Gladio, and argued that the Islamist militant forces then advancing out of Idlib toward Aleppo, Hama and Homs appeared to be "yet another iteration of that long-running op."[1]

## Connection to Syria and ISIS

The same commentary linked the Gladio framework to the pipeline by which Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was said to have shipped weapons out of Benghazi in 2012 through Turkey and into Syria, an effort the Brief described as the origin of [[isis|ISIS]] and as necessarily coordinated with Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]].[1] The author argued that NATO, the [[cia|CIA]] and British intelligence remain deeply entrenched in Turkey and may still exert influence over Erdogan through coercion, a control mechanism he judged brittle and likely to fail.[1]

## Invoked in the 2024 Fico shooting commentary

Following the May 2024 shooting of Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] and the widening of the police investigation beyond a lone-wolf theory, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that his money was on 'the Ukrainian puppet-masters from Langley, Virginia using Operation Gladio networks and tactics.'[2]

## Lemnitzer and the stay-behind apparatus

Historian [[anton-chaitkin|Anton Chaitkin]], quoted by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], described General [[lyman-lemnitzer|Lyman Lemnitzer]]'s move to Paris as head of [[nato|NATO]] military forces after being sacked as Joint Chiefs' chairman, where he inherited "a continent-wide covert apparatus of Mafia killers, Hitler Nazis and Mussolini fascists, French colonial diehards, and white mercenaries fuming about the loss of Africa."[3] Chaitkin wrote that this was the stay-behind network constructed after World War II by the British Secret Service with help from Dulles and logistical support from Lemnitzer himself, and that its existence was not publicly revealed until October 1990, when Italian Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti disclosed the network later named after its Italian section, Gladio.[3] Ehret attributed to this apparatus the murders of [[ngo-dinh-diem|Diem]], Trujillo, Lumumba, Mattei, [[john-f-kennedy|JFK]], [[robert-f-kennedy|RFK]] and Martin Luther King Jr.[3]

## Watkins primer: Gladio as a generic term

In a June 2024 primer for Badlands Media, Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] argued that "Operation Gladio" is best understood not as a single program but as a generic term for the [[strategy-of-tension|Strategy of Tension]] — the deliberate manufacture of chaos so that control can be imposed in response.[4] She framed a forthcoming series around the claim that Jim Jones and Jonestown, the Unification Church, the Israeli attack on the USS Liberty, SS officer [[otto-skorzeny|Otto Skorzeny]], and the assassinations of John F. Kennedy and Patrice Lumumba all share a common thread, and that the relocation of [[nato|NATO]] headquarters from Paris to Belgium was related to it.[4]

Watkins traced the operational lineage to [[stay-behind-units|stay-behind units]]: caches of weapons, explosives, communications gear and money left with small cells of civilians who would create chaos behind enemy lines if territory were lost.[4] She held that the postwar network was inherited from the Axis [[werewolf-units|werewolf units]] organized by [[reinhard-gehlen|Reinhard Gehlen]] and [[karl-wolff|Karl Wolff]], with Skorzeny running much of the training.[4]

## Anti-communism as a pretext

A central claim of the primer is that the label "communist" was applied by what Watkins calls the [[international-syndicate|International Syndicate]] to any leader who refused resource-extraction terms — typically 14% of net profit to the host country against 86% for the syndicate — and that renegotiation or nationalization triggered the NATO paramilitary capability of Gladio to overthrow the government.[4] She offered Central America as the illustration: the [[nicaraguan-contras|Contras]] were funded as "the good guys" by the Reagan and Bush administrations while the [[sandinista-national-liberation-front|Sandinistas]] were cast as communists, though in her account the Sandinistas were fighting a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-installed dictator after a prior government objected to [[united-fruit-company|United Fruit]] holding 42% of the country's farmable land, acquired by bribing officials to confiscate it from middle-class coffee farmers.[4] Once the dictator fell, she wrote, Special Forces, Gladio-trained Cuban exiles and mercenaries were sent to topple the new government.[4]

## Continuity to the present

Watkins argued the network remains active, pointing to Ukraine, where the [[right-sector|Right Sector]] and [[azov-battalion|Azov]] battalions trace their heritage to Skorzeny-trained [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] and [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]] of the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]], now a Ukrainian political party, and to Taiwan, where the [[kuomintang|KMT]] party descends from the army of [[chiang-kai-shek|Chiang Kai-shek]].[4] She also contended that media manipulation of the [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]] type predates the CIA entirely.[4]

## Funding: narcotics, trafficking and the mafia

The second installment of the Badlands Media Gladio series argues that the operation was financed through organized crime absorbed into the [[cia]].[5] Colonel [[paul-helliwell]] first proposed incorporating organized crime into the intelligence community after meeting [[chiang-kai-shek]], commander of the [[kuomintang]], while assigned in Southern China.[5] Helliwell, [[allen-dulles]], [[james-jesus-angleton]] and [[mi6]]'s [[william-stephenson]] designed a network funnelling money through Helliwell to the [[world-commerce-corporation]] created by OSS chief [[william-donovan]].[5]

Opium came from Chiang, who was resettled on Formosa — renamed [[taiwan]] — by [[claire-chennault]], [[william-pawley]] and Dulles.[5] Refining was arranged through the [[corsican-mafia]] by [[lucien-conein]], who worked alongside [[e-howard-hunt]].[5] American distribution ran through [[lucky-luciano]] and [[meyer-lansky]]'s [[the-commission-mafia|Commission]], with [[vito-genovese]] building the inner-city network and [[santo-trafficante]] playing the largest trafficking role from Cuba, Miami and Tampa.[5] The whole pipeline was code-named "[[operation-x]]", its finances managed through the Miami shell company [[general-development-corporation]] and, later, the [[vatican-bank]].[5]

Enabling events included [[operation-underworld]] and [[operation-husky]], after which mafia figures were installed as officials across occupied Italy, and the commutation of Luciano's sentence by Governor [[thomas-dewey]] under pressure from Secretary of the Navy [[james-forrestal]], Dulles and the [[office-of-naval-intelligence]].[5] The article notes that [[benito-mussolini]] had suppressed both the mafia and the masonic lodges in Italy, and that both made a roaring comeback under Gladio.[5] Its conclusion is that aspects of the United States government resemble an international crime syndicate posing as a government.[5]
