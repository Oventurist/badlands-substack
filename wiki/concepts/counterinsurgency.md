# Counterinsurgency

**Counterinsurgency (COIN)** is defined, in the formulation cited from Q drop #4363, as "comprehensive civilian and military efforts taken to simultaneously defeat and contain INSURGENCY and address its root causes."[1]

In "[[trump-doctrine-peace-through-space|Trump Doctrine: Peace Through Space]]," counterinsurgency supplies the interpretive key to Q drop #1350, which references a network of brave, united men and women whose strength and fight are sufficient to bring about peace and which closes with the phrase "[[peace-through-strength|Peace Through Strength]]" — read by the author as indicating a non-kinetic, non-violent solution.[1] Where many equate that network with the US military, the author and [[gmoney|GMONEY]] argue it is more correctly equated with a counterinsurgency comprising both civilian and military elements.[1]

Supporting this reading, [[michael-flynn|General Michael Flynn]] is quoted as saying "We have an army of Digital Soldiers" and "This was run like an insurgency."[1] The COVFEFE 2.0 Dig, co-authored by GMONEY and the article's author, extends the framework across Trump, insurgency, counterinsurgency, legitimacy, JFK, secret societies, shadow governments, [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and non-kinetic power projection.[1]

## Lange's application of COIN doctrine

In "What's Taking So Long?" (December 28, 2022), [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] used the doctrine as the organising framework for his answer to the question of why justice against the alleged insurgency had been so slow in coming.[2] Drawing on the [[counterinsurgency-guide|Counterinsurgency Guide]] linked by Q in posts #4008, #4363, #4364 and #4700, he stressed the word "comprehensive" — meaning complete and including everything necessary — and argued that the plan requires simultaneous civilian and military efforts, the civilian side playing out publicly in the courts and the media while the military side remains clandestine.[2]

Lange summarised the guide's account of insurgent political strategy as a combination of persuasion (ideology, money, social services, control of land, positions of authority, and above all propaganda), subversion (penetrating, manipulating, undermining or disrupting government institutions), and coercion (intimidation and violence against government supporters and community leaders).[2] He held that this was the environment [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] faced on the day he was sworn in, and that a military plan had been in place before Trump announced his candidacy, making him a wartime president in an unconventional war whose main battlefield is the media.[2]

He then mapped the guide's four functional components onto events since 2016.[2] The **information** component he identified with the Q operation itself and with the "digital army of truth tellers" described by [[michael-flynn|General Flynn]], arguing the establishment media's lies were now exposed within hours and its narrative control broken.[2] The **security** component he identified with security sector reform, and specifically with exposing corruption in the justice institutions — citing [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]]'s exposure of the Russian collusion framing and Congress's planned oversight investigations of the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[justice-department|DOJ]] in 2023.[2] The **political** component he identified with the [[make-america-great-again|MAGA]] slogan, which he said forced the insurgency to campaign against popular policies while defending the Obama–Biden record.[2] The **economic** component he identified with job creation, energy independence, tax cuts and deregulation under Trump.[2]

Lange also applied the guide's three phases of control.[2] **Establishment** he associated with tariffs, cancelled trade deals, the border wall, expanded ICE enforcement and Trump's "Fake News" branding of the press.[2] **Consolidation** — described in the guide as the longest phase, lasting years or decades, and as being about control of population groups rather than territory — he identified as the phase then under way, warning with the guide that consolidation must proceed neither too slowly nor too quickly.[2] **Transition**, the handover from military forces to local civilian authority, he presented as the destination, to be reached through a court-triggered constitutional crisis followed by a temporary military role and a new election.[2]

He further quoted the guide's observation that COIN is population-centric rather than enemy-centric, that it always involves loss of life, and that it is "highly controversial politically," involving "ambiguous events" that are "extremely difficult to interpret" — a passage he used to explain both the slow pace of justice and episodes such as the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]].[2]
