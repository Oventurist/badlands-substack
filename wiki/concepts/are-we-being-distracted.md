# Are We Being Distracted?

"Are We Being Distracted?" is an opinion essay published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on August 1, 2024, written by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] and subtitled "Obviously ... But What Are We Being Distracted From?" The article argues that the extraordinary run of news events in July 2024 — the assassination attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s withdrawal from the presidential race, [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s elevation to the Democratic ticket, and the opening ceremony of the [[paris-2024-olympics-opening-ceremony|Paris Olympics]] — functioned collectively as a [[bread-and-circus|bread and circus]] operation designed to divide and distract the public from what the author regards as the real story: Trump's intention to dismantle and rebuild the American financial system.

## Method: asking what is being obscured

Carlson opens with a heuristic he says he has developed over recent years. When his social media feed becomes saturated with a single strange story or narrative, and it appears that "we've reached peak craziness," he immediately asks, "what am I being distracted away from?" He observes that the dissident community is "really good at finding things to be outraged over, but in the end, we rarely actually do anything about it" — some protest school board meetings, others boycott with their wallets, but most simply get upset and move on to the next controversy.

## The financial thesis

The article restates and extends an argument Carlson made in his earlier essay [[a-week-to-remember|A Week to Remember]]. He contends that the chaos of mid-2024 did not begin with Biden's exposure of his diminished mental state during the presidential debate; rather, the decision to remove Biden was made before the debate. In Carlson's reading, Trump signalled to the [[deep-state|Deep State]] that he intended, on returning to the White House, to "tear down the walls of the financial system to its foundation," and he did so through the seemingly minor policy of [[tax-free-tips|tax-free tips]].

The chain of reasoning is set out explicitly: "Tax-free tips lead to a capped income tax, which leads to the abolishment of income tax, which leads to the destruction of the financial system as we know it." The turbulence of July 2024, Carlson writes, "has nothing to do with Biden and the Democrats, and has everything to do with Trump letting the Deep State know their control of the American financial system is almost at its end." He notes that Trump left the financial system intact during his first presidency because "the timing wasn't right then," and argues that the demolition is planned to begin in 2025.

## The assassination attempt as a distraction

Carlson asks his readers how many of them spent the previous month focused on the economy, and how many instead focused on identifying who was responsible for the assassination attempt on Trump — how many shooters there were, who fired the bullet that struck Trump, and with whom the alleged shooter had been in contact. He says his own first reaction was that the event "couldn't have happened the way they are saying it happened," and notes that within a week [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson Lee]] died, Biden stepped down, and [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]] testified before [[congress|Congress]] regarding Jackson Lee's oversight of the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]].

He then declines to pursue the question further, asserting that "Trump's people already know exactly who was responsible for the shooting and who controls them," and asking why he should spend months trying to determine what Trump's team already knows. The attempt on Trump's life, in his framing, was an effort to end what he calls the hostile takeover of the Deep State, premised on the mistaken belief that the movement would die with Trump. It would not, he writes: "If Trump died today, the patriots would continue on. Don't think for a second there isn't a contingency plan."

## Bread and circus, and the lukewarm citizen

The essay invokes the Roman formula of bread and circuses: keeping the people entertained and fed makes them content, distracted, and easily controlled, and "content and distracted people tend not to stand up to, or in some cases, even notice government oppression." Carlson adds a corollary of his own — that "lukewarm is the most controllable state." People at the moderate centre complain but rarely act, and he argues that political views are deliberately manipulated toward the middle precisely because it is those at the extremes who protest and who actually change policy.

## The Paris Olympics opening ceremony

A substantial portion of the article addresses the July 26, 2024 [[paris-2024-olympics-opening-ceremony|Olympic opening ceremony]] in Paris, which Carlson agrees was genuinely offensive but treats as engineered outrage: "do you think this wasn't their intention? Do you think anger isn't exactly the reaction they wanted from the world?" He argues that a divided nation is a weak nation and a divided world a dangerous one, and that division and distraction together make populations controllable.

He observes that the [[international-olympic-committee|Olympic Committee]] had, in his account, effectively barred transgender athletes from competition unless they had fully transitioned by the age of twelve, on the ground that later transition confers an unfair advantage. From this he infers that the committee does not in fact care about transgender people or their rights: "They are being used by the Cabal to upset and distract the world," while believing themselves appreciated. He notes as a supporting observation that female-to-male athletes do not appear to compete against men, whereas male-to-female athletes frequently dominate women's fields, and remarks that if transgender athletes "were finishing in the middle of the pack, no one would care."

Carlson also singles out the ceremony's tableau resembling the Last Supper, describing the figure at the head of the table in deliberately provocative terms — "a morbidly obese, lesbian, Jewish woman" — and asking, "Can you possibly check off any more boxes? Can you create any more hate and disgust?" His prescription is not approval but self-awareness: readers should admit when they have been played and move on. He notes, comparatively, that his own outrage lasted a week rather than the two years of [[covid-19-pandemic|Covid]] or the ten years that followed 9/11, and calls this "marked improvement."

## Trump at the Bitcoin Conference

The concealed story, in Carlson's view, was what Trump was doing while the Olympic controversy raged: speaking at the [[bitcoin-2024-conference|Bitcoin Conference]]. There, according to the article, Trump stated clearly that he saw [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] as an important part of the American economy going forward and that the U.S. government should stockpile it. Carlson is careful to disclaim investment advice — he is not telling anyone to buy cryptocurrency — and stresses that Trump was not proposing that Bitcoin replace the dollar but that it be used to strengthen it: "By the US Government stockpiling Bitcoin, every American benefits."

In the same vein, he suggests readers consider why Trump chose [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as his running mate, pointing to Vance's connections to Big Tech and cryptocurrency, rather than following "legendary journalists reporting on who forced Biden out" and how Biden retaliated by endorsing Harris.

## Division, anger and fear

Carlson catalogues the emotional effects he attributes to the sequence of events: 30% of liberals believing the Trump shooting was staged by Trump himself; widespread perception that Harris is neither likeable nor competent; and offence taken at the opening ceremony by "Jews, Christians, Muslims, Hindus, Buddhists and agnostics alike." He argues that fear compounds the division, since Americans already knew what a Biden presidency looked like whereas Harris represents an unknown, and "unknown situations are the scariest."

## Conclusion

The essay closes with an exhortation rather than a prediction. Carlson tells readers not to support anything they find morally repulsive — "Vote with your dollars, vote with your conscience, and vote these puppets out of office" — but to recognise manipulation when it occurs and to seek out what the manipulation conceals. He asserts that "the patriots are in fact in control," that they are not distracted or manipulated even if offended, and that "the dismantling of the old guard is underway, and nothing can stop it from happening." The final lines are religious: "While God is mocked today, he will get the last laugh. In the end, God wins. In the end, we win."

The article carries Badlands Media's standard disclaimer that contributor pieces represent the opinions of their authors and not necessarily the views of Badlands Media itself, and directs readers to Carlson's personal Substack.
