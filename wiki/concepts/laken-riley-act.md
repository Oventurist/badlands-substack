# Laken Riley Act

The Laken Riley Act is an immigration enforcement law signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] early in his second term.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the law as evidence of the pace of Trump's second term, noting that while conventional Washington wisdom treats a single major bill as a whole-of-government effort for a president's first 100 days, Trump signed the Laken Riley Act into law on day nine.[1] She placed the signing alongside his reshaping of immigration, education, foreign policy, trade policy, economic policy, innovation policy and health policy within 44 days in office, concluding that "it's almost like the gridlock in our government was intentional."[1]

## Signing

President Trump signed the Laken Riley Act into law on Wednesday, January 29, 2025, expanding the scope of those who can be arrested, detained and deported by federal immigration officers.[2] It was the first bill of his second presidency to become law, arriving as he promised stricter enforcement of immigration laws and increased deterrents to legal migration.[2] The measure passed with bipartisan support, marking a major shift for Democrats, who had declined to advance the legislation in the Senate the previous year.[2] In his afternoon remarks Trump thanked Democrats for helping move the measure, saying, "It's a landmark law that we are doing today, it will save countless innocent American lives."[2]

In one of her first official appearances, Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] attended the bill signing, as did several Republican senators who championed the measure, along with Pennsylvania Democrat [[john-fetterman|John Fetterman]], the first Democrat to cosponsor the bill in the Senate.[2]

## Senate floor consideration (January 2025)

On the evening of Monday, January 13, 2025, the Laken Riley Act cleared another hurdle when senators voted 82-10 on a motion to proceed to consideration of the bill, opening it to debate and amendments.[3] The outcome followed a Thursday procedural vote in which 33 Senate Democrats had joined Republicans, and 33 Democrats again supported beginning debate.[3] The momentum marked a sea change from the previous Congress, when Democrats controlled the chamber and then-Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] blocked the House-passed version from Senate consideration; a handful of Democrats even expressed support for the unamended bill, a sign of their recognition of the electoral potency of Republican calls for border security.[3] The measure would require the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to take into custody illegal aliens arrested, charged, or convicted of burglary, theft, larceny, or shoplifting, and had passed the House the preceding Wednesday.[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called what happened to [[laken-riley|Laken Riley]] and others under the Biden administration a stain on America whose preventability justified the widespread, apolitical outrage, and looked forward to incoming border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] taking office.[3] She questioned whether the legislation was merely symbolic or was curing a genuine defect in the United States Code, asking why a new statute was needed to compel an agency created to secure the homeland to detain foreign criminals inside it.[3]
