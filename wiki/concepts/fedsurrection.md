# Fedsurrection

**"Fedsurrection"** is a term used in independent and alternative media to describe the theory that federal, state, and local government operatives played a substantial instigating role in the January 6, 2021 breach of the United States Capitol.[1]

## Documented operative counts

[[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] had previously identified 20 confirmed incidents and operations involving government operatives who infiltrated the Trump crowds on January 6, 2021, each confirmed either by the mainstream press or by the government in court documents.[1] In April 2023 the outlet reported that recent court filings showed the Biden [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] admitting to at least 40 undercover operatives associated with the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] on January 6.[1] The outlet estimated that the true total could approach 100 operatives "leading the charge" on the Capitol, while acknowledging that the actual number remained unknown.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], characterized the disclosure as "we told you so," describing 40 government confidential human source (CHS) agents undercover on January 6 in addition to 40 undercover operatives and 8 informants inside the Proud Boys, and further assets within the [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]].[1] He noted that the roughly 100 assets confirmed so far excluded non-federal anarchist provocateurs.[1] DeLarme reasoned that if local chapters of militant left-wing groups such as [[antifa|ANTIFA]] or Insurrection USA each hosted an agent or informant capable of influencing a dozen additional participants, the total number of assets involved would exceed one hundred.[1]

He added that Ukrainian militants and operatives from an unknown number of other countries were also present in the crowds, making the full picture an "impossible mess to sort through."[1] DeLarme nonetheless expressed belief that ANTIFA mapping had begun long before, that modern intelligence-gathering capabilities made the task tractable, and that he hoped justice would soon be served for those still held in what he called the "DC Gulag."[1]

## Gateway Pundit operative catalogue (2023)

In February 2023 [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] republished and expanded a running list, begun in 2021, of government operatives it said had worked inside the crowd at the U.S. Capitol on January 6, 2021, asserting that dozens if not hundreds of such operatives had infiltrated the protests.[2]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the word "entrapment" applied, that everyone targeted by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the [[fbi|FBI]] over January 6 conduct should be released immediately, and that many of those incarcerated had not been charged with a crime.[2] He characterised the events as a cover-up of election theft that demonised contemporaneous scrutiny of the 2020 election night, when he said five states reversed their voting trend overnight, and noted the claim that the [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] opened gates and doors to admit people.[2] Contrasting the treatment of [[antifa|Antifa]] and [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] with that of January 6 defendants, he called for scrutiny of [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s involvement with the FBI and the Capitol Police.[2]

## September 2023 developments

The FedSurrection framing was applied to two events in the week of September 18, 2023. Former Capitol Police Chief [[steven-sund|Steven Sund]] testified at the [[2023-house-capitol-security-oversight-hearing|House Capitol security oversight hearing]], reiterating that [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] and Democratic leadership were culpable for the failure to secure the Capitol and that he had been forced out as a scapegoat.[3] The following day, [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]] was charged with a single count of disorderly conduct after two and a half years without prosecution.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the Epps charge was an afterthought designed to distract from the hearing's exposure of the FedSurrection, and cited polling indicating that roughly two thirds of Americans believed the "Insurrection" had been orchestrated by the government.[3]
