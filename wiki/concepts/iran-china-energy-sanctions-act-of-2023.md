# Iran-China Energy Sanctions Act of 2023

The Iran-China Energy Sanctions Act of 2023 is U.S. legislation designed to curtail Chinese purchases of Iranian crude oil by targeting the financial institutions that facilitate them.[1]

## Provisions

The bill proposes to "impose restrictions on correspondent and payable-through accounts in the United States with respect to Chinese financial institutions that conduct transactions involving the purchase of petroleum or petroleum products from Iran."[1] It expands secondary sanctions involving [[iran|Iran]] to cover all transactions between Chinese financial institutions and sanctioned Iranian banks that transact for the purchase of petroleum and petroleum products.[1] Under the bill the United States would also be required to make an annual determination as to whether Chinese financial institutions had engaged in sanctionable conduct.[1]

The background section of the bill states that Iran's crude oil exports stood at a four-year high of 1.5 million barrels per day, roughly 80 percent of which went to [[china|China]]'s independent refiners, the so-called teapots.[1]

## Legislative history

The measure was approved unanimously by the House Financial Services Committee in November 2023.[1] Following the Iranian drone and missile attack on Israel over the weekend of April 13–14, 2024, the [[us-house-of-representatives|U.S. House of Representatives]] scheduled votes for Monday, April 15 on several bills toughening sanctions on Iran, including this one, under an expedited procedure.[1]

## Context

Tougher U.S. enforcement of sanctions against Iranian oil exports had been widely anticipated by analysts after the attack on Israel.[1] Some investment banks and analysts did not expect a Middle East escalation severe enough to directly hamper oil production and exports, but most expected stricter U.S. sanctions enforcement.[1]
