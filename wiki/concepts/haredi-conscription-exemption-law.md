# Haredi Conscription Exemption Law

The Haredi conscription exemption law is Israeli legislation described in the Badlands Brief as codifying into statute the long-practiced exemption of ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) yeshiva students from compulsory military service.[1] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] sided with the Ultra-Orthodox faction in the [[knesset|Knesset]] to enact it in mid-2026.[1]

According to the Brief, the law provides that the government cannot arrest draft-dodgers, meaning that more than 80,000 able-bodied men from the Haredi community would not report to boot camp when conscripted.[1] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the measure compounded an existing manpower crisis and that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] was on the verge of collapse as shortages worsened and morale waned, raising the possibility of mutiny.[1] The same commentary held that the IDF was never built for a multi-year, multi-front war of attrition, preferring quick skirmishes lasting no more than a few weeks.[1]

The law's passage was presented as a structural constraint on Israeli ambitions in the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel Project]], arriving precisely as Washington pressed for Israeli withdrawals from southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and southern [[syria|Syria]].[1]

## Background: the 2025 arrest crisis

The statutory fight was preceded by an enforcement crisis in July 2025, when Israeli authorities detained several draft dodgers and [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] chairman [[yitzhak-goldknopf|Yitzhak Goldknopf]] warned that the ultra-Orthodox would leave the country en masse if arrests of yeshiva students continued.[2] Goldknopf told the newspaper Makor Rishon that "if there is no choice and the ultra-Orthodox have to leave the country, then they will leave the country," and that "there will be a state without ultra-Orthodox."[2] He claimed that the army's shortfall of some 50,000 soldiers could be met from roughly 100,000 draft dodgers already living in Tel Aviv and Gush Dan.[2]

Badlands commentary read the episode as an existential threat to the Israeli state, on the theory that the Hasidic community forms the foundation of the Zionist community and that a Haredi exodus would diminish the culture of Jewish supremacy, worsen the failing economy, and demoralize a country unaccustomed to prolonged war.[2]

## July 2025 coalition rupture

The exemption fight came to a head in July 2025. Badlands commentary traced the storyline to the [[israeli-supreme-court|Israeli Supreme Court]] ruling of the previous summer holding that yeshiva students from the Haredi communities are not exempt from the mandated two-year conscription to the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]].[3] In the week before the rupture, while Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was in Washington seeking American assent to Israeli annexation in Palestine, IDF generals moved to begin drafting the 54,000 eligible yeshiva students, with an outlined plan to have all of them in uniform by the end of the month — prompting Netanyahu to abandon his Washington schedule and rush home to avert the collapse of his coalition.[3]

The Haredi objection was specific: the parties did not believe any exemption bill could pass the [[knesset]], and the version proposed by Netanyahu's committee failed to include the thousands of yeshiva students who had received draft letters in the year since the court ruling.[3] Were that bill to pass and those students not report, they would be classified as draft dodgers subject to arrest and imprisonment.[3]

[[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] left the coalition on Monday night, July 14, 2025; its seven-seat departure cut Netanyahu's majority in the 120-seat Knesset from 68 to 61 — the bare minimum for him to remain prime minister.[3] [[shas]] was set to vote the following day on whether to follow, a move whose eleven seats would reduce the coalition to roughly 50 and force snap elections toward the end of the year.[3] Badlands commentary further argued that Netanyahu's earlier failure to codify the exemption had nearly collapsed his government the previous month and had spurred him into attacking Iran to unite the bickering factions, a narrative short-circuited by Trump's maneuvering.[3]
