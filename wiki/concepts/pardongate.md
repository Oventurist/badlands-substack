# Pardongate

Pardongate is the name given to the controversy over the clemency [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] granted on January 20, 2001, his final day as president. As recounted in the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]], Clinton issued 140 pardons and 36 commutations that day, a volume the author argues was "a purposeful distraction" intended to reduce scrutiny of a single highly controversial act.

That act was the pardon of the fugitive commodities trader [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]], who had been indicted by the Justice Department in 1983 for tax evasion, racketeering and wire fraud and was, at the time, the largest tax evader in American history, owing roughly $50 million. The pardon, the article notes, "created a firestorm that launched congressional and criminal investigations" and outraged Americans across party lines, including Democrats.

The essay's explanation for why Clinton would accept that political cost is money. Rich, it argues, had made his fortune busting [[sanctions|sanctions]] — buying [[iran|Iranian]] oil below market during the 1979 hostage crisis and reselling it to other pariah states — and had the contacts and front companies needed to move oil to [[north-korea|North Korea]] under the 1994 [[agreed-framework|Agreed Framework]]. The author suggests "the odds are pretty high" that Rich helped the Clintons profit from such a transaction, and that this is "the real reason that Clinton pardoned Rich, even though he knew he would catch hell for it."

The article also traces the consequences of the pardon into later Clinton fundraising, noting the New York Post's finding that Rich's "business partners, lawyers, advisers and friends have showered millions of dollars on the Clintons in the decade and a half following the scandal" — chief among them [[gilbert-chagoury|Gilbert Chagoury]] and [[sergei-kurzin|Sergei Kurzin]]. It closes the thread with the observation that the Clintons' own unreported foundation donations amounted to the same offence, tax evasion, for which Rich was pardoned.
