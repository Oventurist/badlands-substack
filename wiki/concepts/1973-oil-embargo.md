# 1973 Oil Embargo

The **1973 oil embargo** was imposed by the [[organization-of-arab-petroleum-exporting-countries|Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries]] (OAPEC) in response to Western support for [[israel|Israel]] during the [[yom-kippur-war|Yom Kippur War]].[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treats the episode as the moment Israel's strategic utility to the Western [[foreign-policy-establishment|foreign policy establishment]] was established.[1]

## Effects

The embargo had profound effects on the [[liberal-world-order|Liberal World Order]] and significantly impacted everyday people; scenes of cars lined up for miles to get gasoline were common as an economic crisis began to emerge in the United States.[1]

## Israel's role and the aftermath

DeLarme writes that Israel's military victory in the Yom Kippur War was credited with breaking the embargo and the associated crisis, effectively stabilizing the oil situation in the Middle East.[1] This was the moment that Israel's ability to advance U.S. and European interests was solidified, after decades in which Israel had been seen mainly as a liability requiring significant international support to survive.[1]

The episode also marked the acceleration of U.S. efforts to strengthen ties with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], and for more than fifty years thereafter Israel and Saudi Arabia were "the two apples in Uncle Sam's eye."[1] That arrangement held, in DeLarme's telling, until the 2015 signing of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]].[1]
