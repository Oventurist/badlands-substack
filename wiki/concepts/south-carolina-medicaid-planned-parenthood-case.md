# South Carolina Medicaid Planned Parenthood Case

On June 26, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruled that a South Carolina woman and [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]] lacked legal standing to sue over South Carolina's decision to exclude the organization from the state's [[medicaid|Medicaid]] program on the grounds that it provides abortions.[1]

## The ruling

By a 6–3 vote, in an opinion written by Justice [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]], the Court held that the provision of the Medicaid Act requiring states to ensure patients can obtain care from "any qualified provider" does not create the kind of clear and unambiguous individual right required to support a private lawsuit enforcing it.[1] The decision ended the suit brought by Julie Edwards, a patient who had been treated by Planned Parenthood and sought to shift all of her gynecological and reproductive health care there; she therefore could not challenge the state's exclusion decision.[1]

Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] dissented, joined by Justices [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] and [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]], writing that with the ruling "the project of stymying one of the country's great civil rights laws continues."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary framed the outcome as a continuation of the post-*Roe* devolution of abortion policy to the states, arguing that activists on the issue must now fight in state legislatures and citing Colorado — which enshrined publicly funded abortion in its state constitution — as a focal point.[1]
