# Army–McCarthy Hearings

The Army–McCarthy hearings were the 1954 congressional proceedings in which Senator [[joseph-mccarthy|Joseph McCarthy]] pursued allegations of communist infiltration involving the United States Army.[1] McCarthy's chief counsel during the hearings was [[roy-cohn|Roy Cohn]], who also assisted McCarthy in his broader investigations of suspected communists.[1]

## Significance in the Badlands account

The Badlands Media article treats the hearings as the point at which the anti-communist campaign of the 1950s collided most directly with entrenched interests in the government and military.[1] It was in the course of these Senate proceedings and related hearings that McCarthy applied his mocking nickname "Sanctimonious Stu" to his critic [[stuart-symington|Stuart Symington]], a usage the article compares to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nickname "De-Sanctimonious" for [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]].[1]

The hearings matter to the article chiefly as the biographical bridge between two eras: Cohn's service to McCarthy in 1954 and Cohn's later mentorship of Trump in New York real estate, presented as one of the "too many connections to dismiss things as being coincidence."[1] The article situates the hearings within the wider phenomenon of [[mccarthyism]] and the alleged communist penetration of American institutions.[1]
