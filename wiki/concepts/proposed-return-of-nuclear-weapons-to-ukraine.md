# Proposed Return of Nuclear Weapons to Ukraine

In November 2024 the New York Times reported that US and European officials had discussed a range of options intended to deter Russia from taking more Ukrainian territory, including providing Kiev with nuclear weapons.[1] The report noted that "several officials even suggested that Mr. Biden could return nuclear weapons to Ukraine that were taken from it after the fall of the Soviet Union."[1]

## Context

Following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election earlier that month, the United States and its [[nato|NATO]] allies began rushing weapons to Ukraine and granting Kiev the ability to strike targets inside Russian territory with long-range weapons.[1] Western officials reportedly believed the Kremlin would not significantly escalate the war before Trump was sworn in in January, and some officials told the Times that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would not escalate substantially until Trump returned to the Oval Office.[1]

American officials briefed on intelligence community assessments told the Times that the additional weapons would not alter Kiev's difficult position: "US spy agencies have assessed that speeding up the provisions of weapons, ammunition and matériel for Ukraine will do little to change the course of the war in the short term."[1] Desperate to bolster Ukraine's standing before the January 20 transfer of power, the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was said to be weighing a range of serious escalations, including "deterrence as a possible security guarantee for Ukraine, such as stockpiling a conventional arsenal sufficient to strike a punishing blow if Russia violates a cease-fire."[1]

AntiWar.com, which relayed the report, also noted that much of the American political class had cast Trump and [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] as agents of Russia, though extensive investigations into Trump's ties to the Kremlin had come up empty and the Times had reported the previous week that there was no evidence Gabbard was in any way an asset of Putin.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Burning Bright placed the story inside the broader narrative of the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]], arguing it follows something close to a Fichtean Curve plot structure of escalating crises.[1] On that reading it is impossible to know which peak is being approached, but "the mass psychological reckoning with the SEEMING inevitability of WW3" had always topped his prediction charts, ever since references to "necessary" scare events appeared in the Q drops.[1] The writer described the resulting paradox in the anon mind — simultaneously exulting in the ratcheting tension and recognising it as theater — while cautioning against complacency, since "the future we want to build can't be given, but must be taken."[1] He linked this to Trump's frequent invocation of a "mandate," which he read as a mandate not merely in the vote but to rebuild the world, achievable only by tearing the old one down; a process he argued need not be stressful but could be exhilarating and unifying with the right mindset.[1]
