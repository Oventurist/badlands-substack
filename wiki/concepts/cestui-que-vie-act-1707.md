# Cestui Que Vie Act 1707

The Cestui Que Vie Act 1707 (1707 CHAPTER 72, 6 Ann) is cited by Badlands Media as documentary evidence for the claim that citizens are treated as legally incompetent wards of the state.[1] In the essay "Trump Knows," [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that within the secret law system upon which the visible one operates, people are considered spiritually dead lunatics unable to manage their own affairs competently in harmony with God's will, and are therefore handled as contemptible wards.[1] The statute is offered as the citation for that proposition, alongside the related [[cestui-que-vie-trust|cestui que vie trust]] concept.[1]

## Role in the argument

The reference appears in a passage arguing that Americans lack meaningful control over their lives from a rule-of-law perspective, not merely a political one.[1] Elite lobbying, media manipulation and the manipulation of public officials are said to reduce ordinary influence over government to the marginal, leaving "WE THE PEOPLE" without real representation or any lawful means to rein in a hijacked government.[1] Deschamps then invoked the Declaration of Independence's clause that whenever a form of government becomes destructive of its ends it is the right of the people to alter or abolish it.[1] The 1707 Act functions in this framework as the hidden legal machinery of the modern [[legal-system|legal system]], and as part of the reason [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s inaugural pledge to return power to the people was read as addressing an unacknowledged condition of conquest.[1]
