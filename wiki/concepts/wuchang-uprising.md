# Wuchang Uprising

The Wuchang uprising was the 1911 revolt that eventually brought down the [[qing-dynasty|Qing dynasty]] and inaugurated the [[republic-of-china|Republic of China]].[1]

News of the uprising went public while [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]] was on an autumn 1911 lecture and fundraising tour of the United States and Canada in support of the revolutionary movement he had led since 1893.[1] He cut the tour short in William Gilpin's state of Colorado and departed for China to oversee the dismantling of the dynastic system, taking the mantle of the new republic's first president.[1]

[[henry-wallace|Henry A. Wallace]] later dated the modern political awakening of Asia to the 1911 revolution, describing it as the first occasion on which an Asiatic people rejected monarchy and hereditary rule outright.[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] notes that the hopes of the 1911 revolution appeared to be slipping away by the time Sun wrote in despair after World War I that "if China perishes, the guilt will be on our own heads."[1]
