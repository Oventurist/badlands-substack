# 2024 Mexican General Election

Mexico's general election of June 2, 2024 resulted in the projected victory of [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]], who became the first female president in the country's 200-year history.[1]

## Result

The president of Mexico's National Electoral Institute reported that Sheinbaum had between 58.3 percent and 60.7 percent of the vote, opposition candidate [[xochitl-galvez|Xóchitl Gálvez]] between 26.6 percent and 28.6 percent, and Jorge Álvarez Máynez between 9.9 percent and 10.8 percent.[1] Sheinbaum's [[morena|Morena]] party was also projected to hold its majorities in both chambers of Congress.[1] Greeting supporters in Mexico City's colonial-era main plaza, the Zócalo, Sheinbaum said: "I promise that I am not going to let you down."[1]

Sheinbaum was the favored successor of outgoing president [[andres-manuel-lopez-obrador|Andrés Manuel López Obrador]] and vowed to continue the direction he had set, though as a cool-tempered scientist she offered a sharp contrast in style and a break with Mexico's male-dominated political culture.[1] The election season was marked by the assassination of 37 political candidates.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentary treated the outcome with alarm, describing Sheinbaum as a PhD climate scientist and contributor to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change and linking that body to [[malthusianism|Malthusianism]].[1] It catalogued her record as mayor of Mexico City — a six-year environmental plan, gender-neutral and pro-trans school policies, "decolonization" measures including replacing a statue of Christopher Columbus, and advocacy for the legalization of abortion, which took effect in 2021 — alongside her COVID-era insistence that citizens heed only state authorities and her imposition of lockdowns and mask advocacy, for which she was nominated for the World Mayor Prize in 2021.[1] Critics cited by the brief noted the collapse of Line 12 of the Mexico City Metro, which killed 26, injured 80 and left 5 missing, arguing she should have prioritized crumbling infrastructure.[1]

The commentary further speculated about Sheinbaum's ancestry — a Lithuanian father and Bulgarian mother, and atheist leftist parents "involved in the sciences" — framing those nationalities as historic adversaries of Russia and questioning how soon she might turn Mexico against Moscow, given that her predecessor had rejected Western sanctions on Russia in 2022.[1]
