# Treasury Buyback Program

The Treasury buyback program is a US Department of the Treasury operation under which the department repurchases older, less frequently traded government securities in order to improve liquidity in the Treasury market.[1] The Treasury began its first regular buyback program since 2000–02 in 2024, targeting off-the-run securities that trade less actively than newer, so-called on-the-run Treasuries.[1]

## 2025 bond market selloff

In April 2025, Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] cited the buyback program as part of a "big toolkit" available to the department should dislocation in the bond market require intervention.[1] Speaking to Bloomberg Television during a visit to Buenos Aires, Argentina, Bessent played down the recent selloff in Treasuries and rejected speculation that foreign nations were dumping their holdings.[1] "I don't think there's a dumping" by foreign investors, he said, pointing to what he described as increased foreign demand at auctions for 10-year and 30-year Treasury securities the previous week and reiterating his interpretation that the decline was mainly a product of deleveraging.[1] "I have no evidence that it's sovereigns" behind the drop, he added.[1]

Bessent said the Treasury was "a long way" from needing to act, but that "we have a big toolkit that we can roll out" if necessary, including the option to "up the buybacks if we wanted."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary highlighted Bessent's disclosure that he has breakfast weekly with [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], and framed expanded Treasury buybacks as a substitute mechanism for quantitative easing should the Fed decline to act to support the Treasury market.[1]
