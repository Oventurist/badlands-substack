# Familial DNA Testing at the Border

Familial DNA testing at the border was a [[customs-and-border-protection|US Customs and Border Protection]] program that verified claimed family relationships among migrants crossing the US–Mexico border, described by reporting as a key deterrent to fraudulent entry and to [[child-trafficking|child trafficking]].[1]

## Origins

The testing was originally implemented during the Trump era and used by CBP following a court order related to the separation of migrant children from their families, and in response to evidence that drug cartels were using children to create fake family units in order to sneak illegal immigrants across the border.[1]

## 2023 termination

According to a memo obtained by [[just-the-news|Just the News]], the Biden administration set the program to end when its vendor contract expired: "The U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) familial DNA contract with [[bode-technologies|BODE Technologies]] will end on May 31, 2023 and all familial DNA testing will conclude on that date," the memo sent to frontline border agents read.[1]
