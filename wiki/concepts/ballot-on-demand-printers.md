# Ballot-on-Demand Printers

Ballot-on-demand (BOD) printers are devices used at polling places to print a voter's correct ballot style at the moment of check-in, rather than relying on pre-printed stock. Their malfunction in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] became the proximate cause of the disruption in the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 Arizona general election]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

According to the county's own account as relayed in the November 19, 2022 letter from Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]], "at least 60 voting locations had issues related to some ballot-on-demand (BOD) printers having printer configuration settings that were non-uniform, which appeared to have resulted in ballots that were unable to be read by on-site ballot tabulators."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Although the equipment had been tested the day before the election, printer and tabulator failures began within thirty minutes of polls opening on November 8, 2022.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Ballots rejected by tabulators were routed into the [[door-3-ballots|"Door 3"]] receptacles.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Wright's letter demanded a comprehensive report identifying, for every affected polling location, the precise times, personnel and remedial steps involved.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] singled out the seventh request — asking whether technicians were physically deployed to each site or whether "the network administrator was able to make universal changes" — as implying that the machines may have had network connectivity, contrary to public assurances that none of them were connected to the internet.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)
