# Mukden Incident

The Mukden Incident, also known as the Manchurian Incident, is cited in the Badlands Media essay "False Flags and the Occult" as one of the clearest historically admitted examples of a [[false-flag-operation|false flag]].[1]

In 1931 Japanese troops set off a small explosion on a train track and falsely blamed it on China in order to justify an invasion of Manchuria.[1] The Tokyo International Military Tribunal found that several of the participants in the plan, including the high-ranking Japanese army officer Hashimoto, had on various occasions admitted their part in the plot and stated that the objective of the "Incident" was to afford an excuse for the occupation of Manchuria by the Kwantung Army.[1]

The article presents the episode as item one on a widely circulated list of forty-two government-admitted false flags, offered as evidence that although governments rarely openly admit orchestrating such operations, it does happen.[1]
