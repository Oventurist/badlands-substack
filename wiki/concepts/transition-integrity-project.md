# Transition Integrity Project

The Transition Integrity Project (TIP) is a bipartisan-styled election scenario group founded and facilitated by Rosa Brooks and housed at the Georgetown University Law Center, which conducted "war games" ahead of the 2020 United States presidential election.[1]

## Activities

In a Washington Post op-ed published in advance of the 2020 election, titled "What's the worst that could happen? The election will likely spark violence — and a constitutional crisis," Brooks described how her team had "built a series of war games, sought out some of the most accomplished Republicans, Democrats, civil servants, media experts, pollsters and strategists around, and asked them to imagine what they'd do in a range of election and transition scenarios."[1]

The exercises concluded that "the concept of 'election night,' is no longer accurate and indeed is dangerous," that "a determined campaign has opportunity to contest the election into January 2021," and that "the administrative transition process itself may be highly disrupted."[1]

## Congressional investigation

In March 2024 the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] formally opened an investigation into TIP ahead of the 2024 election.[1] In a letter to Brooks, Representative Pete Sessions described the project as an "intensely partisan exercise" that was "intended to sew dissension and subversion regarding the American election system and the institution of the president of the United States."[1] Sessions wrote that he had been "particularly concerned by the activities of TIP in 2020, and any similar endeavor in advance of the 2024 election, because this exercise seems to have been administered adjacent to the Georgetown University Law Center."[1] The investigation was first reported by [[the-post-millennial|The Post Millennial]].[1]

## Benz disclosures and the 2023 elector prosecutions

[[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] of the [[foundation-for-freedom-online|Foundation for Freedom Online]] described the Transition Integrity Project as a non-governmental effort to control the outcome of the 2020 election led by [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] and the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]], stating in December 2022 that "TIP spent summer 2020 wargaming how to overturn election results if candidate Trump won the election."[2] The project's activities included former FBI general counsel [[james-baker|James Baker]] leading the National Task Force on Election Crises.[2] The material regained prominence in July 2023 when Michigan Attorney General [[dana-nessel|Dana Nessel]] charged 16 Republican 2020 electors with eight felonies each, prompting commentary that the accusers had documented plans of their own — plans previewed publicly in Time magazine's account of "The Secret History of the Shadow Campaign That Saved the 2020 Election."[2]

## Founding and leadership

TIP was launched in late 2019 out of stated concern that the Trump Administration might seek to manipulate, ignore, undermine or disrupt the 2020 presidential election and transition process.[3] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] stresses that this founding predated the arrival of the first Covid-positive American at SeaTac airport from Wuhan and preceded the US lockdowns by several months, which he treats as evidence that the group's later emphasis on mail-in ballots was not a reaction to the pandemic.[3]

The project was founded and led by law professor [[rosa-brooks|Rosa Brooks]], also a journalist, author and foreign-policy commentator educated at Harvard, Yale and Oxford.[3] Carlson notes that Brooks served on the board of the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] US Programs Fund and consulted for the Open Society Institute, both funded by [[george-soros|George Soros]].[3] Brooks' partner in the project was [[nils-gilman|Nils Gilman]], Senior Vice President of Programs at the [[berggruen-institute|Berggruen Institute]], an organisation Carlson associates with biotechnology, artificial intelligence and transhumanism, and who is further linked to the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] and to Noema Magazine.[3]

## The June 2020 exercises

In June 2020, within three months of the first US Covid lockdowns, TIP convened a self-described "bipartisan" group of more than 100 current and former senior government and campaign leaders and other experts for a series of 2020 election crisis scenario-planning exercises.[3] Carlson argues the bipartisan framing was misleading, the participants consisting of Democrats, establishment Republicans and neoconservatives, and that the resulting 22-page report amounted to an attempt to work out how [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] could be kept from continuing as president.[3]

The group examined four scenarios: a narrow Trump win, a Trump landslide, a narrow [[joe-biden|Biden]] win and a Biden landslide.[3] In each case it predicted that Trump and his supporters would subvert the legal system and turn violent, while Biden and his supporters would work within the law and remain peaceful.[3] The exercises were described by their authors as producing alarming results, forecasting a chaotic legal and political landscape and a high likelihood that Trump would contest the outcome by legal and extra-legal means, conclusions the group said were bolstered by the historical precedent of Bush v. Gore in 2000 and other electoral dysfunctions.[3]

## Specific predictions

TIP anticipated that Trump would declare victory on election night and refuse to allow mail-in ballots to be counted, and asserted that voter fraud was virtually non-existent while Trump would nonetheless allege it to mobilise his base and lay grounds for contesting a loss.[3] It judged the potential for violent conflict high, particularly if Trump encouraged supporters to take up arms.[3] The report characterised Trump's deployment of Homeland Security agents to Portland as suppression of "racial justice protestors," a framing Carlson rewrites as federal agents being sent to stop [[antifa|Antifa]] from terrorising the city.[3] The group even predicted that, in the event of a Trump win, violence at Biden-supporter protests would actually be caused by Trump supporters in disguise — a mirror, Carlson suggests, of what he believes occurred at the [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6 Capitol protest]].[3]

In a Trump-loss scenario, the group speculated that Trump would use Attorney General [[william-barr|Bill Barr]] and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to remain in office, federalize the [[national-guard|National Guard]] or invoke the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act]] to deploy active-duty military domestically with DOJ legal cover, and force the Postmaster General to seize ballots in the mail.[3] Were he to concede, they expected him to maximise the flow of federal money into Trump businesses, negotiate foreign business deals, purge incriminating documents, launch "MAGA TV" from the White House possibly headed by [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], pardon himself and his family and associates, provoke a foreign crisis to shift the media narrative, and claim Biden had suffered a heart attack and was unfit to serve.[3] Carlson reads this list as projection — accusations describing crimes the accusers themselves commit.[3]
Other forecasts held that a victorious Trump would consolidate power while the GOP maximised control through redistricting; that a defeated Trump would face [[mitt-romney|Mitt Romney]] persuading moderate Senate Republicans to recognise Biden as president-elect with [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]]'s backing on 2022 electoral grounds; that [[infowars|Infowars]] would dox Biden electors and tie them to [[george-soros|George Soros]] and child sex-trafficking; and that commentators such as [[rush-limbaugh|Rush Limbaugh]] would link Biden to China.[3] Ultimately the group concluded Trump would refuse to leave the White House, after which a victorious Biden would begin uniting the country in early 2021.[3]

## Mail-in ballots and the corporate strike scenario

The report insisted that all mail-in ballots be counted even if the process ran for months past January 20, 2021.[3] Carlson treats this as the group's true purpose, noting the incongruity that [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]] only became a major issue after the Covid narrative emerged months after TIP's founding.[3] Among the group's scenarios was one in which Democrats would encourage corporate CEOs to mount a universal strike, shutting down the country until every mail-in ballot was counted.[3] Carlson argues this presented Trump with a choice between halting the count and triggering a nationwide corporate shutdown and economic collapse, or allowing the election to be stolen — and that Trump deliberately chose the latter, comparing the decision to the biblical judgment of King Solomon in which the true mother relinquishes the child rather than see it destroyed.[3]

The group also modelled a Biden-loss scenario in which the Biden campaign encouraged California, Oregon and Washington to secede as "Cascadia," and predicted statehood for the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico, the splitting of California into five states to gain Senate seats, and the elimination of the [[electoral-college|Electoral College]] — a cause its founders were said to champion.[3]

## Criticism

Carlson observes that essentially none of TIP's dozens of predictions about Trump and MAGA behaviour came true, and asks how more than 100 experts could be so wrong.[3] His answer is that the group was mirroring its own side's playbook, and that the actions attributed to Trump — weaponising the DOJ, enrichment through foreign business dealings, turning protests violent by posing as the opposition, and suppressing free speech — were carried out instead by the Biden administration.[3] He also identifies an internal contradiction: TIP dismissed Trump's fraud concerns as preposterous because election fraud is practically non-existent, yet grounded its own predictions in Bush v. Gore, an election it treats as having been stolen.[3]
