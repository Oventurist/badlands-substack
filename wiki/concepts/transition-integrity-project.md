# Transition Integrity Project

The Transition Integrity Project (TIP) is a bipartisan-styled election scenario group founded and facilitated by Rosa Brooks and housed at the Georgetown University Law Center, which conducted "war games" ahead of the 2020 United States presidential election.[1]

## Activities

In a Washington Post op-ed published in advance of the 2020 election, titled "What's the worst that could happen? The election will likely spark violence — and a constitutional crisis," Brooks described how her team had "built a series of war games, sought out some of the most accomplished Republicans, Democrats, civil servants, media experts, pollsters and strategists around, and asked them to imagine what they'd do in a range of election and transition scenarios."[1]

The exercises concluded that "the concept of 'election night,' is no longer accurate and indeed is dangerous," that "a determined campaign has opportunity to contest the election into January 2021," and that "the administrative transition process itself may be highly disrupted."[1]

## Congressional investigation

In March 2024 the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] formally opened an investigation into TIP ahead of the 2024 election.[1] In a letter to Brooks, Representative Pete Sessions described the project as an "intensely partisan exercise" that was "intended to sew dissension and subversion regarding the American election system and the institution of the president of the United States."[1] Sessions wrote that he had been "particularly concerned by the activities of TIP in 2020, and any similar endeavor in advance of the 2024 election, because this exercise seems to have been administered adjacent to the Georgetown University Law Center."[1] The investigation was first reported by [[the-post-millennial|The Post Millennial]].[1]

## Benz disclosures and the 2023 elector prosecutions

[[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] of the [[foundation-for-freedom-online|Foundation for Freedom Online]] described the Transition Integrity Project as a non-governmental effort to control the outcome of the 2020 election led by [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] and the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]], stating in December 2022 that "TIP spent summer 2020 wargaming how to overturn election results if candidate Trump won the election."[2] The project's activities included former FBI general counsel [[james-baker|James Baker]] leading the National Task Force on Election Crises.[2] The material regained prominence in July 2023 when Michigan Attorney General [[dana-nessel|Dana Nessel]] charged 16 Republican 2020 electors with eight felonies each, prompting commentary that the accusers had documented plans of their own — plans previewed publicly in Time magazine's account of "The Secret History of the Shadow Campaign That Saved the 2020 Election."[2]
