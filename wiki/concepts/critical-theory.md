# Critical Theory

**Critical Theory** is described in Badlands Media commentary as a philosophical and sociological approach to understanding power and inequality in society, explaining power as unevenly used and distributed in order to maintain and reproduce social inequalities.[1]

## Origins

A January 2025 Badlands feature by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] traces Critical Theory to the [[frankfurt-school|Frankfurt School]] in pre-Nazi Germany in the 1930s, attributing it to the same intellectual current that produced [[political-correctness|political correctness]] — which the author says was unleashed in American universities in the 1990s as a precursor and is "the parent of wokeness."[1]

## Variants

Carlson summarizes the family of derivative theories: [[critical-race-theory|Critical Race Theory]] explains how the people in power — white people — use racism to hold down minorities; Critical Feminist Theory explains how men use sexism to hold women down; Critical Queer Theory follows the same template.[1] Alongside these, Marxism is used to explain how the ruling class exploits the working class to maintain its power.[1] In his account all of these concepts have been glorified in American schools for roughly thirty years, with a marked uptick over the past ten to fifteen.[1]

## Claimed effects

The article argues that Critical Theory helps explain how a cold-blooded killer who murders a member of the perceived ruling class can be glorified as a Robin Hood-like figure, and how someone from the ruling class can be motivated to destroy the system that benefits them.[1] It is likewise offered as an explanation for popular support in the West for Middle Eastern terrorists framed as resisting oppressors.[1] Carlson contends that Critical Theory was created and is used to manufacture confusion and division, to convince people that the cards are permanently stacked against them, and to cultivate hatred and a victim mentality — ultimately persuading rich and poor alike that Communism is the only way.[1] He insists that "there is nothing organic about what is happening today," describing the worldview as conditioned rather than spontaneous.[1]
