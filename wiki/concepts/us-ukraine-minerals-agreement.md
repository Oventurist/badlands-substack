# U.S.–Ukraine Minerals Agreement

The U.S.–Ukraine minerals agreement is an arrangement granting the United States access to Ukrainian mineral resources, negotiated during the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Oval Office confrontation

The agreement was scheduled to be finalized at a White House meeting involving President Trump, Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]].[1] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] had played a role in negotiating the deal and was present during the discussions.[1]

According to a book by New York Times reporters [[maggie-haberman|Maggie Haberman]] and [[jonathan-swan|Jonathan Swan]], with excerpts cited by RT and attributed to material published by The Guardian, Bessent privately voiced concerns about Zelensky's behavior ahead of the meeting, reportedly saying the Ukrainian president "acted like Mr. Bean on crack."[1] The meeting deteriorated into a confrontation after disagreements over U.S. policy toward Ukraine and Russia, Zelensky was asked to leave the White House, and the minerals agreement was ultimately signed about two months later.[1]

Badlands Media commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded to the reporting by describing Bessent as "the pipe-hitting gangster in the Trump Administration."[1]

## March 2025 revival after the Oval Office rupture

On March 4, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he appreciated [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s willingness to sign the minerals deal and come to the negotiating table under his leadership, and told Congress that Zelensky had made the declaration in a letter received earlier that day.[2] Zelensky had posted on X that Ukraine was ready to sign the deal and talk peace, calling the previous week's contentious Oval Office meeting — after which the signing was put on hold — "regrettable."[2] Four people familiar with the situation told Reuters that the administration and Ukraine planned to sign the deal that Tuesday, and three sources said Trump had told advisers he wanted to announce an agreement in his address, while cautioning that the deal had yet to be signed and the situation could change.[2]

## Trump's push for a "bigger, better deal"

CBS News reported on Tuesday, March 4, 2025, citing a person familiar with the talks, that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was seeking an improved minerals deal between Washington and Kiev — three days after the brief falling out over the issue between Trump and Ukrainian leader [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]].[3] Zelensky had visited the White House the previous week to finalize an agreement granting the United States rights to Ukraine's rare-earth minerals as restitution for military aid, but his meeting with Trump and Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]] devolved into an argument in front of journalists.[3] Zelensky subsequently said the Washington meeting "did not go the way it was supposed to be" and posted on X that "Ukraine is ready to sign [the agreement on minerals] in any time and in any convenient format."[3] Multiple sources told CBS the deal had not been finalized, and one person familiar with the matter said its parameters could change because the president was pursuing a "bigger, better deal."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Zelensky or his handlers had never read *The Art of the Deal*, in which Trump's first offer is his best offer and walking away from the table forfeits it.[3] He cited the veiled threats Zelensky made against the United States, the allegation that he called Vance a "bitch" in Ukrainian ("supa") under his breath, and the general display of disrespect, concluding that Zelensky should be grateful to get any deal at all.[3]
