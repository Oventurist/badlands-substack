# Qatar–Turkey Pipeline

The Qatar–Turkey pipeline is a long-dormant natural gas project that would connect Qatar to Türkiye and onward to European markets by way of Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Syria. Its revival became an immediate topic of discussion after the collapse of the Assad government in December 2024.[1]

## Revival after the fall of Assad

Turkish Energy and Natural Resources Minister [[alparslan-bayraktar|Alparslan Bayraktar]] said on Monday, December 16, 2024, following a cabinet meeting in Ankara, that the project could be revived following the fall of the decades-long Assad regime.[1] Bayraktar conditioned this on Syria achieving territorial integrity and stability: "If so, that line must be safe. We hope it will be so. If so, there are many projects to produce."[1] He stressed that electricity is an essential need in Syria, where "with no existing infrastructure, everything is lacking," and said energy should be treated as a tool to meet those needs.[1] [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], Syria's leader for nearly 25 years, had fled to Russia after anti-regime groups took Damascus, marking the collapse of the Baath Party regime in power since 1963.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands Media treats the pipeline as the underlying motive for the long campaign to overthrow Assad, noting that the project had appeared in its News Brief the prior week and connecting it to contemporaneous headlines.[1] Drawing on an interview aired by [[kevork-almassian|Kevork Almassian]] on *Syriana Analysis* with a former Qatari defense minister, the Brief reports that the plot to remove Assad was a joint effort of Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan, Turkey and the West (the United States, NATO and presumably Israel), with a budget of roughly $2 trillion mostly funded by Gulf oil states and an intended schedule of about three months.[1] After that effort stalled, [[bandar-bin-sultan-al-saud|Bandar bin Sultan Al Saud]] took over "The Syria File" in 2013 and likewise failed.[1]

The Brief's writer concludes that the pipeline "represents the interests of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and the NeoCons," and that for that reason alone it should not be allowed to be built.[1]
