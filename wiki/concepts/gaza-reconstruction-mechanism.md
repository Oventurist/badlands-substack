# Gaza Reconstruction Mechanism

The Gaza Reconstruction Mechanism (GRM) is an online database through which the reconstruction of [[gaza|Gaza]] has been managed since 2014.[1] Updated in real time, the GRM records all building material flowing across Gaza's borders, the intended use of that material, and who will receive it.[1]

## Design and criticism

The mechanism was agreed upon by Israel and Palestine and was designed to ensure that resources were not diverted to military use by [[hamas|Hamas]]; it was intended to be temporary.[1] Franceco Sebregondi of the research group Forensic Architecture argues that in practice it places Israel in an "ultimate supervisory role" over Gaza's reconstruction.[1]

## Relationship to smart-city surveillance

Reporting cited in the Badlands Media essay "Our 'Greatest Ally'" describes the digital network monitoring Gaza and its residents as a variation on the data-intensive "[[smart-cities|smart city]]" concept, and characterizes the Israeli-Palestinian conflict as having turned the region into a proving ground for purpose-built surveillance technology that could be plugged into a future smart city.[1]

The article uses the GRM and the wider surveillance apparatus to question the official account of [[october-7-attacks|October 7]]: if Gaza was among the most heavily surveilled places on earth, the author argues, the notion that Hamas planned and executed the [[al-aqsa-flood|Al-Aqsa Flood]] operation undetected becomes harder to accept.[1] It further suggests that reconstruction after the current war is already attracting institutional actors — including the [[united-nations|United Nations]] — positioning themselves to profit, comparable to the Ukraine "reconstruction bank" associated with Chase and BlackRock.[1]
