# COP28

COP28 was the United Nations climate summit held in Dubai, at which nearly 200 countries negotiated a common text on emissions reduction.[1] The summit became a flashpoint in December 2023 over whether the final agreement would commit signatories to a "phase-out" of fossil fuels.[1]

## Dispute over phase-out language

COP28 president [[sultan-al-jaber|Sultan al Jaber]] publicly insisted that there was "no science" behind calls to phase out fossil fuels, and challenged critics to "show me for a phase-out of fossil fuel that will allow for sustainable socio-economic development, unless you want to take the world back into caves."[1] The remark prompted a widely covered exchange with senior women at the conference, including U.N. Environment Program Executive Director [[inger-andersen|Inger Andersen]], who replied that she lives in Kenya with solar power and clean electricity from the local utility and was "not living in a cave."[1]

Al Jaber's position drew criticism from scientists and stood in contrast to the stance of [[antonio-guterres|Antonio Guterres]], Secretary-General of the [[united-nations|United Nations]], who told the summit that "the science is clear: the 1.5C limit is only possible if we ultimately stop burning all fossil fuels. Not reduce, not abate. Phase out, with a clear timeframe."[1]

## Draft agreement

On Monday, December 11, 2023, the *Financial Times* reported that a draft summit agreement had dropped all references to a phaseout of fossil fuels following opposition from oil and gas producing countries led by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[1] The draft instead set out an optional range of actions countries "could" take to reach net zero by 2050, including reducing "consumption and production of fossil fuels, in a just, orderly and equitable manner."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the outcome as the predictable consequence of hosting the summit in a petrostate setting: "the climate cult is big mad, but they invited the oil barons into the club."[1] She criticized figures such as John Kerry for flying private jets to climate conferences, characterized climate hysteria as "just communism" aimed at global power and control, and concluded "Team Oil for the win."[1] Coverage aggregated by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] similarly argued that a hard phase-out commitment would drive energy price inflation harming the poor most, and would create logistical problems shifting developed nations onto less reliable primary energy sources.[1]

## Hillary Clinton's extreme heat remarks

Speaking at the conference, [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] told attendees that "extreme heat" had killed half a billion people, most of them women and girls, without citing a source.[2] She claimed extreme heat had killed 61,000 people in Europe the previous summer and estimated roughly 500,000 additional deaths across Africa, Asia and Latin America.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the remarks as evidence that a coordinated climate messaging push was beginning ahead of the 2024 US election season, and linked them to [[bill-nye|Bill Nye]]'s contemporaneous claim that voting was the most important climate action an individual could take.[2]
