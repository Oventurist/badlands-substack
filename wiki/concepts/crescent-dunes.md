# Crescent Dunes

Crescent Dunes is a failed concentrated solar power plant in Nevada financed with federal support, and the subject of a qui tam lawsuit alleging that taxpayers incurred significant losses from the project.[1] The project's origins trace back to the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration, and reporting on it in late 2024 raised questions extending through the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration.[1]

## Election night DOJ move

Breitbart News reported exclusively that on 5 November 2024 at 5:20 p.m. ET — less than an hour before the first polls closed, and once exit polls made clear that Democratic presidential candidate [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] was likely to lose — an attorney with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] emailed lawyers for the plaintiffs in the [[qui-tam|qui tam]] action, stating that the DOJ intended to dismiss a lawsuit it had previously allowed to proceed.[1] The move would undercut the private effort to recoup taxpayer losses from the failed plant.[1]

Breitbart described the story as having "tentacles" back through the Biden administration to the Obama administration, potentially jeopardizing the broader [[green-new-deal|green energy agenda]] and the future of solar projects on federal lands.[1] At least one congresswoman was already formally pressing the DOJ for answers, with more members of [[congress|Congress]] expected to become involved.[1]

## Environmental criticism

Beyond its failure to turn a profit, the plant drew criticism because birds were dying in the intense heat produced by its panels, with some reports indicating birds had burst into flames in mid-flight.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the green new deal as a money laundering operation, drawing a direct line from [[solyndra|Solyndra]] — which received $535 million from the Obama administration before going out of business — to Crescent Dunes.[1] She argued that the DOJ was tying off investigations for nefarious purposes before incoming Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and her team could examine them, since legitimate cases would be left to continue into the new administration.[1] Extending the argument, she contended that environmentalist policy consistently produces worse environmental outcomes — citing wind turbines and beached whales, plastic bags replacing paper bags and becoming the nation's leading pollutant, and hydroelectric power disrupting river ecosystems — concluding that "it's never about the environment. It's about money, power, and control."[1]
