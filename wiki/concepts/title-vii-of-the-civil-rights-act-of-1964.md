# Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964

Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 is the federal statute prohibiting employment discrimination, including racial harassment in the workplace.[1] Under Title VII, employers who receive harassment complaints are required to take prompt and appropriate action to investigate and stop the conduct, and retaliation against employees who oppose harassment is itself unlawful.[1] Enforcement authority rests with the [[equal-employment-opportunity-commission|Equal Employment Opportunity Commission]], which may investigate and sue on the statute's behalf.[1]

## Application in the Tesla case

The statute supplied the legal basis for the EEOC's September 2023 suit against [[tesla|Tesla]], filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California after a commissioner's charge by EEOC Chair [[charlotte-burrows|Charlotte A. Burrows]] alleged that Black employees had been subjected to an unlawful hostile work environment and that complainants had been retaliated against.[1]

## Contested framing

Writers at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] questioned whether Title VII enforcement of this kind was tracking actual discriminatory conduct, arguing that in a climate where cultural non-conformity is readily relabelled as racism, statutory anti-discrimination tools risk becoming a mechanism for sanctioning dissidents.[1] Contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] warned that such usage would erode the credibility of genuine discrimination claims, and connected the trend to the deplatforming of figures such as [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]] on the basis of accusation alone.[1]
