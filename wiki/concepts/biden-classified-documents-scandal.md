# Biden Classified Documents Scandal

The Biden classified documents scandal refers to the discovery, beginning in late 2022, of classified government records at multiple private locations associated with [[joe-biden]], and the subsequent federal investigations into how those materials came to be removed from secure custody.[1]

## Origins and expansion

The matter began with the discovery of an initial trove of classified documents at the [[penn-biden-center]], a Washington, D.C. think tank. Before that discovery, Biden attorney [[patrick-moore]] had shipped additional boxes from the think tank to his law firm in Boston.[1] The [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] learned of the Boston transfer on November 3, 2022, when it contacted Biden's personal counsel to arrange collection of the remaining boxes; it notified the [[department-of-justice]] Office of Inspector General the following day, and the material was collected on November 9 and secured at the John F. Kennedy Presidential Library.[1]

Acting Archivist [[debra-steidel-wall]] disclosed the previously unreported figure — nine boxes retrieved from Moore's Boston office — in a letter responding to a February 24 inquiry from Senators [[ron-johnson]] and [[chuck-grassley]].[1] The letter was first obtained by Fox News Digital.[1]

## Scope and commentary

Commentary published in the [[badlands-news-brief]] emphasized the steady widening of the scandal: from a few misplaced pages in a closet, to documents in a garage, to searches of a beach home and the University of Delaware, and finally to boxes of records and handwritten notes dating from Biden's vice presidency and back to his years in the Senate.[1] The same commentary credited Grassley and Johnson with methodically assembling a documentary record, and noted that Biden family members — Joe, James and [[hunter-biden]] — were under scrutiny by U.S. Attorney David Weiss and Special Counsel [[robert-hur]], with the DOJ Inspector General also receiving notifications as developments occurred.[1] It further argued that mainstream outlets gave the story minimal coverage relative to its significance.[1]

## Fight over the Hur interview audio

In late April and early May 2024, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] came under mounting pressure from [[congress|Congress]] and outside organizations to release audio recordings of Biden's interview with [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], the special counsel who investigated his handling of classified documents.[2] The department resisted requests from House Judiciary Committee Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-OH), House Oversight Committee Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-KY), conservative groups such as [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]], and [[cnn|CNN]], producing escalating legal pursuits, a tense impasse with Congress, and substantial criticism.[2]

Judicial Watch President [[tom-fitton|Tom Fitton]] called the refusal "yet another brazen cover-up," saying that "the Biden Justice Department's political gambit in asserting Joe Biden's privacy concerns in order to withhold audio of his criminal interviews with the special counsel really takes the cake."[2] Judicial Watch, the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], and CNN filed legal complaints seeking to enforce Freedom of Information Act requests for the recordings.[2] Hur had found after a yearlong investigation that Biden mishandled classified documents and disclosed classified information to the ghostwriter of his 2017 book *Promise Me, Dad*.[2]

## University of Delaware searches (February 2023)

CNN reported on February 15, 2023 that the [[fbi|FBI]] had conducted two previously undisclosed searches at the [[university-of-delaware|University of Delaware]] in connection with the investigation into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s handling of classified documents.[3] The searches occurred in recent weeks with the consent and cooperation of the president's legal team, and investigators retrieved materials from two university locations on two different days; the material did not appear to carry classified markings but was under FBI review.[3] The university library, at Biden's alma mater, houses an extensive collection of his Senate papers.[3]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that the searches were going deeper into the Biden orbit — homes, offices and now the alma mater holding some 1,850 boxes from his Senate years — and that because access to those documents requires Biden's consent, he was granting it in order to avoid being issued a search warrant.[3] Just Human added that the investigation's scope appeared to extend well beyond misplaced documents from Biden's vice presidency.[3]


## Comparison with other classified-document cases

Badlands commentary in January 2023 placed the Biden documents alongside a widening set of cases, including materials found in the possession of [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] and the guilty plea of retired Air Force intelligence officer [[robert-birchum|Robert Birchum]], who admitted keeping 135 classified files on a thumb drive and computer at his Tampa home.[4] Contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] argued that retention of classified material had been cast months earlier as "the crime of the century" when it concerned [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], but was reframed as a minor offense once others were implicated, and stressed that only Trump, as president, had held declassification authority.[4] Separately, The Gateway Pundit reported a second email on [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s laptop containing classified [[state-department|State Department]] information he had no clearance to hold.[4]

## Justice Department's rejected role in the searches (January 2023)

The Wall Street Journal reported in January 2023 that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] had considered having [[fbi|FBI]] agents monitor the search by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s lawyers for classified documents at his homes, but decided against it — both to avoid complicating later stages of the investigation and because Biden's attorneys had promptly turned over a first batch and were cooperating.[5] After Biden's lawyers discovered documents marked classified dating from his vice presidency at a Washington think-tank office on November 2, the department opened an inquiry into why and how they got there, and the legal team prepared to search his other properties.[5] The two sides ultimately agreed that Biden's personal attorneys would inspect the homes themselves, notify the Justice Department as soon as any additional potentially classified records were identified, and arrange for law-enforcement authorities to collect them.[5]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the report as "seeding" that added spice to the merging Hunter laptop and Biden documents storylines, noting that this episode also featured the FBI and that a highly respected mainstream outlet doing the reporting left "a copious amount of egg on the face of the U.S. Administrative State."[5] He observed that the public was being told the DOJ appeared to be covering for the Bidens, whether or not that was entirely true, and argued that how the public came to view the U.S. intelligence agencies through the saga bore watching.[5]

## Reports of no charges (November 2023)

In mid-November 2023, multiple reports citing sources familiar with the matter indicated that following a "thorough investigation," Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]] was unlikely to charge anyone in connection with the classified document scandal.[6] Hur's report was expected to sharply criticize [[joe-biden|Biden]] and his aides for their handling of classified documents — described by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] as the standard treatment for a protected establishment class whose name does not end in Trump.[6] The outcome was said to raise obvious questions about [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] sham investigations: if a special counsel probe into potential mishandling of sensitive national documents produces only criticism and no legal repercussions, commentators asked what message that sends about accountability at the highest levels of government.[6]

The investigation, begun in January 2023 after Hur's appointment by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]], had spanned nearly a year and involved interviews with roughly 100 Biden aides, including [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], who separately faced federal gun charges; the president himself was interviewed the previous month.[6] [[white-house|White House]] Counsel's Office spokesperson [[ian-sams|Ian Sams]] confirmed the president's interview in a statement.[6] ZeroHedge noted that Hunter Biden had listed Joe Biden's Wilmington, Delaware home — where classified documents were found — as his address when receiving $260,000 in Chinese wire transfers tied to the family's dealings with CCP-linked businessmen.[6]
