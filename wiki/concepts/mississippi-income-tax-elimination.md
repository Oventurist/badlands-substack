# Mississippi Income Tax Elimination

The **Mississippi income tax elimination** is a 2025 state law signed by Governor [[tate-reeves|Tate Reeves]] that phases out [[mississippi|Mississippi]]'s personal income tax, making the state the first in the nation to eliminate an existing income tax entirely.[1]

## Provisions

The law gradually reduces the then-current 4% income tax rate, reaching 3% by 2031.[1] Further reductions were originally conditioned on revenue growth triggers, but a typographical error in the bill nullified these safeguards, accelerating the phase-out.[1] Some lawmakers reportedly realized the mistake only after voting for the measure.[1]

## Fiscal debate

Reeves hailed the move as a "generational change" intended to attract businesses and workers to the state.[1] Critics counter that the loss of income tax revenue — estimated at $2.6 billion out of a roughly $7 billion state budget — could devastate essential public services.[1] Mississippi, the poorest state in the United States, relies heavily on federal funds, and potential federal cuts could worsen the resulting financial strain.[1]

Economist Neva Butkus warned that the wealthiest residents would benefit most from the change, while lower-income households would bear the burden through regressive offsets including a gasoline tax increase of nine cents per gallon.[1]
