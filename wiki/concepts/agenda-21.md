# Agenda 21

**Agenda 21** is the United Nations sustainable-development framework launched at the 1992 [[rio-earth-summit|Rio Summit]] in Brazil, which [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]] chaired.[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Agenda 21 and the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] as expressions of a single worldview crafted by Strong across his career — a career that included running Canada's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, founding the [[canadian-international-development-agency|Canadian International Development Agency]], mentoring a young [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], co-founding the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in 1971, chairing the first UN Forum on Population in 1972, and serving on the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg]] Steering Committee.[1]

Ehret notes that it was the Rio Summit that inaugurated the term "sustainable development" alongside Agenda 21, and quotes Strong's 1990 musing on the controlled disintegration of society: "Isn't the only hope for the planet that the industrialized civilizations collapse? Isn't it our responsibility to bring that about?"[1] In his reading, the framework's ultimate function is population control through the imposition of low-density energy sources.[1]
