# 2024 G7 Summit in Italy

The 2024 summit of the Group of Seven ([[g7|G7]]) convened in Italy in mid-June 2024, with the war in Ukraine and the disposition of frozen Russian sovereign assets dominating the agenda.[1] The gathering opened on Wednesday, June 12, and its principal deliverable was announced the following day.[1]

## Ukraine aid decision

On Thursday, June 13, 2024, G7 leaders agreed to deliver [[volodymyr-zelensky|Ukraine]] $50 billion in aid by the end of the year, financed not by direct seizure but by the profits generated from roughly $300 billion in sovereign Russian assets frozen in Western countries — an outcome announced by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] The mechanism followed months of debate among Western governments over whether and how to leverage the immobilized funds for Ukraine's defense or postwar recovery.[1] The United States had proposed outright seizure, but European leaders balked over legal and financial exposure; the European Union had instead agreed the previous month to use only the interest thrown off by the assets, estimated at between $2.7 and $3.3 billion annually.[1] Analysts warned the arrangement could expose Western assets inside Russia to retaliatory confiscation under a decree signed by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] earlier in 2024.[1] See [[frozen-russian-assets]].

## Attendance and the "snub" narrative

Badlands commentary treated the summit as a moment of humiliation for what it terms the Globalist Empire, focusing on the roster of invited non-member states that declined to appear.[1] Reporting on the summit's first day had indicated that Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Turkey would attend; the UAE declined, and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] withdrew at the last minute after receiving Zelensky in an unannounced Riyadh visit en route to the summit.[1] Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] skipped the opening day, flying instead to Spain to issue a joint statement with the Spanish president on the Gaza conflict — a snub the article linked to new British sanctions against Turkey announced as the summit opened, themselves attributed to Ankara's reported interest in joining BRICS.[1] China and Brazil had already said they would not participate in Ukraine diplomacy that excluded Russia.[1] The New York Times described the assembled leaders as weakened, a framing the Badlands writer cited approvingly.[1]

## Framing in Badlands commentary

The Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] traced the G7's origins to the 1973 oil crisis provoked by the Yom Kippur War, arguing it was created as a forum for the most powerful industrialized nations to "solve" the world's problems, and found it poetic that the Israel–Hamas conflict — whose October 7, 2023 trigger fell near the fiftieth anniversary of that war — served as the backdrop for what he called the death knell of the G7.[1] He characterized the $50 billion decision as globalists looting their own national coffers and laundering the proceeds through Ukraine, while a [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] of non-Western states declined to participate.[1]
