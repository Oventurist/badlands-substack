# 2025 California Redistricting Counterpunch

In August 2025, California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] announced that California would move forward with a plan for a special election in early November to place its own redistricting plan before voters as a ballot measure.[1]

## The plan

The announcement came while the controversy surrounding Texas's redistricting efforts still raged.[1] Newsom said the move was a counterpunch to Texas and was being done in a transparent fashion, but acknowledged it would likely see Democrats pick up five seats if the measure were adopted.[1] The plan would allow Democrats to temporarily bypass the state's independent redistricting commission and adopt a new congressional map ahead of the 2026 elections.[1]

"We are talking about emergency measures to respond to what's happening in Texas and we will nullify what happens in Texas," Newsom said at a Friday press conference, flanked by several California lawmakers and some Texas Democrats who had fled their state.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that "Our Boy New is staging a fake election to give himself cover for redistricting," calling the maneuver "hilarious."[1]

## Proposition 50 at the ballot

The counterpunch was formalized as [[california-proposition-50|Proposition 50]], a legislatively approved constitutional amendment placed before voters at the November 4, 2025 statewide special election, with a registration deadline of October 20.[2] The measure would allow temporary congressional district maps starting in 2026, framed in its own text as a response to partisan redistricting in Republican-led states including Texas, Florida and Ohio; a "No" vote would preserve the maps drawn by the [[citizens-redistricting-commission|Citizens Redistricting Commission]] until the 2030 Census.[2] Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and other supporters described it as a way to counter mid-decade redistricting that could expand Republican control of Congress.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that California's open admission of partisan intent — coming just after large numbers of migrants were removed from the state — could create the "judicially cognizable" interest the Supreme Court found lacking in [[texas-v-pennsylvania|Texas v. Pennsylvania]], making an inverted rerun of that case possible and inviting suits from Texas, voters, candidates and citizens.[2]
