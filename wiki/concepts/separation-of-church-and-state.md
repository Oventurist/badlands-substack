# Separation of Church and State

**Separation of church and state** is the doctrine, commonly held to describe the American founding, that religion and government occupy distinct spheres. In the Badlands Media essay "God and the Declaration of Independence," published March 14, 2023 by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], the phrase is examined and given a narrower reading than the one the author says is prevalent in modern secular culture.[1]

## Contested interpretation

The essay observes that some argue America was founded on "the separation of church and state," a formulation seeming to imply that God and religion have nothing to do with governance or law, and states flatly that this is not an accurate interpretation.[1] On the author's reading, the founders did not remove God from government; they declared that mankind is inherently free because that is how God created him.[1]

The practical implementation of that freedom, the essay argues, is to let men and women worship — or not worship — as their conscience compels them, provided such forms of worship do not involve the unlawful destruction of the life, liberty or property of others.[1] Separation of church and state therefore means only that government is not the proper entity to prescribe particular forms of worship.[1]

## The self-defeating objection

The essay presses a structural objection against the broader secular reading: how can a government claim to derive its authority from the Creator while at the same time denying the Creator?[1] It calls this an unsustainable, self-defeating proposition, and notes that without at least the concept of God there would be no [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]] at all, since the document says so on its face.[1]

The author further situates the debate historically, arguing that the separation of theology and politics is a recent phenomenon and that secular states were virtually unheard of little more than a century ago.[1]
