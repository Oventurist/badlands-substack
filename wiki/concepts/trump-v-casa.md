# Trump v. CASA

**Trump v. CASA** is a decision of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] handed down on Friday, June 27, 2025, dealing with the limits on the power of federal district courts to issue [[universal-injunction|universal injunctions]].[1]

## Holding and significance

Described by the New York Post as one of the biggest decisions of the term, the ruling moved to free the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from what commentators characterized as an onslaught of orders from district judges seeking to block the president in areas ranging from the downsizing of government to immigration.[1] Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the outcome as "a banging precedent, affirming lower court jurisdictional limitations," arguing that federal courts derive their power from Congress and had been operating "way outside their lane."[1]

## Barrett–Jackson exchange

The case produced an unusually sharp public clash between Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]], writing for the majority, and Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] in dissent.[1] The tenor of Jackson's language was said to have shocked court watchers and her colleagues alike, with critics comparing its rhetoric to the placards carried at the [[no-kings-protests|"No Kings" protests]] weeks earlier and accusing the most junior justice of effectively branding her colleagues toadies for tyranny.[1] The majority pushed back on what the Post called overwrought rhetoric; while restrained by the standards of congressional debate, the exchange amounted to "a virtual cage match" for the Court.[1] Badlands commentary singled out Barrett's response as the highlight of the opinion and arguably of the session, describing Jackson's dissent as a reimagining of the Constitution.[1]
