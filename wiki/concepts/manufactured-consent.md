# Manufactured Consent

Manufactured consent is the term Badlands Media's May 2025 essay "Wilted Ivy" uses for the system by which academic, financial and media institutions produced an apparent public consensus in support of a particular global order.[1] The article's central claim is that "the era of manufactured consent is ending."[1]

## The academic pillar

The essay argues that the [[ivy-league|Ivy League]] functioned as a "training ground for a managerial class loyal not necessarily to truth, but to the continuity of a particular global framework of power," manufacturing consent rather than knowledge.[1] Drawing on [[carroll-quigley|Carroll Quigley]]'s [[tragedy-and-hope|Tragedy and Hope]], it holds that [[harvard-university|Harvard]] reinforced an orthodoxy of narratives, institutions and experts, so that the collapse of its perceived invincibility is "not just symbolic, it's tectonic."[1] If Harvard can fall, the author writes, "the entire scaffolding of manufactured consensus can fall with it."[1]

## The financial pillar

Where the universities trained the enforcers, the article says, [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[vanguard|Vanguard]] and [[state-street|State Street]] financed the enforcement, using endowment management and corporate governance to embed [[esg|ESG]] compliance, [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]] orthodoxy, climate pledges and ideological hiring mandates.[1] The essay describes this as "shaping the cultural operating system itself, using capital flows to engineer compliance where debate might otherwise have flourished" — a process it calls seamless and invisible "until it wasn't."[1]

## Fragmentation

"Wilted Ivy" predicts that narrative fragmentation will spread through formerly synchronized outlets, with mainstream media fracturing "into confusion, contradiction, and sudden silence on topics once pushed with religious fervor."[1] It cites the audience and staffing losses at [[cnn|CNN]] and the cultural unraveling of [[disney|Disney]] as early evidence.[1] The greatest shift, the author argues, will be in perception rather than headlines: the public will "begin to feel it: the empire they trusted was rigged," an awakening described as gradual but irreversible.[1]
