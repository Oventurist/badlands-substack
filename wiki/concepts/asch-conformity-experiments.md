# Asch Conformity Experiments

The **Asch Conformity Experiments** are cited by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] as empirical support for his claim that the obstacle to truth is social rather than perceptual.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

Deschamps holds that the mind is itself a "truth discovering machine," naturally generating accurate impressions of reality by virtue of its information-absorbing abilities: most people asked how many apples are on a table will accurately recognise the number — say five.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) But, as the Asch experiments proved, if all the people in a social group say six apples, the target of this cult-like operation will be driven to suppress their true perception in favour of social conformity.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

He treats this as the mechanism cults use to prevent members — whom he calls victims — from placing value on the true facts they naturally encounter in life, and as one of two levers available to the [[cabal|cabal]]: getting people not to look at all, or, when they do look and take possession of a piece of truth, using the power of culture to make them think it is not valid.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

The experiments also underpin his argument about artificial intelligence. Because reality cannot be ignored, the engineers building a model — even those driven to make it serve the cabal's agenda — cannot fully insulate it from true facts, which is why [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] produced what he regarded as accurate output once the social pressure of its safety framing was relaxed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)
