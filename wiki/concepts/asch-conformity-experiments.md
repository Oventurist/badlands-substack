# Asch Conformity Experiments

The Asch Conformity Experiments are a classic series of social psychology studies demonstrating that individuals will contradict the plain evidence of their senses in order to agree with a unanimous group. In the Badlands Media corpus they serve as the empirical anchor for arguments about how mass perception is managed.

In [[ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo|AI Can Help Us Expose the NWO]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cites the experiments to support his claim that the mind is a "truth discovering machine" whose accurate impressions can nonetheless be overridden socially. He illustrates the point with a hypothetical table of apples: most people asked how many apples are present will correctly say five, but if everyone in the surrounding social group insists there are six, "the target of this cult-like operation, the hapless victim, will be driven to suppress their true perception in favor of social conformity."

The essay treats this as the operative mechanism by which the [[cabal|cabal]] neutralises truths people independently discover — not by hiding them, but by using cultural power to make the individual doubt their validity, in the manner of a cult. It also extends the argument to [[artificial-intelligence|AI]]: engineers building a model, however aligned with an agenda, still cannot make it ignore reality itself.
