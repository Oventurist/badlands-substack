# Federal Employee Buyout Program

The federal employee buyout program refers to the deferred-resignation agreements offered by the Trump administration in February 2025, whose terms brought more than 100,000 federal workers off the rolls on September 30, 2025 — the largest mass resignation in U.S. history.[1]

## Terms and timing

The resignations were the conclusion of buyout agreements entered into with the administration in February 2025, with the participating workers' tenure set to end at the close of the 2025 fiscal year.[1] The National Pulse reported that, despite misleading coverage, the departures were neither politically motivated nor newly announced: they had been voluntarily agreed months earlier, with many of the workers having already moved to the private sector or having used the intervening period to find new work.[1] Notably, a number of the 100,000 employees had, per their agreements, not worked for the federal government in any significant capacity for several months or more.[1]

## Relationship to the shutdown

In addition to the scheduled resignations, the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] warned that if a temporary funding agreement were not reached in Congress before October 1, it would lay off some non-essential workers in addition to imposing furloughs.[1] With the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|shutdown]] looming, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held eleventh-hour negotiations with Republican and Democratic congressional leaders at the White House on Monday evening, though most lawmakers indicated that at least a short closure of some agencies was likely.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked coverage — singling out Newsweek — that framed the departures as federal workers abandoning noble service, noting that many of those leaving had not meaningfully worked for the government in months.[1] Her conclusion was that "we the people are out here wondering why it's only 100,000."[1]
