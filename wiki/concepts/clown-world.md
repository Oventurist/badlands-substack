# Clown World

**Clown World** is the term used in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — What is Clown World?" to describe the present state of global affairs: a "giant circus" in which world events, and public opinion about them, are said to be driven by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], glossed in the piece and in QAnon usage as "Clowns in America."[1]

## Origin of the term

The author notes that within the [[qanon|Q]] drops, "clowns" is used many times as shorthand for CIA officers and allied personnel, and argues that the phrase should be read more literally than most readers assume: Clown World is, in his framing, the world that intelligence agencies and their secret-society counterparts actively wish to build on Earth.[1]

## Connection to the Koryos and werewolf symbolism

The essay is the eleventh installment of a series tracing werewolf symbolism to an ancient cult, the [[koryos|Koryos]], said to have originated in the Ukraine/Western Russia region.[1] The author reports that the anonymous researcher Rogue Hypnotist described the early Koryos werewolf cult as "giant red haired albinos," and speculates that the standard clown costume — powder-white face with red hair — may be inspired by, or otherwise meaningfully connected to, that cult.[1] Supporting examples offered include claims that [[genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]] and Montezuma were red-haired albinos, and Maori traditions of red-haired albino giants who ate children, which the author speculates may have been done to obtain [[adrenochrome|adrenochrome]].[1]

The essay proposes that the Koryos are a "physicalized" version of ancient clown-demons, and that the proliferation of clown aesthetics in popular culture has served to increase those beings' influence over mankind.[1]

## Recap of the series' claims

For newcomers, the article restates the series' prior assertions: that the Koryos pioneered horseback warfare, chariots and wagons; that their beliefs closely resemble those of modern dark occultists running the [[deep-state|Deep State]], with child sacrifice and child grooming central to their practices; that they held one could be male or female according to self-perception; that they combined sex and death thematically in ritual; that their "ancestor worship" amounted to possession by demons; that they went berserk under the full moon clad in wolf pelts; that they raided rather than farmed; and that the 1960s cultural revolution brought their practices into the mainstream.[1] The series also identifies their homeland with [[khazaria|Khazaria]] and [[tartaria|Tartaria]], notes claimed evidence of their presence in South America and New Zealand as support for ancient trans-oceanic contact, and posits that the world once spoke a single language best preserved in Ancient German.[1]

The series' proposed decoding of the symbolism is: vampires represent the elite cabal, werewolves their mercenaries, and zombies the sleeping masses — "useless eaters" or "consumers."[1] The author further claims the Trump and Kennedy families have opposed this cult for generations, and that calling [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] an "agent of Russia" may be a comm pointing to an ancient benevolent society.[1]

## Psychopathy and entertainment

Clown World is characterized in the essay as "a psychopath's playground," on the reasoning that psychopaths wish to be perpetually entertained and are unconcerned with the possibility that life has purpose beyond pleasure.[1] The author connects this to what he calls the nihilism of the secular leftist worldview.[1] He also notes that the belief that all of life is a game has long been treated as an indicator of sociopathy.[1]

## Secret societies and grooming

The essay concludes that secret societies have spent centuries infusing popular culture with "negative spells and demon worship," a process the author describes as grooming — desensitization to negative influences by reframing them as fun and friendly.[1] He explicitly cautions that he is not claiming everyone who dresses as a clown is complicit, noting that many entertainers sincerely wish to make people laugh and have never encountered this material.[1]

## Clown World as a memeplex

Part 4 of [[the-white-pill-emporium|The White Pill Emporium]] (15 February 2025) treats Clown World as one side in a battle of [[memeplex|memeplexes]], defining the term parenthetically as "the set of ideas, narratives, and beliefs that the masses must believe in order for Clown world to remain viable."[2] The essay describes Clown World and its agents as attempting to steer mankind in one direction — dark occultism being characterised as the manipulation of reality through the manipulation of human minds — while the Q team acts in direct opposition by seeding ideas mimetically into the collective using [[anons|anons]] as signal repeaters.[2]

The comparison the author draws is one of resources: Clown World is said to have operated with a shadow budget of untold trillions of dollars, while true followers of Q have had almost nothing but the truth itself on their side.[2] He proposes four diagnostic questions about the past decade — whether awareness of the [[deep-state|Deep State]], awareness of elite child trafficking, trust in mainstream news, and awareness of the contrived nature of current events have risen or fallen — and concludes that, notwithstanding heavy censorship and media resources deployed to discredit Q, [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Q have defeated the Clown World memeplex, citing Trump's 2024 victory as the answer to the narrative war.[2]
