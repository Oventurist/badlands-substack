# Crocus City Hall Attack

The **Crocus City Hall attack** was a mass shooting at a concert hall outside Moscow in March 2024 that killed more than 140 people.[1]

## US intelligence warnings

The United States did not share all of the information it had about the terrorist plot ahead of the attack, according to a report published by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] on Thursday, March 28, 2024.[1] The paper attributed the withholding to the "adversarial relationship between Washington and Moscow," which "prevented US officials from sharing any information about the plot beyond what was necessary, out of fear Russian authorities might learn their intelligence sources or methods."[1]

The [[us-embassy-in-moscow|US Embassy in Moscow]] had issued a public warning on March 7 that specifically told Americans in Russia that "extremists have imminent plans to target large gatherings in Moscow, to include concerts," advising them to avoid large gatherings for 48 hours.[1] The United States also passed the warning to Russia privately; [[fsb|FSB]] chief [[aleksandr-bortnikov|Aleksandr Bortnikov]] said the warning was "of a general nature."[1]

Sources told the Times that Russia tightened security after the warning but may have relaxed it after no attack occurred within the 48-hour window, and that it was unclear whether US intelligence was wrong about the timing or whether the perpetrators noticed the heightened security and decided to wait.[1]

## Russian response

Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said he was unaware of information about the United States withholding intelligence and cast doubt on the report, saying that "the information of The New York Times, citing sources, is information that should be treated with great caution."[1]

## Casualties and arrests

Russian investigators said the 22 March 2024 attack at Moscow's Crocus City
Hall killed at least 144 people, including three children, and injured at least
550 others.[2] Nine suspects, identified as citizens of Tajikistan, were
arrested after gunmen opened fire on concertgoers with automatic weapons and
then set a fire that engulfed the complex, according to Russian
authorities.[2]

## US intelligence bulletin

In the wake of the attack, a US intelligence bulletin warned that
[[isis|ISIS]] operatives worldwide and "lone wolves" might respond to the
group's statements touting the Moscow attack by striking public venues in the
United States, and law enforcement officials expressed concern that domestic
radicals might answer such calls.[2] Badlands contributor
[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed the warning as
fearmongering by the [[cia|CIA]], arguing that ISIS is an Agency creation and
that the alerts formed part of a psyop aimed at destabilizing the United States
and pushing it toward war with [[iran|Iran]].[2]

## Court proceedings against the suspects

A Moscow court convened late on Sunday, March 24, 2024 to hear prosecutors' demands on pre-trial detention for a group of men detained after the attack, which claimed the lives of over 130 people.[3] The suspects, all nationals of Tajikistan, appeared individually; terrorism charges were levelled against 32-year-old Dalerdzhon Mirzoyev, who faced a life sentence if convicted.[3] Prosecutors told Judge Timur Vakhrameev that Mirzoyev had admitted his guilt, and the court opted to rule behind closed doors.[3]

## Immediate aftermath and Putin's response

In the days following the assault, the official Russian death toll reached 133 killed, with some unofficial reports suggesting 140 or more, and three children remained hospitalized, one in critical condition.[4] The mall and complex at the Crocus City Hall music venue burned for many hours through the night, and Russia's Investigative Committee said additional bodies were recovered from the smoldering building.[4] Authorities announced on the Saturday after the attack that four suspected gunmen had been captured, amid unconfirmed social media videos purporting to show detained suspects.[4] Eleven people in total were detained in connection with the attack, and the [[federal-security-service|Federal Security Service]] said the group included "four terrorists who were directly involved in the terrorist attack on Crocus," none of whom were Russian nationals.[4]

The four alleged gunmen were detained "not far from the border with Ukraine," which raised suspicion in the Kremlin about Ukrainian involvement despite widespread Western media reporting attributing the attack to ISIS-K; Russian state media did not echo the Western coverage of an ISIS-K claim of responsibility.[4] [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] vowed in a five-and-a-half-minute video address to punish the perpetrators of the "barbaric terrorist act," charging that Ukraine had prepared a "window" to help the attackers escape and declaring that "all perpetrators, organizers and customers of this crime will suffer fair and inevitable punishment."[4]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the [[us-embassy-in-moscow|US Embassy in Moscow]] advisory telling Americans to avoid large gatherings 48 hours before the massacre as "some of the most on the nose Narrative Seeding yet," arguing that acceleration provokes escalation, escalation provokes exposure, and exposure prompts awakening as the collectivist West continues to "poke the bear."[4]

The attack also figured in reporting on a broader resurgence of the [[islamic-state|Islamic State]], which claimed responsibility for the massacre; it was described as the deadliest militant attack on Russian soil since the 2002 Moscow theater hostage crisis.[4]

## Russian criticism of the US attribution

Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] said in late March 2024 that the United States had backed itself into a corner by rushing to blame the [[islamic-state|Islamic State]] for the attack while the incident was still in progress.[5] Speaking on a Sputnik radio broadcast, she said the fact that "within the first 24 hours, even before the fire was put out, the Americans started screaming that it wasn't Ukraine" was itself "a piece of incriminating evidence."[5] She called the speed with which Washington reached forthright conclusions astonishing, saying it took only a few hours "to get to a microphone, turn on the lights, summon the press and draw a conclusion about who is to blame for this horribly bloody terrorist attack."[5] Zakharova argued that by naming ISIS the US prompted political scientists and international relations experts to recall "what ISIS really is," concluding: "You are behind all those ISIS-type structures, you — the United States, Great Britain — yourselves brought them into being."[5]
