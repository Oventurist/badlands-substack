# Eugenics

Eugenics is described by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in the February 5, 2024 Badlands Media essay "Control Through Language" as the belief in controlling who reproduced and how often.[1]

Carlson writes that at least some, if not all, of a circle of early twentieth-century British and American figures — [[george-orwell|George Orwell]], [[h-g-wells|H.G. Wells]], [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]], [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]] and [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]] — were supporters of eugenics.[1] He states that Adolf Hitler took many of his ideas from eugenics supporters in early twentieth-century America and England.[1] Sanger, in his telling, was the early public face of abortion and of [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]].[1]

Within the essay's wider argument, eugenics belongs to the same family of long-range population-management projects as the [[cabal|Cabal]] plan Carlson believes was shared with those authors and disclosed through their fiction.[1]

## Fabian Society advocacy

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] writes that the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] favoured eugenics — which she defines as the selective murdering or euthanasia of any non-productive person — and argues that the modern term "racist" is too tame a description of the Society's belief system.[2] In her account the same milieu produced [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]] of Planned Parenthood, whose programme she casts as culling those the elite regarded as an inferior herd, the goal being that only strong members reproduce so as to maximize their investments.[2]

## Anglo-American origins of Nazi race science

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that eugenics — which he identifies with the science of Social Darwinism as developed by Thomas Huxley's X Club associate Herbert Spencer and by Darwin's cousin Sir Francis Galton — was the governing scientific ideology of the Third Reich, and that it never originated in German minds or German institutions.[3] In 1932 New York hosted the [[third-eugenics-conference|Third Eugenics Conference]], co-sponsored by JP Morgan banker [[william-draper-jr|William Draper Jr]] and the Harriman family, at which leading eugenicists came to study America's application of eugenics laws begun in 1907 under [[theodore-roosevelt|Theodore Roosevelt]].[3] Speaking there, [[fairfield-osborn|Fairfield Osborn]] argued that eugenics encourages "the survival and multiplication of the fittest" while checking "the multiplication of the unfitted."[3]

During the Great Depression, eugenics laws were applied in two Canadian provinces and spread widely across Europe and America, with 30 U.S. states using them to sterilize the unfit.[3] The [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] went on to fund German eugenics and specifically Joseph Mengele.[3] Ehret writes that [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] was himself a devout eugenicist whose racial purification policies emerged through the funding of the Rockefeller and Carnegie Foundations as well as the British establishment, and also a devout Malthusian who predicted that "the day will certainly come when the whole of mankind will be forced to check the augmentation of the human species."[3]

## Teilhard de Chardin's religious eugenics

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that because evil had no real existence in the system of the Jesuit [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]], nothing prevented him from extolling racially targeted eugenics within a closed system of limited resources.[4] In *Human Energy* Chardin asked what attitude "the advancing wing of humanity" should take toward "fixed or definitely unprogressive ethnical groups," to what extent the earth should "tolerate, racially or nationally, areas of lesser activity," and "to what extent should not the development of the strong… take precedence over the preservation of the weak?"[4] Ehret describes this as pure Nietzsche, Galton and [[thomas-malthus|Malthus]].[4]

Chardin nonetheless favoured expanding eugenics for all races, complaining that his generation regarded "with distrust all efforts proposed by science for controlling the machinery of heredity, of sex-determination and the development of the nervous systems."[4] In 1951 he called for "a nobly human form of eugenics, on a standard worthy of our personalities," concluding that "eugenics applied to individuals leads to eugenics applied to society."[4] Ehret links this program to Chardin's friend [[julian-huxley|Sir Julian Huxley]], President of the [[british-eugenics-society|British Eugenics Society]], whose [[unesco|UNESCO]] manifesto called for reviving eugenics as the most important of all sciences.[4]

## Malthusian roots and the American movement

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes eugenics as "the applied pseudoscience of social Darwinism," a destructive philosophy holding that man should intervene to alter human traits by preventing reproduction of the "unfit," "inferior races" and "useless eaters."[5] Its techniques, in his account, include selective breeding, marriage restriction, forced racial segregation, forced abortion, forced sterilization, human experimentation, the killing of institutionalized populations, infanticide and subversive genocide.[5]

He traces its intellectual descent from [[thomas-malthus|Thomas Malthus]], whose *Essay on the Principle of Population* held that population grows exponentially while agriculture grows arithmetically, through [[charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]] to Darwin's half-cousin [[francis-galton|Sir Francis Galton]], who coined the term from the Greek *eu* and *genés* and is considered the father of the field.[5] Galton's 1863 theory that talented people should marry only one another culminated in an experiment in which the Galton, Darwin, Huxley and Wedgwood families bred exclusively among themselves; within two generations over 90% of their offspring died at birth or were disabled.[5] [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]], brother of [[aldous-huxley|Aldous]], drew on this lineage in coining "trans-humanism" as a member of the British Eugenics Society.[5]

In the United States the movement was carried by [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]]'s money: the [[bureau-of-social-hygiene|Bureau of Social Hygiene]], [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]]'s [[american-birth-control-league|American Birth Control League]], and the research of [[alfred-kinsey|Alfred Kinsey]].[5] By 1927 forced sterilization laws were on the books in 25 states, and in 1928 punch card computers supplied by [[ibm|IBM]] founder [[thomas-watson|Thomas Watson]] were put to use in the field and later in the Nazi death camps.[5] The [[american-eugenics-society|American Eugenics Society]] forcibly sterilized over 64,000 people during the 1950s before renaming itself the Society for the Study of Social Biology.[5]

## Rockefeller and Harriman funding

"The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 5" defines eugenics as a program of scientifically applied genetic selection to maintain and improve "ideal" human characteristics, including birth and population control, and traces it to [[francis-galton|Sir Francis Galton]]'s conclusion that prominent members of British society were prominent because they had "eminent" parents.[6] In the late nineteenth century the United States joined fourteen other nations in passing eugenics legislation; thirty states provided for the sterilization of mental patients and "imbeciles," and at least sixty thousand such "defectives" were legally sterilized.[6]

In 1910 the Eugenics Records Office was established as a branch of the Galton National Laboratory in London, funded by Mrs. E. H. Harriman, wife of railroad magnate Edward Harriman and mother of the diplomat [[averell-harriman|Averell Harriman]]; she sold her substantial shares of New York's Guaranty Trust bank to J. P. Morgan, assuring his control over that institution.[6] After 1900 the Harrimans — the family that gave the [[prescott-bush|Prescott Bush]] family its start — together with the Rockefellers funded more than $11 million to create a eugenics research laboratory at Cold Spring Harbor, New York, and eugenics studies at Harvard, Columbia and Cornell.[6]

The first International Congress of Eugenics convened in London in 1912 with Winston Churchill as director, and many researchers claim the concept of "bloodlines" was highly significant to these figures.[6] When the Congress met in New York in 1932, the Hamburg-Amerika Shipping Line, controlled by Harriman associates George Walker and Prescott Bush, brought prominent Germans to the meeting — among them Dr. [[ernst-rudin|Ernst Rudin]] of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Genealogy and Demography in Berlin, who was unanimously elected president of the International Federation of Eugenics Societies for founding the German Society for Race Hygiene, a forerunner of Hitler's racial institutes.[6] The article contends that eugenics continues today, disguised in various ways, and that the Rockefeller capture of medical education served the same end.[6]
