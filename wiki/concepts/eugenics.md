# Eugenics

Eugenics is described by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in the February 5, 2024 Badlands Media essay "Control Through Language" as the belief in controlling who reproduced and how often.[1]

Carlson writes that at least some, if not all, of a circle of early twentieth-century British and American figures — [[george-orwell|George Orwell]], [[h-g-wells|H.G. Wells]], [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]], [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]] and [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]] — were supporters of eugenics.[1] He states that Adolf Hitler took many of his ideas from eugenics supporters in early twentieth-century America and England.[1] Sanger, in his telling, was the early public face of abortion and of [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]].[1]

Within the essay's wider argument, eugenics belongs to the same family of long-range population-management projects as the [[cabal|Cabal]] plan Carlson believes was shared with those authors and disclosed through their fiction.[1]

## Fabian Society advocacy

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] writes that the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] favoured eugenics — which she defines as the selective murdering or euthanasia of any non-productive person — and argues that the modern term "racist" is too tame a description of the Society's belief system.[2] In her account the same milieu produced [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]] of Planned Parenthood, whose programme she casts as culling those the elite regarded as an inferior herd, the goal being that only strong members reproduce so as to maximize their investments.[2]

## Anglo-American origins of Nazi race science

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that eugenics — which he identifies with the science of Social Darwinism as developed by Thomas Huxley's X Club associate Herbert Spencer and by Darwin's cousin Sir Francis Galton — was the governing scientific ideology of the Third Reich, and that it never originated in German minds or German institutions.[3] In 1932 New York hosted the [[third-eugenics-conference|Third Eugenics Conference]], co-sponsored by JP Morgan banker [[william-draper-jr|William Draper Jr]] and the Harriman family, at which leading eugenicists came to study America's application of eugenics laws begun in 1907 under [[theodore-roosevelt|Theodore Roosevelt]].[3] Speaking there, [[fairfield-osborn|Fairfield Osborn]] argued that eugenics encourages "the survival and multiplication of the fittest" while checking "the multiplication of the unfitted."[3]

During the Great Depression, eugenics laws were applied in two Canadian provinces and spread widely across Europe and America, with 30 U.S. states using them to sterilize the unfit.[3] The [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] went on to fund German eugenics and specifically Joseph Mengele.[3] Ehret writes that [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] was himself a devout eugenicist whose racial purification policies emerged through the funding of the Rockefeller and Carnegie Foundations as well as the British establishment, and also a devout Malthusian who predicted that "the day will certainly come when the whole of mankind will be forced to check the augmentation of the human species."[3]
