# Bondi Beach Mass Shooting

The Bondi Beach mass shooting was an attack on December 14, 2025 in which 15 people were killed during a Hanukkah celebration at Sydney's Bondi Beach, Australia's most famous beach. Australian federal police commissioner Krissy Barrett said on Tuesday, December 16, that it was "a terrorist attack inspired by Islamic State."[1]

## Attack and casualties

The suspects were a father and son, aged 50 and 24. The older man, named by state officials as Sajid Akram, was shot dead by police; his son was hospitalized and in custody.[1] Those killed ranged in age from 10 to 87 years old. Twenty-five people remained in hospital after the massacre, ten of them in critical condition, including three patients at a children's hospital. Among the wounded was Ahmed al Ahmed, who was captured on video tackling and disarming one assailant, pointing the man's weapon at him and then setting it on the ground.[1]

The Tuesday news conference by political and law enforcement leaders was the first time officials confirmed their beliefs about the suspects' ideologies. Prime Minister [[anthony-albanese|Anthony Albanese]] said the assessment rested on evidence obtained, including "the presence of Islamic State flags in the vehicle that has been seized."[1]

## Gun law response

Albanese announced that Australia would tighten its firearms laws, creating a national firearms registry and providing A$100 million in funding for the states. New measures included limits on gun ownership and a requirement that buyers be Australian citizens. He said "strong, decisive and focused action was needed on gun law reform as an immediate action," and emphasized the need for national unity against antisemitism, while critics highlighted failures in handling rising antisemitic incidents.[1]
