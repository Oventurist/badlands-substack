# 2024 Trump Abortion Position Statement

On Monday, April 8, 2024, former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said that abortion rights should be left to the states, offering what CNN described as his clearest stance yet on one of the most delicate and contentious issues in American politics.[1]

## The statement

In a video posted to his Truth Social account, Trump said: "My view is now that we have abortion where everyone wanted it from a legal standpoint, the states will determine by vote or legislation, or perhaps both. And whatever they decide must be the law of the land. In this case, the law of the state."[1] He added that "many states will be different," with differing week limits and differing degrees of conservatism, and that "at the end of the day, this is all about the will of the people."[1]

Trump had previously suggested he could support a 15-week federal ban with exceptions for incest, rape and the life of the mother.[1] His ultimate decision to punt the politically fraught issue to the states rather than back a national ban was swiftly denounced by a major anti-abortion rights organization, which said his position did not go far enough.[1]

In the same video Trump said he was "proudly the person responsible" for the Supreme Court's overturning of [[roe-v-wade|Roe v. Wade]], which he said took the issue "out of the federal hands and brought it into the hearts, minds, and vote of the people in each state."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the left cannot simultaneously paint Trump as a pro-life radical and a threat to "women's health" while all of conservative media calls him weak on abortion, calling the outcome ideal politically and necessary for eliminating what he termed the Uniparty Left's anti-MAGA turnout narrative.[1] He wrote that Trump had already gotten Roe overturned, that the matter is now a state issue, and that activists should win the issue in their own states rather than demand absolute purity from "the President of all Americans."[1] In his reading, Trump was using the Controlled Opposition dynamic against itself: with the Uniparty Left calling him terrible and the Uniparty Right calling him insufficiently committed, both were effectively branding him the only acceptable choice.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that conservatives had spent decades demanding Roe's reversal and celebrating when it came, only to condemn Trump for declining to enact a federal ban.[1] She argued the position was strategically smart given that abortion ranks as a top-three issue for many voters and that it would calm fears the regime had cultivated among young women, while calling for a federal ban at that moment "could be outcome determinative."[1] She further contended that pro-life lobbying organizations in Washington were losing money because a federal ban was off the table in an election year, concluding that the fight was "about money and power" rather than healthcare or life.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] warned against allowing "the Doom Brigade in the Con Inc. media" to pile the sins of the establishment uniparty and the devolution of American culture onto one man.[1] He argued Trump did not solve the problem but returned it to "the free market battlefield," removing the ability of activists to default to "Muh Roe v. Wade," and noted that Trump's reference to the outcome "everyone wanted" was qualified in the legal realm rather than the social or cultural one — a distinction he said left the cultural work to citizens rather than "presidents and god emperors."[1]
