# 2024 US Weapons Holdup to Israel

The **2024 US weapons holdup to Israel** refers to the decision by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, reported in early May 2024, to delay shipments of two types of [[boeing|Boeing]]-made precision bombs to Israel in order to send a political message about the conduct of the war in Gaza.[1]

## The pause

According to a U.S. official and six other people with knowledge of the deliberations, the United States had yet to sign off on a pending sale of Boeing's Joint Direct Attack Munitions — both the munitions themselves and the kits that convert them into smart weapons — as well as Small Diameter Bombs.[1] Six industry and congressional sources described the state of the discussions.[1]

The administration did not formally deny the potential sale.[1] Instead, as a U.S. administration official familiar with the process told Politico, it was "essentially taking action through inaction" — holding off on approvals and other aspects of the weapons transfer process in order to send a message to Israel.[1] The officials involved were granted anonymity to discuss sensitive internal deliberations.[1]

## Related developments

The holdup coincided with a delay in the [[state-department|State Department]]'s congressionally mandated report on whether Israel had violated U.S. and international humanitarian law during the war in Gaza; see the [[2024-state-department-report-on-israels-conduct-in-gaza|report on Israel's wartime conduct]].[1] It also coincided with a rare public statement by the Saudi Foreign Ministry concerning attacks by Israeli settlers on Jordanian humanitarian aid convoys bound for Rafah.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Ben Shapiro and the Daily Wire had drawn a parallel to the impeachment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the withholding of aid, but dismissed that framing as "low-brow" and "lazy."[1] He argued instead that the weapons pause functioned as a smokescreen for the far larger story of the pending humanitarian-law determination, which he described as a lose-lose for the administration: affirming the allegations would leave [[benjamin-netanyahu|Bibi Netanyahu]] exposed, while exonerating Israel would trigger a fresh wave of domestic unrest.[1]
