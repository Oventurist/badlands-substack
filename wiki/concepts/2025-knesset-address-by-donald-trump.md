# 2025 Knesset Address by Donald Trump

The 2025 Knesset address by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] was a speech delivered before the Israeli parliament on Monday, October 13, 2025, in which the US president declared "the historic dawn of the new Middle East" as the final 20 living hostages were released from [[gaza|Gaza]] under a US-brokered ceasefire deal.[1] Trump characterized the moment as marking "not only the end of war, but the end of an age of terror and death and the beginning of the age of faith and hope and of God."[1]

## Announcement of the visit

Trump signalled the trip days in advance. On October 8, 2025, immediately after announcing that [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] had signed off on the first phase of his Gaza peace plan, Trump told Axios in a brief phone interview that he was "likely going to Israel in the coming days" to address the [[knesset|Knesset]].[2] "It's a great day for Israel and for the world," he said.[2] He described having just had a "great" call with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]: "He is so happy. He should be. It is a great achievement. The whole world has come together to reach this deal, including countries that were enemies."[2] Netanyahu said after the call that he had invited Trump to address the Knesset, and Trump replied, "I will definitely do that if they want me to."[2] The [[white-house|White House]] said Trump could depart for the region some time after a physical exam that Friday.[2] Axios noted that ending the war in Gaza was one of Trump's key campaign promises and that the agreement was the biggest diplomatic achievement of his second term.[2]

Badlands commentary published before the visit predicted its tone. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Trump was "seizing control of the situation by using the momentum of the peace plan to exert himself onto the Israeli political landscape," asking who in Israel could defy him in the moment or dare oppose peace.[2] The commentary contrasted the anticipated visit with Trump's May 2025 trip to Arabia, which it described as a celebration of fraternal relations, mutual investment and excitement for a coming Golden Age; the Israel visit, by contrast, would be "more stern and sobering," about "putting people in line and bringing order to chaos," likened to a parent returning to a house of unsupervised children.[2] In blunter terms, the Brief said Trump was "going to Israel to give out spankings and remind everybody who is in charge."[2]

The same commentary cited as evidence a report that Trump had a [[qatar|Qatari]] official present in the Oval Office alongside Netanyahu the previous week to supervise a telephoned apology to Sheikh [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim Al-Thani]], describing the episode as a "peak humiliation ritual."[2] It predicted Trump would look "radical zealots" in the eye and declare that the Age of Peace had arrived, while doubting they would honor any pledge, since in the Badlands reading they are animated by a "crusader complex" and a belief that their destiny is to conquer the Middle East and establish [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] in anticipation of the coming messiah, Mashiach.[2] Provoking another war, likely with Iran, would remain their priority, and only the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] could stop them.[2]

## Content of the address

Trump told Israeli lawmakers that [[israel|Israel]] had no more to achieve on the battlefield and must now work toward peace across the region, and indicated he would carry a similar message to regional leaders in Egypt later the same day.[1] He pressed Israel to look past the war, arguing it had accomplished all it could "by force of arms."[1] In the speech's most-quoted passage, he said: "You've won. I mean, you've won. Now it is time to translate these victories against terrorists on the battlefield into the ultimate prize of peace and prosperity for the entire Middle East."[1] The address was reported as rapturously received by the assembled members of the [[knesset|Knesset]].[1]

During the same address Trump publicly called for Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to receive a pardon in his ongoing corruption trial, an intervention he later described to reporters aboard Air Force One as unplanned and prompted by the strength of the applause Netanyahu was receiving at that moment.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Trump had "exerted himself onto the situation" and in doing so "completely rewrote the direction of the story."[1] The commentary disputed the premise that Israel had won anything substantive, describing the Israeli economy as "in shambles," damaged by the IDF's mandated reservist program, and Israel's international public relations as poor.[1] It further contended that no one in Netanyahu's circle could articulate a coherent plan for Gaza or the West Bank beyond asserting divine entitlement, and that at least some figures in the Israeli government intended ethnic cleansing of Palestine.[1]

The Badlands reading held that Trump was supplying the Israeli political class with a narrative sense of accomplishment, much as he had done in naming and ending the earlier attack on Iran — codifying an engagement into the histories by declaring it finished.[1] Under this interpretation the speech functioned as an ultimatum to the Knesset: stop warmongering and pursue peace, or risk Trump walking away.[1]

A second take by [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] framed the episode against the 2021–2024 period, arguing that neoconservatives and "ConInc" commentators — characterized as "Israel First GOPe normies" — had worked to keep Trump out of the position from which he could end the "Israel constantly under threat" narrative.[1]

## Announcement and Likud boycott

Ahead of the address, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told Axios in a phone interview from Air Force One en route to Israel that the Gaza peace deal "could be the biggest thing I was ever involved in," and said his message to the Israeli people would be "Love and peace for eternity."[3] The speech was scheduled for the Monday morning after the [[sharm-el-sheikh-gaza-signing-ceremony-2025|Sharm el-Sheikh signing ceremony]] in Egypt, during a brief visit in which he was also to meet hostage families and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that while there was plenty of fanfare among the Israeli population for the diplomatic triumph, members of the Likud Party were boycotting the speech as an act of defiance, angry that "their war of conquest is being shutdown," and warned that such moments historically make "bad-twin" Israelis exceptionally dangerous.[3]
