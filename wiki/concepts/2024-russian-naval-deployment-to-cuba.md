# 2024 Russian Naval Deployment to Cuba

The **2024 Russian naval deployment to Cuba** was the June 2024 transit of a small Russian flotilla past Florida's eastern coast en route to a port call in [[cuba|Cuba]], shadowed by American and Canadian naval and air assets.[1]

## Shadowing operation

Open-source maritime and aerial tracking data revealed that U.S. naval and air assets appeared to be shadowing the Russian warships.[1] OSINT analysts posted updates on Tuesday, June 11, 2024 showing the coastguard vessel CG *Stone*, the [[us-navy|U.S. Navy]] destroyers USS *Truxtun* and USS *Donald Cook*, and the Royal Canadian Navy frigate HMCS *Ville de Quebec* travelling southward down the Florida coast, apparently following the Russian ships bound for Cuba.[1] Overhead, at least one U.S. Navy P-8A Poseidon and a Canadian CP-140 Aurora appeared to be conducting surveillance.[1] The OSINT channel TheIntelFrog wrote on X late on Monday that the American and Canadian assets "may be shadowing" the Russian flotilla.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the deployment as "Putin and the lads buzzing the tower," and predicted the reaction would display "the unbridled hubris and entitlement of warmongering elites" accustomed to America's geographic isolation.[1] He noted that Russia defends a border roughly 14,000 miles long shared with fourteen nations, not counting Alaska, which lies only 2.4 miles from the nearest Russian island in the Bering Strait — circumstances he argued force a mastery of diplomacy that American hegemony has discouraged.[1]

He anticipated a complaint from Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] over Russian warships conducting the same Caribbean exercises they had run annually throughout the 2010s, noting that ten years earlier Russia had sent four ships to Havana including the nuclear-powered icebreaker/tug *Nikolay Chiker*, which returned as part of the 2024 armada.[1] While [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] described the trip as a show of force, the commentary stressed it comprised only four ships and roughly 190 sailors — against six U.S. fleets, with the Sixth Fleet alone fielding around 40 ships and 21,000 sailors and the Seventh Fleet nearly 70 ships and 27,000 sailors, routinely operating near Russian waters.[1] As a direct mirror, the piece cited the 2020 U.S. dispatch of four ships into the Barents Sea, in Russia's naval backyard.[1] The conclusion offered was that "we are watching a movie," with a jocular wish for a [[donald-trump|Trump]] flotilla to link up with the Russians as an "iconic memetic event."[1]

## Western naval responses

A Canadian navy patrol ship sailed into Havana early on Friday, June 14, 2024, hours after the United States announced that a fast-attack submarine had docked at its Guantanamo naval base in [[cuba|Cuba]], both vessels arriving on the heels of the Russian warships that had reached the island earlier that week.[3] [[reuters|Reuters]] described the confluence of Russian, Canadian and U.S. vessels around a Communist-run island 145 km (90 miles) south of Florida as a reminder of old Cold War tensions and of fraught ties between Russia and Western nations over the Ukraine war, while noting that both the United States and Cuba said the Russian warships posed no threat to the region and that Russia characterized their arrival in allied Cuba as routine.[3]

The Admiral Gorshkov frigate and the nuclear-powered submarine Kazan, the latter half submerged with its crew on deck, had sailed into Havana harbor on Wednesday after conducting "high-precision missile weapons" training in the Atlantic Ocean, according to Russia's defence ministry.[3] Canada's Margaret Brooke patrol vessel began maneuvers early Friday to enter Havana harbor as part of what the Canadian Joint Operations Command called a port visit in recognition of the long-standing bilateral relationship between Canada and Cuba.[3] Hours earlier, [[us-southern-command|U.S. Southern Command]] said the fast-attack submarine Helena had arrived on a routine port visit to Guantanamo Bay, a U.S. naval base on the tip of the island about 850 km (530 miles) southeast of Havana.[3]

## Initial reporting, June 2024

A senior administration official told McClatchy and the Miami Herald in early June 2024 that Russia was preparing to deploy aircraft and combat naval vessels to the Caribbean for military exercises in the coming weeks — its first Western Hemisphere exercises involving both air and sea activity in five years.[2] The Biden administration did not express concern, with the official saying the deployment posed "no direct threat to the United States," but assessed that Moscow intended the exercises as a "messaging tactic" after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] gave Ukraine permission to fire U.S.-made weapons across its border into Russia.[2]

Officials expected Moscow to "conduct heightened naval and air activity near the United States," likely including port calls by combat vessels in [[cuba|Cuba]] and possibly [[venezuela|Venezuela]], two longstanding Russian allies that have hosted occasional Russian naval visits over the previous two decades, along with possible aircraft deployments and flights in the region.[2] A second U.S. official said the administration suspected Cuba approved the port call "at least in part" because of a 2023 incident in which a U.S. nuclear submarine docked at Guantanamo Bay Naval Base, angering Havana.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the deployment as [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] "operating from a position of logic, reason, and accountability" against a Globalist Hegemony he compared to a scorned ex-lover descending into vengeful psychosis, arguing Washington's leadership had forfeited legitimacy by ignoring election integrity and cost-of-living concerns.[2]
