# Ben Sasse University of Florida Spending Investigation

The Ben Sasse University of Florida spending investigation refers to the August 2024 scrutiny of former Nebraska senator [[ben-sasse|Ben Sasse]] over financial issues during his brief tenure as president of the [[university-of-florida|University of Florida]], an inquiry called for by Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and chronicled by the Conservative Treehouse.[1]

## Resignation and timing

Sasse quit his job as president of the University of Florida a few weeks before the report, giving as his stated reason the need to care for his wife, who has epilepsy — a rationale the Conservative Treehouse said looked sketchy in light of subsequent revelations about his spending.[1] The outlet framed the matter as one that would become increasingly critical as DeSantis positioned himself for a renewed White House run in 2028.[1]

## The alleged quid pro quo

The Conservative Treehouse laid out a multi-year sequence it characterized as a completed quid pro quo.[1] It had noted years earlier that DeSantis was positioning for a 2024 presidential bid, citing as a supporting datapoint a $20 million donation to DeSantis from the Republican Governors Association at a time when Nebraska Governor [[pete-ricketts|Pete Ricketts]] was the RGA chairman.[1] After that donation, DeSantis used the influence of his office to support Nebraska Senator Sasse for the University of Florida presidency; as Politico reported, "Sasse's sherpa through the university search process was Gov. Ron DeSantis' chief of staff [[james-uthmeier|James Uthmeier]]," who was put in contact with Sasse after Sasse quietly expressed interest in the post.[1]

Once Sasse accepted the University of Florida position and resigned from Congress, Nebraska Governor Jim Pillen appointed former Governor Ricketts to fill Sasse's Senate seat, with Ricketts serving two years ahead of a 2024 special Senate election.[1] In the Conservative Treehouse's telling, DeSantis took the $20 million from Chairman Ricketts and then opened Ricketts's path to the Senate by using his office to move Sasse — a sequence transparent to those paying attention despite unfolding over two years.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] greeted the story as overdue exposure of establishment self-dealing, writing that such schemes had likely been running for years and that the difference now was public attention.[1] He offered the episode as a concrete illustration of his contention that establishment Republicans are "the true enemies of the Republic," describing them as grifters who had been draining the society's life-force for generations.[1] He urged readers to convert anger into study of how the enemy operates — how they lie, cheat and steal, send young men into foreign wars and profit from them — so that a future [[donald-trump|Trump]] restoration could disinfect the federal government efficiently.[1]

He argued that anything short of a complete reckoning of the federal apparatus and the parasitic organizations that leech off it, including the GOP itself, would be judged a failure by future generations.[1] He also suggested that investigators should "squeeze Sasse" for information about what he termed the child-trafficking cartel controlling Washington before any prison sentence, predicting Sasse would cooperate to purchase leniency unless the cartel held more intimidating leverage over him.[1]
