# Colorado Capitol Trump Portrait Dispute

The Colorado Capitol Trump portrait dispute was a March 2025 controversy in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly demanded that the state of Colorado remove a painting of him hanging in the State Capitol, which he described as "purposefully distorted."[1]

## Trump's statement

In a Sunday post on Truth Social, Trump wrote that "nobody likes a bad picture or painting of themselves, but the one in Colorado, in the State Capitol put up by the Governor, along with all other Presidents, was purposefully distorted to a level that even I, perhaps, have never seen before."[1] He added that the same artist had also painted President Barack Obama, who "looks wonderful," while the portrait of himself was "truly the worst," and remarked that the artist "must have lost her talent as she got older."[1] Trump said he would prefer having no picture at all to keeping the one on display, and stated that many Coloradans had contacted the state seeking to have it changed or removed.[1]

## The artist

The portrait was painted by [[sarah-a-boardman|Sarah A. Boardman]], a British-born, Colorado-based portrait artist, and had been on display in the Capitol since 2019 during Trump's first presidential term.[2] Boardman also painted the Colorado State Capitol's portrait of President Barack Obama, which Trump described as "wonderful"; both works were based on photographs rather than live sittings.[2] Trump said Colorado Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] should "be ashamed of himself" for putting up the painting and wrote, "I would much prefer not having a picture than having this one."[2] Reporting framed the criticism as part of broader tensions between the [[white-house|White House]] and the leadership of the Democratic-leaning state.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the painting demonstrated real technique but that its lack of contrast between lights and darks made it look flat and lacking depth, "like a skilled high schooler painted it," well short of what should be expected for a portrait of a head of state displayed in a state capitol.[2] He speculated that the governor had recruited an amateur artist operating on "the old government metrics," and concluded that while the work might not be technically bad, it should not serve as an official representation of the president.[2]
