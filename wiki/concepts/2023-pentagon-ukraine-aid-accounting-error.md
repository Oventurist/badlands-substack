# 2023 Pentagon Ukraine Aid Accounting Error

The **2023 Pentagon Ukraine aid accounting error** was the [[pentagon|Department of Defense]]'s June 20, 2023 disclosure that it had overestimated the value of weapons sent to Ukraine by $6.2 billion over the preceding two years — roughly double earlier estimates — producing a surplus that would be applied to future security assistance packages.[1]

## The error

Pentagon spokeswoman [[sabrina-singh|Sabrina Singh]] said a detailed review found that the military services had used replacement costs rather than the book value of equipment drawn from Pentagon stocks and shipped to Ukraine.[1] Final calculations placed the error at $3.6 billion in the then-current fiscal year and $2.6 billion in fiscal 2022, which had ended the previous September 30.[1] The practical consequence was that the department held additional money to support Ukraine's counteroffensive against Russia, arriving as the fiscal year wound down and congressional funding was beginning to dwindle.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] treated the disclosure sardonically, framing it as the arithmetic of "a massive, decade-long money laundering operation for the [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]]" and noting that the [[associated-press|Associated Press]] had presented the surplus as a silver lining that freed up still more money for Ukraine.[1] He asked what other accounting errors had slipped through the cracks and argued the episode was a case for greater civic activism, contending that citizens hold the greatest power to rein in out-of-control government but must educate, organize and mobilize to realize the founders' vision of a competent citizenry exercising collective oversight.[1]
