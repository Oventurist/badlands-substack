# Gangster Rap

Gangster rap is the subgenre of hip-hop that emerged in the late 1980s, characterized by lyrics about crime, drug dealing, and violence against police.[1]

## Alleged engineered origin

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] dates the shift to roughly five years before the 1994 [[oj-simpson|O.J. Simpson]] murder trial, describing a drastic change in which rappers who had rapped about positivity, unity, and ending violence gave way to rappers focused on shooting cops and selling drugs.[1] He argues this apparently organic change was in fact spurred on by the [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[cia|CIA]], which he says had come to run record labels, leaving no place in the genre for artists who did not rap about crime.[1]

## Alleged purpose

Carlson contends gangster rap was designed to influence young Black men to pursue a life of crime, at a moment when crime rates were falling and the newly privatized prison system needed to be filled.[1] He links the effort to the [[crime-bill-1994|1994 Crime Bill]] advanced by [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and to [[cia|CIA]] drug importation into South Central Los Angeles, describing a single system in which culture, narcotics, and sentencing law combined to populate private prisons and generate kickbacks to cooperating politicians.[1]
