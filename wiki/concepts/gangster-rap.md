# Gangster Rap

Gangster rap is presented by Erik Carlson as an artificially engineered musical genre created to drive young black men toward criminality and thereby fill newly privatized prisons.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## The claimed shift

In the late 1980s, Carlson writes, a drastic change occurred in rap music: artists who had rapped about positivity, unity and ending violence gave way to artists rapping about shooting cops and selling drugs, and gangster rap was born.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) What may have appeared to be an organic evolution was, he asserts, spurred on by the [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[cia|CIA]], motivated by falling crime rates and the need to populate the privatized prison system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Alleged mechanism

Carlson claims the FBI and CIA were by then running record labels, and that a hip-hop artist who was not rapping about committing crimes and killing police had no place in the genre.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) Gangster rap was, in this account, designed to influence young black men to pursue a life of crime, dovetailing with the [[crime-bill-1994|1994 Crime Bill]] and [[prison-privatization|prison privatization]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) The timing is placed roughly five years before the [[oj-simpson|O. J. Simpson]] murder trial.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)
