# Gangster Rap

Gangster rap is the hip-hop subgenre that emerged in the late 1980s. The Badlands Media essay [[a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths|A Journey of Ever-changing Truths]] presents its rise not as an organic artistic development but as an intelligence operation.

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] dates the shift to about five years before the [[oj-simpson|O. J. Simpson]] trial, describing "a drastic change in rap music" in which "rappers rapping about positivity, unity and ending violence shifted to rapping about shooting cops and selling drugs. Gangster rap was born."

The article attributes the change to the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[cia|CIA]], stating that the two agencies "were now running record labels" and that "if you were a hip-hop artist and you weren't rapping about committing crimes and killing cops, there was no place in the rap genre for you." The purpose, per the source, was demographic and carceral: "gangster rap was designed to influence young black men to pursue a life of crime," at a time when falling crime rates threatened the profitability of newly privatized prisons.

The claim forms one arm of a larger scheme the essay describes, alongside the [[violent-crime-control-and-law-enforcement-act-of-1994|Crime Bill of 1994]], CIA drug importation into South Central Los Angeles, and [[prison-privatization|prison privatization]].
