# New START Treaty

**New START** is the strategic nuclear arms limitation treaty whose expiry was treated by the Badlands Substack team as one of the defining geopolitical events of early 2026. Episode 5 of [[badlands-blitz|The Blitz]], published February 7, 2026, was subtitled "The END of the START ...", framing the treaty's termination as a central subject of that week's coverage.[1]

## Treatment in Badlands coverage

The Blitz discussed the end of New START alongside what the hosts called "Iranian Nuclear Rugs," presenting the two nuclear storylines as part of a single week of signal to be extracted from the surrounding noise.[1] The episode's guiding question was framed explicitly as: "From Iranian Nuclear Rugs to the END of the New START treaty, when is escalation actually de-escalation?"[1]

That framing reflects the outlet's recurring interpretive posture, in which apparently alarming developments — the lapse of a nuclear arms control regime among them — are read as potential indicators of an underlying de-escalation rather than as straightforward drift toward confrontation.[1] The hosts placed the treaty's end within their broader category of "Sovereign Signal," the term they use for developments they regard as meaningful beneath surface-level media noise.[1]

## Related themes

The episode paired the treaty discussion with Iranian nuclear matters, connecting the subject to [[iran|Iran]] and to the wider question of how nuclear posture is communicated publicly.[1] It also considered whether the correct lessons were being drawn from the proliferation and amplification of the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein Narrative]], treating both as case studies in narrative management.[1]

## Aftermath of expiration

Writing in *The Wall Street Journal* in February 2026, Senator [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]] called the treaty's lapse "a watershed moment in American nuclear strategy," arguing that "far from a failure of diplomacy, this expiration is an overdue correction of a strategic mistake that left America vulnerable to two nuclear rivals: Russia and China."[2] The expiration coincided with rising "arms race" rhetoric and with the first air transport of a [[nuclear-microreactor|nuclear microreactor]] prototype by the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] and the [[pentagon|Department of War]], though no new US nuclear weapons policy was announced.[2]

## Expiration without renewal (2026)

By February 2026 the treaty — the last remaining major U.S.-Russia agreement limiting deployed strategic nuclear warheads to 1,550 and delivery systems to 800 per side — was set to expire imminently without renewal.[3] The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had not responded to Russia's September offer of a one-year extension without inspections, with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] indicating he preferred a new deal including China and downplaying expiration risks: "If it expires, it expires. We'll just do a better agreement."[3] Russia cited demands for the involvement of Britain and France, modernization of systems not covered by the treaty, and the prior suspension of U.S. inspections amid Ukraine tensions as complicating factors.[3]

Experts and press reports warned of arsenal expansions by both nations, a broader global arms race involving China, the loss of verification mechanisms, and heightened instability.[3] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the coverage differently, arguing that the [[media-protectorate|Media Protectorate]] was belatedly translating a pattern it did not understand and that the "New Arms Race" is entirely narrative — escalatory framing that grants "shielding" to all three poles of the [[sovereign-trinity|Tripolar Alliance]] for accelerated modernization and defense investment, precisely because no terminal kinetic escalation is coming.[3]

## Trump's January 2025 call for denuclearization talks

Addressing the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos by video link on January 23, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he wanted to hold talks with [[russia|Russia]] and China about reducing nuclear weapon stockpiles.[5] During his first term Trump had failed to bring China into negotiations to extend New START, the arms treaty with Russia that placed key limits on deployed nuclear weapons and was due to expire in February 2026.[5] US and Russian participation in the treaty had effectively frozen during the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, as Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] sought to impose costs on Washington for supporting Ukraine militarily; Putin had also threatened the use of nuclear weapons against Ukraine to deter US and European military support for Kyiv.[5]

Trump recounted talks with Putin ahead of the 2020 US election about denuclearization and said "China would have come along."[5] He told the forum: "We want to see if we can denuclearize, and I think that's very possible. And I can tell you that President Putin wanted to do it, he and I wanted to do it. We had a good conversation with China, they would have been involved, and that would have been an unbelievable thing for the planet."[5]

Badlands contributor Jon Herold linked the remarks to a 1987 article describing Trump's "ultimate deal" — a "Trump Plan" for "a deal with the soviets" to halt nuclear weapons proliferation — concluding "Trump = Peacemaker."[5] Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the denuclearization comments within the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] framework, arguing that Trump had disclosed in a single day that Putin had wanted a disarmament deal two decades earlier, that China could again be brought aboard through [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], and that Saudi Arabia would invest in the American economy under [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[5]

## Expiration (February 5, 2026)

The treaty expired on February 5, 2026, removing the last binding caps and verification regime governing US and Russian strategic nuclear arsenals.[4] The expiration coincided with the announcement that the United States and [[russia|Russia]] would reestablish high-level military-to-military communications following trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi.[4] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on Truth Social that rather than extend "NEW START" — which he called "a badly negotiated deal by the United States" that was "being grossly violated" — US nuclear experts should work on "a new, improved, and modernized Treaty that can last long into the future."[4] In the same post he asserted that he had rebuilt the US military, added Space Force and battleships, and stopped nuclear wars from breaking out between Pakistan and India, [[iran|Iran]] and Israel, and Russia and [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[4]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the lapse was a contradiction the media should have welcomed as escalatory but did not, concluding that the Obama-era START framework "was never meant to ease tensions, but to keep them frozen in perpetuity," along with all advancement along nuclear lines.[4] In his reading, Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] are gearing up for peace by removing systemic shackles from technologies that will power an "Age of Abundance," and by bringing their backchannel communications into public view.[4]

## 2024 expert warnings on expiration

In a June 2024 editorial in the journal *Science*, Harvard professor [[matthew-bunn|Matthew Bunn]] warned that the 2010 New START Treaty was the last remaining agreement limiting U.S. and Russian nuclear forces, that it expires in 2026, that Russia was blocking the inspections it requires, and that no new talks were underway.[6] Bunn argued that "the risk of nuclear war has not been so high since the Cuban Missile Crisis" and that the world could soon face an unrestrained arms competition for the first time in over five decades — a more complex one involving more countries and more technologies.[6]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the editorial as a narrative deployment rather than a neutral assessment, arguing that invoking a "Harvard Expert" served to make audiences not merely fear but expect a nuclear crisis from what he called the "New Axis."[6]

## February 2023 suspension legislation

On February 21, 2023, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] announced a freeze on Russian participation in New START during a speech to both houses of the Russian parliament, in which he also repeated accusations that the West was seeking to destroy Russia.[7] Moscow stated the same day that Russia would nonetheless continue to observe the treaty's limits on the number of nuclear warheads it can deploy despite the decision to suspend participation.[7] Later that day Putin submitted a draft law on the suspension to the [[state-duma|State Duma]], which speaker [[vyacheslav-volodin|Vyacheslav Volodin]] said would consider the measure on Wednesday, February 22, and take an immediate decision.[7]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] read the move as posturing rather than a prelude to nuclear war, arguing that the Biden administration had "undone 50 years of diplomacy" while its base supported confrontation with Russia, and speculating that a negotiated de-escalation brokered by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would effectively secure his 2024 campaign.[7]

## Putin's October 2025 one-year offer

Speaking ahead of a Security Council meeting on Monday, October 6, 2025, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said Moscow would continue to adhere to the treaty for one year after its expiration the following February, provided Washington agreed to do the same and refrained from actions that could break the nuclear status quo, such as deploying interceptor systems in space.[8] Putin said global strategic stability had been increasingly threatened by what he described as the destructive actions of Western nations, and stressed that while Moscow was ready to meet any threat it had always preferred political and diplomatic means of resolving conflicts.[8] RT noted that the 2010 treaty establishes limits on the number of intercontinental-range nuclear weapons each side may deploy.[8] Badlands host [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Putin and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were publicly telegraphing an intention to eliminate nuclear weapons worldwide while simultaneously signing bilateral agreements establishing civil nuclear energy programs in even the poorest countries, such as Burkina Faso.[8]
