# January 2026 Government Shutdown Standoff

The January 2026 government shutdown standoff was a federal appropriations impasse driven by the political fallout from immigration enforcement in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]].

## The impasse

As reported in the January 28, 2026 Badlands Brief, a Friday funding deadline threatened a partial shutdown after Senate Democrats vowed to block a Republican-backed six-bill spending package unless it excluded [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] provisions containing an additional $10 billion for [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]], on top of allocations from the prior year's [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill Act]]. [[polymarket|Polymarket]] odds of a shutdown by January 31, 2026 stood at roughly 79 percent. Agencies at risk included DHS — encompassing ICE, FEMA, TSA, and the Coast Guard — along with the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], Labor, Transportation, and HUD.

The trigger was the national uproar over federal enforcement tactics in Minneapolis, where two U.S. citizens, [[alex-pretti|Alex Pretti]] and [[renee-good|Renee Good]], were fatally shot by federal agents. Senator [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] described the victims as individuals who were "from all appearances... not violent criminals."

Senate Minority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] called White House and GOP proposals "unrealistic," demanding reforms including a prohibition on federal agents wearing masks during operations and a requirement for judicial search warrants before entering homes. Schumer said the five non-DHS bills could pass immediately if separated and warned Republicans would bear responsibility for a shutdown. Republicans refused to strip the DHS language, citing difficulty securing House votes for a revised bill while the chamber was in recess.

## De-escalation and commentary

The administration pursued de-escalatory steps: demoting [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] official [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]] from the Minneapolis operations in favor of border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], presidential phone calls to Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]], and DHS Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] agreeing to testify before the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]].

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] placed the standoff in a sequence: the 35-day 2018 shutdown, also over immigration enforcement, and the 43-day December 2025 shutdown over eligibility changes removing migrants from SNAP and Medicaid. She characterized a shutdown of services for Americans in defense of migrant interests as treason in principle, while noting she favors government shutdowns on their own merits.
