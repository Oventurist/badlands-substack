# El Paquete Semanal

*El Paquete* is the popular but illegal offline media distribution system in [[cuba|Cuba]], described in the Badlands Media essay "Glimpses into Hell — Part 1" as the equivalent of putting a week's worth of news, music, videos and other material on USB drives and selling them.[1] The essay characterizes it as a roughly one-terabyte collection of digital material distributed through Cuba's underground market — "like viewing a tiny, curated portion of the internet without actually being online."[1]

## Cost and context

A copy costs 5 CUC (Cuban convertible pesos), which in Cuba's economy consumes a large share of the average citizen's weekly earnings.[1] The trade exists because the Cuban state holds a monopoly on connectivity: there are no private internet providers, and the alternative is expensive public access points with poor connection quality.[1] The essay notes that Cubans were not even permitted to own cell phones until around 2008, and that possession could result in jail.[1] These restrictions became salient during the [[2021-cuban-protests|2021 Cuban protests]], when the state cut internet access outright.[1]
