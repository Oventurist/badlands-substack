# Federal Reserve Act

The Federal Reserve Act of 1913 is the statute that created the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] System and, with it, the modern American central bank. Across the Badlands Media corpus it is treated as the founding act of the debt-based monetary order; in [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s article [[are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom|"Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?"]] it is identified as a specific legal target of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s alleged long-range strategy.

The article argues that the state-level bans on [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDCs]] and the objections raised by members of [[congress]] are not obstacles but deliberate provocations designed to generate litigation between the Federal Reserve and the states. That litigation, Lange writes, concerns "not just the definition of money, but also the constitutionality of the central bank itself." He contends that Trump prioritized reshaping the federal judiciary in order to secure precedent-setting rulings from the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], which would revisit the Legal Tender Cases and the Gold Clause decisions and "decide, once and for all if an all powerful central bank is even constitutional."

The intended outcome, as the article describes it, is the removal of [[fiat-currency|fiat currency]] and the central bank, a return to the original constitutional monetary provisions, and the establishment of a digital dollar backed by gold and redeemable in it.
