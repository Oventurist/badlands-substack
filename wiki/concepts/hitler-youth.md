# Hitler Youth

The **Hitler Youth** was the youth organisation of the German Nazi Party, whose origins can be dated to the period 1922–1926 and whose heyday ran from 1936 to 1945.[1] Its stated purpose was to indoctrinate German children into the Nazi Party and the Nazi way of thinking, drawing them away from the influence of parents and other traditional authorities.[1] For scale, the total German population in 1936 was roughly 69 million.[1]

## Growth and compulsion

By December 1936 membership had reached over five million, and in that same month membership became mandatory for so-called Aryans under the Hitler Youth Law.[1] The legal obligation was reaffirmed in March 1939 with the Youth Service Duty, which conscripted all German youths into the organisation even over parental objection; parents who refused to allow their children to join were subject to investigation by the authorities.[1] From that point the vast majority of Germany's teenagers belonged to the Hitler Youth, and by 1940 it had eight million members.[1]

Even before membership was formally compulsory, German youth faced heavy social pressure to join. Students who held out were frequently assigned essays with titles such as "Why am I not in the Hitler Youth?", were taunted by teachers and fellow students, and could be refused their diploma — which made university admission impossible.[1] A number of employers refused apprenticeships to non-members, and by 1936 the organisation held a monopoly on all youth sports facilities in Germany, effectively locking non-members out of athletics.[1]

## Hitler's stated aims

[[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] spoke openly of the regime's ability to manufacture Nazis out of German youth, declaring in 1938 that boys and girls entered the organisations at ten years of age, passed four years in the Young Folk and four more in the Hitler Youth, were then "smoothed out" for a further six or seven months in the Labor Service, and finally had any remaining class consciousness or social status removed by the Wehrmacht.[1] The pipeline was designed so that no stage of adolescence escaped ideological formation.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands Media in November 2023, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used the Hitler Youth as the historical template for what he termed the [[obama-youth|Obama Youth]] — arguing that the mechanics of mockery, ostracism, exclusion from sports, schooling and employment map closely onto American Covid-19 vaccine mandates and onto pressure to accept transgender and LGBTQ+ narratives.[1] Carlson contended that "join the Hitler Youth" can be substituted with "take the jab" in many US cities, states and school districts, and to a lesser degree with "accept the Trans and LGBTQ+ narrative".[1] He argued that the shared purpose of both programmes was to steal a generation away from its parents and grandparents, because children are more easily molded than adults through propaganda, fear, and fear of being ostracised.[1]

Carlson further argued that the level of mind manipulation required to convince a generation that Jews needed to be exterminated is comparable to that required to produce a "woke" generation, and that in both cases many participants did not sincerely believe the doctrine but played along to survive or to remain comfortable.[1] He drew a distinction, however: in Nazi Germany non-compliance meant death, whereas American compliance was purchased without any threat of violence.[1]
