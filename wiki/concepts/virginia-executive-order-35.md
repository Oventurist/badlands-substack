# Virginia Executive Order 35

Executive Order 35 is a directive issued by Virginia Governor [[glenn-youngkin|Glenn Youngkin]] on August 7, 2024, requiring that all votes in the 2024 presidential election in Virginia be cast by paper ballot and codifying the election security protocols established during his governorship for all future elections.[1]

## Provisions

The most notable requirement of the order is the exclusive use of paper ballots with a traceable chain of custody, providing a more concrete record of votes cast than widely used electronic voting machines.[1] Other elements of the Virginia protocol include the use of Department of Motor Vehicles data to verify voter identity and the use of offline counting machines.[1] Youngkin summarized the model as: "We use 100% paper ballots with a strict chain of custody. We use counting machines, not voting machines, that are tested prior to every election and never connected to the internet. We do not mass mail ballots. We monitor our drop boxes 24/7."[1] Announcing the order, he said, "The Virginia model for Election Security works. This isn't a Democrat or Republican issue, it's an American and Virginian issue," adding that "every legal vote deserves to be counted without being watered down by illegal votes or inaccurate machines."[1]

## Reception

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the order "bold and a slap in the face to a regime that prioritizes election access over accuracy," while questioning the claim that Virginia's machines are never connected to the internet — a assertion made by many states over the years that has repeatedly proven untrue.[1] The commentary predicted that [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]] "and his flying monkeys" would be drafting lawsuits in response.[1]

The timing was described as significant: the order was issued exactly 90 days before the November 5 election, clearing a timeliness hurdle that had thwarted similar efforts in the past.[1] Because that 90-day threshold had now passed nationally, the brief observed, voters outside Virginia were locked into whatever election systems their jurisdictions already had.[1] Ashe in America suggested that if Virginia could conduct a free and fair election, the state could serve as a control and comparison against jurisdictions "that opt for shenanigans," and urged readers not to be demoralized: "Your vote is your voice. Don't be silenced."[1]
