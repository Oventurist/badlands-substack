# Murthy v. Missouri

Murthy v. Missouri is the United States Supreme Court case, decided in June 2024, concerning federal government coordination with social media companies over content moderation.[1] Badlands commentary characterized the decision as having "obliterated the First Amendment's right to free speech" by clearing the way for renewed government-platform contacts.[1]

## Aftermath and FBI censorship coordination

Following the ruling, according to a Department of Justice memo dated July 12, 2024 from Associate Deputy Attorney General George D. Turner, the Biden administration sought to censor information it deemed "foreign malign influence" (FMI).[1] The memo noted that after the Supreme Court's October 2023 stay of a Fifth Circuit injunction restricting [[fbi|FBI]] collaboration with Big Tech, the DOJ "began developing a standardized approach for sharing FMI information with social media companies that continued to appropriately account for First Amendment considerations."[1]

The FBI began using that standard operating procedure in "early February 2024" and has been "actively sharing FMI threat information with social media companies on a continuing basis," with the bureau set to resume regular meetings with the platforms to brief and discuss potential FMI threats ahead of the 2024 presidential election.[1]

## Origins and procedural history

The case originated in the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals in July 2023 and was fueled by allegations that federal agencies such as the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] were coercing platforms into removing content related to vaccine safety and the 2020 presidential election results.[2] Missouri's then-Attorney General [[eric-schmitt|Eric Schmitt]] filed the suit on the grounds that the Biden administration had violated First Amendment free-speech rights online in a bid to suppress politically conservative voices.[2] The Supreme Court was expected to decide whether federal agencies are permitted to remain in contact with social media firms about potential disinformation.[2]

## Resumption of agency–platform contacts (May 2024)

In May 2024, Senator [[mark-warner|Mark Warner]], chairman of the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]], told reporters at the RSA Conference that key federal agencies including CISA and the [[fbi|FBI]] had resumed discussions with social media companies about removing disinformation as the November presidential election approached.[2] The move marked a stark reversal after the Biden administration had for months frozen communications with social platforms while the case was pending.[2] According to Warner, contacts resumed roughly around the time that multiple justices appeared to favor the executive branch's position during oral argument.[2]
