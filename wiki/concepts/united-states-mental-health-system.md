# United States Mental Health System

The United States mental health system — the network of outpatient clinics, inpatient beds, involuntary-commitment law, and publicly funded programs serving Americans in psychiatric crisis — became a focus of Badlands commentary in September 2025 following the killing of Ukrainian refugee [[iryna-zarutska|Iryna Zarutska]] by [[decarlos-brown-jr|DeCarlos Brown Jr.]], a man his family described as severely schizophrenic.[1]

## The screening bottleneck

[[michelle-dewitt|Michelle Dewitt]], Brown's mother, described being turned away when she sought help for her son, recounting that clinics told her "We don't have enough space" or asked "Is he trying to kill himself or someone else?" — and that a negative answer ended the encounter with the instruction to obtain a court order.[1] Brown had been arrested in January 2025 for misuse of the 911 system, telling police that "man-made" materials inside him were controlling his body, and was released on a written promise to appear in court; his mother said he should never have been left free as his condition deteriorated.[1]

## Badlands critique

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the same screening question preceded the death of her brother-in-law, who was told after seeking help that the earliest appointment was three months away and who took his own life hours later.[1] She argued that the bureaucratic approach to mental health care for Americans in crisis is not merely inadequate but immoral, a violation of the Hippocratic oath and of basic human rights, and a standing threat to public safety — the country having the resources to help but lacking the will.[1]

She called for throwing out the current playbook, including defunding the NGOs she said absorb resources allocated to the problem, and using those funds to build state hospitals instead.[1] At the same time she maintained that Brown should receive the death penalty regardless of his delusions, noting his statement that "whoever was controlling the material lashed out at her" and asking at whom else such individuals would lash out.[1] Ashe also acknowledged competing theories that the killing was a skilled hit with a geopolitical motive — invoking MK Ultra and Jason Bourne-style speculation about implanted "materials" — but concluded that schizophrenia best fit the available evidence, including audio of Brown after his arrest.[1]
