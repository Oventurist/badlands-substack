# Smith-Mundt Modernization Act of 2012

The Smith-Mundt Modernization Act of 2012 amended the original [[smith-mundt-act|Smith-Mundt Act]] of 1948 so that materials produced by the [[state-department|State Department]] and the Broadcasting Board of Governors could be made available within the United States.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] describes the amendment as the moment when the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration made it legal once again to propagandise the American people.[1]

## Carlson's "speed limit" analogy

Carlson argues that between 1948 and 2012 domestic propaganda was like speeding: against the law, but unlikely to be punished unless practised blatantly — meaning the media generally altered the truth only a little.[1] In his reading, the original statute therefore kept the press's lies from becoming parody-like, which he considers not a good thing, because a restrained media appeared believable and retained public trust.[1] The 2012 amendment, he writes, was like removing all speed limits, allowing media to operate free of legal ramifications with only the loss of audience as a check.[1]

## Alleged cultural consequences

Carlson dates the take-off of "fake news" to 2012 and attributes to the post-amendment environment a rapid escalation of cultural change: political correctness intensifying into wokeness, Affirmative Action becoming DEI, LGB becoming LGBTQ+, transgender bathrooms becoming an overnight controversy, moderate conservatives being portrayed as right-wing extremists, and climate activism becoming disruptive.[1] He further argues that media and social media were used not only to push propaganda forward but to stamp out resistance to changing cultural values, with pre-2012 beliefs recast as toxic and free speech recast as a dirty term.[1]

Paradoxically, in his account the removal of the restraint destroyed the media's credibility: post-2012 output exceeded most Americans' tolerance for falsehood and caused the majority to reach a boiling point and abandon trust in the press entirely.[1]

## Restoration

Carlson notes that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] did not restore the 1948 restrictions in either term, and concludes that this was deliberate — propaganda remained useful for dismantling the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1] He predicts the original act will not be re-enacted until the mission is complete, and that its re-enactment will signal both that most Americans are awake and that "the game is over."[1]
