# Badlands Brief: African Acceleration, Powell's Replacement, & MEGA Democracy

This edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], published January 30, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], covers the nomination of a Justice Department "fraud czar," the House Republican [[mega-act|MEGA Act]] election overhaul, the rapid normalization of United States relations with [[venezuela|Venezuela]], the impending replacement of Federal Reserve Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], accelerating realignments in [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] and the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], and reported [[state-department|State Department]] contacts with the [[alberta-prosperity-project|Alberta Prosperity Project]]. Commentary is supplied by [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].

## Fraud czar nomination and the MEGA Act

The Brief reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced late on January 28, 2026 the nomination of [[colin-mcdonald|Colin McDonald]] to a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] role focused on combating fraud against taxpayers and federal programs, described as targeting large-scale misuse of federal funds and related financial crimes. Trump confirmed the nomination publicly through [[truth-social|Truth Social]], and the substance of the announcement was corroborated by national news outlets. The source notes that primary reporting did not describe the position as limited to election fraud, though election-related financial crimes may fall within its scope.

Trump's statement announced McDonald as "the first ever Assistant Attorney General for National FRAUD Enforcement, a new Division at the Department of Justice, which I created to catch and stop FRAUDSTERS that have been STEALING from the American People," citing fraud schemes uncovered "in States like Minnesota and California, where these thieves have stolen Hundreds of Billions of Taxpayer Dollars." He described McDonald as "a very Smart, Tough, and Highly Respected AMERICA FIRST Federal Prosecutor" and closed, "Together, we will END THE FRAUD, and RESTORE INTEGRITY to our Federal Programs. Congratulations Colin — STOP THE SCAMS!" The new body is treated in the corpus as the [[national-fraud-enforcement-division|National Fraud Enforcement Division]].

Separately, House Republicans unveiled the MEGA Act on January 29, 2026, proposing nationwide standards for federal elections including voter identification requirements, citizenship verification, changes to mail-in voting rules, and restrictions on ballot harvesting.

### Jon Herold: "Just not until 2027"

Jon Herold's commentary, headed "Make Elections Great Again... Just not until 2027," argues that the Make Elections Great Again Act "has a massive problem": the effective date for most key provisions is 2027, after the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|midterms]]. He quotes the operative language of four sections — photo ID for voting ("shall apply with respect to elections for Federal office held in 2027 or any succeeding year"), computerized statewide voter registration list requirements ("on and after January 1, 2027"), requirements for applicants who register by mail (same date), and the receipt, processing and counting of absentee and mail-in ballots (2027 or later). Coming immediately after "major news out of Fulton County relating to election fraud," Herold reads the timing as the [[uniparty|uniparty]] moving "to placate you ahead of the midterms with an election integrity solution using something that won't actually matter for the midterms."

### Burning Bright: Narrative becoming Actual

Burning Bright writes that "the 2020 Election Fraud narrative never died for some of us," and that 2026 brings "the first unmistakable merging of Narrative and Actual in a way that threatens to shatter long-held illusions on a mass scale." On the back of the [[fulton-county-election-raid|Fulton County raid]], he argues, "the deployment itself is the signal": after years of Narrative-only activity, "an Actual probe pierces the battlespace in the heart of one of the most contested counties in modern electoral history," followed by the "more enduring Actual" of the new Fraud Enforcement Division. Taken together, he argues, these are "chapters from the same playbook—a System of Systems deploying intelligence apparatus, both domestic and foreign to stage managed (i.e., 'smooth') transitions of power": "American Manchurian Candidates. [[color-revolution|Color revolutions]] at home." His closing line: "They don't fear what we've been calling for. They fear what some are only now daring to believe possible."

## Venezuelan re-normalization

Trump said Thursday, January 29, that the United States would reopen commercial airspace over Venezuela following the US military's capture of Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] on January 3. He instructed US officials, including the Transportation Secretary and military leaders, to enable the reopening, and said Americans would soon be able to travel to Venezuela. [[american-airlines|American Airlines]] announced plans to resume direct flights for the first time in over six years, pending government authority approval and security assessments; Chief Commercial Officer Nat Pieper said the carrier had "a more than 30-year history connecting Venezolanos to the US" and was "ready to renew that incredible relationship."

Following Maduro's capture the US government eased certain sanctions on Venezuela's oil sector with a general license authorizing US firms to purchase, sell, transport, store, market and refine Venezuelan-origin crude oil, while not covering new production or lifting sanctions on production activities. The same Thursday, Venezuela's Acting President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] signed an overhaul of the national hydrocarbons law opening the oil industry to foreign and private investment after passage by the National Assembly.

The normalization followed Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s testimony before the [[senate-foreign-relations-committee|Senate Foreign Relations Committee]] on Wednesday, in which he said Venezuela's new leaders were moving toward closer ties with Washington and that there was no immediate need for further US military action. "The authorities there deserve some credit," Rubio said. "They have passed a new hydrocarbon law that basically eradicates many of the Chavez era restrictions on private investment in the oil industry. It probably doesn't go far enough to attract sufficient investment, but it's a big step from where they were three weeks ago. So that's a major change."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry reads the sequence as Venezuela "being activated, as the decades-long persecution of [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chavez]] and Nicolas Maduro comes to a head." He argues that while Rodríguez "feigns contempt for Trump and the NeoCons in the GOP, she is still helping to accelerate capital investment in Venezuela," and that Trump's "arrest" of Maduro "allowed Rodriguez to put the government into a state of continuity, which I think may have fortified it against subsequent attempts to overthrow it," clearing the way for both leaders to focus on the economy so Venezuela "can join the [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]]."

## Trade deficit, markets, and the Fed succession

Speaking at the premiere of *Melania* on Thursday, Trump said he would announce a replacement for [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chair Jerome Powell on Friday morning; Powell's term as chair runs to May 15, 2026. The [[us-census-bureau|US Census Bureau]] and [[bureau-of-economic-analysis|Bureau of Economic Analysis]] released the international trade report the same day, showing the trade deficit increased 94.6 percent from October to November amid ongoing trade and tariff negotiations, with financial media noting the October 2025 deficit had been the lowest since 2009.

Oil prices rose more than 2 percent Thursday — with some reports citing gains above 3 percent — as markets reacted to reports that Trump was weighing possible military strikes on [[iran|Iran]]. US software stocks sold off sharply, [[servicenow|ServiceNow]] falling around 11 percent amid concerns that [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] could disrupt traditional software business models, while [[apple-inc|Apple Inc.]] reported strong first fiscal quarter 2026 results driven by rebounding iPhone sales in Greater [[china|China]].

Ashe in America reported that the shortlist for Fed chair had been vetted by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] and comprised four names: [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]], a Fed Governor from 2006 to 2011 and the widely regarded frontrunner, who reportedly met Trump at the [[white-house|White House]] Thursday; [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]], director of the [[national-economic-council|National Economic Council]] and an early favorite, though Trump indicated a preference to keep him in place; sitting Governor [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]], seen as providing continuity and appointed during Trump's first term; and [[rick-rieder|Rick Rieder]], [[blackrock|BlackRock]]'s Chief Investment Officer for Global Fixed Income, though some reports Thursday alleged Rieder had been told he would not receive the nomination. All the candidates support cutting interest rates, the major point of contention between Trump and Powell, whose policies Trump has claimed obstructed his economic strategy. Ashe's comment: "Just pick whichever chairman is going to end the Fed, and let's get on with the golden age."

## Africa: Sahel consolidation and the Congo

Burkina Faso acceded to the Vienna Convention on Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage, a legal step cited alongside reporting on expanding nuclear cooperation with [[russia|Russia]], while a Dubai-based industrial group reported development of a 200-MW thermal power plant in the country with an estimated €180 million investment. Burkina Faso's military government formally dissolved all political parties by decree on Thursday, ordering party assets transferred to the state and repealing laws on party status, financing, and the opposition leader's role. Former president [[paul-henri-sandaogo-damiba|Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba]] was arrested in [[togo|Togo]] and returned to Burkina Faso amid junta allegations of links to a coup and assassination plot.

In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, [[corneille-nangaa|Corneille Nangaa]], leader of the Rwanda-linked [[alliance-fleuve-congo|Alliance Fleuve Congo]], publicly denounced the US–DRC minerals partnership as unconstitutional, a claim the DRC presidency rejected. Agenzia Fides reported that former Congolese president [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]] was killed on January 25 in an explosion while meeting representatives of a guerrilla group, an explosion said to have killed around 40 people, with no official confirmation of his death.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry organizes the continent into three conflicts: "Putin is involved in Conflict A. Trump is involved in Conflict B. (The Arabs are running point in Conflict C.)" Conflict A is the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]], where he predicts "the 'muh democracy' folks are about to go apoplectic" over Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]]'s dissolution of political parties on grounds that they had become subversive — noting that Burkina Faso has over 100 parties of which only 15 hold parliamentary seats. He quotes the charges against Damiba: "criminal embezzlement of public funds, illicit enrichment, corruption, aggravated receiving of stolen goods and money laundering," per Togo's justice ministry. With Russia preparing a nuclear plant and the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] having just completed a thermal plant, he expects Burkina Faso to "become a case study to show how quickly a third world nation where only 20% of the population has electricity can be brought into the Golden Age at an accelerated rate."

Conflict B is the Congo, where Kabila stands accused of backing the [[m23-movement|M23]] militias that crossed from [[rwanda|Rwanda]] and seized mining towns. Kabila was charged with treason in 2025, and the prosecutor's request for the death penalty was granted upon his in absentia conviction for the failed coup. The commentary recalls Kabila as the president in office "when [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] bribed his way into a relationship," and reports rumors that Kabila was killed on January 25 by a drone strike while meeting M23 rebels in Uvira. It notes that [[congress|Congress]] was discussing sanctions against Rwanda — "which has long been an ally of [[israel|Israel]] and a faithful purveyor of the Zionist lifestyle" — and that the M23 leader was publicly accusing Trump and President [[felix-tshisekedi|Tshisekedi]] of violating the law. Adding that China planned to move 100,000 tons of copper to the USA, he concludes: "The [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] lives, and that doesn't bode well for globalist wannabes."

## Alberta and the State Department

Citing the *Financial Times*, the Brief reports that State Department officials met with members of the [[alberta-prosperity-project|Alberta Prosperity Project]] three times since April 2025 in Washington, with [[jeffrey-rath|Jeffrey Rath]], APP co-founder and legal counsel, attending. "The US is extremely enthusiastic about a free and independent [[alberta|Alberta]]," Rath said. A fourth delegation was planned for the following month. The APP is seeking a $500 billion line of credit from the US government to support Alberta in the event of a successful independence referendum; none has been called. A State Department spokesperson said, "The department regularly meets with civil society types. As is typical in routine meetings such as these, no commitments were made." Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] said he expects the administration to respect Canadian sovereignty and that Trump has never raised Alberta separatism with him.

Ashe in America welcomed the development, writing that it is "not surprising that the most productive part of [[canada|Canada]] wants to leave the globalist proxy state and join the greatest nation in the history of earth," and that although she is "generally opposed to foreign aid," Canadian liberation is "a worthy cause–for both us and them." She argued that "having the global regime on the US northern border is ostensibly our greatest national security threat," noted that Alberta provides 80 percent of Canada's oil and 60 percent of its natural gas, suggested other provinces may follow, and concluded that "President Trump should call [[king-charles-iii|King Charles]] and make a deal."

## Bonus item: Senate funding vote fails

The Brief closes with a bonus item reporting that the US [[senate|Senate]] failed to advance a House-passed government funding bill after a procedural vote fell short of the 60 votes required, increasing the risk of a partial government shutdown. The measure would have funded most federal agencies, including the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], but Senate Democrats opposed moving forward without changes related to immigration enforcement policy. Following the failed vote, Senate leaders discussed separating DHS funding from the broader spending package and pursuing a short-term continuing resolution while negotiations continue. The [[white-house|White House]] and congressional leaders stated that talks were ongoing, and that any revised measure would still require approval by both chambers of Congress to avert a shutdown.
