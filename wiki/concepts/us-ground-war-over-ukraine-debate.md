# Debate over a US ground war over Ukraine

The debate over a US ground war over Ukraine refers to the question, raised in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary in October 2022, of whether the American public had ever been asked to consent to sending US troops into direct combat with the [[russia|Russian Federation]] in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1]

## Origin

The immediate trigger was a [[cbs-news|CBS News]] report, circulated on Truth Social by [[jack-posobiec|Jack Posobiec]], that the [[white-house|Biden White House]] had positioned the [[101st-airborne-division|101st Airborne Division]] on the border of the territories newly annexed by Russia in Ukraine.[1] Columnist [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] argued that deploying one of America's most combat-ready units to [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s doorstep amounted to a signal to Moscow that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would commit ground troops, and constituted a dramatic escalation between the US/[[nato|NATO]] bloc and Russia.[1]

## The consent argument

Cates framed the issue as one of democratic consent. He noted that Biden had refused to set any limit on the billions of taxpayer dollars or the weaponry sent to Ukraine, and was now preparing to send "another precious resource" — America's sons and daughters.[1] He said he could not recall Biden ever delivering a major address to the country making the case for Americans fighting and dying in Eastern Europe, and asked what signs the public had given that it found any such argument compelling.[1]

A related complaint concerned polling. Cates asked what public polling had been conducted on the proposition and argued that, if the case had been made, every polling organization in the country should be measuring the public's position; their apparent silence, he suggested, meant military families should speak out without waiting to be surveyed.[1]

## Risk and competence arguments

Cates distinguished the prospective conflict from the earlier invasions of [[iraq|Iraq]] and [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]], into which he said the public had been talked and which it had grudgingly accepted as decades-long occupations.[1] Ukraine, by contrast, was not a "Middle Eastern backwater" but a theater in which US forces would enter a direct shooting war with a Russian military already blooded in Eastern Europe — a scenario he warned "could very easily spiral into a nuclear exchange."[1]

He also questioned the competence of those who would prosecute such a war, citing the handling of the Afghanistan withdrawal and characterizing senior military leadership as having spent recent years more enamored of virtue-signalling about being "woke" than of preparing soldiers, sailors and aircrews for a hot war with a major power.[1]

## Conclusion

Cates urged all Americans to raise their voices immediately, "before it's too late," and produced a meme intended to help the White House initiate the national conversation he said was owed to the country before any further step in Eastern Europe.[1]
