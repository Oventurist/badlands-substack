# 2023 Louisiana gubernatorial election

The 2023 [[louisiana|Louisiana]] gubernatorial election was held on Saturday, 14 October 2023, and resulted in Republicans flipping the state's governor's mansion for the first time since 2016.[1]

## Result

Louisiana Attorney General [[jeff-landry|Jeff Landry]] was projected by the Associated Press to win the race outright.[1] Landry competed alongside more than a dozen candidates in the state's jungle primary, a system in which all candidates appear on the same ballot regardless of party; had no candidate exceeded 50 percent, the contest would have advanced to a November runoff between the top two vote-getters.[1] Because Landry had won more than half the vote as of late Saturday night — reported at roughly 51 percent — he was projected the outright winner, stunning pundits who had widely expected a second round against Democrat Shawn Wilson.[1]

Landry, described as a hard-right Republican with the backing of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], had been viewed as the favorite going into the contest.[1] His strong showing was read as giving the GOP, and particularly the party's conservative populist wing, momentum ahead of November gubernatorial races in the red states of Kentucky and Mississippi.[1] He will succeed term-limited Governor [[john-bel-edwards|John Bel Edwards]].[1]

## Ballot measure on private election funding

In the same election, a Louisiana ballot measure prohibiting the use of private funds in the administration of elections passed with overwhelming support at 73 percent.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the outcome as ending eight years of divided government — a Republican legislature checked by a veto-heavy Democratic governor — and enabling Republicans to deliver the conservative outcomes their voters sought.[1] The commentary also noted Landry's role in [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri et al v. Biden]], the multi-state First Amendment lawsuit over federal collusion with technology companies to censor protected speech, whose favorable appellate developments in early October gave Landry, in that framing, a second reason to celebrate.[1]
