# Hormuz Transit Fee Proposal

The Hormuz transit fee proposal — described in the Badlands Brief as a "toll scheme" — was a plan floated by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to impose a 20% U.S. "reimbursement fee" on commercial transit through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], on the rationale that American naval power was underwriting the security of the waterway.[1] In July 2026 Trump reversed the plan in favor of trade and investment deals with Gulf states, a turn the Brief filed under its recurring "[[taco-tuesday|TACO Tuesday]]" framing of Trump reversals.[1]

## Reversal and the al-Zaidi meeting

The unwinding of the toll plan occurred during a [[white-house|White House]] meeting between Trump and the recently elected Iraqi Prime Minister [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali al-Zaidi]].[1] Brief contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the symbolism: Trump praised al-Zaidi as a young, capable leader, while al-Zaidi described himself as a businessman with a master's degree in finance who was new to politics — in the contributor's phrasing, an Arab version of Trump.[1] Trump used the meeting to announce free trade in the Persian Gulf, converting a proposed levy into an investment opportunity through what the contributor called geopolitical jiu-jitsu.[1]

## Iranian response and shipping disruption

[[iran|Iran]]'s deputy foreign minister rejected the notion that military pressure would force Tehran into negotiations, while the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] warned that U.S. aggression would only delay the strait's reopening.[1] Shipping traffic through the waterway fell sharply, dropping to just six vessels on one recent day — the lowest count in five weeks — raising alarms over freedom of navigation.[1] The disruption heightened risks to global energy supplies and to seafarer safety while fueling concerns about wider regional escalation.[1]

The [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] reserved the right to respond and [[india|India]] summoned Iran's ambassador, with analysts noting the potential for prolonged disruption if tit-for-tat exchanges continued.[1] The episode underscored the strategic stakes of control over Hormuz and the difficulty of containing the conflict without broader economic and security fallout.[1]
