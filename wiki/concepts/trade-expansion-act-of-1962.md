# Trade Expansion Act of 1962

The Trade Expansion Act of 1962 is a United States trade statute that significantly expanded executive authority over tariffs beyond the delegation begun with the [[reciprocal-trade-agreements-act-of-1934|Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act of 1934]].[1]

## Provisions

The Act authorized President John F. Kennedy to negotiate across-the-board tariff cuts rather than only bilateral, product-by-product reductions.[1] It also created Section 232, which allows presidents to adjust imports on national security grounds — a power that presidents of both parties have since invoked to impose tariffs unilaterally.[1]

## Relevance to the Trump tariff litigation

The statute figures in the historical argument, advanced by Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], that decades of congressional delegation make President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariff program lawful — an argument tested when the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] agreed in September 2025 to hear the administration's expedited tariff appeal.[1]
