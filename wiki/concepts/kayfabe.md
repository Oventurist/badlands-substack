# Kayfabe

Kayfabe is a professional-wrestling term for staged conflict presented to audiences as genuine. In Badlands analysis it describes deliberate public feuds staged by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his allies to manipulate media attention — the phenomenon the community also labels "Trump-a-Mania."[1]

## Application to the Trump–Bannon split

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified the August 2017 "break-up" between Trump and [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] as possibly the first real instance of kayfabe, arguing it was implausible that two men who had worked together to defeat the Clintons and the Bushes would abruptly develop bad blood. Trump's later "Sloppy Steve" taunts were, on this reading, part of the performance.[1]

The assumed purpose was narrative shielding: taking a press corps then more fixated on Bannon than on Trump himself off his back so he could build a media operation capable of challenging the mainstream. The Brief additionally speculated that the same shielding may have let Bannon ingratiate himself with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], whose later contacts with Bannon the mainstream press and ConInc now cite as proof that Bannon is a black hat.[1]

A related example offered from Trump's pre-political career is his use of Omarosa Manigault Newman on *The Apprentice* as a deployable agent of confusion among contestants, with Trump debriefing her afterward in what resembled intelligence gathering — a test of how people handle pressure as much as of competence.[1]


## Trump and Bukele (2025)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] applied the kayfabe framing in January 2025 when El Salvador's [[nayib-bukele|Nayib Bukele]] was invited to [[second-inauguration-of-donald-trump|Donald Trump's second inauguration]], writing that Trump's earlier Truth Social posts blaming Bukele for sending MS-13 gang members to the United States "really were kayfabe after all."[2]

## Gaetz versus McCarthy (2023)

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the October 2023 confrontation between Representative [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] and Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] as "perhaps the most obvious bit of Kayfabe at work in the Info War," arguing that both men were [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s choices — one for Speaker and the other for the opposition to that Speaker.[3] On this reading the [[motion-to-vacate|motion to vacate]] drama was less a genuine power struggle than a staged conflict whose function was exposure, consistent with his broader claim that "the 'Biden Admin' is all about Exposure, and it's going swimmingly."[3]

## Prigozhin and the Russian Ministry of Defence (May 2023)

In May 2023, Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] applied the kayfabe frame to the publicized feud between [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]]'s [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] and the [[russian-ministry-of-defense|Russian Ministry of Defence]], writing that Russia-aligned citizen journalists regarded the quarrel as obvious kayfabe because Prigozhin "would be in a gulag if his continued (and highly publicized) tirades were not directly approved by the Kremlin."[4]

## Trump–DeSantis feud (May 2023)

The same commentary treated the escalating [[donald-trump|Trump]]–[[ron-desantis|DeSantis]] feud as a question whose authenticity did not matter for practical purposes, arguing that Trump remained the past, present and immediate future of the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]] and that anyone attempting to move supporters off the Trump train was seeking to divide rather than unify.[4]

## 2024 Republican National Convention and the CrowdStrike outage

Kayfabe became a recurring frame in Badlands Media commentary in July 2024. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that on that Friday's Liberty Den the team had discussed "the layers of kayfabe within our reality," adding that she was "significantly more comfortable with the concept" after [[hulk-hogan|Hulk Hogan]] tore his shirt off at the [[2024-republican-national-convention|Republican National Convention]].[5] Hogan's performance was itself a demonstration of the technique: he flexed and revealed a "Trump/Vance 2024" tank top in character, then stepped out of it to speak as Terry Bollea about his friendship with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], before returning to character to coin "[[trumpamania|Trumpmania]]."[5]

Applying the concept to the [[2024-crowdstrike-global-outage|CrowdStrike global outage]], Ashe noted that kayfabe is "intended to entertain and elicit certain reactions from the audience" and judged the outage's effect on public awareness of smart devices and networked voting equipment to be "brilliant."[5]
