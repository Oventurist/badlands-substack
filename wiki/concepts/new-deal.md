# New Deal

The New Deal was the program of banking reform, credit issuance and public works enacted under President [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] beginning in 1933, following the exposures of the [[pecora-commission|Pecora Commission]].[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] frames it as the successful precedent for breaking financial oligarchy at a moment of economic breakdown, fascism and looming world war.[1]

## First hundred days

In his first hundred days Roosevelt was able to impose [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall]] banking separation, forcing Wall Street banks to break up their functions and preventing speculators from gambling with productive assets.[1] He created the [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation]] to protect citizens' savings from future crises, and the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] to provide oversight of Wall Street, appointing [[ferdinand-pecora|Ferdinand Pecora]] as a commissioner in 1934.[1]

The [[reconstruction-finance-corporation|Reconstruction Finance Corporation]] was unleashed as a national bank that bypassed the private [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], channeling $33 billion into the real economy by 1945 — more than all private commercial banks combined.[1] Protective tariffs were imposed on agriculture, metals and industrial goods to stop dumping of cheap products in America and rebuild the physical economy.[1]

## Public works

The New Deal created vast public works, including the [[tennessee-valley-authority|Tennessee Valley Authority]], the Grand Coulee and Hoover dams, the St. Lawrence development, and countless hospitals, schools, bridges, roads and rail projects.[1] Ehret compares this program's role to that of China's Belt and Road Initiative in the modern age.[1] He notes that Roosevelt died before this new form of political economy could be internationalized abroad in the post-war years as an anti-colonial program.[1]
