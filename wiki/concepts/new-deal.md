# New Deal

The New Deal was the program of economic reform, public works and financial regulation undertaken in the United States during the 1930s under President [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]]. In the Badlands Media essay [[a-re-alignment-of-world-systems|A Re-Alignment of World Systems]], it serves as the positive counter-model against which the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] is measured.

## Role in the source

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes "the protectionism, bank regulation, state credit and central authority exerted by America during the 1930s New Deal (or Eurasian New Silk Road policy today)" as fundamentally different from the green framework: the New Deal model "empowers sovereign nations, and increases the productive powers of labor and energy flux density of humanity while increasing quality of life," whereas the green agenda ties monetary incentives to reducing the earth's carbon footprint.

The article also credits the American rejection of fascism under Roosevelt with defeating an earlier attempt to convert economic collapse into dictatorship, referencing Ehret's earlier piece "How to Crush a Bankers' Dictatorship: How the Great Reset of 1933 Was Thwarted by FDR." The [[glass-steagall-act|Glass-Steagall Act]] of 1933, described as "the bedrock of financial regulation," belongs to this same period.
