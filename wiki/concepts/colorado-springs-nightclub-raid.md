# Colorado Springs Nightclub Raid

The Colorado Springs nightclub raid was an anti-narcotics and immigration enforcement operation carried out in the early hours of Sunday, April 27, 2025, at an "underground" nightclub in Colorado's second largest city.[1] Federal law enforcement officials said they detained more than 100 people unlawfully living in the country, and the [[drug-enforcement-administration|Drug Enforcement Administration]] reported seizing cocaine and firearms during the operation.[1]

## Gang and organized crime allegations

U.S. Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] linked the raid to organized crime in a post on X, stating that the club was frequented by members of the Venezuelan prison gang [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] and the international gang [[ms-13|MS-13]].[1]

The raid was the second major nightclub operation in Colorado in 2025. In January, the DEA's Rocky Mountain Field Division executed an early-morning search warrant at a "makeshift nightclub" in Adams County, detaining 49 individuals, of whom 41 were said to be unlawfully residing in the country.[1]

## Sanctuary policy debate

Supporters of stricter enforcement pointed to the raid as confirmation that criminal elements had embedded themselves among the tens of thousands of immigrants who arrived in Colorado over the preceding two years, arguing that local "sanctuary policies" were the attracting factor.[1]

## Badlands account

Badlands contributor Ashe in America reported that Colorado residents awoke to a coordinated media deployment of photos and video from local FBI, DEA, and ICE channels, alongside national posts from Bondi and border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]].[1] She noted that the [[united-states-postal-service|US Postal Inspector]] and the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] were also on scene.[1]

According to sources with knowledge of the investigation cited in the brief, the raid had been planned for months in anticipation of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s return to office and was put into motion on Inauguration Day.[1] The area was described as a criminal stronghold where police standard operating procedure called for three to four units on even routine calls due to the threat level, making the operation both ambitious and dangerous.[1] The commentary suggested that more information was expected on those detained, with indications that the roll-up extended beyond unlawfully present partygoers.[1]
