# Holodomor

The Holodomor — Ukrainian for "killing by starvation" — was a mass starvation event in Ukraine in 1932–1933 that led to the deaths of millions of people.[1] Estimates of the death toll vary widely: some researchers put the figure at three million, others at five million, with precise accounting made difficult by a lack of records and the suppression of information at the time.[1]

## Conditions during the famine

Badlands' account describes Ukraine in the 1930s as "a very grim place," noting that tens of thousands of people resorted to cannibalism to survive, and likely to murder as well.[1] Thousands were formally prosecuted when caught in the act, though the number of actual practitioners was presumed to be far higher.[1] The commentary argues that it was disproportionately "the decent people who perished" — those who refused to resort to crime and cannibalism to stay alive.[1] Contemporary maps circulated in the article show the percentage of the population that perished across affected regions between 1929 and 1933.[1]

## Attribution debate

Badlands states that while there is consensus that the famine was manmade, evidence is lacking that Joseph Stalin specifically ordered the forced starvation of any particular region of the USSR — allowing for the possibility of bad luck and poor management as contributing causes.[1] This is a contested framing; mainstream and Ukrainian state historiography generally treats the Holodomor as a deliberate genocide.

## Political afterlife

[[stepan-bandera]] and the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] used the crisis to fuel their propaganda for Ukrainian independence, and when the Nazis arrived a few years later Bandera was eager to work with them against Poles and Russians.[1] Although Galicia — Bandera's home region — was largely spared by the famine, the Badlands account argues that the conditions across [[ukraine]] supplied Bandera with "all the ruthless men he would ever need picking over the corpse of Ukraine."[1]
