# Shadow Cabal

**Shadow cabal** is the term used in Badlands coverage for the cross-institutional network of activists, election officials, labor groups, lawyers, and private-sector organisations described in a February 2021 *Time* magazine article as having "fortified" the 2020 US presidential election — and, in the outlet's reading, for the analogous coordination being assembled ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]].[1]

## The 2021 Time article

The *Time* piece, published on February 4, 2021, described "a conspiracy unfolding behind the scenes, one that both curtailed the protests and coordinated the resistance from CEOs."[1] It stated that the scenario the shadow campaigners feared was not a Trump victory but an election so calamitous that no result could be discerned at all.[1] According to the article, their work touched every aspect of the election: they got states to change voting systems and laws, helped secure hundreds of millions in public and private funding, fended off voter-suppression lawsuits, recruited armies of poll workers, got millions to vote by mail for the first time, pressured social media companies to take a harder line against disinformation, used data-driven strategies to fight viral smears, ran national public-awareness campaigns about how the count would unfold over days or weeks, and after Election Day monitored every pressure point to ensure [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] could not overturn the result.[1]

The article quoted [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]], a prominent lawyer and former Obama administration official who recruited Republicans and Democrats to the board of the Voter Protection Program, saying the untold story of the election was "the thousands of people of both parties who accomplished the triumph of American democracy at its very foundation."[1]

## The 2026 sequel

Politico reported in June 2026 that Senate Democrats were conducting private strategy sessions and scenario-planning exercises ahead of the midterms, focused on potential election disruptions, foreign interference, federal law-enforcement activity at polling places, and post-election legal disputes.[1] Democratic lawmakers, election officials, and outside advisers were developing litigation, communications, and response plans for a range of hypothetical scenarios, and Democrats complained they were receiving limited election-security briefings from the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1]

[[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] convened a slate of experts including former Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]], Democratic election attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], election-law expert Norm Eisen, and the heads of the left-leaning legal group [[democracy-forward|Democracy Forward]] and the nonpartisan nonprofit [[protect-democracy|Protect Democracy]]; the group helped senators prepare for three possible scenarios and boost coordination with state and local officials who oversee election administration and turnout operations.[1] Politico framed the planning as defensive preparation for possible election-related conflicts, while critics drew the comparison to the 2020 "fortification."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] asked whether the effort was "a remake or a rerun," noting that the current and 2020 efforts are "both the brain-children of Norm Eisen and his minions."[1] She observed that Marc Elias was "strategizing election interference instead of preparing for his inevitable criminal trial," and remarked that it was at least encouraging the plans were being announced in advance this time, whereas in 2020 the participants waited three months before publicising their work.[1] She closed by hoping it was a remake rather than a rerun, "and that they change the ending."[1]
