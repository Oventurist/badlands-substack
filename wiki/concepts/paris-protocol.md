# Paris Protocol

The Paris Protocol — formally the Protocol on Economic Relations — is the 1994 agreement signed by the State of Israel and the [[palestine-liberation-organization|Palestine Liberation Organization]] as part of the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]], alongside the Gaza-Jericho Agreement. Together these instruments created the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] (PA) and granted it strictly limited powers of governance, while leaving Israel in complete control of taxation in Palestine.[1]

The arrangement was designed to be temporary, intended to last only five years until the Two-State Solution envisaged by the Oslo Accords was fully implemented. The system nonetheless remains in place decades later.[1]

## The tax clearance system

Under the tax clearance system established by the Protocol, Israel collects taxes on behalf of the PA. These transfers are the PA's largest source of income, accounting for roughly 70–75 percent of total revenue; in 2014–2015 the reported figure was about $160 million per month.[1]

Three categories of tax are collected by Israel on Palestine's behalf: tariffs, value added tax (VAT), and income tax, the last of which also includes insurance fees automatically deducted from Palestinian paychecks.[1]

All Palestinian trade with foreign countries is conducted through Israeli ports under Israeli supervision, and the Protocol gives Israel unilateral authority to establish new tariffs and raise rates. The Protocol also established VAT on goods and services in both Israel and Palestine; while Israel collects VAT only on goods and services sold in Israel, it holds unilateral authority to adjust the rate, and the PA is required to match any adjustment.[1]

On income tax, Section 194 of the Israeli Income Tax Ordinance grants tax collectors broad leeway and discretion to assess what a taxpayer may owe, with almost no recourse to challenge the assessment. Israeli citizens are almost never jailed for non-payment, whereas Palestinians are routinely subjected to enforcement and imprisonment for debt.[1]

## Withholding as leverage

Because clearance revenues dominate PA finances, withholding them functions as economic leverage. In May 2024 the PA announced it could pay only 50 percent of March public sector salaries after Israel withheld the April transfer, with Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] reported to be holding back NIS 170 million ($46 million) for nine days in protest at PA efforts against the State of Israel — an apparent reference to Ramallah's push for the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] to issue arrest warrants for senior Israeli officials.[1]

## Resistance: Beit Sahour

The most cited act of resistance to the system predates the Protocol itself: in 1989 the affluent Christian-Palestinian town of Beit Sahour, outside Bethlehem, organized a non-violent boycott of Israeli goods under the slogan "No taxation under Occupation," consciously invoking the American Founding Fathers. The [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] responded with a 42-day curfew, blocked food imports, cut telephone lines, impounded vehicles, and seized over $1.5 million in cash and property from the town's 300 wealthiest families; the town's 40 top leaders were arrested and given multi-year sentences, and schools, medical services and food supply chains were shut down. The movement was crushed within nine months.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by GhostofBasedPatrickHenry used this history to argue that American tax dollars underwrite the arrangement, concluding, "We have become the Great Britain of 1776."[1]
