# Michigan Fake Electors Case

The Michigan "fake electors" case was a state criminal prosecution brought in 2023 against a slate of Republican electors who signed documents asserting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had won Michigan's electoral votes in the 2020 presidential election, which he lost in the state to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

## Charges

Sixteen people were initially charged with eight felonies each, relating to forgery and conspiracy, by Michigan's Democratic Attorney General [[dana-nessel|Dana Nessel]].[1] One defendant had his charges dropped after agreeing to cooperate with the prosecution, leaving fifteen defendants.[1]

## Dismissal

On September 9, 2025, District Court Judge [[kristen-simmons|Kristen Simmons]] dismissed the felony charges, ruling that the state had not produced "evidence sufficient to prove intent," a requirement for fraud cases.[1] Simmons emphasized that the case did not concern the intent of those who orchestrated the alternate-elector scheme — such as Trump attorney [[kenneth-chesebro|Kenneth Chesebro]] — but rather the intent of the individuals who actually signed the documents.[1] "I believe they were executing their constitutional right to seek redress," she said of the signatories.[1] With the dismissal, the Michigan electors will not go to trial.[1]

## Reception

Mainstream coverage characterized the ruling as a further blow to efforts to hold Trump and his allies legally accountable for attempting to overturn the 2020 result.[1] Badlands Media host [[canncon|CannCon]] called the dismissal a "HUGE Victory in the Lawfare War," while cautioning that in [[lawfare|lawfare]] the process itself is the punishment: the defense had already cost the electors millions of dollars in legal fees.[1] In related commentary, [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] noted that [[executive-order-13848|Executive Order 13848]], the national emergency concerning foreign interference in United States elections, had been extended for another year.[1]

## Search warrants on Google and X (2024)

In April 2024, CNN reported that [[google|Google]] and [[x|X]], formerly Twitter, had recently provided hundreds of files to Michigan prosecutors for the state's 2020 election subversion probe, complying with search warrants obtained after CNN revealed secret social media accounts belonging to pro-Trump lawyer [[kenneth-chesebro|Kenneth Chesebro]], who played a major role in the fake electors plot.[2] The previously unreported warrants gave prosecutors access to new Chesebro emails and his private direct messages on Twitter, and made clear that Attorney General [[dana-nessel|Dana Nessel]] was still gathering new information nine months after charging the state's fake electors with forgery and other crimes for signing certificates falsely claiming [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won Michigan in 2020.[2] A top member of Nessel's team testified that the investigation remained ongoing and that Trump is an unindicted co-conspirator in the case, which was not expected to go to trial before the November election.[2] According to the new documents, Chesebro fruitlessly tried to bring several controversial pro-Trump figures to Washington, DC, to watch his "fake electors" strategy unfold on January 6, 2021.[2] Michigan was among a number of states investigating fake elector schemes, with Arizona prosecutors filing criminal charges against pro-Trump electors and allies there in the same month.[2]
