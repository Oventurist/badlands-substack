# 2009 Moldovan Twitter Revolution

The 2009 "Twitter Revolution" — sometimes called the "revolution of Twits" — was a near color revolution in [[moldova|Moldova]] in which some 30,000 youths in Chisinau attempted to overthrow the government by declaring the recent elections fraudulent.[1] Slogans shouted by the crowds included "we want Europe", "we are Romanians" and "down with Communism".[1]

Beginning on April 7, 2009, mobs took over and set fire to government buildings and replaced Moldovan flags with Romanian and European Union flags.[1] The effort was quashed by police crackdowns, but a taste of the potential for regime change was felt by all, and then-President Vladimir Voronin called it a "coup d'état".[1] Romania had joined [[nato|NATO]] in 2004, and the movement to absorb Moldova into Romania was supported by many in the West wishing to see NATO's unabated growth.[1]

In hindsight, the article argues, the growth of the media empire under the control of [[vlad-plahotniuc|Vlad Plahotniuc]] and the vast investments in "democracy building" by the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] indicate the causal hand behind these events, though at the time the culprits never came to light.[1] U.S. Ambassador [[asif-chaudhry|Asif Chaudhry]] acted as a major power broker throughout the crisis.[1] In its wake, three pro-Western opposition parties created the Alliance for European Integration, winning 53 seats and pushing the Communist Party out of power.[1]
