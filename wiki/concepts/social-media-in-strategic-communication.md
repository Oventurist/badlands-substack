# Social Media in Strategic Communication

Social Media in Strategic Communication (SMISC) was a program launched in 2011 by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency ([[darpa|DARPA]]) to build automated tools for analyzing and shaping social media discourse.[1]

## Purpose and origins

DARPA said the SMISC tools were used "to help identify misinformation or deception campaigns and counter them with truthful information," beginning with the Arab Spring uprisings in the Middle East that spawned [[isis|ISIS]] over a decade earlier.[1]

## Domestic adaptation

By January 2023, the U.S. government's campaign against "misinformation" had expanded to adapt military-grade artificial intelligence once used to silence the Islamic State in order to quickly identify and censor American dissent on issues such as vaccine safety and election integrity, according to grant documents and cyber experts.[1] The [[national-science-foundation|National Science Foundation]] had recently awarded several million dollars in grants to universities and private firms to develop tools eerily similar to the original SMISC toolset.[1]

## Commentary

[[sean-morgan|Sean Morgan]] described the development as the Biden administration weaponizing government against patriots and as an application of artificial intelligence in [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], with weapons once pointed at foreign enemies like ISIS "swiveled around at our own citizens."[1] He called for rapid engagement in the technological arms race around AI and convergent technologies to establish safeguards, and argued that the reporting was a smoking gun showing government infiltration by traitors, noting that the two issues the deep state most feared were vaccine safety and election integrity.[1]
