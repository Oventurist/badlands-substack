# Voter Roll–Immigration Data Sharing

Voter roll–immigration data sharing refers to a prospective formal agreement between the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] permitting federal authorities to use state voter registration data in immigration and criminal investigations.[1] Reporting on the negotiations, attributed to sources familiar with the discussions, appeared in [[cbs|CBS News]] in March 2026.[1]

## Mechanics of the reported plan

Under the reported plan, the DOJ's Civil Rights Division would provide voter roll data collected from the states to Homeland Security Investigations, the investigative arm of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]], with the objective of identifying non-citizens registered to vote or who may have voted unlawfully in past elections.[1] The formal request for access was expected to be submitted by [[todd-lyons|Todd Lyons]], the senior official performing the duties of ICE director.[1]

Officials were additionally considering building a system enabling federal investigators to cross-reference voter registration records against DHS immigration databases through direct query-based matching between the two systems.[1]

## Official position

A DOJ spokesperson said the department is "devoting significant resources to ensure that elections are free, fair, and transparent," including litigation to ensure voter roll maintenance and "a clear focus on ensuring that American elections are decided solely by American citizens."[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the reporting as effectively confirmed by the DOJ statement and dismissed privacy objections, writing that "privacy is a myth" and that the premise that the federal government cannot detect election, entitlement, or immigration fraud from its own datasets "can only be held by those intent on committing frauds."[1] She further observed that state governments objecting to the arrangement had raised no such concerns when previously sharing data with federal partners.[1]
