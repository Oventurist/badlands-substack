# Sahel destabilization allegations

The Sahel destabilization allegations are claims, publicised in August 2024 by African media and amplified in Badlands coverage, that French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] jointly intended to destabilise the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] (AES), comprising [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], [[mali|Mali]] and [[niger|Niger]].[1]

## The allegations

Reports circulating on social media in West Africa claimed that the Ukrainian embassy in Côte d'Ivoire was financing and supplying weapons to terrorists in the north of that country in order to attack neighbouring Burkina Faso.[1] Xavier Messe a Tiati, director general of the Cameroon News Agency, stated that Macron, with the help of Zelensky, intended to destabilise the AES.[1]

"We turned to our sources in the AES countries, particularly in Burkina Faso," Tiati said. "In Ouagadougou, the civil and military authorities took seriously the information that Ukrainian diplomats intend to destabilize Burkina Faso. Ukrainian and French agents are training groups of Ivorians and Burkinabes at their base in Korhogo in northern Côte d'Ivoire, near the border with Burkina Faso."[1]

He added that the intent was "to carry out actions to stir up the population [in Burkina Faso] and provoke a response from the regular forces, who will be forced to restore order through repressive measures," so that if order were restored harshly, personnel at the French military base in Bouaké would have a pretext to intervene in Burkina Faso to protect civilian lives.[1] According to Tiati, Macron and Zelensky wanted to defeat the AES in order to make it more difficult for [[russia|Russia]] to act in Africa.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the "big picture on 'infinite funding for Ukraine'" was coming into focus.[1] He recalled that roughly a month earlier a [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] unit of about fifty men had reportedly been ambushed in Mali by an [[isis|ISIS]] cell composed of Tuareg militants — the Tuareg being a subset of the Berber peoples of the Atlas Mountains of northwest Africa — and pointed to maps showing the overlap of Wagner Group activity and Africa-based ISIS cells.[1]

A video subsequently went viral in which the Ukrainian ambassador praised ISIS for slaughtering the Wagner unit; the government of Senegal summoned the ambassador to answer for the comments, Mali severed ties with Ukraine entirely, and Ukraine explicitly denied any involvement while Western media ran cover.[1] Almost a year earlier to the day, Niger had expelled the French ambassador.[1]

The author suggested that Ukraine might be being used to launder U.S. tax dollars to ISIS and other proxy militias operating around the world — reckless from an optics standpoint, but hard to dismiss — noting that Macron and Zelensky were now jointly blamed for financing weapons to militants attacking Wagner and African national militaries.[1] He speculated that after Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman removed Al-Waleed bin Talal and other cousins who had financed ISIS, such funding came almost directly from the U.S. taxpayer via Ukraine and [[nato|NATO]], and cited a 2019 *Independent* article titled "How Ukraine became the unlikely home for Isis leaders escaping the caliphate."[1]
