# Martial Law

**Martial law** is the substitution of military authority for ordinary civil governance. Within the interpretive literature of the [[qanon|Q]] movement, the concept appears chiefly as a legal framework governing when the United States military may lawfully operate among the American population.[1]

## Treatment in Q for Dummies

Chapter 3 of [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] examines drop #22 of November 1, 2017, which the author characterises as one of Q's periodic "history lessons."[1] The drop asks who controls the [[national-guard|National Guard]], whether the Guard can work alongside the [[united-states-marine-corps|Marines]], and whether conditions must be met for that to occur.[1]

The author's answer is that command of a Guard unit ordinarily rests with the governor of the state to which it belongs, but that the President may federalise any unit and place it under federal control — something he says has occurred many times in American history, including during the 1992 Los Angeles riots and in the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina.[1] For Marines and Guardsmen to serve together domestically, he writes, [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus]] must be declared and [[habeas-corpus|habeas corpus]] suspended.[1]

## Lincoln precedent

The chapter identifies the presidency of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] as the case in which those conditions were met, describing a congressionally-approved martial law status under which the U.S. military was used to stifle spies, dissenters and others seeking to destroy the Republic during the Civil War.[1]

## Purpose of the discussion

The author reads the highlighted portion of the drop as being less about legal mechanics than about public psychology: that people panic and lose the capacity for logical thought when a crisis arrives, and that a large part of the purpose of the drops was to educate readers in advance so that they could remain calm and help keep chaos at bay.[1] He notes that Q closes the drop by assuring readers there would be no civil war and no war on American soil, with the phrase "clean and swift."[1]
