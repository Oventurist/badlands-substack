# DHS Israel Boycott Certification Requirement

The DHS Israel boycott certification requirement was a short-lived U.S. federal grant condition, reversed on August 4, 2025, that required cities and states to rebuke boycotts of Israeli companies in order to receive federal disaster funds.[1]

## The policy and its reversal

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] reversed course on the requirement on Monday, August 4, 2025, and deleted the earlier policy from its website.[1] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] removed a statement specifying that states must certify they would not sever "commercial relations specifically with Israeli companies" in order to qualify for funding.[1]

Reuters reported the same day that the language applied to at least $1.9 billion in funds that states rely on to cover search-and-rescue equipment, emergency manager salaries, and backup power systems, among other expenses, based on eleven agency grant notices reviewed by the wire service.[1] The reversal marked a shift for an administration that had previously sought to penalize institutions not aligned with its views on [[israel|Israel]] or antisemitism.[1]

## Commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote in the Badlands News Brief that the headline circulated early in the day and drew the sharp criticism it rightfully deserved.[1] He noted that DHS posted on X that no such requirement existed and was met with a Community Note clarifying that the department had only just removed the requirements, which had in fact previously existed.[1] His assessment of the net effect was that the requirement had been removed and that the public now knew it had once been [[fema|FEMA]] policy.[1]
