# Election Integrity Task Force

The Election Integrity Task Force is a federal task force announced on April 29, 2025 by [[alina-habba|Alina Habba]], the U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey, to preserve and protect the integrity of elections conducted in that district.[1]

## Structure and mandate

The task force consists of a team of federal prosecutors from the U.S. Attorney's Office working in conjunction with law enforcement officers from the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], and other federal agencies, with the stated purpose of implementing the directives of the President's March 25, 2025 executive order concerning elections.[1] Habba emphasized that her office and its federal partners were committed to ensuring that the administration of all elections in the district complies with federal law.[1]

Its published objectives include facilitating information sharing between federal, state and local officials so that election officials can efficiently remove ineligible individuals from state voter lists; prioritizing the investigation and prosecution of election crimes — including voter registration fraud, casting of fraudulent ballots, voting by non-citizens, individuals voting multiple times in the same election, and foreign nationals contributing funds to U.S. elections; and taking steps to ensure compliance with the voter list maintenance requirements of the National Voter Registration Act and the Help America Vote Act.[1]

## Badlands assessment

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the information-sharing provision as "reverse ERIC," speculating that federal authorities may have taken control of the Electronic Registration Information Center and describing its databases and codebases as "an active crime scene."[1] She endorsed the enumerated crime categories wholesale, noting that the list covers fake voters, fake ballots, fake donations and foreign interference while omitting fake counts, which she suggested would require a separate executive order.[1]

She singled out list maintenance as the most consequential element, arguing that cleaning voter rolls of fake registrations limits the number of fraudulent ballots naturally circulating during an election event and that inflated rolls are "critical path for the election fraud machine."[1] Her principal criticism was jurisdictional: the task force's writ extends only to the District of New Jersey, leaving the rest of the country uncovered.[1] She indicated the task force would be discussed further on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]], and predicted it would yield indictments if its investigation were conducted properly.[1]
