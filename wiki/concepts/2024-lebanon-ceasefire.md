# 2024 Lebanon Ceasefire

The 2024 Lebanon ceasefire was an agreement halting hostilities between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], announced by the Israeli cabinet at 10:30 p.m. on Tuesday, 26 November 2024 by a vote of 10 to 1, according to a statement from the Prime Minister's Office.[1] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] announced that the deal would take effect at 4 a.m. the following Wednesday morning.[1]

## Terms and guarantors

In a joint statement, Biden and French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] said the deal would provide the conditions needed for a return to calm in Lebanon, and that France and the United States would both work to ensure the ceasefire was fully implemented.[1] The agreement followed several days of strained last-minute negotiation in which Israel pushed for the removal of France as guarantor of the security situation in [[lebanon|Lebanon]], citing diplomatic tensions between the two countries.[1]

The deal passed through the Israeli war cabinet and the general cabinet before final approval. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] also ran the terms past the heads of local authorities in northern Israel, many of whom responded with anger at being told the deal would not allow an immediate return to their homes.[1]

## Negotiation and the Trump connection

U.S. envoy [[amos-hochstein|Amos Hochstein]] was the principal mediator. Lebanese and Israeli media reported on 8 November 2024 that Hochstein was acting under direct instruction from President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who was quoted telling him: "Go and finish your work and make a deal with Lebanon."[1] Lebanon's Parliament Speaker [[nabih-berri|Nabih Berri]] separately attributed Lebanese optimism to Hochstein's confirmation that he had a "green light" from Trump.[1]

## Immediate aftermath

Israel reportedly continued bombing [[beirut|Beirut]] up to the moment the deal was finalized — allegedly even after it had been agreed in principle — and after the agreement took effect the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] bombed a bridge on the Lebanese–Syrian border, likely used by Hezbollah to move troops between the two countries.[1] Netanyahu publicly emphasized that the ceasefire's value lay in allowing Israel to shift focus to fighting [[iran|Iran]], and warned Hezbollah that fighting would likely resume.[1] He also claimed the Biden administration had been withholding arms from Israel, an assertion Hochstein had previously called untrue.[1]

Iran's Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] issued a statement demanding that Netanyahu be executed for war crimes once the [[international-criminal-court|ICC]] arrest warrant against him is fulfilled.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] approached the milestone "with combined trepidation and enthusiasm," crediting Hochstein's work and loyalty to Trump but predicting that the fragile peace would not last.[1]

## Ceasefire as a "gift" to Trump

Ahead of the deal, Israeli officials described the emerging Lebanon ceasefire as an early foreign policy win intended for President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The Washington Post reported in November 2024 that a close aide to Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had told Trump and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] that week that Israel was rushing to advance a ceasefire in Lebanon, according to three current and former Israeli officials briefed on the meeting.[2] [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]], Netanyahu's minister of strategic affairs, made Mar-a-Lago the first stop of his U.S. tour that Sunday before travelling to the White House to update Biden administration officials on the state of the Lebanon talks — a sign, the Post wrote, of how swiftly America's political center of gravity had shifted after Trump's electoral victory.[2] One Israeli official, speaking anonymously, said: "There is an understanding that Israel would gift something to Trump … that in January there will be an understanding about Lebanon."[2] Dermer's office said he had discussed a wide range of issues on the trip without elaborating; Netanyahu's office, a Trump spokesperson and Kushner's spokesperson declined or did not respond to requests for comment.[2]
