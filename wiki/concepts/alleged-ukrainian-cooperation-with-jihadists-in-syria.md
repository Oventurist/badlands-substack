# Alleged Ukrainian Cooperation with Jihadists in Syria

In November 2024, the Syrian government told RT that Ukrainian agents had been working with [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] in Syria, offering drone warfare training and some U.S.-supplied weapons in exchange for manpower.[1]

## The allegation

The claim centered on Jabhat al-Nusra, since rebranded as [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS), which by that point had been reduced to parts of Idlib province in Syria's northwest — an outcome attributed in part to Russian assistance to the Syrian government against rebel militants including the [[islamic-state|Islamic State]].[1]

RT correspondent Roman Kosarev reported visiting Syria and seeing what he described as "undeniable evidence" that Kiev had made an alliance with HTS.[1] A Russian soldier identified only by the callsign "Gilza" told the outlet, "We have real confirmation of the Ukrainian instructors' presence in Syria," saying Ukrainian operatives had been teaching HTS militants to fly suicide drones and supplying such weapons.[1] Video footage filmed aboard a ship purported to show a U.S.-made Switchblade 600 drone being delivered to Syrian militants in crates labeled as humanitarian aid, and further footage showed a man wearing a black T-shirt with a Ukrainian trident symbol talking with a militant in Idlib.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands Media argued that if true, the reports indicated that the final kinetic battle of what it calls the War of Sovereignty — World War Three — would be fought in the Syrian desert.[1] The commentary framed the development as bringing the story full circle: the [[syrian-civil-war|Syrian civil war]] began in 2012 with intervention by Russia and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], and ran parallel to the 2014 Russian move into Ukraine, which the outlet attributes to a CIA-led Maidan coup against the elected Ukrainian government.[1]

The piece drew a mirror-image parallel between Ukrainian special operations forces allegedly training Al-Qaeda and ISIS fighters in Syria and earlier reports of the [[cia|CIA]] transporting ISIS fighters from the Middle East to Ukraine to fight the Russians, asking whether Americans were prepared to learn that ISIS and Al-Qaeda had been U.S. mercenaries all along.[1]

It enumerated the parties then present in Syria as Iran and Hezbollah, Ukraine and ISIS, Russia and [[wagner-group|Wagner]], the United States and [[nato|NATO]], the [[kurds|Kurds]], [[turkey|Turkey]] and its proxy militias, and the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], with the Chinese navy anchored to the east in the Persian Gulf guarding oil supply lines to Iran and the U.S. navy to the west in the Mediterranean protecting Israel and regional interests.[1] The commentary closed by describing the stage as set for a battle of biblical proportions in a theater biblical in its history, coinciding with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s return to the [[white-house|White House]], and asked whether readers believe in coincidences.[1]
