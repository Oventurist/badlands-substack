# Georgia Senate Bill 189

**Georgia Senate Bill 189** is election legislation approved by Georgia lawmakers on Thursday, March 28, 2024, ahead of the November general election.[1] The measure gives clearer guidelines for voter eligibility challenges, strengthens the security of paper ballots, and introduces new rules for qualifying for the state's presidential ballot.[1]

The bill was a response to an influx of voter challenges in Georgia following former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s loss in the 2020 election, which he claimed was the result of voter fraud; Republican lawmakers in swing states have been attempting to tighten voting restrictions going into the 2024 elections.[1]

## Provisions

SB 189 clarifies what constitutes "probable cause" when a voter's eligibility is challenged. Probable cause could be established if a voter has been registered in another jurisdiction, has obtained a homestead exemption in a different country, or has given a non-residential address such as a post office box.[1]

The legislation also provides that any political party that has qualified to appear on the presidential ballot in twenty states will have access to Georgia's ballot.[1] This eases access for independent presidential candidates such as [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], who otherwise is required to gather enough petition signatures in all fifty states; Kennedy's super PAC claimed he had already qualified for Georgia's ballot.[1]

## Opposition

Some Democratic lawmakers objected to the "probable cause" provision, characterizing it as another attempt to disenfranchise voters.[1]
