# Opium Wars

The Opium Wars were two nineteenth-century conflicts between Britain and China arising from the British trade in opium, and are treated in [[dope-inc-book|*Dope, Inc.*]] as the founding episode of the modern global narcotics economy.[1]

## Origins

During the eighteenth century England's domestic manufacturing began to fall behind, and England ran a trade deficit with China, importing far more than it exported and rapidly draining its silver reserves.[1] To remedy this, [[king-george-iii|King George III]] mandated the [[british-east-india-company|East India Company]] to ship large quantities of opium from British-controlled Bengal, India, into China — a policy that would both balance the trade deficit and, in the account given, create a population of addicts who would be more easily controlled.[1] In 1715 the British East India Company opened its first Far East office at the Chinese port city of Canton.[1]

## Escalation

The British drug trade expanded rapidly after the American Revolutionary War. In 1801 the British sold 5,000 chests of opium per year; by 1836 the figure surpassed 30,000 chests.[1] By the 1830s England's trade deficit with China had reversed to a ratio of eight to one in Britain's favor.[1]

## First war and its settlement

In 1839 the Emperor of China, confronting a country full of opium addicts, moved to crack down on British opium imports, prompting Britain to go to war.[1] The Royal Navy forced China to accept British opium, pay compensation for confiscated opium, and cede sovereignty over several offshore islands — the mechanism by which Britain came to control [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]].[1] As the account puts it: if China would not let England destroy it with drugs, England would destroy it with battleships.[1]

## Second war and aftermath

After a second Opium War in 1860, the [[hsbc|Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corporation]] was established, described in *Dope, Inc.* as "the central clearinghouse for all Far Eastern financial transactions relating to the black market in opium and its heroin derivative."[1] By 1860 Britain was said to control seven-eighths of the vastly expanded trade into China.[1]

## Downstream effects

By the mid-1800s London opium traffickers began diversifying into the cotton trade, with the slave and cotton trade of the American South run by many of the same figures.[1] Opium money purchased cotton that was shipped to England for manufacture; the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild family]] and [[lehman-brothers|Lehman Brothers]] made their initial entry into the United States through the pre-Civil War cotton and slave trade, and British opium traders financially backed the South in the American Civil War.[1]
