# Unacknowledged Special Access Programs

Unacknowledged special access programs (USAPs) are the most tightly compartmented category of United States classified activity, in which the existence of the program itself is denied rather than merely its contents.[1]

Commentary published in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] argued that USAPs grant the intelligence community "well-nigh limitless power to lie, manipulate, cover up, and otherwise limit the release and public recognition" of [[unidentified-anomalous-phenomena|unidentified anomalous phenomena]], whether terrestrial or extraterrestrial in nature.[1] That framing underpinned the argument that whistleblower [[david-grusch|David Grusch]]'s allegation of covert craft-retrieval programs was at least plausible, since the military-industrial complex has repeatedly advanced technologies denied to exist and only later acknowledged.[1]

## Post-9/11 restructuring in the Q framework

Chapter 4 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series argues that nearly every special access program was changed or altered after the September 11 attacks, with more intelligence becoming compartmentalized and the ability of agencies to work together expanding.[2] The author holds that this arrangement, used benevolently, creates a very powerful tool for protecting America, but that no president until [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] used the power toward benevolent ends.[2] Because the military came to possess the same special access programs as the civilian alphabet agencies, the chapter argues, Trump could exercise his authority as Commander in Chief to see any and all intelligence he desired, a capacity grounded in what the author describes as the Constitution's guarantee of complete presidential control and clearance over intelligence operations carried out under his watch.[2] The same framing underlies the chapter's reading of [[military-intelligence|military intelligence]] as the mechanism by which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] is to be defeated.[2]
