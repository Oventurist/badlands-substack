# 2023 Gabon Coup

The 2023 Gabon coup was the military seizure of power in [[gabon|Gabon]], an OPEC member and former French colony, four days after the country's disputed presidential election of August 26, 2023.[1]

## Events

Soldiers took power and Gabon's dollar bonds sank.[1] Army officers appeared on Gabonese state television to announce that they had cancelled the vote and dissolved the country's institutions, per BBC and Agence France-Presse reports.[1] President [[ali-bongo|Ali Bongo]] had been seeking to extend his family's 56-year hold on power in a contest the opposition called fraudulent.[1]

If the takeover held, it would represent the ninth military takeover in sub-Saharan Africa in three years, following [[2023-niger-coup|the Niger coup]] of the previous month.[1] Bloomberg attributed the run of takeovers to economic malaise and weak governance that fed civilian frustration, together with the spread of extremist violence in West Africa.[1]

## Interpretations

Badlands writers treated the coup as a geopolitical marker rather than an isolated national event. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] counted it as the eighth such coup in west and central Africa since 2020 and as "another blow to France and the west," anticipating that Western governments would eventually blame Russia.[1] She argued the timing, proximity and consistent messaging of the uprisings indicated coordination, while it remained unclear whether the goal was to reclaim Africa from globalist forces or to advance the "great reset."[1] Gabon's military urged "calm and serenity" among the population.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] emphasised that reporters on the ground described a festive public mood, similar to scenes in [[niger|Niger]], contradicting the framing by media, the State Department and the EU of these events as violent takeovers.[1] He noted that most of the eight coups since 2020 had been directly or indirectly supported by China and Russia, and posed the question: "Total chaos or Sovereign Alliance?"[1]

The coup came days after French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] said France would not withdraw from Niger despite warnings from the junta there and that the French ambassador would remain in place.[1]
