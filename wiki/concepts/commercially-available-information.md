# Commercially Available Information

"Commercially available information" (CAI) is the intelligence-community term for personal data that federal agencies purchase from private data brokers rather than obtain through warrants or compulsory process.[1]

## Scale and use

Federal agencies in the United States have been purchasing massive amounts of information about U.S. citizens from data brokers, information that Badlands Media notes could potentially be used for blackmail, stalking, harassment and public shaming.[1] The practice was documented in a report issued under Director of National Intelligence [[avril-haines|Avril Haines]] and published by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]].[1]

## The ODNI warning

The report warned that "CAI Increases the Power of the Government," noting that "the government would never have been permitted to compel billions of people to carry location tracking devices on their persons at all times, to log and track most of their social interactions, or to keep flawless records of all their reading habits."[1] The core objection is that commercial purchase allows the state to acquire, legally and quietly, a dataset it could never have lawfully compelled into existence.[1]

Badlands frames CAI as a central mechanism of contemporary [[mass-surveillance|mass surveillance]], purchased with taxpayer dollars and justified by the creed that "those who have nothing to hide have nothing to fear."[1]
