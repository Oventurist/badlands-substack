# Multipolar Margin Call

The "Multipolar Margin Call" is an analytical thesis advanced by Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], first laid out in October during what he describes as the height of manufactured economic tensions between [[donald-trump|Trump]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]. Its core claim is that the US–[[china|China]] trade war "wasn't a bug of the dissolution of the rules-based international order; it was the core feature," because it forces the rerouting of monetary flows, supply chains and manufacturing capacity back toward sovereign balance sheets on both sides of the Pacific.

## Mechanism

In the version restated in the [[badlands-brief-323|Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026]], the thesis is reinforced by the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] squeeze: Xi offering to help reopen the waterway, both sides agreeing it must remain open, and China signaling it will buy more American oil to reduce reliance on "the old collectivist chokepoints." The result is described as a pincer in which energy and capital are pulled out of the "globalist circulatory system" and redirected into "sovereign arteries," with Regions of Responsibility hardening and appeals to borderless governance "dashed on the rocks of pragmatic nationalism."

## Relationship to Sovereign Disentanglement

The thesis feeds directly into the broader [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] framework, in which apparent conflict serves as cover story for structural decoupling. Burning Bright sequences the cascade as [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] opening with [[ukraine]], Trump continuing through [[venezuela]], [[iran]] and a looming [[cuba]] chapter, and Xi positioned to complete the arc with [[taiwan]] — "the last remaining globalist forward operating base of any real consequence." A clean sovereign resolution there would finish a trilogy of lessons: Iran on the folly of relying on distant nations for energy security, [[russia]] on national security, and China on logistical and technological security in semiconductors, rare earths and advanced manufacturing. He argues that when this lands, the on-shoring and reshoring pursued by both leaders "will look less like protectionism and more like prophecy."
