# Voter Citizenship Checks Executive Order

The voter citizenship checks executive order is a directive issued by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] establishing citizenship verification procedures for voter rolls and imposing tighter rules on mail-in voting.[1]

## Litigation and the Nichols ruling

Democratic plaintiffs sought a preliminary injunction to block the order. In May 2026, U.S. District Judge [[carl-nichols|Carl Nichols]] cleared the order to move forward, ruling that the plaintiffs had failed to demonstrate legal standing or sufficient harm.[1] Nichols found that the order does not directly require the plaintiffs to take any action, and that no federal agency had yet implemented steps under it that could cause harm.[1]

He rejected as speculative the argument that the order would disenfranchise millions of voters, noting that it mandates procedures allowing individuals to access, review, and correct information in any state-by-state citizenship lists used to maintain voter rolls.[1] He likewise dismissed privacy objections to interagency sharing of basic voter data such as name, age, and residence, holding that such sharing does not constitute injury sufficient for Article III standing.[1] The court left open the possibility of renewed motions if the Postal Service issues a final rule directly affecting plaintiffs, or if State Citizenship Lists omit specific individuals due to particularized flaws.[1]

The decision removed a key legal obstacle, allowing the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] to proceed with implementing provisions on policing voter rolls for citizenship and restricting mail-in voting; no rollout timeline was specified.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the ruling to criticize state attorneys general — citing [[colorado|Colorado]]'s AG and his reported 65 lawsuits against the Trump administration, counted as campaign accomplishments in a gubernatorial run — arguing that such suits amount to obstruction rather than justice, and that they are largely losing.[1]
