# Family Jewels

The "**Family Jewels**" is the colloquial name for the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s internal compilation of its own misdeeds, the most well known of the internal agency reports made available to the 1975 [[church-committee|Church Committee]].[1]

## Contents and use by investigators

The report outlined the agency's misdeeds dating back to the Eisenhower administration.[1] Along with comparable internal reports found in other agencies, it provided road maps that the committee's staff investigators used to piece together complicated histories of domestic, foreign and military intelligence programs during the Cold War era — an arduous organizing and analytical task even with a peak staff of 150.[1]

## Criticism

Although the Family Jewels are widely lauded as the most explosive secrets of the Central Intelligence Agency, Badlands Media notes that many see the report as a cherry-picked and sanitized placation rather than a genuine attempt to come clean, pointing out that the report itself admits much of its content comes from "the recollections of the senior people in this office."[1] The article extends this skepticism to the committee's document access generally, observing that while investigators enjoyed seemingly unprecedented access to never-before-public materials, whether those materials were strategically selected before delivery is unknown.[1]
