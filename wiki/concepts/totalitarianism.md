# Totalitarianism

**Totalitarianism** is treated by Badlands Media as the end state toward which [[menticide]] and induced [[mass-psychosis]] are directed.[1]

## Method of establishment

Would-be totalitarian rulers prime a population through waves of terror, saturating propaganda, and the isolation of individuals from friends, family and coworkers, which makes them far more susceptible to delusion.[1] Once the population is alone, confused and exhausted, and the masses crave a return to an ordered world, the rulers offer a "way out" at a price: the surrender of self-reliance and personal responsibility in exchange for submissive obedience.[1] Reason and common human decency become scarce, an atmosphere of terror prevails, and an imagined "enemy in our midst" is projected so that society turns on itself at the urging of the authorities.[1]

## Character of totalitarian order

The order produced by the totalitarian world is described as pathological and psychopathic.[1] By enforcing strict conformity and requiring blind obedience, totalitarianism rids the world of the spontaneity that produces many of life's joys and of the creativity that drives societies forward; regardless of whether it is fascist, communist, or bears some title future generations may dream up, its total control breeds stagnation and death on a mass scale.[1] The article illustrates this aesthetically by contrasting the Winter Palace in Saint Petersburg — official residence of the Russian Emperor from 1732 to 1917 under the theistic Romanov culture — with the Great Hall of the People at the western edge of Tiananmen Square in the communist state.[1]

## Resistance

Countermeasures identified include ordering one's own mind after the manner urged by [[carl-jung|Carl Jung]], disseminating information that counters propaganda since censorship is essential to the rulers' success, ridiculing demagogues, and building [[parallel-structures|parallel structures]] on the model of [[vaclav-havel|Václav Havel]].[1] The article quotes [[thomas-paine|Thomas Paine]]: "Tyranny, like hell, is not easily conquered. Yet we have this consolation with us, that the harder the conflict, the more glorious the triumph."[1]
