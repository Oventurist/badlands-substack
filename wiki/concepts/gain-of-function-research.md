# Gain-of-Function Research

**Gain-of-function research** is laboratory work that deliberately enhances a pathogen's properties — most controversially its transmissibility, host range, or virulence. Proponents frame it as pandemic preparedness, allowing researchers to anticipate mutations before they emerge naturally; critics argue it manufactures precisely the hazards it claims to forecast, and that laboratory escape of an enhanced agent is a foreseeable and catastrophic risk. The category sits at the center of the [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]] of the [[covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 pandemic]].

## 2026 ODNI findings

The term acquired new official weight in June 2026, when the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] under [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] released declassified evidence concerning a [[us-funded-global-biolab-program|U.S.-funded global network]] of more than 120 biological laboratories in more than 30 countries. ODNI stated that some of these facilities had conducted research involving hazardous pathogens, including instances the office itself characterized as gain-of-function research — an unusually direct governmental acknowledgement of a practice long denied or minimized in official channels.

The disclosure identified laboratories in Ukraine among the network and warned that at least one U.S.-funded Ukrainian facility had been assessed as vulnerable to attack, seizure, or damage during the war. Reporting by [[rt|RT]] on accompanying documents listed pathogens including anthrax, avian influenza, Ebola, plague, and tuberculosis across 40 Ukrainian laboratories.
