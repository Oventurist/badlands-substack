## Democratic midterm "cabal" planning

The Brief summarises reporting by Politico of June 11, 2026, that Senate Democrats were conducting private strategy sessions and scenario-planning exercises ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. The reported focus was on potential election disruptions, foreign interference, federal law-enforcement activity at polling places, and post-election legal disputes, with lawmakers, election officials, and outside advisers developing litigation, communications, and response plans. Democrats were also said to complain of receiving limited election-security briefings from the Trump administration. Politico framed the planning as defensive; critics compared it to the cross-institutional coordination described in a February 4, 2021 [[time-magazine|Time]] magazine article that characterised the 2020 election as having been "fortified."

Ashe in America opens her commentary with the question "Is this a remake or a rerun?" She quotes Politico's report that [[chuck-schumer|Senator Chuck Schumer]] "last week convened a slate of experts that included former Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]], prominent Democratic election attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], election-law expert [[norman-eisen|Norman Eisen]] and the heads of the left-leaning legal group [[democracy-forward|Democracy Forward]] and the nonpartisan, nonprofit [[protect-democracy|Protect Democracy]]," who helped senators prepare for three possible scenarios and coordinate with state and local election officials.

She then reproduces an extended passage from the 2021 Time article describing "a conspiracy unfolding behind the scenes, one that both curtailed the protests and coordinated the resistance from CEOs," and asserting that the shadow campaigners feared not a Trump victory but "an election so calamitous that no result could be discerned at all." The quoted passage credits that network with getting states to change voting systems and laws, securing hundreds of millions in public and private funding, fending off voter-suppression lawsuits, recruiting poll workers, expanding mail voting, pressuring social media companies to take "a harder line against disinformation," running public-awareness campaigns about the pace of the count, and monitoring "every pressure point" after Election Day. It quotes Norm Eisen, described as a former Obama Administration official who recruited members of both parties to the board of the Voter Protection Program, saying the untold story was "the thousands of people of both parties who accomplished the triumph of American democracy at its very foundation."

Ashe in America characterises that passage as an "astounding admission" and argues that the same actors are preparing to repeat the exercise, describing both the current and the 2020 efforts as "the brain-children of Norm Eisen and his minions." She writes that Marc Elias is "strategizing election interference instead of preparing for his inevitable criminal trial," finds it "encouraging that they're announcing their plans to psy-op the nation in advance this time" rather than waiting three months to boast, and closes: "I hope this is a remake instead of a rerun and that they change the ending."

## Iran: cancelled strikes and a reported deal

The second item reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appeared to pull back from threatened military escalation against [[iran|Iran]] after signalling that negotiations with Tehran might be nearing a breakthrough. Days after warning that the United States would "bomb the shit out of them" absent an agreement, Trump said a major peace deal could be signed soon and indicated planned strikes had been suspended. The Brief notes continued clashes in and around the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], with US strikes on Iranian targets and a naval blockade of Iranian shipping met by Iranian missile and drone attacks and threats to disrupt regional energy flows, each side accusing the other of violating ceasefire arrangements. Iran publicly stated that no final agreement had been approved and that sanctions relief and other demands remained unresolved, while warning that renewed US attack would draw a harsher response.

A late-night update summarises Axios reporting of June 12 on a draft United States–Iran memorandum of understanding that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz without tolls, provide sanctions relief tied to Iranian compliance, and extend a ceasefire for 60 days including in Lebanon while nuclear negotiations proceeded. Axios reported a tentative agreement reached Wednesday night after talks involving the Qatari mediator Ali Al-Thawadi and Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]]. Under the framework Iran would commit not to acquire a nuclear weapon and work toward resolving disputes over its enriched uranium stockpile, with one option being down-blending highly enriched uranium inside Iran under United Nations supervision, subject to a later and more detailed agreement. Trump said he expected a signing ceremony over the weekend; an Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson said Tehran had not made a final decision. If signed, the accord would be known as the [[islamabad-agreement|Islamabad agreement]], mediated jointly by [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[pakistan|Pakistan]].

He reports that Trump "broke Mace" by endorsing one of her opponents in the six-candidate South Carolina gubernatorial primary, in which Mace finished last with 12 percent and failed to reach the June 23 run-off; that Mace then vowed on X to seek revenge for Trump "ending her political career"; and that she subsequently endorsed [[alan-wilson|Alan Wilson]] in the run-off in order to defeat Trump-endorsed Lieutenant Governor [[pamela-evette|Pamela Evette]].

## IDF mixed-gender tank crews

The Brief reports that the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] said men and women would not serve inside the same tanks after the leaders of twelve [[hesder-yeshiva|Hesder yeshivas]] reportedly threatened to stop sending students to Armored Corps units over a female tank-integration pilot. The army said it still "needs every male and female combat soldier" but clarified that integration options do not include mixed crews in the same vehicle. The dispute is politically significant because Hesder graduates are heavily represented in Israeli combat units and the officer corps, making a yeshiva boycott of armored service a genuine recruitment threat.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry notes ironically that Israel is "known for being a highly progressive society," with Tel Aviv described as the "gayest city in the world." He observes that in April Israel's High Court of Justice ruled that the IDF must proceed with integration, and that the rabbis warn this will drive an exodus of soldiers at a moment when Israel cannot afford losses of experienced personnel. He argues that enforcement would damage IDF morale and effectiveness just as Netanyahu contemplates expanding the war and opening new fronts, that tension between the Ultra-Orthodox community and the IDF is already severe, and that the edict could "accelerate the collapse of the IDF" given reports of exhausted, overworked soldiers, lagging recruitment, and reduced enthusiasm to serve. He ends with a single word: "Accelerate."

## ActBlue, WinRed, and campaign-finance allegations

The fifth item reports that congressional scrutiny of the online political fundraising platforms ActBlue and WinRed intensified as House Republicans held a hearing on allegations of fraud, foreign contributions, and weaknesses in donor verification. ActBlue chief executive [[regina-wallace-jones|Regina Wallace-Jones]] repeatedly invoked her Fifth Amendment rights. Democrats on the panel argued Republicans were targeting Democratic fundraising while noting similar concerns about WinRed; Republicans said questions remained about suspicious donation patterns, identity verification, and possible straw donors or foreign funds. The hearing produced no new evidence of criminal wrongdoing by either platform but underscored that investigations would continue into the 2026 midterms.

Ashe in America recalls an April 2025 House Republican report stating that ActBlue changed its fraud policy twice during the 2024 campaign, adopting "more lenient" standards that let foreign and other suspicious donor money flow into US campaigns, and that the committee intended to seek transcribed interviews with employees responsible for fraud prevention and to review suspicious activity reports. She concludes that the Republicans "were telling the truth," that ActBlue "is now being prosecuted — which is why Wallace Jones pled the fifth," and recounts that ActBlue launched in 2004 to harness grassroots small donors: "And boy did they harness that power."

Crediting [[peter-bernegger|Peter Bernegger]], she lists federal statutes she believes may have been violated: 18 U.S.C. § 1956 (money laundering), § 1957 (monetary transactions in criminally derived property), § 1001 (false statements to federal agencies), 52 U.S.C. § 30122 (contributions in the name of another, under FECA), 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (wire fraud), § 1341 (mail fraud), § 371 (conspiracy to defraud the United States), § 1028 (identity and document fraud), § 1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act), 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809 (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act), and 31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq. Rejecting the defences "This is just how campaign finance works now!" and "Both sides do it!", she calls the conduct "a portfolio of crime" and urges: "Prosecute every one of them."

## Bonus: USPS ballot-mail rules

A bonus item reports that the United States Postal Service proposed new regulations governing mail-in and absentee ballots for federal elections, citing President Trump's [[executive-order-14399|Executive Order 14399]] on election integrity. Ballots for federal general, special, and runoff elections would have to use USPS-approved envelope designs bearing the official Election Mail logo and unique Intelligent Mail barcodes on both outbound and return envelopes. Election officials would submit ballot-mailing and participation data through a new Federal Ballot Mail Portal, including information associating mailed ballots with official election records and supporting reconciliation of mailings. USPS said the proposal would standardise tracking and verification and improve visibility into ballots moving through the postal system, and would establish a verification process before accepting outbound ballot mailings, with non-compliant mailings returned for correction.
