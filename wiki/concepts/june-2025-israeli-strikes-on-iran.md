# June 2025 Israeli Strikes on Iran

The June 2025 Israeli strikes on Iran were a wave of attacks launched by [[israel|Israel]] against targets in Tehran in the early hours of Friday, June 13, 2025 (local time), described by Israeli officials as a "preemptive strike" and a targeted military operation intended to "roll back the Iranian threat to Israel's very survival."[1]

## The operation

Explosions boomed across Tehran as simultaneous raids struck nuclear and military sites as well as the homes of high-value targets with knowledge of the Iranian nuclear program.[1] Iranian state media reported that General [[hossein-salami|Hossein Salami]], commander in chief of the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]], was killed; reporting also indicated that other "top Revolutionary Guard generals" and acclaimed scientists died in the strikes.[1]

Israeli Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] first announced the operation, declaring "a special state of emergency" in Israel and warning of potential drone and missile attacks against Israel and its civilians.[1]

## United States response

U.S. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] issued a statement saying, "Tonight, Israel took unilateral action against Iran. We are not involved in strikes against Iran and our top priority is protecting American forces in the region."[1] Rubio added that Israel had advised Washington that it believed the action was necessary for its self-defense, that President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and the administration had taken all necessary steps to protect U.S. forces, and warned that "Iran should not target U.S. interests or personnel."[1] Iranian Defense Minister Aziz Nasirzadeh had previously warned that [[iran|Iran]] could attack U.S. bases if nuclear talks with the Trump administration broke down.[1]

## Iranian response

Iran vowed to respond harshly to the strikes on its nuclear facilities.[1] Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] warned that Israel would face "severe punishment" for the attacks.[1]

## Continuation of the campaign

On Saturday, June 14, 2025, the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] said it had killed more than 20 Iranian commanders, including the head of Iran's Intelligence Directorate and the commander of Iran's surface-to-surface missile array.[2] A former IDF spokesperson characterized the preemptive attack as a turning point for the Middle East that damaged Iranian deterrence across the region, and said Israel's overriding goal was ensuring that Tehran never obtains a nuclear weapon.[2]

On Sunday, June 15 — the third day of the campaign — Israel attacked dozens of sites in Iran, including energy sites, radar systems, and ballistic missiles and their launchers, and killed Iran's top intelligence officers.[2] The IDF also bombed an Iranian refueling plane at Mashhad Airport in northeast Iran, some 2,300 kilometers (1,430 miles) from Israel, which it said was the most distant strike since the operation began.[2] Early Monday morning the army said it struck surface-to-surface missile launch sites in central Iran, shortly after the IDF Home Front Command told Israelis to remain near shelters ahead of an expected missile barrage that did not materialize.[2] Israeli statements described the opening of an "air corridor to Tehran."[2]

## Assessments of damage

Nonproliferation and Iranian experts told the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] that the airstrikes on Iran's nuclear sites had damaged some aboveground research facilities and infrastructure but did not appear to have eliminated the thousands of centrifuges buried deep underground that enrich near-weapons-grade uranium, nor the hundreds of pounds of material already produced.[2] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said the Iranian program posed an existential threat Israel intended to destroy, stating "We struck at the heart of Iran's nuclear enrichment program" in an address early Friday.[2] Experts nonetheless concluded, based on reports, statements, videos and overhead imagery of the sites hit and those still unscathed, that no irreversible damage had been done.[2]

## Requests for US participation

Israeli officials asked the Trump administration to join military operations against Iran's nuclear program, including a strike on the fortified [[fordow-fuel-enrichment-plant|Fordow]] enrichment site, [[axios]] reported on June 14, warning that Israel lacked the capability to destroy the deeply buried facility alone.[2] A White House official denied an Israeli claim that Trump had suggested he would consider joining, and a second U.S. official said the administration was not then considering involvement.[2]

## Diplomacy and market effects

Trump said on Sunday he was open to Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] serving as a mediator in the Israel-Iran conflict following an hour-long phone call the previous day, after which the Kremlin said Putin had condemned Israel's strikes and expressed concern over escalation while reiterating Russia's willingness to facilitate negotiations.[2] Gold rose toward a record on Monday as the escalating conflict drove investors toward haven assets, climbing as much as 0.6% in early Asian trading to around $3,450 an ounce, about $50 short of an all-time peak set in April.[2] The strikes also threatened to crowd out the agenda of the [[2025-g7-summit|G7]] leaders' gathering hosted by Canada that week.[2]

## Badlands commentary

Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] treated the escalation primarily as an information-war event. [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] recalled Trump's earlier remark that "we can blow up whatever we want, but nobody's getting killed. We can blow up a lab, but nobody's going to be in the lab," arguing that what audiences were seeing was not necessarily what was happening and that most on-the-ground footage was out of context — particularly in the immediate aftermath of the release of the Veo 3 AI video module, which he described as a paradigm-destroying development for media credibility.[1] He identified three macro themes: global chaos, Trump as "reluctant warrior; resolute peacemaker," and an inversion of the Axis-versus-Allies paradigm into a "New Sovereign Axis" contrasted with "Old Collectivist Allies," speculating about how soon the United States, Russia and China would be pulled in and forced to mandate peace.[1] He also argued that the near-universal anticipation of this sequence of events within the info war suggested deliberate narrative seeding from both sides.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he had pre-recorded a Geopolitics show predicting that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] would either conduct a false flag attack or strike Iran in a way calculated to bait a hasty overreaction, allowing him to resume a posture of perpetual victimhood.[1] He characterized the strikes as an unprovoked attack on a sovereign nation based on a suspicion repeated for forty years without ever being definitively proven, said Israel had broken a number of international laws and was clearly the aggressor, and predicted it was cementing its legacy as a pariah state.[1] He noted that China and Russia had both accused Israel of violating international law and that both had significant interests invested in Iran.[1]

In the following brief, [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] questioned what conclusive proof existed that the strikes had occurred at all, arguing that audiences reflexively believe war reporting from the same neoconservative sources they otherwise dismiss, and urged readers to "withhold belief" rather than be mobilized emotionally toward World War III.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the sequence as "Escalation. Denuclearization. Normalization," arguing that if the announced narrative endpoint is known in advance, the reported interim "actuals" may not have needed to happen, and that Trump's stated end goal is peace.[2] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry connected the language about a "corridor" to Tehran to the "David's Corridor" concept through Syria he had discussed for months, predicted escalation that Trump would allow under a "let them fight" posture, and read Trump's remarks about Putin's readiness as evidence the two had been discussing the file for some time.[2] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] offered an allegory of two feuding neighbors' houses throwing rocks at each other, concluding that the wiser course was to mind one's own business.[2]
