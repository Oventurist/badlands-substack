# 2020 Democratic Presidential Primary

The **2020 Democratic presidential primary** was the nominating contest that selected [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as the Democratic Party's candidate against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. In [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary it is treated less as a conventional nomination fight than as the field on which Trump allegedly engineered the choice of his own general-election opponent.[1]

## Early field dynamics

Beginning in 2019, Biden was widely described as the frontrunner, a status attributed largely to name recognition accumulated over eight years as [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s vice president.[1] His initial polling lead eroded almost immediately, settling at support from a little over 30% of Democratic voters, and his fundraising fell short of what a frontrunner would normally command.[1] Commentary emphasized his apparent cognitive decline, which limited rallies and time on the campaign trail and undercut his perceived viability as the candidate best able to defeat Trump.[1]

Biden lost decisively in the Iowa caucuses and again in the New Hampshire primary, and a sentiment spread among Democratic voters that he could not win a general election.[1] Biden nonetheless insisted publicly that he would prevail, telling CNN that Trump "is obsessed with me, and he should be, with good reason" and that Trump was "deathly afraid to face me."[1]

## Electability as the dominant issue

Polling throughout the cycle showed Democratic voters prioritizing electability over policy.[1] One survey cited found that 97% of likely Democratic voters believed beating Trump was very or extremely important, while only 28% were certain Democrats would win.[1] [[npr|NPR]] reported comparable figures: nearly 90% of Democratic voters called it "extremely important" to beat Trump, fewer than a third believed the eventual nominee would "certainly" win, and 61% of Republicans believed Trump would "certainly" win reelection.[1]

The NPR reporting also documented [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]]'s rise in popularity and small-dollar fundraising at a moment when Biden was slipping, alongside persistent doubt that Warren could beat Trump — doubt attributed in part to the political damage Trump had inflicted through the "Pocahontas" nickname and the [[elizabeth-warren-dna-test|DNA test episode]].[1]

## The "chosen opponent" thesis

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argued that Trump deliberately elevated Biden by attacking him personally and continuously in the media, granting Biden enormous free coverage and starving rival candidates of attention.[1] Once Biden began fighting back, Trump-hostile Democrats coalesced around him and the press shifted toward defending and promoting him.[1] Anonymous leaks from purported Trump campaign insiders claiming Biden was the only rival they feared functioned, in this reading, as free political advertising for Biden.[1]

[[politico|Politico]] reported in April 2019 that Trump's insults masked genuine concern, quoting advisers who said Trump asked privately, "How are we gonna beat Biden?" and pushed back on reassurances with "But what if he does?" — an assessment tied to Biden's perceived strength in the Rust Belt states of Wisconsin, Michigan and Pennsylvania.[1] Lange treated these reports as evidence of the manipulation rather than of authentic fear, concluding that Biden "was the perfect opponent" and that Trump "masterfully manipulated the media" into producing him.[1]
