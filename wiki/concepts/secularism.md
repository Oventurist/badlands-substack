# Secularism

**Secularism** — which the Badlands Media essay "God and the Declaration of Independence" identifies with nihilism, calling nihilism "secularism by another name" — is treated in that essay as the philosophical opponent of the American founding's theological premises.[1]

## The problem of authority

The essay frames the question of authority as a binary: either authority is given to man from a source outside himself, or it is claimed by him as a matter of his own choosing.[1] By denying a transcendent origin of rights and authority, the secular-nihilistic viewpoint necessarily forces the second conclusion.[1] The author notes that in most authority structures throughout history there has been a chain of authority beginning with Heaven, God or a consortium of higher beings, and argues that any such chain requires a starting point — which for American society the [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]] identifies as the Creator.[1]

Observing that mankind instinctually seeks a source of authority transcending himself, the essay adds that on a deep psychological level most people feel they cannot trust someone who sees no true authority outside himself, and that the same holds for entire legal systems.[1] Even tyrants who believe in nothing but their own power, it says, often pretend to rule on behalf of some higher power — citing [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]]'s [[shadow-men|*Shadow Men*]] as "basically an encyclopedia" on that type.[1] The abuse of the principle by false rulers, the author maintains, does not invalidate the principle.[1]

## Moral relativism and majority rule

Without any purpose to existence, the essay argues, civilizational procedures, laws and customs can be justified on no basis other than that people happen to prefer them; morality becomes a purely human invention, wholly relative and reducible to social custom.[1] On this view mass murder is not intrinsically wrong but only "wrong" to those who dislike it, and whether freedom or tyranny reigns on Earth carries zero cosmic significance.[1]

The closest thing to an objective standard secularism possesses, the author writes, is the idea that it is better to do what the majority wants, which is why secularism champions democracy as the highest ideal.[1] Drawing on [[walter-lippmann|Walter Lippmann]]'s [[public-opinion-book|*Public Opinion*]], the essay counters that majority will is largely a mirage and that real power lies in the ability to sway the masses.[1] It warns that a society can vote itself into a totalitarian state and that, absent guardrails against encroachment on individual rights, democracy devolves into mob rule.[1] Along the way it criticizes what it calls the Marxist education system for reducing oppression to race, when a person can become a minority merely by holding a differing belief.[1]

## Incompatibility with the American system

The essay concludes that secularism is utterly antithetical to the spirit and philosophy of the Declaration, making it heretical to the political system as designed by the founders.[1] It argues that it makes as much sense for an atheist to be a politician in America as for an atheist to become a pastor, asking how one could vow to uphold a system of laws fundamentally at odds with one's own conscience.[1]

The modern West is described as a civilization-wide experiment in what happens when God is denied as the true source of rights and authority, with results the essay considers undesirable and unsurprising.[1] Addressing the claim that only the secular view is consistent with science, the author denies that denying God is necessary to engage the scientific method, notes that promises that war, slavery, pollution and superstition would end with the abandonment of belief in God were never made on legitimately scientific grounds, and observes that no compelling evidence has been offered for a connection between the denial of God and the improvement of civilization.[1] Atheists who propose reason alone as the source of rights, it adds, still face the problem that the source then reverts to man.[1]
