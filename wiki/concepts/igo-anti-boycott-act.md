# IGO Anti-Boycott Act (H.R. 867)

The **IGO Anti-Boycott Act**, designated House Resolution 867, is a bipartisan bill introduced in the U.S. House of Representatives that would extend existing federal anti-boycott law to cover boycotts of Israel called for by international governmental organizations (IGOs). Prominent figures within President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "Make America Great Again" movement publicly opposed the measure on the grounds that it would criminalize boycotts and constitutionally protected political speech directed at Israel.[1]

## Legislative history

The bill was filed on January 31 by Representative [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]], a New York Republican, together with Representative [[josh-gottheimer|Josh Gottheimer]], a New Jersey Democrat.[1] It is structured as an expansion of an existing statute enacted by the Republican-controlled [[congress|Congress]] in 2018, which made participation in boycotts against Israel illegal and carried penalties of up to twenty years in prison and fines of up to one million dollars.[1]

Lawler's bill would broaden that framework to encompass international governmental organizations and would permit the prosecution of individuals who publicly shared information intended to connect people working for such IGOs with the Israeli or American governments.[1]

## Cancelled vote and backlash

A vote on the measure had been slated for a Monday in early May 2025, but House leadership pulled the bill from consideration on Monday morning following intense online pushback, including accusations that Lawler was engaging in treason by endorsing the legislation.[1] A spokesperson for Gottheimer told *Newsweek* that it was "beyond outrageous and offensive that House leadership bowed to extreme-right forces and pulled this commonsense, bipartisan bill that makes antisemitic and hate-driven boycotts illegal."[1]

## Debate

Critics of H.R. 867 argue that the legislation conflicts with the right to free speech established by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] to the U.S. Constitution.[1] Supporters maintain that it targets antisemitic actions, including boycotts of Israel. The bill drew both bipartisan support and bipartisan opposition.[1]

Commentary published in the *Badlands News Brief* by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] endorsed the criticism of Lawler and called for the entire Congress to be investigated for potential foreign influence.[1]
