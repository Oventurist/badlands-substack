# COVID-19 Emergency Declarations Termination

The termination of the United States' twin COVID-19 emergency declarations refers to the decision announced by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on Monday, January 30, 2023 to end both the national emergency and the public health emergency for COVID-19 on May 11, 2023.[1]

## Announcement

Biden informed Congress of the plan as most of the world had returned closer to normalcy nearly three years after the declarations were first issued.[1] Ending the declarations would formally restructure the federal coronavirus response, treating the virus as an endemic threat to public health that could be managed through agencies' normal statutory authorities rather than emergency powers.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cautioned that the end of the emergency "might not be the boon we think it is," pointing to [[event-201|Event 201]] and to [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] as indications that recurring pandemics were anticipated.[1] He argued that COVID had to be formally closed out before another declared health crisis could be initiated, and invited readers to speculate on when the next "global health crisis" would be triggered.[1]

The same brief noted that the expiry of COVID-era tax breaks was expected to strain household finances, a change flagged by tax analysts as part of a "double whammy" alongside reduced refunds administered by the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]].[1]
