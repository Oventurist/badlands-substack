# Badlands Brief: Criminal Invader Crashouts & Honeypot Hoaxes

"Criminal Invader Crashouts & Honeypot Hoaxes" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dated February 10, 2026. Following the Brief's standard format, each item pairs a summary of mainstream reporting with commentary from a named Badlands contributor. This edition carried analysis from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and covered a [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-fifth-circuit|Fifth Circuit]] ruling on the detention of noncitizens during removal proceedings, ongoing [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]-related fallout in the United Kingdom and the United States, the Republican push for documentary proof of citizenship in federal elections, and a reported split within the Republican Party over artificial intelligence policy. Bonus items covered an international round-up and an arrest connected to a suspected unlicensed biological laboratory in Las Vegas.

## Fifth Circuit ruling on pre-deportation detention

The Brief reported that on Friday the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-fifth-circuit|US Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit]] reversed two district-court orders that had required bond hearings or release for detained noncitizens, holding that the government may detain certain "applicants for admission" without bond during removal proceedings under 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A). The ruling came in *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Bondi*, consolidated with *Padron Covarrubias v. Vergara*.

Following the decision, Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] posted that [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] attorneys had secured a "crucial legal victory" supporting the administration's immigration agenda. [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] posted that courts had ordered releases on the basis of claims that DHS was breaking the law, and described the ruling as supporting the department's position.

In commentary, Ashe in America argued that the notion the government cannot detain what she terms "criminal invaders" on their way out of the country is "a perversion of our laws and standards." She contends that full sovereignty requires territory and therefore borders, that borders are governed by law, and that broken law must carry consistent consequences — which is what "rule of law" means, even if, in her view, "it's debatable if we've actually had that in our lifetimes." She describes a sovereign state as one exercising supreme authority over its territory, argues this authority is especially critical in America because of the country's distinctive perspective on liberty and rights, and warns against "turning our nation's liberty into licentiousness." Following what she calls the Biden-era criminal invasion, she writes, "every state is a border state," giving the American government a duty — not merely a right — to remove such individuals. She concludes that thanks to the Fifth Circuit, once located, detainees "will remain detained until their one-way flight to their final destination."

Burning Bright's commentary treats the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein narrative]] as a deliberately contaminated information environment. ## SAVE America Act and the voter ID fight

The Brief reported that Republicans are moving forward with the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]] (H.R. 7296), legislation that would amend the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act of 1993]] to require documentary proof of US citizenship to register to vote in federal elections. Acceptable documentation listed in the bill includes a US passport, a REAL ID–compliant identification indicating citizenship, or other official proof of citizenship. The bill was introduced by Rep. [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]] on January 30, 2026, and referred to the House Committee on House Administration. While no final [[house-of-representatives|House]] vote had yet occurred, House leadership had taken steps toward floor consideration, with Roy indicating a vote would occur that week. If passed by the House, the measure would require [[senate|Senate]] consideration before reaching the President's desk.

On Monday, House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] criticized the SAVE America Act and broader voter ID efforts in an interview with Dana Bash on [[cnn|CNN]]'s "State of the Union," characterizing them as voter suppression and accusing Republicans of attempting to rig or steal elections.

Ashe in America responded that congressional minority leadership has "decided" that opposing voter ID is a winning issue for its caucus, calling the choice "mental." Citing CNN chief data analyst Harry Enten, she wrote that 83% of Americans favor requiring photo ID to vote, including 71% of Democrats, and that voter ID is supported by 82% of Latinos and 76% of Black Americans. "Voter ID is not controversial," she argued. "Democrat opposition to it, therefore, should be." She predicted Democrats would "screech about suppression" and that there is a high likelihood Republicans will "cave and chicken out," despite the polling, closing with the exhortation "Accelerate."

## Republican split over AI policy

The Brief summarized a Politico report describing a growing divide within the Republican Party over artificial intelligence policy, noting that the outlet cites the public statements of several prominent Republicans and relies on anonymous sources. Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] has made AI oversight a priority in his final year in office, supporting state-level regulation and warning that unchecked AI development could harm workers and strain energy infrastructure; Republican allies in the Florida Senate introduced an Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights at the start of the 2026 legislative session. Senator [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] has taken the most aggressive stance, criticizing AI as a threat to workers and social order and condemning a Trump administration executive order seeking to preempt state AI regulation. Utah Governor [[spencer-cox|Spencer Cox]] has pushed for regulations focused on children and social harms while supporting AI education and workforce training.

Trump has opposed most AI regulation, arguing US companies must be free to innovate to compete globally, particularly with China, and his administration issued an executive order in December seeking to block states from adopting their own AI rules. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] have publicly aligned with Trump's approach. Politico concluded that AI policy could become a defining issue for Republicans in 2028, potentially fracturing the party's coalition of working-class voters, social conservatives, and business-aligned interests.

Burning Bright characterized the piece as "another calculated Narrative" from the Media Protectorate, framing an internal tech fight as an existential threat to the [[maga|MAGA]] core and Trump's mandate, casting Hawley and DeSantis as defenders of workers and families against unchecked AI while positioning Trump as opposed to those values. He agrees with the premise that Trump is "at his core, an accelerator" — but considers it a virtue. Citing Trump's executive orders on nuclear energy, AI development, and supersonic flight as evidence of a consistent [[accelerationism|accelerationist]] stance, he argues that preempting a patchwork of state regulations creates a unified national framework letting American companies move faster, while the concurrent nuclear push will power the data centers AI requires and bring manufacturing and energy independence home.

He acknowledges that the MAGA coalition's internal opposition is gilded in legitimate conservative concerns — protecting workers from automation, guarding family values against digital overreach, preserving human dignity — and calls these "real issues worth debating," but warns that the resistance risks slowing momentum needed to break free of globalist constraints. He argues the Politico piece "gives away the game": the authors quote at length from critics as authentic populist voices, yet admit they do not know where heavyweights like Vance and Rubio will land. That contrast, he writes, shows the Protectorate knows which "MAGA" voices are useful to its narrative but is uncertain about the real power centers in the administration. He draws two conclusions: that Trump's second term is not the monolithic, perfectly aligned machine some in the Truth Community imagine or fear, and that the globalist system is as uncertain about true alliances and endgame as anyone else — so it is "not so much guiding Narratives as deploying them into the fog of the Info War, hoping to create hesitation and division at a moment when the public mandate for bold action is finally within reach."

## Las Vegas biolab arrest

A further item reported that authorities arrested [[ori-solomon|Ori Solomon]], an Israeli national who managed a Las Vegas short-term rental property where officials discovered what they described as a possible unlicensed biological laboratory, according to *The Washington Times* citing court records. Solomon faces Nevada state felony charges related to hazardous waste disposal and a federal firearms charge alleging unlawful possession as a non-immigrant visa holder; he has not been charged with operating a biolab or handling biological agents. Investigators seized laboratory equipment and more than 1,000 samples of unknown liquids, and Solomon was released pending trial. The investigation is examining links to Chinese nationals, including the property owner, who has been connected in reporting to a prior illegal biolab case in Reedley, California. Solomon holds Israeli and French passports and was in the United States on an E-2 visa, a non-immigrant visa for foreign investors and key employees from treaty countries. The item corresponds to the wiki's coverage of the [[las-vegas-biolab|Las Vegas biolab]] case.
