# Voting Rights Act of 1965

The Voting Rights Act of 1965 is the landmark federal statute enacted to enforce the voting guarantees of the Fifteenth Amendment and to dismantle discriminatory barriers to the franchise. Within the Badlands Media corpus it appears chiefly through Section 11(b), the provision prohibiting intimidation, threats, and coercion directed at voters or those aiding voters.

Two of the three counts in [[naacp-v-useip|NAACP et al. v. USEIP et al.]] were brought under Section 11(b). As described in "Always Fight Back", the plaintiffs relied on a reading of the section advanced in the 2015 NYU Social Justice Law Review article "Voters Strike Back: Litigating against Modern Voter Intimidation," which argued that "section 11(b) was a deliberate attempt to expand the existing laws against voter intimidation, including by eliminating any legal requirement of racial targeting," and that modern intimidation takes "subtler" forms such as aggressive poll-watching, anonymous threats, frivolous registration challenges, and employer coercion.

The essay's author, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], contends that this theory was stretched to cover constitutionally protected activity — questioning election results and door-to-door canvassing — without any objectively intimidating act, and that Judge [[charlotte-sweeney|Charlotte Sweeney]]'s July 2024 ruling rejected the application.
