# 2025 Military Directive Against Drug Cartels

In August 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] secretly signed a directive to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to begin using military force against certain Latin American drug cartels that his administration had designated as terrorist organizations, according to people familiar with the matter cited by the *New York Times*.[1]

## Scope of the order

The decision to bring the American military into the fight was described as the most aggressive step to date in the administration's escalating campaign against the cartels, signaling Trump's willingness to use military forces to carry out what has primarily been considered a law enforcement responsibility to curb the flow of fentanyl and other illegal drugs.[1] The order provided an official basis for the possibility of direct military operations at sea and on foreign soil against the cartels, and US military officials began drawing up options for how to target the groups.[1]

## Mexican rejection

Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] rejected any entry of US forces into [[mexico|Mexico]]: "The United States is not going to come to Mexico with the military. We cooperate, we collaborate, but there is not going to be an invasion. That is ruled out, absolutely ruled out."[1] She added that such action "is not part of any agreement, far from it. When it has been brought up, we have always said no."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the story "weird," noting that Trump "secretly" signed something dispatching the military to Mexico according to anonymous sources, that the story was leaked to the *New York Times*, and that Sheinbaum then responded to the leak — prompting the question of whether Sheinbaum herself was the source, given that the two leaders had spoken a little over a week earlier.[1] She argued that if the United States defeats the cartels on its own soil, sends them back south, and prosecutes their American co-conspirators, Sheinbaum "would be begging Trump for US Military help" — by which point it would be too late and "not our problem anymore."[1]
