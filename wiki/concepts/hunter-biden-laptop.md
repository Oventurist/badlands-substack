# Hunter Biden Laptop

The "Hunter Biden laptop" refers to a computer abandoned at a repair shop whose contents — emails, financial records and personal material belonging to [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] — became the subject of a major dispute over media suppression and intelligence-community intervention after their publication shortly before the 2020 United States presidential election.[1] A group of former Central Intelligence and other intelligence officials publicly cast doubt on the material, characterizing it as bearing the hallmarks of a foreign information operation, a framing that news organizations and social platforms used to limit its circulation.[1]

## Authentication

The Daily Caller News Foundation verified the laptop's contents in October 2020, when a cybersecurity expert authenticated a Hunter Biden email relating to [[burisma|Burisma]].[1] The [[washington-post|Washington Post]] and the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] each acknowledged the authenticity of the laptop's material in March 2022, more than a year later.[1] Subsequent reporting by the Daily Caller and other outlets drew on the material to document the Biden family's extensive dealings with Chinese companies, including a presentation Hunter Biden gave promoting American shale and natural gas to Chinese businessmen.[1]

## Congressional investigation

On 1 March 2023 [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] and [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]] chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] sent 29 letters to the Central Intelligence officials who had discredited the laptop story, stating that the officials had not responded to previously requested interviews.[1] The letters, first obtained by the Daily Caller, reiterated the requests, asked the officials to "comply promptly," and sought both scheduled transcribed interviews and the production of outstanding material.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that "the patriot hammer just might be coming down on DS actors," while cautioning that the extent to which the House Republicans' moves would expose [[deep-state|Deep State]] figures remained unclear.[1] He suspected the letters were "just the beginning of a much longer and coordinated effort to expose corruption," and argued there was a case that the laptop had been left at the repair shop intentionally, making the episode the culmination of a sting operation forming "one thread of a much larger tapestry of sting operations."[1]

## Morris testimony on coordinated censorship

In July 2023, [[emma-jo-morris|Emma-Jo Morris]] — an editor at [[breitbart|Breitbart]] and the former [[new-york-post|New York Post]] editor who handled the original laptop reporting — testified before the [[house-weaponization-committee|House Weaponization Committee]] about the censorship of the story.[2] Her testimony described the coordinated nature of the social media blackout and the potential collusion between federal agencies and media outlets such as [[politico|Politico]] to derail the story as "Russian disinformation" immediately before the 2020 election.[2]

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], which covered the hearing, placed the laptop's "complete erasure from public view" alongside the favorable framing of Black Lives Matter riots and the attempted deplatforming of Zero Hedge over its Wuhan lab coverage as evidence that social media had functioned as a tool for controlling public perception, with government using Big Tech as a "corporate hitman."[2] It noted that congressional investigation had established [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]]'s role in organizing the suppression of the [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]].[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the censorship record as the clearest illustration of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] operating as a [[system-of-systems|System of Systems]].[2]

## Hunter Biden's acknowledgment of ownership (February 2023)

On February 1, 2023, attorneys for Hunter Biden sent letters to state and federal agencies urging investigations into what they described as attempts by close allies of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and others to "weaponize" the laptop's contents.[3] The letters marked the first time Hunter Biden and his legal team publicly acknowledged that the data found on the machine — dropped off at a Delaware electronics repair shop in 2019, according to the shop's owner — was his own personal data.[3] They were sent by his lawyer [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] to the Delaware attorney general, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s National Security Division, and the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]].[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the admission as a signal that "the powers that would be have relinquished a good deal of the previous control they wielded over the Narrative," arguing that the legal team's public acknowledgment of the laptop and its surrounding controversies would prompt "sleeping, slowly-waking fellows" to ask how much else had been hidden from them.[3] He predicted the laptop was "one of many that will be making news in MAJOR ways over the months ahead."[3]

## Use as committee evidence (2023)

[[james-comer|James Comer]] said in July 2023 that Rep. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]]'s display of a sexually explicit image from the laptop, together with plane tickets and payment records, constituted evidence that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] had violated the [[mann-act|Mann Act]].[4] Breitbart journalist [[emma-jo-morris|Emma-Jo Morris]], who first revealed the laptop story in 2020, testified that same week before the House [[house-weaponization-committee|Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]].[4]

## Douglas Wise admission (January 2023)

On January 16, 2023 the New York Post reported that [[douglas-wise|Douglas Wise]], a former deputy director of the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]], had admitted he knew a "significant portion" of the recovered laptop files "had to be real" while nevertheless signing the October 19, 2020 public letter characterizing the Post's reporting as Russian disinformation.[5] Wise was one of 51 former intelligence officials who issued that letter, five days after the Post began its series on Hunter Biden's overseas business dealings, and he said he did not regret dismissing the exposé: "All of us figured that a significant portion of that content had to be real to make any Russian disinformation credible."[5] He had not responded when the Post sought an explanation in March 2022 but spoke to The Australian in January 2023.[5]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the admission as an illustration of [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]], writing that the Hunter Biden laptop was "likely one of the LEAST damaging" of the laptops the community had tracked but the one "most ready for public consumption" in terms of the corruption it allegedly revealed about the nation's would-be first family, and questioning the timing of the admission.[5]

## June 2023 Badlands commentary

Writing amid the [[trump-classified-documents-case|Trump classified documents case]], contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that at least 459 documented crimes — including money laundering and tax fraud — were found on the laptop, and that the [[fbi|FBI]] had possessed the device since December 2019 and knowingly covered it up while the media concentrated on Trump.[6] He also cited [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s own admission that he had given half of his salary to his father for the previous three decades, tying the laptop material to the wider [[joe-biden-bribery-allegation|Biden bribery allegations]].[6]

## BidenLaptopMedia.com archive (2023)

In June 2023, nearly 10,000 photographs from the laptop were uploaded to a new website, [[bidenlaptopmedia-com|BidenLaptopMedia.com]], which was intermittently unavailable after launch because of overwhelming traffic.[7] The site was the work of former Trump White House aide [[garrett-ziegler|Garrett Ziegler]], who had worked as an aide to economic adviser [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]].[7] Ziegler said the project took a couple of months, largely spent reviewing the roughly 10,000 photos and redacting genitalia, and stated that "the number one thing we're about... is truth and transparency."[7] He said that if the American people wanted to know what the first family is like they would get it, and that photos painting the Bidens in a good light would not be removed.[7]

Besides pictures carrying location metadata, the archive linked to emails, suspicious activity reports, material on influence peddling, a transcribed diary attributed to [[ashley-biden|Ashley Biden]], and extensive personal details.[7] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the laptop scandals had remained near the forefront of the information war and that the story refuses to die despite obfuscation by the political class and the media, suggesting that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] himself might play a larger role in the unraveling of his family than many assume.[7]
