# Hunter Biden Laptop

The "Hunter Biden laptop" refers to a computer abandoned by [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] whose contents — principally an email archive, published in searchable form at bidenlaptopemails.com — have been used as documentary evidence in reporting on the Biden family's foreign business dealings.[1]

The archive underpins much of the record of Hunter Biden's relationship with the Ukrainian gas company [[burisma|Burisma]] and with the consulting firm [[blue-star-strategies|Blue Star Strategies]]. Searches for [[sally-painter|Sally Painter]] within it show contact with Hunter Biden dating to 4 March 2010 and her 2011 recruitment of him to the board of the [[truman-national-security-project|Truman National Security Project]].[1] The same archive contains the October–November 2015 thread "Revised Burisma Proposal, Contract and Invoice," in which [[vadym-pozharskyi|Vadym Pozharskyi]] set out the "ultimate purpose" of Burisma's contract with Blue Star as securing influential current and former US policy-makers to "close down for any cases/pursuits against Nikolay in Ukraine" — a reference to [[mykola-zlochevsky|Mykola Zlochevsky]].[1]

Further messages in the collection document [[eric-schwerin|Eric Schwerin]]'s confirmation that US officials' names were deliberately left out of the written scope, [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]]'s requests for calls between the Blue Star and Burisma teams, a 2 December 2015 conference call between Blue Star and [[white-house|the White House]] about the Vice President's Ukraine trip, and a 2016 message to [[rosemont-seneca|Rosemont Seneca]] noting "No help from our friend(s) however."[1]

## NewsGuard's handling of the story

When the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] published its October 2020 report on the laptop, [[newsguard|NewsGuard]] cast immediate doubt on it, with co-CEO [[steven-brill|Steven Brill]] calling the story a "hoax" in interviews and echoing the establishment framing that it was Russian disinformation.[2] After the New York Times and other outlets confirmed the laptop's authenticity in 2022, NewsGuard downgraded [[the-daily-beast|The Daily Beast]] from 87.5/100 to 57/100 while Axios, BuzzFeed News, USA Today and the Washington Post kept perfect 100/100 scores despite peddling the same skepticism - an asymmetry Badlands Media's "The Information Imperium" presents as selective credibility enforcement.[2]

## Cited as an example of shifting misinformation labels (2024)

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] used the Hunter Biden laptop story as his leading illustration of how the misinformation of yesterday becomes the accepted fact of today.[3] Arguing against [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]]'s claim that there is no guarantee of free speech for misinformation or hate speech, DeLarme wrote that under such a rule the reporters who actually decided to cover the Hunter Biden laptop fiasco in 2020 would have been criminally charged — as would anyone questioning the logic of social distancing, the safety of masking children all day, or the proposition that COVID-19 originated in a Wuhan laboratory rather than a wet market.[3]

## Allegations cited in Badlands commentary (January 2024)

A January 2024 Badlands feature by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] referenced the laptop material as the source of allegations that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] had a strange relationship with his niece, an ongoing relationship with his late brother's wife, and numerous prostitutes written off on his taxes.[4] Carlson invoked these claims alongside the [[ashley-biden|Ashley Biden]] diary rumors as reputational context in a piece otherwise focused on Biden-family proximity to [[little-st-james|Little St. James]].[4]
