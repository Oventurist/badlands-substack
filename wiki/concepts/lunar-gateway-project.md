# Lunar Gateway Project

The Lunar Gateway Project is a planned lunar-orbiting space station, intended to be co-created by Japan, Europe, the United States, and Russia, and to serve as a strategic component of human space exploration architecture beyond low Earth orbit.[1]

The gateway concept was formalized in a joint statement signed by [[nasa|NASA]] and [[roscosmos|Roscosmos]] on September 27, 2017, which stated that both agencies "see the gateway as a strategic component of human space exploration architecture that warrants additional study," with NASA having already engaged industry partners in gateway concept studies.[1] NASA spokesman Michael Gold later said of Russian involvement in the [[artemis-accords|Artemis]] framework, "As we look at the Russian cooperation on Artemis, I expect a great deal of it. It may center around the gateway."[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that Russia had already begun cooperating on the Artemis program through the gateway, contradicting Western mainstream media narratives that the accords excluded Russia.[1] He writes that "within seconds of [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s entry into the White House," Russia left the Lunar Gateway Project, which now "sits stagnant on a drawing board," while Russia raced to embrace China's lunar program.[1] NASA subsequently negotiated with the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] to provide an airlock for the gateway.[1]
