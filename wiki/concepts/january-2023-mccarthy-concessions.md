# January 2023 McCarthy Concessions

The January 2023 McCarthy concessions were the package of House rules changes and committee assignments that [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] granted to conservative holdouts in order to win the speakership on the fifteenth ballot on Saturday, 7 January 2023.[1]

## Badlands framing: the "overdrive" inflection point

Badlands Media contributor Erik Carlson treats 1/7/23 as the precise moment the political tempo of the era shifted "from a jogging pace to a sprint."[1] He explicitly rules out the earlier candidate events — [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s April 2022 announcement that he would buy [[twitter|Twitter]], the August 2022 Mar-a-Lago raid, the death of the Queen in September, Musk's completed purchase in late October, the Republican House victory in November, the hammer attack on Paul Pelosi, the Ye controversy, the Balenciaga story, the collapse of FTX, and even the first release of the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]].[1] In his account the change was not McCarthy's election as such — that was expected once Republicans took the House — but the moment McCarthy demonstrated he would "play ball" and not obstruct [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s agenda.[1] Carlson notes a [[qanon|Q]] post dated 1/7/18 containing "win" and "15" in brackets, inviting readers to draw their own conclusions from the fifteen-ballot outcome.[1]

## The concessions

The reported concessions included:[1]

- Any single member may call for a [[motion-to-vacate|motion to vacate]] the Speaker's chair, replacing the prior five-member threshold.
- A McCarthy-aligned super PAC agreed not to intervene in open Republican primaries in safe seats, which Carlson identifies as a major problem in the 2020 cycle.
- Floor votes on key conservative bills, including a balanced budget amendment, congressional term limits, and border security.
- Any effort to raise the debt ceiling must be paired with spending cuts.
- The twelve appropriations bills must move individually rather than as an omnibus, making it harder to conceal government waste and corruption.
- Greater Freedom Caucus representation on committees, including the [[house-rules-committee|House Rules Committee]].
- Discretionary spending capped at fiscal 2022 levels, lowering both defense and domestic budgets.
- A seventy-two hour window to review bills before floor consideration.
- Expanded member ability to offer floor amendments.
- Creation of an investigative committee on the "weaponization" of the federal government, realized as the [[select-subcommittee-on-the-weaponization-of-the-federal-government|weaponization subcommittee]].
- Restoration of the [[holman-rule|Holman rule]], allowing the salaries of individual federal officials to be reduced.

## Committee payoffs to the holdouts

Carlson reproduces the assignments received by the twenty-one holdouts, all members or allies of the [[house-freedom-caucus|House Freedom Caucus]]: [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] kept Judiciary and Oversight and chaired the Judiciary subcommittee on Crime and Federal Government Surveillance; [[dan-bishop|Dan Bishop]] stayed on Judiciary and Homeland Security and joined the weaponization subcommittee; [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] won a seat on Oversight and Accountability while remaining on Natural Resources; [[josh-brecheen|Josh Brecheen]] won Homeland Security and Budget; [[mike-cloud|Mike Cloud]] won [[house-appropriations-committee|Appropriations]] and the [[select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|Covid origins select committee]]; [[andrew-clyde|Andrew Clyde]] joined Appropriations; [[eli-crane|Eli Crane]] joined [[house-homeland-security-committee|Homeland Security]]; [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]] was named the speaker's designee on the Steering Committee and won a seat on [[house-financial-services-committee|Financial Services]]; [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] stayed on Judiciary and joined the weaponization subcommittee; [[bob-good|Bob Good]] took Budget and Education and Workforce; [[paul-gosar|Paul Gosar]] chaired the Natural Resources subcommittee on Oversight and Investigations; [[andy-harris|Andy Harris]], a physician, stayed on Appropriations and chaired the Agriculture, Rural Development, FDA subcommittee; [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]] won Oversight and Natural Resources; [[mary-miller|Mary Miller]] remained on Agriculture; [[ralph-norman|Ralph Norman]] was named one of nine Republicans on Rules and added Budget to Financial Services; [[andy-ogles|Andy Ogles]] won Financial Services; [[scott-perry|Scott Perry]], the Freedom Caucus chairman who brokered the deal, kept [[house-foreign-affairs-committee|Foreign Affairs]] and won a new Oversight seat; [[matt-rosendale|Matt Rosendale]] stayed on Natural Resources; [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]], a co-negotiator, took Budget and Rules while keeping Judiciary; [[keith-self|Keith Self]] joined Foreign Affairs; and [[victoria-spartz|Victoria Spartz]] remained on Judiciary.[1]

Carlson stresses that many of these members were freshmen who nevertheless received significant assignments, that non-holdout Trump ally [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] was placed on Homeland Security, and that [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] were stripped of important committee seats.[1] He argues the committees involved map directly onto the live controversies of the moment: agriculture and farmland purchases by China and [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], energy dependence under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], federal surveillance, border and trafficking enforcement, Biden family corruption, Covid-19 origins, financial crimes, and January 6.[1]

## Aftermath narrative

Carlson catalogues the events he attributes to the post-concession period: the discovery beginning 9 January 2023 of classified documents in Biden's University of Pennsylvania office, his garage beside his Corvette, and a residence [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] reportedly rented from his father for $50,000 a month; Chinese spy balloons and the shootdown of hobbyist objects with $400,000 missiles; fires at egg and food production facilities; toxic train derailments; additions to the [[clinton-body-count|Clinton body count]], including a plane crash killing five environmental consultants departing Clinton National Airport in Little Rock after investigating an Ohio toxic spill; and evidence implicating Biden and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] in the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream pipeline sabotage]].[1] He predicts the Bidens will be made the fall guys, and that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] will be thrown under the bus over Covid while Big Pharma's profits survive with only the revolving door closed.[1]

Carlson closes with numerological observations — the S&P 500's March 2009 bottom at 666, and [[tesla|Tesla]] stock falling roughly 66.6% between Musk's 14 April 2022 announcement and 1/7/23 before rising about 86% afterward — which he reads as a sign the cabal has lost control, and with a Goodfellas analogy in which the mob begins killing the Lufthansa Heist underlings, noting the heist took place at JFK International.[1]
