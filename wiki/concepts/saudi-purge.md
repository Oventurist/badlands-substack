# Saudi Purge

The "Saudi Purge" refers to the November 2017 anti-corruption sweep in Saudi Arabia, in which [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] detained scores of princes, ministers and businessmen. Within Badlands commentary it is treated as a foundational event — described by contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] as "the biggest Q proof of all time" and as heralding the Golden Age to come.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

In this reading, the purge was never mere theater for Western applause or "performative anti-corruption cosplay," but rather a proof-of-concept and "delayed-fuse prototype" for what a sovereign leader could achieve once the Deep State's puppets were cut loose and national wealth was redirected away from propping up Treasury bonds for a decaying unipolar empire and toward independence and abundance creation.[1]

Q drops are said to have aligned with the purge in real time, signaling that the Kingdom's "great drainage" would mirror — and in fact precede — America's own, described as a temporal pincer movement in which the Saudis moved first while [[donald-trump|Trump]], constrained by the theatrical necessities of the 2020 election and subsequent chaos, prepared the American battlefield.[1] In this framing the delays were features rather than bugs: the purge bought cycles and "reclaimed bricks for the golden path" while the American collective mind endured its own engineered fracture.[1]

## Closing of the circuit

Badlands commentary holds that the circuit closed during Trump's second term, days after the November 2025 Washington summit, when MBS arrived not as supplicant or junior partner but as benefactor and co-architect of abundance.[1] The Kingdom's previously promised $600 billion in US investments was upgraded, with capital directed toward advanced manufacturing in the American heartland, gas turbines in Greenville, South Carolina, critical-minerals joint ventures intended to break China's rare-earth stranglehold, and civilian nuclear partnerships restarting American reactors and building small modular systems.[1]

Trump's dismissal of the [[jamal-khashoggi|Khashoggi]] question — "A lot of people didn't like that gentleman" — is presented as sealing an inversion in which the journalist is recast as a Brotherhood-adjacent operative while the Crown Prince emerges elevated, his [[vision-2030|Vision 2030]] platform reframed from desert fantasy to working blueprint.[1] Saudi Arabia is thereby described as "the single greatest accelerator of the Golden Age transition we have yet identified."[1]

## Contemporary Q commentary

Chapter 12 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series, covering drops posted on November 5, 2017, addresses the purge as it was unfolding, noting that this was the period in which the Saudi royal family was being swept and [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] was consolidating power, with [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] among those arrested.[2] The chapter argues that the [[las-vegas-shooting|Las Vegas shooting]] preceded the purge and followed [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s visit to the Kingdom, and that an assassination of the Crown Prince would therefore have been of great value to the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[2]

Drop #95 is read as stating that what happened in Saudi Arabia will happen in the United States — a domestic purge of corruption — and the author remarks on the coincidence that the drop is dated November 5, the same calendar date as the 2024 U.S. election, while doubting that the American cleanup would arrive on that day.[2] Q's accompanying instruction to keep digging and to organise findings into graphics, with "critical" set in parentheses, is interpreted as an emphasis on staying organised and prepared.[2] The chapter closes by describing the Hillary–Saudi connection as a very deep line of inquiry that Q was encouraging researchers to pursue.[2]

## Chapter 14 treatment

Chapter 14 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series returns to the purge through drop #99, in which Q asks who was arrested in Saudi Arabia and instructs readers to define their backgrounds.[3] The chapter answers that the round-up extended well beyond the royal family to figures from the military, business and media sectors, with well over 300 people swept up in what it calls a swift and complete cleaning out of the country.[3] The common thread, the author argues, is that every detainee held power over the direction and rule of Saudi Arabia, and all of them were taken down at once.[3]

[[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Al-Waleed]] heads the list of detained royals and is described as vastly important — the figure Trump and the drops call "Dopey," at one point the largest shareholder of [[twitter|Twitter]], and a funder of Barack Obama's rise to power.[3] The chapter notes that some of those arrested held dual U.S.–Saudi citizenship and that a few had ties to [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], which it connects to the earlier Q reference "HUMA" — read not as Huma Abedin but as the Harvard University Muslim Alumni, an organisation with chapters and foundations nationwide that the author says are funded by [[george-soros|Soros]].[3]

On the question of who was killed, the chapter distinguishes refuted reports of Prince Aziz bin Fahd's death while resisting from the confirmed death of [[muqrin-bin-abdulaziz|Prince Muqrin]] in a helicopter crash.[3] Reading drop #259's claim that seven in ten plane crashes are targeted kills, the author concludes the helicopter was shot down as Muqrin fled the clean-up, and that [[white-hats|white hats]] were behind it — a response to the [[las-vegas-shooting|Las Vegas shooting]] a month earlier and framed alongside the ballistic missile fired from Yemen at Riyadh the day before the crackdown, which was intercepted.[3] Q emphasises that nothing is as it seems, and tells readers the same purge will happen in the United States.[3]

## Chapter 18 treatment

Chapter 18 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series, covering drops #118 and #119, returns to the purge by way of Q's question about why events in Saudi Arabia are relevant, and answers that some of those taken down likely had their hands in the [[september-11-attacks|9/11]] pie — information the author expects to be revealed at some point during a second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[4] The chapter cites a Gateway Pundit report linking several of the top Saudi officials arrested that weekend to the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]], noting that the Saudis had extensive business dealings with the firm and that the firm subsequently closed for business.[4] Q's pairing of human trafficking with the purge leads the author to assume that [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed]] most likely had his hands in that sector as well, and to conclude that the consequences of the 2017 purge have barely begun to roll downhill.[4]

## Drop #111 and the severed pipeline

Chapter 16 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series devotes an entire installment to [[q-drop-111|drop #111]] of November 5, 2017, in which Q asks "what just happened in SA."[6] The chapter answers that a major crackdown and purge of corruption in the Saudi royal family and government had just taken place, that [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]] was among those arrested and had his funds seized, and that the endless pipeline of corrupt money flowing from Saudi Arabia into the United States government was thereby cut off.[6] The events are presented as important because they severed the control levied on the American government by the Saudi royal family and, by extension, the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]].[6]

The chapter also links the purge to an unannounced October 2017 trip to Saudi Arabia by [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], reported by Politico, with speculation that he met [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], the figure who ordered the takedown.[6] It further claims that [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s last tweet before the purge was a coded green light for the operation, and that the [[2017-trump-twitter-account-deactivation|eleven-minute deactivation of his Twitter account]] on November 3, 2017 was an attempt to prevent that message from going out.[6]

## Chapter 10 commentary on drops #72–#80

Chapter 10 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series, covering drops #72 through #80 of November 4–5, 2017, presents the purge as the central subject of that block of drops.[5] In that reading Q indicated that the United States was not the primary target of the operation then under way and that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] was, with the [[house-of-saud|Saudi Royal Family]], the Clintons and the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]] later named as the three main families being taken down by [[white-hats|white hats]] at that time.[5] Earlier drops appearing to promise imminent arrests of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] are characterised as red herrings and markers — deliberate [[disinformation|disinformation]] that left the [[deep-state|Deep State]] expecting its own arrests while the corrupt establishment within the Saudi Royal Family was in fact about to fall.[5]

Drop #73 asks what Trump's last tweet prior to the purge was; the answer given is a tweet concerning [[saudi-aramco|ARAMCO]], read superficially as a signal of American willingness to work with the Saudis and, on the deeper reading, as approval for the operation about to take place.[5] Drops #76 through #78 are reposts of earlier Saudi material, which the chapter interprets as Q signalling that anons had still not fully grasped the situation.[5] The chapter treats Mohammed bin Salman as having cleared out the corruption within the Royal Family, and argues on that basis that Saudi Arabia has since played a vital role in restraining escalation in the Middle East.[5] Q's [[alice-and-wonderland|"Alice & Wonderland"]] cipher, resolved in drop #80, identifies the Kingdom as the "Bloody Wonderland" of the sequence.[5]
