# Hunter Biden Special Counsel Appointment

The **Hunter Biden special counsel appointment** refers to Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]]'s decision on Friday, August 11, 2023, to elevate Delaware U.S. Attorney [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] to special counsel in the long-running federal investigation of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], the son of President Joe Biden.[1] Garland described the matter as involving "extraordinary circumstances," and the move was widely characterized as a surprise that intensified the probe ahead of the 2024 presidential election.[1]

## Background

Weiss had already been investigating Hunter Biden's financial dealings for years before the appointment, and he personally asked to be named special counsel, a designation that granted him broad authority to investigate and to report out his findings.[1] The elevation came only after plea deal talks in the case broke down, collapsing an arrangement that had been close to resolving the matter just weeks earlier.[1]

## Charges and context

At the time of the appointment the case against Hunter Biden centered on tax evasion and a federal gun charge.[1] The unraveling of the negotiated resolution raised fresh questions about the durability of the government's case and about what additional exposure the president's son might face.[1]

The appointment landed while the [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was simultaneously taking the unprecedented step of indicting former President Donald Trump — President Biden's chief rival in the 2024 race — in two separate cases, placing questions about the Biden family at the forefront of the campaign.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the parallel tracks were not coincidental, framing them as simultaneous storylines: what he called the "canned hunt" of Trump by the Justice Department on one side, and the "cover-up" of Biden family corruption on the other.[1] He contended that these developments matched what the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] and [[qanon|Q]] followers had been predicting for years, and criticized the reflexive pessimism such moves provoke among that same audience.[1] In his reading, the collapse of Hunter's plea deal was not a defeat because it left the president's son newly exposed to potential Foreign Agents Registration Act liability, and Trump's trial would in effect become his campaign.[1]
