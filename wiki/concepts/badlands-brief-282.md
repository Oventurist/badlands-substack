# Badlands Brief: Jesus Walks

"Jesus Walks" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary digest published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated April 6, 2026 and timed to Easter. The edition opens with the greeting "Happy Easter! Christ has risen!" and carries contributions from [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]. Its six segments cover the return of Kanye West to live performance, escalating American ultimatums against [[iran|Iran]] over the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], the strain the war has placed on [[nato|NATO]], NASA's Artemis II lunar flyby, an admitted U.S. attempt to arm Iranian protesters, and inconclusive ballistics in the [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] assassination case.

## Kanye West's Easter concerts

The Brief reports that Kanye West, also known as Ye, performed a sold-out concert at SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles before roughly 70,000 fans, his first major U.S. show in about five years. The performance came months after the release of a song titled "Heil Hitler" and weeks after West issued a public apology for antisemitic remarks. Lauryn Hill joined West on stage in what the source describes as their first live collaboration. According to figures the Brief attributes to Bloomberg, the Friday night show grossed about $18 million and the two nights together about $33 million; the Brief characterises the $18 million figure as the highest revenue ever generated by a single performer's concert. West was scheduled to headline all three nights of the Wireless Festival in London in July as part of a European tour, a booking that drew criticism over his past antisemitic statements. Pepsi and Diageo withdrew sponsorship from the festival, and British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] called the decision to book West "deeply concerning."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the timing as deliberately Christian: West mounted his comeback across Good Friday and Easter Sunday, "arguably the two most important days on the Christian calendar: The day Christ died; and the day He was resurrected." The commentary notes the stage design — a giant half-sphere lit to appear to be spinning — and West's choice to perform "Jesus Walks" on Good Friday. That song appeared on West's 2004 debut album, released, the Brief observes, two weeks after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] debuted the first episode of *The Apprentice*. Its lyrics are read as describing how [[hollywood|Hollywood]] despises Jesus to the point that mentioning Him invites censorship from radio and career destruction.

The author extends this claim, arguing that Hollywood and "the entire [[deep-state|Deep State]] Establishment" hate Jesus Christ and have tried to erase Him from American culture, citing the treatment of [[mel-gibson|Mel Gibson]] over *The Passion of The Christ*, also released in 2004. Gibson and West are placed together in "the pantheon of modern heroes" for creating works that glorified God in spite of what the piece calls the Satanic cabal controlling American culture; West's 2019 album *Jesus is King* is described as the most commercially successful gospel album of all time.

The segment acknowledges that many readers, particularly self-identified Christians and Christian Zionists, refuse to extend West grace given his public behaviour. The Brief's counter-argument is that West showed "immense courage" in standing alone against Satan in Hollywood, risking hundreds of millions of dollars in net worth and being smeared as an antisemite. It recounts that what provoked West's "Defcon Three" remarks about "the Jews" was the backlash to his October 2022 appearance at Paris Fashion Week alongside Candace Owens in shirts reading "White Lives Matter." The author interprets that stunt as drawing attention to what he calls psychological warfare operations aimed at eradicating white people in North America and Europe, and as an assertion that all lives matter equally. West's own account, as relayed, is that he suffered personal anguish at the hands of a network of "Jewish Hollywood lawyers," some working directly for him, who threatened his business interests and then called for his cancellation.

Concluding, the Brief says West has clarified that he is not a Nazi and offered reconciliation with the Jewish people, but will be remembered as "the force of personality that broke the seal on the Israel Question," and that whatever demons he battles, God has used him as a vessel to spread the gospel to young people and to challenge the status quo regarding [[israel|Israel]].

## Trump's Hormuz ultimatum

The second segment reports that the United States and Iran were weighing a proposed framework for an immediate ceasefire as tensions escalated over the Strait of Hormuz. Trump said on Sunday that the United States would begin targeting Iranian power plants and bridges starting Tuesday evening if Iran did not reopen the waterway, setting a deadline of 8:00 p.m. Eastern Time after extending a prior ultimatum. Simultaneously, a proposal assembled by [[pakistan|Pakistan]] was shared with both Washington and Tehran outlining a two-phase plan: an immediate ceasefire and reopening of the strait, formalised through a memorandum of understanding, followed by negotiations on a broader settlement. Iran had not agreed to the proposal as the deadline approached.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's commentary observes that Trump was "praising Allah and dropping F bombs on Easter, while promoting his peace ultimatum deadline like a pay-per-view fight night," and calls the situation "total discombobulation." He describes the weekend as goofy: on Saturday a number of U.S. military aircraft were reportedly shot down in or around Iranian airspace, followed by a large rescue operation for the pilots of an F-15. He judges this "one of the fakest" of the available narratives, noting that all the planes and helicopters were purportedly shot down within hours of each other, and that Trump followed the episode with "cartoonish TrumpaMania rhetoric" while telling [[fox-news|Fox News]] in the same breath that [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and the Iranians were close to a deal that could be in place as soon as Monday.

The conflict is dated to February 28, 2026, when U.S. and Israeli forces launched strikes against Iran, straining transatlantic relations and raising European concerns about the war's legality. He contrasts Russia and China operating from "positions of strategic depth and industrial leverage" with a United States that retains reserves, production capacity and a renewed mass psychological mandate to accelerate a domestic renaissance of drilling, nuclear revival and fusion acceleration, while "the Collectorate convulses." ## Artemis II

The Brief summarises [[nasa|NASA]]'s release of the first images of Earth captured during Artemis II, showing the planet from lunar distance. Artemis II is the first crewed mission to travel around the Moon since Apollo and is designed as a flyby rather than a landing, carrying four astronauts testing spacecraft systems. NASA and partners made mission tracking tools publicly available, and the mission includes new onboard technologies, among them a redesigned space toilet intended for long-duration spaceflight.

Ashe in America's commentary reports that the toilet allegedly broke again over the weekend and earned a Space.com feature on Sunday, quoting: "As the four astronauts of NASA's Artemis 2 mission to the moon close in on their historic lunar flyby — the first by a human crew in more than 53 years — an unusual star has risen aboard their Orion moon ship. But it's not an astronaut — it's the space toilet. That moon loo has been part of the Artemis 2 conversation since day one of the mission, when its urine function went offline and was fixed by mission specialist Christina Koch, with guidance from Mission Control." She objects that the device is not a "moon loo" since it is not on the Moon, asks whether it is strange that the toilet is stealing the thunder of a historic lunar flyby, and questions why the mission is not landing and walking. She jokes about a "wellness check on the camera man they left behind in 1969," picturing him "like Number One from Umbrella Academy."

## Charlie Kirk ballistics

The Brief reports, citing PBS NewsHour, that a forensic analysis of bullet evidence in the case involving the suspected killer of Charlie Kirk produced inconclusive results, meaning investigators could neither match nor exclude the suspect on ballistic testing; experts cited said such outcomes are common and do not amount to clearing a suspect. The summary also references legislative activity in Idaho reported by the Idaho Statesman, and the official autopsy of Martin Luther King Jr, which determined he died of a single gunshot wound.

Ashe in America quotes a March 27 court filing by lawyers for Tyler Robinson stating that the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives was "unable to identify the bullet recovered at autopsy to the rifle allegedly tied to Mr. Robinson." She notes that the inconclusive finding produced weekend reports that "the bullet didn't match Tyler Robinson's gun," which fact checkers clarified was non-determinative either way, "but that was lost in the noise."

Preparing an America 250 segment on the anniversary of King's death, she was struck that the bullet that killed King was allegedly a .30-06. She cites commenter Chris Paul asking whether Charlie Kirk is "the 'conservative' Martin Luther King Jr in 30 years? Seems like a reboot," and a commenter called Fred who supplied an analysis of King's autopsy: a single bullet entered the right side of King's face about an inch right of and half an inch below his mouth, fractured his jaw, exited the lower face, reentered through the neck, severed multiple vital arteries, fractured the spine in several places and came to rest beneath the skin on the left side of the back, travelling downward and rearward. The recovered projectile was a deformed .30-06 rifle slug designated exhibit Q64, found beneath the skin near the left shoulder blade; a Remington 760 Gamemaster rifle designated Q2 was recovered near the scene. When the House Select Committee on Assassinations convened a firearms panel, the experts found the bullet's class characteristics matched the rifle — both six grooves and six lands with a right twist — but concluded they were "unable positively to identify or eliminate the Q64 bullet as having been fired from the Q2 rifle." Ashe draws the parallel that matching bullet to rifle "proved far more difficult than investigators hoped" in both assassinations "of prominent liberty activists at transformational moments of populism in America," and notes both inquiries found no evidence of U.S. government agency participation, to which she replies: "Sure."
