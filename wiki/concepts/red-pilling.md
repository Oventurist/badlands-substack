# Red-pilling

"Red-pilling" is the term used within the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] milieu for the process by which a person comes to reject the mainstream narrative and adopt a dissident understanding of political and institutional power. The metaphor derives from the 1999 film *The Matrix*, in which the protagonist chooses a red pill to see reality as it is.

In "President Trump's Call to Arms," [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] described red-pilling as accelerating worldwide following the [[2023-trump-cpac-speech|2023 Trump CPAC speech]], writing that "people are being red-pilled across the world, from Twitter to TikTok, at family gatherings, online, offline, and beyond, as the Truth has grown impossible to escape."[1] He argued that concepts "once difficult to comprehend become more and more believable to the masses" as each successive revelation lands, and that claims that were shocking in 2015, 2016 and 2017 had become "accepted fact" by 2023.[1]

Getz offered his own conversion as a case study: a supporter of the Bush family for nearly two decades, he found [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2016 primary demolition of [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] and the Republican establishment "jarring to behold," but described it as having "laid the groundwork for my true Awakening."[1] He similarly cited a 2017 [[qanon|Q]] suggestion that 70% of elected politicians were corrupt as "a tall statement at the time, but after all we've seen since 2015, easily believable," noting that whereas the public had previously known only of Democratic machinations involving [[barack-obama|Obama]] and [[george-soros|Soros]], Trump had "drew out the 70% like snakes" on the Republican side as well.[1]

## Barriers to awakening

In "The Truth," Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] examined why red-pilling succeeds with some people and fails with others, arguing that intelligence alone is insufficient because "there are plenty of smart people who live their lives believing lies."[2] He assigned a larger role to psychology and ego: some hearers can discard prior beliefs without injury, while others "reject the truth because the idea of being wrong or being fooled is too much for their ego to handle."[2] Others simply find the effort excessive — declining to read past the first search result or to weigh whether an author has a paid or ideological agenda, as with the unsurprising fact that Pfizer employees "tend to be pro-vaccine."[2]

Carlson also identified material cost as a barrier, noting that accepting a disfavored truth can mean losing a job, a relationship, social status, or access to public life, and likened the resulting calculation to the psychology of cult members who weigh the costs of leaving rather than the merits of the arguments presented to them.[2] He sketched a profile of the person most likely to accept truth: innate intelligence and discernment, self-confidence sufficient to stand alone, courage and willingness to sacrifice, and maturity paired with belief in God.[2]

Describing his own conversion, Carlson wrote that the [[qanon|Q]] drops were "like a bucket of cold water being thrown on me," after which he could never "go back to sleep again," and that he began telling people about the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the Illuminati bloodlines and [[false-flag-operation|false flags]] until he overwhelmed them.[2] He concluded that people who are inundated tend to "go right back to sleep again after being overwhelmed by the media's obfuscation," and that awakening "can't be forced; it can't be sped up" — people wake at their own pace.[2]

## The pandemic as a mass red pill

[[pepe-lives-matter|Pepe Lives Matter]]'s December 7, 2022 essay "Thrive in the Transition" argued that the 2020 "Plandemic" backfired on its architects by functioning as an unintended mass red pill: without that demonstration of tyranny, he wrote, many of his readers would never have begun to question anything.[3] Isolated at home over a virus he characterised as having an over 99 percent survival rate and an available cure, people sensed something was wrong, started researching for themselves, and then could not stop digging — the stage he said all reachable people need to reach.[3]

The essay allowed that not every red pill sinks in with each person in the same manner, but held that once the truth latches on, the experience resembles Neo opening his "real" eyes for the first time in *The Matrix*, and that once a mind awakens it never goes back to sleep.[3] It also distinguished audiences, arguing that the daily "precipice" narratives are engineered to bring the normie and the half-awake to their own red pill moment and are not what the already-awakened patriot should be consuming — the masses required a "red pill enema" that the [[anons|anon]] no longer needed.[3] See [[thriving-in-the-transition|Thriving in the Transition]].[3]
