# Havana Syndrome

Havana Syndrome is the common name for a set of "Anomalous Health Incidents" (AHIs) reported by US intelligence officers, diplomats and government officials, involving serious neurological harm attributed by some victims to energy weapons.[1]

## Whistleblower allegations

In late December 2024, a whistleblower alleged that the [[cia]] and other intelligence agencies had continually downplayed or outright denied the causes and effects of AHIs.[1] In an interview with journalist [[catherine-herridge|Catherine Herridge]], a former CIA officer using the pseudonym "Alice" detailed how she and many other intelligence agents and government officials were targeted by energy weapons that caused serious neurological harm.[1] Of particular interest in the account was the claim that the CIA denied suggestions that at least some of the AHI attacks were carried out by Russia — described by the reporting as a cover-up of foreign involvement.[1]

## Official position

The [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] released a report in March 2023 concluding that it was "very unlikely" that foreign adversaries caused these incidents.[1] The Badlands News Brief carried the whistleblower story as a bonus item under the framing of intelligence-community gaslighting.[1]

## Revised intelligence assessments (January 2025)

A report released by the [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] in January 2025 hinted that a "foreign actor" might be responsible for Havana Syndrome symptoms afflicting hundreds of US personnel and veterans.[2] While most of the intelligence community continued to judge it "very unlikely" that an adversary was linked to the ailment, two of seven agencies diverged from that consensus for the first time, concluding there was roughly a 50/50 chance that foreign actors could have developed the radiofrequency technology associated with the syndrome.[2] Sources told The Atlantic that one of the agencies that changed its assessment was the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], and that its revised evaluation rested on "intercepted communications" linked to a foreign actor.[2] The shift was regarded as a major development for sufferers who had felt dismissed and gaslit by the government for years and who believe their debilitating symptoms were caused by concealed energy weapons.[2]
