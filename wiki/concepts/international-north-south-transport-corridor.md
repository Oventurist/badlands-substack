# International North-South Transportation Corridor

The International North-South Transportation Corridor (INSTC) is a multimodal rail, road and sea freight route launched in 2002 as a joint Indian–Iranian–Russian program, and revived with new energy in recent years.[1] It is described as one of the keystone projects driving the multipolar development agenda associated with the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]].[1]

## Scope and participants

The INSTC enjoys the cooperation of twelve participating nations.[1] Its northern extension has recently been moved from St Petersburg further north to the port of Lavna in Murmansk, Russia.[1] China's western "middle corridor" branch of the east–west [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], running through Xinjiang, features several rail and road corridors that tie directly into the INSTC, alongside Arctic far-eastern connections.[1]

## Strategic significance

Commonly framed by geopoliticians as an "anti-BRI" mega-project, the INSTC is better understood as a sister program to the BRI serving Eurasian, Southwest Asian and African industrial growth.[1] When fully completed, it will circumvent the NATO-controlled Mediterranean zone and the congested Suez Canal route, cutting roughly ten days of transit and about 40 percent of transportation costs relative to the current Suez route.[1]

## Related Arctic development

The corridor is linked in the same program to Russian Arctic development, including the announced construction of five major Arctic cities of up to one million people each under the direction of [[sergey-shoigu|Sergey Shoigu]], and the China-Russia Arctic Research Center established under a 2019 scientific cooperation agreement as part of the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]].[1]
