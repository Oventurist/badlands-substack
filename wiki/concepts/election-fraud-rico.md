# Election Fraud RICO

The "election fraud RICO" is the term Badlands writers use for a hypothesized federal racketeering case addressing the systemic manufacture of election outcomes, as distinct from the prosecution of individual voter fraud.[1]

## Distinction from voter fraud prosecutions

In May 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] publicized a wave of individual election-crime cases: multiple criminal matters in New Jersey, including a former mayoral candidate who admitted forging voter registration applications and a separate federal case alleging that non-citizens voted in federal elections and made false statements about eligibility.[1] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] had been investigating election fraud in Franklin County, Ohio since January, according to public records released in the spring.[1] Additional investigations and court orders were reported in Georgia, Michigan, Wyoming, and California.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that while such prosecutions are warranted, "prosecuting voter fraud in fake elections won't give us real elections," and that volume of individual cases should not be mistaken for progress.[1] The underlying claim is that the system itself "is designed to defraud the voters and fabricate their consent," so that only a racketeering case reaching the architecture of the system — the election fraud RICO — is decisive.[1]

## Countervailing developments

Not all 2026 developments were read as favorable. In New Mexico, a county clerk committed a misdemeanor violation of election law by releasing results early, but the state declined to bring charges, describing the matter instead as "a teachable moment for all county clerks."[1] The Pennsylvania Department of State issued a directive in April 2026 prohibiting counties from cooperating with federal election investigations.[1] In Washington state, hundreds of ballots spanning multiple election cycles were reportedly found in a Renton dumpster.[1] Badlands classified Wyoming and New Jersey as encouraging and Pennsylvania and New Mexico as obstructive.[1]

## Official posture

Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] told Fox News that the department had "been very aggressively going after anybody in this country who is in any way voting illegally," calling it a priority of the Justice Department.[1] Badlands linked the RICO framing to the broader thesis that there can be no "golden age without a justice phase and real accountability."[1]
