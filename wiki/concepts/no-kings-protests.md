# No Kings Protests

"No Kings" is the branding used for a nationwide series of demonstrations organized against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his administration's immigration enforcement campaign, promoted through the Mobilize event platform.[1]

## June 2025 mobilization

As the [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-enforcement-protests|Los Angeles anti-ICE unrest]] spread in June 2025, "No Kings" events were scheduled in cities across the country, including one in San Antonio.[1] Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] announced on Tuesday, June 10 that members of the [[texas-national-guard|Texas National Guard]] would be deployed to various locations in the state ahead of the planned protests that week.[1]

The demonstrations formed part of a broader wave of protests against [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and the Trump administration that reached New York, Chicago, Seattle, Denver, San Francisco and Atlanta, arriving as ICE raced to meet the [[white-house|White House]]'s immigration arrest quotas.[1]

## June 2025 "Summer of Love Kick Off"

Writing on June 13, 2025, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|9th Circuit]]'s stay meant that for what she called "the No Kings Summer of Love Kick Off," the [[national-guard|National Guard]] remained under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s control.[2] She argued that "the regime wants the optics of Trump as George III," and predicted that protest-rioters would attempt to provoke a photographic response fitting that frame.[2]

## Connection to the Minnesota shootings

The June 14, 2025 "No Kings" protests took place in cities nationwide the same day President Trump presided over the [[2025-army-250th-birthday-military-parade|Army 250th birthday military parade]], with critics of the parade calling Trump a "king."[4] Following the [[2025-minnesota-lawmaker-shootings|targeted shootings of two Minnesota lawmakers]] that morning, the Minnesota State Patrol reported that a stack of "No Kings" flyers had been found inside a vehicle connected to suspect [[vance-boelter|Vance Boelter]].[4] Statewide "No Kings" events planned for Minnesota were canceled after the shootings.[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary criticized the ensuing rush by figures in both parties to assign the assassin to a partisan camp.[4]

## October 2025 rallies

A further round of 'No Kings' demonstrations drew what coverage described as millions of participants on Saturday, October 18, 2025.[3] The rallies produced an unusual moment on [[fox-news|Fox News]] when host [[jesse-watters|Jesse Watters]] admitted on *The Five* that his liberal mother, Anne Purvis, had joined a march on Long Island: "I know my mom was there. Can you believe my mom was there? Sometimes I think I was adopted."[3] Watters argued the protest wave was ineffective, saying, "They do these things — the Women's March, BLM, the Musk stuff. The issues change but the one thing that stays the same is Trump."[3]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded with an essay reading the marches through the esoteric concept of [[loosh]] — the notion that engineered discord generates emotional energy harvested by parasitic forces — and warned the truth community against farming the same energy in reverse through schadenfreude at their opponents' unraveling.[3]

## October 2025 second round

Thousands gathered in the nation's capital on Saturday, October 18, 2025, repeating the refrain "No kings," joining thousands of other gatherings around the country organized by Democratic groups and activists in a second round of rallies following the initial nationwide day of protest in June.[5] While many protesters told NBC News of their dissatisfaction with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a large share of left-leaning and liberal demonstrators made clear they were also unhappy with their own Democratic leaders.[5] One construction worker who traveled to Washington from northern Virginia said he did not have a lot of faith in the Democrats, who he said lacked spine and a single message and were too disorganized to put up a good fight, adding that "Trump's goons are f-----g organized compared to the Dems right now."[5]

Badlands contributor [[brad-zerbo|Brad Zerbo]] responded that the irony of the mass demonstrations is that if Trump actually were Hitler, as critics had insisted for ten years, the crowds would have been wiped out immediately — asking readers to imagine an "Anti-Hitler" rally in 1940 Berlin — and concluded that Trump is instead a reasonable, regular man whom the protesters will never see clearly.[5]
