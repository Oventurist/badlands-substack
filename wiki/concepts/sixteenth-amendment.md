# Sixteenth Amendment

The Sixteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution, which authorized the federal income tax, was ratified in 1913.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Its ratification followed decades of agitation: during the late nineteenth century various groups including the [[populist-party|Populist Party]] favored the establishment of an income tax at the federal level, demanding a graduated income tax in the 1892 platform, mirroring the Democratic Party led by William Jennings Bryan, who advocated for the income tax law passed in 1894 and proposed an income tax in the 1908 platform.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

After ratification, Democratic leaders agreed to seek passage of the [[revenue-act-of-1913|Revenue Act of 1913]], which would dramatically lower tariffs and implement an income tax.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The article presents the amendment as one leg of a three-part 1913 program alongside the Revenue Act and the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Ratification and the removal of a popular vote

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] dates the ratification to February 3, 1913, and stresses its proximity to the formation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], which he says is not a coincidence.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) In his reading the amendment granted [[congress|Congress]] authority to levy an income tax without apportioning it by population — meaning, in his phrasing, that the people would not be allowed to vote on it.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)

Carlson argues that politicians knew Americans would never approve a [[federal-income-tax|federal income tax]] at the ballot box, so they simply removed the power from the people, accepting the risk of losing re-election because they were "paid handsomely for their treasonous actions."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat) He contends the founders had specifically made a federal income tax unconstitutional, and that its absence kept government small; once enacted, it allowed government to grow and taxpayer money to be siphoned to officials and those who control them.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/americas-greatest-threat)
