# Bedouin

The Bedouin, or badawī ("desert dweller") in Arabic, are the nomadic tribes inhabiting the Arabian peninsula, the Levant, Africa, and modern-day Syria and Iraq.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) They originated in the Syrian and Arabian deserts and spread across the Middle East and Africa during the Golden Age of Islam.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Society

The Bedouin are contrasted with the ḥāḍir (sedentary people), though it is universally accepted that all ḥāḍir originated as Bedouin — as, the article suggests, did all human societies.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) In Arabia, for both groups, diplomacy, politics, and personal identity operate through a tribal system built on bloodlines, with the nuclear family as the base unit: many families make a clan, many clans a tribe, many tribes a "Skull."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Historically the relationship between ḥāḍir and Bedouin was symbiotic, though the modern era has brought growing political pressure to marginalize those living "off the grid."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Current estimates put the global Bedouin population at 25 million, with one of the largest concentrations — about 2 million — in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], whose total population exceeds 32 million.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Decentralized oral history

The article ascribes a mystical, esoteric aspect to the Bedouin: civilizations of the ḥāḍir come and go, but the Bedouin have remained constant since the dawn of man.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) They maintain an oral history that is both collaborative and decentralized — tribes that meet share stories, and if one tribe fades, others carry its history forward.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Unlike a centralized written record, which can be altered and manipulated, such a decentralized history is nearly impossible for any authority or government to change.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) As an example, Bedouin accounts of ancient Babylon — including the origins of blood magic and the gods worshipped there — were incorporated into the Quran despite not being corroborated in the Bible or older texts, and were subsequently confirmed by 20th-century archaeology.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Character and military role

The author finds considerable commonality between the Bedouin and the American spirit: fiercely independent, self-reliant, militant yet God-fearing, while recognizing the practical need for diplomacy and trade, with strength drawn from esoteric knowledge of how to traverse the desert safely.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) High-born Saudi boys from the ḥāḍir have traditionally been sent on coming of age to live with an allied Bedouin tribe to learn desert survival and Bedouin toughness, comparable to American boys joining the Boy Scouts.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The most elite units of the Saudi military recruit directly from Bedouin tribes and are organized by tribal affiliation, both to instill camaraderie and to prevent tribal feuds from degrading military effectiveness.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Traditional Saudi Bedouin dress, often read in the West through the lens of Islamic misogyny, in fact predates Islam by thousands of years and served to protect the skin from sun and wind-blown sand.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The Bedouin were central to the rise of [[mohammed-bin-saud|Mohammed bin Saud]], whose desert skill and merchant caravans won their loyalty, and to the appeal of [[wahhabism|Wahhabism]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
