# Online Safety Bill

The **Online Safety Bill** (OSB) is United Kingdom legislation passed by the [[uk-parliament|U.K. Parliament]] in September 2023 and described in Badlands Media coverage as the most recent escalation in a recurring, election-cycle censorship offensive.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Its long-form title is "A Bill to make provision for and in connection with the regulation by OFCOM of certain internet services; for and in connection with communications offences; and for connected purposes."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Regulatory mechanism

The bill sets what its promoters call "tougher standards" for social media platforms such as Facebook, YouTube and TikTok, and places the UK's Office of Communications, [[ofcom|Ofcom]], at the heart of enforcement.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) In practice the OSB compels technology platforms to comply with its ruleset or face very large fines; where fines are ignored, the escalation path leads to a decision on whether the platform itself should be banned from the country.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Badlands Media compared this to the pressure the French government applied to [[rumble]] a year earlier, when France demanded removal of Russian news accounts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Contested clauses

Critics point to vague but consequential provisions buried in otherwise dull statutory jargon.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Section 10 details offences of transmitting "harmful, false and threatening communications" without clearly defining what counts as "harmful" or what criteria establish that something is "false."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Clause 156 makes it a crime to "send a message" containing false information, while clause 157 immediately grants immunity to every newspaper, television channel and streaming service — an asymmetry that, in the article's reading, exempts legacy media from the regime it imposes on independent publishers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Framing and criticism

Mainstream coverage of the bill foregrounded the combating of child pornography, a framing the article calls window-dressing, noting that the overwhelming majority of such material exists on the deep web, outside the legislation's practical purview.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The author likens the OSB to the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]]: evocative framing used to secure public consent for powers that will be applied to something quite different from the advertised threat.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The predicted use case is the issuing of threats to platforms demanding removal of effective critics of the state on the grounds that they are "peddling harmful conspiracy theories."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

The U.K. Parliament claimed the bill would make the UK "the safest place" in the world to be online; Badlands Media read this as meaning a far more censored, locked-down internet for British users, and as a precedent template for other states aligned with the Western hegemonic establishment.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The bill is presented as directly connected to the pressure campaign against [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]] and to the potential removal of Rumble from the UK market.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)
