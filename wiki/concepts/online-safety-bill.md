# Online Safety Bill

The **Online Safety Bill** (OSB) is United Kingdom legislation passed by the [[uk-parliament|UK Parliament]] in September 2023 that sets "tougher standards" for social media platforms such as Facebook, YouTube and TikTok.[1] Badlands Media characterized its passage as the most recent effort in an ongoing wave of censorship attacks preceding the 2024 election cycle.[1]

## Long-form title and regulator

The bill's long-form title is "A Bill to make provision for and in connection with the regulation by OFCOM of certain internet services; for and in connection with communications offences; and for connected purposes."[1] The UK's Office of Communications, [[ofcom|Ofcom]], sits at the heart of the regime, and its Chief Executive [[melanie-dawes|Dame Melanie Dawes]] welcomed the bill's passage as "a major milestone in the mission to create a safer life online for children and adults in the UK."[1]

## Mechanism

In substance, the OSB forces technology platforms to comply with its ruleset or face enormous fines; ignoring those fines can lead to a decision on whether the platform will be banned outright.[1] Badlands compared this leverage to France's 2022 censorship demands against [[rumble|Rumble]].[1]

## Contested clauses

The bill contains what the author describes as vague but alarming clauses buried in dull jargon. Section 10 details offences of transmitting "harmful, false and threatening communications" while refusing to clearly define what counts as "harmful" or what criteria establish that something is "false."[1] Clause 156 makes it a crime to "send a message" containing false information, while clause 157 grants immunity to every newspaper, television channel and streaming service.[1]

## Criticism and framing

Mainstream coverage foregrounded the bill's "combating child pornography" framing, which the author argues is window-dressing given that the overwhelming majority of such material exists on the deep web, outside the legislation's purview.[1] The article likens the OSB to the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]], arguing both use evocative framing to justify powers far removed from their stated purpose, and predicts Parliament will issue threats to platforms demanding removal of effective critics of the state on grounds of "peddling harmful conspiracy theories."[1] Badlands further argues the OSB will establish a precedent for other nations aligned with the Western hegemonic establishment, and that its ultimate trajectory — combined with the [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]] affair — is the complete removal of Rumble from the UK.[1]
