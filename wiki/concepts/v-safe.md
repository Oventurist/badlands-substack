# V-safe

V-safe is a text-message-based surveillance system created by the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] to monitor for possible side effects of COVID-19 vaccines.[1] Users enrolled after vaccination and were periodically prompted to report how they felt.[1]

## Withheld data and litigation

The CDC for years declined to make the V-safe data public, instead publishing studies that described the reports as providing reassurance about the safety of the shots.[1] Data released in 2022 nonetheless showed that nearly 8 percent of the system's 10 million users required medical attention or hospital care after vaccination, with many others reporting missed school, work, or other normal activities — figures drawn from check-the-box surveys rather than narrative responses.[1]

In January 2024 the same judge who had ordered release of the topline data, U.S. District Judge [[matthew-kacsmaryk|Matthew Kacsmaryk]], ordered the agency to disclose the free-text entries in which individuals described their experiences in their own words.[1] Kacsmaryk, appointed by former President [[donald-trump]], rejected the government's argument that processing and redacting the responses would require too much work.[1]

## The released reports

The resulting disclosure comprised roughly 780,000 reports received shortly after the COVID-19 vaccines were rolled out, describing a wide range of post-vaccination problems including facial paralysis, heart inflammation, miscarriages and seizures.[1] One person wrote of "loss of consciousness and seizure immediately following injection" followed by an ambulance trip to the emergency room; another reported being "diagnosed with Bells Palsy today due to left-sided facial numbness and paralysis."[1]
