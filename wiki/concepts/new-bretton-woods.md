# New Bretton Woods

The **New Bretton Woods** is a proposed fixed exchange rate system which, according to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the governments of Russia and China have already expressed much interest in revisiting in the face of the current collapse.[1] Ehret presents it as the third and most vital element of a reform package, complementing the restoration of [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall]] banking separation and the revival of a genuine national banking system.[1]

While gold may or may not serve as a reserve for such a system, Ehret argues it is necessary that the discoveries of [[physical-economy|physical economy]] concerning "increasing energy flux density" be understood as the new metric of determining value.[1] He ties the monetary reform directly to physical projects such as the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait]] crossing and the [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska-Canada Railway]], arguing that Canada and the United States must return to a system of economic value based on future productivity rather than market-driven ventures into [[derivatives|derivatives]] — the American derivatives exposure being marked at approximately $1.2 quadrillion of nominal assets.[1] He also argues that the colonial "resource exploitation" policy dominant in Canada and the USA since [[nafta|NAFTA]] must end, and calls [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s flushing of NAFTA a vital precondition for giving sovereign nation states power over private corporations.[1]

## Alaska summit essay

In his August 2025 essay on the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin Alaska summit]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] again described the New Bretton Woods fixed exchange rate system as the third and most vital complementary policy, one the governments of Russia and China have already expressed much interest in revisiting in the face of the current collapse.[2] He wrote that while gold may or may not serve as a reserve for such a system, the discoveries of [[physical-economy|physical economy]] concerning "increasing energy flux density" must be understood as the new metric of value.[2]
