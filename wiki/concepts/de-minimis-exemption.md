# De Minimis Exemption

The de minimis exemption is a long-standing United States tariff rule under which imported packages valued below a threshold — $800 per shipment — entered the country free of import duties.[1] Its termination in 2025 disrupted international postal flows to the United States.[1]

## 2025 termination and postal suspensions

A decree signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in July 2025 ended the exemption effective August 29, 2025, after which international goods previously exempt under the de minimis rule became subject to import duties.[1] Only letters, books, documents, and small gifts valued at less than $100 remained exempt.[1]

Ahead of the change, postal services in several countries — including India, France, the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]], Germany, Sweden, Denmark, and Italy — announced a pause in shipping many goods to the United States, with the Washington Post reporting that operators worldwide were suspending certain deliveries in anticipation of the exemption's end.[1]

## Small business impact debate

In early September 2025 the Trump administration moved forward with eliminating the loophole that had allowed businesses to avoid tariffs on purchases under $800, and some experts said the change would hit "many, many" small businesses hard.[2] Jacob Bennett, CEO and co-founder of the small business banking platform Crux Analytics, told Forbes that "the de minimis suspension ending is yet another change that has forced every American small business owner to become an accidental trade policy expert overnight," describing a "whiplash effect" producing growing uncertainty among small business owners.[2] [[the-guardian|The Guardian]] urged against panic, conceding that [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s tariff moves had been a burden for businesses buying overseas-origin products and that the change would hurt some small firms, but arguing that in a nation of more than 33 million small businesses it affected only a tiny portion.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], reframed the disruption as a transition toward growth rather than austerity, arguing that Trump's policies and multipolar realignments would make the transition's pains fleeting for ordinary people but fatal for oligarchs.[2] Small businesses embodying a "Too Small to Fail" ethos would, in this reading, thrive in an antifragile ecosystem of lower overheads while mega-corporations reliant on easy money shattered, with a post-inauguration surge in U.S. factory orders cited as evidence.[2]
