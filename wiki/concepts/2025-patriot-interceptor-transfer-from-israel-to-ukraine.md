# 2025 Patriot Interceptor Transfer from Israel to Ukraine

In late January 2025, the U.S. military transferred roughly 90 [[patriot-missile-system|Patriot]] air defense interceptors from storage in [[israel|Israel]] to Poland for onward delivery to [[ukraine|Ukraine]], according to three sources with knowledge of the operation who spoke to Axios.[1]

## Significance

Getting additional Patriot interceptors to Ukraine had been a top priority for the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to help Kyiv defend against Russian attacks on its critical infrastructure.[1] The shipment was the most significant delivery of weapons from Israel to Ukraine since the Russian invasion nearly three years earlier.[1] The Israeli Air Force had officially decommissioned the Patriot system the previous April, more than 30 years after it was first supplied to Israel during the first Gulf War; the system had become less relevant as Israel developed indigenous air defenses, and most batteries were used for training or left in storage.[1]

## Negotiation backdrop

Reporting attributed to Israeli journalist Barak Ravid indicated that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had long opposed the transfer and only came around in September.[1] Before releasing the missiles, Netanyahu sought to speak with Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] to obtain approval for ultra-Orthodox Israelis to make their annual pilgrimage to the Ukrainian city of Uman, where a famous rabbi is buried; according to a Ukrainian official, Zelensky would not take the call until Netanyahu approved the Patriot deal.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary connected the transfer to the appointment of former CIA analyst [[michael-dimino|Michael DiMino]] as the Pentagon's chief policy advisor for the Middle East, a move that reportedly angered establishment Republicans such as [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]], who was incredulous that DiMino had "slipped through the cracks" during Senate vetting.[1] The commentary attributed to DiMino the views that the United States has no national security interest in maintaining a Middle East military presence, that aid should go to Palestine rather than Israel, and that U.S. military support for the IDF should cease.[1] The commentator further used the Uman pilgrimage to argue at length about the age and content of Haredi religious practice and the Talmud, while maintaining that the Israel–Gaza situation is ultimately geopolitical, cautioning that Christian faith is being weaponized for political ends and that going to war with Islam or any religion will not bring Americans closer to God.[1]
