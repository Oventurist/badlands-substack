# 2025 Beijing Humanoid Robot Half-Marathon

The 2025 Beijing humanoid robot half-marathon was an athletic event held in Beijing, China, in April 2025, presented as what was presumably the world's first combined robot-and-human half-marathon.[1] The course spanned 21.1 kilometres, with a physical barrier separating the lanes used by human runners from those used by the machines.[1]

## Format and rules

Human competitors ran under the ordinary rules of a half-marathon, while the robotic entrants were granted several concessions, most notably scheduled battery-swap stops along the route.[1] In addition to the overall finishing order, organizers presented awards for endurance, gait design, and design innovation, reflecting the event's dual character as both a race and an engineering exhibition.[1]

## Results

The winning machine, a robot identified as the Tiangong Ultra, crossed the finish line in just over 160 minutes.[1] Even the winner did not complete the course cleanly: it fell down once and required three battery changes over the distance.[1] Human runners still took the top overall spots, and a number of robots lost control or suffered other failures during the race.[1] Of the 21 robots that started, only six reached the finish line; roughly 12,000 humans took part, and the number of human finishers was assumed to be far greater than six.[1]

## Commentary

Coverage of the event concluded that human runners did not yet need to worry about "robot overlords," while noting that the machines were improving steadily.[1] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] took a more skeptical view, arguing that publicly demonstrated robotics are effectively toys and that defense contractors and their nation-states have possessed far more advanced capabilities for generations before such technology reaches the public.[1] The commentary framed the marathon as a glimpse of a "brave new world" of ubiquitous robotics and urged "moral and upright people" to pursue careers in STEM fields.[1]
