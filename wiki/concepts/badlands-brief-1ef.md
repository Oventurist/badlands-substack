# Badlands Brief: Self Incrimination, Crime Magnets, & Gay Tourism

"Self Incrimination, Crime Magnets, & Gay Tourism" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published on April 21, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The Brief is described by its publishers as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war," pairing summaries of mainstream reporting with commentary from named Badlands contributors. This edition carried 129 likes at the time of archiving and featured analysis from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], covering the aftermath of the Iran operation, Hungary's posture toward [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], election-integrity litigation, European defense-spending corruption, and [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]'s defamation suit against [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]].

## Trump, Iran, and the "Venezuela model"

The Brief opens with two [[truth-social|Truth Social]] posts by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] published on the Monday preceding the edition. In the first, Trump wrote that "Israel never talked me into the war with Iran," attributing his decision instead to "the results of Oct. 7th, added to my lifelong opinion that IRAN CAN NEVER HAVE A NUCLEAR WEAPON." He dismissed "FAKE NEWS Pundits and Polls" as "90% ... lies and made up stories," asserted that polls are "rigged, much as the 2020 Presidential Election was rigged," and concluded: "Just like the results in Venezuela, which the media doesn't like talking about, the results in Iran will be amazing — And if Iran's new leaders (Regime Change!) are smart, Iran can have a great and prosperous future!"

A follow-up post hours later accused Democrats of "doing everything possible to hurt the very strong position we are in with respect to Iran." Trump enumerated the durations of prior American wars — World War I at four years, three months and fourteen days; World War II at six years and one day; the Korean War at three years, one month and two days; the Vietnam War at nineteen years, five months and twenty-nine days; and Iraq at eight years, eight months and twenty-eight days — to rebut claims that he had promised to defeat Iran in six weeks. He denied being "under 'pressure' to make a Deal," declared that "Time is not my adversary," and framed the operation as finally straightening out, "after 47 years," a mess left by prior presidents. He again invoked the comparison directly: "This is being perfectly executed, on the scale of Venezuela, just a bigger, more complex operation. The result will be the same." He credited his first term with building "the Greatest Military our Country has ever seen, including adding Space Force."

The Brief juxtaposes these claims with reporting from [[cnn|CNN]], which stated that no regime change had in fact occurred in [[iran|Iran]] and that the current government is substantially the same as the prior one. Mona Yacoubian, director of the Middle East program at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, is quoted saying, "This regime is more hardline, less prone to compromise and, frankly, more nakedly tied to the IRGC (Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps)," adding that "We've seen the decapitation of the reigning leader in Iran at the time, but that has not translated into dramatic change in terms of who holds the power, or their position vis-à-vis the United States." Separately, [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] was reported as finding that [[venezuela|Venezuela]] is going well.

Burning Bright argues that the decisive detail is not Trump's exclamation of "Regime Change!" but his reference to "the results in Venezuela, which the media doesn't like talking about." On this reading, the Iran operation is deliberately following the same "model" Trump named at the time of deployment, validating the [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] framework Burning Bright laid out in January. The claimed outcome in Caracas is "continuity beneath the optics, sovereign control preserved and oil flowing on terms that serve the multipolar reordering rather than the old scarcity cartel." Burning Bright describes "a cascading series of Prussian Proxy Dominos," in which each new regime "looks suspiciously like the old one once globalist interests have been surgically disentangled," clearing a path toward the "political reconciliation" the [[state-department|US State Department]] referenced following its reopening of the US Embassy in Caracas. In this framing Trump claims the win, the "Collective Mind" receives its narrative catharsis, and sovereign realignment proceeds "without the blood and treasure the Hegemon demanded," advancing genuine [[multipolar-alliance|Multipolarity]]. The trap, the commentary concludes, is not for Trump nor for cooperative elements within the Iranian regime, but for the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]] — "the faceless collectivist architecture that has worn nations like skin suits for generations."

## Magyar, Netanyahu, and the ICC

The second segment reports that Hungary's incoming prime minister, [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]], invited Netanyahu to visit Budapest later in the year, including for a ceremony marking the 70th anniversary of the 1956 Hungarian uprising. The invitation followed a call described by officials as "warm," signaling diplomatic continuity after the ouster of [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]. Simultaneously, Magyar stated that [[hungary|Hungary]] intends to remain in or rejoin the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] framework, which would legally oblige it to act on the ICC's 2024 arrest warrant for Netanyahu should he enter the country. Magyar explicitly acknowledged that if Hungary is an ICC member, any individual subject to a warrant must be detained. The Brief characterizes the resulting contradiction as emblematic of a broader European dilemma between diplomatic ties to [[israel|Israel]] and legal commitments to international institutions: Orbán had defied the ICC and moved to withdraw, whereas Magyar's position reverses that course.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry adds that [[russia|Russia]] is now exercising suspicion and hostility toward Israeli travelers, likely presuming them to be undercover [[mossad|Mossad]], which he reads as an escalation in Israeli–Russian relations. On Hungary, he notes that Magyar defeated Orbán in the recent election yet is "ostensibly keeping nearly all of Orban's work in place," with the major deviation being his attitude toward Netanyahu and Israel. He calls it "beyond savage" to invite a foreign leader and in the same conversation warn him of arrest, and argues that the episode is less about the prosecution of Netanyahu personally than about "Israel and its citizens being ridiculed and ostracized from the international community," a dynamic he expects to drive Israel's tensions with Europe to fever pitch.

## Detroit ballots, DiGenova, and ActBlue

The Brief reports that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] requested access to roughly 865,000 ballots from Detroit as part of a federal review of election procedures, warning that legal action could follow non-compliance. The demand is tied to federal record-retention and election-oversight laws, including Civil Rights Act provisions requiring jurisdictions to preserve election materials. Local election authorities raised concerns about scope and logistics, including storage, chain-of-custody, and administrative burden.

In a related development, the Justice Department selected Joseph DiGenova, who previously represented Trump in challenges to the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 election]], to take over the criminal investigation into former [[cia|CIA]] director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]. DiGenova's appointment followed the removal of prosecutor Maria Medetis Long and placed him in a senior advisory role to acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]], overseeing the case out of the Southern District of Florida — the district of U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon. Chris DeLorenz, a former law clerk to Judge Cannon during the classified documents probe, also joined the case.

The [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] issued a new report stating: "New reporting has confirmed our findings: ActBlue accepts illegal foreign donations en masse. ActBlue tried to cover this up by misleading and withholding documents from Congress. But it didn't work."

Ashe in America recalls the Judiciary Committee's report from a year earlier, which promised transcribed interviews with current [[actblue|ActBlue]] employees, "including the ones responsible for ActBlue's fraud-prevention operation," and review of suspicious activity reports of fraud on the platform. She had written at the time that "the relaxation of fraud prevention measures by Democrats during the primaries and before the general presidential election is like a Hunter Biden suspicious activity report – alarming and illegal but buried and forgotten." Now, she notes, those same employees invoked the Fifth Amendment to avoid self-incrimination 146 times.

Ashe situates smurfing — the structuring of large contributions into many small ones attributed to other names — as an innovation of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s campaigns, a topic she and Brian have discussed for years on Why We Vote. She cites a 2008 New York Times item debunking Obama's small-donor claims, a debunk echoed across major outlets as "Obama's Small Donor Myth," and contrasts it with the 2012 reversal in coverage, including a Campaign Finance Institute analysis reporting that "Nearly half of the donors to Obama's reelection campaign in 2011 gave $200 or less, more than double the proportion seen in 2007." Meanwhile the underlying scandal was resolved in 2013, when, as POLITICO reported, "President Obama's 2008 campaign was fined $375,000 by the Federal Election Commission for campaign reporting violations — one of the largest fees ever levied against a presidential campaign."

She notes that ActBlue launched in 2004 to harness grassroots small donors, and argues it instead became "a highly coordinated portfolio of crime." Citing a list compiled by [[peter-bernegger|Peter Bernegger]], the Brief enumerates federal statutes ActBlue and [[winred|WinRed]] potentially violated: 18 U.S.C. § 1956 (money laundering); § 1957 (monetary transactions in property derived from unlawful activity); § 1001 (false statements to federal agencies); 52 U.S.C. § 30122 (contributions in the name of another); 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (wire fraud); § 1341 (mail fraud); § 371 (conspiracy to defraud the United States); § 1028 (identity theft and document fraud); § 1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act); 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809 (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act); and 31 U.S.C. 5311 et seq. ## EU defense spending as a "magnet" for criminals

The Brief summarizes an RT report in which the head of the European Anti Fraud Office warned that rapidly increasing military and defense spending across Europe is becoming a "magnet" for criminal activity. The concern centers on the scale and speed of rearmament funding tied to the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], which officials say creates vulnerabilities for fraud, corruption, and misuse of funds; investigators are already monitoring defense contracts and procurement pipelines for organized-crime infiltration. Emergency-style spending fast-tracked with reduced oversight is described as weakening anti-corruption safeguards, and the office says it is increasing scrutiny and coordination with member states. OLAF Director-General Petr Klement told the Financial Times that the EU defense industry has issues with "manipulation with public tenders, blown-up prices, clientelism [and] corruption," and that the billions of euros slated for weapons factories and hardware procurement are a "magnet for the criminals."

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry reads this as clarifying why Trump has maintained the Ukraine War "to whatever extent it was actually occurring," despite campaigning as a peacemaker: Trump forced Europeans to purchase American weapons rather than receive them as aid, compelling European governments to reallocate funding, with Woke/DEI programs the first casualties even as those states slide toward bankruptcy amid decaying economies.

He connects the corruption warning to Ukrainian oligarch Ihor Kolomoisky, whom he had discussed the prior night on The Book of Trump while covering the Azov Battalion. Kolomoisky, once the richest man in Ukraine, built his wealth in the 1990s after the collapse of the Soviet Union, creating PrivatBank and the umbrella Privat Group, which on paper owned thousands of companies mostly in energy and raw materials — most of them, the commentary asserts, fake entities in a money-laundering scheme. A vocal public leader of the Maidan Coup in 2014, Kolomoisky was appointed governor of Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, used his wealth to create the Donbass Battalion, and pumped money and resources into the already-formed Azov Battalion and other volunteer militias. After his dismissal as governor, he recruited a comedy actor from a television production company he owned to run for president: [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]], who ultimately imprisoned Kolomoisky for corruption and revoked his citizenship. He still awaits trial. The commentary closes by asking how many other Ukrainian oligarchs would exploit weapons deals for personal gain, and whether that makes "every weapon a potential honeypot."

## Kash Patel's defamation suit against The Atlantic

CNN reported that Kash Patel is seeking to compel testimony from The Atlantic and one of its journalists in an ongoing legal dispute, with his legal team arguing that communications and reporting tied to the outlet are central to his claims. The suit raises questions about the scope of discovery and whether journalists can be required to disclose sources or internal reporting processes; the magazine and its reporter are resisting, citing press-freedom protections and reporter privilege.

Ashe in America writes that "The [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] is a hill to die on," while noting that defamation remains a reviewable legal domain and that a public official must show actual malice. Where defamatory rhetoric is known to be false and shared anyway to cause harm, she argues, press protections reach their limits. She cites Trump's $15 million recovery from [[abc|ABC]] in 2024 and $16 million from [[cbs|CBS]] in 2025, calling him "the most public and most defamed person in history." Truth is an absolute defense, she notes, "but you have to be able to prove the truth," and concludes that Patel's lawsuit "appears poised to pierce a Smith-Mundtian shield for the fake news."
