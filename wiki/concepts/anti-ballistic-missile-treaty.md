# Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty

The Anti-Ballistic Missile (ABM) Treaty of 1972 ensured that both the Russian and American militaries would cease deploying, testing and developing sea, air, space and mobile land-based anti-missile systems for intercepting strategic ballistic missiles.[1] Its abrogation by the United States is presented in Badlands Media's account as one of the decisive acts that foreclosed the second post-Cold War opportunity for US–Russian partnership.[1]

## US withdrawal

The network of unipolarists surrounding the incoming administration of [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] is credited with ensuring that the United States would quit the ABM Treaty, a withdrawal Bush announced on December 13, 2001.[1] The withdrawal made the ballistic missile shield being built up around the perimeters of Russia and China an unbearable existential threat, and launched a new arms race between offensive and defensive systems.[1]

A day after the United States officially left the ABM Treaty, Russia announced its withdrawal from the [[start-ii-treaty|START II Treaty]], which would have banned the use of multiple warheads on intercontinental ballistic missiles and vastly reduced the total number of warheads.[1]

## Aftermath

President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] later called out this threat in his 2007 Munich Security Conference speech, which laid out Russia's understanding of the offensive properties of the ballistic missile systems built up across her borders and set firm red lines regarding [[nato|NATO]]'s continued encroachment.[1]
