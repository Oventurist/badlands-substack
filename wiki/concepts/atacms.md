# MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS)

The MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System, or ATACMS, is a U.S. long-range guided missile that became a focal point of the debate over American military aid to Ukraine in 2023.[1]

## Senate push for transfer

In September 2023, Senators [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]] of Arkansas, [[roger-wicker|Roger Wicker]] of Mississippi, [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] of Maine and [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] of South Carolina wrote to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] urging him to send ATACMS to Ukraine, arguing that failing to do so would "only prolong the war" between Ukraine and Russia.[1] The letter followed the [[senate-armed-services-committee|Senate Armed Services Committee]]'s unanimous approval for the Department of Defense to send long-range missiles to Ukraine.[1]

The senators claimed the United States could provide the weapons "without any appreciable risk to its own combat capability," that Ukraine "only requires a small portion of our stockpiled ATACMS," and that U.S. inventory could easily be replenished.[1] The Gateway Pundit called the proposal misguided and perilous for the United States and for global stability, noting its own prior reporting that Biden had acknowledged the United States was running low on military ammunition — contradicting the notion of an endless supply of missiles to spare.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentary by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] used the letter to argue that the neoconservatives blamed for the abuses and excesses of the Bush-era War on Terror had never left, and had regained public approval through anti-Trump rhetoric and a well-marketed war in Ukraine.[1] The piece recalled a period when the more discerning parts of the Left criticized NATO expansion and endless-war policy — citing anti-war advocacy by bands such as Anti-Flag and Rage Against the Machine — and observed that many of those same figures had become partisans eager for war, which it attributed to the power of partisan programming.[1]

While MAGA-aligned members of Congress had so far resisted neoconservative influence, the commentary held that the letter demonstrated the GOP remains infested with security-state assets. It described the signatories as members of the [[military-industrial-complex|Military-Industrial Congressional Complex]] who could not name a sane reason to liquidate the nation's remaining wealth into another endless-war cash grab, concluding that "there are madmen at the wheel."[1]

## Excluded from the June 2024 cross-border authorization

When President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] approved guidance in late May 2024 allowing Ukraine to use U.S.-supplied weapons against targets inside Russia in defense of Kharkiv, officials emphasized that the change did not alter the standing policy directing Ukraine not to use American-provided ATACMS or other long-range missiles and munitions to strike offensively inside Russia.[2] Republican Senator Mike Rounds of South Dakota, a member of the [[senate-armed-services-committee|Senate Armed Services Committee]], confirmed that strikes with U.S. weapons had taken place under the narrower authorization.[2]
