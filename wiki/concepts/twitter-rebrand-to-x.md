# Twitter Rebrand to X

The Twitter rebrand to X was the July 2023 replacement of the social media platform [[twitter|Twitter]]'s name, logo and color scheme with the single letter X, carried out by owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] after months of public teasing.[1] The platform discarded its widely recognized bird logo in favour of a plain X and changed its color scheme from blue to black.[1] Musk, formerly styled "Chief Twit," announced that he would henceforth be called "Chief Nothing Officer."[1]

## Background

X.com was an early online bank founded by Musk, initially funded by Musk and Greg Kouri, who went on to fund Musk's later ventures [[tesla|Tesla]] and [[spacex|SpaceX]].[1] Musk has used the letter X across several of his business interests, including SpaceX and Tesla vehicle model names.[1] In March 2023 he established [[x-corp|X Corp.]] as the parent company of Twitter, a corporate step that cleared the way for the rebranding.[1]

## The "everything app" strategy

The rebrand was presented as part of Musk's plan to build an [[everything-app|"everything app"]] — a single application combining social, communication and financial functions, a model already popular in China.[1] In April 2023 Twitter introduced a feature giving users access to crypto markets and other financial market services from within the app.[1] Musk cited Chinese applications such as WeChat, which are used for text and video calls and also allow users to pay bills, book cabs and reserve restaurants without leaving the app.[1] He argued that if executed correctly, X could become a leading platform for finance, banking, payments and data, with the potential to become "half of the global financial system."[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she found it hard to care about a logo change while "half the truth speakers remain banned and all the truth speakers are shadow banned."[1] She characterised an everything app as a single point of failure from a technology standpoint and questioned how the shift would connect to an eventual [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]], with behavioral, psychological and financial data consolidated in one place.[1] Her broader argument was that "centralization requires complexity, and complexity breeds and hides corruption," and she concluded that the strategy shift made her inclined to spend less time on the platform.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a timeline framing: Musk founds X.com in the early 2000s as the internet's first direct payment system; [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] founds PayPal, which forms out of X.com; Musk founds Tesla and becomes a culture war figure; Musk buys Twitter and promises payment processing; Thiel starts what Burning Bright called the "Crisis Cascade" resulting in the bankruptcies of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] and [[first-republic-bank|First Republic Bank]]; and Musk then rebrands Twitter as X with the ambition of capturing half the global financial system.[1] The implied question was whether the sequence had been "planned LONG ago."[1]
