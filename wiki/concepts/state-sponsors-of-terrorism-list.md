# State Sponsors of Terrorism List

The State Sponsors of Terrorism list is a designation maintained by the United States [[state-department|State Department]] identifying governments determined to have repeatedly provided support for acts of international terrorism. Designation triggers sweeping restrictions on US foreign assistance, defense exports and financial transactions.[1] The list was created in 1979, and [[syria|Syria]] was among the countries named at its inception.[1]

## Proposed removal of Syria (2026)

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would remove Syria from the list — the first time Syria would be delisted since the list's creation in 1979.[1] Trump made the comments while seated next to Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] at the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Turkey, saying al-Sharaa had "done a great job."[1]

The administration notified [[congress|Congress]], beginning a mandatory 45-day review period before the removal could become final.[1] Delisting would ease major restrictions on US aid, defense exports and financial transactions, and followed Trump's earlier move to unwind parts of the US sanctions program against Syria after the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the move as "the Venezuela Model all over again," arguing that as hostile media rhetoric ramped up against Turkey and Syria, Trump was pivoting to bring both deeper into his orbit.[1] Citing ABC News reporting that lifting the designation could facilitate investment in Syrian oil, banking, technology and real estate, he argued the delisting could produce an economic sea change and greater stability.[1] He also noted the economic performance Syria achieved under Assad in the 2000s despite the designation, pointed to [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]]'s angry reaction as evidence the decision was sound, and suggested Israel should be considered for the list instead.[1]
