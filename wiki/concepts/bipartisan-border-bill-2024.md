# Bipartisan Border Bill of 2024

The **Bipartisan Border Bill of 2024** was a Senate immigration and border-security package negotiated over several months in late 2023 and early 2024 and unveiled in early February 2024. It became the subject of intense intra-Republican conflict almost immediately upon release, with critics inside the Senate Republican conference describing the negotiation process as secretive and the substance of the bill as a capitulation on immigration policy.[1]

## Negotiation and secrecy

The measure was negotiated primarily between Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] and Senate Minority Leader [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]], with Senator [[james-lankford|James Lankford]] (R-OK) given the public-facing role of lead Republican negotiator.[1] Schumer publicly confirmed the depth of the leadership partnership, telling reporters that he had "never worked more closely with Leader McConnell on any piece of legislation as we did on this."[1] Critics inside the Republican conference argued that rank-and-file senators were denied access to the bill text for months while being told that opposing it would be unwise and even unpatriotic.[1]

## Republican backlash

Senator [[mike-lee|Mike Lee]] (R-UT) publicly called for McConnell to be replaced, characterizing the outcome as a "historical pro-migration border surrender" produced by "secrecy, lies, and broken promises," and labeling it a "disqualifying betrayal."[1] Lee did not name McConnell directly in all of his remarks but questioned whether the leader was in fact working on behalf of his own conference.[1] Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted that the Schumer–McConnell working relationship had precedent, citing prior cooperation on large spending bills and on American funding for Ukraine's war effort.[1]

## Substance and criticism

Badlands commentary characterized the bill as containing what it described as instant amnesty provisions and a daily migrant threshold of roughly 8,500 people per day — figures the commentary translated to as many as 3.1 million additional people per year — while also directing substantially more money overseas than to border enforcement itself.[1] The same commentary argued that the package amounted to trading American security for foreign interests, invoking the oath phrase "all enemies, foreign and domestic."[1]

## Fate in the House

Speaker of the House [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] announced on Sunday, February 4, 2024, that the bill was dead on arrival in the House of Representatives, and many House Republicans reacted with open anger.[1] Senate supporters of the bill were described as downplaying its more controversial provisions even as several Republicans publicly promoted it.[1]

## Aftermath: the ICE budget shortfall

The bill's failure carried direct fiscal consequences for immigration enforcement. It would have provided $6 billion in supplemental funding for [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] operations and erased a $700 million ICE budget shortfall.[2] After Republican lawmakers blocked it, ICE officials began circulating an internal proposal to release thousands of detainees and cut detention levels from 38,000 beds to 22,000 — the opposite of the enforcement increases Republicans said they wanted — according to four officials at ICE and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]].[2] The crunch arrived alongside the House vote to impeach Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] and ahead of an expected spring spike in illegal crossings.[2]
