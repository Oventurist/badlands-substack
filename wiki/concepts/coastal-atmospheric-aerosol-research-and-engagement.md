# Coastal Atmospheric Aerosol Research and Engagement (CAARE)

The Coastal Atmospheric Aerosol Research and Engagement project, or CAARE, is a marine cloud brightening experiment conducted from the deck of a decommissioned aircraft carrier in San Francisco Bay, reported in April 2024 as America's "first outdoor test to limit global warming."[1]

## The experiment

The project uses specially built sprayers to shoot trillions of sea salt particles into the sky in an effort to increase the density — and therefore the reflective capacity — of marine clouds.[1] The experiment takes place, when conditions permit, atop the USS Hornet Sea, Air & Space Museum in Alameda, California, and was scheduled to run through the end of May 2024 according to a weather modification form the team filed with federal regulators.[1] Coverage relayed by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted that details had been held back to "avoid (a) public backlash."[1]

Reporting noted there is little data on the potential negative effects of such geoengineering projects and that they "could also harm communities and ecosystems in unexpected ways."[1] Shuchi Talati, executive director of the Alliance for Just Deliberation on Solar Geoengineering, remarked that the test had been "kept under wraps" with little public engagement whatsoever.[1]

## Comparison to the cancelled Swedish experiment

A similar experiment set to be conducted over Sweden by Harvard University, which had previously received funding from [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], was cancelled in March 2024 after opposition from campaigners and Indigenous groups.[1] See also [[solar-geoengineering|solar geoengineering]] and [[weather-modification|weather modification]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that not long ago weather modification was treated as a full-blown conspiracy theory, with anyone discussing cloud seeding or the pumping of metals into the sky mocked as a science denier.[1] Recounting hours of [[nasa|NASA]] eclipse coverage she had watched, she described presenters repeatedly touting efforts to combat "space weather" and upcoming experiments to protect space workers from its dangers, quipping that "they're really pumped about helium" and predicting that space workers would be "the next protected class" as NASA finalizes plans for a permanent presence on the Moon.[1] She argued that while the world's elites decry meat, methane, fossil fuels and plastic straws, they are "actively polluting the atmosphere with fad theories — and using all of us as their guinea pigs," asking what the carbon footprint of APEP rockets is before closing with "Trust the science."[1]
