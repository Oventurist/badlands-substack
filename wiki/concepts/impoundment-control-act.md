# Impoundment Control Act

The Impoundment Control Act is the federal statute governing the executive branch's ability to withhold or cancel funds appropriated by [[congress|Congress]].[1]

## 2025 USAID litigation

In August 2025, the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-dc-circuit|U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit]] overturned a lower court ruling and allowed the Trump administration to proceed with cutting billions of dollars in [[usaid|USAID]] funding that Congress had already approved.[1]

In a 2–1 decision, Judges [[karen-henderson|Karen Henderson]] and [[gregory-katsas|Gregory Katsas]] determined that the nonprofits challenging the cuts lacked standing to sue under the Impoundment Control Act, holding that only the head of the [[government-accountability-office|Government Accountability Office]] has authority to bring such a case.[1] The court avoided ruling on whether the cuts themselves were constitutional.[1]

The decision reversed a February order by U.S. District Judge [[amir-ali|Amir Ali]] that had temporarily blocked the funding cuts; both the D.C. Circuit and the Supreme Court had previously declined the administration's requests to lift that order.[1]
