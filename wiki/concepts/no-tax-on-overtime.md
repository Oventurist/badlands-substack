# No Tax on Overtime

"No tax on overtime" is the tax proposal announced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at a rally in Tucson, Arizona in September 2024, under which he pledged to "end all taxes on overtime" if returned to the [[white-house|White House]].[1]

## The proposal

Trump told the rally that he had consulted economists about the idea: "I went to some economists, great ones, I said, 'What do you think?' They said, 'It would be unbelievable, you'll get a whole new workforce by doing that.' No taxes on overtime."[1] He added that "the people who work overtime are among the hardest-working citizens in our country," and that "when you're past 40 hours a week, your overtime hours will be tax-free."[1] The plan followed his earlier proposal to eliminate taxes on tips, which was also adopted by his Democratic opponent, Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the tax announcements as "masterful."[1] The argument had three parts: the proposal is an effective appeal to working-class voters most affected by taxation, a class that existing policy squeezes while leaving the welfare class alone and permitting the ultra wealthy to hide income; it places the establishment in the politically untenable position of opposing tax cuts that cannot be caricatured as benefiting only the rich, since they target the service industry and everyone required to work more than 40 hours a week; and it opens a nationwide debate about who, if anyone, should actually be taxed by what the commentary calls the American Oligarchy.[1]

Badlands treated Harris' adoption of the no-tax-on-tips plan as proof the strategy was working, and posed the open question of whether she would copy the overtime proposal as well.[1]
