# Asymmetric Warfare

Asymmetric warfare is a form of conflict in which two opposing sides possess unequal military power, and the weaker party compensates for its material disadvantage by adopting unconventional tactics such as guerrilla operations or terrorism.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described it as "a small, nimble fighter taking on a much larger opponent by using creative and unconventional strategies," and framed it as the modern-day equivalent of David versus Goliath, in which powers of equal kind are no longer the only actors capable of waging war.[1]

## Core principles

The central lesson of asymmetric warfare, in Deschamps's account, is that even large forces can be defeated by small decentralized or tightly concentrated organizations employing highly skilled tactics; manpower and size have been displaced by intelligence, inventiveness, and sophistication.[1] Applied to domestic politics, he argued that one of the most significant contemporary instances of asymmetric warfare is the practice of ideologically capturing small, highly radicalized groups of people in order to alter the fabric of an entire nation.[1]

## Relationship to narrative warfare

Deschamps argued that asymmetric operations depend on [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]] to conceal the underlying asymmetry.[1] So long as compromised media, big tech platforms, and the post-secondary education system can keep a small percentage of citizens radicalized and can amplify their voices, the asymmetry persists regardless of majority sentiment — meaning that the failure of anti-[[donald-trump|Trump]] messaging to reduce his support does not by itself defeat the operation.[1] He tied this dynamic to the [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election]], contending that manipulation of the result was accompanied by continuing psychological operations designed to keep extremist voices in the foreground and to discredit fraud allegations.[1]

Within the framework of [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], Deschamps held that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] hybridizes asymmetric and narrative methods to exploit the emotional states of both the political left and right, degrading the cognitive capacity of each side as it reacts to weaponized stories.[1] He cited a widely engaged Twitter poll circulated by Elijah Schaffer and the reaction of commentator [[christina-buttons|Christina Buttons]] as evidence that conventional socio-political analysis misreads such episodes, which he said are better understood through a fifth-generation warfare lens.[1]

## Canadian application

Deschamps applied the concept to Canada, asking whether the country's electoral system functions as an ongoing asymmetric political warfare operation by granting the democratic power of a majority to a radicalized minority.[1] He noted that [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] formed government on roughly 33% of the vote and that [[olivia-chow|Olivia Chow]] became mayor of Toronto with about 37%, and argued that the [[canadian-anti-hate-network|Canadian Anti-Hate Network]] and state-funded broadcasters supply the narrative component that sustains the asymmetry.[1]

## Counter-strategy

Deschamps concluded that opponents of the [[new-world-order|New World Order]] in the United States and Canada must themselves wage asymmetric warfare, because the alternative media networks they have built lack the concentrated power and resources of the conglomerates they face; he urged a shift toward a more localized and dispersed media force rather than treating majority status as sufficient protection.[1]
