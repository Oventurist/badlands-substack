# Regina Manifesto

The Regina Manifesto was the founding programme of the [[cooperative-commonwealth-federation|Cooperative Commonwealth Federation]], published in Regina, Saskatchewan, in 1933.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats it as an explicit call for a technocratic order managed by experts, issued from the hometown of [[joshua-haldeman|Joshua Haldeman]] while he ran the Saskatchewan branch of the party.[1]

## Content

The manifesto declared: "WE AIM TO REPLACE the present capitalist system, with its inherent injustice and inhumanity, by a social order from which the domination and exploitation of one class by another will be eliminated, in which economic planning will supersede unregulated private enterprise and competition," and called for the establishment of "a planned, socialized economic order, in order to make possible the most efficient development of the national resources and the most equitable distribution of the national income."[1]

It further described the work of a planning commission: "The task of the Commission will be to plan for the production, distribution and exchange of all goods and services necessary to the efficient functioning of the economy; to co-ordinate the activities of the socialized industries; to provide for a satisfactory balance between the producing and consuming power; and to carry on continuous research into all branches of the national economy in order to acquire the detailed information necessary to efficient planning."[1] The document concluded that "it is now certain that in every industrial country some form of planning will replace the disintegrating capitalist system."[1]

## Interpretation

Ehret reads the manifesto's emphasis on continuous research and detailed information-gathering as continuous with the data-collection obsession of [[thorstein-veblen|Thorstein Veblen]]'s [[technical-alliance|Technical Alliance]] and with the Energy Survey of North America later run by [[howard-scott|Howard Scott]]'s [[technocracy-inc|Technocracy Inc.]], which Haldeman joined in 1936 after concluding that Canadians would not embrace scientifically managed governance through electoral politics.[1]
