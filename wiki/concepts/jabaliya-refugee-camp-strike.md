# Jabaliya Refugee Camp Strike

The Jabaliya refugee camp strike was an Israeli airstrike on the densely populated Jabaliya neighborhood north of Gaza City on October 31, 2023, during the opening weeks of the Israel–Hamas war.[1]

## The attack

The [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli military]] acknowledged the strike, stating that a senior [[hamas|Hamas]] commander who had helped plan the October 7 terror attack was eliminated in it.[1] Al Jazeera, citing health sources at the nearby "Indonesian Hospital," reported that at least 50 Palestinians had been killed, mostly civilians, with the toll expected to rise; Sky News reported that "hundreds" had been killed or injured.[1] The Gaza Health Ministry, which is controlled by Hamas, said the strikes killed and wounded "hundreds" of people, a statement that could not be immediately verified, while a doctor at a nearby hospital said the facility was receiving hundreds of injured and that dozens were dead.[1]

Israel's military said its fighter jets had conducted a "wide-scale" attack on Hamas militants, and that the commander's "elimination was carried out as part of a wide-scale strike on terrorists and terror infrastructure belonging to the Central Jabaliya Battalion, which had taken control over civilian buildings in Gaza City."[1] It reiterated a warning for Gazans to evacuate south as Israeli ground troops and tanks pushed deeper into the Gaza Strip toward the territory's main city.[1] More than half the population of two million people had been displaced since Israel expanded its blockade of the enclave in response to the Hamas attacks of October 7, which killed 1,400 people, mostly civilians.[1]

## IDF response to civilian casualties

Pressed by [[cnn|CNN]]'s Wolf Blitzer over why Israel chose to attack while knowing hundreds of civilians were present, an IDF spokesman explained in substance that eliminating the Hamas commander was more important than safeguarding civilian lives.[1] The exchange, reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], drew wide attention as the war's civilian toll mounted.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] assessed the episode against his [[scare-event-theory|"Scare Event" theory]], concluding that while the Israel–Gaza situation did not yet meet the four criteria of a mass psychological awakening event, "it's bringing us closer to an event that does."[1]
