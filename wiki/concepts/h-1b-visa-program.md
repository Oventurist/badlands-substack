# H-1B Visa Program

The H-1B visa is a US foreign guest worker program administered in part through the [[department-of-labor|Department of Labor]], alongside the Program Electronic Review Management (PERM) labor certification system.[1] Both programs became the subject of a major fraud and human trafficking investigation announced by the Department's Office of Inspector General in July 2026.[1]

## 2026 fraud and trafficking investigation

The OIG said the abuses under investigation "undermine the integrity of Department of Labor programs designed to address genuine labor shortages — not to line the pockets of bad actors at the expense of American jobs," and committed to dismantling "human trafficking and forced-labor networks that exploit the foreign guest worker visa system."[1] Inspector General [[anthony-desposito|Anthony P. D'Esposito]] characterized the conduct as exploitation of vulnerable workers, forced labor, displacement of American workers, and abusive human trafficking rather than mere paperwork fraud.[1] A nationwide reporting campaign was launched to solicit tips from US and foreign workers, with possible rewards for information leading to prosecutions.[1]

## The 2024 H-1B controversy

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the investigation to the December 2024 H-1B dispute that "nearly tore us apart and ultimately cost us [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek]]."[1] Quoting the Badlands News Brief of December 30, 2024, she reiterated that "you can't have 'America First Globalism'": President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s promise to reinvest in American workers, companies, and industries was held to be at odds with a global order whose exploitation of the world's people, Americans included, is "the actual problem statement."[1]

The 2024 argument held that the week Americans spent debating H-1Bs was a good thing, elevating public knowledge of visa types, talent pools, and skill gaps, and leaving the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]] more united around its goals with a soberer view of the barriers to achieving them.[1] "We should never sacrifice what is right for a consensus outcome. We are not trading one set of globalists for another."[1] In her 2026 assessment, that outrage "educated and elevated the public mind to actually grasp the widespread corruption and criminality of US labor markets" and seeded conditions for legitimate American support for accountability and change.[1]

## December 2024 MAGA "civil war"

At the end of December 2024, the H-1B program touched off a week of public infighting within Trump World, described by columnist David Marcus as a MAGA "civil war."[2] On one side, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], the twin heads of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]], appeared at first to call for expansion of the program, which allows employers to use foreign labor when they cannot find qualified Americans.[2] On the other, [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and a host of prominent America Firsters all but called for an end to the skilled foreign worker program, a policy Marcus argued would cause considerable chaos and disruption.[2]

President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] weighed in on the Saturday, telling the New York Post that he likes the visa program and uses it himself, but stopping short of endorsing any expansion — the same Trump, Marcus noted, who had fired board members of the Tennessee Valley Authority for using foreign workers over Americans.[2] By Sunday morning tensions had eased considerably, and Marcus argued both sides had listened, compromised, and arrived at a clearer set of positions for the Republican Party.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] drew three lessons from the episode: that Americans remain highly susceptible to emotional stimuli, that globalists do not stop being globalists when they "win" an "election," and that U.S. labor dynamics are an important discussion the country needs to have.[2] Her bottom line was that "you can't have 'America First Globalism'" — Trump's promise to reinvest in American workers, companies, and industries is at odds with a global world order whose exploitation of the world's people, including Americans, is the actual problem statement.[2] She judged the week of debate a good thing, since Americans elevated their knowledge of visa types, talent pools, and skill gaps and emerged more united around the goals of the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]], concluding that "we are not trading one set of globalists for another."[2]

## $100,000 supplemental fee and the physician exemption fight (September 2025)

In September 2025 the [[white-house|White House]] announced that all new H-1B visa petitions would carry a $100,000 supplemental fee.[3] On September 25, the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]] and 53 other medical societies wrote to Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] asking that physicians be exempted, so that "H-1B physicians can continue to be a pipeline that provides health care to U.S. patients."[3] The AMA said 23 percent of licensed U.S. physicians in the prior year were foreign-trained and that such doctors serve areas with higher rates of poverty and chronic disease, citing an Association of American Medical Colleges projection of a shortage of up to 86,000 physicians by 2036.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the argument, writing that for 20 years companies had pursued labor arbitrage across industries while claiming "Americans just can't do those things," and that Americans had been made to train their own replacements.[3] She argued that realigning incentives was the correct move to restore American professionals to American jobs, and that the administration's parallel work in academia and trade schools would build "America First talent pipelines" — coining the slogan "Make American Business American Again (MABAA!)."[3]

## November 2025 MAGA backlash

In an interview with Fox News' Laura Ingraham that aired Tuesday, November 11, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] reinvigorated debate over H-1B visas by defending the program as necessary to "bring in talent" to the United States, drawing criticism from within his own [[america-first-movement|Make America Great Again]] base.[4] When Ingraham argued that the United States already has talent at home, Trump replied: "No, you don't. No, you don't. You don't have, you don't have certain talents, and people have to learn! You can't take people off an unemployment line and say I'm gonna put you into a factory where we're gonna make missiles."[4] The visas allow U.S. companies to hire highly skilled foreign workers for up to six years.[4] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded to the intra-coalition fight with the quip, "Maybe the real H-1B visa controversy was the friends we made along the way."[4]

## Teamsters and Sanders criticism (January 2025)

Amid renewed debate over the program at the start of 2025, Teamsters Union President [[sean-obrien|Sean O'Brien]] said the H-1B visa program "displaces" Americans from their jobs while allowing billionaires at the top of corporations to exploit foreign workers.[5] Coverage described the program as permitting companies to import hundreds of thousands of foreign workers, primarily from India, to take white-collar American jobs; Senator [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] (I-VT) blasted it as nothing more than an outsourcing scheme, and O'Brien took to X to say he "100%" agreed with Sanders.[5]
