# Hunter Biden Tax Charges

The Hunter Biden tax charges were a nine-count federal indictment returned in California on Thursday, December 7, 2023 against [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], son of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], as the special counsel investigation into his business dealings intensified against the backdrop of the looming 2024 election.[1]

## The charges

The new charges comprised three felonies and six misdemeanors, and were in addition to federal firearms charges in Delaware alleging that Hunter Biden broke laws against drug users possessing guns in 2018.[1] They followed the implosion of a plea deal over the summer that would have spared him jail time, putting the case on track toward a possible trial while his father campaigned for reelection.[1]

Special counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] said in a statement that Hunter Biden "spent millions of dollars on an extravagant lifestyle rather than paying his tax bills."[1] The charges centered on at least $1.4 million in taxes owed between 2016 and 2019, a period during which Hunter Biden has acknowledged struggling with addiction; the back taxes have since been paid.[1]

## Guilty plea, September 2024

On Thursday, September 5, 2024, [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] entered a guilty plea to all nine federal tax charges brought against him by special counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]], his attorney told the court, a move that shocked federal prosecutors.[2] He had initially pleaded not guilty, and his attorney [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] said earlier that day in federal court that Biden intended to switch his plea.[2] Federal prosecutor [[leo-wise|Leo Wise]] responded that "this is the first we are hearing about this," and a source familiar with the special counsel's team told Fox News Digital that the plea was not a done deal but merely an offer the defense had put on the table.[2]

Judge [[mark-scarsi|Mark Scarsi]] had Hunter Biden agree under oath that he would no longer remain silent and advised him that pleading guilty meant surrendering all of his rights to a trial.[2] Before a thirty-minute recess, Scarsi observed that "the court doesn't need the government's agreement to accept an Alford plea."[2] The president's son remained free on bond pending sentencing set for December 16, 2024.[2] The development came after his lawyers had prepared to argue at trial that he was too intoxicated by drugs or alcohol to pay his taxes.[2]
