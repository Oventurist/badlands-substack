# Voter Registration

**Voter registration** is the process by which eligible citizens are enrolled on the rolls used to determine who may cast a ballot. In the election-integrity analysis of [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]], the corruption of voter registration — rather than the manipulation of voting machines — is the central vulnerability of American elections.[1]

## Keshel's registration-first thesis

Keshel argues that corrupted voter registration is the single biggest indicator that a state will vote blue in any given election, and he has published state-by-state analyses to that effect.[1] He describes a sequence of manual (as distinct from electronic) election corruption that begins with registration: rolls are corrupted most quickly when a state adopts [[automatic-voter-registration|Automatic Voter Registration]], which reduces the risk of getting caught committing registration fraud because deliberate fraudulent registration becomes less necessary.[1]

From there the sequence expands mail-in balloting toward the universal model in which every registration automatically receives a ballot — as practiced in Washington, Oregon, California and five other states — legalizes or declines to penalize [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]], employs professional ballot collectors who know which mail ballots land at addresses with no real recipient thanks to compromised and shared data, and finally adds the key ingredient of time through expanded [[early-voting|early voting]].[1]

## Prioritization over machines

Keshel has acknowledged taking criticism from within the election-integrity movement for prioritizing registration over machines, defending the choice as pragmatic rather than utopian.[1] He contends that displaying clear evidence of voter-roll corruption in blue-wall states is far more achievable than convincing skeptics that machines flip votes, since the only broadly persuasive argument against machines is their lack of transparency and auditability.[1] He cited as illustrative a reported case of a woman in Bellevue, Washington, who received sixteen mail ballots at her apartment.[1]

He also argued that [[doge|DOGE]] audits across Washington, D.C. were surfacing names, data, addresses and records that line up directly with corrupted state voter-registration databases.[1]
