# Alter Personalities

"Alters" is the shorthand used in the dissociative-disorders literature for alternate personalities — the non-host identities that present in a patient with [[dissociative-identity-disorder|dissociative identity disorder]].[1] Badlands Media's introduction to cult ritual abuse treats alters as the central clinical phenomenon through which [[ritual-abuse|ritual abuse]] and [[mind-control-programming|trauma-based mind control]] become visible to a therapist.[1]

## Characteristics

Alters may take the form of children, teenagers or adults, and need not match the biological sex of the host: Dr. [[james-randall-noblitt|James Randall Noblitt]]'s patient "Susie" produced both male and female identities.[1] They typically hold compartmentalized memories inaccessible to the host — a four-year-old alter called "Little Susie" volunteered historical information the adult patient had never disclosed, and the adult afterward had no recollection of the conversation at all.[1] Handwriting style can change dramatically between alters, a change Noblitt tracked by asking patients to keep daily journals.[1]

Emotional presentation varies sharply. Many alters expressed gratitude and relief at being listened to, some wept while recounting abuse, and others narrated it with the blank, glassy expression characteristic of shock.[1] Secrecy was a paramount theme, coexisting with an urgency to express pent-up feeling.[1] One child alter explained that "people inside aren't s'posed to be out" because "Sharon's father said so … when the body was little."[1]

## Enforcer and role-named alters

A recurring type is the hostile "enforcer," which denies the abuse accounts and the diagnosis itself, and may threaten the therapist. Susie's male alter "Billy" insisted the other fragments' stories were fabricated before disappearing abruptly; Badlands describes such enforcers as common and as a significant indicator of sophisticated ritual abuse and mind control.[1] Another of Susie's alters announced, "I am the 'Evil One,'" which Noblitt eventually understood as an internal representation of the devil rather than merely an "evil" identity.[1]

Other alters are named for the function assigned to them by the perpetrator group. Noblitt's patient "Sharon" produced a frightened child alter who gave her name as "Sacrifice," explaining she had been so named because her job was to be the "sexual sacrifice" at rituals held in the family home, usually on Friday evenings and typically with both parents present.[1]

## Relation to programming

Because alters are compartmentalized, they can be addressed individually by anyone who knows the correct cue, which is what makes [[psychogenic-triggers|psychogenic triggers]] effective.[1] Badlands states that future installments of the series would cover the various types of programmable alters, how they are created, and how perpetrator groups build internal filing systems within their victims — as well as how survivors can reintegrate the fractured aspects of themselves.[1]
