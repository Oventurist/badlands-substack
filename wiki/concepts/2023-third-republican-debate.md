# 2023 Third Republican Presidential Debate

The third debate of the 2024 [[republican-party|Republican Party]] presidential primary was held in November 2023 with a field reduced to five candidates, following [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]]'s withdrawal from the race and another decision by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] not to attend.[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on November 20, 2023, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] reported that the general consensus of the corporate press was that former South Carolina governor [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] had been the "clear winner," a judgment he argued most of the Republican voter base would dispute.[1]

## Media coverage

DeLarme catalogued what he called pro-Haley propaganda published in the debate's wake by the [[washington-post|Washington Post]], the [[new-yorker|New Yorker]], USA Today, [[time-magazine|Time]], [[the-hill|The Hill]] and the [[new-york-times|New York Times]].[1] He noted that the polling underpinning those headlines came from the Washington Post/[[ipsos|IPSOS]] survey, which he characterized as the kind of poll designed to seek a specific outcome.[1] His broader argument was that establishment corporate outlets whose stated values conflict with Haley's platform were nonetheless elevating her, and that this contradiction was itself evidence she existed to serve the interests of the political establishment against the anti-corporate and anti-militarist [[america-first-movement|America First]] wing of the party.[1]

## Audience and the donor class

DeLarme wrote that throughout the debate the audience delivered thunderous applause for Haley and bitter contempt for [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], and that such audience reaction functions as a cue for uninformed viewers about who is "winning."[1] He emphasized that the audience — particularly the front rows — consists largely of representatives of the donor class and the establishment, and that historically these debates have served as a contest to win donors' favor rather than the favor of the American people.[1] On that reading, the debate signalled that "the donors have spoken" and that Haley was the establishment pick for the 2024 nomination.[1]

## Aftermath

DeLarme observed that Haley had begun scavenging donors from [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]]'s faltering campaign, and that while her polling had improved relative to [[chris-christie|Chris Christie]] and Ramaswamy and was closing on DeSantis, her numbers remained minuscule compared with Trump's.[1] He argued the donor-class investment in her candidacy might reflect either institutional habit or a strategy contingent on removing Trump from state ballots.[1]
