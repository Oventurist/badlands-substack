# FBI Subpoenaed Document Production to the House Judiciary Committee

In March 2025 the [[fbi|FBI]] produced hundreds of pages of subpoenaed material to the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] related to past Republican investigations, records committee members said they had never received from the Biden administration under Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]].[1]

## Contents of the production

An FBI assistant director wrote in a letter obtained by the Washington Examiner to the Republican-led committee that the tranche of documents concerned the bureau's inquiries into threats to school administrators, the pipe bombs discovered near the Capitol on January 6, 2021, and violent extremists' alleged ties to a faction of Catholicism.[1] The material also included records of the FBI's engagement with social media companies about foreign interference in the 2020 election.[1]

## Yates letter of March 7, 2025

The FBI said it was handing over the subpoenaed documents in response to a letter from House Judiciary Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] to Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] calling for "transparency and accountability" at the bureau and identifying information withheld under former Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]].[2] In a letter dated March 7, 2025, FBI Assistant Director Marshall Yates transmitted documents and information about the alleged pipe bombs found near Democratic and Republican headquarters in Washington in January 2021, the FBI's communications with social media companies, and investigations into threats against school officials.[2]

"As a sign of good faith, we are providing this initial production more than a week ahead of the Committee's subpoena deadline," Yates wrote, adding: "But this will not be the last production we will send to satisfactorily comply with the Committee's subpoena. To that end, we are diligently working to completely comply with your subpoenas."[2] Citing "the diverse and broad subject matters implicated" in the requests, he characterised the tranche as an initial response, with further documents to be identified and submitted; material sent to Jordan's office would carry "minimal redactions" to protect sensitive law enforcement information.[2]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that the bureau was promising compliance only after reviewing and redacting to protect itself, and argued that expectations for accountability were high precisely because of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and allies such as Patel — who introduced the ideas of "24x7 declas machines" and "museums of the deep state" — and Jordan, whose oversight had built a thick record of government crime without consequences.[2] Consequences must be by the book to stick, she wrote, concluding that Jordan had "run up a high tab on good faith" and that it was time for results.[2]
