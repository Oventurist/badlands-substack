# COVID-19 Vaccines

The COVID-19 vaccines — referred to colloquially in the Badlands Media corpus as "the jab" — are identified in [[a-virus-or-a-metaphor|A Virus, or a Metaphor?]] as the single most prominent reason former supporters give for abandoning [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. The essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] concedes that "on the surface, it doesn't look good," and frames the vaccines as the central test case for its broader argument about strategic compromise.

## The detractors' case

According to the article, Trump detractors hold that he told people to get vaccinated while knowing the COVID vaccines were dangerous, and that this proves he is part of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]. This objection is presented as outweighing, for many, all other considerations.

## The strategic defense

The essay's counterargument is that the vaccines were the only available exit from the lockdowns of the [[covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 pandemic]]. It asserts that Trump forced Big Pharma to speed up development, on the belief that drug makers would otherwise have spent years developing vaccines in order to prolong the shutdown, and that Trump weighed the possible damage from vaccines against the damage of extended lockdowns and judged the lockdowns significantly worse. The article adds that if Trump had not fully embraced vaccines, the media would have accused him of causing millions of deaths; "He couldn't just allow them; he had to appear to support vaccines."

## Choice versus mandate

The pivotal evidence offered is the distinction between endorsement and compulsion. The article stresses that Trump "made it clear he thought vaccines should be a choice, not a requirement," and reasons: "If Trump truly believed vaccines were effective and completely safe, he would have required they be taken by everyone. He didn't do this. Why not?" Where "everyone else in government, and corporate America was forcing vaccines on people," Trump did not — a divergence the author treats as the decoded message beneath the public endorsement. The section closes with a wartime analogy: "sometimes you can't avoid casualties; sometimes the best you can do is to minimize them."
