# COVID-19 Vaccines

The COVID-19 vaccines are, in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s account, "by far the most prominent reason" cited by people who once backed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and no longer do.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## The charge

Trump detractors say that he told people to get vaccinated while knowing the Covid vaccines are dangerous, and that he must therefore be part of the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Carlson concedes the point has surface plausibility — "on the surface, it doesn't look good" — but replies that sometimes one needs to compromise in order to get things done.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## Carlson's defense

He argues Trump forced [[big-pharma|Big Pharma]] to speed up development of a Covid vaccine, knowing this was the only thing that would allow the [[covid-19-lockdowns|lockdowns]] to end before permanent damage was done to society; absent that pressure, he believes Big Pharma would have spent years developing vaccines precisely in order to keep everything shut down longer.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Trump, on this reading, weighed the possible damage from vaccines against the damage from extended lockdowns and concluded the lockdowns were significantly worse — a tough choice, but one that had to be made with no alternative available.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

Carlson adds a media-management dimension: had Trump not completely embraced vaccines, the press would have accused him of millions of deaths for failing to support them, so he could not merely permit them but had to appear to support them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## The choice-not-mandate tell

The decisive piece of evidence in Carlson's argument is that Trump made clear he thought vaccines should be a choice and not a requirement.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) If Trump truly believed the vaccines were effective and completely safe, Carlson reasons, he would have required everyone to take them — and he did not.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Everyone else in government and in corporate America was forcing vaccines on people, but not Trump; the situation therefore required the public to read between the lines and decode the message being sent.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Carlson closes the argument with a wartime analogy: when fighting a war one sometimes cannot avoid casualties, and the best that can be done is to minimize them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## 2026 FDA publication block

In 2026 the FDA blocked publication of several studies supporting the safety of widely used COVID-19 vaccines made by Pfizer and Moderna, withdrawing the research before release.[2] HHS said the authors had drawn conclusions unsupported by the data, while Badlands commentary argued the suppression showed the data in fact undercut the safety case and that the coercive rollout of the vaccines should therefore be treated as a crime against humanity.[2]

## The 2023 booster reformulation

In September 2023 U.S. regulators authorized reformulated COVID-19 vaccines from [[moderna|Moderna]] and [[pfizer|Pfizer]] explicitly to counter the poor effectiveness of the existing slate of shots, extending availability to Americans as young as six months of age.[3] The updated products targeted the XBB.1.5 Omicron subvariant, which had already been largely displaced by newer strains such as EG.5 by the time of authorization.[3] Critics at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued the clearance proceeded without clinical trial data and pointed to reports of fibrous clots and cardiovascular harm.[3] See [[2023-fda-authorization-of-updated-covid-19-vaccines]].[3]


## Athlete collapses and myocarditis speculation (December 2023)

The December 16, 2023 on-field cardiac arrest of [[tom-lockyer|Tom Lockyer]], captain of [[premier-league|Premier League]] club [[luton-town|Luton Town]], prompted widespread speculation on X that mRNA COVID-19 vaccination was implicated, with commenters citing Pfizer's acknowledgment that authorized or approved mRNA COVID-19 vaccines "show increased risks of myocarditis (inflammation of the heart muscle) and pericarditis (inflammation of the lining outside the heart)."[4] No official cause for the collapse was established, with reporting describing it as a "mystery."[4]

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued that the pattern of young, elite athletes collapsing during play is not normal and should not be normalized, noting that the English Premier League maintained a vaccine mandate for players during the COVID-19 period.[4] He speculated — while acknowledging it as speculation — that the relative absence of comparable collapses in United States leagues might indicate owners or leagues secured placebo doses to protect their investments.[4]

## 2023 Berlin conference study

A study published on 13 June 2023 in *JAMA Network Open*, a journal of the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]], found that all 109 attendees of a Berlin health conference known to have tested positive for COVID-19 afterward had received at least two vaccine doses; among those testing negative, 98 percent were vaccinated.[5] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cited the paper as confirming that "the pandemic is of the vaccinated," argued that mRNA vaccines direct cells to manufacture spike proteins that disperse and cause health problems, and predicted that heavily propagandized populations would accept further interventions in a future pandemic.[5] See [[2023-berlin-health-conference-covid-19-outbreak-study|2023 Berlin health conference COVID-19 outbreak study]].[5]

## Suppression of questioning after the Hamlin collapse (January 2023)

In "You Are a Horrible Person" (January 4, 2023), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that many in the "truther" movement not only distrust vaccines, big pharma and the medical community but believe vaccines are intentionally engineered to harm humans — a statement he said he did not disagree with, while declining to argue the safety question in that essay.[6] His subject was instead the speed with which anyone suggesting that vaccines might have contributed to [[damar-hamlin|Damar Hamlin]]'s cardiac arrest was demonized as an insensitive, uncaring, horrible human being.[6]

Carlson argued that it was incoherent to treat speculation about an extremely rare blunt-force chest trauma as sensitive while treating speculation about vaccines as beyond the pale, and asked why the question could not be posed at all — noting that understanding what killed a young, fit person is ordinarily considered important so that future deaths might be avoided.[6] He cited the many recent cases of athletes collapsing on international soccer pitches and other sporting fields as context that he and many others found too much to ignore.[6] He allowed that even if blunt force trauma were the proximate cause of Hamlin's arrest, that would not establish that vaccines had not contributed to the net effects.[6]

He also anticipated a narrative in which every vaccine-related condition would be explained away as a rare but real phenomenon, and predicted the taboo would extend to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s promised [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] on [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]].[6]
