# Astana Format

The Astana Format is the diplomatic track through which Russia, Iran and Türkiye brokered ceasefires in Syria, first in 2017 and again in 2020.[1] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] described it to [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] in an interview aired on December 5, 2024, as "a useful combination of players."[1]

Lavrov summarized the track's purpose plainly: "The rules of the game are to help Syrians to come to terms with each other and to prevent separatist threats from getting strong."[1] He contrasted that objective with what he said the United States was doing in eastern Syria — "grooming some Kurdish separatists using the profits from oil and grain sold, the resources which they occupy."[1] He added that Russia wished to discuss with all partners in the process how to "cut the channels of financing and arming" the militants then on the offensive.[1]

Badlands commentary noted the significance of Lavrov's account for reading Turkey's posture: despite Turkish-backed militias fighting alongside [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|HTS]], Lavrov told Carlson that Erdogan was working with Russia and Iran to stabilize the country.[1]
