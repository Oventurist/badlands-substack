# 2024 Biden Mass Clemency Action

The **2024 Biden mass clemency action** was an announcement made by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on December 12, 2024, in which he pardoned 39 people and commuted the sentences of nearly 1,500 others — described at the time as the largest single-day act of clemency in American history.[1]

## Scope

The 39 pardons went to individuals convicted of nonviolent crimes such as drug offenses, according to the [[white-house|White House]].[1] Those receiving commutations had been placed in home confinement during the COVID-19 pandemic and, in the White House's framing, "have successfully reintegrated into their families and communities."[1] Officials did not release the names of any of the individuals granted relief, describing them only in general terms: a nurse who helped spearhead COVID-19 vaccination efforts, an addiction counselor who volunteers with young people, and a military veteran who assists frail fellow church members.[1]

In a statement, Biden said, "America was built on the promise of possibility and second chances," adding that as President he had "the great privilege of extending mercy to people who have demonstrated remorse and rehabilitation," and of "taking steps to remove sentencing disparities for non-violent offenders, especially those convicted of drug offenses."[1]

## CARES Act linkage

The accompanying White House fact sheet stated that the nearly 1,500 individuals receiving commutations had been serving their sentences at home for at least one year under the COVID-era [[cares-act|CARES Act]].[1] The clemency action thus tied a criminal-justice measure directly to the emergency pandemic-relief architecture enacted in 2020.[1]

## Context and timing

The announcement followed Biden's earlier pardon of his son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], who had been convicted of illegal possession of a firearm and pleaded guilty to intentionally not paying taxes.[1] Biden was set to leave office on January 20, 2025, having bowed out of his reelection bid, after President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defeated Vice President Kamala Harris in the November race.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that although the names of recipients were withheld, the White House fact sheet was "full of signal."[1] She noted the irony of the clemency program resting on the CARES Act — a $2.2 trillion spending bill signed by Trump — and observed in an aside that the 2020 CARES Act stimulus was roughly $1.4 trillion larger than the $831 billion stimulus passed in 2009 to combat the Great Recession, while economists declined to characterize the present moment as a recession.[1]

She further highlighted the fact sheet's language that the President "is intent on granting clemency to individuals convicted of non-violent crimes who were sentenced under outdated laws, policies, and practices," questioning why criminal justice reform had not been an administration priority earlier and suggesting the framing read as "a story set up."[1]

### Categorical pardons

Ashe in America drew particular attention to the fact sheet's reference to "categorical pardons to certain groups of people" and its claim that "the President's categorical approach to clemency has also inspired leaders across the country to take similar action."[1] Because presidential pardons do not cover state crimes, she noted, governors following suit with categorical pardons would close that gap.[1] She speculated on hypothetical categories — anyone involved in Crossfire Hurricane and related investigations, anyone involved in prosecuting January 6 including the congressional investigation, anyone who investigated Trump for any reason, the Biden–Harris State Department, and the Obama administration — and predicted further secret pardons and commutations, describing the setup as "a sweeps-style drama for January 19-20, 2025."[1]
