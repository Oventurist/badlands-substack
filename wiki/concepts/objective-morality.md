# Objective Morality

**Objective morality** is the position that right and wrong are real and can be measured much like other real things in the material world.[1] Badlands Media treats it as the essential hedge against tyranny and as the condition that makes a society resistant to [[mass-psychosis]] and [[menticide]].[1]

## Contrast with moral relativism

Objective morality stands opposed to [[moral-relativism|moral relativism]], which holds that morality is subjective and grounded in the beliefs of an individual or group.[1] The article argues that secular atheistic ideologies and cultures are more susceptible to mass psychosis than religion-centric cultures precisely because of moral relativism, and that religions — for all their dysfunctions — remain among the few social institutions founded on the concept of objective morality.[1]

## Function as a guide

Because a religion explicitly defines what is good and bad and what is valuable, through instruments such as the Golden Rule or the Ten Commandments, it acts as a conscious and measurable guide for human behavior; a person weighing whether to steal from a neighbor can reason from that standard to a conclusion.[1] Without a socially reinforced guide, moral evaluation sinks below the surface of consciousness where reason and logic have far less power, and social mood, whim and the desire to fit in steer the masses into collective psychosis.[1] Stated simply, a person who does not consciously know the principles of right and wrong has no yardstick for measuring good and bad behavior.[1]

## Source of rights and values

The article locates the source of rights, morals and ethics in God, invoking the founders' formulation that all men "are endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights."[1] It characterizes God in this context as "the philosophic singularity from which all categories emerge and unite — an ontological singularity."[1] It further claims that the most brilliant atheists in history were never able to produce an alternative theory of the origin of rights and values, and that [[secular-humanism|secular humanism]] arrives at a conceptually equivalent position by grounding rights in objective truth, under labels such as universally preferable behavior or mutually agreeable rights.[1] The United States is offered as an example of a society anchored to a legal, moral and ethical system founded on objective morality and theism, with the caveat that the American Republic will fall into corruption if the people forget how to think critically about rights, freedom and ethics.[1]

## The problem of moral illiteracy

The article stresses that objective moral systems only function when individuals actively think about how rules apply to situations — the domain of ethics.[1] Historically most people were illiterate and were not permitted to read the Bible, leaving a priest class to act as intermediaries who decided what to tell the people and how to interpret sacred texts, a system used to inaugurate the Crusades, justify colonial conquest under the doctrine of discovery, and prosecute heretics.[1] When critical thinking is removed, even a perfectly written moral system becomes "just another set of rules in the hands of a bureaucracy," making moral illiteracy the real problem.[1] The article also notes that religions are not immune to mass psychosis, citing at least two dozen cultures that practiced religious child sacrifice in antiquity, and concludes that the safeguard is not religion as such but "a consciously defined set of principles of right and wrong" that are enumerated, definable and namable.[1]
