# Whitmer Kidnapping Plot Trials

The Whitmer kidnapping plot trials were the state and federal prosecutions arising from an alleged 2020 conspiracy to kidnap Michigan Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]]. The final trial concluded on Friday, September 15, 2023, when a Michigan jury found three men not guilty.[1]

## Final trial

The jury found Eric Molitor and twin brothers William and Michael Null not guilty of providing material support to a terrorist act and of possessing a firearm in the commission of that act. Deliberations began the previous morning and followed fourteen days of testimony.[1] "You gentlemen are free to leave," Judge Charles Hamlyn told the three men.[1]

Outside the courthouse a juror approached Molitor and, according to defense attorney William Barnett, "said he was very sorry for all he had gone through," shaking his hand and giving him a hug.[1] Barnett said jurors privately told the judge that the evidence simply did not add up to "material support" for a kidnapping plot, the key phrase in the charge. "They went after three peoples' lives and destroyed them for three years," Barnett said of the attorney general's office. "I'm just lost for words. This is an emotional moment."[1]

## Wider prosecution

Molitor and the Null brothers were the last of fourteen defendants prosecuted over the alleged plot. Prosecutors argued the men planned to kidnap Whitmer because they believed the governor was infringing on their freedoms while Michigan dealt with the impact of the coronavirus.[1] The [[fbi|FBI]] became involved after a former member of the group grew disgruntled with the group's message of violence; earlier trials had already produced acquittals for two other defendants.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the verdict as a win and argued that by acquitting the three men the jury had in effect convicted the FBI for its role in manufacturing the case.[1] The commentary asked whether the Bureau would answer for its actions, whether any agents would be fired or prosecuted, and warned that absent accountability the victory — however profound for the defendants — would remain shallow for the wider public and for the future of the FBI.[1]
