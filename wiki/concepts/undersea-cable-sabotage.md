# Undersea Cable Sabotage

Undersea cable sabotage refers to the deliberate or suspected severing of subsea telecommunications and power cables, a pattern of incidents that emerged around the Baltic Sea and later near Taiwan and was widely attributed in Western media to Chinese- and Russian-linked shipping.[1]

## Taiwan incident, January 2025

On January 3, 2025, [[chunghwa-telecom|Chunghwa Telecom]] reported to Taiwan's [[taiwan-coast-guard-administration|Coast Guard Administration]] that a subsea communications cable near Keelung had been severed. The CGA intercepted the Hong Kong-owned freighter Shunxin-39 (also known as Xing Shun 39, IMO 8358427) near Yehliu; AIS and satellite tracking indicated the ship had dragged its anchor across the cable's location. Rough seas prevented boarding, and international law barred seizure because too much time had passed.[1]

The episode was described as mirroring the security challenges faced by Baltic nations over the previous year and as highlighting the vulnerability of global underwater infrastructure.[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that evaluating such events is nearly impossible because of the number of unknown variables, and situated them within the framework of a [[global-north-and-global-south|Global North versus Global South]] conflict.[1]

He wrote that he would need more evidence that the attacks on undersea cables were actually being conducted by China and Russia, since the incidents seemed more congruent with the behavioral history of Global North intelligence agencies, which he described as known for conducting false flag operations against their own citizenries to build support for kinetic warfare. In his reading, Russia and China have no incentive to strike civilian infrastructure, because doing so would galvanize public support for the very intelligence agencies that are their true adversaries and that are already in a downward spiral of self-destruction. He argued that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] have publicly acknowledged this dynamic and have demonstrated patience and restraint in the face of severe provocation, concluding that "the only winning move is not to play."[1]

## Western intelligence assessment of accidental causes (January 2025)

US and European intelligence officials told the Washington Post, on condition of anonymity, that a string of undersea cable damage incidents in Europe was the result of accidents rather than Russian sabotage.[2] Three incidents over the preceding 18 months, in which vessels damaged undersea cables and gas pipelines in Europe — some by dragging anchors along seabeds — had fuelled suspicion that the Kremlin was responsible, particularly against the backdrop of Russia's alleged hybrid war against Europe using arson and explosions.[2] US officials said intercepted communications and other classified material available to investigators indicated the damage was caused by inexperienced crews serving aboard aging vessels, with no evidence pointing to Russian involvement, and officials from two European intelligence services said they concurred without elaborating on the underlying evidence.[2]
