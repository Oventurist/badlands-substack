# 2024 Bangladesh Government Overthrow

The 2024 overthrow of the government of Bangladesh saw Prime Minister [[sheikh-hasina|Sheikh Hasina]] ousted after weeks of unrest, and was later alleged by a former cabinet minister to have been backed by [[usaid|USAID]] and the family of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[1]

## Events of 2024

In August 2024, weeks of student-led protests against job quotas spiraled into nationwide violence, claiming more than 700 lives according to the interim government's tally.[1] Hasina, who had led Bangladesh for 15 years at the head of her Awami League party, fled the country as crowds stormed her residence.[1] Nobel Peace Prize laureate [[muhammad-yunus|Muhammad Yunus]] subsequently became chief adviser of the interim government.[1]

## Allegations of foreign backing

More than a year after Hasina's fall, [[mohibul-hasan-chowdhury|Mohibul Hasan Chowdhury]] — a minister in Hasina's cabinet and her government's chief negotiator during the crisis — told RT in an exclusive interview that the riots were backed by USAID and Hillary Clinton's family.[1] He pointed to "certain actions of some NGOs, especially from the United States — naming a few, I mean USAID, for example, or the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]]," which he said had been running campaigns against his government since 2018.[1]

Chowdhury characterized the unrest not as a spontaneous youth revolt but as a "carefully planned" operation bankrolled by Western interests, alleging that "there is a nexus between the Clinton family, and the interim Yunus regime from a very long past" and that "funding of clandestine NGOs was going on" by parties "hell-bent on changing the government in Bangladesh."[1] He also questioned where millions of dollars in USAID funds had gone.[1]
