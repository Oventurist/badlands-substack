# Concept of the Foreign Policy of the Russian Federation

The Concept of the Foreign Policy of the Russian Federation is an approximately 11,000-word doctrinal document adopted by decree of Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in late March 2023 and published on the [[kremlin|Kremlin]] website.[1] Coverage of the decree, relayed via International Business Times, characterized it as a threat by Putin to "eliminate" United States dominance in the world.[1]

## Characterization of the United States

The doctrine describes the United States as a "source of major risks" to Russia and to international peace.[1] It states that "Russia's course towards the United States is of a combined nature, taking into account the role of this state as one of the influential sovereign centers of world development and at the same time the main inspirer, organizer and executor of the aggressive anti-Russian policy of the collective West, the source of the main risks for the security of the Russian Federation, the international world, balanced, fair and progressive development of mankind."[1] This dual framing acknowledges American weight in world affairs while assigning Washington principal responsibility for what Moscow regards as a coordinated Western campaign against it.[1]

## Multipolarity and the removal of "vestiges of dominance"

A central objective set out in the text is the adaptation of the world order to what it calls the realities of a [[multipolar-world|multipolar world]].[1] To that end the document declares that "the Russian Federation intends to give priority attention to eliminate the vestiges of dominance of the USA and other unfriendly states in world affairs, creation of conditions for the refusal of any state from neo-colonial and hegemonic ambitions."[1]

## The "hybrid war" claim

Alongside the doctrine, Putin claimed that the United States and its allies were using the conflict in Ukraine to "unleash a new type of hybrid war" against Moscow.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the doctrine as raising more questions than it settled, asking whether the object of Russian hostility was America itself or "the illegitimate regime that has been installed here," and observing that the American people had not voted to have the Nord Stream pipeline destroyed.[1] He questioned whether Putin was addressing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] personally or the interests he believed were directing the administration, and noted that Badlands contributors did not agree among themselves about how closely Putin and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] might be aligned with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the patriot movement, given warnings from military-intelligence figures that Russia and China sought to sow damage and dissent inside the United States.[1] DeLarme concluded that even if Putin were simply a sinister actor, the "megalomaniac globalists" he saw as controlling a stolen United States and much of Europe posed a far greater threat than Putin ever had.[1]
