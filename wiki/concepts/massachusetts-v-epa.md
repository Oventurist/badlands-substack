# Massachusetts v. EPA

Massachusetts v. EPA is the United States Supreme Court decision holding that greenhouse gases fall within the [[clean-air-act|Clean Air Act]]'s definition of air pollutants and that the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]] must determine whether they endanger public health and welfare.

The February 11, 2026 [[badlands-brief-05f|Badlands Brief]] treats the ruling as the enabling precedent for the 2009 [[endangerment-finding|endangerment finding]]. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] writes that, "enabled by Massachusetts v. EPA, this bureaucratic nightmare unlocked sweeping carbon mandates across energy, manufacturing, transportation, and logistics," and argues that reversing the finding "slams the brakes on regulatory overreach." The article also notes that environmental groups planning litigation against the rescission intend to rely on Supreme Court precedent upholding the finding, making the decision central to both sides of the coming legal fight.
