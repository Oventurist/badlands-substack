# Eagle Pass Border Standoff

The **Eagle Pass border standoff** was a January 2024 confrontation between the State of Texas and the federal government over control of a stretch of the Rio Grande frontier at Eagle Pass, Texas, and over the razor wire Texas had installed there to deter illegal crossings.[1]

## Abbott's invasion declaration

Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] escalated the dispute by formally declaring the migrant crisis an "invasion" and invoking Texas's constitutional authority to defend and protect itself, putting the Biden administration on notice. Abbott stated that "that authority is the supreme law of the land and supersedes any federal statutes to the contrary," and that the [[texas-national-guard|Texas National Guard]], the [[texas-department-of-public-safety|Texas Department of Public Safety]], and other Texas personnel were acting on that authority as well as state law to secure the border.[1]

## Situation on the ground

The situation reached a boiling point at Eagle Pass, where the Texas National Guard took control of a local park that illegal immigrants had used as a landing zone after crossing the Rio Grande. The state placed razor wire along miles of the river to block entry into the United States. The [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] had just allowed the Biden administration to remove that wire while the underlying legal battle played out in a lower court, and Texas responded by installing still more of it.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Discussing the standoff on the [[devolution-power-hour|Devolution Power Hour]], Badlands commentators separated the "Actual" from the "Narrative." From the Actual perspective, [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that comparatively little border was actually in dispute — a fairly inconsequential plot of land used by Border Patrol as a matter of convenience via easement. From the Narrative perspective, [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued the episode was setting up a prime states-versus-federal-government legal battle over the first flashpoint sociopolitical issue [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] staked his name and reputation to in 2016 when he pledged to build the wall.[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] additionally flagged the timing: Secretary of Homeland Security [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] was set to face impeachment proceedings the following week, with the border legal battle following on February 7.[1]
