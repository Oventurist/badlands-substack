# Hormesis

**Hormesis** is defined, in the citation used by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in "Parenting For Freedom" (May 12, 2023), as "a phenomenon in which a harmful substance gives stimulating and beneficial effects to living organisms when the quantity of the harmful substance is small," a formulation he attributes to Sakai (2006) via ScienceDirect.[1] The hormetic effect is the case in which an otherwise harmful substance imparts healing and strengthening under the right conditions.[1]

## Extension beyond diet

Deschamps notes that the term is usually reserved for dietary phenomena, but argues the principle extends far beyond that domain.[1] He treats the [[zone-of-proximal-development|zone of proximal development]] as the psychological equivalent of the hormetic effect, analogizing it to a weight trainer who damages muscle tissue through training so that it heals stronger.[1] Learning just beyond one's abilities, on this account, strengthens a person in many ways and activates potentials that would not have been activated without the challenge's presence.[1]

## Neurological illustration

To illustrate hormetic stress in a human context, the article cites the case of defending oneself from a physical attack on the street — an intense and trauma-inducing situation.[1] Deschamps writes that when the truth of such a situation is embraced, mid-brain centers come online, naming the xiphoid nucleus identified by researchers at Stanford in mice, who believed a human equivalent was likely at work.[1] He further cites literature indicating that courage and bravery not only support successful outcomes in challenging situations but limit trauma and the formation of PTSD, while acknowledging that mainstream science is still working out the mechanics.[1] When threatened, he notes, the brain stimulates the amygdala, a fear-identifying and response-inducing system.[1]
