# Streisand Effect

The Streisand Effect is the phenomenon in which attempts to suppress, hide or forbid attention to a piece of information cause that information to attract far greater attention than it otherwise would.[1]

## Use in Badlands Media analysis

Badlands Media invokes the Streisand Effect to explain what it argues is the real function of the [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]].[1] In the article's reading, the ADL does not exist to protect Jewish people but to serve [[deep-state|Deep State]] narratives: by ostentatiously and aggressively shielding Jews from criticism, it provokes the very curiosity it claims to guard against — prompting audiences to ask "What are they trying to keep me from seeing?" and "Why else would they need so much protection?"[1]

The article contends that this dynamic amplifies the myth that Jews run the world while diverting attention away from what it calls the Cabal's actual puppet masters.[1] The same logic is applied to the rapid viral spread and near-immediate scrubbing of a 21-year-old letter attributed to [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]] during the Israel–Hamas conflict.[1]
