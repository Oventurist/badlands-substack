# Industrial Model of Schooling

The "industrial model of schooling" is the term used in Badlands Media commentary for the factory-patterned system of mass education that, in the account given by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]], replaced older initiatory forms of learning across Europe and America during the 18th and 19th centuries.[1] The model is described in "Hijacked Consciousness - Sacred Origins," the first installment of the [[hijacked-consciousness-series|Hijacked Consciousness]] series, published July 8, 2025.[1]

## Structure

Under this restructuring, schools are said to have become "factories of information": students seated in rows, bells dictating rhythm, subjects divided and siloed, wonder replaced by order, and creativity narrowed into utility.[1] What the author characterises as a sacred path of becoming was converted into "a pipeline," and the teacher — earlier described as a "midwife of coherence" — became a deliverer of instruction whose knowing narrowed, whose intuition was discounted, and whose vocation was systemised.[1]

The article treats the division of subjects as the founding act of the system rather than an administrative convenience: "Math here. History there. Science on Tuesdays. Art, if you're lucky, squeezed in like decoration."[1] Separating disciplines, in this reading, causes the learner to forget the web that once held them together, so that the body still knows while the mind is trained to forget.[1] The author summarises the substitutions as the bell replacing the breath, the desk replacing the circle, the worksheet replacing myth, and the test replacing experience — school shifting "less about tuning, and more about taming."[1]

## Curricular stripping

The essay describes a progressive removal of tactile and rhythmic subjects from the curriculum, naming woodshop, metal shop, home economics and textile work, along with music, movement and "making," replaced by metrics, drills and standardized scripts said to float "nowhere near the body, nowhere near the land."[1] The stated purpose of this was not to teach but "to flatten."[1] By the early 2000s the author regards the system as complete — "a technocratic symphony of silence" of desks in rows, hourly bells, digital dashboards, emotional rubrics and reward charts, with facilitators in place of teachers and learners in place of initiates.[1]

## Sorting function

Testing is presented not as an instrument of learning but of sorting, scanning, "weeding out the unpredictable" and tracking potential threats to the algorithm; what could not be sorted was labeled, what could not be labeled was medicated, and what could not be medicated was removed.[1] The article links this filtering to the rise of a credential class and an "Ivy obedience grid," naming [[harvard-university|Harvard]] as "obedience, well-dressed" and [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]] as "the ritual dressing room of power."[1] The chemical component of the same programme is treated under [[pharmaceutical-compliance-in-schools|pharmaceutical compliance in schools]].[1]

## Relation to sacred learning

The industrial model is framed as the terminal stage of a longer drift that began with [[the-enlightenment|the Enlightenment]]'s separation of knowledge from knowing, and stands in the series as the direct antagonist of [[sacred-education|sacred education]].[1] The author holds that the system did not need to outlaw the sacred, only to overwrite it: grades replaced growth and influencers replaced elders, and school "taught them how to forget what they are."[1]
