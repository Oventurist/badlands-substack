# Lake v. Hobbs

Lake v. Hobbs et al. was the election challenge brought by Arizona Republican gubernatorial candidate [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] over the conduct of the 2022 general election in Maricopa County. Judge Thompson dismissed the case on the evening of Monday, May 22, 2023.[1]

## Signature verification evidence

At trial, Lake attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]] told the court that "11 of the signature verification workers approved 170k signatures at a rate of less than 0 and 2.99 seconds with a 99.97% approval rating."[1] In closing argument Olsen stated that approximately 274,000 ballot signatures were compared and verified in less than three seconds each.[1] The Gateway Pundit account carried by Badlands summarized the ruling bluntly: it did not matter, because Thompson ruled that anything goes and that laws are suggestions.[1]

## Day two of trial (May 18, 2023)

On day two of Lake v. Hobbs et al., Team Lake used Maricopa County's own data to argue that [[signature-verification|signature verification]] did not take place in accordance with the law, the narrow legal matter before the court being whether the county conducted verification per the Arizona statute.[2] Under Arizona law, signature verification means that a human looks at a signature on a mail-in ballot envelope and compares it to other signatures in the county record.[2]

The data presented in court showed that reviewers compared signatures at an astonishingly rapid rate: 779,330 signatures in less than six seconds each, 512,597 of those in less than four seconds, and 321,495 in less than three seconds.[2] The county's own data also showed a correlation between faster speed and higher approval rates; plaintiffs cited the reviewer User 79, who processed 54,298 signatures at a 98.9 percent approval rate, including 45,217 in under six seconds at 99.91 percent, 37,524 in under four seconds at 99.97 percent, and 27,196 in under three seconds at a 100 percent approval rate.[2]

Plaintiffs' handwriting and signature verification expert [[erich-speckin|Erich Speckin]] testified that the 45,670 signatures approved by User 26 and User 9 should be removed from the 321,495 total because those comparisons were completed too fast to have conceivably been performed by a human.[2] The plaintiffs' argument followed that if machines were conducting signature verification, then verification under Arizona law and statutes did not take place.[2]

Upon the plaintiffs resting their case, the defendants immediately moved for summary judgment without presenting a defense; after an impassioned back and forth the judge declined the motion, and the defense called its only witness, [[ray-valenzuela|Ray Valenzuela]], who was questioned by attorney Liddy with Lake's attorneys due to cross-examine him the following day.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] provided live special coverage of the proceedings throughout, with commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].[2]

## Aftermath

At a press conference the following day, Lake made two announcements: a new get-out-the-vote effort described as "chasing ballots" — which Badlands noted leftist social media accounts were already misconstruing as an admission that she was chasing fraudulent ballots, characterized as disinformation — and an intention to appeal further, with her team exploring multiple paths of appeal that she was not yet ready to disclose.[1] The press conference itself became a story after 12 News reporter [[brahm-resnick|Brahm Resnick]] swore at Lake from the floor.[1]

## Closing arguments (May 19, 2023)

In his closing argument on Friday, May 19, 2023, Lake attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]] argued that at least 70,000 mail-in ballots had not been properly verified in accordance with Arizona law and that the election therefore had to be set aside.[3] He noted that the November race between Republican [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] and Democratic Governor [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] "was decided by 17,117 votes… out of 2.59 million votes, a difference between the two candidates of 0.67 percent."[3] Olsen added that "the number of ballots at issue whether the court were to set aside for illegality, the 70,000, the 274,000 in a proportional manner … this election should be set aside," and told the judge, "Your Honor, the election was unlawful. [Arizona statute] 16-550 was not complied with. Defendants don't dispute our expert. They don't dispute the evidence."[3]

## Competing definitions of the burden of proof

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that, awaiting Judge Peter Thompson's ruling, the decision would turn on how the judge interpreted the legal standard and Lake's burden of proof, with both sides publicly confident and offering very different definitions of what Lake had to prove.[3] Team Hobbs claimed Lake's burden was to show that no [[signature-verification|signature verification]] took place at all during the November 8, 2022 midterms, and argued that Lake's own witnesses had verified signatures with great precision.[3] Team Lake's definition was that verification conforming to the statute did not take place for a sufficient number of ballot packets to have changed the outcome.[3]

At trial, Team Lake demonstrated this for at least 70,000 and possibly 274,000 signatures, against a margin of fewer than 17,000 votes.[3] Ashe in America, whose team covered every second of the trial for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] and reviewed the pre-trial arguments and exhibits, wrote that Lake's witnesses had in fact conducted signature verification according to the statute, demonstrating that lawful review takes longer than three seconds, whereas 274,000 signatures were approved in under three seconds and 70,000 in under two.[3] She concluded that statutory signature verification could not have and did not take place for a staggering number of ballot packets.[3]

She expected the ruling within days — Judge Thompson had taken less than three days to rule the first time around in December — and anticipated that whichever party lost would appeal, so that the saga of Lake v. Hobbs et al. would continue.[3] Over the weekend legal analysts speculated that there had been enough missteps in the lower courts for the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|US Supreme Court]] to take up the appeal.[3]

## Public significance

Ashe in America argued that the trial's central value was that, for the first time, millions of Americans witnessed the electoral negligence of [[mail-in-voting|mail-in balloting]], for which signature verification is the single control.[3] She noted as a "fun side note" that Republicans continued to align with Democrats in encouraging their supporters to embrace mail-in and early voting, and cited [[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] polling from the previous month showing that 62 percent of Americans do not trust elections, a figure that had steadily increased since November 2020.[3]

## May 2023 remanded trial

On remand, Maricopa County Superior Court Judge [[peter-thompson|Peter Thompson]] reaffirmed a prior minute entry setting trial dates of May 17, 18 and 19, 2023 on Lake's remanded fraudulent signature verification claim.[4] Thompson ruled that arguments by [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] and [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] to dismiss Count III, concerning fraudulent signature verification, were "not persuasive," but declined to reconsider Count II, which alleged intentional election day tabulator failures.[4] Lake's attorneys had sought to introduce newly discovered evidence — including technical analysis and whistleblower testimony about a secret logic and accuracy test they said Maricopa County had hidden from the court and from Arizona voters — arguing it showed "clear misconduct and intent" behind the failure of 260 of 446 tabulators on election day.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the paradox that plaintiffs could now prove intent, the standard on which Count II failed at the December 2022 trial, but the judge would not hear the evidence on the ground that Count II had already been fully adjudicated.[4]

The order set a demanding standard for the surviving count, requiring Lake to "demonstrate at trial pursuant to her concessions that Maricopa County's higher level signature reviewers conducted no signature verification or curing and in so doing had systematically failed to materially comply with the law," and also to "prove a competent mathematical basis to win."[4] Plaintiffs maintained they could show that the county's [[signature-verification|signature verification]] practices violated the law in numbers large enough to have affected the outcome of the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 general election]].[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried live coverage of the three-day proceeding beginning at noon EDT on May 17.[4]
