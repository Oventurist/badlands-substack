# Nyet Means Nyet

"Nyet Means Nyet" is the title of a February 2008 secret U.S. diplomatic cable written from Moscow by then-Ambassador [[william-burns|William Burns]], later CIA Director under President Biden, and subsequently published by WikiLeaks.[1]

## Contents

The cable assessed that Ukrainian and Georgian NATO aspirations "not only touch a raw nerve in Russia, they engender serious concerns about the consequences for stability in the region," warning that Moscow perceived encirclement and feared unpredictable consequences affecting Russian security interests.[1] Burns reported expert judgment that Russia was particularly worried that deep divisions inside Ukraine over NATO membership — with much of the ethnic-Russian community opposed — could produce a major split involving violence or, at worst, civil war.[1]

The most-cited passage holds that Ukrainian entry into NATO is "the brightest of all redlines for the Russian elite (not just Putin)," and that in more than two and a half years of conversations ranging "from knuckle-draggers in the dark recesses of the Kremlin to Putin's sharpest liberal critics," Burns had found no one who viewed Ukraine in NATO as anything other than a direct challenge to Russian interests.[1]

## Use in the Gabbard confirmation debate

Badlands Media invoked the cable to defend [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] against senators, including [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]], who attacked her tweets about NATO expansion and the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|Russian invasion of Ukraine]] during her [[director-of-national-intelligence|Director of National Intelligence]] confirmation hearing.[1] The publication argued that Gabbard's contested statements mirrored exactly what Burns had written in 2008, a point developed at length in the Badlands long-form article "A Bright Red Line."[1] The article further noted that Estonian Prime Minister [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] had recently suggested Russia be broken up into smaller states, asking how such proposals could be read as anything other than a legitimate security concern from Moscow's perspective.[1]
