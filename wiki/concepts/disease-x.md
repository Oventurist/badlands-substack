# Disease X

**Disease X** is a placeholder designation coined by the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] in 2018 for a hypothetical, as-yet-unknown pathogen capable of causing a future global epidemic. The term is used in pandemic-preparedness planning to signal that the next major outbreak may arise from an unrecognized agent rather than from a currently catalogued disease.[1]

## 2024 Davos session

Disease X returned to prominence in January 2024, when the World Health Organization used the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos to warn world leaders that a future pandemic could be as much as twenty times worse than COVID-19. Global health officials appeared on a WEF panel formally titled "Preparing for Disease X," at which scientists urged governments to coordinate on strategies to prevent or manage such an event.[1]

Among the issues raised at the session was the development of better communication strategies intended to reduce what officials characterized as misinformation and conspiracy theories surrounding public health emergencies.[1]

## Reception in the independent media

The framing of the panel drew immediate criticism online, with commentators on X (formerly Twitter) describing the session itself as a conspiracy against freedom. Badlands Media, in reproducing the coverage as a "bonus item" in its news brief, presented the episode as part of a broader pattern in which institutional pandemic-preparedness messaging is treated skeptically by the independent media audience — particularly given that Disease X is, by the WHO's own description, not a real disease but a planning abstraction.[1]
