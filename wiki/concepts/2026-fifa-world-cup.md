# 2026 FIFA World Cup

The 2026 FIFA World Cup — referred to in the Badlands Brief as "America's World Cup" — was the men's football world championship hosted in the United States and concluded with a final played at MetLife Stadium on Sunday, July 19, 2026.[1] [[spain|Spain]] defeated [[argentina|Argentina]] 1-0 after extra time, capturing the country's second men's world championship, with substitute Ferran Torres scoring the match's only goal in the 106th minute.[1]

## The final

Spain controlled much of the match through possession and shot volume before breaking through in extra time.[1] Argentina finished with ten players after midfielder [[enzo-fernandez|Enzo Fernández]] was sent off during extra time and was unable to find an equalizer before the final whistle.[1] The Badlands Brief noted that Argentina had recorded no shots on goal as late as the point at which Fernández received his first yellow card, describing the performance as "absolutely abysmal" for a team universally favored by pundits.[1] Spain also had two additional goals disallowed — one for offside after the winning goal, and one earlier, while the game was still scoreless, after an Argentine player flopped and a foul was called.[1]

## Officiating controversy

The Brief's commentary, written by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], argued that referees had systematically favored Argentina throughout the tournament, citing video compilations from Argentina's matches — including the semi-final against England — in which Argentine players appeared to push, shove and foul opponents without penalty while opposing players received yellow cards.[1] The commentary summarized soccer's contact rules: incidental contact after playing the ball is generally ignored, while contact without playing the ball stops play, with yellow cards for dangerous contact and red cards for malicious or excessively dangerous contact, forcing the offending team to play a man down.[1]

The most viral cited incident involved an Argentine player jumping on a Spanish player, grabbing his collar and digging cleat spikes into his calf while bringing him to the ground, for which no card was issued and which announcers described as "an interesting technique."[1] The Brief alleged a longstanding pattern in which [[fifa|FIFA]] is accused by fans of favoring Argentina to boost [[lionel-messi|Lionel Messi]]'s scoring and win record for marketing and advertising reasons, noting that the FBI had announced an investigation into the Argentine football association for money laundering and that FIFA officials were arrested in a 2015 corruption scandal also involving money laundering.[1]

## Political framing

The Brief argued that because pundits, celebrities, political leaders and what it called the "Zionist Diaspora" backed Argentina, much of the internet cheered for underdog Spain.[1] It attributed that alignment to [[argentina|Argentina]]'s president [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]] being a close friend of [[israel|Israel]], while Spain has led a trend of European countries breaking with unconditional support for Israel.[1]

## Post-match brawl

After the final whistle, the _Daily Mirror_ reported a confrontation on the field involving Argentina midfielder Leandro Paredes and several Spain players.[1] The Brief added that Argentine defender Nahuel Molina punched the Spanish team captain as the two passed one another, triggering a brawl it called "the cherry on top of an ostensibly dirty match."[1]

The commentary credited President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with bringing the tournament to the United States through a contract secured during his first term, framing it as an addition to America's 250th anniversary celebrations.[1]

## Trump's remarks on Iranian participation

In March 2026, ahead of the tournament, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that the Iranian national soccer team was "welcome to The World Cup," but that he did not believe it appropriate for them to attend "for their own life and safety," amid the ongoing US–[[iran|Iran]] conflict.[2] In a separate post he said the United States looked "very much forward to hosting the FIFA World Cup," claimed ticket sales were "through the roof," and described it as "the Greatest and Safest Sporting Event in American History," with all players, officials, and fans treated "like the 'STARS' that they are."[2]

The 2026 tournament is jointly hosted by Canada, Mexico, and the United States — the first time three nations have co-hosted — running from June through July.[2] Badlands commentary noted the apparent tension between Trump's morning post warning the Iranian team away and his evening post declaring the war effectively won, and questioned whether the Iran conflict would still be underway when the tournament began in June.[2]

## USMNT opening match

The United States men's national soccer team opened its 2026 World Cup campaign with a 4–1 victory over Paraguay in June 2026; ESPN reported that the four-goal performance was the highest scoring output by the USMNT in a World Cup match.[3] Badlands contributor Ashe in America placed the win alongside the [[ufc-freedom-250|UFC Freedom 250]] event held at the [[white-house|White House]] on [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s 80th birthday as evidence of a rising "Spirit of America" ahead of the country's 250th anniversary.[3]

## Balogun red card and US elimination

In the Round of 16, US forward [[folarin-balogun|Folarin Balogun]] was shown a red card during the United States' match against Belgium, prompting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to publicly criticize [[fifa|FIFA]], call the decision unfair, and direct administration officials to raise concerns with FIFA leadership.[4] The intervention drew criticism from Democratic lawmakers and ethics experts, who argued the [[white-house|White House]] should not seek to influence an international sporting organization, while administration officials said the president was advocating for fair treatment of the US team.[4] FIFA gave no indication it would revisit the officiating decision, and the United States was eliminated with a 4–1 loss to Belgium, which advanced to the quarterfinals.[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded dismissively, maintaining that hockey is the only true sport and calling soccer "ceremonial silliness."[4]

## Group stage: United States and Iran

Team USA defeated Australia on a Friday evening in June 2026, securing the top position in Group D and guaranteeing advancement out of group play into the single-elimination round regardless of the result of its Thursday match against Turkey.[5] Badlands Media highlighted a stadium in Seattle filled with Americans singing John Denver and circulated a montage of reactions from watch parties across the country when the U.S. won.[5]
The outlet framed the run as an underdog story, noting that soccer is the one sport in which the United States can barely compete with the rest of the world, that even the poorest countries play it well, and that Team USA had failed to qualify for the 2018 tournament.[5] Badlands urged readers dismissive of soccer to embrace the competition, calling the sport "the great equalizer when it comes to the clash of civilizations."[5]

[[iran|Iran]] played Belgium to a 0-0 draw featuring what Badlands called one of the most remarkable goalkeeper saves imaginable; an Iranian penalty-kick goal was called back for offsides, a decision widely disputed online.[5] Badlands said it would be "hilarious" to see Iran advance deep into the tournament, because "the Israelis and their slaves would seethe."[5] England was described as dominating the tournament and considered a top-three side in terms of talent even as Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] prepared to resign.[5]
