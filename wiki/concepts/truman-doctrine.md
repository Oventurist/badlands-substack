# Truman Doctrine

The Truman Doctrine was the 1947 United States policy commitment, signed by President Harry S. Truman, vowing to "support democracies against authoritarian threats" as part of a comprehensive effort to counter what were perceived as threats from Soviet Russia.[1] Badlands Media commentary treats it as the political framework out of which [[nato|NATO]] emerged two years later, in 1949.[1]

The same year the doctrine was announced, Truman also signed the National Security Act, creating an intelligence agency — the [[cia|CIA]] — that the Badlands account describes as tasked with "brainwashing the world's populations to love America and hate communism," or at least, the commentary adds, that was what the agency claimed to be doing.[1]

## Turkish Straits crisis

The article identifies the immediate driver of the doctrine as a standoff between the Soviets and Turkey at the end of the Second World War: Moscow wanted access to the Mediterranean from the Black Sea through the Turkish Straits.[1] The Allies pressured Turkey to refuse, and when Britain ended its naval assistance in 1947, Truman dispatched the Franklin D. Roosevelt aircraft carrier battle group to keep the Straits under Turkish control, accompanied by $100 million in "foreign aid."[1] Turkey joined NATO in 1952.[1]

## Place in the Badlands NATO genealogy

The commentary situates the Truman Doctrine within a longer sequence: the 1900 Anglo-French Alliance, the 1948 Western Union that added the Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg, and then NATO's formal creation in 1949.[1] The argument advanced is that these steps were "pursued for purportedly noble purposes — to halt authoritarianism — yet the exact opposite is the truth."[1]
