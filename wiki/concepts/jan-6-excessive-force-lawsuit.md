# Jan. 6 Excessive Force Lawsuit

The Jan. 6 excessive force lawsuit is a proposed federal class action filed in Florida by participants in the January 6, 2021 Capitol breach, who are suing the federal government for tens of millions of dollars in damages on the theory that indiscriminate use of force by law enforcement caused them physical and emotional injuries.[1]

## Claims

The suit targets the conduct of both the [[united-states-capitol-police|US Capitol Police]] and the [[metropolitan-police-department-of-the-district-of-columbia|Metropolitan Police Department]] of Washington, DC, alleging that the use of rubber bullets, chemical spray, flashbangs, and other crowd-control measures was reckless and improperly directed at individuals who were not engaged in violence.[1] The plaintiffs characterize the police response as a "wanton barrage" of non-lethal munitions that caused widespread injury among people gathered on the Capitol's West Front.[1]

## Parties and posture

Among the lead plaintiffs is A.J. Fischer, a member of the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] previously convicted of assault and later pardoned by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], along with Patrick and Marie Sullivan, who say they were in the crowd and were struck by pepper balls and exposed to chemical spray.[1] The proposed class would represent dozens of individuals present on the West Front.[1] The case has been assigned to US District Judge [[paul-byron|Paul Byron]], an Obama appointee sitting in Florida.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], described being gassed by Capitol Police on January 6 while peacefully protesting what she called an obviously stolen election, saying gas was launched away from the violence and toward peaceful protesters with no riot declaration issued and officers milling among a jovial crowd beforehand.[1] She argued that the episode was subject to months of official gaslighting until a single video was released by The Gateway Pundit, and that the lawsuit must proceed in order to "put the truth on the record," framing accountability as "a condition of the golden age."[1]
