# Operation Metro Surge

Operation Metro Surge is the name of an aggressive federal immigration enforcement operation conducted in Minnesota by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] under the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] during the winter of 2025–2026.

According to the March 4, 2026 Badlands Brief, the operation produced sustained clashes in Minneapolis and St. Paul and led to the fatal shootings of two United States citizen protesters: [[renee-good|Renee Good]] on January 7 and [[alex-pretti|Alex Pretti]] on January 24, 2026. The deaths became a focus of the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing of March 3, 2026, at which Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] defended the operation and blamed "violent protesters" for the disorder, while Democratic senators accused officers of excessive force and criticized Noem for describing the victims as "domestic terrorists."

## Conclusion (February 2026)

Federal authorities announced in February 2026 that the operation was coming to an end. Border Czar [[tom-homan]] said he had proposed concluding the surge and that [[donald-trump]] concurred, citing a significantly reduced [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] target list, unprecedented county-level coordination on jail releases of criminal aliens, a decrease in what he termed unlawful agitator activity, and increased engagement by state and local law enforcement against agitators.

Begun in December 2025, the operation produced more than 4,000 arrests in the Minneapolis–St. Paul area along with statewide deployments of federal immigration agents, and was accompanied by thousands of arrests and violent protests. Personnel deployed to [[minnesota]] were to be redeployed to immigration enforcement operations elsewhere in the country.
