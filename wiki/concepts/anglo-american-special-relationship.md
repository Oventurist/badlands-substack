# Anglo-American Special Relationship

The Anglo-American "special relationship" is the doctrine of exceptional political, military, and intelligence intimacy between the United Kingdom and the United States. In the account given by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] for Badlands Media, it is not a natural friendship between kindred nations but a mechanism of imperial capture, and its collapse is presented as an opportunity that America must not miss.[1]

## The fallacious history of friendship

For much of the nineteenth century, Ehret argues, Americans understood their anti-colonial origins better than their descendants do.[1] Although the last official war between Britain and America was the [[war-of-1812|War of 1812]], Britain's failure to destroy the republic militarily caused British foreign policy to refocus on undermining America from within — through British-sponsored ideologies in the American school system on one hand, and British banking practices among Wall Street's ruling class on the other.[1] This attack from within required more patience but proved more successful, bringing America near collapse in 1860 when [[lord-palmerston|Lord Palmerston]] threatened to recognise the Southern slave power's independence from the Union.[1]

During the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]], [[robert-gascoyne-cecil|Lord Robert Cecil]] told Parliament that "the Northern States of America never can be our sure friends because we are rivals, rivals politically, rivals commercially," while with the Southern States "the case is entirely reversed" — an agricultural people who furnished Britain's raw material and consumed its manufactures, and who "at once recurred to England as their natural ally."[1] Britain's covert military support for the Confederate cause was exposed by the end of the war and led to a $15 million settlement paid to America under the [[alabama-claims|Alabama Claims]] in 1872.[1]

## Formal birth after Roosevelt

The special relationship in its modern form is dated to the period immediately following [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]]'s death.[1] According to Ehret, the British operatives who then took over American foreign policy initiated the special relationship, turned the USA into an imperial beast, wiped out the remaining pro-American forces in the [[state-department|State Department]], disbanded the [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]], took control of the [[united-nations|UN]]'s constitution at a founding conference held two weeks after Roosevelt died, and reconstituted America's intelligence services as the MI6-modelled [[cia|CIA]] in 1947.[1] The unnecessary dropping of nuclear bombs on a defeated Japan is said to have undermined America's moral foundations while signalling to Russia that hopes for a US–Russia–China postwar order founded on mutual development were over.[1]

## Opposition

Opposition to the special relationship has a military lineage. Brigadier General [[billy-mitchell|Billy Mitchell]] fought it "tooth and nail" and pushed the American military to prepare [[war-plan-red|War Plan Red and War Plan Orange]], made doctrine in 1930 and shelved only when Washington judged it more urgent to put down London's fascist creation than to fight Britain directly.[1] The 2010 documentary "The Special Relationship is for Traitors" is cited as showcasing this current of American military thought.[1]

## The case for ending it

Ehret argues that the collapse of the special relationship would be a very good thing, because it would let America rediscover its anti-imperial nature by working with Russia, China, India, and other nations under a cooperative framework of space exploration and the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].[1] The return of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to the White House is presented as the opening in which the British imperial hold on America might finally be cut off.[1]
