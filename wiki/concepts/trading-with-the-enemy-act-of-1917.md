# Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917

The Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917 is one of the statutes cited as the legal authority for [[executive-order-6102|Executive Order 6102]], the April 5, 1933 order by which [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]] forbade the hoarding of gold coin, gold bullion, and gold certificates within the continental United States.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] groups the Act with the [[emergency-banking-act-of-1933|Emergency Banking Act]] of March 1933 as measures "just as repugnant to the spirit of freedom that was woven into the founding documents of the United States of America" as the gold order they underwrote.[1]

The Act figures in the essay's broader argument about emergency powers: that in the United States the government has used various "emergencies" to claim a power it never had — near-total control over the way citizens engage in business and commerce.[1] While the lawful basis for invoking emergency powers can be debated, the essay argues, citizens almost always have to rein in the government to recover their rights, and rarely possess the time, energy, and cohesive support to do so.[1]
