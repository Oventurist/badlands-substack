# California Dream For All

**Dream For All** is a California state down-payment assistance program created to help low-income, first-time homebuyers enter the housing market by supplying up to 20 percent of a home's purchase price.[1] The program was established through the passage of Assembly Bill 140 in 2021 and is administered through the state's housing finance agency.[1]

## Structure

Assistance is delivered through the Dream For All Shared Appreciation Loan.[1] Under this mechanism the state provides a portion of the buyer's down payment in exchange for a share in the property itself; the loan, together with a portion of the home's appreciated value, is repaid when the property is later resold.[1] The design therefore makes the state a co-participant in the equity of the home rather than a simple grant-maker.[1]

## Exhaustion of funds (April 2023)

Demand for the program overwhelmed the system almost immediately after launch. Its $300 million budget was depleted in fewer than 12 days, and the housing finance agency paused applications effective April 7, 2023.[1] By that point the program had assisted more than 2,400 first-time homebuyers with their down payments in its first two weeks.[1]

Senate President Pro Tempore Toni Atkins (D-San Diego), the legislator responsible for proposing the program, responded optimistically in an April 14 statement, calling the launch "incredible and inspiring" and describing the uptake as "terrific."[1] The episode was reported by [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]].[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the program as a "slavery disguised as opportunity" play, arguing that subsidizing loans for buyers priced out of desirable metropolitan and suburban markets results in the government "taking ownership of a piece of your loan."[1] The commentary contended that the arrangement leaves the buyer indebted simultaneously to the bank and to the state, and questioned whether any meaningful distinction between the two exists.[1]
