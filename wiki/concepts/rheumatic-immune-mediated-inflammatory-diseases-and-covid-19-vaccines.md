# Rheumatic immune-mediated inflammatory diseases and COVID-19 vaccines

Rheumatic immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (R-IMIDs) are conditions involving inflammation of the joints, tendons, muscles, and bones arising from an unknown cause.[1] In October 2023, a review study reported that [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] "may trigger" such diseases, including arthritis, vasculitis, lupus, and adult-onset Still's disease.[1]

## Findings

The study, led by researchers from the National Health Service in the United Kingdom and published in the journal Vaccines, examined 271 participants drawn from 190 case studies published worldwide.[1] On average, patients developed rheumatic disease 11 days after vaccine administration.[1] Seventy-five patients — over 27 percent — experienced total disease remission, and roughly 50 percent improved following treatment; eight were admitted to intensive care and two died from their symptoms.[1]

More than 80 percent of the affected patients developed symptoms after their first or second dose, and most were treated and improved with corticosteroids.[1] By product, almost 57 percent of the injured patients had received the [[pfizer|Pfizer]] vaccine, nearly a quarter the [[astrazeneca|AstraZeneca]] vaccine, and 12 percent of the rheumatic diseases manifested after administration of the [[moderna|Moderna]] vaccine.[1]

The authors wrote that "the short time span between COVID-19 vaccine administration and the onset of R-IMIDs suggests the potential possibility of a cause-and-effect relationship."[1] The findings were reported by [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] and carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief.[1]
