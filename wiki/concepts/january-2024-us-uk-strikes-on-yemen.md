# January 2024 US-UK Strikes on Yemen

The **January 2024 US-UK strikes on Yemen** were a joint American and British bombing campaign against Ansarallah-led forces in Yemen, notable for the refusal of Europe's major powers to participate in or endorse the operation.[1]

## The strikes

Washington and London carried out late-night strikes on Yemen on a Thursday evening in January 2024, targeting several areas of the country including the capital, Sanaa.[1] The stated purpose was to degrade the ability of Yemen's Ansarallah-led forces — commonly referred to as the [[houthis|Houthis]] — to target Israeli-linked shipping in the [[red-sea-shipping-crisis|Red Sea]], a campaign the movement had undertaken in response to Israel's bombing of Gaza, which many observers characterized as genocide.[1] Reporting emphasized that Yemen is one of the world's poorest countries.[1]

## European division

Germany, Denmark, the Netherlands, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Korea and Bahrain signed a joint statement backing the bombing.[1] Europe's major powers — France, Italy and Spain — refused to take part in the strikes and declined to sign the supporting statement, leaving the continent visibly divided over the operation.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], framed the episode through what he called a "Bicameral" view distinguishing Actuals from Narratives, arguing that the refusal of the European great-power bloc to endorse the [[joe-biden|Biden administration]]'s regional "peacekeeping" had flown under the radar.[1] He traced the pattern back to those states' earlier refusal to commit to [[operation-prosperity-guardian|Operation Prosperity Guardian]] and suggested that, if [[devolution|Devolution]] were in play, legacy EU powers declining to endorse the strikes pointed toward the emergence of a peaceful, multipolar world order.[1]
