# 2018 Midterm Elections

The 2018 United States midterm elections are treated in Badlands Media commentary as a decisive node in the conflict between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and what Q drops call the [[old-guard|Old Guard]].[1] In the July 2025 article "Shall We Play a Game?", [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller special counsel investigation]] was deliberately timed to run up to the midterms, quoting Q drop 2381's assertion that the special counsel was "designed to 'stall-for-time' until MIDTERM ELECTIONS to TERMINATE all HOUSE / SENATE ongoing investigations" and to mobilise Democratic turnout and donations.[1]

## Outcome and its significance

Democrats did take back the House of Representatives, but in Lange's framing they were "prevented" from taking the Senate, which allowed the investigations into the alleged Obama-era spying operation to continue in the Senate Judiciary Committee under [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]].[1] The inquiries into [[fisagate|FISAgate]] and [[obamagate|Obamagate]], he writes, simply migrated from the House to the Senate.[1]

Lange identifies three further consequences of Republican control of the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] after 2018. It ensured confirmation of Trump's cabinet nominees; more importantly, it ensured confirmation of his judicial nominees, including Supreme Court justices, which he credits with flipping the federal courts.[1] Third, and in his account most critically, it made removal by [[first-impeachment-of-donald-trump|impeachment]] impossible, since conviction requires a two-thirds Senate vote — a protection he says Q had signalled from the beginning.[1] Trump was impeached twice and acquitted both times by the Senate.[1]

## Game theory framing

The article repeatedly returns to a device it labels "GAME THEORY," a series of claims that Trump anticipated each enemy move in advance: that Democrats would call for a special counsel, that it would be [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]], that they would impeach him, and that the Senate would not remove him.[1] Under this reading the midterm result was not a setback partially averted but an outcome accounted for within a pre-existing plan, with [[declas|DECLAS]] serving to override any blockade mounted by the newly Democratic House.[1]
