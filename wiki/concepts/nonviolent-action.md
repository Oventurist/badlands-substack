# Nonviolent Action

"Nonviolent action" is the term of art used by the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]], the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] network, and allied scholars to describe organized civil resistance. Badlands Media treats it as a euphemism — the rebranding of tactics set out in the declassified CIA manual [[psychological-operations-in-guerrilla-warfare|Psychological Operations in Guerrilla Warfare]] into "something more deceptive and palatable."[1]

## Origins of the term

The article dates the term's adoption to the 1980s, when the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], special forces, intelligence agencies, and government-funded "civil society" cut-outs had honed their infiltration skills.[1] The year after the Contra-era manual was published marked what the author calls the "great restructuring," when the [[usaid|USAID]] and NED network began rapidly proliferating institutions to serve as CIA cut-outs, each with its own area of interest.[1]

## Badlands' definition

The article offers a deliberately blunt glossary, defining "nonviolent action" as engineered mob violence; rent-a-riot smart mobs hurling bricks through storefront windows; blocking highways, attacking infrastructure, and arson; and in effect all violence short of intentional first-degree murder, occasionally including accidental death.[1]

## Applications

Organized nonviolent action is described as the primary ingredient in foreign [[color-revolution|color revolutions]] and domestic "rent-a-riots," deployed in the Middle East, the former Yugoslavia, and Ukraine, and more recently turned inward against the rise of populism in the United States.[1] The military can conduct comparable operations through special forces, but the article argues it is far more deniable when the activity appears to originate in "civil society" rather than the US military.[1]

This fits within the NATO "from tanks to tweets" doctrine popularized in 2019 by former Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]], the premise being that most modern warfare occurs in the cognitive battlespace and that shaping public consensus is critical to achieving objectives, often regime change.[1] The article notes that while Stoltenberg framed disinformation as an adversary threat, [[nato|NATO]], the United States, and the EU effectively wrote the book on hybrid warfare and have deployed it globally.[1]

## Practitioners and vocabulary

[[maria-stephan|Maria Stephan]] and [[erica-chenoweth|Erica Chenoweth]] are identified as the leading contemporary organizers and theorists of nonviolent action, working through USIP, the [[horizons-project|Horizons Project]], and the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]].[1] Associated jargon — what critics call "CIA-speak" — includes "nonviolence scholars," "strengthening democracy," and "[[democratic-backsliding|democratic backsliding]]."[1]
