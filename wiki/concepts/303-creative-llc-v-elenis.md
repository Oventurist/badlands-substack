# 303 Creative LLC v. Elenis

303 Creative LLC v. Elenis is a United States Supreme Court case decided on Friday, June 30, 2023, in which the Court sided with Christian graphic web designer [[lorie-smith|Lorie Smith]] against the state of Colorado's anti-discrimination law as applied to expressive wedding-website design for same-sex couples.[1]

## Background

Smith, owner of the design company 303 Creative, sued Colorado over a statute prohibiting businesses from denying services based on a customer's sexual orientation. She contended that the law would compel her and other artists to produce customized messages that contradicted her religious beliefs and violated her rights under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]]. Smith's firm had previously served gay customers, but she sought to restrict her wedding-website business to heterosexual couples.[1]

## Decision

The [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruled 6–3, split along ideological lines. The conservative majority held that the First Amendment bars Colorado from forcing "an individual to speak in ways that align with its views but defy her conscience about a matter of major significance."[1]

## Reception in the Badlands corpus

The ruling was covered in the July 3, 2023 Badlands News Brief as one of a run of Supreme Court decisions — alongside rulings on [[affirmative-action|affirmative action]] in college admissions and the striking down of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s student loan relief program — that Badlands writers characterized as victories for a sovereign, constitutionalist American mindset achieved while a Democratic administration held the presidency.[1] [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]] described the same batch of rulings as evidence the Court was "creeping dangerously towards authoritarianism."[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] connected the case to the earlier and continuing litigation against baker [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]] of [[masterpiece-cakeshop|Masterpiece Cakeshop]], arguing that despite Smith's win, the [[colorado-civil-rights-commission|Colorado Civil Rights Commission]] and state courts had shown that adverse appellate rulings did not prevent renewed enforcement actions against religious dissenters.[1]
