# IRS–ICE Taxpayer Data Sharing Agreement

The IRS–ICE taxpayer data sharing agreement is a 2025 arrangement between the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] and [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] permitting immigration authorities to request protected tax records in support of deportation operations.[1]

## Disclosure and terms

The agreement was disclosed in a court filing on Monday, April 7, 2025, with a partially redacted version submitted in the case.[1] Under its terms, ICE officials can ask the IRS for information about people who have been ordered to leave the United States or whom they are otherwise investigating.[1] The filings stated that the two agencies had not yet shared any information.[1]

## Legal context

Federal law tightly controls taxpayer information, protecting home addresses, earnings, and other data from disclosure even to other government agencies.[1] The deal is described as a fundamental departure from decades of practice at the tax collector, which had sought to keep information submitted by undocumented immigrants confidential.[1] IRS officials raised concerns for weeks about the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's plan to use the agency to assist deportations, warning during negotiations over the agreement that it could be illegal.[1]

## Fallout at the IRS

Several top IRS officials, including the acting commissioner, prepared to resign over the arrangement.[1] The deal fueled further turmoil at the top of an agency already rocked by mass layoffs and several leadership changes during filing season, its busiest period of the year, when millions of Americans submit their taxes.[1]
