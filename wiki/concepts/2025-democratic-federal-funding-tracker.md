# 2025 Democratic Federal Funding Tracker

The 2025 Democratic federal funding tracker is a public accounting published on Tuesday, April 29, 2025 by top Democratic funding negotiators in the House and Senate, intended to document federal funding blocked under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] It accused his administration of targeting at least $430 billion in funds.[1]

## Contents

Compiled by Democratic appropriations staff, the tracker details what those offices describe as the "minimum amount" of funds believed to be frozen, cancelled, or that the administration is fighting to block in court.[1] It initially highlighted 114 programs that Democrats said had seen their funds frozen, cancelled or terminated during the president's first 100 days in office.[1]

Democrats acknowledged that the list included some programs the administration had already been ordered to release, in cases where Trump officials were "actively fighting in court to block it."[1] They cautioned in an accompanying release that "any relief provided by the courts at this stage is only temporary," and noted instances in which federal funds remained inaccessible to intended recipients even after a court order.[1] One high-profile example was the $12 million appropriated for [[radio-free-europe|Radio Free Europe]], restored by order of Judge [[royce-lamberth|Royce Lamberth]] the same day.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] welcomed the confrontation — "Bring on the impoundment fight" — arguing that the Uniparty knows it cannot win, as shown by Democrats' own admission that court-ordered relief is temporary.[1] His constitutional claim was that the President has, and must have, authority to defund the agencies and operations of the Executive Branch over which he holds total plenary authority, may stop their work and fire every employee, and should do so across the board as fast as reasonably possible.[1]
