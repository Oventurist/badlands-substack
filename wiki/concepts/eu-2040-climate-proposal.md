# EU 2040 Climate Proposal

The EU 2040 climate proposal was the [[european-commission|European Commission]]'s plan for a new 2040 greenhouse-gas reduction target, which was substantially watered down in February 2024 in response to the [[2024-european-farmer-protests|European farmer protests]].[1]

## Dropped provisions

Under the revised plan, "all sectors" were said to need to contribute to the effort, but the previously mandated 30 percent cut to agricultural production by 2040 was removed.[1] The revised draft also excluded a mandate for citizens to make lifestyle changes such as eating less meat, and dropped a push to end fossil fuel subsidies.[1]

European Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra, who unveiled the revision, said: "We need to make sure we have a balanced approach. The vast majority of our citizens sees the effects of climate change, does want protection, but is also worried about what that implies for their livelihood."[1]

Separately, the EU scrapped plans to halve pesticide use across the bloc by 2030 as part of the so-called Green Deal.[1] The changes were spearheaded by the center-right European People's Party — the political family of Commission president [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] and the largest group in the EU Parliament.[1]
