# Bering Strait Tunnel

The **Bering Strait Tunnel** is a proposed 200 km rail tunnel connecting Russia and Alaska, described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as a century-old project with "the power to transform international relations and redefine economic value forever."[1]

## Modern proposals

Ehret writes that on May 7, 2014, the Chinese government, speaking through the state-owned Beijing Times and China Daily, gave its support to the project.[1] Many North Americans, he notes, will be surprised to learn that the proposal was first advanced by Russia in August 2007; with China behind it, the tunnel can no longer be ignored.[1] In 2011 the Russian government re-emphasized its commitment to the project, and like Bush Jr. in 2007, President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] once again ignored [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s offers.[1]

## Relation to the land-bridge tradition

The tunnel is the missing link in the Eurasian land-bridge tradition championed in the nineteenth century by [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]], whose 1890 *Cosmopolitan Railway* advocated a trans-continental railway extending into Eurasia.[1] In the present it would join Russia's Far Eastern Vision, the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]] and China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] to North America via the [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska-Canada Railway]].[1]

## 2026 US–Russia design agreement

Kremlin special envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]] announced that Russia and the United States would sign an agreement on June 5, 2026 to advance the design phase of a tunnel connecting Russia's Chukotka region with Alaska across the Bering Strait.[2] "Regarding the tunnel. We will have news tomorrow: we are signing an agreement to continue the tunnel design. The tunnel will be built," Dmitriev said, describing it as one of the most significant infrastructure initiatives envisioned between the two countries.[2] The proposal envisions a fixed land link between Asia and North America and has been promoted by Russian officials as a potential symbol of expanded economic cooperation with the United States.[2]

Dmitriev first introduced the idea in October 2025, estimating that a conventional build could exceed $65 billion while suggesting costs could fall below $8 billion if construction methods associated with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[the-boring-company|The Boring Company]] were used.[2]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the agreement in explicitly historical and religious terms, writing that [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and Nikita Khrushchev had once attempted to bridge the two continents before that project was shuttered by what he described as an insurgency that seized control of the United States with Kennedy's assassination on November 22, 1963.[2] In his account the same network killed [[jfk-jr|JFK Jr.]] and is now being confronted by [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s counterinsurgency.[2] He presented the signing as marking the formal emergence of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] in the public consciousness, cast a Russo-American alignment as a resurgence of Christianity, praised [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s restraint, and dubbed the project "the Putin-Trump Tunnel."[2]

## Nineteenth-century origins and the 1905 proposal

The project traces to December 1864, when President [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] told Congress that the proposed overland telegraph between America and Europe by way of Bering's Straits and Asiatic Russia had been undertaken "under very favorable circumstance," with most South American states ready to cooperate in constructing lines tributary to that world-encircling communication.[3] Russian Alaska was sold to the USA only three years later, explicitly around the notion of facilitating this program.[3]

In 1905 Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]], following the advice of [[sergei-witte|Sergey Witte]], gave national support to the Bering Strait tunnel and funded a team of American engineers to conduct feasibility studies.[3] The plans were completed in the same year that [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] financed Japan's victory in the [[russo-japanese-war|Russo-Japanese War]], and the project was overtaken by an age of bankers' wars, anarchist assassinations and economic turmoil.[3]

## 2007 revival and Arctic integration

In 2007 Russia revived the 150-year-old idea, which had once enjoyed the support of leading Republicans of [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]]'s nineteenth-century America, to unite rail lines in America and Eurasia through a 65-mile tunnel beneath the Bering Strait.[4] Russia re-emphasized its commitment to the $64 billion project in 2011.[4] With China's [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]] extending the traditionally east-west development corridor into the Arctic, and with China and Russia increasingly merging the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] with the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues this development dynamic offers economic opportunity to all Arctic nations and an escape from military confrontation.[4] He links it to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order reviving the [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska-Canada Railway]] as part of an [[open-systems-vs-closed-systems|open-system]] Arctic strategy.[4]

## Economic reform and the Bering Strait

In "The Strategic Importance of the Alaska-Canada Railway," [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the Bering Strait Rail Tunnel, together with the [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska-Canada Railway]], gives North America the opportunity to break out of British geopolitics while rebuilding former industrial cities ravaged by decades of globalized outsourcing and building new permanent cities.[5] He writes that the greatest benefit of building the Bering Strait crossing, and of its associated policy of nation building, is that Canada and the USA would need to return to a sane system of economic value based on future productivity rather than market-driven ventures into [[derivatives|derivatives]], noting that the American derivatives exposure stands at roughly $1.2 quadrillion of nominal assets.[5]

On this account systemic change requires a twofold restoration: [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall]], known in Canada as the "Four Pillars," to separate commercial banking assets fully from high-risk assets such as derivatives; and the revival of a genuine national banking system, as opposed to a private central banking system, as an issuer of productive credit.[5] A third, international element is the [[new-bretton-woods|New Bretton Woods]] fixed exchange rate system.[5] Ehret concludes that only the Bering Strait project offers all the preconditions for war avoidance, the defeat of "green suicide" and nation building, with the necessary effect of increasing the energy flux density of society as a whole.[5]

## Lineage from Lincoln to the 2025 Alaska summit

In "Trump, Putin, Alaska and the Future of Multipolarity," [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] traces the tunnel corridor to President [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s 1864 State of the Union address to Congress and argues that the choice of Alaska as the venue for the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|August 15, 2025 Trump–Putin meeting]] carried major symbolic value, since Russia's 1867 sale of Alaska to the United States flowed from the special US–Russia relationship that had helped save the Union during the Civil War.[6] He notes that the strait is separated by a gap of only 100 miles, with the two small Diomede islands in between, and describes the tunnel as a long overdue "science driver" capable of organizing long-term economic thinking for the USA and the growing Eurasian powers.[6]

After Lincoln's murder launched what Ehret calls an era of geopolitical destabilizations, assassinations and coups, the project was revived by Russian Prime Minister [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]] and Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] in 1906, who sponsored teams of American engineers to survey the route; a March 23, 1906 New York Times headline read "For Bering Strait Tunnel — Czar Approves Recommendation for All-Rail Route to America."[6] In 1902 the French engineer [[loicq-de-lobel|Loicq de Lobel]], employed by Nicholas II, predicted through trains within five or six years, promising "no more seasickness, no more dangers of wrecked liners, a fast trip in palace cars with every convenience."[6] Two early color revolutions and the murder of the czar halted that trajectory, and the plan was revived again by American Vice-President [[henry-wallace|Henry Wallace]] and his Russian counterparts in 1942 before the Cold War derailed it.[6]

The economist [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] began promoting the tunnel through hundreds of conferences and white papers starting in 1993.[6] In 2007 President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] formally offered his American counterparts the chance to build the project, with Russia paying 60% of the estimated $60 billion cost; the offer was rejected by George W. Bush and, when renewed in 2011 as an alternative paradigm to Arctic militarization, rejected again by the Obama White House.[6] Also in 2007, former Alaska governor [[walter-hickel|Walter Hickel]] told a Moscow conference sponsored by Russia's Council for the Study of Productive Forces that "the place to start is the Bering Strait," calling for a tunnel, a fiber optic cable link and long-distance transmission lines to the 1.6 billion people on earth without electricity.[6] Chinese state media began promoting the project in 2014, and by 2018 it gained new life with the announcement of the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]] as the Arctic extension of the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].[6]
