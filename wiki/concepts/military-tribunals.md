# Military Tribunals

Military tribunals are judicial bodies convened under military rather than civilian authority to try offenses such as treason, sedition, and crimes against humanity. In Badlands Media commentary they occupy a central place: they are presented as the venue in which alleged crimes by "Biden, Inc." and associated [[deep-state|Deep State]] actors would be adjudicated, bypassing civilian courts described as compromised by political and financial influence.[1]

## Claimed mechanics

**Jurisdiction under the UCMJ.** Tribunals are described as operating under the [[uniform-code-of-military-justice|Uniform Code of Military Justice]], addressing offenses that threaten national security and permitting prosecution of actors whose conduct is framed as wartime rather than ordinary criminality.[1]

**Sealed evidence.** Sensitive materials — whistleblower testimony, classified intelligence, intercepted communications, encrypted emails, private recordings, and financial trails — are said to remain sealed to protect national security and the integrity of ongoing operations. The article groups this with anticipated disclosures it labels the "full" Durham report, the Epstein files, the "Diddy files," the Weiner laptop files, and expanded Twitter Files.[1] Handling classified material without public exposure is presented as a core advantage of the military venue.[1]

**International reach.** Because the alleged crimes — funding bioweapons labs in Ukraine, trafficking operations spanning multiple countries — cross borders, the framing argues that a judicial process transcending traditional national boundaries is required. The article further claims that Vladimir Putin's conflict with Ukraine produced a body of crimes-against-humanity evidence entered officially before the United Nations.[1]

**Secrecy and strategic timing.** Maintaining secrecy is said to allow cases to be built meticulously and presented at the moment of maximum public impact and comprehension.[1]

**Historical precedents.** The Nuremberg Trials after the Second World War and post-9/11 counterterrorism commissions are cited as precedents for using sealed evidence until public disclosure is judged feasible, with present-day operations characterized as an adaptation to a battlefield of information, technology, and global reach — described as "5GW" or "5-D Chess."[1]

## Pardons and enemy-combatant designation

A key argument in this literature is that presidential pardons are "moot" in the tribunal context. Once an individual is designated an enemy combatant or enemy of the state, the claim goes, adjudication shifts to the UCMJ and civilian legal protections — including pardons, whether preemptive or retrospective — carry no weight.[1] Crimes against humanity in this telling include bioweapons development, human trafficking, and mass medical experimentation, all said to be prosecutable under international and military law.[1] The [[hunter-biden-pardon|pardon of Hunter Biden]] is therefore read as symbolic signaling rather than legal protection.[1]

## Relationship to Continuity of Government

Tribunals are described as functioning within [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocols, under which military leadership assumes control of critical functions when civilian institutions are judged compromised, keeping investigations, intelligence gathering, and judicial processes unimpeded.[1] The [[judge-advocate-generals-corps|Judge Advocate General's Corps]] is named as the body that "could be" handling the Biden-related cases.[1]

## Assessment

No evidence supports the existence of secret military tribunals trying American civilian political figures. Under U.S. law, military commissions have jurisdiction over a narrow category of defendants, principally alien unprivileged enemy belligerents, and their proceedings are docketed and publicly reported. The tribunal narrative described here is an interpretive framework advanced by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributors rather than a documented legal process.[1]
