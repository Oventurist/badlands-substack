# May 2026 Postponement of the U.S. Strike on Iran

In May 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that he had called off a military attack on the Islamic Republic of [[iran|Iran]] scheduled for the following day, after a request from three Gulf leaders.[1]

## The announcement

In a statement posted to [[truth-social|Truth Social]], Trump said he had been asked by the Emir of [[qatar|Qatar]], [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]], the Crown Prince of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud]], and the President of the United Arab Emirates, [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan]], to hold off on the planned military attack because serious negotiations were under way and, in their opinion as "Great Leaders and Allies," a deal would be reached that would be very acceptable to the United States and to all countries in the Middle East and beyond.[1] Trump specified that the deal would include, importantly, "NO NUCLEAR WEAPONS FOR IRAN."[1]

Citing his respect for those leaders, Trump said he had instructed Secretary of War [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff General [[dan-caine|Daniel Caine]], and the United States military that the scheduled attack would not proceed, while further instructing them to be prepared to go forward with a full, large-scale assault of Iran on a moment's notice in the event an acceptable deal was not reached.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Tamim, MBS and MBZ were the three leaders Trump visited on his Middle East trip the previous year, where he was greeted as a conquering hero, and described them as America's real allies in the region — leaders "choosing peace and prosperity over war and death."[1] He argued that Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE have made substantial progress cleaning up extremism in their countries over the past ten to twenty years, and predicted that they would receive no credit for the diplomacy from what he called "radicalized Christian Zionists," singling out Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] as demanding further bloodshed.[1] He called for the United States to address its own domestically radicalized pro-war constituency, framing the argument in explicitly religious terms as a call to abandon false idols and return to the teachings of Jesus.[1]
