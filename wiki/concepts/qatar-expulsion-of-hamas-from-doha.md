# Qatari Expulsion of Hamas from Doha

The Qatari expulsion of Hamas from Doha refers to reporting in November 2024 that, at the request of the United States, [[qatar|Qatar]] had told [[hamas|Hamas]] it must close its diplomatic office in the Qatari capital.[1] Senior Biden administration officials disclosed the decision to the [[times-of-israel|Times of Israel]], which reported on Friday, November 8, 2024, that the instruction had been delivered more than a week earlier.[1] The item was carried in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] edition "The Media Tucks Tail as Peacemakers Take the Field."[1]

## Background

Qatar had hosted Hamas officials in Doha since 2012, when the group moved its headquarters out of Damascus amid the Syrian civil war, and after successive US administrations of both parties urged Qatar to serve as a conduit to the organization.[1] Following the Hamas-led assault of October 7, 2023, the United States informed Qatar that Doha would not be able to maintain "business as usual" with the group, but the administration held off on demanding closure of the office because it viewed the communication channel as critical to brokering a ceasefire and hostage release.[1] Those talks produced a week-long deal in November 2023 but failed to secure a permanent ceasefire or the release of the remaining 101 hostages.[1]

A US official told the Times of Israel that Hamas's execution of American-Israeli hostage Hersh Goldberg-Polin along with five other captives in late August 2024, and the group's subsequent rejection of further ceasefire proposals, led the administration to conclude that Hamas's continued presence in Doha was "no longer viable or acceptable."[1]

## Related Gaza governance offer

In a companion item, the [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] reported that Israeli broadcaster KAN said Hamas had offered [[fatah|Fatah]] officials responsibility for managing the Gaza Strip, including control of border crossings and security apparatuses.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the two developments together as "a proof, of sorts, of President Trump's grand plan," tying them to a call [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held with Palestinian President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] in which Trump said he was ready to work toward a "comprehensive peace" between Israel and Palestine.[1] The analysis traced a chain of events from Trump's 2017 Riyadh trip and his relationship with Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]], through the [[qatar-diplomatic-crisis-2017|2017 Qatar embargo]] led by [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], to the collapsed 2017 Fatah–Hamas reconciliation deal and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s alleged transfer of more than $32 million in cash to Gaza to keep Hamas solvent.[1] The writer concluded that Tamim was "working with Trump against the forces trying to start WW3."[1]
