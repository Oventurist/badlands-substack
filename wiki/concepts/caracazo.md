# Caracazo

The **Caracazo**, also known as the *sacudón*, is the name given to the wave of protests, riots, looting, shootings and massacres that began on 27 February 1989 in Venezuela's capital, Caracas, and the surrounding towns.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treats the episode as the origin point of the political sequence that produced [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] and, ultimately, the long confrontation between [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and the United States foreign policy establishment.[1]

## Causes

The riots and protests erupted primarily in response to the Venezuelan government's neoliberal economic reforms, which triggered sharp increases in gasoline and transportation costs.[1] The reforms belonged to the broader pattern of debt-driven structural adjustment that DeLarme, following [[john-perkins|John Perkins]], describes as the standard mechanism by which the [[imf|International Monetary Fund]] and the [[world-bank|World Bank]] convert resource-rich states into compliant clients.[1]

## Repression

Over nine days of intense clashes, Venezuelan security forces and the military killed an estimated 2,000–3,000 people.[1] The urban counterinsurgency blueprint deployed against the rioters, [[plan-avila|Plan Ávila]], would later be revived with the quiet assistance of U.S. military advisors during the anti-Chávez campaigns of the early 2000s.[1]

## Aftermath

The Caracazo riots sparked widespread public outrage and paved the way for Hugo Chávez to launch a coup attempt three years later, in 1992.[1] Although the revolt failed spectacularly and landed Chávez in prison, it simultaneously transformed him into a national hero, particularly among the swelling ranks of the impoverished, who viewed him as an outsider capable of ending the political elite's cycle of corruption, scandal, and debt.[1]
