# 2025 Gabbard Treasonous Conspiracy Disclosure

The **2025 Gabbard treasonous conspiracy disclosure** refers to the release, in July 2025, of material by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] that she described as evidence of a "treasonous conspiracy in 2016 committed by officials at the highest level of our government."[1]

## The disclosure

The evidence Gabbard released pertained to the intelligence community's conclusion that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] decided to interfere in the 2016 presidential election on behalf of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The [[washington-post|Washington Post]] Fact-Checker asserted that the evidence on which Gabbard based her findings was "paper-thin" and discounted more substantiated intelligence findings.[1]

## Obama's response

[[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s office issued a rare admonishment of the Trump administration on Tuesday, July 22, 2025, calling the treason claims "a weak attempt at distraction."[1] The statement asserted that the intelligence findings "were affirmed in a 2020 report by the bipartisan Senate Intelligence Committee."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded that the Senate Intelligence Committee had released a heavily redacted report whose conclusions relied on testimony from the very people Trump says committed treason — meaning Obama was, in effect, arguing that "testimony from people like [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] and [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] affirms the now proven to be falsified and fabricated conclusions of people like John Brennan and Andrew McCabe."[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as a contest between two competing narratives — the "Satanic" label associated with the Epstein material and the "Treason" label associated with Russiagate — asking which would map more cleanly onto the collective American mind and thereby give Trump a mandate to act.[1] He argued the enemy does not fear "the list" but rather accountability, while cautioning that theories of fifth-generation warfare, pincer moves and narrative traps are "far from Copium," and asking why the opposition preferred to talk about the narrative that could bring down the whole structure rather than the one Trump says will.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] focused on the Washington Post Fact-Checker line, noting that "all assets [were] deployed" and that [[mollie-hemingway|Mollie Hemingway]] had been rebutting similar claims from NBC News the previous day.[1] She argued that the [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] released to the public — which formed the basis of a multi-year, $40 million special counsel investigation — was not "more substantiated" than the unmanipulated intelligence assessment produced before Trump won in 2016; rather, it was ordered by Obama as "a command — from the Commander in Chief — to create a false predicate" designed to "undermine and neutralize the sitting President."[1] Her conclusion: "It's literally treason. And they've told us now, with receipts and enough corroboration to convict."[1]

[[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] added that the "Deep State Distraction op with Epstein" would inevitably lead to more disclosures damaging to the establishment, so that it would "boomerang quite nicely," and that its promoters "must be REAL desperate if they're going to play with that fire."[1]
