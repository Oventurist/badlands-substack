# Google Autocomplete Trump Assassination Controversy

The Google autocomplete Trump assassination controversy was a late-July 2024 dispute over the apparent absence of search suggestions relating to the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] on July 13, 2024.[1]

## The complaint

Users searching [[google|Google]] for the attempted assassination reported that the autocomplete feature failed to surface predictions relating to the shooting, drawing accusations that the company was attempting to influence the presidential election.[1] Screenshots circulated showing Google instead recommending searches about the failed assassination of Ronald Reagan, the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand whose death sparked World War I, the shooting of Bob Marley, and the failed attempt on President Gerald Ford.[1] Users reported that even the keywords "Trump assassination attempt" yielded no additional suggested terms.[1]

[[donald-trump-jr|Donald Trump Jr.]] wrote on X that "Big Tech is trying to interfere in the election AGAIN to help [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]," calling it "intentional election interference from Google" and "truly despicable."[1]

## Google's response

A Google spokesperson told FOX Business that there had been no "manual action taken on these predictions," explaining that "our systems have protections against Autocomplete predictions associated with political violence, which were working as intended prior to this horrific event occurring," and that the company was "working on improvements to ensure our systems are more up to date."[1] The item was carried as a bonus entry in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief of July 30, 2024.[1]
