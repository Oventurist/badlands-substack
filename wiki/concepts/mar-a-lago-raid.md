# Mar-a-Lago Raid

The **Mar-a-Lago raid** refers to the FBI's search of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Mar-a-Lago property in Palm Beach, Florida, executed on August 8, 2022 as part of a federal investigation into his alleged improper retention of classified records.[1] Badlands Media and allied commentators treat the raid as an unprecedented act against a former president and a defining episode of what they describe as the weaponization of federal law enforcement.[1]

## Background and aftermath

Following the search, Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] was appointed to investigate the matter.[1] Smith ultimately brought 37 felony counts against Trump, including willful retention of national defense information, conspiracy to obstruct justice, and false statements; Trump pleaded not guilty to all counts.[1] The prosecution was later derailed when Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] ruled that Smith had been unlawfully appointed, and the Supreme Court's ruling on presidential immunity further undercut the government's position.[1]

## Trump's $100 million claim

In August 2024, reporting indicated that Trump intended to sue the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] for $100 million in damages over the raid, with his lawyers arguing the search was conducted with "clear intent to engage in political persecution."[1] A memo obtained by [[fox-news|Fox News]] alleged "tortious conduct by the United States against President Trump."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the suit as a turn from defense to offense, noting that the government would now have to justify its own conduct and that discovery could surface new revelations about the Biden administration's alleged plot to weaponize federal power against its most formidable political opponent.[1] The commentary invoked Trump's 2016 promise that supporters would grow "tired of winning," concluding that "the tide is turning."[1]

## January 19, 2021 declassification letter

In June 2023, the social-media commentator [[dc-draino|DC Draino]] published a letter signed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and dated January 19, 2021 — the day before he left office — which [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] reported as declassifying all of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] documents.[2] Those documents were described as proving that [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], [[james-comey|James Comey]], [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], [[james-clapper|James Clapper]], the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[cia|CIA]], the [[state-department|State Department]] and the Deep State had spied on Trump and knew the Trump–Russia collusion allegations were a hoax.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the letter provided new insight into the August 2022 raid and into what she characterized as the improvisational conduct of the [[justice-department|Department of Justice]] since.[2] The commentary noted that a signed letter is not legally required for a president to declassify, but that the supposed absence of such documentation had been central to the claim that Trump uniquely acted unlawfully — even after [[joe-biden|Biden]], [[mike-pence|Pence]], and Obama were each found to have retained documents.[2] DC Draino summarized the implication as: "Now you know why they raided Mar a Lago. To steal back evidence of their crimes."[2]
