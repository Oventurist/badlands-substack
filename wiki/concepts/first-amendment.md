# First Amendment

The First Amendment to the United States Constitution guarantees freedom of speech and is described by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as the most important right in the Bill of Rights.[1] The central proposition of his argument is that repellent speech is still free speech: a person has the legal right to voice even the most reprehensible positions, whether that is support for Hamas's actions or, conversely, calls for the indiscriminate killing of Palestinians.[1]

## Selective defense

In the years before the [[october-7-attacks|October 7th attacks]], numerous self-described free-speech champions emerged on the right, including [[bari-weiss|Bari Weiss]], [[dave-rubin|Dave Rubin]], [[christopher-rufo|Christopher Rufo]] and outlets such as the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]] and the Babylon Bee, all rallying against [[cancel-culture|cancel culture]] and especially against Americans being fired for their beliefs.[1] DeLarme argues these same figures fell silent or approved when Americans were fired for criticizing Israel, revealing a commitment tethered to tribal loyalty rather than principle.[1]

## Erosion by legislation

The article identifies the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]] as concrete threats to the amendment, and warns that politicians seeking to erode it typically preface their intentions with formulas such as "college campuses are allowed free speech, BUT…".[1] DeLarme concludes that true defenders of the First Amendment must reject selective outrage and defend all speech, because once a precedent for [[government-sponsored-censorship|government-sponsored censorship]] is set, it will be turned against everyone.[1]
