# First Amendment

The First Amendment to the United States Constitution protects freedom of speech, and is described by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as the most important right in the Bill of Rights.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) His essay "A Glaring Double Standard" is framed as a defense of the amendment against selective application by partisans of both left and right.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Protected but repellent speech

DeLarme's core principle is that repellent speech remains free speech.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He argues that the national leadership of [[national-students-for-justice-in-palestine|National Students for Justice in Palestine]] expressing support for Hamas's October 7 attack was protected speech, however reprehensible, and that statements wishing for the death of every man, woman, and child in Gaza — sentiments he attributes to figures including [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] and Lindsey Graham — enjoy equal protection.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) Florida State Senator [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] posted tweets claiming there were no innocent Palestinian civilians, calling them "demons that live on Earth" who "deserve death."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

He also states that people should be allowed to hold opinions no matter how unpalatable, and that it is dangerous when employers attempt to impose beliefs on employees under threat of firing — a position he says he did not abandon after October 7, 2023, unlike many prominent conservative commentators.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Threats identified

DeLarme identifies the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]] as censorship frameworks that threaten to criminalize dissent under vague definitions of [[hate-speech|hate speech]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He warns that those seeking to erode the First Amendment always preface their intentions with formulations such as "College campuses are allowed free speech, BUT…", with the true intention following the "but."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) His conclusion is that true defenders of the amendment must reject selective outrage and defend all speech, because once a precedent is set the [[deep-state|blob]] will not hesitate to turn it against everyone.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Litigation as a threat to protected speech

The amendment's protections were tested directly in [[naacp-v-useip|*NAACP et al. v. USEIP et al.*]], a Colorado federal suit that a prevailing pro se defendant described as an unprecedented attack on the First Amendment by the leftist [[lawfare]] machine, brought under the guise of fighting voter suppression.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back) The plaintiffs' theory treated two protected activities — questioning the outcome of an election and door-to-door [[canvassing]] — as inherently intimidating conduct without establishing any objectively intimidating act.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

Defendants argued that [[election-denial|questioning election outcomes]] has a long, well documented, and fully protected history in America running from 1800 to 2016, and that canvassing is likewise protected by settled case law.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back) The [[us-district-court-of-colorado|District of Colorado]] entered judgement for the defendants on partial findings in July 2024, finding no evidence of intimidation.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

## The 2026 war for the First Amendment

In March 2026, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] threatened media organizations with treason charges over their coverage of the war with [[iran|Iran]], writing on Truth Social that "fake news" outlets were spreading false information he said Tehran had fabricated using artificial intelligence, including AI-generated imagery of strikes on U.S. refueling aircraft and the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln.[3] Asked about reports of a marine expeditionary deployment to the Middle East, Trump told a reporter, "You are a very obnoxious person," before repeating the AI-fabrication claim.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the treason threat as one stanza in a much larger war for the First Amendment that he had covered for three years — what he calls a Zionist plot to dismantle the amendment using "antisemitism" as a Trojan Horse.[3] He traces the effort to the adoption of the [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|IHRA definition of antisemitism]] via legislation and executive orders in 38 states, with [[florida-hb-269|Florida HB 269]] — sponsored by [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] and signed by [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] in Jerusalem — as the most egregious expansion.[3] He warns the same logic will eventually criminalize criticism of the U.S. government on incitement grounds.[3]

The campaign against [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] — Fine's "most dangerous antisemite" label, [[max-miller|Max Miller]]'s call for imprisonment amplified by [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]], [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]]'s lobbying of the Justice Department, and the claimed [[cia|CIA]] referral under the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]] — is presented as the sharp end of that dynamic.[3] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued Trump was baiting both the pro-Israel faction and the corporate press into a collision over the amendment, and concluded: "Accelerate."[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] grounded the case in history, quoting [[george-washington|George Washington]]'s 1783 address to his troops warning that "if Men are to be precluded from offering their sentiments... the freedom of Speech may be taken away—and, dumb and silent we may be led, like sheep, to the Slaughter."[3] Citing Hiram Johnson's 1917 line that truth is the first casualty of war and Sun Tzu's maxim that all warfare is based on deception, she argued that no reason, in war or peace, can justify limiting speech.[3] She noted that calls to censor [[badlands-media|Badlands]] hosts intensified that week, and credited [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] with holding the line: "There is no other hill that truly matters than the right of people to say what they believe."[3]

## The antisemitism-enforcement debate (March 2026)

In March 2026, Badlands commentary framed the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]]'s post-shooting advocacy as an attempt to build public consent for dismantling the First Amendment.[4] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the strategy pursued by [[jonathan-greenblatt|Jonathan Greenblatt]] "is actually sound, if your intended endgame is to formally dismantle the First Amendment," calling it an example of the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]].[4]

He pointed to Greenblatt blaming podcasters and social media influencers for inciting the [[west-bloomfield-synagogue-attack|Michigan synagogue attack]], to his invocation of the [[pager-attack|pager operation]] against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] as a model of ingenuity for fighting [[antisemitism|antisemitism]], and to the ADL's development of an AI designed to detect antisemitic attitudes online and report them to relevant authorities.[4] He concluded that Americans may face a choice between maintaining [[israel|Israel]] as an ally and keeping the First Amendment.[4]

## Foreign censorship of US speech (2026)

An interim staff report by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] released in April 2026 warned that [[brazil|Brazil]]'s [[censorship-regime|censorship regime]], led by Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]], was targeting speech inside the United States and pressuring American social media companies to comply or face legal action and removal from the country.[5] The committee said that forcing American companies to censor US residents' speech creates a broader threat to First Amendment protections, and that it would continue oversight and pursue legislative responses to counter foreign censorship threats.[5]

## Press freedom and the Natanson search (January 2026)

The January 14, 2026 [[fbi|FBI]] search of [[washington-post|Washington Post]] reporter [[hannah-natanson|Hannah Natanson]]'s home became a focal point in the debate over First Amendment press protections, with press freedom groups warning it could chill investigative journalism and the Post's editorial board describing it as a serious threat to constitutional press protections.[6] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] defended the action on national security grounds.[6]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] articulated a tension within the movement's own commitments, placing First Amendment freedoms at the top of the rights she would defend while arguing that the oath of office phrase "all enemies, foreign and domestic" contains no exemption for those holding press credentials.[6] She invoked the principle "all means all," drawing on a judge's rejection during the [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] appeal oral arguments of the state's argument that destroying election records was acceptable so long as backups existed.[6]

## Parody of public officials: the Afroman verdict (2026)

On March 19, 2026 an Ohio jury ruled unanimously in favor of rapper [[afroman|Afroman]] in a civil suit brought by seven Adams County Sheriff's Office deputies over satirical songs and videos he made about a fruitless no-knock raid on his home.[7] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] called the verdict an important affirmation of the First Amendment right to parody and criticize public officials, noting that a contrary result would have chilled artistic and political speech nationwide and that parody and song have been used to criticize government officials since the founding of the Republic.[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], describing herself as a First Amendment maximalist who had already defended her right to speak about elections in federal court and won, used the verdict to warn against a growing appetite on the political right for viewpoint discrimination now that its side holds power, calling the position unprincipled and dangerous.[7]

## Proposals to limit the amendment (December 2025)

On December 29, 2025, [[shlomo-kramer|Shlomo Kramer]], the Israeli-American CEO of [[cato-networks|Cato Networks]], appeared on CNBC and argued for limiting the First Amendment in order to "protect it" from AI-generated deepfakes and misinformation he said could polarize society.[8] Kramer proposed AI-driven "authenticity scores" for social media content and greater government-platform control over information verification, framing the measures as national security necessities amid rising cyber warfare risks.[8] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the proposal within what he described as a wave of Israeli billionaires, politicians, and influencers challenging free speech, and argued that the ethos behind it — that the security of the state outranks the individual — is foreign to the American constitutional tradition.[8]

## Jankowicz v. Fox News

The May 2023 defamation suit brought by [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]] against [[fox-news|Fox News]] was framed by Badlands commentators as a direct test of First Amendment principles.[9] Because Jankowicz had headed the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]] under the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], she was in that view a representative of the government, and criticizing the government is among the most profoundly protected rights under the amendment.[9] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that a government official seeking exemption from criticism by a major news network was "stunning," particularly one charged with policing speech, and warned that a ruling in her favor would show that constitutional protections for media entities and individuals were no longer in place.[9]

## Press freedom and the Assange case

Writing in August 2023, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she had once believed First Amendment protections for the press were impenetrable, recalling that the entire media establishment rallied to Fox News's defense when the Obama administration sought to exclude it from the press pool.[10] She contrasted that with the absence of solidarity for journalist Simon Ateba, who sued the Biden administration over press-pool discrimination, and argued that the treatment of [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] made this environment inevitable: "a government that punishes journalists is the definition of tyranny."[10]

## Davos hate speech prediction (2023)

At the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in January 2023, [[european-commission|European Commission]] Vice President [[vera-jourova|Věra Jourová]] predicted that the United States would soon adopt criminal hate speech laws of the European kind.[11] Commentators noted that the Supreme Court has repeatedly and unanimously reaffirmed that there is no hate-speech exception to the First Amendment.[11] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that whatever the Davos class intended, "we have something in America called the 1st Amendment, and we are willing to defend it."[11]
