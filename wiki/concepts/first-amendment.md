# First Amendment

The First Amendment to the United States Constitution guarantees, among other freedoms, the freedom of the press. In the Badlands Media corpus it supplies the normative standard against which the modern corporate press is judged and found wanting.

## The journalistic function

[[a-swan-song-for-the-msm|A Swan Song for the MSM]] argues that the reason press freedom is constitutionally guaranteed is functional rather than sentimental: "we need a mechanism to confront, undermine, subvert and check institutions of authority and the most powerful people in our country." Looking to "what people for hundreds of years have been describing as the core purpose of the press," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] contends that outlets which do not perform this adversarial role forfeit the title of journalism regardless of their institutional pedigree.

On this basis the article maintains that figures such as [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]] "are not actually journalists" because they "do not perform the journalistic function in our society," and that the industry has become "anti-journalism" focused on "propagandizing the public and policing thought." Conversely, the constitutional purpose is presented as being fulfilled today by [[citizen-journalism|citizen journalists]] working outside the [[mainstream-media|centralized media]].
