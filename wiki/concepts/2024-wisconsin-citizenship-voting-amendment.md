# 2024 Wisconsin Citizenship Voting Amendment

The **2024 Wisconsin Citizenship Voting Amendment** was a ballot measure approved by Wisconsin voters on November 5, 2024, amending the state constitution to require that a person be a United States citizen in order to vote in elections held in the state.[1]

## Provisions

The Wisconsin constitution previously provided that "Every United States citizen age 18 or older who is a resident of an election district" is a qualified voter; the amendment replaced the phrase "every United States citizen" with "only a United States citizen," converting a floor into an exclusive condition.[1]

## Result

The measure carried with 75 percent approval with more than 95 percent of the statewide vote tallied.[1]

## Background

The amendment followed calls from leaders across Wisconsin for a process allowing the state to check its voter rolls for noncitizens and remove them in the name of election integrity.[1] At the time, state election commissions could not cross-check their rolls against Wisconsin Department of Transportation records to verify that an estimated 90,000 individuals legally present in the state and eligible for driver's licenses had not registered to vote.[1] Several voting groups opposed the measure, including the League of Women Voters.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the lopsided margin to needle the premise of the opposition — "Imagine believing these voters voted for Democrats in other races" — while arguing that the broader problem is unresolved.[1] He wrote that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had secured an undeniable public mandate but that "the elections are still fake," and that the remedy for what he called stolen elections must come from the states and the people rather than from the presidency alone.[1]
