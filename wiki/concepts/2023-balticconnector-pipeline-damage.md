# 2023 Balticconnector Pipeline Damage

The 2023 Balticconnector pipeline damage was an incident in October 2023 in which a subsea natural gas pipeline and a parallel telecommunications cable linking [[finland|Finland]] and [[estonia|Estonia]] beneath the Baltic Sea were damaged in what the Finnish government said may have been a deliberate act.[1]

## Incident

The [[balticconnector|Balticconnector]] gas pipeline was shut down early on Sunday, 8 October 2023, after operators detected concerns that gas was escaping from a hole in the 77-kilometre (48-mile) line.[1] Finnish operator Gasgrid indicated that repairs could take months or longer.[1] The damage to the gas pipeline was believed to have occurred in Finnish waters, while the breach of the telecommunications cable took place in Estonian waters, according to Finnish authorities.[1] Finnish telecommunications operator Elisa, which operates the damaged cable, told [[reuters|Reuters]] that the distance between the cable and the pipeline was "significant," but declined to specify the exact separation.[1]

## Official response

Finnish President [[sauli-niinisto|Sauli Niinistö]] stated on Tuesday, 10 October, that "it is likely that damage to both the gas pipeline and the communication cable is the result of outside activity," adding that the cause was not yet clear and that the investigation would continue jointly between Finland and Estonia.[1] Finnish Prime Minister [[petteri-orpo|Petteri Orpo]] called the damage "worrying" but said Finland's energy supply remained stable and that the cable breach had not affected the country's overall connectivity; he noted that gas accounts for about 5 percent of Finland's energy supply and cautioned that "it is too early to draw conclusions on who or what caused the damage."[1]

[[nato|NATO]] Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] said the alliance was sharing information about the damage and stood ready to support the allies concerned.[1] Finland had joined NATO in April 2023, while Estonia had been a member since 2004.[1]

## Commentary and narrative dispute

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the episode as "another attack on Russian energy," arguing that the party benefiting most from sabotage in the Baltic was the US establishment.[1] She noted that as news of the suspected sabotage broke on Tuesday afternoon, anonymous "inside sources" circulated claims on Reddit and Twitter/X that Russia had attacked the pipeline.[1] Comparing the episode to the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream sabotage]], she predicted the eventual official explanation would again point to "pro-Ukrainian" militants with no formal ties to the Ukrainian state.[1] The framing gave the Badlands brief in which it appeared its "Nordstream 3" shorthand.[1]
