# North Korean Troop Deployment to Russia

The reported deployment of [[north-korea|North Korean]] troops to Russia in October 2024 became a major escalation storyline in the Russia–Ukraine war, prompting [[south-korea|South Korea]] to weigh direct arms shipments to Ukraine.[1]

## Intelligence reports

South Korea's spy agency, the National Intelligence Service (NIS), said in mid-October 2024 that North Korea had shipped 1,500 special forces personnel to Russia's Far East for training and acclimatisation at local military bases ahead of future combat alongside Moscow's troops in Ukraine.[1] Local media citing the NIS reported that Pyongyang had decided to dispatch 12,000 troops in total, organized into four brigades.[1] Seoul's assessment rested in part on satellite imagery.[1]

## South Korean response

A senior official in the office of South Korean President Yoon Suk Yeol said on Tuesday, October 22 that Seoul could consider providing defensive and lethal weapons to Ukraine depending on developments: "We would consider supplying weapons for defensive purposes as part of the step-by-step scenarios, and if it seems they are going too far, we might also consider offensive use."[1] The statement signalled Seoul's most proactive posture toward arming Ukraine to that date, and underscored the potential for a divided Korean peninsula to become entangled in the conflict.[1]

## North Korean denial

North Korea dismissed the allegations as baseless during a United Nations General Assembly committee meeting, with a representative saying: "As for the so-called military cooperation with Russia, my delegation does not feel any need for comment on such groundless, stereotype rumors aimed at smearing the image of the DPRK and undermining the legitimate, friendly, and cooperative relations between sovereign states."[1]

## Drunken rampage allegations (May 2025)

In May 2025, British press reports accused North Korean soldiers serving with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s army of going on drunken rampages and terrorising local Russian communities.[2] Pyongyang had sent around 11,000 soldiers to Russia the previous year to boost Kremlin troop numbers on the front lines, and they were deployed to the Kursk region, where they fought Ukraine's army and helped retake most of the territory seized by Kyiv's forces the preceding August.[2] The reports alleged binge drinking, theft, and attempted rape.[2]

## U.S. confirmation (23 October 2024)

On Wednesday, 23 October 2024, Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] confirmed that the United States had evidence North Korean troops were in Russia, the first official U.S. acknowledgment of deployments that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and South Korea had previously warned about.[3] "There is evidence that there are DPRK troops in Russia," Austin said at a press conference, adding that what the troops were doing there remained to be seen and declining to specify the number involved.[3]

Austin warned of the consequences should the deployment prove operational: "If they're co-belligerents, if their intention is to participate in this war on Russia's behalf, that is a very, very serious issue," noting ramifications for both Europe and the Indo-Pacific.[3] He also suggested the development could indicate that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] "may be even in more trouble than most people realize."[3] Both [[north-korea|North Korea]] and [[russia|Russia]] denied the reports of the troop deployment.[3] Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had warned the previous week that at least 10,000 North Korean troops would soon join Russia in the war against his country, and South Korea had stepped up its alerts about the movements in the preceding days.[3] Commentators framed the deployment as the latest sign of a strengthening alliance between Moscow and Pyongyang, two of Washington's staunchest adversaries.[3]

## North Korean confirmation (April 2025)

On Monday, 28 April 2025, [[north-korea|North Korea]] confirmed for the first time that its troops had been fighting alongside Russia in the war against [[ukraine|Ukraine]], saying they had helped Moscow retake control of the Ukrainian-held portion of the Kursk region.[4] American, South Korean and Ukrainian officials had said North Korea sent as many as 12,000 troops to Russia in the fall to fight in Kursk, which Ukrainian forces seized in a surprise incursion in August; Pyongyang had neither confirmed nor denied those reports until then.[4]

North Korean leader [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] ordered the deployment under the mutual defense pact he and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] signed in June 2024, North Korea's Central Military Commission said in a statement carried by the state-run news agency KCNA.[4] The treaty includes a pledge of mutual defense if either country is attacked.[4] The statement said the operation to regain control of Kursk had been "victoriously concluded" and that it was "an honor to have an alliance with such a powerful state as the Russian Federation."[4] It cited Kim as saying the deployment was meant to "annihilate and wipe out the Ukrainian neo-Nazi occupiers and liberate the Kursk area in cooperation with the Russian armed forces," and quoted him declaring, "They who fought for justice are all heroes and representatives of the honor of the motherland."[4]

## US and South Korean demands for withdrawal (October 2024)

On 30 October 2024, US defence secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] called on North Korea to withdraw its troops from Russia, speaking at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] alongside his South Korean counterpart [[kim-yong-hyun|Kim Yong-hyun]].[5] Washington's assessment at the time was that 10,000 North Korean troops had been deployed for possible action against Ukrainian forces.[5] Austin said such personnel would be "co-belligerents" and that "you have every reason to believe that … they will be killed and wounded as a result of battle," echoing a [[white-house|White House]] statement that Pyongyang's forces would become "legitimate military targets" if they fought against Ukraine.[5] Despite the demand, Austin conceded there was a "good likelihood" Moscow would proceed regardless.[5]

Speaking before the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]], US envoy [[robert-wood|Robert Wood]] framed the warning more starkly, saying Pyongyang's forces "will surely return in body bags" and advising [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong-un]] to "think twice about engaging in such reckless and dangerous behaviour."[5] Reporting characterized the deployment as a significant escalation in a deepening Russia–North Korea political and military alliance that had grown as the Ukraine war dragged on.[5]
