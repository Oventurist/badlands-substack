# Pay to Slay

"Pay to slay" is the informal name for the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]]'s program of payments to imprisoned and released Palestinians convicted of terrorism-related offenses, and to the families of those killed carrying out attacks. It is discussed in the Badlands Media article "Bad Actors" by way of a Newsweek opinion piece.

## Legal basis

According to the account quoted in the article, the program is enshrined in Palestinian law. In 2004 the PA passed a law guaranteeing salaries for convicted terrorists and their families, with the legislation specifying that "prisoners and released prisoners are a fighting sector and integral part of the fabric of Arab Palestinian society." In 2010 the PA passed a series of resolutions stipulating that every Palestinian inmate in an Israeli prison convicted of a "terrorism-related offense" is to receive a monthly payment. In 2013 the then prime minister, [[rami-hamdallah|Rami Hamdallah]], amended the law to offer additional benefits upon release: any male ex-prisoner incarcerated for ten or more years, and every female ex-prisoner who served five, is entitled to a position in the PA.

## Relevance to U.S. aid

The Badlands Media article invokes the program to argue that the $221 million transferred to the Palestinian Authority in the final hours of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s presidency — released through [[usaid|USAID]] on the instruction of Secretary of State [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] — amounted to American taxpayer money flowing to a governing body that pays terrorists and their families for violence against Israelis, a practice the article notes had been publicly known for many years. The payment was subsequently frozen by the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].
