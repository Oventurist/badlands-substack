# Reverse Kissinger

"Reverse Kissinger" is a term used by American strategic commentators to describe an alleged effort by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to draw [[russia|Russia]] away from [[china|China]] — inverting Henry Kissinger's 1970s opening to Beijing, which was designed to split China from the Soviet Union.[1]

## Trade war context

Discussion of the concept intensified in April 2025 amid Trump's tariff campaign against China, because his sweeping global tariffs notably excluded Russia.[1] Analysts asked whether the tariff war contained an element intended to pull Moscow away from Beijing so that the global balance of power would remain favorable to Washington.[1]

Although sanctions-hit Russia does not trade heavily with the Western world, commentators speculated that a U.S.–China trade rift could marginally increase demand for some Russian products.[1] Russian agricultural exporters were said to potentially benefit, with South America emerging as a market and China becoming a buyer of Russian wheat and energy; Russia might also replace U.S. LNG supplies to China.[1] A further argument held that if the entire world became consumed by a trade war, attention to Russia and [[ukraine|Ukraine]] would diminish.[1]

Regardless of whether the trade war materially benefits Russia, [[eurasian-times|Eurasian Times]] noted that Trump has consistently held that it is against American interests for Russia and China to grow closer.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the real long-term dynamic should be read from the conduct of Trump and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] themselves, and that the trade war is not fundamentally between the United States and China but between what he calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and the [[central-banking|central banking system]].[1]

## Speculation during the April 2025 Ukraine talks

As Russia intensified strikes on Ukraine in early April 2025 and a top Russian envoy signalled that talks with Washington could resume, analysts believed Moscow was deliberately slow-walking negotiations.[2] Washington's continued engagement with Moscow fuelled speculation that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was attempting a "Reverse Kissinger" — courting Russia in order to isolate China.[2]
