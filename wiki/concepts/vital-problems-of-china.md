# Vital Problems of China

*Vital Problems of China* is a 1917 work by [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]] in which he set out an analysis of British imperial statecraft and its manipulation of China and the wider world.[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats the book as evidence that Sun's attack on the "old, evil capitalist system" was directed not at free enterprise as such, but at the principle of empire masquerading behind liberal theories of free trade with the intention of dividing to conquer.[1]

## The British method

Sun wrote that "the British are as cunning as the fox and as changeable as the weather and they are not ashamed of themselves," and that Britain "seeks friendship only with those which can render her services," discarding friends once they are too weak to be useful.[1] He compared Britain's regard for her friends to farmers rearing silkworms, which are destroyed by fire or fed to fish once the silk has been drawn, adding that "the present friends of Britain are no more than silkworms."[1]

Throughout the book Sun sought to demonstrate how the British oligarchy manipulated all the major wars of the 19th and early 20th centuries in pursuit of a "balance of power."[1] He summarized the technique: "the key policy of England is to attack the strongest enemy with the help of the weaker countries and join the weakened enemy in checking the growth of a 3rd country," noting that "the British foreign policy has remained basically unchanged for two centuries."[1]

Sun observed that when a nation is on the ascent, Britain allies with declining powers to undermine it, then reverses the policy once those allies grow strong — a pattern Ehret links to [[lord-palmerston|Lord Palmerston]]'s assertion that the empire has no permanent friends but only permanent interests.[1] In Sun's words: "When an enemy has been shorn of his power, he is turned into a friend, and the friend who has become strong, into an enemy. England always remains in a commanding position; she makes other countries fight her wars and she herself reaps the fruits of victory."[1]
