# Shadow Cabinet Proposal (2024)

The shadow cabinet proposal of November 2024 was a plan advanced by Representative [[wiley-nickel|Wiley Nickel]], Democrat of North Carolina, for congressional Democrats to organize a British-style opposition cabinet to contest the incoming second administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Background

Following the 2024 general election, Trump was headed back to the [[white-house|White House]] and Republicans held majorities in [[congress|Congress]], leaving Democrats with few procedural options to obstruct the majority's agenda.[1] Nickel argued in a Washington Post op-ed that the minority party should look to the United Kingdom, where the opposition maintains a team that mirrors the government's cabinet and publicly challenges, scrutinizes, and offers alternatives to its decisions — what he described as "another form of checks and balances — a quiet guardrail that keeps power accountable."[1]

## Structure

The plan called for appointing 26 Democratic leaders in Congress, each mirroring one of Trump's Cabinet-level officials and challenging that official's initiatives.[1] Reported examples included Senator-elect [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] as shadow attorney general, positioned to contest the replacement of career Justice Department attorneys with political loyalists, and Representative [[gregory-meeks|Gregory W. Meeks]] as shadow secretary of state, positioned to oppose reductions in U.S. support for Ukraine.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Badlands Media read the proposal as an open declaration of intent to subvert a duly elected president, prevent his government from functioning, and effectively overthrow it, and argued that it obviated the need for lengthy investigations to expose actors in Washington operating on behalf of what the outlet called a transnational crime syndicate.[1] The commentary drew a historical parallel to the Federalist Party, which it said was abolished at the end of the War of 1812 amid accusations that it had sold out to British interests, and treated Nickel's explicit citation of the British model as a modern echo of that pattern.[1] It further linked the London financial establishment to a "matrix of NGOs" including the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], and called for Nickel to be investigated for sedition.[1]
