# Ariosophy

**Ariosophy** was an occult sect that, in Matthew Ehret's account, formed the esoteric core of the Nazi leadership's belief system. It was led by a mystic, rune-obsessed poet named [[guido-von-list|Guido von List]], who took the Theosophy of [[helena-blavatsky|Madame Helena Blavatsky]] and infused it with an Aryan racial-superiority twist and a heightened focus on Wotan (Odin) myths.[1]

## Relationship to Theosophy

The Ariosophists were an outgrowth of the earlier occult order of the Theosophists, created by Blavatsky in 1875.[1] Blavatsky's system introduced the Western world to the swastika, Hermeticism, Kabbalah, esoteric Buddhism, and Kali-Isis earth mother goddess worship, and posited a spiritualized materialism in which spirit beings moved humanity through evolutionary phases of "root races," culminating in a Teutonic race expressed by pure-blooded Germanic tribes.[1] In *The Secret Doctrine* this Teutonic race was described as the last phase of the Aryan fourth root race, destined to usher in a fifth root race of trans-humans, or Übermenschen.[1] Ehret argues that Hitler's embrace of Darwinism, Nietzscheanism, and eugenics was therefore not as materialistic as modern scholars assume, but the material application of a Luciferian conceptual system first promoted by Blavatsky.[1]

## Doctrine and practice

Leading Ariosophist figures included von List and [[lanz-von-liebenfels|Lanz von Liebenfels]], who published a Teutonic mystical magazine called *Ostara* to which a young [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] was a devoted subscriber.[1] Within the sect, the swastika and other runic symbols — the Othala rune, the Ehlaz or life rune, the Sig runes later adopted by the [[schutzstaffel|SS]], and the wolfsangel — were treated as sacred images endowed with magical power.[1] Von List organized his following into an inner and outer core, with an "elect" learning a secret interpretation of the runes inside an elite occult body called the High Armanen Order, of which he served as Grand Master.[1]

The occult scholar Samuel Koehne is quoted describing Liebenfels as supporting the notion of Christianity as a "racial cult religion," reading the Old Testament as a warning about the dangers of racial admixture, treating Moses as a "Darwinist" and preacher of "racial morality," rewriting the Ten Commandments as laws of racial purity, and advocating a dualistic heresy in which the Aryan race-men and their savior Frauja — a Gothic name for Jesus — call for the sacrificial extermination of "sub-men," the "apelings," and all other racial inferiors.[1]

## Political consequences

The philosophy promoted by the Ariosophists and the [[thule-society|Thule Society]] priesthood held that Christianity was a Jewish plot to shackle the pure spiritual culture of the Wotan/Viking spirit of the pure-blooded Teutonic warriors who inhabited the Black Forest under the Roman Empire, a culture supposedly managed by a druidic high priesthood that used runes to interface with pagan deities.[1] This racist occult Aryanism, with its Theosophical aim of infusing Hindu and Buddhist mysticism into a new post-Christian age, became extremely popular among the noble families of Europe, promising an "Age of Aquarius" that would supersede an obsolete "Age of Pisces" associated with Socrates, Plato, and Christ.[1] Out of the High Armanen Order grew the Thule Society, and the occult network shaping the Nazi high command later organized a central headquarters at [[wewelsburg-castle|Wewelsburg Castle]].[1]
