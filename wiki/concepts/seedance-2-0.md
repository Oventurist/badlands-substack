# Seedance 2.0

Seedance 2.0 is a generative artificial-intelligence video tool released in China in February 2026 by the technology company [[bytedance|ByteDance]]. The system produces short cinematic videos from combinations of text, image, audio, and video inputs.

## Release and viral outputs

According to the February 17, 2026 [[badlands-brief-0a2|Badlands Brief]], clips generated with the model circulated widely online within days of release. The clips featured recognizably realistic portrayals of Hollywood actors and characters, including sequences interpreted by viewers as resembling scenes from big-budget productions.

## Studio response

The outputs prompted rapid legal action from major American studios. The [[walt-disney-company|Walt Disney Company]] and [[paramount-pictures|Paramount]] sent cease-and-desist letters to ByteDance alleging that Seedance 2.0 uses copyrighted material and characters without authorization. The letters were not publicly distributed but were first reviewed and reported by [[axios|Axios]]. Disney's attorneys wrote that "over Disney's well-publicized objections, ByteDance is hijacking Disney's characters by reproducing, distributing and creating derivative works featuring those characters," calling the conduct a "virtual smash-and-grab of Disney's IP" that is "willful, pervasive and totally unacceptable," and adding, "We believe this is just the tip of the iceberg – which is shocking considering Seedance has only been available for a few days."

The [[human-artistry-campaign|Human Artistry Campaign]] described the launch as "an attack on every creator around the world," argued that "stealing isn't innovation," and said the model's "unauthorized [[deepfake|deepfakes]] and voice clones of actors violate the most basic aspects of personal autonomy." ByteDance responded by promising improved safeguards against intellectual-property theft.

## Interpretation in the Badlands corpus

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed Seedance 2.0 as a stress test of US and global intellectual-property enforcement, comparing the moment to the [[napster|Napster]] episode in music. She argued that studios attempting to enforce traditional rights against a technology their customers embrace will accelerate rather than prevent the transformation, that if courts stop Seedance another tool will appear "before the signature on the temporary restraining order is dry," and that the studios' franchises — their most valuable assets — are in the process of being "democratized, reclaimed by the people."
