# The Law of Nations

*The Law of Nations, or Principles of the Law of Nature* is the treatise by Swiss jurist [[emerich-de-vattel|Emerich de Vattel]], widely circulated among the American founders and cited in Badlands Media's analysis of the legal foundations of the United States monetary system.[1] In that analysis it functions as the authority that resolves the apparent contradiction between popular sovereignty and the binding force of federal corporate contracts.[1]

## Use in the Badlands analysis

The article poses the question of how Americans can simultaneously be the sovereign authority under [[natural-law|Natural Law]] and be made liable, by federal action, under corporate contracts they never negotiated.[1] It answers by quoting the English translation of 1805: "IT IS EVIDENT FROM THE LAW OF NATURE, THAT ALL MEN BEING NATURALLY FREE AND INDEPENDENT, THEY CANNOT LOSE THOSE BLESSINGS WITHOUT THEIR CONSENT."[1] The passage is drawn from chapter 3 and referenced through an Internet Archive copy of the work *Pied Pipers of Babylon*.[1]

## Significance

Because consent is the hinge, the treatise supplies the doctrinal basis for the article's claim that undisclosed arrangements — in this case the delegation of credit issuance to [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] — cannot lawfully strip Americans of their natural freedoms.[1] It is presented alongside the Declaration of Independence and the maxim of [[throckmorton-v-united-states]] as part of a chain of authority for the argument that no genuine national consent was ever given.[1]

The Law of Nations is also listed among the many distinct bodies of law — including Common Law, Civil Law, Admiralty Law, Maritime Law, Criminal Law, Constitutional Law, Ecclesiastical Law, Corporate Law and Trust Law — that the article says diverge, recombine and encapsulate one another within the American legal landscape.[1]
