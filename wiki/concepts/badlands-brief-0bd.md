# Badlands Brief: Mossad Bait & Labor Tackles

"Mossad Bait & Labor Tackles" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily news commentary series published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated July 10, 2026. Like other editions, it pairs blockquoted summaries of mainstream reporting with signed commentary from Badlands contributors — in this issue [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The Brief carries a standing disclaimer that the opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## Ukrainian strike on a Turkey-linked gas facility

The lead item reports that [[gazprom|Gazprom]], Russia's state-controlled energy company, said Ukrainian drones targeted the Krasnodarskaya compressor station serving the [[blue-stream-pipeline|Blue Stream]] natural gas pipeline, which carries Russian gas to Türkiye beneath the Black Sea. According to Gazprom the strike caused minor damage to a building but did not interrupt deliveries, because repairs were carried out promptly and backup measures kept supplies flowing. [[ukraine|Ukraine]] had not publicly commented at the time of reporting. The Brief notes that the separate [[turkstream-pipeline|TurkStream]] pipeline also moves Russian gas through Türkiye onward to several European countries, and that Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] condemned the attack as a threat to critical international energy infrastructure, saying [[russia|Russia]] expected affected countries, including Türkiye, to respond. The strike is placed in the context of Ukraine's expanded long-range campaign against Russian energy infrastructure and continued Russian missile and drone attacks on Ukrainian cities.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry, citing RT, argues that the attack was intended to disrupt gas flows to [[turkey|Turkey]] and that it followed within days of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s visit to Turkey for the [[nato|NATO]] summit, where Trump repeated high praise for President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] — a move the author says triggered "widespread rebuke and condemnation among the Israeli Diaspora and Mossad Media assets." He surmises that the events are connected and that Ukraine's indirect attack on Turkey was carried out on behalf of [[israel|Israel]], which he says "has been pounding the war drums toward Turkey for months." He adds that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had earlier that week identified [[greece|Greece]] as one of Turkey's intended targets, "completing a constellation of datapoints for a theory I have been evolving over the past month."

The author concedes he had originally judged such a development unlikely given how close Trump appeared to be to ending the wars, but counters that the same circumstances "create a sense of desperation and panic that could lead to such a reckless move." He now considers a Ukraine–Turkey conflict more likely than ever, with Greece potentially coming to Ukraine's aid, and advises readers to watch the island of [[cyprus|Cyprus]] in the eastern Mediterranean, the TurkStream route across the Black Sea, and Turkey's Aegean west coast as potential targets of what he calls a NATO/Israeli operation. He concludes: "Israel would love nothing more than to see European countries at war with Turkey."

## Trump dismisses the remaining EAC commissioners

The second item reports that on Thursday, July 9, 2026, Trump dismissed the remaining members of the [[election-assistance-commission|US Election Assistance Commission]], the independent federal agency that certifies voting systems, accredits voting system testing laboratories, administers federal election grants, and maintains the national mail voter registration form. One Republican commissioner resigned while the two Democratic commissioners were notified by email that their positions had been terminated. The [[white-house|White House]] had not announced replacement nominees, though the president has authority to appoint new commissioners subject to [[senate|Senate]] confirmation.

The Brief catalogues a wave of concurrent election litigation and rulemaking. In New York, officials paused ballot counting in a closely contested Onondaga County Legislature race after allegations of voting irregularities prompted a court challenge. In South Carolina, the state Republican Party filed suit seeking to end the state's open primary system. The [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]] approved a rule requesting access to the Secretary of State's election operations center on election night, and Colorado enacted an omnibus elections bill during its 2026 legislative session. A federal judge ordered the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to keep its citizenship verification database offline pending further litigation, while the Trump administration continued efforts to encourage states to adopt additional election integrity measures through federal grant conditions.

Ashe in America writes that "election news in an election year is always a heavy lift, but this year it's downright transformational." She characterises the EAC as the institutional "expert" that assures the public that erroneous code, code read errors, glitches, or database coding mismatches "are no big deal," arguing that such bureaucracies always speak to the public with the primary goal of preserving trust in elections "even if it means lying to the public." From that premise she concludes that "US elections are incapable of delivering outcomes that the people can trust, because US elections are run in unauditable black boxes with fake voters, fake ballots, fake counts, and a whole lot of obstruction," and that elections are therefore "just that: stories." She states that she believes real elections are possible but only with "honesty and accountability for how fake they've been," that firing the EAC principals is not enough, and that those involved "should be prosecuted along with everyone else who participated in lying to the public about fake elections under the banner of 'preserving trust.'" She closes: "Man, that is uncomfortably Orwellian. Accelerate."

## Markets shrug off Middle East escalation

A third item reports that US stock futures moved higher Thursday while oil retreated as investors looked past the latest US–Iran military exchanges. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted gains led by technology shares, while West Texas Intermediate crude fell about 2% to near $72 per barrel as concerns eased over a prolonged interruption to shipping through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. Despite renewed US strikes on Iranian targets and Tehran's retaliatory attacks on US facilities in the Gulf, traders focused on the absence of major damage to oil production or export infrastructure, though prices remained on track for weekly gains.

Burning Bright frames this as evidence for a thesis developed on the previous Wednesday night's *Devolution Power Hour* with Jon Herold. He argues that after nearly six months of "on-again, off-again real and fake war" with [[iran|Iran]], a pattern has formed that "even the normie and alternative layers of the Mindscape are catching on" to. Financial and alternative media, he says, now respond with blanket skepticism to both de-escalation and escalation. That markets — "largely influenced by major market makers, from hedge funds to banks" — are ignoring the week's major escalations he calls "actually shocking," noting that three months earlier "every Truth Social post by Trump was being met with 72 hours of total market chaos."

He attributes the change to what he calls [[trumps-discombobulator|Trump's Discombobulator]]: by "crying wolf on war AND peace every week for six months," Trump and the so-called Iranian Regime have forced futures and oil markets to price in "ACTUALS" — supply and demand dynamics rather than the stories about them — locking the collective mindscape "in a clarifying pincer." He adds parenthetically that the "unstable stability" in markets probably also owes something to Trump's direct threats to the US oil cartel. On the "normie" layers, he writes, the story of Trump's war with Iran "is, well, played out," and that resulting apathy "isn't a side effect... it's one of the core features of Trump's distillation process." The discombobulator, in his account, "isn't meant to confuse permanently. It's meant to clarify, and to separate the real from the unreal." He concludes that Trump "has NEVER been waging a real war" but "HAS been waging a War of Stories."

## Reported Iranian plot against Trump

The Brief reports that US officials were recently informed by Israeli intelligence of what was described as a new Iranian plot to assassinate President Trump, per reporting by the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]], CNN, and [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] citing officials familiar with the matter. The intelligence had not previously been identified by US agencies and was being evaluated; neither the White House nor the Iranian government commented publicly. The alleged threat was described as consistent with longstanding Iranian vows to retaliate for the 2020 US strike that killed General [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]]. Speaking during the NATO summit in Ankara, Trump acknowledged that he remains a target of Iranian threats and said he has been aware of assassination risks for years. The report came amid renewed US–Iran hostilities following the collapse of a recent ceasefire.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry responds with open incredulity, noting that "it was President Trump who actually seeded this narrative into the news cycle during his NATO press conference." He suggests Trump may be "baiting Mossad Media and the NeoCon-wing of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]]" into running with the story, given that they have argued for two years that Iran was behind the 2024 Butler campaign rally shooting. He contends Israel is the more plausible culprit for any attempt on Trump's life, reasoning that a successful assassination would let Israel immediately blame Iran, obtain permission for expanded surveillance of the American people, and secure a free pass to wage war on Iran and suspected collaborators — "like Iraq and Afghanistan all over again." Drawing an analogy to [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]], he argues that killing Trump while he still has an ostensibly positive public relationship with Israel would "freeze" that position permanently, ensuring Trump could never narratively turn on Israel. Citing Trump's public relationships with Muslim leaders in the region — highlighting Erdoğan and Syria's al-Sharaa that week — he presumes the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] and [[mossad|Mossad]] are displeased with Trump's regional foreign policy, and concludes that "only a fool would rule out Israel as a suspect in any assassination attempt against President Trump."

## Labor Department H-1B investigation

The Brief reports that the [[united-states-department-of-labor|US Department of Labor]]'s [[office-of-inspector-general|Office of Inspector General]] launched a major investigation into fraud and human trafficking tied to the [[h-1b-visa|H-1B visa]] and [[program-electronic-review-management|Program Electronic Review Management]] (PERM) systems. The OIG press release stated: "These abuses undermine the integrity of Department of Labor programs designed to address genuine labor shortages — not to line the pockets of bad actors at the expense of American jobs," and described the investigation as reflecting "the OIG's unwavering commitment to dismantle human trafficking and forced-labor networks that exploit the foreign guest worker visa system."

Inspector General [[anthony-desposito|Anthony P. D'Esposito]] is quoted: "This isn't just paperwork fraud—it's the exploitation of vulnerable workers, forced labor, the displacement of American workers, and abusive human trafficking. As the Inspector General, my top priorities are exposing fraud, protecting American workers, and putting criminals in cuffs." The OIG announced a nationwide reporting campaign urging US and foreign workers to report suspected program abuses, with rewards possibly available for information leading to successful prosecutions.

Ashe in America observes that D'Esposito "appears to be front-running a 'this is just paperwork' narrative from the administration's critics," and dryly notes the repeated invocation of trafficking: "'Abusive human trafficking' … is there any other kind?" She calls the investigation encouraging and "a callback to the 2024 H-1B scandal that nearly tore us apart and ultimately cost us [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek]]," quoting at length from the December 30, 2024 Badlands News Brief: "You can't have 'America First Globalism.' Trump has promised to reinvest in American workers, companies, and industries, unleashing a golden age for Americans. This goal is at odds with the global world order, whose exploitation of the people of the world, including Americans, is the actual problem statement... We are not trading one set of globalists for another. [[america-first|America First]]." She judges that the 2024 outrage "educated and elevated the public mind to actually grasp the widespread corruption and criminality of US labor markets" and "seeded the conditions to build legitimate American support for accountability and change."

## Bonus item: research grants and the new climate office

A bonus item reports that the Trump administration is advancing changes to the federal research grant process that would require agencies to place greater emphasis on national priorities, transparency, and practical outcomes when awarding scientific grants. Researchers and university groups criticised the proposal as likely to reduce funding for basic research and increase political influence over grant decisions. Separately, the administration established a new White House climate office led by climate policy critic [[david-legates|David Legates]], a former [[national-oceanic-and-atmospheric-administration|National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration]] official who has questioned aspects of mainstream climate modeling. Administration officials said the office would review federal climate science and policy to ensure analyses are objective and free from political bias, while critics contended the move could reshape how climate research informs policymaking and weaken existing climate initiatives.
