# Project 2025

Project 2025 is a conservative policy and personnel blueprint for a second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] administration, widely discussed in 2024 as a roadmap for restructuring the federal executive branch.[1]

## Federal workforce anxiety

By October 2024, the document had become an object of intense study among career federal employees. Reporting cited by Badlands Media described [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] employees shuffling to what they considered "safer" agencies, and an [[department-of-the-interior|Interior Department]] worker delaying the purchase of a new car while poring over Project 2025.[1] Civil servants across the executive branch reported fears that they might soon be fired.[1]

Those anxieties were reinforced by campaign rhetoric. Trump pledged to "demolish the deep state," while his running mate, Ohio Senator [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], said Trump ought to fire "every civil servant in the administrative state."[1] In the final days of his first term, Trump sought to make it easier to remove federal employees, a change quickly reversed by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration.[1] Agencies issuing environmental rules were identified as prime targets should Trump return to office in January 2025.[1]

## Project Esther

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Project 2025 manual contains a section, [[project-esther|Project Esther]], outlining a plan to convert state-level antisemitism speech measures into federal policy.[1]

## Democratic congressional working group (June 2024)

In June 2024, Representative [[jared-huffman|Jared Huffman]] warned that Project 2025 would take "a wrecking ball" to America's democratic norms and institutions, speaking ahead of the launch of a congressional working group designed to sound the alarm about the roadmap and ensure [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] never had the chance to implement it.[2] The near 900-page handbook was produced by the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] as a manifesto for expanding the power of the presidency while dismantling layers of government, recommending the purging of many appointed roles from the federal ranks and the stacking of agencies with loyalists.[2] Huffman argued it would lay waste to democratic institutions, checks on executive power, individual rights and freedoms, and church-state separation, citing the "weaponising of the Department of Justice" and the clearing away of "any norms that limit executive power."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that Democrats were claiming Project 2025 would take a wrecking ball to the institutions "after taking a wrecking ball to America's democratic norms and institutions," calling it peak gaslighting.[2] She argued that the Republic was suffering a crisis of public trust that only accountability could restore, that Uniparty Democrats and Republicans alike had corrupted the institutions so badly that salvaging them was an ambitious goal, and that Project 2025 was therefore "a necessity."[2] The ruling elite, she wrote, fear accountability for their decisions and their outcomes, and the people who brought about "a great reset" and global war "do not get another at-bat."[2]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers treated the wave of federal-employee anxiety as corroboration of their broader thesis that a permanent bureaucratic class — what contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called domestic enemies — was working to avoid accountability, noting that the oath of office explicitly requires defending the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] against enemies "foreign and domestic."[1]

## Use as shutdown leverage

During the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]], the BBC reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was using the conservative blueprint as a threat to force Democrats to accept his budgetary demands.[3] Trump publicly linked the plan to its co-author [[russ-vought|Russ Vought]] — "he of PROJECT 2025 Fame" — whom he was holding up as a budgetary angel of death ready to cut programs favored by Democrats.[3] Project 2025 was described as having been assembled primarily by former Trump officials while Republicans were out of power and as having featured prominently in the prior year's campaign, when Democratic nominee [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] repeatedly called it Trump's "dangerous plan" for the future.[3]
