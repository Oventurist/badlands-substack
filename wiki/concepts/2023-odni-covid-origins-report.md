# 2023 ODNI COVID-19 Origins Report

The 2023 ODNI COVID-19 origins report was a 10-page declassified assessment released on Friday, June 23, 2023 by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] examining potential links between the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] and the origins of COVID-19.[1] The long-anticipated document offered new detail on the US intelligence community's findings but declined to state definitively whether the source of the coronavirus was exposure to an infected animal or an event at a laboratory.[1]

## Findings

The report stated that "all agencies continue to assess that both a natural and laboratory-associated origin remain plausible hypotheses to explain the first human infection."[1] It laid out divisions within the intelligence community, some of which had been previously acknowledged in public reporting.[1]

The [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] and four unnamed agencies assessed that natural exposure to an infected animal was the most likely scenario for the first human infection.[1] The [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] and the [[fbi|FBI]] assessed that a laboratory-associated incident was the more likely cause.[1] The [[cia|CIA]] and one unidentified agency "remain unable to determine the precise origin of the COVID-19 pandemic, as both hypotheses rely on significant assumptions or face challenges with conflicting reporting," the report stated.[1]

According to the coverage cited by Badlands, "almost all" intelligence agencies agreed the virus was not genetically engineered, and all agencies agreed that COVID-19 was not manufactured as a biological weapon.[1] "Most" agencies additionally agreed that the virus was not laboratory-adapted — that is, that it had not sustained natural, random mutations via human-enabled processes in a laboratory.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the report as an exercise in plausible deniability, defined as the ability of senior officials in a chain of command to deny knowledge of or responsibility for actions committed by members of their organizational hierarchy.[1] She argued that the way to achieve such deniability in the COVID-19 scandal was to have "17 'different' agencies full of 'experts'" assess the crime, report contradictory findings, ignore established scientific facts, and hold no one accountable.[1]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] tied the report to prior intelligence-community products he considered discredited — the WMD assessments used to justify the Iraq war and the letter signed by 51 former intelligence officials casting the [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] laptop as Russian disinformation before the 2020 election.[1] He argued that despite the community's ostensibly superior access to information, the report read as a confirmation of the official narrative of the previous three years.[1] Deschamps found it strange that the intelligence community could make confident assessments about the 2016 election while professing itself unable to "determine the precise origin" of COVID-19, and noted that [[gain-of-function-research|directed evolution or gain-of-function work]] had been conducted in US-funded biological research facilities for years, with the outbreak beginning near one such laboratory.[1] Pointing to the report's use of the qualifier "most agencies," he asked who the dissenters were and whether they might be patriots within the intelligence community, closing with a quotation attributed to [[william-casey|William J. Casey]]: "We'll know our disinformation program is complete when everything the American public believes is false."[1]
