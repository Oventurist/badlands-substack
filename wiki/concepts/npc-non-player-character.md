# NPC (Non-Player Character)

**NPC**, an abbreviation of "non-player character" borrowed from video-game terminology, is used in Part 8 of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series to denote a person held to have no real capacity for independent thought or action.[1] The essay treats the concept as nearly identical to that of the zombie in occult symbolism.[1]

## Diagnostic characteristics

The evidence offered for NPC status is the observable tendency to speak only in slogans and clichés; original thought, by contrast, is presented as a tell-tale sign that a person is in touch with their spirit.[1] The author writes that propagandized acquaintances from childhood are incapable of making coherent statements about current events that do not sound auto-generated by "a discount version of ChatGPT" trained solely on mainstream-media talking points, and that they become highly agitated when others do not share their programming — behaviour he characterizes as a genuine symptom of [[zombification|zombification]].[1] The article carries a disclaimer that nothing in it constitutes professional psychological or medical advice.[1]

## NPCs, demons and robots

A central argument of the essay is that NPCs, demons and robots are alike in lacking the self-directed *telic* capacity exhibited by spirits engaged in actualizing their unique essences; all three are described as *anti-telic*.[1] Their inability to act or perceive outside pre-made programs — stereotypes, prejudices, unexamined assumptions, predictable moods — is offered as the evidence.[1] The author allows that demons arguably possess a greater capacity for will than robots or NPCs, but declines to split hairs.[1] Because bot-driven comment sections display the same repetitiveness, the NPC concept is linked directly to the [[dead-internet-theory|dead internet]] and to [[cyber-necromancy|cyber-necromancy]].[1]

## Zombie symbolism

Zombie symbolism is glossed as the state between life and death in which bodily processes continue but the spirit is absent, the defining characteristic being the absence of independent will.[1] Zombies "eat brains," which the author reads as a metaphor for ideological indoctrination inflicted on free thinkers; the elite epithet "useless eaters" is read as a veiled reference to zombies, and to the reduction of human beings to mere consumers.[1] The essay further argues that a mechanical, purposeless universe as imagined by secular materialism amounts to "one giant NPC unto itself."[1]
