# 2002 Venezuelan Coup Attempt

The **2002 Venezuelan coup attempt** was a short-lived, U.S.-backed removal of President [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] in April 2002, described by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as one episode in a decades-long campaign of regime change directed at [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[1]

## Events

Chávez survived the coup and was restored to power after roughly 48 hours by mass protests from thousands of loyalists — an interruption of his 1999–2013 presidency lasting about forty-seven hours.[1] Months later, the country's business elite, determined to block Chavista plans to redirect oil profits into social programs, launched an "owners' strike" that paralyzed petroleum production at [[pdvsa|PDVSA]].[1] Venezuelan GDP plunged by an estimated 27 percent and Chávez's approval ratings cratered, but by early 2003 the strike had collapsed, allowing him to channel oil revenues into sweeping health, education, and housing programs.[1]

## Consequences

Having survived both the coup and the strike, Chávez moved quickly in 2002–2003: he fired 19,000 PDVSA managers, replaced them with loyalists, and redirected petrodollars into social spending.[1] Washington's response was swift and multipronged, including a quadrupling of the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]]'s Venezuela budget and the quiet revival of the [[plan-avila|Plan Ávila]] counterinsurgency framework.[1] The opposition's final serious attempt to unseat Chávez by constitutional means came with the August 2004 recall referendum, which he won with 58 percent of the vote.[1]
