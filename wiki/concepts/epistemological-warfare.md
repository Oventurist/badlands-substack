# Epistemological Warfare

Epistemological warfare is the concept that the decisive battleground shaping history is the contest over how human beings know things — the standards of truth, causality and reason a civilization accepts — rather than merely the contest over territory, money or weapons.[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] endorsed the concept in his November 15, 2025 essay "Don't Be Symbol-Minded," crediting [[peter-duke|Peter Duke]] with appreciating it as "the principled battleground shaping history."[1]

Ehret argued that a proper study of epistemological warfare requires attention to both levels of reality: not just the lower realm of materialism and contingent mechanics, but the higher realm of first principles, which he identified with metaphysics and the realm of Platonic ideas — what he termed [[universal-principles|Universal Principles]].[1] Restricting analysis to the lower realm, he wrote, produces the "pattern-recognition" thinking or pure bottom-up empiricism that leads observers to conclude that every fight for liberty has been controlled opposition in a dialectic managed by a satanic occult priesthood.[1]

He named Plato, Cicero, St. Augustine, Leibniz and [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] as authorities on the higher realm of causality, and treated both [[neuro-linguistic-programming|NLP]] and AI summarization as substitutes for that higher-order reasoning.[1] Applied to symbolism, epistemological warfare implies a continuous battle over the meaning of symbols such as the [[phrygian-cap|Phrygian cap]], the pentagon, the triangle and red-and-white stripes, in which the same emblem may be used by opposing sides for opposite ends.[1] Applied to geopolitics, it implies that similar banking mechanisms or diplomatic language may conceal a genuine, non-Hegelian opposition of intentions between rival systems.[1]
