# Incorruptibility

Incorruptibility is a concept in Catholic tradition holding that the failure of a body to decompose "indicates a divine intervention that stops or slows decomposition as a sign of holiness."[1]

## The 2023 Missouri case

The doctrine returned to public attention in May 2023 when the exhumed body of [[wilhelmina-lancaster|Sister Wilhelmina Lancaster]] of the [[benedictines-of-mary-queen-of-apostles|Benedictines of Mary, Queen of Apostles]] in Gower, Missouri, was found to show little decay nearly four years after her 2019 death.[1] The local diocese began investigating the case as a possible instance of incorruptibility, and Canon Law permits the inspection of an "incorruptible saint," a provision under which pilgrims were allowed to view the remains.[1]

Commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] stressed the evidentiary oddity rather than asserting a verdict: because Lancaster had not been embalmed and was buried in a wooden coffin set in very moist clay, rapid decomposition would have been the expected outcome, making the preservation "intriguing and counterintuitive."[1] Reported daily crowds of 10,000–15,000 pilgrims illustrated the popular pull of the claim, with one visitor describing the gathering of "people from all races from around the country" as beautiful amid "all the negativity in the world."[1]
