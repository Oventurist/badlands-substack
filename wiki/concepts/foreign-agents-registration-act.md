# Foreign Agents Registration Act

The Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA) is a United States law requiring persons acting as agents of foreign principals to register with the government and disclose their activities. In March 2026 it became the legal instrument at the center of a dispute over press freedom during the war with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## The Carlson referral claim

Commentator [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] said he had learned that the [[cia|CIA]] was preparing a criminal referral over his contacts with people in Iran, characterizing the alleged offense as "talking to people in Iran before the war" and asserting that intelligence agencies had read his text messages.[1] Former CIA officer [[john-kiriakou|John Kiriakou]] publicly voiced concern for Carlson's safety in light of the claim.[1] Administration officials later told Axios reporter Marc Caputo that there was no CIA investigation of Carlson.[1]

Activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] said she had lobbied members of Congress, law enforcement and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to act against Carlson, and Republican Congressman [[max-miller|Max Miller]] called for Carlson's imprisonment if he had worked with Iran.[1]

## Significance in the speech debate

Badlands writers treated the invocation of FARA against a domestic journalist as an escalation in a broader campaign to criminalize dissent, comparable in their analysis to [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|IHRA-based]] speech statutes and inseparable from the wider fight over the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1] The concern voiced was that a registration-and-disclosure statute could be repurposed into a vehicle for prosecuting commentary about a foreign adversary during wartime.[1]

## Use against Gal Luft (2023)

In July 2023 the Justice Department charged Israeli think-tank co-director [[gal-luft|Gal Luft]] with failing to register under FARA, alongside Iranian sanctions violations, days after he publicly detailed allegations against the family of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[2] Prosecutors alleged he had conspired with others to act in China's interest, including recruiting and paying a former high-ranking U.S. government official to support pro-China policies.[2] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the statute's language is so ambiguous that it can be applied to almost anything, and that such vagueness invites abuse by corrupt actors and the government.[2]

## Proposed referrals against Hunter Biden (2023)

[[james-comer|James Comer]] said in July 2023 that the criminal referrals he intended to file against [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] could include violations of the Foreign Agents Registration Act, alongside allegations of money laundering, racketeering and wire fraud.[3]

## The Wynn suit and asymmetric enforcement claims

In May 2022 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] filed a FARA suit against casino magnate [[steve-wynn|Steve Wynn]], alleging he had used his relationship with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to advocate for the Chinese Communist Party's sought-after return of US asylum seeker [[miles-guo|Miles Guo]].[4] Assistant Attorney General Matthew G. Olsen said at the time that the filing "demonstrates the department's commitment to ensuring transparency in our democratic system," adding that "where a foreign government uses an American as its agent to influence policy decisions in the United States, FARA gives the American people a right to know."[4] The case was ultimately dismissed by US District Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]].[4] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the episode as an example of asymmetrical justice, noting that no comparable FARA statements had come from the attorney general's office regarding the [[biden-crime-family|Biden family]]'s documented dealings with the Chinese Communist Party and Ukraine.[4]
