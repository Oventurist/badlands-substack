# Stop Cop City

Stop Cop City is an American protest campaign described in a September 2025 [[capital-research-center|Capital Research Center]] report as linked to [[antifa|Antifa]].[1]

## Prosecutions

The report stated that activists associated with the Stop Cop City campaign faced more than 40 domestic terrorism charges and 60 racketeering indictments.[1] The campaign was cited as having been endorsed by the [[sunrise-movement|Sunrise Movement]], one of the organizations the report identified as a recipient of [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] funding.[1]

## Significance

Because the campaign's prosecutions rested on domestic terrorism and racketeering theories, Badlands commentary treated it as a template for the broader RICO exposure that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been signaling against funders such as [[george-soros|George Soros]].[1]

## January 2023 raid and shootout

Since June 2021, Antifa-aligned and other far-left activists from across the United States had occupied a wooded area south of Atlanta at the site of the future Atlanta Public Safety Training Center, declaring it an "autonomous zone" to prevent construction of what they called "cop city."[2] On Wednesday, January 18, 2023, the [[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|Georgia Bureau of Investigation]] and other agencies raided the encampment; a Georgia State Patrol trooper was shot and severely injured by a man camped in the area, and officers returned fire and killed the gunman.[2] Seven militants, all from out of state, were charged with domestic terrorism, and journalist Andy Ngo reported the arrests.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the shooting prompted militants to call for violence against police and anyone they deemed not a "comrade."[2]

## Killing of Tortuguita and the January 2023 riot

In January 2023, a 26-year-old environmental activist known as [[tortuguita|Tortuguita]], described as a nonbinary person using they/it pronouns, was killed by authorities who said the activist had shot a Georgia state trooper.[3] On the following Saturday night, hundreds of demonstrators marched up Atlanta's Peachtree Street in mourning, and a subsection of masked activists dressed in all black threw rocks and lit fireworks outside the skyscraper housing the [[atlanta-police-foundation|Atlanta Police Foundation]], shattering large windows, burning a police cruiser and covering walls with anti-police graffiti.[3] Solidarity rioting followed in Boston, where [[jared-dowell|Jared Dowell]] was arrested on assault and property-damage charges.[3] [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] compared the unrest to the 2020 "summer of love" and criticised FOX 5 reporter Tyler Fingert for calling the protests "mostly peaceful" while a police cruiser burned behind him.[3]
