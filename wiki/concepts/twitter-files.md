# Twitter Files

The Twitter Files are a set of internal disclosures from the pre-acquisition era of the platform now known as [[x-platform|X]], cited by Badlands Media as documentary evidence of the censorship apparatus it describes.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

The article invokes the Twitter Files to argue that, while an open platform inevitably surfaces opinions users find reprehensible, it is by far more dangerous to have the federal government and intelligence agencies colluding with big tech to determine which conversations the public may engage in.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) This contrast forms the core of the author's defense of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s stewardship of the platform against the criticisms advanced by [[media-matters|Media Matters]] and the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Anticipated Fauci installment

Writing on December 20, 2022, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[j-b-white|J.B. White]] described the Twitter Files as an "ongoing process of revelation" exposing wrongdoing by what he called a RICO-worthy collection of officials, and read the releases as evidence of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s efforts to prevent competition for its preferred narratives, efforts and protocols.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/anthony-fauci) He explicitly wrote his column on [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] as a companion piece to a Twitter Files exposé on Fauci that he expected would be published at some future date.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/anthony-fauci)

## Congressional testimony, March 2023

On March 9, 2023, the two principal Twitter Files reporters, [[matt-taibbi|Matt Taibbi]] and [[michael-shellenberger|Michael Shellenberger]], testified before the [[house-weaponization-committee|House Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]] about the material.[3] Taibbi summarized the findings as "a sweeping effort ... to use machine learning and other tools to turn the internet into an instrument of censorship and social control," with the U.S. government "playing a lead role" such that "news media became an arm of a state-sponsored thought-policing system."[3] Ranking Member [[stacey-plaskett|Stacey E. Plaskett]] dismissed the pair as "so-called journalists" and "public scribes" for [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] releasing cherry-picked, out-of-context material, a characterization Taibbi rejected on the record.[3]

## Installment #21 and the Russian-bot narrative

The twenty-first installment of the Twitter Files, publicized in late April 2023, was presented by The Gateway Pundit as further evidence that the claim that Russian accounts materially impacted U.S. elections was a lie.[4] The outlet argued that before the 2016 election the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[barack-obama|Obama]] camps, fearing that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might win and investigate them, manufactured the story that Trump was tied to Russians — while the Obama administration had itself approved the sale of a significant quantity of U.S. uranium to Russian interests in the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] transaction.[4]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the "Russian bot" label was applied so promiscuously to ordinary dissenters that [[twitter|Twitter]] even tagged Jill Stein as Russian, and argued that the platform not only censored at the direction of the government but allowed the government to access user account data and shape propaganda — making it, in his view, a state actor rather than a private corporation.[4]

## Disclosures on the Global Engagement Center

The Twitter Files - described as a trove of internal records about the censorship decisions made within the social media company - revealed that the State Department's [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] funded groups such as the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]]'s [[digital-forensic-research-lab|Digital Forensic Research Lab]], which compiled blacklists of Twitter accounts supposedly tied to foreign disinformation campaigns and forwarded them to Twitter for deactivation.[5] These disclosures underpinned later litigation and the department's December 2024 statement that it was "substantially likely" to shut the center down.[5]

## Privileged access for Taylor Lorenz (May 2023)

In May 2023 The DisInformation Chronicle published Twitter Files material reporting that [[twitter|Twitter]] had granted privileged access to [[washington-post|Washington Post]] reporter [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], characterizing her as a "banning queen."[6] The files indicated that Lorenz had successfully banned an account only weeks before publishing a November 2022 article warning that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] was "opening the gates of hell" by reinstating previously banned accounts, and that other files showed her behind additional bans.[6] Twitter also appeared to maintain a special relationship with Lorenz, circulating an internal alert after [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] ridiculed her reporting in a [[fox-news|Fox News]] segment.[6]

One account she pushed to ban, @fearthefloof, had researched her background and family connections before being removed.[6] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued the disclosures showed manipulation "far more insidious and pervasive than we ever imagined," and that platforms' deliberately vague content rules prevent any objective, repeatable standard for who is banned.[6]
