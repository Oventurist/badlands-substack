# Manhattan Project

The Manhattan Project was the wartime American program that designed and built the first atomic bombs, led scientifically by [[j-robert-oppenheimer|J. Robert Oppenheimer]] and militarily by [[leslie-groves|Leslie Groves]], and dramatized in Christopher Nolan's 2023 film *Oppenheimer*.[1]

## From research to development

The Badlands review describes the project as the culmination of an abrupt disciplinary turn: after Cockcroft and Walton split the atom in 1932, the greatest scientific minds of the era moved seemingly overnight from researching the universe to developing the atomic bomb.[1] Work at Los Alamos in the New Mexico desert produced the [[trinity-test|Trinity test]], the first-ever detonation of an atomic bomb.[1]

## Internal fears

Project physicists debated whether detonation might destroy the world. Manhattan Project physicist [[edward-teller|Edward Teller]] worried that the heat of the explosion would cause atmospheric hydrogen to undergo fusion, setting off a catastrophic chain reaction around the globe; the film transfers that concern to Groves.[1] The review argues that the scientists' repeated admissions of uncertainty became unsympathetic precisely because they pressed on into military application anyway.[1]

## Counterfactual embedded in the film

The film's sympathetic treatment of [[communism|communist]] scientists sets up an alternate history in which Hiroshima and Nagasaki might have been spared and the [[nuclear-arms-race|nuclear arms race]] and Cold War prevented, had American authorities listened to the communists, shared information with the Soviets, and paused development to debate the ethics of the bomb.[1] Badlands identifies that counterfactual as the template now being reused in calls to pause [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] development.[1]
