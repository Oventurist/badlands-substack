# Hand-Counted Paper Ballots

Hand-counted paper ballots refers to an election method in which voters mark paper ballots by hand and those ballots are tabulated manually by people rather than by electronic tabulators. Within Badlands Media's election-integrity commentary, the method is treated as the end-state alternative to the technology-dependent apparatus created by the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]].[1]

## As an election-reform end-goal

In her March 23, 2023 article "Rooting Out Election Corruption," [[holly-at-altitude|Holly at Altitude]] argued that the conceptual goal of election reform is to eliminate technology from the election machine, which she called "the root of all the problems that create the hydra of control."[1] Her described end-state has voters casting ballots on a single day, with election day made a national holiday so that everyone can participate; voting at their local precinct on paper ballots marked by hand; counting beginning only after the polls close and performed by hand under live-streaming high-definition cameras; precinct totals released to the public at the precinct once tallied; and those totals rolling up into a state total that is then released to the public and the media.[1]

She noted that there is no one-size-fits-all path to this goal, because states and counties operate their elections differently, and framed the transition as a matter of finding "objective weaknesses that create opportunities for correction."[1]

## Cost as a lever

One of the article's five proposed lines of local action was to challenge election equipment budgets by constructing a comparison budget between the cost of electronic voting equipment and the cost of conducting the same election with hand-counted paper ballots, then presenting that cost difference publicly to elected officials.[1] Resources for that budgeting work were attributed to [[cause-of-america|Cause of America]].[1] The article recommended centring the case against electronic voting equipment on the financials and making clear that officials are wasting money.[1]
