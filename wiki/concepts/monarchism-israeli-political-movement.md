# Monarchism (Israeli political movement)

"Monarchism" is the label applied by journalist [[david-sheen|David Sheen]] to a fifth current in Israeli politics that he says emerged during the 1980s, distinct from the four conventional movements — Theocrats, Nationalists (Zionists), Liberals and Socialists — into which he divides the [[knesset|Knesset]]'s many parties.[1]

## Doctrine

In Sheen's account, the Monarchists blend what he characterizes as the "worst ideas" of the Nationalist and Theocratic movements. From the secular Nationalists they take a willingness to act politically and militarily; from the Theocrats they take the demand for a state cleansed of gentiles. The synthesis produces a doctrine of eliminationism that, he argues, necessarily requires genocide. The movement is also expansionist, pursuing the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel project]].[1] Badlands commentary described adherents as radical zealots "every bit as deranged and dangerous as radical Islamic terrorists," and noted Sheen's warning that the faction becomes most dangerous whenever Israel approaches a peace agreement with the Palestinians.[1]

## Kahanist lineage

The Monarchists are described as following the teachings of [[meir-kahane|Meir Kahane]], characterized in the Badlands account as a Jewish terrorist who plotted to blow up the Al Aqsa Mosque in order to trigger a "forever war" between Arabs and Jews, and who also called for the burning of churches and the murder of Christians, whom he regarded as "the greatest enemy of Jews."[1] The article cites a 2015 interview in which Kahane follower [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]] described Christians as "vampires and bloodsuckers."[1]

## Role in the Netanyahu coalition

After facing corruption charges in 2020, [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] lost the backing of many centrist Nationalists — [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] among them — and of some Theocrats, obliging him to build a coalition with more radical Theocrats and with the emergent Monarchists.[1] Ministers identified with this current in his cabinet include [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] and [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]], who in the Badlands assessment "hold Netanyahu's leash," possessing the power to dissolve his government should he end the war in [[gaza|Gaza]] — a condition they have stated explicitly.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the removal of this faction as an objective of what he described as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s counterinsurgency operation, arguing that the group now dominates the Israeli government and, given its stated hostility to Christians, is "no friend of the United States."[1]
