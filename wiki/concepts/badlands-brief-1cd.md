# Badlands Brief: Saving the SAVE Act, Ending the Forever War & What to Make Of Joe Kent

This edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], published March 18, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], covers President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s public campaign for the SAVE Act, the resignation of counterterrorism director [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]] over the U.S. war in [[iran|Iran]], the refusal of NATO allies to join a Strait of Hormuz coalition, a congressional subpoena of Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] over the Epstein files, the reported killing of Iranian security chief [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]], and a bonus item on Venezuela's World Baseball Classic championship. Commentary is supplied by the Brief's regular contributors [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]].

## Trump and the SAVE Act

The Brief reports that Trump urged [[congress|Congress]] to pass the SAVE Act, describing it in a [[truth-social|Truth Social]] post as "one of the most IMPORTANT & CONSEQUENTIAL pieces of legislation in the history of Congress, and America itself." According to the source, Trump framed the bill as the means to ensure "NO MORE RIGGED ELECTIONS" by requiring proof of U.S. citizenship and government-issued photo identification for federal elections, and by banning unrestricted mail-in voting practices that he said make the United States "the only Country in the World" permitting them.

Trump bundled the measure with other cultural priorities, listing "Voter I.D., Proof of Citizenship, No Rigged Mail-In Voting … No Men in Women's Sports, No Transgender MUTILIZATION of our Children" and calling these "90% to 99% ISSUES ALL!" He warned that "only sick, demented, or deranged people in the House or Senate could vote against THE SAVE AMERICA ACT," pledged to withhold endorsement from any lawmaker who opposed it, and predicted "a guaranteed loss" for such members in future campaigns.

As the bill's sponsor, Sen. Mike Lee (R-Utah) rebutted Democratic objections during [[senate|Senate]] debate. He answered claims by Sen. Dick Durbin that the measure would force voters to obtain passports costing $186 with weeks of delay, or would burden married women needing additional documentation. Lee explained that the [[save-america-act|SAVE Act]] contains built-in accommodations, including free sworn attestations for voters lacking traditional paperwork — for instance if documents were lost in a fire, or "their dog ate it" — along with state verification against existing records and reciprocity agreements between states. He stressed that the process "will not cost them a dime" and that "no one will be excluded."

Ashe in America's commentary argues that fear of losing Congress in the midterms is beside the point, because "we can't get legislation passed now, and the Congress is actively fighting against the most important and consequential piece of legislation ever to exist." She contends that keeping the status quo of elections "services only the appearance of legitimacy," and that while the SAVE America Act is good legislation, "the body called upon to negotiate and pass it is illegitimate and invested in its demise." She reads Trump's habit of attaching gender-related issues to his SAVE Act posts as evidence that he is not making a serious effort to persuade senators, and, combined with what she calls "the video game bombing videos in the run up to NDAA authorization," suggests "something else is going on" — namely that Trump is "revealing the apex parody of governance: The United States Congress."

## The resignation of Joe Kent

The Brief reports that Joe Kent, director of the National Counterterrorism Center and a top aide to Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], resigned as the first senior official in the Trump administration to quit over the ongoing U.S. war in Iran. In a strongly worded resignation letter, Kent said he could not support the conflict in good conscience, declaring that "Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation" and that the war had been started under pressure from [[israel|Israel]] and its powerful American lobby.

Kent accused high-ranking Israeli officials and influential American media of running a misinformation campaign that undermined Trump's "[[america-first|America First]]" foreign policy, comparing the tactics to those used to draw the United States into the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]]. A combat veteran who deployed eleven times and lost his wife Shannon in a previous conflict, Kent urged Trump to return to the non-interventionist posture of his 2016, 2020 and 2024 campaigns and his first term.

Trump called Kent "a nice guy" but "weak on security." White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] rejected the claims as "insulting and laughable," while Trumpworld figures described Kent as a suspected leaker and a "crazed egomaniac." Administration officials were said to be bracing for further fallout, including a planned interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry compares the resignation to the death of Larijani as "another unexpected and strange announcement," noting that Kent became a nationally recognized figure through repeated appearances on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s War Room during the Biden administration. In the hours after the resignation, Gabbard broke her long silence on the Iran war. Bannon defended Kent by citing his Middle East deployments and called for an investigation into the letter's claim that Israel controls U.S. foreign policy; Kent's wife, also serving in the Middle East, was killed in an attack in Syria, and Kent lays blame for her death at the feet of the [[cia|CIA]]. General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] also responded publicly. The commentary concludes that "plotlines like this one don't just fizzle out," describes [[uniparty|Uniparty]] figures participating in "a communist struggle session against Joe Kent," suggests Gabbard may be the next target, and asks why "all Israel dissidents in the administration are being hunted down and removed from office."

## Allies refuse the Strait of Hormuz coalition

According to the Brief, [[nato|NATO]] allies flatly refused Trump's calls for an international coalition to secure the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], with multiple European nations declaring the conflict "not our war." French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] said France is "not party to the conflict" and "will never take part in operations" to reopen the strait. Germany's Defense Minister Boris Pistorius questioned the point of sending European frigates when the U.S. Navy is already involved, while Spain, Italy and Greece also declined. Trump criticized the reluctance as a "one-way street" but insisted the United States neither needs nor desires NATO assistance, claiming recent military successes have set Iran back by up to a decade.

The source notes that some in the alternative media describe the situation as a classic "Kobayashi Maru" no-win scenario for the administration: retreating risks a Suez Crisis-style geopolitical defeat, while continuing the campaign threatens prolonged energy market chaos and higher global inflation.

Burning Bright responds that the alternative media is now adopting his own framing of the Middle East as "Donald Trump's Impossible Task," mimicking the Kobayashi Maru metaphor he says he has used since 2023, "but they're not quite there." In his reading, "the Iranian Knot is not Donald Trump's Kobayashi Maru. It is the no-win scenario he has deliberately engineered for the Globalist Enemy that has long disguised itself as our so-called Allies." He argues that Trump has placed "the entire architecture of the rules-based international order, the neoconservative war machine, the energy cartels and the scarcity engineers into a pincer movement from which there is no clean or face-saving exit," that Europe and NATO now "find themselves twisting in the very winds they helped generate," and that Trump's calm assurances that the conflict will end "soon" and that oil prices will drop dramatically show "method to the madness" rather than luck. The analysis rests, he writes, on "the complete inversion of the Axis and Allies paradigm."

## Comer subpoenas Bondi over the Epstein files

The Brief reports that [[house-committee-on-oversight-and-accountability|House Oversight and Government Reform Committee]] Chair [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.) formally subpoenaed Attorney General Pam Bondi on Tuesday, requiring her to appear for a deposition on April 14 regarding the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s handling of the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] investigation and its compliance with the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]]. The subpoena followed an earlier committee vote prompted by Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] (R-S.C.), who raised concerns that the DOJ had not turned over all required records.

Lawmakers highlighted more than 65,000 missing documents, over 2,000 unreleased videos, and problematic redactions that in some cases appear to shield Epstein's associates while exposing victim identities. The subpoena passed with full bipartisan support from every Democrat on the panel plus several Republicans, and the committee's top Democrat, Rep. Robert Garcia, accused Bondi of leading a "White House cover-up" of the full files.

The Justice Department called the subpoena "completely unnecessary," stating that lawmakers had already been invited to review unredacted files in person at the DOJ and that Bondi had made herself available for multiple briefings, calls and meetings with Congress on the transparency law. The Brief notes that subpoenas are compulsory but that enforcement ultimately rests with the Justice Department if they are ignored.

Ashe in America argues that the Epstein files fell out of public conversation because of the war, so "Comer and Bondi are dramatically bringing it back." She quotes the DOJ statement in full and observes that Bondi had offered to brief the committee on Wednesday, "so, on Tuesday, they made a public spectacle and pounded tables." She contends that legacy media reporting ties the "missing files" claims to sexual assault allegations against Trump, making Comer's move "a newsmaking political stunt" that supplies "unipartisan lawmakers a new set of narrative cycles to engage in public character assassinations" before the midterms. Noting that Congress costs Americans $16.7 million to $21.7 million per day when in session, she concludes: "But we're paying for their campaign ads? Money well spent."

## The killing of Ali Larijani

Iranian state media confirmed the death of Ali Larijani, Secretary of the [[supreme-national-security-council|Supreme National Security Council]] and one of Tehran's highest-ranking officials, who the Brief says was killed along with his son, a deputy and bodyguards in U.S.-Israeli airstrikes at his daughter's home. Israel separately announced the elimination of Gholamreza Soleimani, longtime commander of the Basij militia, in the same wave of strikes.

In retaliation, Iran escalated into what it calls a "new phase of oil war," launching drone swarms and attacks on energy infrastructure including the Shah oil and gas field in the UAE, Iraq's Majnoon field, and targets in Saudi Arabia. Iranian officials continued to assert control over the Strait of Hormuz, warning that the security situation in the waterway has permanently changed.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry calls the development notable chiefly because Larijani was the Iranian official responsible for implementing a form of "[[devolution|Devolution]]" — [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] — in August 2025 after the 12-Day War. Because of that protocol, he argues, a successful "decapitation strike" is unlikely, since authority has already been devolved to lower-level commanders. He notes that Iran was slow to affirm the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s claim and by some reports initially denied it, and that someone subsequently took control of Larijani's X account to post about the death. Asking what reason Iran would have to lie, he offers the possibility that the denial served to protect Larijani by moving him into hiding.

Citing a [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] article, the commentary notes that [[russia|Russia]] is pushing Iran to stay in the fight, signaling that it views continuation of the conflict either as a negotiating chip or as an earnest proxy war against Israel, which has been attacking Russian allies in the Middle East since the October 7th attack. [[china|China]] is also said to be trying to keep Iran in the fight, with both powers feeding intelligence to Tehran. If true, this contradicts the presumption that Russia and China want stabilization in the Persian Gulf. The author's explanation is that they have a vested interest in preventing a strategic defeat for Iran, which would reflect poorly on the worldwide coalition of alliances the two have been building — the network Badlands calls "[[sovereign-alliance|The Sovereign Alliance]]" — whose credibility depends on power projection against unipolar hegemony: "if China and Russia can't protect Iran and keep it sovereign, then what chance do smaller countries with lesser military capabilities have?" He further asks whether Xi and Putin now view Israel as a growing threat to the region and to global trade and logistics, noting that the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]] has invested billions in the North South Transportation Corridor, with Iran as the critical central node connecting Asia to Moscow, and asking whether the corridor can operate effectively if Iranian infrastructure is destroyed.

## Bonus item: Venezuela wins the World Baseball Classic

In a bonus item, the Brief reports that [[venezuela|Venezuela]] defeated Team USA 3-2 in the final of the 2026 World Baseball Classic on Tuesday night at LoanDepot Park in Miami, claiming the country's first championship in the tournament. The game was played before a heavily pro-Venezuela crowd energized by the large Venezuelan immigrant community in South Florida.

Venezuela built a 2-0 lead in the third inning on a sacrifice fly by Maikel Garcia that scored Salvador Pérez, followed immediately by a solo home run from Wilyer Abreu. Team USA tied the game in the eighth when Bobby Witt Jr. drew a leadoff walk and Bryce Harper hit a two-run homer. In the ninth, Luis Arráez walked, Javier Sanoja stole second as a pinch runner, and Eugenio Suárez delivered a go-ahead RBI double. Closer Daniel Palencia then retired the side in order, striking out Roman Anthony with a 99.7 mph fastball to seal the victory. Venezuela had advanced after a pool-stage loss to the Dominican Republic by beating Japan and Italy; for Team USA it was a third straight WBC final appearance and a second consecutive loss after falling to Japan in 2023. Venezuela manager Omar López praised the team's unity amid the country's political tensions, saying they played "together with no color, political colors or ideology."
