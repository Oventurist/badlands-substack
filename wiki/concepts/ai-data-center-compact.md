# AI Data Center Compact

The AI data center compact is a voluntary agreement reportedly being drafted by the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration under which the largest American technology companies would publicly commit to bearing the infrastructure costs of their artificial intelligence data centers. It is described in the February 12, 2026 [[badlands-brief-405|Badlands Brief]] on the basis of *[[politico|Politico]]* reporting.

## Reported contents

*Politico*, citing two administration officials granted anonymity to discuss private conversations, reported on February 9, 2026 that the administration was courting the world's largest technology companies to sign the compact. The outlet said it had seen a draft laying out commitments designed to ensure that energy-hungry data centers do not raise household electricity prices, strain water supplies or undermine grid reliability, and that the companies driving the new demand also carry the cost of building the infrastructure to serve it.

The Badlands commentary enumerates the reported participants — OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta — and the obligations attributed to the draft: financing new power generation and transmission upgrades, entering long-term contracts to shield residential ratepayers from price spikes, meeting water positivity mandates, making grid reliability commitments, and undertaking community mitigation and planning. The stated purpose is to preempt an energy crunch as data center demand is projected to triple by 2028.

## Political context

The compact emerged alongside two other developments the Brief treats as related. The administration announced the repeal of the [[endangerment-finding|endangerment finding]], eliminating the legal basis for a body of [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] climate regulation including motor vehicle emission standards. Simultaneously, a *Politico* poll found that 37% of US adults believed Democrats care more about protecting them from spiking natural gas, heating and utility bills, against 25% for Republicans — an inversion of historical trends. A *[[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]]* opinion piece, "Even Trump Can't Go Back to the Future," argued separately that structural shifts toward natural gas and renewables, and the decline of coal on cost grounds, constrain what any administration can reverse.

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] reads the compact as a precise counter-deployment in what he calls the Golden Age War, arriving on the same day the endangerment finding repeal dominated surface coverage. He argues the poll and the surrounding coverage are narrative operations aimed at Trump's energy acceleration agenda, framing an abundance push and a nuclear renaissance as the cause of rising bills rather than the remedy.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] takes a more ambivalent view, writing that requiring corporations to enter compacts "feels right in an age where corporations are more powerful than nation states," especially technology firms. She observes that Trump's position is that planned growth must be centrally planned, which is why he seeks to prohibit states from acting independently while "luring corporate countries into treaties (essentially)." She says she hates the precedent but finds the premise sound, comparing the moment to the reshaping of the landscape by solar fields and arguing that letting companies define the future without accounting for the public produced the dystopia the country is exiting.
