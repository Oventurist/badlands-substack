# Patriot Missile System

The Patriot is a US-made air defense system that became one of the most contested items of Western military assistance to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] during the war with Russia.[1] Its transfer chain — from the United States to allied holders and onward to Kiev — has repeatedly raised questions about which countries are quietly participating in the conflict.[1]

## Israeli transfer to Ukraine

Israeli Ambassador to Kiev Mikhail Brodsky revealed in an interview with Ukrainian journalist Marichka Dovbenko that [[israel|Israel]] had quietly transferred US-made Patriot systems to Ukraine, after previously insisting it was providing only humanitarian assistance.[1] "The Patriot systems we once received from the United States are now in Ukraine," Brodsky said, describing them as Israeli systems deployed in the early 1990s that Israel agreed to transfer.[1] He added that the transfer "hasn't been widely discussed," but that claims Israel had not helped militarily were "simply not true."[1] Asked about reports that Israel had been sending military equipment to Ukraine via third countries, Brodsky called it "a sensitive issue" that should not be discussed publicly.[1]

West Jerusalem had previously claimed it was delivering only humanitarian aid, reportedly out of concern over provoking Russia, which maintains a naval facility and an airbase in neighboring Syria; Israel has stressed that it seeks to maintain good relations with both Ukraine and Russia.[1] Axios reported in January that the United States transferred around 90 Patriot missiles from Israel to Poland for onward delivery to Ukraine, and the New York Times later reported that Kiev would receive a full Patriot system previously stationed in Israel.[1] According to the Times of Israel, eight systems were retired from Israeli service in 2024.[1]

## Russian reaction

Russia's envoy to the UN, Vassily Nebenzia, warned that any Israeli decision to transfer Patriots to Ukraine would carry "political consequences."[1] Moscow has repeatedly argued that foreign arms shipments only escalate the conflict and will not prevent Russia from achieving its objectives, and the Kremlin has listed a halt to Western arms deliveries as a precondition for any ceasefire.[1]

## The "17 Patriots" ambiguity (July 2025)

At a July 2025 Oval Office meeting with [[nato|NATO]] Secretary-General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] would receive "17 Patriots" from NATO countries, saying a European member of the bloc would supply the American-made air defense systems but not specifying whether he meant whole batteries or separate launchers.[2] Major General [[vadym-skibitsky|Vadim Skibitsky]], deputy head of Ukraine's military intelligence agency (HUR), said Kiev was unsure what Trump meant.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] explained the operational stakes: one battery comprises six to eight individual launchers, so Trump could be sending 17 launchers or as many as 136.[2] Ukrainian generals need that figure to plan deployment sites, staffing — each battery requires 50 to 100 soldiers, implying up to 1,700 personnel for 17 full batteries — and resupply of the individual projectiles.[2] When the press asked for clarity, Trump responded that the Patriots were already in the mail being shipped to [[germany|Germany]], offering no figure, leaving the Ukrainians in a holding pattern.[2] He characterized Trump as "the Chaos Conductor," wielding ambiguity as a deliberate instrument.[2]

German Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]] had already agreed to purchase several Patriot surface-to-air systems for Ukraine, a strategy [[italy|Italy]] declined to follow.[2] The parliamentary leader of [[die-linke|Die Linke]], [[ulrich-thoden|Ulrich Thoden]], argued that routing Patriots to Kiev through European NATO members may create "strong profit prospects" for the American [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]] while doing nothing to end the conflict.[2]

## Reported downing of a Ukrainian F-16 (August 2024)

Ukrainian lawmaker Maryana Bezuglaya claimed in late August 2024 that a Patriot air defense battery operated by [[ukraine|Ukraine]] shot down one of the country's newly delivered US-made F-16 fighters in a friendly fire incident, killing pilot Alexey "Moonfish" Mes.[3] She attributed the loss to poor coordination between military units and accused the Air Force Command of falsely characterizing the event as a crash.[3]

## Alleged use in the 2024 Il-76 shootdown

The [[russian-investigative-committee|Russian Investigative Committee]] concluded in June 2024 that the Il-76M transport carrying Ukrainian prisoners of war that crashed on January 24, 2024 had been downed by a US-made Patriot interceptor missile.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Russian authorities had recovered fragments of the aircraft and of two missiles and determined by forensic analysis that both projectiles were MIM-104 Patriots of American manufacture.[4]
