# DoD Law of War Manual

The Department of Defense Law of War Manual (abbreviated LOWM in the source article) is the U.S. Department of Defense's consolidated statement of the law of armed conflict as it applies to American forces. In the Badlands Media article "Countering an Ocean of Deceit," the manual is invoked as the legal basis for the claim that the United States is presently under a condition of hostile occupation.[1]

## The occupation argument

The article quotes Chapter 11, section 11.2.2 of the manual: "Territory is considered occupied when it is actually placed under the authority of the hostile forces."[1] The author argues that when district attorneys pursue prosecutions against ordinary Americans while permitting illegal entrants, criminals and terrorists to act with impunity, the enemy has effectively hijacked the authority of government, satisfying the manual's criteria for occupation.[1]

A second element of the argument is the manual's recognition that a state of occupation prevents the normal operation of the rule of law within the occupied territory — meaning, in the author's reading, that a different set of rules applies than would apply under ordinary circumstances.[1] This is deployed as a rebuttal to [[17th-special-citizens-operations-group|17SCOG]]'s claim that the [[q-operation|Q operation]] must be illegal under Title 50 § 3093, DoD Directive 3600.01 and [[executive-order-12333|Executive Order 12333]].[1] The author reinforces the point by noting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described himself as a "wartime president," which is taken to indicate that conditions of war apply to the present situation.[1] The author acknowledges the argument is preliminary and states an intention to research the topic further.[1]
