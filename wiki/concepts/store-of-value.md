# Store of Value

A store of value is an asset, currency or commodity that maintains its worth over a long period; an item qualifies if its value is stable or increases over time rather than depreciating.[1] If an item can be held and converted into money in the future without a decrease in value, it is considered a good store of value, and various commodities qualify by virtue of their divisibility, durability and portability.[1]

## Gold as the ultimate safe haven

Gold is regarded as the ultimate safe-haven asset because its store of value does not deteriorate in an economic crisis, it is always in demand, and it is easily convertible.[1] Badlands Media argues that both what it calls the cabal and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] have long understood that gold is the one true store of value.[1]

## Inflation and the destruction of value

[[alan-greenspan|Alan Greenspan]] is quoted arguing that under the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] a free banking system protects an economy's stability and balanced growth, that abandoning it allowed welfare statists to use the banking system for unlimited credit expansion, and that "in the absence of the gold standard, there is no way to protect savings from confiscation through inflation."[1] Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] adds that inflation discourages saving — interest on deposits falls far below the inflation rate — and that rising living costs have driven an explosion in credit card debt.[1]

## Central banks' own reasoning

Surveys of central banks found consistent themes: gold can be mobilized in a crisis, serves as an emergency reserve, a contingency against unforeseen events, a form of insurance, or in the Bank of England's words a "war chest" and the "ultimate asset to hold in an emergency."[1] Banks also cited gold's high liquidity, its usefulness in raising foreign-currency liquidity and foreign exchange intervention, its role as an inflation hedge, and the fact that physical gold — limited in supply and mined into existence — carries no counterparty, credit or default risk because no government issues it.[1] These properties are contrasted with [[fiat-currency|fiat currency]], which the article argues is losing value as a global reset approaches.[1]
