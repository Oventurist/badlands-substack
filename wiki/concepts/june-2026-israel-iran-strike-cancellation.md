# June 2026 Israel–Iran Strike Cancellation

In early June 2026, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] intervened to prevent a broader renewal of fighting between [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]], warning Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that Israel could find itself "on your own very soon" if it expanded military operations against Iran.[1] Trump made the comments to Axios; Reuters later confirmed them and reported that Trump said he had cautioned Netanyahu against resuming a wider war.[1]

## Sequence of events

Axios reported that Netanyahu canceled what would have been Israel's largest strike on Iran since the April ceasefire, following discussions with Trump.[1] The episode came amid renewed hostilities that began with Iranian missile launches against Israel and subsequent Israeli strikes on Iranian targets — the first reported Israeli strikes on Iran since the April ceasefire took effect.[1]

On June 8, Trump publicly called on both countries to "immediately stop shooting."[1] Axios reported that he spoke again with Netanyahu and asked him to halt planned strikes on Iran.[1] Trump also said Iran had informed the United States that it did not intend to conduct further attacks and had requested that Washington relay that message to Israel.[1]

Following the intervention, Netanyahu said Israel would "hold fire" against Iran while indicating that Israel reserved the right to respond if attacked in the future.[1] The developments temporarily reduced fears that the exchange of missile and air strikes would escalate into a broader regional conflict.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as vindication of a thesis he had advanced for months: that Trump "is actually waging a war against Israel, using Iran as his [perhaps unwitting] proxy."[1] He acknowledged that the claim had sounded crazy and that "almost nobody was buying it," but argued that its implausibility is precisely what makes it effective — "it's the last thing anybody would expect."[1]

In his account, the only way Trump could sell the idea that he was an ardent Zionist was for his own support base to believe it, which required intentionally damaging his own reputation in order to sell the ruse to the public, the press, and "the zealots running the Israeli government and the Deep State insurgency occupying DC."[1] He pointed to Trump's earlier exchange with a reporter — asked whether Israel had forced his hand to launch strikes against Iran, Trump replied, "No, I might have forced [Israel's] hand" — and to Trump's reference to negotiating with "these lunatics," suggesting the lunatics in question may have been Netanyahu's cabinet rather than the Iranians.[1]

He offered [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]]'s turn against Trump as corroborating evidence, noting Levin's accusation that Trump was "desperate for a deal" and his complaint, "I don't know why he keeps bashing the Prime Minister of Israel! …Why the constant beating up of Netanyahu?!"[1] For GhostofBasedPatrickHenry this signaled "trouble in paradise" and an awareness that the United States had already decided to abandon Israel's military campaign in the Middle East — leaving Israel to face Iran and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] alone.[1]
