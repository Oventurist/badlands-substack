# Ancient German Language Thesis

The **ancient German language thesis** is the claim, advanced across [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]]'s [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], that the entire world once spoke ancient German or something almost identical to it.[1] The essay "On Languages and Cover-ups" presents the thesis as a corrective to what the author calls the hypnotic spell of mainstream history, and as a research tool by which suppressed history may be recovered.[1]

## Origins of the claim

The thesis was introduced in part five of the series and is credited to the researcher [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]], described as the only person responsible for making the information available to the English-speaking world.[1] Dodson in turn is said to have learned much of it from the German author [[erhard-landmann|Erhard Landmann]], and the author states that the best way to learn the material is to learn German and consult the original source, something he intends to do some day.[1] The principal English-language text cited is [[extraterrestrial-linguistics|*Extraterrestrial Linguistics*]].[1]

The author argues that German being mankind's first language "makes a lot of sense," on the reasoning that *germ*, like *seed*, often denotes the first of something, as in *germinate*.[1] He further notes that many religious mythologies — most famously the [[tower-of-babel|Tower of Babel]] story — claim mankind once spoke a single language, so evidence of such a tongue should not be surprising.[1]

## Comparative word lists

The essay offers sample vocabulary comparisons from four languages, following earlier installments that compared Aztec with ancient German.[1]

**Amharic**, described as an ancient language first spoken by an isolated people in Ethiopia and later made one of that country's two official languages, supplies *fikiri* for love against modern German *ficken*; *debebi* for stupid against German *Depp*; *aliga* for bed against German *Liege*; and *habitami* for rich against the ancient German phrase *hab itamal*, "to have a celebration."[1]

**Maori**, the language of the New Zealand tribe whose origin stories describe red-haired, man-eating giants, supplies *auwehi* for sad or dejected against ancient German *auweh*; *ka mau* for fear, glossed as "queasy energy" from German *mau* (queasy) plus *ka* (energy); and *piro*, to extinguish fire, against Greek and ancient German *pyro*, fire — a reversal the author attributes to translators hearing the word as a fire was being put out and hastily concluding its meaning.[1] The author flags the red-haired giants of Maori tradition as highly significant, since the [[koryos|Koryos]] werewolf cult was said to consist of red-haired albino cannibals.[1]

**Xhosa**, a people of South Africa, supplies *inqanawa* for ship, said to contain the ancient German elements *qan* (vessel) and *awa* (water); *umfazi* for woman against a German vulgarity; *ukutya* for food, glossed as "it's good, yes"; and *iqanda* for eggs, again containing *qan*, on the reasoning that eggs are vessels through which life enters the world.[1]

**Basque**, conventionally classified as a language isolate — a classification the essay rejects — supplies *behar* (need) against German *beharren*, to insist upon; *beldur* (fear), linked to the Norse Baldr and the Canaanite deity Ba'al, with the note that *baal* also means whale in Basque and that Fijians use *bulu* for the underworld; and *eder* (beautiful) against German *edel*, noble or excellent.[1]

## Academic suppression

The essay asserts that the world's languages are far more connected than almost anyone in the modern world knows, and that as early as 1866 mainstream academia made it anathema to discuss unified origins of languages.[1] The author contends this became official policy purely for its own sake rather than because the evidence warranted a taboo on reasonable investigation.[1] He predicts that if the academic world ever took the research seriously it would herald a revolution in human self-understanding and bring prosperity and freedom closer — which he offers as precisely the reason the material is discussed nowhere in mainstream academia.[1]

## Words as artifacts

The organizing metaphor of the piece is that words are precious archaeological artifacts: because histories are constantly attacked and rewritten, only the study of words allows an accurate story of the past to be reassembled.[1] This underpins the companion argument that total control over a population requires control over language itself.[1]

## The Aztec case and method

Part 5 of the series, "The Ancient Origin of Modern Conflict," presents the thesis as its central subject, stating that all the peoples of the world once spoke ancient German, that almost no one in the English-speaking world is aware of this, and that the claim is nonetheless demonstrably true.[2] The essay opens with the epigraph "Words are archeological artifacts," attributed to [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]].[2]

That installment sets out the comparative method the series uses. Over long spans of time languages transform, so a German speaker and a speaker of ancient Aztec would likely hear no connection; the resemblance, the author argues, becomes impossible to ignore only when words are broken into smaller components and written out.[2] What matters is overall resemblance rather than identity — the example given is the ancestral [[donald-trump|Trump]] family name *Drumpf*, whose essential structure survives a shift in pronunciation.[2] Meaning is treated the same way: words whose senses merely point in the same direction are counted as related, as with Aztec *wolkaan* (sky) and German *wolkan* (clouds).[2] Thematic, phonetic and written resemblances all count, and where two languages are near-isomorphic the author holds that contact is more likely than coincidence, invoking [[qanon|Q]]'s question of how many coincidences it takes before something is mathematically impossible.[2]

The essay's evidentiary core is a [[aztec-german-word-correspondences|list of roughly forty Aztec words paired with ancient German counterparts]], drawn from [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]]'s *Extraterrestrial Linguistics*.[2] The author cautions that ancient German is not the same as modern German, which is why readers may not recognise the German forms, and states that ancient German words or close derivations of them can be found in languages all over the planet.[2]

## Implications drawn

The thesis is used in that installment to support several further claims. Because [[christopher-columbus|Columbus]]'s "discovery" of the Americas is incompatible with worldwide contact in antiquity, the mainstream account is treated as a cover story.[2] Because the peoples of the world once shared a language and later fell into relative isolation, the essay asks what event confounded human languages and separated mankind for so long.[2] And because both the negative and positive "elites" would descend from that shared linguistic world, the author expects — and claims to find — ancient German names among elite families, citing Drumpf and Schwaub as examples.[2]

Finally, the author frames etymology as a strategic weapon: knowing the origin of words teaches how the collective psyche has morphed over the years, and in an information war knowing the origin of things confers an advantage.[2] He suggests the "elite" may be elite simply because they have retained an information advantage over mankind going back to the beginning.[2]
