# Penn Treaty of 1682

The Penn Treaty of 1682 was an agreement between [[william-penn|William Penn]] and the Delaware natives of Pennsylvania, celebrated in a 1771 painting by [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]] and presented in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as an unprecedented model of intercultural diplomacy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Terms

The article reproduces several of the treaty's articles in full. Article 1 held that all of Penn's people or Christians and all the Indians should be brethren, as children of one father, joined together as with one heart, one head and one body.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Article 2 declared that all paths should be open and free to both Christians and Indians.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Article 3 required that the doors of Christian houses be open to the Indians and Indian houses open to the Christians, each making the other welcome as friends.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Article 4 stipulated that neither side should believe false rumours or reports about the other, but should first come as brethren to inquire, burying such reports "as in a bottomless pit."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

Article 5 required each side to acquaint the other speedily with any ill news that might injure them, as true friends and brethren.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Article 6 barred harm to persons or creatures on either side, providing that where wrongdoing occurred the injured party should complain, right be done, and the injury forgotten and buried once satisfaction was made.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Article 8 obliged Indians and Christians to assist one another against all wicked people who would disturb them.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Article 9 required both peoples to acquaint their children with the league and firm chain of friendship, keeping it "bright and clean without rust or spot" between their children and children's children "while the creeks and rivers run and while the sun and moon and stars endure."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Significance

Ehret emphasises Article 4 in particular against the background of intelligence operations — especially those he attributes to the [[jesuits|Jesuits]] — that conducted slanders and promoted gossip in order to induce hostility between colonists and natives, and manipulated native groups from Canada and Quebec into carrying out massacres.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The provision that participants talk to each other before reacting to gossip or provocation is described as making all the difference.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

The treaty's mode of thinking is presented as emblematic of the [[peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]] of 1648 and its Principle of the Benefit of the Other, established just forty years before Penn's treaty as the basis of international relations and of the modern sovereign nation state, premised on the Christian concept that respect for one's neighbour is bound up with one's own self-interest.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Despite failings in the decades that followed, the treaty was renowned as one of the only agreements of its kind to endure.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
