# Hong Kong Pro-Democracy Movement

The pro-democracy movement is a political camp in [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]] that supports increased democracy — namely the universal suffrage of the Chief Executive and the Legislative Council as provided by the [[hong-kong-basic-law|Hong Kong Basic Law]] under the "[[one-country-two-systems|One Country, Two Systems]]" framework.[1]

## Origins and electoral strength

The movement has existed since before the 1997 handover and has typically received about 55–60% of the vote in each election.[1] Its activists emerged from the youth movements of the 1970s and began taking part in electoral politics as the colonial government introduced representative democracy in the mid-1980s.[1]

## Suppression

[[samuel-chu|Samuel Chu]], Managing Director of the [[hong-kong-democracy-council|Hong Kong Democracy Council]], described the movement as "so central and vital for the 30+ years in Hong Kong," a movement his father led and in which he himself participated, and said that today all of its leaders "are either in jail, under house arrest, living in exile, or just stripped of their rights to speak out."[1]

Many members of the movement were prosecuted for the peaceful protests of 2014; by the time they were sentenced in 2018, [[china|China]] had proposed the extradition law that triggered the [[2019-hong-kong-anti-extradition-protests|Anti-Extradition Law Amendment Bill Movement]].[1] Chu's father received a two-year sentence in Hong Kong courts for organizing the 2014 protests — a sentence Chu said would have been for life on the mainland.[1] Since the imposition of the [[hong-kong-national-security-law-2020|National Security Law]], candidates considered insufficiently loyal to China have been barred from running for Hong Kong's electoral council.[1]

## Key episodes

- The [[umbrella-movement|Umbrella Movement]] and Hong Kong Democracy Protests of 2014.[1]
- The Anti-Extradition Law Amendment Bill Movement protests of 2019.[1]
