# Deep State

The "Deep State" is the term used across the Badlands corpus for the entrenched network of intelligence, financial and political actors held to operate independently of elected government, and in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s June 2023 essay it functions as the agent behind both the political maneuvering in Washington and the cultural program he calls the [[communist-takeover-of-america|Communist takeover of America]].[1]

## Panic response

Carlson treated the burst of disruptive events following [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s confirmation as Speaker — boxes of classified documents being discovered, train derailments, Chinese spy balloons, and the uncovering of the Nord Stream pipeline sabotage — as evidence that the Deep State viewed the new speakership as a threat and had gone into "full-on panic mode."[1]

## The Cabal banking system

Carlson referred to a "Cabal banking system that controls the country and the world," and asked whether the [[2023-debt-ceiling-agreement|2023 debt ceiling agreement]] and a worsening economy were necessary preconditions for its destruction before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] returns to power.[1]

## Coercion of corporations and consumers

The Deep State is also cast as the force compelling corporations into [[wokeness|wokeness]] under threat of losing portfolio status and financing, and — following [[andy-frisella|Andy Frisella]]'s argument — as the party that benefits when consumers boycott and destroy [[americana|Americana]] brands.[1] Carlson warned that Americans may be "doing the Deep State's bidding for them" without realizing it.[1]

## Surveillance and AI

Carlson compared the [[artificial-intelligence-rollout|public rollout of artificial intelligence]] to the FBI and CIA progressing from tapping phones and hacking computers to persuading targets to invite them into their homes willingly and even excitedly.[1]

## Political cover

Figures such as John McCain, Mitt Romney and the Bushes are named as prior examples of players "commissioned to appear conservative," a template Carlson raised as one possible reading of [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]].[1] The bipartisan continuity this implies is described elsewhere in the essay as the [[uniparty|Uniparty]].[1]
