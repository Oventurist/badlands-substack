# FBI Recall of 2020 China Election Intelligence

The FBI recall of 2020 China election intelligence refers to the bureau's decision to withdraw and order the destruction of a raw intelligence report alleging that the Chinese government was manufacturing fraudulent mail-in votes for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], a matter that resurfaced publicly in June 2025 through disclosures by [[kash-patel|FBI Director Kash Patel]].[1]

## The underlying allegation

A confidential human source told [[fbi|FBI]] counter-intelligence in summer 2020 that China's communist government was shipping fake driver's licenses to the United States in order to manufacture "tens of thousands of fraudulent mail-in votes" for Joe Biden, according to a raw intelligence report distributed to federal agencies and reviewed by Just the News.[1] The subject line of the bulletin described the alleged plot as Chinese government production and export of fraudulent U.S. driver's licenses to Chinese sympathizers in the United States in order to create tens of thousands of fraudulent mail-in votes for presidential candidate Joe Biden, in late August 2020.[1]

## Distribution and recall

The report was sent to U.S. intelligence agencies on August 24, 2020, as an uncorroborated advisory, then suddenly recalled with little explanation other than that the bureau wanted to "re-interview" the source.[1] The recall notice specifically instructed spy agencies to erase or delete the original memo, stating that recipients should destroy all copies of the original report and remove it from all computer holdings.[1] Officials told Just the News the recall prevented the FBI and other agencies from fully investigating allegations that Beijing was meddling in the election to Biden's benefit, even though corroborating evidence arrived from another law enforcement agency: [[customs-and-border-protection|U.S. Customs and Border Protection]] had captured 19,888 of the fake driver's licenses — mostly from Hong Kong and China in late July 2020 — en route to battleground states in the Midwest.[1]

## Disclosure in 2025

The report was one of two sent on Monday, June 16, 2025, by Patel to [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] Chairman [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]].[1]
