# Artificial Food Dyes

Artificial food dyes are synthetic colorings whose dangers, according to Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], have been known for decades — decades during which scientists were bankrolled by the food industry to produce contrary results, tweaking details in study abstracts to elicit the desired conclusion.[1] The dyes still on the market today are linked to cancer and neurological problems.[1]

## Individual dyes

Blue 1 and Blue 2 are found in beverages, candy, baked goods, and pet food, and have been linked to rapid cancer growth in mice; both are banned in Norway and Sweden.[1] Red 3, used to color cocktail cherries, grenadine, candy, and baked goods, has been shown to cause thyroid tumors.[1] Green 3, most commonly found in candy and beverages, has been linked to bladder cancer.[1] The widely used Yellow 6, added to beverages, sausages, gelatine, baking ingredients, and candy, has been linked to tumors of the adrenal gland and kidneys.[1]

## Behavioral effects in children

Multiple studies cited in the article indicate that artificial colorings may contribute to behavioral problems in children and to a significant reduction in IQ.[1]
