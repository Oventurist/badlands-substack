# 1992 Los Angeles Riots

The 1992 Los Angeles riots followed the acquittal by jury of four [[lapd|LAPD]] officers tried for the 1991 beating of [[rodney-king|Rodney King]].[1]

## Course of the unrest

The verdict sparked mass rioting across the Los Angeles area, with widespread violence, destruction, and looting.[1] A white truck driver was famously dragged from his truck and beaten, and inner-city Korean convenience store owners defended their businesses with rifles from rooftops.[1] [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] characterizes the episode as a race riot.[1]

## Consequences

According to Carlson, whatever public support the Black community had gained following the King beating was completely erased by the riots.[1] He treats the 1992 unrest as one node in an alleged thirty-year cycle of manufactured racial division alongside the [[harlem-riots-1935|1935 Harlem riots]], the [[watts-riots-1965|1965 Watts riots]], and the 2020 [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] protests, arguing the cycle exists because more than thirty years without a racially divisive incident might allow racism to fade.[1] He situates the early 1990s specifically as an important moment to push racial division because prison privatization under [[bill-clinton|President Bill Clinton]] required a reversal of falling crime rates.[1]
