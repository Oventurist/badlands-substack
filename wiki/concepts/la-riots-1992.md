# 1992 Los Angeles Riots

The 1992 Los Angeles riots followed the acquittal by a jury of four [[lapd|LAPD]] officers tried for the 1991 beating of [[rodney-king|Rodney King]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Events

Violence, destruction and looting broke out across the Los Angeles area. A white truck driver was famously dragged from his truck and beaten, and inner-city Korean convenience store owners defended their businesses with rifles from the rooftops. Erik Carlson characterizes the episode as a race riot, and observes that whatever public support the black community had gained following the King beating was, by its end, completely gone.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Interpretation

Carlson treats the riots as the intended second act of a staged sequence beginning with the King footage, and as one node in an alleged pattern of [[manufactured-race-riots|manufactured race riots]] recurring roughly every thirty years.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) He situates the early 1990s as a period in which racial division was especially useful, coinciding with [[prison-privatization|prison privatization]] under President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and the drafting of the [[crime-bill-1994|1994 Crime Bill]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)
