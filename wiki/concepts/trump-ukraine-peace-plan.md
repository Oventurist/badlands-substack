# Trump Ukraine Peace Plan

The Trump Ukraine peace plan is a 28-point framework for ending the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|war in Ukraine]] that emerged in November 2025, notable for pairing painful Ukrainian concessions with an unprecedented American security guarantee.[1]

## Article 5-style security guarantee

According to a draft obtained by [[axios|Axios]], the plan includes a security guarantee modeled on NATO's [[article-5|Article 5]], committing the United States and European allies to treat an attack on [[ukraine|Ukraine]] as an attack on the entire "transatlantic community."[1] The 28-point document presented to President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] states simply that "Ukraine will receive reliable security guarantees," but the U.S. presented a separate draft agreement alongside it.[1] That companion text provides that any future "significant, deliberate, and sustained armed attack" by [[russia|Russia]] on Ukraine "shall be regarded as an attack threatening the peace and security of the transatlantic community," to which the U.S. and its allies would respond accordingly, including through military force.[1] Obtaining a robust U.S. and European security guarantee had been Zelensky's top objective in peace talks, and this was the first time Trump was willing to put one on the table.[1]

## Presentation and terms

U.S. Army Secretary Dan Driscoll presented the 28-point plan to Zelensky on Thursday, November 20, 2025.[1] Per Badlands commentary, the plan bars [[nato|NATO]] from deploying any troops into Ukraine and reduces the Ukrainian military from over 800,000 to 600,000 personnel.[1] The entirety of the Donbas region would fall under Russian control, current battle lines would be frozen, and Russia would retain the portions of Ukraine on its side of those lines.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that an Article 5-style guarantee means very little if Russia and the United States become close allies, and that the real story is that the 28-point plan was assembled without input or feedback from the European Union or Ukraine — ostensibly a bilateral negotiation between Washington and Moscow.[1] He noted the plan arrives as Ukraine is consumed by a mass corruption scandal that may collapse its government, at least in personnel terms, as officials resign in disgrace over money laundering and embezzlement schemes.[1] He predicted the Trump–Putin peace moment would arrive before Christmas.[1]

## 2024 antecedents

During the 2024 campaign, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] boasted that he would end the war by the time he was inaugurated in January, and used the June televised debate to decry the billions of dollars spent on Ukraine's defense, saying Kyiv was "not winning the war."[2] On July 3, 2024, Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskiy]] publicly challenged him to reveal the plan, saying "If Trump knows how to finish this war, he should tell us today," while warning that any proposal must not violate Ukrainian sovereignty or statehood.[2]

In parallel, a Daily Mail report claimed [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had sent interior minister [[vladimir-kolokoltsev|Vladimir Kolokoltsev]] to Washington under cover of a United Nations police-chiefs meeting to transmit a "new peace plan" under which Russia would share sovereignty of [[crimea|Crimea]] with [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the two men had effectively proposed the same settlement: Ukraine remaining a sovereign — in his phrasing, proxy — state; Russia not encroaching across the Dnieper River; and Ukraine staying out of [[nato|NATO]].[2]

He noted the NATO point was not something Trump was espousing publicly, but was one he was positioning to actualize by threatening withdrawal from the alliance unless European members matched US defense spending proportionally — a demand he argued EU nations were financially incapable of meeting, meaning "the US's days in NATO are numbered."[2]

## June 2024 Kellogg-Fleitz plan

In June 2024 Reuters reported that [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]] and [[fred-fleitz|Fred Fleitz]], both former chiefs of staff in [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s National Security Council, had presented him with a plan to end the war if he won the November 5 election: Ukraine would be told it would receive more US weapons only if it entered peace talks, while Moscow would be warned that refusing to negotiate would bring increased US support for Kyiv, with a ceasefire based on prevailing battle lines during negotiations.[3] Fleitz said Trump responded favorably without necessarily endorsing every word, and spokesman [[steven-cheung|Steven Cheung]] said only statements from Trump or authorized campaign members should be treated as official.[3] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the plan mirrored [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s own terms — denazification and annexation of the Donbas and a pledge that Ukraine would not join [[nato|NATO]] — and concluded "the deal is already made."[3]

## Secret US-Russia drafting (November 2025)

Axios reported on November 19, 2025 that the Trump administration had been working secretly in consultation with [[russia|Russia]] to draft the plan, inspired by Trump's successful push for a deal in [[gaza|Gaza]], and that a top Russian official was optimistic about it.[4] The 28 points were said to fall into four buckets: peace in Ukraine, security guarantees, security in Europe, and future U.S. relations with Russia and Ukraine; it was unclear how the draft handled contentious issues such as territorial control in eastern Ukraine, where Russian forces had been inching forward but still controlled far less land than the Kremlin demanded.[4] Trump envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] led the drafting and discussed it extensively with Russian envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]], who runs Russia's sovereign wealth fund and spent three days with Witkoff and other members of Trump's team during a visit to Miami from October 24-26, 2025.[4] Dmitriev told Axios he was optimistic because, unlike past efforts, "we feel the Russian position is really being heard."[4] Witkoff had been expected to meet Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] in Turkey that Wednesday but postponed the trip.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Europe and Ukraine were apparently not even aware of the negotiations, which he said demonstrated that the real power in the conflict lies with the United States and Russia; when the two make peace — which he judged imminent — it would change the balance of power and signal the start of the [[multipolar-world|multipolar world]].[4]
