# Biden Supreme Court Reform Proposals

The Biden Supreme Court reform proposals were a package of three changes to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] unveiled by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on Monday, July 29, 2024, days after he abandoned his own reelection campaign.[1] Biden outlined the proposals in a Washington Post op-ed that the paper identified as written by the President of the United States, and the package was widely read as aimed at curbing the powers of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should he return to office.[1]

## Contents of the package

Biden described the Court as being in an "ethical crisis" and proposed three key changes: a constitutional amendment limiting presidential immunity, term limits for justices, and a binding code of conduct.[1] The term-limit proposal envisioned a new justice being appointed every two years to serve an eighteen-year term.[1] Biden argued that the Court's existing voluntary ethics code was inadequate and should be replaced by a binding one.[1]

The centerpiece was the response to the Court's ruling granting presidents broad immunity for crimes committed while in office, branded by Biden as the "No One Is Above The Law Amendment."[1] Biden objected to the 6-3 decision of July 1, which he argued effectively removed constraints on presidential action, writing: "This nation was founded on a simple yet profound principle: No one is above the law. Not the president of the United States. Not a justice on the Supreme Court of the United States. No one."[1] He warned that "if a future president incites a violent mob to storm the Capitol and stop the peaceful transfer of power — like we saw on Jan. 6, 2021 — there may be no legal consequences."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that "the husk of Joe Biden isn't going quietly into the night" and predicted that Biden would deploy a series of narratives before the election that would simultaneously build support for Trump and drive his own party to distraction.[1] He questioned the authorship of the op-ed outright — "there isn't a single soul in America who believes Joe Biden is penning Op Eds in the Washington Post, but who is doing so on his behalf?" — and suggested that, read through a Devolution and "Plan" framing, the crassly branded "No One is Above the Law Amendment" was the sort of measure "bound to boomerang with force."[1] He left open whether the package represented genuine regime proposals or veiled warnings directed at the regime itself.[1]
