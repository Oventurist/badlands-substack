# Battle of the Monongahela

The **Battle of the Monongahela** was an engagement of the [[french-and-indian-war|French and Indian War]] fought on July 9, 1755 in the area of present-day Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, roughly 30 miles from Butler.[1] In Badlands Media commentary the battle is invoked chiefly for the survival of the future first president [[george-washington|George Washington]], which is presented as a documented instance of providential protection.[1]

## Course of the battle

During the two-hour engagement, the 23-year-old Colonel Washington rode back and forth across the battlefield delivering the general's orders to other officers and troops.[1] The Native American combatants fighting alongside the French deliberately targeted officers: of the 86 British and American officers present, 63 became casualties, and Washington was the only officer on horseback who was not shot down.[1]

## Washington's letter

In a letter written to his brother after the battle, Washington described his escape in providential terms: "By the all-powerful dispensations of Providence, I have been protected beyond all human probability or expectation; for I had four bullets through my coat, and two horses shot under me, yet [I] escaped unhurt, although death was leveling my companions on every side of me!"[1] Accounts of the episode note that when Washington removed his coat after the battle he found four bullet holes through it but no wounds on his body.[1]

## The chief's testimony

The version popularised by Dr. [[bo-wagner|Bo Wagner]] in an August 15, 2013 *Cleveland Corner* column holds that fifteen years later an old and respected Indian chief sought Washington out and identified himself as the leader of the warriors who had opposed him.[1] The chief is quoted as saying that he had directed his men to concentrate their fire on the "tall and daring warrior," with rifles "which, but for you, knew not how to miss," but that "a power mightier far than we shielded you," and that seeing Washington under "the special guardianship of the Great Spirit" they ceased firing at him — the chief coming to "pay homage to the man who is the particular favorite of Heaven, and who can never die in battle."[1] Wagner concluded that God raised Washington up specifically to lead the nation against Britain in its hour of greatest need.[1]

## Use in Badlands commentary

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] drew on the battle in July 2024 as the historical parallel for the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] in Butler, Pennsylvania, noting that the two events occurred about 30 miles apart and 269 years and four days apart.[1] He argued that both Washington and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been chosen and protected by God — one to make America, the other to make America great again — and that the parallel gave literal force to the phrase "nothing can stop what is coming ... not even an assassin's bullet."[1]
