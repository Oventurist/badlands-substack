# Balfour Declaration

The Balfour Declaration is the British statement that, in the account given by Badlands Media, paved the way for the creation of the modern state of [[israel|Israel]].[1] The Badlands article "On Infiltration and Israel" emphasizes that the declaration was addressed to "none other than 'Lord Rothschild'," and states that the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild family]] funded much of Israel's creation.[1]

## Placement in the Badlands narrative

The article argues that the modern state of Israel "was not created in a vacuum" but by specific people in pursuit of a specific purpose — one the author says has nothing to do with the long-term wellbeing of any Semitic peoples.[1] The financiers held responsible are the same class the writer calls "ziobanksters," a term borrowed from Chris Langan, and the same class said to have funded and promoted the [[kalergi-plan|Kalergi Plan]].[1]

## Mid-century context

Badlands situates the declaration's consequences within a cluster of mid-twentieth-century institutional developments: the [[bretton-woods-agreement|Bretton Woods agreement]], which the author says turned Federal Reserve notes into the world's reserve currency; the founding of the [[united-nations|United Nations]]; and the establishment of Israel — all formed within a few years of one another around the conclusion of the Second World War.[1] The article treats this clustering as evidence of coordinated design rather than coincidence.[1]

## Authorship and imperial motive

Part 6 of the "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler" series states that the 1917 declaration — which it calls the Balfour Accords — was co-authored by [[arthur-balfour]] alongside [[leo-amery]], [[alfred-milner|Lord Milner]] and Walter Rothschild, with Prime Minister Lloyd George overseeing the project.[2] Balfour is described there as a leading strategist of the Rhodes-Milner Round Table Group and, like [[winston-churchill]], a white supremacist, Zionist and supporter of fascism.[2]

The article notes that Balfour had earlier led the passage of anti-Jewish immigration laws in England in 1905 that barred persecuted Russian Jews from the UK, and that he wrote in 1919 that Zionism would "mitigate the age-long miseries created for Western civilization by the presence in its midst of a Body which it too long regarded as alien and even hostile, but which it was equally unable to expel or to absorb."[2] On this reading the declaration served two purposes at once: providing an excuse to purge the Jews from Europe, and creating a weapon for destabilization at the geopolitical pivot of Halford Mackinder's Heartland.[2]

## Rothschild framing (December 2024 analysis)

A December 2024 Badlands Media analysis described the 1917 Balfour Declaration — the letter from British Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour to Baron Lionel Rothschild promising British support for a Jewish homeland in Palestine — as a calculated geopolitical move rather than a gesture of goodwill.[3] The [[rothschild-family|Rothschild family]], deeply embedded in European banking, is said to have seen the establishment of Israel as a means of securing British influence in the Middle East.[3]

The article treats the Declaration as the opening step in a longer sequence culminating in the [[un-partition-plan-for-palestine|UN Partition Plan]] of 1947 and the founding of modern Israel in 1948, an event it presents not merely as a response to the Holocaust but as the culmination of decades of maneuvering by globalist power brokers seeking control of the Middle East.[3] The displacement of over 700,000 Palestinians after the 1948 Arab-Israeli war is described as laying the groundwork for decades of conflict and ensuring Israel's strategic dependence on the West.[3]

## Origin as a wartime bargain (October 2023 account)

Badlands writer [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] traces the Declaration to a straightforward transaction made while Britain was losing [[world-war-i|World War I]] badly to the Germans: Prime Minister [[david-lloyd-george|David Lloyd George]] proposed to Jewish leaders in England that if they could bring the United States into the war, the English elite would promise the Jews their own homeland.[4] Carlson writes that the Americans were staying out of the war — a sizable percentage of the population being of German descent — but that after the proposal Jewish-American bankers began lending to the British war effort, anti-German propaganda depicting Germans as "baby killers" was pushed in Jewish-American-run media, German products were pulled from Jewish-American-owned retail stores, and the [[sinking-of-the-lusitania|Lusitania was sunk]] in what he calls a false flag, after which the US entered the war.[4]

"The Balfour Declaration was this promise kept," he writes, describing the 1917 public statement issued by the British government promising to establish a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine, with [[arthur-balfour|Lord Arthur Balfour]] issuing the letter on behalf of the cabinet and addressing it to Lord Rothschild, whom Carlson considers a member of the British Cabal elite.[4]

## Consequences claimed

Carlson states that decades later, in 1948, the Palestinians were removed from the land they occupied to make room for the Zionists, in some cases with Palestinian houses bulldozed while families were still inside.[4] He estimates 17 million deaths from World War I and 85 million worldwide from [[world-war-ii|World War II]], arguing that much of what happened on the world stage in the first half of the twentieth century led to the creation of the State of [[israel|Israel]].[4]

Declining to adjudicate whether the Zionists had a right to the land or whether England had the right to give it, he compares the act to Canada telling Mexico it could have the United States, and describes the result as placing a giant hornet's nest in the region while periodically whacking it with a Louisville Slugger — tension he calls 100% intentional.[4] Noting that many of the British elites vital to Israel's creation were reportedly antisemites, he concludes they honored a deal they were afraid to break while exploiting it to maintain permanent conflict at a safe distance from Western Europe.[4] He cites [[haaretz]] op-eds of January 27 and October 26, 2017 on the myth of [[winston-churchill|Churchill]]'s "special friendship" with the Jewish people and on British policy-elite notions of "Jewish race," "Jewish power" and Palestinian Arab "impurity"; a November 1, 2017 Institute for Palestine Studies piece stating "It's time to admit that Arthur Balfour was a white supremacist"; and a June 24, 2020 Middle East Monitor headline calling Churchill an anti-Semite who embraced [[zionism|Zionism]] as a colonial tool.[4]
