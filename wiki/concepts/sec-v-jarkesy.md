# SEC v. Jarkesy

**SEC v. Jarkesy** is a decision of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] handed down at the close of the Court's October 2023 term, in which the Court held that defendants accused of fraud by the Securities and Exchange Commission have the right to a jury trial rather than being adjudicated solely within the agency's in-house administrative tribunals.[1]

## Significance in Badlands coverage

Badlands Media's July 2, 2024 news brief treated *Jarkesy* as one of the most consequential rulings of a term dominated in the headlines by the presidential-immunity case, arguing that presidential immunity "wasn't even at the top of the list this week" in terms of structural importance.[1] Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the ruling as a blow to the administrative state, noting with evident satisfaction that critics were already lamenting that regulators would be able to bring fewer enforcement cases for fear of exhausting their budgets once jury trials became the norm.[1] The brief observed that jury trials for SEC fraud defendants had not previously been standard practice, a fact it presented as itself an indictment of the administrative adjudication system.[1]

## Relationship to Chevron

The brief paired *Jarkesy* with the Court's contemporaneous decision overturning the [[chevron-deference|Chevron deference]] doctrine, describing the two rulings together as shifting the benefit of the doubt away from federal agencies and toward the governed.[1] Both decisions were credited in part to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Supreme Court appointments, which the brief said had been "packing quite a punch."[1]
