# NATO-Funded US Arms Plan for Ukraine

The NATO-funded US arms plan for Ukraine is a scheme announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Monday, July 14, 2025, under which the United States would manufacture and sell American-made weapons to European [[nato|NATO]] members, who would then fund and forward them to [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1]

## Announcement

Trump pledged the new deliveries during an Oval Office meeting with NATO Secretary-General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]]. "We're not buying it," Trump said. "We will manufacture it, and they're going to be paying for it."[1] Trump noted that Washington sees the plan as a business opportunity.[1] Rutte said six countries — Finland, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, the Netherlands and Canada — were willing to take part in the arms procurement scheme.[1]

## European surprise and opt-outs

According to Reuters, citing European officials, several NATO member states were not notified in advance that they would be asked to fund the deliveries; high-ranking sources at the embassies of two of the named countries said they only learned of their supposed participation when the announcement was made.[1]

The [[czech-republic|Czech Republic]] became the first member publicly to opt out, with Prime Minister [[petr-fiala|Petr Fiala]] saying Prague was focusing on other ways to help Ukraine.[1] [[italy|Italy]] was reported by La Stampa to lack the budget to participate, declining to follow the strategy of German Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]], who had already agreed to purchase several [[patriot-missile-system|Patriot]] systems.[1] In Germany, [[die-linke|Die Linke]] parliamentary leader [[ulrich-thoden|Ulrich Thoden]] criticized the plan as profitable for the American [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]] but useless for ending the war.[1] Moscow likened the effort to the United States preparing a deadly meal for Ukraine and forcing others to pay for it.[1]

## Related ambiguity over "17 Patriots"

Trump's statement that Kiev would receive "17 Patriots" from NATO countries left Ukraine unable to determine whether whole batteries or individual launchers were meant, according to Major General [[vadym-skibitsky|Vadim Skibitsky]] of Ukraine's HUR military intelligence agency.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the announcement was a ploy designed to undermine and bankrupt Europe, noting that member states learned of their obligations from the Trump–Rutte press conference and were therefore never "read in."[1] He described the pattern of refusals as proof of the theory, observing that European governments that had demanded mobilization against Putin were unwilling to fund the fight themselves and expected the US taxpayer to carry it — with an exception carved out for Italy's [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]], described as having opposed the war and advocated peace.[1] He warned that such states may find themselves in more direct conflict with Russia without the means to fight it, and characterized Trump as "the Chaos Conductor."[1]
