# 2023 Syracuse Street Party Violence

On the early morning of Sunday, June 11, 2023, a street party that drew hundreds of people on the West Side of Syracuse, New York turned violent, leaving at least 13 people injured.[1]

## Incident

At least four people suffered gunshot wounds and five others had apparent knife wounds; other victims were apparently struck accidentally by vehicles while fleeing what police described as a "large gathering of hundreds of people."[1] Police Lieutenant Matthew Malinowski said all of the injured — three males and ten females, aged between 17 and 25 — were expected to survive, and that the 13 known victims were located on the street or later at hospitals.[1]

According to [[abc-news|ABC News]], the crowd was a mix of high school students, recent graduates and college students, many of whom had just returned home for the summer.[1] Police Chief Joe Cecile said, "The party was advertised on social media. No permit was issued, and the streets were not officially blocked off or policed."[1]

## Media commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the way the story evolved was bizarre, with every outlet reproducing the same vague summary and settling on the phrase "an apparent street party."[1] She noted that for almost 18 hours there were no details about why hundreds of people had gathered in the street in the middle of the night, no account of what triggered the violence or whether stabbings preceded gunfire, and — beyond age and gender — no demographic information about the victims.[1] In her reading, details were being closely held by police and the coverage felt sanitized relative to how community violence is usually reported.[1]

She contended that an event of this scale should have generated scoops and interviews with traumatized attendees rather than police-sourced summaries alone, calling the resulting coverage "controlled, unnatural."[1] The only interview she located was with a neighborhood resident, Richard Webb, who told Spectrum News, "Each time this event takes place my house ends up with bullets in it. Just recently last night, there were four more bullets... Put down the guns and stop this madness. Life is short, and it's a beautiful thing. Why end it that quickly?" — a call for disarmament that, she observed, addressed the guns but not the stabbings or vehicular injuries.[1] She closed by noting that all 13 victims were expected to recover, better news than had become typical of mass-violence incidents in 2023 America.[1]
