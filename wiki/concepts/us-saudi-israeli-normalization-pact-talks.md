# US–Saudi–Israeli Normalization Pact Talks

The US–Saudi–Israeli normalization pact talks were a series of high-stakes diplomatic meetings conducted in early January 2024 among American lawmakers and diplomats, Saudi leadership, and Israeli officials, aimed at producing a far-reaching treaty framework to end the Israel–Hamas war and reshape the Middle East.[1]

## The Al Ula meetings

Roughly eleven days before the January 18, 2024 reporting, Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] (R-S.C.) arrived for a private meeting in a lavish tent furnished with ruby red rugs and low burgundy cushions in the western Saudi Arabian oasis town of Al Ula, home to ancient Nabatean ruins. The tent was guarded by layers of Saudi security protecting the nearby winter camp of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1]

Graham participated in a series of meetings with the crown prince involving American lawmakers and diplomats hoping to rekindle a potential treaty between Saudi Arabia, Israel, and the United States. The stated goal was to hammer out a framework for concluding the Israel–Hamas war, stabilizing the Middle East, and paving the way for some form of Palestinian self-governance in the Gaza Strip.[1]

## The central question

The principal obstacle was whether the Israeli government — and the Israeli public — would accept a path to a Palestinian state in exchange for an American-backed peace treaty with Saudi Arabia. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and hard-line members of his right-wing coalition had spent months publicly dismissing the idea of a Palestinian state following the October 7 Hamas terrorist attack.[1]

Nevertheless, according to six people familiar with the talks, a normalization deal with Saudi Arabia that ended the war was viewed as a potential political win for Netanyahu. Nadav Eyal, a columnist for the Israeli newspaper Yediyot Ahronot, said that "any deal for normalization with Saudi Arabia right now would be a major win politically speaking for Netanyahu and an exit strategy."[1]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that with the Middle East devolving into an engineered variant of the chaos that has afflicted the region for generations, the Establishment was seeding the idea that only the Saudis could broker peace. He countered that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "had this buttoned up" during his presidency through the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], and maintains a better relationship with Mohammed bin Salman than any other Western leader.[1]

Burning Bright framed the diplomacy as consistent with the emergence of a multipolar world order engineered by sovereign-minded, military-backed change agents, predicting a bicameral outcome: the globalist arms of the US and EU accumulating further public embarrassment, while Trump, MBS, Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping, and other sovereign leaders form a new mesh of lasting peace.[1]

## MBS assassination warnings (August 2024)

In August 2024, Politico reported that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] had told members of [[congress|Congress]] that he was putting his life in danger by pursuing the grand bargain with the United States and [[israel|Israel]] that would include normalizing Saudi-Israeli ties.[2] He invoked [[anwar-sadat|Anwar Sadat]], the Egyptian leader slain after striking a peace deal with Israel, asking what the U.S. had done to protect Sadat, and cited the threats he faced in explaining why any deal must include a true path to a Palestinian state — a requirement sharpened by the war in Gaza having heightened Arab fury toward Israel.[2] People familiar with the conversations said the crown prince nonetheless appeared intent on striking the mega-deal, viewing it as crucial to Saudi Arabia's future.[2]
