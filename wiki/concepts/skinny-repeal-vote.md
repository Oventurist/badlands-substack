# Skinny Repeal Vote

The "skinny repeal" vote was the July 2017 United States Senate vote on a pared-down measure to roll back the [[affordable-care-act|Affordable Care Act]], defeated when Sen. [[john-mccain|John McCain]] cast a decisive thumbs-down vote against it.[1]

## Background

The Senate had been debating Obamacare repeal for two months, and the effort came down to a single night.[1] Sens. [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] (R-ME) and [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] (R-AK) had held firm in opposition for weeks and were expected to vote against the Republican plan even in its "skinny" form, which did little more than repeal the individual mandate and which some senators hoped would set up negotiations with the House over a fuller health care plan.[1] With two Republican defections already accounted for, a single additional no vote on the Republican side was enough to kill the measure.[1]

## McCain's vote

The entire country waited on McCain's decision; he held the power to kill the mandate or preserve it.[1] The Badlands account stresses that McCain had campaigned on repealing Obamacare during his re-election campaign, and that the measure before the Senate was not even a full repeal — only the removal of a government mandate the article describes as unconstitutional.[1] McCain voted no, which the article characterizes as going against his word to Arizona voters and as proof that he did not care about the American people.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The article argues the vote was not merely Republicans versus Democrats or McCain versus [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], but a continuation of a narrative war in which McCain had been "activated" by a puppet master identified as Prince [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1] It attributes McCain's motive to money, pointing to Obamacare's function as a vehicle for fraud — the Paragon Health Institute estimated fraudulent exchange enrollment at 100 to 150 percent of the federal poverty level at four to five million people in 2024, costing upwards of $15 to $20 billion, with some states reporting more enrollees in the bracket than residents.[1] The article also notes that [[jonathan-gruber|Jonathan Gruber]], called the architect of Obamacare, had admitted the law relied on a lack of transparency to pass.[1]

## The surgery question

Shortly before the vote, McCain underwent surgery after a diagnosis of brain cancer.[1] Q drop 52 of November 2, 2017 asked where McCain obtained his surgery, why that was relevant, "what surgery did he supposedly have," how many days passed until he was back in Congress sitting on the Senate Armed Services Committee, and what his net worth was and how it reconciled.[1] Q drop 5 raised the possibility that McCain never had surgery and that the diagnosis was cover for "a future out if needed against prosecution."[1] The Badlands author argues McCain sought "health cover" because he feared evidence of treason connected to his [[mccain-syria-trips|trips to Syria]] would surface under the new Trump administration.[1]
