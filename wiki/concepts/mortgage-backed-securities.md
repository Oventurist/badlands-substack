# Mortgage-Backed Securities

**Agency mortgage-backed securities** (MBS) are packaged securities containing a basket of home loans that banks and financial institutions sell on to investors.[1]

## Role on the Fed balance sheet

MBS are the second-largest asset category on the [[federal-reserve-balance-sheet|Federal Reserve balance sheet]], behind government securities such as Treasury bills, notes, and bonds.[1] The [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] buys or sells mortgage-backed securities alongside Treasury bonds to infuse money into markets or absorb it from them.[1] In 2008 the Fed purchased large quantities of both government and mortgage-backed securities, expanding the balance sheet by roughly 250 percent within a few months, on the reasoning that financial markets would collapse and banks become illiquid otherwise.[1]

## Role in the 2008 collapse

Mortgage-backed securities were, in Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s account, "one of the big things that got banks in trouble in 2008," because so much of Wall Street's exposure was tied to home loans.[1] The instruments therefore sit at the center of both the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]] and the central bank response that followed it.[1]
