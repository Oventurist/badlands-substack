# 2025 Israeli Strike on Doha

The 2025 Israeli strike on Doha refers to Israeli missile and bomb attacks carried out on September 9, 2025 against the Qatari capital, Doha, targeting figures associated with [[hamas|Hamas]].[1] The attack on the territory of a sovereign Gulf state produced a sharp increase in military-political tensions across the Middle East and drew condemnation from Russia and Gulf partners.[1]

## Russian condemnation

Speaking on September 11, 2025 at a Russia–[[gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] Strategic Dialogue session in Sochi, Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] said the strike demonstrated Israel's unwillingness to end the humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza and warned it would further destabilize the region.[1] He noted that the meeting was taking place "against the backdrop of a sharp increase in military-political tensions in the Middle East due to Israeli missile and bomb attacks on Doha, the capital of [[qatar|Qatar]], on September 9," which Russia had received with deep concern.[1]

Lavrov characterized the attack as "a gross violation of international law, primarily the UN Charter, an encroachment on the sovereignty and territorial integrity of an independent state," and "a step leading to further destabilization of the situation in the Middle East."[1] He emphasized Qatar's role as a key mediator in the indirect negotiations between Israel and Hamas over ceasefire terms in the Gaza Strip, calling it a particular cynicism that a principal mediator had been struck, and said the Israeli action unambiguously showed an intent to undermine international peace efforts.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the episode as Russia beginning to confront Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over Israeli conduct, and asked what [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Lavrov would do to help Qatari Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Sheikh Tamim]], Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and UAE President [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]] navigate what he called a quagmire of unbridled hubris.[1]

## Trump-Al-Thani meeting

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] planned to meet Qatari Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani]] in New York on the Friday following the strike, a White House official said without elaborating on timing or agenda.[2] Israel had attempted to kill the political leaders of [[hamas]] in the Tuesday attack, a strike that risked derailing US-backed efforts to broker a truce in Gaza and end the nearly two-year-old conflict, and that was widely condemned across the Middle East and beyond as an escalation in an already tense region.[2]

Trump said he was unhappy with the strike, describing it as a unilateral action that did not advance US or Israeli interests, while Washington continued to count [[qatar|Qatar]] as a strong Gulf ally and a principal mediator in ceasefire negotiations, hostage releases, and post-conflict planning for Gaza.[2] Al-Thani blamed Israel for trying to sabotage chances for peace but said Qatar would not be deterred from its mediating role.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Trump had already ordered [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] to draft a defense agreement with Qatar granting it military protection in the event of another Israeli strike, suggesting that was why Rubio had been "summoned to Israel like a dog."[2]

## Conduct of the strike

Around 15 Israeli warplanes fired at least ten missiles during the Tuesday operation, reportedly killing several Hamas members including the son of senior official [[khalil-al-hayya|Khalil al-Hayya]].[3] The [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]], acting in coordination with the security service [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]], struck a compound in the Qatari capital used by [[hamas|Hamas]], which said its top leadership survived and described the attack as an attempt to assassinate negotiators working on a potential settlement.[3]

## Qatari response

Qatari Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman al-Thani]] called the attack a "pivotal moment" for the whole region and condemned it as "state terrorism," saying the surprise strike would not be "overlooked" and that Qatar "reserves the right to respond to this blatant attack."[3] "Today, we have reached a turning point for there to be a response from the entire region against such barbaric conduct," he told a press conference, while taking personal jabs at [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and accusing him of disrupting regional stability for the sake of "narcissistic delusions" and personal gain.[3] Al-Thani said Qatar would continue its mediation effort regardless.[3]

In an exclusive interview with [[cnn|CNN]]'s Becky Anderson, Al-Thani called the attempted assassination of Hamas leaders in Doha "barbaric," saying "we were thinking that we are dealing with civilized people."[3] He argued the strike "killed any hope" for the hostages remaining in Gaza, noting that he had met one of the hostages' families the morning of the attack and that "they are counting on this (ceasefire) mediation, they have no other hope for that."[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] commented that this appeared to mark "the end of the hostage aspect of the story."[3]

## American reaction

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] criticized the airstrike in a Truth Social statement, stressing that the bombing inside "a Sovereign Nation and close Ally of the United States" did not "advance Israel or America's goals," and that "I view Qatar as a strong Ally and friend of the US, and feel very badly about the location of the attack."[3] He emphasized that the operation was "a decision made by Prime Minister Netanyahu, it was not a decision made by me."[3]

The [[white-house|White House]] confirmed that Trump had directed Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] to warn the Qatari government of the impending Israeli attack in advance, and that Witkoff had done so.[3] Press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the administration had been notified that morning by the United States military that Israel was attacking Hamas in a section of Doha, and reiterated that Trump viewed Qatar as a strong ally.[3] The Qatari government denied advance knowledge of the strike and condemned it forcefully.[3]

## Further Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu had "really stepped in it this time," writing that bombing Doha — a city that came to symbolize peace after the [[doha-agreement|Doha agreement]] between Trump and the Taliban in 2020 — might in hindsight prove one of the worst decisions of Netanyahu's political career.[3] He noted that Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Sheikh Tamim]] is one of Trump's oldest friends in the Middle East, and that Trump had mediated the rivalry between Tamim and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] during the [[qatar-diplomatic-crisis-2017|2017 blockade of Qatar]].[3] He concluded that Americans must choose between honoring a new friendship with the Arab states and remaining "captive to the narratives of yesteryear."[3]

## Netanyahu's apology at the White House (September 2025)

On Monday, September 29, 2025, the White House said that Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had apologized to [[qatar]] for striking its territory, during a meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4] The Israeli military had targeted senior Hamas leadership in Qatar with an airstrike earlier that month, also killing a Qatari security official.[4] Trump had distanced himself from the decision to strike a top Middle East ally, writing on Truth Social at the time that it "does not advance Israel or America's goals"; Qatar has been a key host of talks aimed at a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas and hosts about 10,000 U.S. troops.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the White House scene as "nothing short of a humiliation ritual," arguing that the image of Netanyahu telephoning Sheikh Tamim Al Thani of Qatar to apologize for the missile fired into Doha would not soon be forgotten in the Islamic world, and that it was Trump who forced the apology.[4] He contended that the Eastern world respects strength and honor and that such acts would help win Muslim trust on the road to the Golden Age, citing as further evidence Trump's suggestion at the press conference that questions about the proposed peace deal be deferred since the other side had not yet reviewed it, before offering Netanyahu a question from "a friendly Israeli reporter" — an exchange in which Netanyahu was said to stumble for words.[4]

## Diplomatic fallout

The strike on Doha, which killed six people including a Qatari security officer, triggered an emergency summit in the Qatari capital the following week bringing together the leaders of the [[arab-league|League of Arab States]] and the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation|Organization of Islamic Cooperation]].[5] Their final statement called on member nations to "consider the compatibility of Israel's membership in the UN with its Charter" and to coordinate efforts to suspend [[israel|Israel]] from the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[5]

Israel maintained that the strike had targeted [[hamas|Hamas]] officials, but the assembled leaders branded it a "dangerous escalation that exposes the extremist hostility of the Israeli government" and accused Israel of undermining international mediation, since Doha had served as a key venue for peace talks between West Jerusalem and Hamas.[5] The statement urged all states to review diplomatic and economic relations with Israel and take "legal and effective measures" including sanctions and the suspension of arms and dual-use exports.[5] The suspension push is documented at [[arab-and-islamic-call-for-israels-suspension-from-the-united-nations|the Arab and Islamic call for Israel's suspension from the United Nations]].[5]
