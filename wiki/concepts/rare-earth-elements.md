# Rare Earth Elements

Rare earth elements are a group of metals whose principal strategic value lies in the permanent magnets manufactured from them. According to the [[department-of-defense|Defense Department]], rare earth magnets are key components in a wide range of military weapons systems, including the F-35 warplane, drones and submarines.[1]

## Import dependence

The United States was almost entirely dependent on foreign countries for rare earths as of 2023, with [[china|China]] accounting for roughly 70 percent of U.S. imports, according to the [[united-states-geological-survey|U.S. Geological Survey]].[1] That dependence made rare earths a central point of contention in the trade disputes between Washington and Beijing, and it supplied the policy rationale for direct federal intervention in the domestic mining and magnet sector.[1]

## Neodymium-praseodymium oxide

Neodymium-praseodymium oxide, or NdPr, is the specific rare earth compound used to make permanent magnets and the reference commodity in the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s 2025 arrangement with [[mp-materials|MP Materials]].[1] Under that deal the government guaranteed a floor price of $110 per kilogram for ten years for NdPr stockpiled or sold by the company, paying the difference quarterly in cash if the market price fell below the floor, and taking 30 percent of the upside above $110 per kilogram once the company's second magnet facility came online.[1]

## Policy context

Interior Secretary [[doug-burgum|Doug Burgum]] said in April 2025 that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] was considering making direct equity investments in critical mineral companies specifically to break U.S. dependence on China.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the resulting Pentagon investment as evidence that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his team were restructuring the national portfolio — prioritizing strategic bets and reducing foreign dependence in critical industries — while noting that few voters had envisioned the outcome would be private companies with the Pentagon as majority shareholder.[1]
