# Iraq War

The Iraq War functions in the article as the primary case study of war serving as cover for the theft of public money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

A Q drop is quoted asking how much cash the Federal Reserve sent to Iraq during the war.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The answer the article supplies, drawn from CNBC, is that the [[federal-reserve-bank-of-new-york|Federal Reserve Bank of New York]] shipped roughly $40 billion in cash to Baghdad between 2003 and 2008 in what has been called the largest airborne transfer of currency in history — more than 281 million individual bills weighing 363 tons in the first two years alone — loaded at East Rutherford, New Jersey, trucked to Andrews Air Force Base and flown by C-17 to Baghdad International Airport.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) On arrival every flight was met by a single [[coalition-provisional-authority|CPA]] official, known only as Basel, who moved the pallets along Route Irish to the [[central-bank-of-iraq|Central Bank of Iraq]] without military escort and, the article notes, never lost a load.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

Once the money reached Iraq there were no documents tracking it at all, which the article insists was purposeful and is the answer to how tens of billions could be stolen and stashed in bunkers around the world.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) An investigation during the [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] presidency discovered over a billion dollars in a bunker in Lebanon, said to be destined for Hezbollah; Bush squashed the investigation and the story was forgotten.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article states that more than half the money was stolen.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

Iraq is also linked to Libya on the supply side: research by the Combating Terrorism Center at West Point found Libya provided the most foreign terrorists in Iraq relative to population, with the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|LIFG]] training fighters in Libya to kill American soldiers in Iraq after joining [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] in 2007.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article treats Iraq as the template later applied to Libya, describing the latter as a money-laundering operation that was "actually much worse."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)


## Badlands commentary

"Profiting From War" argues that [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] went to war in Iraq to remove [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] on the basis of the "weapons of mass destruction" lie, and that Saddam was nowhere near the threat the [[bush-family|Bush family]] made him out to be.[2] The article highlights an under-remembered episode of the war: [[reuters|Reuters]] reporting that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] flew more than $4 billion in cash on pallets to Baghdad before the June 2004 handover of sovereignty, including $1.5 billion on December 12, 2003, more than $2.4 billion on June 22, 2004 and $1.6 billion three days later.[2] Special inspector general [[stuart-w-bowen-jr|Stuart Bowen]] reported in January 2005 that $8.8 billion given to Iraqi ministries was unaccounted for.[2] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents the missing funds as stolen taxpayer money used to finance American enemies, and the cash-pallet method as a precedent for the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration's later payments to Iran.[2]

## Q drop #117: oil, Halliburton and the WMD pretext

Chapter 17 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series takes up Q drop #117's question of why the United States attacked Iraq and answers that the official justification of weapons of mass destruction was never borne out, as no such weapons were found.[3] The chapter identifies [[halliburton|Halliburton]] — described as the second largest oil company in the world — and the officials tied to it, [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], the latter having served on its board.[3]

Its conclusion is stated as an equation: "Iraq + Oil + Halliburton + Cheney/Bush 2.0 = Iraq invasion," with the chapter insisting the war had nothing to do with Saddam Hussein or weapons of mass destruction and was always about oil.[3] The invasion is set in the aftermath of [[september-11-attacks|9/11]], alongside a twenty-year war in Afghanistan and the passage of the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]], all justified by claims about weapons of mass destruction and terrorism.[3] The chapter also treats war generally as the single biggest generator of multi-billion-dollar payouts from nation states and therefore as a [[money-laundering|money laundering]] mechanism.[3]
