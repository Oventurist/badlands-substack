# Federal Gun Ban for Drug Users

The **federal gun ban for drug users** is a United States statute that prohibits any person who is an "unlawful user" of a controlled substance from possessing a firearm.[1] In March 2026 the statute came before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] in a constitutional challenge under the Second Amendment.[1]

## Oral argument

The Court heard arguments on a Monday in a case challenging the statute, with several justices expressing skepticism about the law's breadth.[1] The challenge arose after lower courts questioned whether the statute is consistent with the Second Amendment under the framework established in *New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen*, which requires firearm regulations to align with the nation's historical tradition of gun regulation.[1]

During argument, multiple justices asked whether historical analogues exist that would justify broadly disarming individuals based solely on current drug use.[1] The federal government defended the statute, arguing that individuals actively using illegal drugs may pose heightened safety risks comparable to groups historically subject to disarmament.[1]
