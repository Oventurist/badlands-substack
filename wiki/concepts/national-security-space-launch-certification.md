# National Security Space Launch Certification

**National Security Space Launch (NSSL) certification** is the process a launch provider must complete before it is permitted to launch [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] payloads.[1]

On May 26, 2015, Lieutenant General Samuel Greaves, Commander of the Air Force Space and Missile Systems Center (SMC), announced certification of the Falcon 9 launch system for national security space missions, making [[spacex|SpaceX]] eligible for award of qualified national security space launch missions as one of two certified providers at the time.[1]

The certification is demanding. For SpaceX it encompassed 125 certification criteria, more than 2,800 discrete tasks, three certification flight demonstrations, verification of 160 payload interface requirements, 21 major subsystem reviews and 700 audits.[1] Because DoD payloads are worth billions, launch vehicles hundreds of millions, launch operations carry extreme risk, and a single failure has national security implications, the process is intended to give the [[united-states-air-force|Air Force]] confidence that national security satellites will safely reach their intended orbits with full mission capability.[1]

The article "[[trump-doctrine-peace-through-space|Trump Doctrine: Peace Through Space]]" treats NSSL certification as the gateway through which [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] came to displace [[united-launch-alliance|United Launch Alliance]] and, by the article's account, become the DoD's largest contractor.[1]
