# Crossfire Hurricane

Crossfire Hurricane was the code name of the counterintelligence investigation opened by the [[fbi|FBI]] in 2016 into alleged links between the campaign of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the Russian government. It is the origin point of the broader episode commonly referred to as [[russiagate|Russiagate]].

## Origins and aftermath

As summarized in the Badlands Brief of May 5, 2026, the investigation's origins and handling have been the subject of successive reviews, most prominently the probe conducted by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]], whose final report in 2023 criticized aspects of the FBI's handling of intelligence but stopped short of establishing a criminal conspiracy involving senior officials.

The Brief describes the [[2017-intelligence-community-assessment|January 2017 Intelligence Community Assessment]] as part of Crossfire Hurricane, noting that United States intelligence agencies had concluded that [[russia|Russia]] interfered in the 2016 election primarily through cyber operations and influence campaigns aimed at undermining confidence in the electoral process and harming Democratic candidate [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].

## Continuing congressional scrutiny

Congressional investigators have continued to examine how the Russia assessments were developed and communicated. The May 2026 disclosure that assessment co-author [[gavin-wilde|Gavin Wilde]] has come forward as a witness was presented by Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] as evidence that a participant in the investigation's key documentary acts is now cooperating with prosecutors, and she pointed to the direction of the assessment by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and its oversight by [[james-comey|James Comey]], [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], and [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] as the chain of command warranting scrutiny.

## The Carter Page civil suit (June 2026)

The Badlands Brief of June 16, 2026 reports that the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] declined to revive the lawsuit brought by former [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign adviser [[carter-page|Carter Page]] against the [[fbi|FBI]]. Page had sought damages arising from the surveillance conducted against him during Crossfire Hurricane, arguing that the government relied on flawed information in obtaining its Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act warrants. The Court's refusal leaves the lower-court rulings against Page in place, foreclosing that avenue of civil accountability for the investigation's FISA applications.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treats the denial as significant less for its legal effect than for its timing, observing that the case "notably br[ought] FISA abuse back into the conversation as [[congress|Congress]] struggles to pass a FISA extension." She groups it with the [[james-comey|Comey]] prosecution and the [[gavin-newsom|Newsom]] investigation as contemporaneous instances of [[weaponization-of-government|weaponized government]].
