# 2023 Money Supply Contraction

The 2023 money supply contraction was a sustained decline in the United States money supply that began in late 2022 and deepened through the first quarter of 2023, reaching rates of decline unmatched in roughly half a century.[1]

## Data

According to the [[mises-institute|Mises Institute]] analysis excerpted in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief of April 25, 2023, money supply growth fell again in February 2023, moving further into negative territory after turning negative in November 2022 for the first time in twenty-eight years.[1] The February drop continued a steep downward trend from the unprecedented highs of the preceding two years; growth had slowed rapidly since April 2021.[1] The previous instance of negative year-over-year change was November 1994, when contraction persisted for fifteen months before turning positive again in January 1996.[1]

Year-over-year growth in February 2023 stood at –6.6 percent, down from –5.0 percent in January 2023 and from +6.9 percent in February 2022.[1] With negative growth dipping below –6 percent, the contraction approached the largest declines seen in decades; at no point for at least sixty years had the money supply fallen by more than 5.6 percent in any month.[1]

## Economic effects

The analysis held that the monetary slowdown had been sufficient to considerably weaken the economy, pointing to falling home prices, soaring credit card debt, rising consumer loan delinquencies, falling job openings and a declining manufacturing outlook.[1] The article's framing question was whether the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] would panic in response.[1]

## Interpretation at Badlands

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] read the contraction not as a policy error but as "monetary warfare," arguing that expansion and contraction of credit determine which sectors of the economy flourish and which are excised, and citing the mid-1990s expansion that financed the rise of big tech up to the dot-com break as a precedent.[1] In his account the oligarchs "stop the music" in the game of musical chairs, displacing workers who then staff the industries associated with [[agenda-2030|Agenda 2030]]; his proposed answer was a [[parallel-economy|parallel economy]].[1]
