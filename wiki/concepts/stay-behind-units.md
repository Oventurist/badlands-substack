# Stay-Behind Units

**Stay-behind units** are the organizing concept behind [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]]: small clandestine cells of civilians, pre-positioned in a territory along with hidden caches of weapons, who are intended to activate behind enemy lines if that territory is lost.[1] Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] presents the term as essential vocabulary for understanding Gladio, noting that the tactic is very old but that its modern application dates to the immediate aftermath of the Second World War, as articulated in [[cynthia-chung|Cynthia Chung]]'s book *The Empire Where the Black Sun Never Sets*.[1]

## Boer War origins

Watkins traces the tactic's modern demonstration to the first Boer War in southern Africa, where the Boers cached weaponry along the British attack route so that fighters could fall in on the arms as the British passed, sandwiching and soundly defeating them.[1] The British regrouped and won the second Boer War with overwhelming force, then interned women, children and the elderly in holding areas she describes as the first known use of concentration camps, where they were subsequently killed.[1] A young British reporter covering the campaign, [[winston-churchill|Winston Churchill]], later institutionalized stay-behind units throughout the United Kingdom and set up the Jedburgh version during the Second World War.[1]

## Axis adoption

[[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] instituted the same concept across all Axis-conquered territories, using intelligence officers including General [[reinhard-gehlen|Reinhard Gehlen]] and General [[karl-wolff|Karl Wolff]] to build the network; these cells were called [[werewolf-units|werewolf units]].[1] Training in firearms, explosives and communications equipment was required across the network, a task assigned in part to SS officer [[otto-skorzeny|Otto Skorzeny]].[1]

## Cell structure

Each unit comprised roughly 10 to 15 citizens with a primary and secondary person in charge.[1] For secrecy, the command structure knew only the two leaders and not the rest of the personnel, preserving plausible deniability.[1] Caches contained at minimum guns, ammunition, explosives, communication devices and money.[1]
