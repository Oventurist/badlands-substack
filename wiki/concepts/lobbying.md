# Lobbying

Lobbying is identified in the article as the second great mechanism, alongside war, by which American taxpayers are made to fund their own enemies.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The argument runs that ostensibly humanitarian or pro-democracy advocacy organisations in Washington, D.C. are in fact established by hostile actors to capture congressional appropriations. The article's principal example is [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]], who headed the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] — presented publicly as raising money for a Syrian rebel army fighting [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] but described here as a lobbying group for [[isis|ISIS]] — and who had earlier run the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] on behalf of the insurgents fighting [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article calls this "a similar name, and the same playbook," concluding that "it is actually easy for the enemy to steal taxpayers money through lobbying."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

These groups are said to be set up by the enemy to fund the very organisations the United States is at war with, and the article maintains that [[john-mccain|McCain]] and many other officials knew exactly whom the lobbyists represented.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Consortium News is quoted describing McCain's Senate office as "a clubhouse for arms industry lobbyists and neocon operatives."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) A related function of the arrangement is legitimation: formal recognition of bodies such as the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as legitimate governments is what allows the money to flow openly.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)
