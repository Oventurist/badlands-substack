# Badlands Brief: A Fiesta of Fake Elections, Fake Files & Fake Crises

"A Fiesta of Fake Elections, Fake Files & Fake Crises" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published on February 11, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] and recorded with 138 likes. Following the Brief's standard format, each segment pairs a summarised news item with commentary from one of the outlet's contributors — in this edition [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The masthead notes that the opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the positions of Badlands Media.

## FBI review of 2020 vote-count deficiencies in Fulton County

The lead item reports that, according to court documents filed on February 10, 2026 in federal court by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the [[fbi|FBI]] is actively investigating potential improprieties in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]]'s handling of ballots from the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]]. The probe is detailed in search-warrant affidavits authored by FBI Special Agent [[hugh-evans|Hugh Evans]] of the Atlanta Field Office and focuses on alleged deficiencies in vote counting and record-keeping: missing ballot images, inconsistent totals across recounts, chain-of-custody documentation issues, duplicate ballot images, and absentee ballots lacking the creases expected of ballots folded and mailed in envelopes.

The affidavits explain that access to the physical ballots is required to assess whether the issues stemmed from administrative error or intentional acts. Agent Evans emphasised that deliberate interference with election records would constitute a violation of federal law even if it did not alter the outcome of Georgia's 2020 presidential race, which [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lost by a narrow margin. The article states that the investigation was initiated following a referral from [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]], a Trump-appointed director of election security and integrity who had previously assisted with legal challenges to the 2020 results.

In January 2026, FBI agents executed court-authorised search warrants at the Fulton County Election Hub and Operation Center to seize relevant materials — the episode covered in the corpus as the [[fulton-county-election-raid|Fulton County election raid]]. Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] was present during the execution of the warrants at the president's request, and subsequently informed [[congress|Congress]] in a letter that interference in U.S. elections poses a threat to national security. The source also notes that many of the specific allegations in the affidavits — including discrepancies between ballot images and reported vote totals raised by a redacted witness described as a chemical engineer — had previously been addressed through the manual recount ordered by Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] in 2020, reviewed by the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]], and litigated in post-election challenges brought by Trump and his legal team, all of which were dismissed by courts for lack of standing or merit. [[white-house|White House]] Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] addressed media coverage of the seizure, drawing contrasts with prior reporting on claims of Russian election interference.

In commentary, Ashe in America writes that she "teared up a bit" reading the unsealed search warrant, observing that "the idea that these items would be useful for the purposes of investigation was, itself, a conspiracy theory like five minutes ago." She enumerates the categories of records now in DOJ custody: missing ballot images from the original count and recount; duplicate ballots in both counts; inconsistent totals reported at different times; missing SHA hash files used to verify the integrity of ballot images; potential tabulator-tape manipulation, such as inconsistency between machines' reported votes and protective counter data; and ballots with broken security seals and unexplained handling. She notes the DOJ's position that the records themselves may prove violations of federal election law, adding that this "is what we've been saying on Badlands for literally years," and citing [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]]'s remark that the warrant reads like a review of the Why We Vote catalog on Georgia. She argues that Fulton County's Fourth Amendment lawsuit is "dead," reporting that she fed a legal AI model the county's complaint, Perdue's testimony and the unsealed warrant, and that the model put Fulton's chances of winning at "less than zero." The section closes with a joking wish for further courtroom appearances by [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]].

## Trump and Netanyahu posturing ahead of the Iran meeting

The second item previews Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s visit to Washington. Trump said Netanyahu "wants a good deal" with [[iran|Iran]] on its nuclear program and described ongoing Oman-mediated talks — following a brief 2025 Israel-Iran war and U.S. strikes on Iranian sites — as "very different" from past negotiations, expressing optimism that Iran "wants to make a deal very badly" while warning of tough military action, including additional carrier deployments, if diplomacy fails. Any agreement, he indicated, would address nuclear facilities and possibly missiles.

By contrast, Trump explicitly opposed recent Israeli moves toward greater control in the [[west-bank|West Bank]], declaring "I am against annexation." The Israeli Security Cabinet had approved measures — praised by [[likud|Likud]] Energy Minister [[eli-cohen|Eli Cohen]] as establishing "de facto sovereignty" that rules out a Palestinian state — including shifting building authority in Hebron, facilitating settler land purchases, repealing restrictions on non-Muslim real estate transactions, and expanding Israeli enforcement into Palestinian-controlled areas. The article states these actions violate the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] and have drawn U.S. concern over regional stability. An Israeli source further indicated Netanyahu would tell Trump that Phase 2 of the October 2025 U.S.-brokered [[gaza|Gaza]] ceasefire — requiring [[hamas|Hamas]] disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawal — "is not moving," with Israel arguing a renewed military operation is needed to enforce demilitarisation.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames the trip as "Mr Bibi goes to Washington," the seventh in twelve months — roughly one trip every seven weeks — and at least the third made to "shill for war with Iran." He explains that the security cabinet measures were implemented via executive power rather than legislation, and allow Israelis to purchase tracts of land in Palestine and register them with the Israeli government rather than the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], placing the land under Israeli jurisdiction. He quotes Trump's remarks to Barak Ravid — "I am against annexation" and "We have enough things to think about now. We don't need to be dealing with the West Bank" — and suggests this is why Netanyahu planned to meet Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] in the morning before seeing Trump.

The commentary then advances a contested claim: that a newly released Israeli government report says Netanyahu knew of Hamas's plan to invade southern Israel as far back as early 2018, meaning he knew of the plan when he arranged for Hamas to receive tens of millions of dollars per month after the Palestinian Authority cut it off financially. On this reading Netanyahu appealed to [[qatar|Qatar]] on humanitarian grounds, received the money, and had his people walk it into Gaza to prominent families with known ties to Hamas — which, the author writes, would mean "Netanyahu knowingly funded [[october-7-attacks|October 7th]]." He adds that Netanyahu intends to tell Trump that the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]]'s Phase Two plan for Gaza will not happen and that the United States needs to start a war with Iran immediately.

## Rescission of the EPA endangerment finding

The Brief reports that the Trump administration is preparing to rescind the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]]'s 2009 [[endangerment-finding|endangerment finding]], the determination that greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide endanger public health and welfare, which provides the foundational basis under the [[clean-air-act|Clean Air Act]] for regulating emissions from vehicles, power plants, oil-and-gas facilities and other sources. The final rule was to be issued on Thursday following a White House ceremony with Trump and EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]], and follows a proposed rescission announced the previous July and an Inauguration Day executive order directing a review of the finding's legality.

Officials including Zeldin described the move as the largest act of deregulation in U.S. history, projecting over $1 trillion in regulatory cost savings — primarily through reduced vehicle compliance expenses averaging more than $2,400 per light-duty car, SUV or truck — and emphasising benefits for energy affordability, fossil fuel production, economic growth and national security. The rescission initially targets motor vehicle emissions standards, eliminating requirements to measure, report, certify and comply with greenhouse gas limits, while paving the way for future rollbacks of power plant and stationary-source rules. Environmental groups including [[earthjustice|Earthjustice]] and the [[environmental-defense-fund|Environmental Defense Fund]] condemned the action as a major assault on federal climate policy, warning of increased pollution, thousands of premature deaths, higher health and fuel costs and accelerated climate impacts, and pledged immediate court challenges based on settled science and Supreme Court precedent upholding the finding. Democratic-led states signalled opposition and potential independent emissions standards, while the fossil fuel industry welcomed the relief. Legal experts anticipate prolonged litigation during which some rules may remain unenforced.

Ashe in America files this under "Glimmers of a Golden Age," arguing that "environmentalism is the cult of Gaia" and has "nothing to do with the environment." She quotes her own November 27, 2024 Brief: "Windmills are beaching whales. Plastic bags replaced paper bags to save trees, but then became the nation's number one pollutant. Hydroelectric power destroys river ecosystems by adjusting river flows based on human demand. Environmentalists' solutions always end in worse environmental outcomes — but not before taxpayers have shelled out hundreds of millions of dollars. It's never about the environment. It's about money, power, and control." She updates the figure to "billions," describes the rescission as detonating the legal foundation of federal climate regulation enabled by [[massachusetts-v-epa|Massachusetts v. EPA]], and predicts that ending the mandates will remove compliance costs baked into power prices, fuel, freight and production, delivering reductions in input costs and consumer prices. She calls it "a structural reset on climate communism."

## Ex-Iranian commander's claim that Saudi Arabia has nuclear weapons

In an interview with RT aired on the Monday, former [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] commander [[hussein-kanani|Hussein Kanani]] claimed that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] currently possesses a nuclear bomb and related capabilities, asserting that the United States is fully aware and that both [[israel|Israel]] and the U.S. have been informed. The claim comes amid escalating U.S.-Iran tensions: U.S. airstrikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in June, anti-government protests in Iran during December and January that Tehran blamed on U.S. and Israeli instigation, and recent U.S. military deployments led by the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier group. Trump has demanded that Iran restrict uranium enrichment and its ballistic missile program, prompting Iran to announce missile upgrades and a shift toward an offensive military doctrine. Kanani warned that Iran could respond by targeting Israel with missiles or closing the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] — potentially disrupting oil flows to China and Russia — and described Washington's strategy as "playing a game of chicken." Iran maintains its program is peaceful, has enriched uranium to 60% purity since the U.S. withdrew from the 2015 deal and reimposed sanctions, and recently concluded indirect talks in Oman, with Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] stressing the need for calm dialogue without threats; the U.S. responded by announcing new sanctions.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry reads the story as "a narrative pincer set by the Muslims against Israel." He notes that Israel has nuclear weapons — knowledge he attributes to John F. Kennedy's efforts to shut down the program, which he suggests contributed to Kennedy's assassination — while Iran has diminished credibility in the West and Saudi Arabia has cultivated a reputation as a reliable partner, especially through the relationship between Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Trump. He argues nobody in the West is worried about Saudi nuclear weapons under MBS, whom he characterises as "a benevolent strongman" willing to move against corrupt relatives and to drag Saudi culture away from radical Islam, and suggests Saudi possession might make the region safer. Finding no real evidence for the claim and no reason for Iran to attack the one party that argued against kinetic action toward it, he hypothesises that the story is narrative bait aimed at provoking Netanyahu's Security Cabinet into publicly attacking Saudi Arabia and demanding verification — noting that Saudi Arabia has signed the relevant international treaties while Israel has not, despite allegedly possessing hundreds of warheads.

Speaking at a government meeting broadcast live on the presidential administration's YouTube channel, Petro said he had "avoided being killed" and described how the aircraft — carrying him and his children — was rerouted to fly for four hours over open sea before landing at an unplanned location. The report, originating from Buenos Aires and published by TASS on February 10, 2026, provided no further detail on perpetrators, investigation status or specific threats.
