# Throckmorton v. United States

*Throckmorton v. United States* (1878) is the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] decision cited by Badlands Media for the maxim that "fraud vitiates everything."[1]

## The holding as cited

The article states that in Throckmorton the Court affirmed the maxim that fraud vitiates everything, while noting the qualification in the opinion that "the maxim that fraud vitiates every proceeding must be taken, like other general maxims, to apply to cases where proof of fraud is admissible."[1]

## Application to the Federal Reserve argument

Badlands Media invokes the case as the legal "light at the end of the tunnel" in its argument that Americans were never informed that [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] is a private entity, nor that state citizenship and federal citizenship are legally distinct statuses under [[fourteenth-amendment|the 14th Amendment]].[1] Because the arrangement was, in the author's view, concealed rather than disclosed, the resulting corporate contract of indebtedness is characterized as fraudulent and therefore void in principle.[1]

The article draws a moral corollary from this: rather than blaming Americans for having "allowed" the arrangement, condemnation should be reserved for the con artist rather than the conned, since the public was never given a reasonable explanation of the monetary system or the educational resources needed to participate as informed parties.[1] Knowing that fraud invalidates everything, the author argues, is the starting point for formulating remedies, because the country never truly consented to let a corporation control its credit.[1] See [[natural-law]].
