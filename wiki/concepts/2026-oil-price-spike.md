# 2026 Oil Price Spike

The 2026 oil price spike is the surge in global crude prices above roughly $110 per barrel in the spring of 2026, driven by concerns about disruption to Middle East supply routes — including the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] — during the [[iran-war|Iran war]].[1] Global markets repriced for a prolonged conflict, with rising bond yields and a stronger US dollar accompanying the move in crude.[1]

## Economic transmission

Higher energy costs fed into inflation and fuel price increases worldwide, including reported fuel hikes in Pakistan.[1] In the United States, mortgage rates rose to about 6.46 percent as Treasury yields climbed on inflation concerns tied to the conflict, while jobless claims fell to roughly 202,000, indicating low layoffs despite broader economic pressure.[1] Economists cited risks to hiring and growth from the combination of higher oil prices and market volatility.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that second-order effects from the closure of the Strait of Hormuz were already hammering energy prices, consumer goods, global stocks, logistics chains and the economic agreements once sold as ironclad anchors of stability, while headlines insisted everyone was sinking together in a storm [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had set in motion.[1] On his reading of the data and the flows, the United States stood best positioned to weather — and ultimately weaponize — the shocks, while the [[collectorate|Collectorate]] was left exposed economically, geopolitically and kinetically.[1]

He argued that the Collectorate was being revealed not merely as a set of paper tigers with hollowed-out military posturing and dissolving joint statements, but as something no longer composed of coherent nation-states at all: vassal entities unable to accept the new masters of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] they once presumed to lord over, now that the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] architecture was being methodically dismantled and the US military-industrial complex sat firmly under Trump's command.[1] The result, in his framing, was a full-spectrum exposure of the fragility of a system marketed and mythologized as the opposite of weakness — an inversion of globalism's promise of collective strength, running from Covid-era supply chain revelations through the trade wars with China to the precision untangling of [[the-iranian-knot|the Iranian Knot]], all part of Trump's engineered [[kobayashi-maru|Kobayashi Maru]].[1] He described the EU bloc as collapsing in real time, with dominoes toppling from Berlin to London to Madrid under the weight of self-inflicted energy and defense dependence.[1]
