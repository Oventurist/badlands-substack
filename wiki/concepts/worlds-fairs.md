# World's Fairs

The nineteenth- and early twentieth-century **World's Fairs** are treated in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — Agents from Russia" as the strongest surviving evidence for a lost, advanced civilization popularly called [[tartaria|Tartaria]].[1]

## The 1893 Chicago World's Fair

The essay notes that mainstream history describes the 1893 Chicago World's Fair as the most photographed event of the 1800s. Congress passed the act authorizing the fair in 1891, leaving only a couple of years for construction of a 690-acre fairground filled with dozens of enormous, ornate buildings.[1] The author questions how such a scale of construction could have been achieved without cranes, motor vehicles, or other modern equipment, and rejects the official account that the entire site was built from "temporary building material."[1] Almost all of the buildings were subsequently demolished.[1]

The essay also reports that [[nikola-tesla|Tesla]] supplied the electricity that illuminated the Chicago fair, citing night-time photographs of the fully lit 690-acre site.[1]

## Other fairs

Similar fairs were held in Buffalo, New York in 1901 and in San Francisco in 1915 — the latter during the First World War — among many others. The author observes that every one of them carries the same cover story: rapid construction followed by rapid demolition.[1] In some cases buildings survived, such as the Palace of Fine Arts from the San Francisco fair, which the author argues was plainly not built from temporary material.[1]

## Architectural styles

The fairs shared a common architectural idiom, traces of which the author says can be found in almost every major city worldwide. Related styles include Gilded Age mansions, Beaux-Arts, and to a lesser degree Art Deco; the author asserts that most such buildings have since been destroyed.[1]

## Trump connection

The essay notes that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] owned a hotel and casino in Atlantic City named "Trump World's Fair," offering this as one of several suggested links between the Trump family and the lost-civilization thesis.[1]

## The fairs as manufactured history

The later installment "On Languages and Cover-ups" treats the fairs as an instance of history being created for mass consumption.[2] It notes that the heyday of the fairs coincided with the publication, between 1883 and 1890, of [[hubert-bancroft|Hubert Bancroft]]'s thirty-nine volume encyclopedia of history, and argues that the fairs introduced an entirely new self-concept to the masses complete with whole world histories to accompany it — that they were intended, literally, to define the world for people.[2] The author states he does not believe it a coincidence that the release of new world histories coincided with the fairs, while conceding he cannot prove the connection.[2]

Every fair, particularly in America, is said to have boasted of the progress of the nations of the day.[2] The essay observes that some fairs, such as the San Francisco fair of 1915, took place amid hostilities between nations yet still carried an international flavour, and suggests the hand of socialist secret societies may be visible in this, pointing readers to *Fire in the Minds of Men*, a history of revolutions and secret societies written by a former curator for the Library of Congress.[2]

## Psychological effect

The author asks readers to consider that most people alive during the fairs lived in colonial shacks and read by candlelight, and cites Encyclopaedia Britannica for the figure of roughly one million patrons attending the Chicago fair of 1893 alone.[2] He describes staging the fairs as the equivalent of a cultural atom bomb which obliterated the past and announced the arrival of "the future."[2] The underlying mechanism he proposes is hypnotic: temporarily suspending people's critical minds through shock, impact or broken expectations opens a window of opportunity in which suggestions can be made.[2]

## Trump's 2026 fair

Although the essay holds that the old fairs were staged by the cabal, it argues that as a communication they symbolise the emergence of a new era.[2] On this reading, [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s talk of holding a massive world fair in 2026 is meant to celebrate America's 250th birthday by ushering in a new era following the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] and the removal of the cabal.[2] The author notes that such communications must remain recognisable and decipherable to the cabal itself, and summarises the equation as "World Fairs = mass societal change" — held last time by the [[deep-state|Deep State]], and this time by Trump, Q and America.[2]
