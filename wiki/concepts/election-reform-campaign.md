# Election Reform Campaign

The **Election Reform Campaign** is a recurring subject of coverage in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] and its weekly recap program [[badlands-blitz|The Blitz]].[1]

## Coverage

Episode 6 of The Blitz, "The Info War Ouroboros" (February 14, 2026), treated the Election Reform Campaign as one endpoint of a wider discussion that also encompassed the never-ending Epstein saga.[1] The hosts approached both subjects through a shared set of metaphors — "Sand Bunkers, Squared Circles and Ouroborosian Rugs" — framing the information environment surrounding them as self-consuming and cyclical, an "info war ouroboros".[1] The article does not set out the campaign's specific legislative or organizational particulars beyond its status as an ongoing storyline in the outlet's reporting.[1]

## Congressional resistance (February 2026)

A February 2026 Politico report found that Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] were ignoring President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s calls for election reform, with Trump asking, "Why would you want [[mail-in-voting|mail-in ballots]] if you know it's corrupt? It's a corrupt system."[2] Several Republicans suggested the President's position went too far: Rep. [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] said he supports mail-in voting while objecting only to unsolicited mass mailing; Rep. [[cory-mills|Cory Mills]] urged caution but emphasized fixing registration; Rep. [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]] cited Florida's treatment of ballots as courtroom evidence; and Rep. [[derrick-van-orden|Derrick Van Orden]] framed Trump's concern as aimed at ineligible voters.[2] Election-related legislative reforms were passing the House but were expected to stall in the [[united-states-senate|Senate]].[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Republicans were "playing politics," and questioned why the coordinated cabinet-wide push began only in the midterm campaign season — suggesting DOGE data and tooling, immigration enforcement experience, or the discovery record from voter-roll lawsuits as possible prerequisites.[2] She judged meaningful reform unlikely in an election year and warned that "the most logical outcome is that we're getting another fake election," while noting [[john-solomon|John Solomon]]'s report that Trump intends to win over members of Congress directly, and referencing her discussion with [[canncon|CannCon]] on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]] of [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]]'s seven proposed laws.[2]
