# Sinking of the Lusitania

The **sinking of the RMS Lusitania** in 1915 is described in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as "an inside job" arranged by Anglo-American — "mostly Anglo" — forces, involving a ship carrying 1,700 people and 173 tons of explosives from the United States to Europe.[1]

The article situates the event within the struggle inside the United States over intervention in the First World War, where nationalists influenced by the non-interventionist tradition of [[george-washington|George Washington]] and [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]] fought to keep the country neutral.[1] Although it took two years of relentless propaganda, the sinking was decisive in fueling anti-German sentiment and winning American support for the war.[1] With the United States entering in 1917, the scales tipped in favor of the allies, the Austro-Hungarian empire was put down, the [[ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]] was dissolved, and imperialists eyed the carving up of the Russian empire after the destruction of the Romanov dynasty.[1]
