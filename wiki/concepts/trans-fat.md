# Trans Fat

Trans fat is an artificially produced fat that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] ranked among the most dangerous substances a person can consume.[1] The fiscal incentives for its use are numerous: chiefly, it greatly extends the shelf life of processed foods.[1]

## Sources and effects

Trans fat is found in deep-fried fast foods and in processed foods made with margarine or vegetable oils, as well as in chips, crackers, and most commercial baked goods.[1] Numerous studies are cited showing that trans fat raises LDL ("bad") cholesterol while lowering HDL ("good") cholesterol, and that it increases the risk of heart attack, heart disease, stroke, and inflammation.[1] Denmark forbids oils and fats on its market if they contain trans fatty acids in excess of 2 percent.[1]

## Hydrogenation and the saturated-fat distinction

The article argues that the link between trans fat and cardiovascular disease should not be ignored, pointing to multiple studies of Pacific Island populations who derive 30–60 percent of total caloric intake from fully saturated coconut oil and show nearly nonexistent rates of cardiovascular disease.[1] The stated conclusion is that not all saturated fats are equivalent: some occur naturally, while others are artificially manipulated into a saturated state through hydrogenation, a man-made process that adds hydrogen atoms to vegetable and seed oils while heating them.[1] The result is described as a rancid substance whose only real beneficiaries are shelf life and profit, with the article noting that essentially all experts now agree hydrogenation does nothing good for human health and actively causes harm.[1]
