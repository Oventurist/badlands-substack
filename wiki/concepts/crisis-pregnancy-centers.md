# Crisis Pregnancy Centers

**Crisis pregnancy centers** are organizations that provide pregnancy-related services without performing or referring for abortion.[1] In 2026 they became the subject of a significant [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] procedural ruling concerning their access to federal courts.[1]

## First Choice ruling

The Supreme Court ruled that [[first-choice-womens-resource-centers|First Choice Women's Resource Centers]], a [[new-jersey|New Jersey]] crisis pregnancy center, may proceed with a federal lawsuit challenging a state investigation, siding with the center's claim that its constitutional rights were implicated.[1] The case involved a subpoena issued by New Jersey Attorney General [[matthew-platkin|Matthew Platkin]] seeking donor and operational records; the center argued the demand could chill speech and association rights protected under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]

The Court did not rule on the underlying merits of the subpoena but determined the center can pursue its claims in federal court rather than being limited to state proceedings.[1] The decision is viewed as a procedural victory for crisis pregnancy centers and similar organizations, allowing broader access to federal courts when challenging state enforcement actions.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the opinion "flew under the radar" beside [[louisiana-v-callais|Louisiana v. Callais]] but was an important one, quoting the Court's statement that "an official demand for private donor information is enough to discourage reasonable individuals from associating with a group."[1] She argued that New Jersey had been harassing reproductive centers that decline to provide abortion, and that the ruling establishes such centers have the same rights to judicial review as their counterparts — part of a broader dismantling of the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]].[1]
