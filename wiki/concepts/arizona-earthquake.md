# Arizona Earthquake!

"Arizona Earthquake! Kari Lake Files an Explosive 70-Page Lawsuit" is a Badlands Media article published on December 12, 2022 by [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]]. It surveys the wave of post-certification election lawsuits filed by Republican and "America First" candidates in Arizona following the [[2022-united-states-midterm-elections|2022 midterm elections]], with primary attention to the gubernatorial election contest brought by [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]. The piece is written in an openly partisan, conversational register, and closes with the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors rather than the outlet itself.

## Certification and the filing window

The article begins from the procedural fact that the Arizona general election was officially certified on Monday, December 5, 2022, by then–Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]]. Gillespie stresses what she regards as the central absurdity of the situation: Hobbs, as the Democratic candidate for governor, certified her own election, which the author characterizes as "a total clown show over here in the wild, wild West." Under Arizona law, candidates had five days after certification to contest the results, making Friday, December 9 the effective deadline — a day the article describes as extremely busy.

## The Lake contest

On the afternoon of December 9, Lake filed what the article calls a "fiery" 70-page lawsuit in Maricopa County Superior Court alleging election fraud. Lake is the sole contestant and plaintiff. The named defendants are Katie Hobbs, both personally as contestee and in her official capacity as Secretary of State; [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]] as [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] Recorder; [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]], Clint Hickman, Jack Sellers, Thomas Galvin, and Steve Gallardo in their official capacities as members of the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]]; Scott Jarrett as county Director of Elections; and the Board of Supervisors itself.

## Liz Harrington's summary of the complaint

The bulk of the article reproduces a Twitter thread by Liz Harrington published the night the suit was filed, which the author presents as a highlight reel of the complaint's allegations.

The suit asserts that the number of illegal votes cast in Arizona's November 8, 2022 general election "far exceeds the 17,117 vote margin," and that witnesses show "hundreds of thousands of illegal ballots infected the election in Maricopa County." It frames its purpose as "restoring trust in the election process—a trust that Maricopa County election officials and Hobbs have shattered."

### Machine failures

A cyber expert who, according to the complaint, spent nine years performing security tests for the laboratory that certified Maricopa's machines — elsewhere described in Harrington's thread as work for the Election Assistance Commission — is quoted as concluding that the "machine failures Arizona voters experienced on Election Day could not have occurred absent intentional misconduct," and that "an unintentional widespread failure of this magnitude occurring could not arise absent intentional misconduct." The complaint estimates conservatively that between 15,603 and 29,257 Republican voters were disenfranchised by the machines.

The suit alleges that 59% of Maricopa County's 223 vote centers experienced printer and tabulator failures, disproportionately affecting Election Day voters, who broke for Lake by roughly 3:1. Texts from technicians hired by the county are quoted: "I'm having a 911"; "Tabulators aren't reading ballots. It's maybe 50/50"; "... line around building all day." Against the Board of Supervisors' claim that tabulation problems were fixed by mid-afternoon, the complaint states that no fewer than 34 vote centers had tabulator breakdowns after 3 p.m. and throughout the day, and that the Board tried to conceal how many vote centers had waits exceeding 90 minutes.

The human cost is illustrated with two examples: a poll worker who testified that roughly 175 voters at his vote center gave up because of long lines, and a 68-year-old woman unable to vote for the first time since 1981 because she could not stand in a two-to-three-hour line. The account cited to @Peoples_Pundit reports Republicans encountering difficulty casting a ballot at a rate of 58.6% versus 15.5% for Democrats.

### Signature verification

The thread describes signature matching as "a joke." A 2020 review of 230,339 envelopes is said to have found 18,000 "egregious" mismatches — more than the entire 10,457-vote margin — and 19,631 that failed standards. In 2022, the complaint alleges, 4,328 of the same names behind the egregious mismatches "voted" again, as did 5,289 names associated with failed standards.

Whistleblower testimony quoted in the suit alleges that level-2 managers were "reversing and approving signatures" that had been rejected, that "most of the work of these level 2 managers was not subject to the accountability of observers," and that rejected ballots were re-run through processing because managers wanted them approved. One witness stated: "There was no way to know who placed 'verified' stickers on ballots. The system was wide open to abuse." A third-party contractor identified as the Star Center is alleged to have cured ballots "completely off site" with no observers present, and without being "able to see the actual ballot with the signature on it."

### Chain of custody and Runbeck

The complaint alleges that over 300,000 mail-in ballots had no chain of custody, with a whistleblower from [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] specifying 298,942 early ballots and stating that Maricopa County did not know how many ballots Runbeck received on election night — which the suit says is against the law. It flatly asserts that "the required chain of custody for these ballots does not exist."

Observers are quoted describing maladministration: "The ballots inside the containers were not counted"; "Ballots delivered without any required documentation"; "Temporary employees moving unsecured metal carts full of ballots without any security or monitoring." The complaint concludes that "the entire ballot transfer process provides opportunities for legal ballots to be lost or illegal ballots to be added." A Runbeck employee is said to have reported that employees were unlawfully permitted to add family members' ballots into batches with zero chain of custody, leaving "no way to tell the number of ballots illegally injected into [the] 2022 election," and that Runbeck can print duplicate ballots at will without traceability.

The suit further alleges that uncounted "Door 3" ballots were co-mingled with already tabulated ballots, and that "there is good reason to believe that the number of Door 3 ballots is far greater than the 16,724 that Maricopa County claims." It also states that Maricopa County added 25,000 ballots to its totals after Election Day, a point the author glosses sarcastically as making the slow count explicable.

### Censorship and conflict of interest

Harrington's thread asserts that Hobbs and Richer "colluded with the federal government to censor Americans and infringe on their First Amendment rights," noting that Hobbs complained about a private Facebook post stating that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won. The complaint also emphasizes that Hobbs oversaw an election in which she was, by the article's characterization, down by 10 points, and that the county recorder administering it had founded a political action committee whose stated mission was to stop Lake and [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]]. The suit's summation, as quoted, is that "the maladministration and illegal votes in Maricopa County caused the State of Arizona to wrongfully name Hobbs as the candidate who received the most votes in the election of Governor of Arizona" — which Harrington condenses to "KARI WON."

## Commentary and the judiciary

The article notes that Michael Patrick Leahy, CEO and editor-in-chief of Star News Digital Media — publisher of *The Arizona Sun Times* — gave an overview of the suit and of the judge assigned to hear it on Steve Bannon's *War Room*, in an interview posted to Rumble. Gillespie offers her own view that "if there's one thing in America that is more evil than most of the politicians, it's most of the judiciary," and that Arizona's judiciary is no exception.

## The attorney general race

The article turns to [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]], the Republican candidate for attorney general, who with the Republican National Committee had filed suit shortly after the general election only to have it dismissed as premature, since Arizona results cannot be contested before certification. Hamadeh refiled after December 5. His race was simultaneously undergoing an automatic recount: "At 511 votes out of 2.5 million, our race is the closest statewide race in Arizona history, it is currently undergoing a recount. Every legal vote deserves to be counted," he said. As reported by *The Hill*, Democrat [[kris-mayes|Kris Mayes]] led by 511 votes at certification, within the 0.5-point margin that triggers an automatic recount under Arizona law; recounts for the attorney general race, the state superintendent race, and a state House race began that Wednesday.

## The Finchem–Zink suit

Mark Finchem, the America First candidate for [[arizona-secretary-of-state|Secretary of State]], joined with [[jeff-zink|Jeff Zink]], the America First candidate in Arizona's Congressional District 3, to file their own suit on the same Friday afternoon. As reported by *Arizona's Family*, the suit names Finchem's Democratic opponent [[adrian-fontes|Adrian Fontes]], Zink's opponent Rep. [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]], and Katie Hobbs. It alleges that Hobbs abused her power by failing to have tabulation machines properly certified and by threatening the boards of supervisors in Mohave and Cochise counties with criminal charges if they did not certify the election; that she should have recused herself as Secretary of State while running for governor; and that her office abused its power when it flagged misinformation on a Twitter account in January 2021.

## Blake Masters

Gillespie flags as notable that she could find no indication that Blake Masters, the Trump-endorsed America First candidate for U.S. Senate, filed any lawsuit by the contest deadline. She speculates whether "someone" told him not to file, or whether he was simply exhausted by a year of campaigning, observing that he had said nothing publicly during the week the other suits landed.

## The author's forecast

In closing, Gillespie speculates that Hamadeh will prevail in the recount and that installing an "America First 'infiltrator'" as the state's top law enforcement official inside the Hobbs administration would produce dramatic results. She describes Finchem as a committed patriot who she believes will eventually take his place as Secretary of State, first in the line of succession to a Lake governorship — contingent on Lake winning her suit. Her expectation for the Lake case itself is that the "corrupt judiciary strikes it down at every turn" until it reaches the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], where, she argues, precedent could be set to overturn both the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020]] and 2022 results up and down the ballot. She ends with an aside that Taiwan had just announced $40.3 billion in semiconductor investment in Arizona, which she credits to Republican rather than Democratic dealmaking, and an exhortation to buy a popcorn maker.
