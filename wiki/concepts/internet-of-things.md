# Internet of Things

The "Internet of Things" (IoT) refers broadly to sensors and other networked technologies that digitize the physical world.[1] It is the foundation of concepts such as [[smart-cities|smart cities]] and instrumented highways, and it is the reason [[operational-technology|operational technology]] has become a distinct and urgent category of cyber risk: connecting physical process control to networks creates entirely new classes of vulnerability.[1]

## Relevance to water infrastructure

The November 2023 compromise at Pennsylvania's [[aliquippa-municipal-authority|Aliquippa Municipal Authority]] occurred through a compromised device in the field — a remotely controlled unit monitoring and regulating water pressure at a pumping station — rather than through a corporate network breach, illustrating the IoT attack surface directly.[1]

At the February 6, 2024 [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Homeland Security Committee]] hearing on the water sector, [[marty-edwards|Marty Edwards]] of [[tenable|Tenable]], whose title is Deputy CTO for OT and IoT, warned that identical devices are deployed across water, electrical, and manufacturing facilities, so a single ransomware campaign could render an enormous fleet inoperable with no supply chain capacity to replace them quickly.[1] The Badlands account observed that the hardware — computers, networking gear, and IoT sensors — shares supply chains across the energy, banking, and communications sectors, and is often foreign made by U.S. adversaries such as China.[1]
