# Gulf War

The **Gulf War** was the conflict in which a US-led coalition removed Iraq's military from Kuwait. US troops represented 73% of the coalition's 956,600 troops in Iraq.[1] Many coalition countries were reluctant to commit military forces; some felt the war was an internal Arab affair or did not want to increase US influence in the Middle East.[1] In the end many governments were persuaded by Iraq's belligerence towards other Arab states, offers of economic aid or debt forgiveness, and threats to withhold aid.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that while [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was creating the wars of the Middle East he simultaneously painted himself as the region's savior, "coercing" a coalition of 42 countries into joining him.[1] In this reading the "great coalition" was assembled through bribery — economic aid and debt forgiveness funded by taxpayers — and through threats to withhold existing aid, so that Bush's portrayal as a great statesman rallying the world against the next "Hitler" masked a transaction.[1]

Lange finds it especially telling that Bush offered debt forgiveness as an inducement, because one of the reasons [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] invaded Kuwait was Kuwait's refusal to forgive Iraqi debt incurred fighting the [[iran-iraq-war|war against Iran]], a conflict that had threatened both Kuwait and Saudi Arabia.[1] Kuwait and Saudi Arabia had lent Iraq large sums, and that war debt was crushing the Iraqi economy.[1]

He further alleges that Bush's father helped install Saddam Hussein as dictator in Iraq, supported him in the war against Iran, helped build up his military before the invasion of Kuwait, and then did nothing to deter that invasion — concluding that "Bush wanted a war with Iraq."[1] Lange asserts that Bush and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] funded both sides of the Iran–Iraq war, costing both countries hundreds of thousands of lives and turning both into debt slaves.[1] Within the broader Badlands framework, war is described as profitable for the cabal but impoverishing for the countries involved, generating generational debt slaves, with the Middle East purposely turned into a war zone for the purpose of money laundering.[1]

## Relation to the Iraq War

The article links the Gulf War to the later [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] invasion of Iraq, quoting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at a 2016 CBS News debate in South Carolina accusing the former president of lying to enter the war: "They lied! They said there were weapons of mass destruction and there were none."[1] Trump called the Iraq War a "big fat mistake" and said, "We should have never been in Iraq. We have destabilized the Middle East."[1] Lange treats these remarks as evidence that Trump already knew the Bush family bore responsibility for destabilizing the region.[1]


## As a distraction from BCCI

"Profiting From War" advances the claim that [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] deliberately started the Gulf War as a distraction from the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scandal, after congressional and criminal investigations had begun and the [[bush-family|Bush family]] had become vulnerable to exposure.[2] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that Bush wanted [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] to invade [[kuwait|Kuwait]] because the invasion would destroy evidence of the family's Kuwaiti dealings while shifting the public narrative to war.[2] Reconstruction afterwards is said to have enriched [[halliburton]] and the [[carlyle-group|Carlyle Group]].[2] Quoting ABC's Ted Koppel in a June 1992 *Nightline* broadcast after the war, the article notes the assessment that Bush, operating largely behind the scenes through the 1980s, initiated and supported much of the financing, intelligence and military help that built Saddam's Iraq into the aggressive power the United States ultimately had to destroy.[2]
