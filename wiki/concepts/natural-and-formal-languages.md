# Natural and Formal Languages

The distinction between natural and formal languages is the interpretive framework used by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] in Badlands Media legal commentary to argue that legal terminology must be read with exacting precision rather than colloquial looseness.[1]

## The two forms

A **natural language**, in the article's account, is a conventional language that arises organically among people as they communicate; within it, words, symbols, and phrases may carry multiple meanings depending on context, convention, and connotation, and may even take on contradictory senses.[1] A **formal language** is one in which words and symbols carry only a single specific meaning; computer code is offered as the archetype, and formalization is described as the process of disambiguating every term in a source language until no ambiguity remains, converting the natural source into a derivative formalized language.[1]

Both are held to be necessary. Natural language is described as easier on the brain and the precondition of poetry, while medicine, engineering, and law are said to require the highest feasible degree of formalization because they demand reliably exact transmission of meaning.[1] The article states that justice in particular "requires we go the extra length to speak in unambiguous formalisms when determining whether someone has broken a law or not," and that if courtroom meanings shifted with the wind the courts would be rendered useless.[1]

## Legalese as a quasi-formal language

The article concludes that legalese, though technically a natural language, has been formalized to the point of functioning as a quasi-formal language in its own right.[1] This premise licenses the piece's method: judicial phrases such as the equation of "state" and "nation" in [[cherokee-nation-v-georgia|Cherokee Nation v. Georgia]], and dictionary entries in [[blacks-law-dictionary|Black's Law Dictionary]], are treated as precise technical statements rather than casual usage.[1]

## Deliberate scrambling of natural language

The author further alleges that social engineers intentionally scramble natural languages to produce confusion and suffering in the masses.[1] The cited example is the phrase [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]], which the article argues was engineered so that a single statement of dissent could be read either as denying the intrinsic value of Black lives or as rejecting the activism conducted under the banner — an ambiguity the author asserts was created on purpose.[1] The same framing produces the article's coinage for coordinated propaganda, "the organized murder of the truth," described as a condition that can be reversed because, unlike physical murder, truth can be brought back to life when individuals learn and speak it.[1]

## Diagnostic metaphor

Closing the argument, the article compares societal communication to intercellular communication in the body: if cells cannot communicate properly the organism perishes, and likewise restoring America is said to depend first on correctly diagnosing what ails it.[1] The research is framed as vital work for [[digital-soldiers|Digital Soldiers]].[1]
