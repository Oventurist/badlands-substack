# Sunni Islam

Sunni Islam is the majority branch of Islam, comprising roughly 85–90% of all Muslims.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Doctrine of succession

Sunnis hold that the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]] did not appoint an heir and intended the Muslim community to choose from among themselves the leader most capable of uniting the people under the sacred hadiths — the guidance given by Muhammad on how to live a pious life.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) This contrasts with the [[shia-islam|Shia]] position that only descendants of [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] may lead.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The elective principle was embodied in the [[rashidun-caliphate|Rashidun Caliphate]] beginning with [[abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Sunnis also take an expansive reading of "Ahl al-Bayt" (People of the House of the Prophet), extending it to all members of the Banu Hashim, whereas the Shia limit it to Muhammad's cousin, daughter, son-in-law, two grandsons, and eleven wives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Geography

[[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] is considered the leader of the Islamic world and is home to Sunni Muslims along with Jordan and Syria — the three nations comprising the area the [[hashemites|Hashemites]] considered the true Arabia.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Afghanistan and Iraq are mixed, conditions the article attributes to deliberate arrangement by the British Crown and East India Company at the end of the First World War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Although most historical caliphs were sharifs or Banu Hashim, the modern "throne" of the Muslim world is held by neither, resting instead with the [[house-of-saud|House of Saud]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
