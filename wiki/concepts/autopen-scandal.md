# Autopen Scandal

The "autopen scandal" is the term used across the Badlands corpus for the contention that executive orders, pardons, and other instruments issued in [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s name during his presidency were signed by autopen or otherwise executed without his knowing authorization, and therefore represent an unconstitutional exercise of presidential power by unnamed staff.

## The argument

The claim rests on two linked premises. The first is that Biden was cognitively incapable of exercising the office — a premise that Badlands writers argue was effectively conceded by Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], whose report described Biden as a "well-meaning elderly man with a poor memory" in explaining the decision not to charge him over classified documents. The second is that a large volume of consequential acts, including pardons for members of Biden's own family, issued under his signature during that period.

If both are accepted, the conclusion drawn in the corpus is that some other set of actors was directing the presidency. As [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] put it in the July 22, 2026 [[badlands-brief-1ae|Badlands Brief]], "the dementia-ridden scrotus puppet is not the primary perp in that. The people running him are." She characterizes the affair as "massively seditious and treasonous."

## Relationship to the Hur recordings litigation

The corpus treats the release of the Hur investigation audio as materially advancing the autopen case, because recordings of Biden speaking privately with ghostwriter [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]] in 2016–2017 are expected to document the trajectory of his condition. The [[dc-circuit-court-of-appeals|D.C. Circuit]]'s denial of Biden's emergency bid to block that release is accordingly read as "a step change in getting us to justice," and is folded into the broader corpus framing that "we can't have a golden age without a justice phase."

## Contested status

The scandal as described is an interpretive framework advanced by Badlands writers rather than an adjudicated set of findings. It sits alongside related corpus themes concerning [[pardongate|pardongate]] and the [[deep-state|deep state]], and its central factual question — who actually authorized the documents — remains, in the corpus's own telling, the object of a hoped-for future accountability process.
