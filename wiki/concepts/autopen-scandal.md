# Autopen Scandal

The "autopen scandal" is the Badlands designation for the contention that executive orders and pardons issued in [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s name during his presidency — including pardons for members of his family — were signed by autopen and authorized by unnamed handlers rather than by a cognitively competent president.[1]

The framework rests on Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s characterization of Biden as a "well-meaning elderly man with a poor memory," which [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treats as evidence that Biden was unfit to exercise presidential authority at all.[1] The argument follows that the documents he "signed" cannot be attributed to him, and that the culpable parties are "the people running him" rather than what she calls "the dementia-ridden scrotus puppet."[1] She frames the conduct as "massively seditious and treasonous."[1]

The [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-dc-circuit|D.C. Circuit]]'s July 2026 refusal to block release of the [[mark-zwonitzer|Zwonitzer]] recordings was read within Badlands as "a step change in getting us to justice" on the autopen question — the position being that there can be no golden age without a justice phase, and that the justice phase is closer than previously thought possible.[1]

## Biden's July 2025 admission

In a phone interview reported on July 13, 2025, Biden escalated his response to Republican claims that he might not have been in control of high-profile clemency decisions issued under his name at the end of his term, and to the broader charge that his cognitive state impaired his functioning in office.[2] "I made every decision," Biden said, asserting that he had directed his staff to use an autopen replicating his signature on the clemency warrants because "we're talking about a whole lot of people."[2] Asked what evidence the White House had that Biden did not authorize the clemency actions, spokesman Harrison Fields said that Biden "should not be trusted," adding that "the truth will come out about who was, in fact, running the country."[2]

Badlands commentary paired the admission with reporting on [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]]'s reported willingness to testify before [[congress|Congress]], treating both as facets of [[state-capture|State Capture]]: the argument that even with free and fair elections, a captured deep state "will just use the Autopen to implement their will."[2] The disclosure that Biden used the autopen to pardon deep state figures was read as a signal that something significant was about to happen, particularly amid the concurrent [[epstein-files|Epstein files]] controversy.[2]

## Trump's renewed attacks (September 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] renewed his criticism of former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and his administration over the use of an autopen to sign important orders, including pardons, during Biden's tenure in the White House.[3] Trump had railed against the practice for months, claiming that thousands of pardons Biden signed were void and that the former president did not know what documents he was signing through the automated device.[3]

Speaking to reporters on Thursday, 18 September 2025 during a trip to the United Kingdom, Trump said: "It was illegally used. He never gave the orders... He never told them what to do. And I guess the only one he signed, or one of the few he signed, was the pardon for his son."[3] A spokesperson for Biden did not immediately respond to a request for comment.[3] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry summarized Trump's framing as an argument that Biden did not actually support open borders and did not know what was being done in his name, but that a group of people were operating behind the scenes.[3]

## Trump's March 2025 declaration that Biden's pardons are void

On the night of Sunday, March 16, 2025, President Donald Trump wrote on social media that he no longer considered valid the pardons his predecessor granted to members of the bipartisan House committee that investigated the January 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol, along with a range of other people Trump regards as political enemies, because they had been signed using an autopen device.[4]

Legal observers noted that there is no power in the Constitution or case law to undo a pardon, and no exception for pardons signed by autopen.[4] The New York Times characterized Trump's assertion as embracing a baseless right-wing conspiracy theory about former President Joseph R. Biden Jr. and as an escalation of his rhetoric.[4] The use of an autopen was described as an ordinarily uncontroversial aspect of governance; it was apparently first used to sign a bill into law at a president's direction in 2011, when Barack Obama was traveling in Europe and wished to sign legislation extending the Patriot Act by four years.[4] After the post, a reporter in the traveling press pool aboard Air Force One asked Trump to elaborate, and he appeared to briefly back away from the idea.[4]

Badlands commentary rejected the autopen framing as the operative issue, arguing instead that the pardons were void because Joe Biden was never a real or legitimate president, which would imply that the primaries, campaigns, and elections were likewise illegitimate and that the country has been under some form of military government.[4] Contributor Chris Paul counted 107 days between the filing of the pardon for [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and Trump's declaration that the "Biden" pardons were null and void, describing anyone who believed the pardons legitimate in that interval as stuck 107 days in an "Informational Past," and the broader public as stuck 1,518 days behind relative to everything the government has done since the inauguration.[4]

## Mass cancellation of autopen executive orders (November 2025)

On Friday, 28 November 2025, President Donald Trump announced that he was canceling every executive order enacted under Joe Biden that Biden had not personally signed.[5] "Any document signed by Sleepy Joe Biden with the Autopen, which was approximately 92% of them, is hereby terminated, and of no further force or effect," Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform, adding that "the Autopen is not allowed to be used if approval is not specifically given by the President of the United States."[5] He asserted that "the Radical Left Lunatics circling Biden around the beautiful Resolute Desk in the Oval Office took the Presidency away from him," that the people who operated the autopen "did so illegally," and that if Biden claimed to have been involved in the autopen process "he will be brought up on charges of perjury."[5]

The announcement followed a joke Trump made before pardoning the turkeys Gobble and Waddle on Tuesday of that week, when he said his predecessor's turkey pardons were invalid because they had been executed by autopen.[5] Because Trump had separately revoked the executive orders Biden did personally sign, the combined effect was that no presidential action from Biden remained in force.[5]

Badlands commentary treated the development as most significant for what it might reopen, with Jon Herold singling out the abruptly shuttered [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] investigation as a door that should be immediately reopened.[5] Herold nonetheless maintained that the autopen is a red herring: in his framing Biden's actions were illegitimate not because of the signing device but because he did not win the 2020 election.[5] Burning Bright read the rescission alongside the administration's hardline immigration posture as evidence that Trump was appearing weak when strong, in response to an increasingly bold contingent of critics.[5] Brad Zerbo wrote that "the illegitimate Biden Presidency has now been erased," and that while the pain it caused remained, any attempt to make the theft of the 2020 election permanent had ended.[5]

## Trump remarks, July 2025

In mid-July 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] again criticized [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over autopen use after the former president defended himself in a rare media interview amid escalating congressional investigations into his administration.[6] Trump repeated that Biden's autopen use was "one of the biggest scandals" in "50 to 100 years" because his predecessor "knew nothing about what he was signing," adding that "it's not supposed to be for signing major legislation."[6] Speaking to reporters at the White House alongside NATO Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]], he said of the congressional inquiries, "They're going to figure it out, and we'll see what happens."[6]

Trump reiterated his claim that Biden aides were "freewheeling" around the former president, and that it was they rather than Biden who favored "open borders" and "transgender for everyone."[6] "I think the radical Left people that took — they took over the White House," he said. "If I didn't win, our country was finished."[6]

Badlands commentary from [[canncon|CannCon]] characterized the exchange as "one of the more bizarre 'weaves'" from Trump, meaningful only in light of an early second-term detail, and issued a correction that the item in question had been "refinished" rather than "checked for bugs," while acknowledging prior speculation to the contrary.[6]

## Heritage Oversight Project findings and the Bailey demand (March 2025)

The controversy first entered mainstream coverage in March 2025, when reporting established that many executive orders signed by former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had been approved with an autopen device, prompting questions from detractors about whether Biden was fully mindful of what he was signing.[7] The [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]]'s Oversight Project first found the circumstances surrounding the autopen use.[7] Those findings led Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] to challenge the legitimacy of the 82-year-old former president's executive orders given concerns about his cognitive decline in recent years, saying: "I am demanding the DOJ investigate whether President Biden's cognitive decline allowed unelected staff to push through radical policy without his knowing approval."[7]

Autopen signatures are created by machine rather than by hand, and many presidents going back over half a century are believed to have relied on the modern autopen.[7] Heritage investigators said that to establish whether Biden actually ordered the signature of the relevant legal documents — or possessed the mental capacity to do so — they would need to learn who controlled the autopen and what checks were in place.[7] The story was reported by The Washington Times, which said it had reached out to a representative for Biden.[7]

Badlands contributor Jon Herold connected the reporting to an account Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] had relayed of Biden denying that he had signed an executive order pausing LNG exports to Europe.[7]

## Power the Future review of climate executive orders (May 2025)

In late May 2025 the pro-energy nonprofit [[power-the-future|Power the Future]] extended the autopen dispute into energy policy, renewing its call for an investigation into more than half a dozen Biden climate-related executive actions that it argued should be deemed null and void.[8] The group reviewed eight executive orders it described as significant shifts in domestic energy policy and said it found no evidence that Biden had ever spoken publicly about any of them, raising the concern that they were signed by autopen without his knowledge.[8]

The orders cited included a 2023 Arctic drilling ban, a 2021 order committing the federal government to net-zero emissions by 2050, an order mandating "clean energy" artificial-intelligence data centers, and an offshore drilling ban issued shortly before Biden left office in 2025.[8] Founder and executive director [[daniel-turner|Daniel Turner]] told Fox News Digital that "these are not obscure bureaucratic memos; these were foundational shifts in American energy policy, yet not once did Joe Biden speak about them publicly."[8] Badlands contributor Chris Paul framed the finding as further evidence "chipping away till we reach the point that Biden's 'presidency' never happened."[8]

## Pardon Attorney probe and autopen subpoenas

In May 2025, newly appointed Pardon Attorney [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]] confirmed that pardons issued by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] at the end of his term were being investigated, pledging that "the integrity of the American Pardon system requires that we examine the Biden pardons and who did what."[9] House Oversight Committee Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said the probe would include autopen subpoenas, stating that investigators had found that "not just with the pardons he issued, but many of the executive orders that the courts are now using to Trump-proof" administration policy "were all signed with the autopen."[9]

## National Archives documents on pardon signings (September 2025)

In early September 2025, [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] of Just the News reported on newly produced documents that he said exposed Biden's excessive use of the autopen.[10] Solomon said that "for the first time, we're seeing documents produced by the National Archives that show that Biden White House lawyers believed that Joe Biden had an obligation to personally sign pardons," and that Biden "may have designated decisions for things like legislation and pardons to other people," with no record of him being in the room when certain items were signed.[10]

Solomon characterized the material as "bombshell documents" that even surprised him regarding what was being discussed behind the scenes at the Biden White House, all of it dug out of the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]].[10] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the relentless pace of such disclosures and flagged the report as due to drop that same day.[10]
