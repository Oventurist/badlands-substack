# All Assets Deployed

"All Assets Deployed," subtitled "Separating the MAGA Wheat from the Chaff," is an opinion essay published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on November 16, 2022 and written by the pseudonymous contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. Recording 515 likes on the newsletter, the piece was composed in the immediate aftermath of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement of his candidacy for the 2024 presidential election and responds to what the author describes as a demoralized mood within the [[maga|MAGA]] and [[america-first|America First]] community following the [[2022-united-states-midterm-elections|2022 midterm elections]].

## Context and argument

The article opens by noting that this is the second time in as many weeks that the author has written during "contentious times" in the movement. It asserts that in the preceding week "many of us feel that we witnessed yet another highly-public and utterly obvious voter fraud campaign" that deprived Americans of favored candidates who might have stemmed what the author calls the political and administrative bleeding produced by the occupation of the White House by the Biden administration under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].

Referring back to an earlier piece on the "Narrative Mastery of one Donald J. Trump," published at Burning Bright, the author describes a baited and then actualized "turn" against the heart of the America First movement by "Con Inc." and a portion of the alternative right — specifically, the author argues, that portion which conveniently retained substantial influence and engagement on "the most notorious Globalist censorious platforms" during the height of Big Tech's crackdown on dissenting thought. The essay's central contention is that this turn by conservative personalities and would-be thought leaders is "not only to be expected, but to be welcomed," because it offers an opportunity to draw a clear line between authentic America First politics and those who would "wear it like a skin suit" in order to ride a more controllable candidate into power. The author writes that such an opportunity should be taken "without hesitation. Every time."

## The expectation game

The author explicitly declines to dismiss the frustration felt by readers, saying he does not consider negative emotions "without merit," and admits he would rather be celebrating "a crushing Red Wave that soundly overwhelmed [their] systems of fraud." Nevertheless he maintains that nothing witnessed in the previous 24 hours, or the previous week, has fundamentally altered the trajectory of the movement. The emotional problem, in his framing, is "the expectation game rearing its ugly head" — a serpent that has bitten everyone, and which should be worn not as a mark of shame but as "the scars of perceptive, engaged and discerning minds."

Anticipating accusations of goalpost-moving or "hopium" — what some in the community call the "Doomer's Refrain" — the essay argues that the movement is engaged in "the most complex Fifth-Generation Psychological and Information War ever waged," both against and for the American people. Because much is necessarily obscured, it stands to reason that "assets" in every sense of the word will be activated when the enemy believes it can strike at a MAGA base it perceives as fracturing. The author insists that "MAGA only fractures if we allow it to," invoking the maxim that sunlight is the best disinfectant and that "exposure and disclosure must come before justice."

## Reading the Mar-a-Lago announcement

Invoking [[sun-tzu|Sun Tzu]] — whom the author describes Trump as "a professed and ardent student of" — the essay argues that one must sometimes appear weak when strong and then "fall like a thunderbolt" when the enemy least expects it. It asks rhetorically whether readers truly expected Trump to conduct an audit of the 2022 midterms on stage at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] before a partisan audience already branded as election-denying extremists by the Media Industrial Complex, or to announce the imminent arrest of the sitting president.

The author states that the purpose of the announcement was, as he had predicted, twofold: to separate the MAGA and America First "wheat" from the [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] controlled-opposition "chaff," and simultaneously to undercut any political move being contemplated by other influential figures gathering conservative energy in an attempt to sweep the base out from under Trump's leadership. The announcement was, he writes, projected by [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and other close Trump allies to "light the media world on fire," which is exactly what it did — not because it was unexpected, but because Con Inc. and the "DeSantis over Trump" crowd, referring to supporters of [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], would smell blood in the water and try to turn hairline fractures into chasms, converting the movement into "an archipelago of intra-faction wars" while enemies drew up the next electoral maps.

In a war named for information itself, the author argues, "knowing who is whom and what is what IS the war itself," and he predicts that when November 2022 concludes the game board — however chaotic — will resolve as significantly less obscured than before.

## The speech itself

The essay concedes that the Mar-a-Lago speech contained no "massive BOOMs" but identifies "a few tantalizing and encouraging tidbits." It quotes Trump saying "The voting will be much, much different in 2024," delivered to a somber audience after he warned that things will get "much worse [before people] see clearly" — a statement the author says aligns closely with the "precipice" theories he and others have advanced for several years.

Trump's emphasis on the practical purpose of the 2024 vote may seem like small consolation given the fraud the author believes occurred in 2020 and 2022, but if the country is in a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] plan, he argues, part of the reason must be to provoke an awakening among "the reachable middle" of American society. Trump, in this reading, suggested that such minds were not yet ready to turn fully on the Globalist masters they believe have their best interests at heart, or on "the System of Systems" used to subjugate rather than lead.

## Systems to be exposed

The article lists the structures it believes must come down before a more sovereign future is possible: the fiat central banking system led by "the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] criminal empire," the international cabal of Globalist planners, and the election systems now so prominent in the American zeitgeist. While the movement licks its wounds, the author contends, the planned exposure of these systems continues before an "increasingly-weary and increasingly-wary citizenry," whose growing discomfort can only produce growing awareness of the collective enemy.

## Devolution

Addressing critics who claim that Trump's 2024 candidacy concedes 2020 or disproves [[devolution|Devolution]], the author recommends a re-reading of [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s Devolution series, published under the Patel Patriot byline, with a specific framework in mind: "Devolution is not meant to take our country BACK from those who have subverted it; it is meant to protect red-line National Security functions so that we HAVE a country to take back when this painful, entirely necessary exposure operation ... plays out … however long it takes."

For this reason, the essay argues, exposure cannot occur with Trump and America First candidates in primary positions of public power, or the failure of the systems would be blamed on them and, by extension, on the movement. The transition from "[their] systems to ours" will involve pain and chaos, and the poor optics of that pain will continue to build "while the puppet and his puppeteers occupy the public positions of Administrative power for all the world to see."

The author recalls that Herold, himself, and several others at Badlands Media argued before the midterms — "firmly and, at the time, without popularity" — that an actualized Red Wave might undo the exposure operation by putting the movement in front of a runaway train car, "ready to take the blame like a miscast Judas." He nevertheless counts real wins amid the fraud, including the projected reclaiming of the House by Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] and "a potentially-interesting deadlock in the Senate," where he says a few key Democratic votes "never seem to quite go the way the powers that would be intend," while acknowledging that the movement has not yet seen the actualized wins many dream about.

## Closing appeal

The essay's closing appeal is to "pay attention to those who scream the loudest while guarding your mind from the very same," and to remember that if ordinary supporters can see "these jackals and crows," so can their chosen sovereigns and representatives, even those not yet installed in public office. Focusing on the enemy's gloating or on a push that fell short of expectations, the author warns, risks missing the ground actually taken and the new battlements erected farther into enemy territory: "For every bit of ground we take will never be ceded again."

The piece ends with the author's customary sign-off — "stay Positive, stay Based and most importantly … stay Bright" — followed by the standard Badlands Media disclaimer that articles represent the opinions of contributing authors and not necessarily the views of Badlands Media itself, and a pointer to the author's own newsletter, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], which focuses on narrative deployments and macro concepts in [[5th-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]].
