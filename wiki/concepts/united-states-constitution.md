# United States Constitution

The **United States Constitution** is the founding legal charter of the American republic. Within the Badlands Media corpus it is treated less as a procedural document than as a defensive instrument — a set of restraints written by the founders against the government they were creating.

## As framed in "America's Greatest Threat"

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s April 24, 2024 essay America's Greatest Threat makes the Constitution the pivot of its argument. According to the source, the founders understood that the gravest danger to the new nation lay not in invading armies or pirates but in the American government itself, and so "created the Constitution" for that purpose. The essay's formulation is that the Constitution "doesn't protect Americans from foreign adversaries directly; it protects Americans from an American Government that is infiltrated by said enemies." Because two oceans already shielded the United States geographically, the safeguard that mattered had to be internal.

The article describes the rights the Constitution protects as pre-political — "given to every man, woman and child by God above" — and enumerates them as the right to be heard, to protect oneself and one's family, and the baseline goods of "life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness." These are characterised as inalienable rights protected "not from outsiders directly, but from conspiring Americans within."

## Amendment as usurpation

The same essay treats constitutional amendment as the mechanism by which the original restraints were undone. It asserts that the founders "specifically stated in the Constitution that a federal income tax was unconstitutional," and that the charter had to be amended and ratified on February 3, 1913 to permit one — the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]], which granted [[congress|Congress]] the power to tax incomes without apportionment by population. Carlson stresses the proximity of this ratification to the founding of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] and insists the coincidence is not accidental.

A related claim concerns the structure of officeholding: the article argues that government positions "were never meant to be full-time jobs" and that career politicians would have been "appalling to America's founding fathers," who envisaged a federal government resembling modern local government, staffed largely by part-time citizens with other occupations.

## Erosion and restoration

Carlson argues that the government has persuaded Americans "that the Constitution should be abolished, free speech is bad, and the right to bear arms makes one less safe," and that during the [[covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 pandemic]] ordinary citizens concluded "that other Americans didn't deserve their Constitutional Rights." His prescription is correspondingly constitutional: nothing will change "until the Constitution is upheld and respected and is in authority over the American Government," until criminals are removed from power, and until government is reduced to a body that represents and works for the people.
