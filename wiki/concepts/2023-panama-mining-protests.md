# 2023 Panama Mining Protests

The 2023 Panama mining protests were a nationwide wave of demonstrations against a long-term copper mining concession granted by the Panamanian government to a local subsidiary of the Canadian company [[first-quantum-minerals|First Quantum Minerals]].[1]

## Course of the protests

Associated Press reporting from October 30, 2023 described protests extending into a second week, with a broad cross-section of Panamanian society demonstrating across the country and demanding that the government rescind the contract.[1] Critics argued the concession endangered Panama's environment and water supply.[1] The administration of President [[laurentino-cortizo|Laurentino Cortizo]] responded by proposing legislation to schedule a December referendum on scrapping the deal, though the national electoral authority said such a vote could not take place before the presidential election the following May.[1] A referendum date of December 17 was later reported.[1]

## The highway shooting

On November 8, 2023, [[kenneth-darlington|Kenneth Darlington]], a 77-year-old retired American lawyer with dual US and Panamanian citizenship, shot and killed two protesters who were blocking a highway.[1] Video of the killings circulated widely and was framed in much coverage as an outburst against "climate change activists," a description that omitted the anti-mining substance of the blockade.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used the episode as a case study in media framing, arguing that the presentation of the shooting concealed the underlying dispute over the mining contract.[1] He speculated that the killings were intended to intimidate Panamanian voters ahead of the referendum, and questioned whether the video was released to encourage deeper scrutiny of mining corruption or, alternatively, to depict opponents of climate activism as violent — an effort he suggested may have backfired.[1] These are the author's speculations and not established findings.[1]
