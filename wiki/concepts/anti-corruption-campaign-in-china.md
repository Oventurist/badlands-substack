# Anti-Corruption Campaign in China

The anti-corruption campaign in China refers to the sustained, multi-year crackdown on graft among officials of the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] conducted under [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], described in reporting cited by Badlands Media as a sweeping campaign that has punished more than one million officials.[1]

## Scale

Free Malaysia Today, quoted in "The Master Builder," reported that more than one million officials had been punished in Xi's crackdown, while noting that critics argue the campaign has also served as a means for Xi to purge political rivals.[1] Figures from China's [[central-commission-for-discipline-inspection|Central Commission for Discipline Inspection]] cited by ABC News Australia showed that more than 500,000 officials and party members were punished for corrupt behavior in 2019 alone, and that corrupt officials had increasingly fled China for Western countries as the crackdown intensified.[1]

## Prominent banking cases

Two financial cases feature prominently in the Badlands account. [[hu-huaibang|Hu Huaibang]], former party secretary and chairman of the [[china-development-bank|China Development Bank]], pleaded guilty in 2020 to illegally receiving 85.5 million yuan between 2009 and 2019.[1] [[lai-xiaomin|Lai Xiaomin]], chairman and internal party chief of [[china-huarong-asset-management|China Huarong Asset Management]], confessed to taking more than 200 million yuan in cash bribes that he stored in apartment cabinets, in a confession broadcast on state television.[1]

## The Hu Jintao removal

The article points to the public removal of [[hu-jintao|Hu Jintao]] from the stage during the [[20th-national-congress-of-the-chinese-communist-party|20th National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party]] as a dramatic public expression of the same campaign, citing Foreign Policy's observation that during Hu's tenure corruption rose — as did public coverage of corruption, online freedom of speech, and, to a limited extent, civil society groups and NGOs.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands Media commentary

Joe Lange argues that the campaign shows Xi battling his own version of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] inside China, and connects the NGO dimension to [[george-soros|George Soros]], suggesting that Soros's criticism of Xi reflects the arrest of officials tied to his network of nongovernmental organizations.[1] The campaign is thereby folded into the article's thesis of a Trump–Xi–Putin coalition against the global central-bank system.[1]
