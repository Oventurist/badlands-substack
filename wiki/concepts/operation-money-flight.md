# Operation Money Flight

Operation Money Flight is the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] investigation into the laundering of funds embezzled from Venezuela's state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]]. It is described in [[badlands-brief-314|the Badlands Brief of February 4, 2026]] as the operation that arrested investment banker [[mathias-krull|Mathias Krull]] in July 2018 after determining that he had laundered $1.2 billion in PDVSA funds.

## Connection to the Maduro lawsuit

The Brief's commentary by [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues that although investigations into PDVSA's stolen funds had been under way for years, the RICO lawsuit filed by [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] against the oil industry in March 2018 — described as infamous but relatively unknown — supplied a trove of evidence that "created a map" leading to multiple raids in both the United States and Europe, including the Krull arrest.

## Claimed link to Credit Suisse's collapse

The central claim advanced in the source is that [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]]'s insolvency was most likely triggered by the massive asset forfeiture conducted through Operation Money Flight. Supporting this, the Brief cites Argentine reporting that PDVSA-related accounts used to hide embezzled money were held at Credit Suisse and were all opened between 2004 and 2015 — the same window cited as the corruption timeline in the Maduro lawsuit, despite that filing naming no bank. Former PDVSA official [[nervis-villalobos|Nervis Villalobos]], arrested in Spain in 2018, is named among the account holders, and [[occrp|OCCRP]]'s 2022 Suisse Secrets reporting is cited for further detail. The collapse ultimately forced the Swiss government-brokered acquisition of Credit Suisse by [[ubs|UBS]] in 2023.
