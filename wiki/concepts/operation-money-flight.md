# Operation Money Flight

Operation Money Flight is a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] money-laundering investigation targeting funds embezzled from Venezuela's state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]].[1] The operation arrested investment banker [[mathias-krull|Mathias Krull]] in July 2018 after determining he had laundered $1.2 billion in embezzled PDVSA funds; Krull later became a cooperating witness for the DOJ in exchange for clemency on a ten-year prison sentence.[1]

Badlands analysis argues that the massive asset forfeiture conducted under Operation Money Flight most likely triggered the insolvency of [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]], which was subsequently forced by the Swiss government into acquisition by [[ubs|UBS]] in 2023.[1] Investigations into PDVSA's stolen funds had been underway for years, but the evidence trove provided by [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]'s March 2018 RICO lawsuit against the oil industry is credited with creating a map that led to multiple raids in both the United States and Europe, including the Krull arrest.[1]

Reporting cited by Badlands states that PDVSA-related accounts at Credit Suisse — used to hide embezzled and laundered money — were all opened between 2004 and 2015, the same corruption timeline cited in the Maduro lawsuit, although the lawsuit itself named no banking institution.[1] Former PDVSA official Nervis Villalobos, arrested in Spain in 2018, is among the figures named in that reporting.[1]


## Origins in the Krull cooperation

The Badlands Brief describes Operation Money Flight as beginning with the cooperation of [[matthias-krull|Matthias Krull]], the German-national banker arrested in Miami and charged with laundering $1.2 billion stolen from [[pdvsa|PDVSA]].[2] The operation was spearheaded by the Southern District of Florida's International Narcotics and Money Laundering Section in cooperation with law enforcement in London, Rome, Madrid, and Malta, with the mission "to identify, investigate and prosecute high-level members of drug trafficking enterprises, bringing together the combined expertise and unique abilities of federal, state and local law enforcement."[2]

The operation is characterized as the effort that connected the white-collar cartel of oil, energy, and minerals interests to the traditional cartel trafficking drugs, guns, and humans, with Krull as the key to unlocking the case.[2] Krull mapped the financial network, helped secure plea deals from two other defendants, and generated information that led Swiss regulators to sanction [[julius-baer|Julius Baer]]; his ten-year sentence was reduced to three and a half years, described as the largest such reduction ever granted by a U.S. federal judge.[2] His information also provided the roadmap for the layered sanctions [[donald-trump|President Trump]] imposed on Venezuela.[2]
