# Golden Crescent

The Golden Crescent is the opium-producing region centered on [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]], Iran and [[pakistan|Pakistan]]. Badlands Media identifies it, together with the [[golden-triangle|Golden Triangle]] in Southeast Asia, as one of the two theaters in which the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] cultivated drug proxies.[1]

## Scale

According to United Nations figures cited in the article, in 2003 the Golden Triangle and the Golden Crescent together accounted for 91 percent of the area devoted to illicit opium production and 95 percent of the estimated product in metric tons. Adding Colombia and Mexico — two other countries where the CIA has worked with drug traffickers — the four areas accounted for 96.6 percent of the growing area and 97.8 percent of estimated product.[1]

## Growth after 1979

Afghan opium production is described as highly responsive to U.S. operations in the area. It rose from 200 metric tons in 1980 — the first full year of U.S. support for the drug-trafficking mujahideen commander [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]] — to 1,980 metric tons in 1991, when both the United States and the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]] agreed to terminate their aid. After 1979, Afghan opium and heroin entered the world market significantly for the first time, rising from roughly zero to 60 percent of U.S. consumption by 1980. In Pakistan there were hardly any drug addicts in 1979; the number had risen to over 800,000 by 1992.[1]

## Financial architecture

Drug trafficking sat at the heart of the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] bank in Pakistan, which handled money laundered by corrupt Pakistani military officers who simultaneously administered the CIA's arms pipeline to the Afghan rebels while supplying much of the heroin that inundated the United States and Europe during the 1980s.[1] Historian [[alfred-mccoy|Alfred McCoy]] is quoted stating that BCCI facilitated Pakistani heroin money reaching $4 billion by 1989, more than the country's legal exports.[1]
