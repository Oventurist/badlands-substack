# Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act

The Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act is a bipartisan bill that could result in a ban of the video-sharing app [[tiktok|TikTok]] in the United States on national security grounds.[1]

## Provisions

Should it become law, the act would grant the president the authority to force the divestiture of social media companies operating in the United States that are more than 20 percent controlled by one of four covered nations or their related entities: China, Iran, North Korea, and Russia.[1] Foremost among the affected companies, and named explicitly in the bill, is TikTok, which is owned by the China-based firm [[bytedance|ByteDance]].[1]

## Legislative progress and Biden's position

The House Committee on Energy and Commerce unanimously approved the measure on a 50–0 vote on March 7, 2024.[1] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] told reporters outside Air Force One that he would sign the bill if [[congress|Congress]] approved it, saying, "If they pass it, I'll sign it."[1]

## Rationale and controversy

Security experts had long warned that TikTok is a weaponized application that could be used to promote [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] propaganda or feed Americans' data directly to the regime.[1] TikTok officials previously acknowledged suppressing and "heating" content at the regime's request but claimed the practice had ended, while the U.S. government investigated ByteDance for allegedly using TikTok geolocation data to stalk and harass U.S. journalists reporting on the company's CCP links.[1]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] observed that Biden had been overtly using TikTok as a campaign platform and TikTok users as a voter demographic, making a flip-flop plausible; alternatively, he suggested the effort could serve as a legal pretext for shutting down social media companies deemed dangerous by the administration, comparing the dynamic to the pretext logic of the Department of Defense's Operation Northwoods memorandum.[1]
