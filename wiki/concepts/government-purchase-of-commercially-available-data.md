# Government Purchase of Commercially Available Data

Government purchase of commercially available information refers to the practice by which United States intelligence and law enforcement agencies acquire detailed personal data about Americans from third-party commercial data brokers rather than through warrants or statutory collection authorities.[1]

## The 2023 ODNI disclosure

The practice became publicly documented in June 2023 when a declassified report prepared for Director of National Intelligence [[avril-haines|Avril Haines]] was reported by [[wired|Wired]] and amplified by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]].[1] The report, written by Haines's own panel of senior advisers after she tasked them in late 2021 with untangling secretive business arrangements between data brokers and intelligence agencies, described soberly and at length the size and scope of a government effort to accumulate data revealing the minute details of Americans' lives.[1] Advisers had informed Haines more than a year before publication that the government was secretly amassing a "large amount" of "sensitive and intimate information" on its own citizens.[1]

## Legal critique

Privacy advocates called the arrangement a "nightmare scenario."[1] [[sean-vitka|Sean Vitka]], an attorney with [[demand-progress|Demand Progress]], said the report revealed "what we feared most" and that intelligence agencies were "flouting the law and buying information about Americans that Congress and the Supreme Court have made clear the government should not have."[1] The central mechanism identified was the use of "craven interpretations of aging laws" to bypass privacy rights, at a time when prosecutors had increasingly ignored limits traditionally imposed on domestic surveillance.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the disclosure as consistent with the historical character of intelligence agencies as a shadowy enterprise operating between official policy and criminal activity under a "by any means necessary" ethos.[1] He argued that the intelligence community functions as the criminal arm of a globalist control apparatus and that governments exist expressly to secure the rights of citizens, so no agency can claim a right to violate that covenant as a primary function.[1]

Deschamps further speculated that the aggregation serves a systems-calibration purpose for an eventual global social credit system tied to central bank digital currencies, with machine-learning capacities supplying controllers subtle tools to manipulate society and to assign and refine initial citizen scores.[1] He closed by framing the matter as a question of whether the public would continue to tolerate institutions that violate its trust without recourse, and predicted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would act as the tip of a grassroots spear against the problem.[1]
