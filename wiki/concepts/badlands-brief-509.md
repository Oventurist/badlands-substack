# Badlands Brief: Election Exposure, Energy Angst & War Rackets

"Election Exposure, Energy Angst & War Rackets" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published on March 10, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The Brief is described by its publishers as "your daily drop of Badlands' takes on the narratives dominating the info war," pairing summarized news reports with commentary from named Badlands contributors. This edition carries five news segments plus a bonus item, with commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. It recorded 129 likes at the time of archiving.

## FBI investigation into Maricopa County elections

The lead segment reports that the [[fbi|FBI]] has obtained election-related records tied to [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona, as part of a federal criminal investigation into election administration spanning 2020 through 2024. Citing [[just-the-news|Just the News]], the Brief states that the records were obtained through a grand jury subpoena seeking "terabytes of data" and other materials connected to voting systems and election procedures. The scope of the subpoena and the specific materials obtained had not been publicly disclosed.

According to the source, the investigation follows concerns raised by [[house-administration-committee|House Administration Committee]] observers during the 2024 election, who inspected a third-party ballot processing facility used by the county. A memo cited in the report states that observers found completed mail ballots and blank ballots stored in the same room at [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]], a private contractor hired by Maricopa County to process ballots. The observers reported that no state government officials were present while ballots were being handled, and that some pallets of blank ballots appeared to have torn wrapping with ballots missing or removed at the facility.

The Brief notes that Rep. [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]] (R-AZ) had previously asked the [[department-of-justice|US Department of Justice]] to investigate Runbeck and the handling of ballots during the 2024 election, and that the Arizona action forms part of an ongoing federal investigation into election issues in multiple states. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] shared the Just the News report on his [[truth-social|Truth Social]] account, amplifying it with the single word "Great!!!"

In commentary, Ashe in America recalls that in January she and [[canncon|CannCon]] hosted former Maricopa County Clerk and Recorder [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]] on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]] (episode 156). Although that conversation was constrained to [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia, Richer at one point downplayed Runbeck's role in the county's 2022 election — "that was the 'drawer three' election," a reference to the [[door-3-ballots|Door 3 ballots]] controversy.

Her central argument concerns what she calls the essence of public–private partnerships in elections: "plausible deniability via ever-moving goal posts." She observes that Runbeck is treated as so specialized that jurisdictions waive competitive bidding requirements to contract with it — citing El Paso County, Colorado, as an example of county-level contract and funding approvals — yet in other contexts, such as efforts to verify ballot chain of custody or obtain loading dock footage, it is characterized as so unspecialized that it is "just like the Staples or the UPS store," as in Maricopa County. She insists "it can't be both": a vendor cannot simultaneously merit a bid waiver for its specialization and escape governmental transparency obligations for being ordinary. That elusive transparency, she writes, "is now the substance of a federal investigation."

Ashe in America also points readers to a timeline she compiled in 2021 cataloguing the occasions on which Maricopa County obstructed justice, overlaid with audit findings and other notable moments during the Maricopa audit, along with a 2021 report on the audit. She concludes that "this conduct is now being investigated. We're closer than we've ever been. Georgia… Arizona… who's next?" and asks readers whether Richer will return to the show.

## Energy shock and oil markets

The third segment reports that Iran warned escalating Middle East conflict could drive global oil prices to $200 per barrel as fighting and attacks on energy infrastructure threaten regional supplies. An [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] spokesman said continued US and Israeli actions against Iran could trigger retaliation affecting oil production and economic infrastructure across the Middle East.

Shipping through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] — a corridor carrying roughly 20% of the world's oil supply — has been severely curtailed, creating fears of a large-scale supply shock; analysts estimate shipments have fallen sharply, removing a significant share of crude exports from the market. Brent crude briefly climbed above $119 per barrel, with traders warning that further escalation could remove large volumes of supply and push prices substantially higher.

The Brief reports that the United States issued a temporary waiver allowing [[india|India]] to purchase [[russia|Russian]] oil cargoes, aiming to prevent shortages and reduce pressure on global energy markets during the crisis. Indian officials responded that the waiver does not determine the country's energy policy, stating that India "has never depended on permission" from any country to buy Russian oil and will continue purchasing crude where available to meet domestic demand.

Burning Bright's commentary contends that "an emergent macro narrative is rising to compete with—and in my estimation, subsume—the engineered war story out of Tehran": energy, its weaponization on the global stage, and the way the crisis is accelerating "the germination phase of the [[nuclear-renaissance|Nuclear Renaissance]] that's been seeded for years." While headlines scream regime change optics and forever-war escalation, he argues, the real story is the deliberate targeting of energy infrastructure — Israeli strikes on Iranian oil facilities, IRGC responses hitting Gulf assets, production shut-ins, and a near-collapse of tanker traffic through Hormuz.

He directs attention to who is being hit and who is being shielded. The "so-called Allies," embodied by Israel and the NATO/European axis, are both escalating the crisis through attacks on Iranian reserves and chokepoints and least equipped to weather the fallout. Europe, still reeling from the engineered loss of Russian pipeline flows and what he calls its suicidal nuclear phase-outs — something [[germany|Germany]] now openly admits was a "severe strategic mistake" — remains over 50% import-dependent, with [[france|France]]'s nuclear-heavy fleet "the lonely exception proving the rule," while Israel "sits inside the same volatile region it is helping destabilize." These, he writes, "are the actors playing checkers with a chessboard on fire."

By contrast, he describes the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] nations as insulated: Russia continues flowing oil to India, its largest buyer, under the US-issued waiver Washington carved out "specifically to keep energy moving to the 'right places'"; India has answered perceived pressure with "uncharacteristic sovereign defiance"; and [[china|China]]-linked carriers transit the narrowest stretches of Hormuz without incident. He terms Russia, China and India the "Sovereign Trinity," with Iran as economic facilitator, "sparring on the narrative level while cooperating on the Actual: rerouting supplies, asserting energy sovereignty and insulating their populations from the very shocks the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]] (Neocons, Globalists and Warmongers) needs to maintain control."

The engineered energy crisis, on his account, exposes both the fragility of fossil fuel dependence and "the suicidal folly of the [[green-new-deal|Green Agenda]]," while disclosing and accelerating the energy transition in "a feedback loop of Mandate Cultivation and Actualization." What rises above $200 oil warnings, Hormuz chokepoints and European gas panics is "the unassailable truth of nuclear power," with baseload reliability immune to geopolitics and weather, fuel stockpiled for years rather than reliant on daily tanker runs, and capacity to meet exploding demand from AI, data centers, manufacturing and electrification "without the scarcity programming the enemy has spent decades embedding into the collective psyche." The Iranian chaos will be disruptive in the short term, he concludes, but it grants Trump an even stronger public mandate to sharpen the Nuclear Renaissance divergence he sent into overdrive starting in 2025 — from executive orders accelerating [[nuclear-regulatory-commission|NRC]] modernization and advanced reactor deployment to the quadrupling of domestic capacity and the embedding of civil nuclear energy into the AI prosperity framework.

## Turkey and Iran dispute over missile incident

The fourth segment reports that Turkish officials said the country's air defenses shot down a second missile that Ankara said had been fired from Iran, intercepted near [[turkey|Türkiye]]'s eastern border. Iran denied launching any attacks against Türkiye or nearby countries, rejecting reports that missiles had been fired toward Turkish territory, [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] or Greek [[cyprus|Cyprus]], with Iranian officials stating that Tehran had not carried out strikes against those locations. Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] said Ankara had warned Iran that Türkiye could defend itself against any threat and that its military has the capacity to repel potential threats. In a related item, Erdoğan accused Israel of taking advantage of shifting global attention to sabotage efforts toward a two-state solution in the [[israel-palestine-conflict|Israeli–Palestinian conflict]], adding that regional developments were increasing pressure across the Middle East.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's second commentary argues that "stabilizing the Middle East with a real and lasting security architecture is the only way to achieve world peace, and thus, the Golden Age." A few years ago, he writes, nobody thought a stable Middle East possible given bad blood, tribal politics, ethnic divisions and religious differences — until Mohammed bin Salman "changed the game," striking "the right balance of heavy-handed justice, congeniality, and forward thinking to pull the region out of its backward orthodoxy and into the modern age." Over the past decade MBS has resurrected the diplomatic practice of bilateral agreements, invested in his own people and the region, and reformed or expelled radical religious elements of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi]] society, going "from uniting the [[bedouin|Bedouin]] tribesmen of the desert to uniting the nation-states of the Muslim Ummah, a task that has never been accomplished purely through diplomacy."

What makes the fragile architecture work, he argues, "is not sunshine and rainbows and 'love for thy brother'" but "a matrix of incentive structures that have been carefully layered so that Arab monarchies and Turkmen democracies, among other ethnic and political systems, can find common ground and negotiate mutually beneficial outcomes" — self-interest, in short. That work now faces its first real stress test. Fortunately, he writes, MBS is not alone: other strongmen share a vision for their people and a common friendship with Trump. Erdoğan "has made it clear that he will not be tricked into a conflict with his neighbors in Iran," and Pakistani Prime Minister [[shehbaz-sharif|Shehbaz Sharif]] has come out in support of Iran despite historical differences and a deep, tearfully expressed love for Trump.

The commentary enumerates a wider roster of regional leaders: [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah]] of [[jordan|Jordan]], President [[joseph-aoun|Aoun]] of [[lebanon|Lebanon]], President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Al Sharaa]] of [[syria|Syria]], [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] of Palestine, [[ilham-aliyev|Ilham Aliyev]] of Azerbaijan, [[hamad-bin-isa-al-khalifa|King Hamad]] of [[bahrain|Bahrain]], General [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Al-Sisi]] of [[egypt|Egypt]], [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]] of the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]], Sultan Haitham bin Tariq of Oman, and Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim Al Thani]] of [[qatar|Qatar]]. ## Afghanistan designation and ISIS prosecutions

The fifth segment reports that on March 9, 2026, US Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] announced that the United States had designated Afghanistan as a "State Sponsor of Wrongful Detention," in response to the Taliban's detention of US nationals and other foreign citizens on grounds considered unjustified or politically motivated. The Brief explains that the designation is a [[state-department|State Department]] classification identifying governments that detain foreign nationals to gain diplomatic leverage or concessions, and that it allows the US government to increase diplomatic pressure and coordinate efforts to secure detainees' release. Afghanistan's designation follows other countries labeled under the same framework.

In a related item, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] announced terrorism-related law enforcement action, charging two [[isis|ISIS]] supporters with attempting to detonate explosive devices during protests. Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pamela Bondi]] said, "This was an alleged ISIS-inspired act of terrorism that could have killed American citizens… We will not allow ISIS's poisonous, anti-American ideology to threaten this nation—our law enforcement officers will remain vigilant, as they were when these devices were brought to a protest." Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] said, "These men allegedly sought to inflict mass casualties in service to ISIS with the hope of exceeding the carnage of the Boston Marathon bombing," thanking the officers who "ran into harm's way to apprehend these individuals and disarm the explosives before anyone was harmed" and pledging prosecution "to the fullest extent." FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] said the defendants "allegedly support ISIS and tried to follow the path of that deadly group by attempting to detonate explosive devices in a crowd," adding that "the FBI and our partners have no tolerance for terrorist organizations or those inspired by them" and commending the brave NYPD officers "who took action to prevent injuries or loss of life on the streets of New York."

Ashe in America's commentary connects the day's news to the [[world-economic-forum|WEF]]'s prediction of a coming "Polycrisis" discussed at the beginning of 2023, remarking that "the globalists always [try to] follow through." She writes that Trump seems to be "playing whack-a-mole with emerging war narratives" but that, despite Lindsey Graham and his donors' best efforts, "the President always seems at least a few steps — or years — ahead." Declaring that she hates violence and that differences should be negotiated in the modern age — "the President is a deal maker first" — she observes that "it's all war narratives lately. War narratives are weapons in an [[information-war|information war]]." She closes with the maxim that "war is a racket" and answers her own question of how to roll up a racket with a single word: "Accelerate," invoking the corpus theme of [[accelerationism|accelerationism]].
