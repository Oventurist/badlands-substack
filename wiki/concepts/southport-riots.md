# Southport Riots

The Southport riots were episodes of civil disorder that broke out in the English town of Southport at the end of July 2024, as the town reeled from a knife attack that killed three children.[1] British media described the disorder as far-right rioting.[1]

## Police response and arrests

More than 50 police officers were injured and five men arrested in connection with the unrest.[1] Merseyside chief constable Serena Kennedy said 53 officers were hurt — 49 from Merseyside Police and four from Lancashire — along with three police dogs named Zoe, Ike and Quga.[1] Kennedy said the rioters were present "purely for hooliganism and thuggery," estimated that 200 to 300 people had been involved, and indicated further arrests would follow.[1]

Those arrested included a 31-year-old man from St Helens, a 31-year-old man from West Derby in Liverpool, and a 39-year-old man from Southport, all held on suspicion of violent disorder.[1] Jordan Davies, 32, of Southport, was charged with possession of an offensive weapon.[1] A fifth arrest, of a 39-year-old man, followed a report of a domestic incident in Southport involving threats to a male family member; the suspect was taken to hospital with a head injury, recognized by officers from footage of the Tuesday disorder in which he allegedly threw rocks at police, and was also reported to have racially abused a patient at the hospital.[1]

Special powers known as section 60 and section 34 orders were put in place, giving officers authority to stop and search individuals and to direct people engaging in antisocial behaviour away from the area.[1]

## Starmer's "standing army" of police

On Monday, August 5, 2024, Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] said a "standing army" of specialist police would be established to deal with rioting, and that the justice system would be scaled up to handle hundreds of arrests after a week of violent disorder across British cities.[2] Starmer convened an urgent meeting and blamed the lawlessness on "far-right thuggery" driven in part by misinformation on social media that inflamed anger over the stabbing rampage at a dance class that killed three girls and wounded ten people; false rumours that the suspect was a Muslim asylum-seeker led to attacks on immigrants and mosques.[2] "Whatever the apparent motivation, this is not protest. It is pure violence and we will not tolerate attacks on mosques or our Muslim communities," he said, promising that "the full force of the law will be visited on all those who are identified as having taken part."[2] On the preceding Sunday, mobs attacked two hotels housing asylum-seekers, breaking windows and setting fires before police dispersed them and residents were evacuated, and dozens of officers were hospitalised over six days after being struck with bricks, bottles and wooden posts.[2]
