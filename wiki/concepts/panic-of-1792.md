# Panic of 1792

The Panic of 1792 is presented by Badlands Media as the first American banker bailout and as a direct historical template for modern financial crises.[1] In the account given by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], the panic occurred roughly six months after the formation of the [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|First Bank of the United States]], which international bankers — having previously used Britain and other nations as proxies for commercial conquest schemes — managed to install as an offshore central bank in the United States by December 1791.[1]

## Mechanics of the crisis

The panic was said to be fueled by a 1791 scheme involving the sale of shares in the new central bank.[1] A collapse in the share price required Treasury Secretary [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] to contract with the Bank of New York to purchase a sizable portion of the shares, an intervention that upset the market.[1] Through securities manipulation of a kind the article compares to modern hedge fund practice, prices rose sharply again in December 1791, then crashed a second time, triggering a bank run.[1] The newly created central bank is said to have deepened the crisis by further restricting credit.[1]

## Comparison to Silicon Valley Bank

Deschamps concluded that the 1791–1792 events were "a carbon copy" of the [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] collapse of early 2023, arguing that the same core problem — a rigged financial and [[legal-system|legal system]] operated for the benefit of an occulted elite — has plagued freedom-seeking Americans from the founding era to the present.[1] The episode is placed in the essay's broader argument that populist revolutions have repeatedly been captured, with elites regaining control shortly after the people appear to win.[1]
