# 2024 CBS 60 Minutes Harris Interview Edit

The 2024 CBS *60 Minutes* Harris interview edit refers to the controversy over [[cbs-news|CBS News]]'s editing of a pre-recorded [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] interview with Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] during the closing weeks of the 2024 presidential campaign.[1]

## The controversy

Edited and unedited portions of the interview were circulated side by side, and critics argued that the comparison made clear that CBS had reworked Harris's answers in order to make her appear less incompetent.[1] Badlands Media characterized the episode as a scandal that was "massive because of its deception," arguing that extensive editing of a presidential candidate's pre-recorded answers in an obvious effort to rehabilitate her responses was antithetical to journalistic integrity.[1]

## FCC complaint

In October 2024 the [[center-for-american-rights|Center for American Rights]] filed a formal complaint with the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] charging that the network had engaged in "significant and intentional news distortion."[1] CAR argued the discrepancies violated FCC rules governing broadcasters' public interest obligations and sought to force CBS to release an unedited transcript.[1] CAR president Daniel Suhr said the matter went beyond "one interview or one network" and concerned "the public's trust in the media on critical issues of national security and international relations during one of the most consequential elections of our time."[1]

Fox News reported on how CBS could face regulatory jeopardy, with comment from one of the FCC's Republican commissioners.[1] Badlands' analysis noted that the FCC rules by committee — five commissioners appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate — and that the balance stood at 3–2 Democrat, with [[jessica-rosenworcel|Jessica Rosenworcel]] as chair alongside Geoffrey Starks and Anna Gomez, against Republicans [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] and Nathan Simington.[1] On that basis the outlet expressed skepticism that the complaint would advance beyond rhetoric, at least until after January 20, 2025.[1]

## Audience context

Badlands placed the controversy against declining mainstream news viewership, noting that per Ad Week the CBS interview was only Harris's third most watched media appearance, drawing 5.7 million viewers.[1] The ranking of her appearances (live plus same-day figures) was: Fox with Bret Baier at 7.8 million; CNN with Dana Bash, alongside Tim Walz, at 6.3 million; CBS *60 Minutes* at 5.7 million; ABC's *The View* at 3.1 million; CBS's *The Late Show with Stephen Colbert* at 2.9 million; and MSNBC with Stephanie Ruhle at 1.8 million.[1] Citing Forbes, the brief added that in 2023 not one cable network maintained a primetime average audience above two million viewers.[1]
