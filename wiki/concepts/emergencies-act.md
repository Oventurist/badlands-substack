# Emergencies Act

The Emergencies Act is the Canadian federal statute granting the government extraordinary temporary powers during a declared national emergency. Its invocation by the government of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] in February 2022 against the [[freedom-convoy|Freedom Convoy]] became the most contested use of executive power in recent Canadian history.[1]

## Use against the convoy

Under the act, authorities cleared the Ottawa protests and blockades and froze the bank accounts of participants and donors. Plaintiff Vincent Gircys, a police veteran of 32 years, had his account frozen for more than a week.[1] The financial measures extended to the regulation of crowdfunding platforms and cryptocurrencies under terrorist-financing authorities.[1]

## Mosley ruling

On January 23, 2024, Federal Court Justice Richard Mosley ruled the invocation unlawful, finding that it did "not bear the hallmarks of reasonableness—justification, transparency and intelligibility—and was not justified in relation to the relevant factual and legal constraints that were required to be taken into consideration."[1] The ruling reversed the practical effect of the earlier commission led by Liberal Justice Paul Rouleau, which had concluded the invocation was justified — a commission Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] described as highly politicized.[1]

Convoy participants responded by preparing lawsuits against the federal government, banks, and police.[1] Badlands commentary held that the dropped charges against two of the [[coutts-four|Coutts 4]] — allegations of plotting to kill an RCMP officer — undercut one of the narratives originally used to justify invoking the act.[1]

## Statutory threshold and appeal

Central to the Federal Court's reasoning was the statutory definition of a threat to national security: Mosley accepted that the 2022 protests harmed Canada's economy, trade and commerce, but held that such harm did not meet the threshold the act requires.[2] The court agreed with civil liberties groups that the Liberal government had gone beyond its powers and violated the Charter in invoking the act in February 2022.[2] Deputy Prime Minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] announced the same day that the government disagreed with the ruling and would appeal.[2]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that convoy organizers had been "quickly declared enemies of the state," with assets frozen, characters assassinated and livelihoods destroyed for peacefully protesting government policy and seeking redress of grievances, and that a Canadian judge had now ruled that treatment unjust.[2] Quoting Thomas Paine's line that "tyranny, like hell, is not easily conquered," she argued that Americans must resist governmental overreach in every domain — especially the courts — and that every victory for liberty is worth celebrating, "even if it's Canadian."[2]
