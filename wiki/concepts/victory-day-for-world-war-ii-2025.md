# Victory Day for World War II, 2025

"Victory Day for World War II, 2025" was a presidential action issued by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and published by [[white-house|the White House]] on 8 May 2025, commemorating the 80th anniversary of the Allied Powers' triumph over national socialism and fascism and the end of the Second World War in Europe.[1] The proclamation described the Allied victory as "one of the most epic victories for forces of freedom in the history of the world" and celebrated "the unmatched might, strength, and power of the American Armed Forces."[1]

## Content

The statement recalled the 7 December 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor as the moment the United States entered what it called "the apex of the eternal battle between good and evil," and noted that more than 250,000 Americans died fighting the Nazi regime over nearly four years.[1] It held that without the sacrifice of American soldiers the war would not have been won and the present world "would look drastically different."[1] It marked 8 May 1945 as the date of the Allies' acceptance of Germany's unconditional surrender.[1]

The proclamation then pivoted from commemoration to policy, invoking "peace through strength" and Trump's stated commitment to "stopping the years of endless foreign wars and preventing the further loss of lives."[1] Quoting his Inaugural Address, it declared that success would be measured "not only by the battles we win but also by the wars we end," and that his "proudest legacy will be that of a peacemaker."[1] It closed with thanks to "every patriot from the Greatest Generation."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers read the proclamation as Trump formally declaring 8 May "Victory Day" over the Nazis, and situated it alongside two same-week developments: the announcement of the United Kingdom as the first trade deal concluded since [[liberation-day-tariffs|Liberation Day]], and the joint declaration by Russia and China in Moscow — on Russia's own [[victory-day|Victory Day]] — of an end to what they described as the Nazi hegemon.[1] The Badlands framing treated these as a single convergent narrative: "Same. Same."[1] A day later, [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Moscow pledged to counter "neo-Nazism and militarism" while defending a multipolar world order, which Badlands presented as confirmation of that reading.[1]
