# Montreal Cognitive Assessment

The **Montreal Cognitive Assessment** (MoCA) is a widely used screening test for cognitive impairment, described in press coverage as the "gold-standard" cognitive exam. It became a recurring feature of American political debate in early 2024 amid questions about the mental acuity of aging presidential candidates.[1]

## Origins and clinical use

The assessment was developed by Canadian neurologist [[ziad-nasreddine|Dr. Ziad Nasreddine]], who invented what became one of the most popular cognitive assessments in clinical practice.[1] Speaking to the New York Post in February 2024, Nasreddine said of testing generally: "It's a good thing that we assess cognition the minute there's any suspicion about cognitive function."[1] He added that "if the test shows normal results, the likelihood of impairment is very low. Then everybody is reassured that this is maybe just normal aging — that we can all be forgetful and make mistakes. But it doesn't mean that there's a significant cognitive disorder happening."[1]

## 2024 political controversy

Asked about mounting public calls for President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], then 81, and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], then 77, to take such a test, Nasreddine sidestepped politics and declined to comment on Biden directly, but suggested it may be a good idea for aging politicians to take the exam.[1] Speculation about Biden's mental acuity had reached a fever pitch after a string of public lapses and the report by special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], which heavily suggested Biden had "diminished faculties" and described him as a "well-meaning elderly man with a poor memory."[1] See [[hur-report]].[1]
