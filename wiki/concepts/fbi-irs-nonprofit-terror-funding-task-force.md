# FBI–IRS Nonprofit Terror Funding Task Force

The FBI–IRS nonprofit terror funding task force is a joint federal initiative reported in March 2026 in which [[fbi|FBI]] and [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] agents investigate nonprofit organizations suspected of funding [[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]] or political violence.[1]

## Structure

Sources told CBS News that the operation, described as a "mission control command center," would be based at the FBI, with IRS Criminal Investigations agents detailed to it on temporary one-year assignments.[1] In parallel, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was assembling its own task force to probe [[antifa|antifa]]-linked funding, and US attorney offices were directed to assign domestic terrorism coordinators.[1]

## Policy origins

The initiative follows a December memo from Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] instructing federal agencies to prioritize investigations of antifa and other extremist groups.[1] Bondi's memo highlighted potential violence, anti-American and anti-religious ideologies, and tax fraud as key concerns, and ordered law enforcement to compile intelligence on such groups for the FBI.[1] That memo in turn built on a September executive order from [[donald-trump|President Trump]] on domestic terrorism, issued after high-profile incidents including the 2025 assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[1]

## Significance

By pairing criminal tax enforcement with counterterrorism authorities, the task force targets the financing layer of political violence rather than individual acts, making the tax-exempt status and donor flows of nonprofit entities a central object of federal investigation.[1]
