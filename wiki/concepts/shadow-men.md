# Shadow Men

"Shadow men" is the term drawn from [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]]'s book *Shadow Men: An Encyclopedia of Mind Control* and adopted in the Badlands Media essay "False Flags and the Occult" for a hereditary class of covert rulers said to have operated behind formal power structures throughout history.[1]

## Characteristics

Napoleon argues that a consistent and reliable modus operandi has been used all throughout history by these figures, who tend to be intelligent psychopaths.[1] Their foundational technique was learning to track the motion of the heavens and to assign socio-religious significance to the movement of celestial bodies, eventually predicting celestial events with staggering accuracy and pairing those predictions with seeded narratives presenting them as proof of communion with the gods.[1] This established their authority over a less intelligent and superstitious citizenry and links [[astrotheology|astrotheology]] to occult power.[1]

## Historical trajectory

In the deep past, small tribes centered on shamanistic practices are said to have developed human sacrifice and psycho-drama, or ritual magic, as standard features of shadow-men control.[1] As societies grew more complex, the shaman or witch doctor could no longer indoctrinate everyone directly, and with the emergence of monarchy that function passed to much larger religious institutions.[1] From these primordial roots, the article argues, more sophisticated dark elite groups emerged to form the civilizations of Sumer, Babylon, Egypt and Phoenicia and onward through history, with their rites modernizing over time into forms such as the [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] ritual.[1]

The [[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mystery Schools]] are cited as a key institutional vehicle, later used to recruit and cement members of the general population into a secret power cult that itself served the shadow men operating behind the scenes.[1] After the fall of the Roman Empire the shadow men are said to have "crypto-ized," clothing themselves in other religions such as Judaism, Islam and Christianity, and later in secularism; the article asserts that today secular systems of power overshadow the older exoteric socio-religious systems while the underlying astrotheological basis for shaping world events remains alive.[1]

## Cited on borrowed divine authority

A separate March 14, 2023 Badlands Media essay on the [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]] cites *Shadow Men* as "basically an encyclopedia" on rulers who believe in nothing but their own power yet still pretend to rule on behalf of some higher power.[2] The reference supports that essay's claim that for most of human history kings, rulers, pharaohs and other authority figures have relied on theological or quasi-theological arguments to justify their rule, and that abuse of this pattern by false rulers does not invalidate the underlying principle that authority is not something one gives oneself.[2]

## Chapter on America's founding

The full title is given elsewhere as *Shadow Men: an Encyclopedia of Mind Control*, and the November 1, 2023 essay "Is the United States a Nation?" calls it [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]]'s "masterwork."[3] That essay highlights a single 91-page chapter devoted entirely to demonstrating the extraordinary vision and circumstances that gave birth to America centuries ago.[3] [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] uses the chapter to support his claim that America's early years posed an exceptional obstacle to mankind's would-be masters, and that the phrase "the land of the free" reflects a historical reality rather than a stereotype.[3]

## Definition in the Nature of Civilizations series

The February 24, 2023 essay "The Steering Wheel of Civilization" defines Shadow Men as psychopathic parasitic elites who employ deception, coercion and mafioso tactics to gain power, and who often go on to become the shaman, priest-class, monarchy or secular rulers of a civilization.[5] The essay stresses that the term does not designate any particular cabal grouping — such as the Davos globalists or the Chinese Communist Party syndicate — but rather the shared psychological profile of members of all such groups.[5]

Shadow Men are characterized there as "ancient psychologists," people who used their disproportionate share of [[parallel-duties-and-perpendicular-freedoms|perpendicular freedoms]] to acquire expert knowledge in manipulating the minds of others; they may be small-time conmen or globalists of massive influence.[5] Their malevolence is said to be so far beyond the norm that ordinary people struggle to comprehend their ambitions, which are commonly misread as a simple desire for money and power when money and power are in fact only tools serving a darker purpose.[5] The essay attributes the coining of the term to [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]], described as a clinically and medically trained psychologist, forensic analyst and lawyer who claims to have worked directly with cabal types in Hollywood and elsewhere.[5] It also frames the eventual overthrow of the Shadow Men as possible, with the resulting prosperity for free people described as almost beyond imagining.[5]

## Cited in The Werewolf Game

The opening installment of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series cites *Shadow Men* for the report that the rulers of this world see themselves as "Gods" in a Darwinist universe and as "apex predators" in a struggle for supremacy over the Earth.[6] That essay also invokes the Shadow Men as a shorthand for how such figures view ordinary humanity, comparing their outlook to Agent Smith's expression of hatred for humanity in *The Matrix*.[6] The reference supports the article's broader thesis that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] amounts to organized psychopathy and that evil is best defined as unbounded parasitism.[6]
