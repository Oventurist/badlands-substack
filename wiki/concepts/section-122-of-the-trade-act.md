# Section 122 of the Trade Act

Section 122 of the Trade Act is the statutory authority under which [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] imposed a 10% global tariff in February 2026, following the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s decision striking down most of the tariffs he had enacted in 2025.[1] The provision is time-limited: the resulting 10% measure was set to expire in July 2026 unless [[congress|Congress]] voted to extend it.[1]

## Litigation

A federal trade court ruled against the Section 122 tariffs in May 2026 but stopped short of broadly halting their collection nationwide.[1] The Trump administration appealed, and the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-federal-circuit|US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit]] issued a short-term administrative stay pausing the lower court's ruling, giving challengers seven days to oppose a longer-term stay while the appellate court weighed whether the block should remain in place during the case.[1]

## Significance

Because Section 122 supplied a fallback authority after the Supreme Court curtailed the administration's earlier tariff architecture, litigation over the provision carries direct consequences for the durability of the tariff program and for the balance of tariff-setting power between the presidency and Congress.[1]
