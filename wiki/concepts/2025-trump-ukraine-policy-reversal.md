# 2025 Trump Ukraine Policy Reversal

The 2025 Trump Ukraine policy reversal was the abrupt shift in United States posture toward the Russia–Ukraine war that became fully visible on the third anniversary of the Russian invasion in February 2025, as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] discarded the Biden administration's framework of unconditional support for Kyiv.[1]

## From Biden's framework to the U-turn

When [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Russia invaded Ukraine three years earlier, Joe Biden rallied the world; the United States and its partners isolated Russia and poured tens of billions of dollars in arms, cash, and loans into [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]]'s battered country even as Putin refused to yield.[1] "America stands up to bullies. We stand up for freedom. This is who we are," Biden said from the [[white-house|White House]].[1]

By the anniversary of the invasion, the Biden administration's position of "nothing about Ukraine, without Ukraine" had been tossed aside by Trump and his advisers.[1] Trump lashed out at Ukraine, blamed its leaders for Putin's invasion, and cut Kyiv out of early negotiations with Russia, to the shock of America's allies.[1] His taunting of Zelenskyy, whom he called a "dictator," was rebuked by European leaders and irritated Ukraine-supporting lawmakers in the United States.[1] "I'm just here to try and get peace," Trump said that month. "I don't care so much about anything other than I want to stop having millions of people killed."[1]

## The resources demand

Running alongside the diplomatic reversal was a proposed resource deal that would give the United States fifty percent ownership of Ukraine's critical natural resources as compensation for what Washington claims is $500 billion in American aid to Kyiv.[1] The proposal became a source of tension between the two capitals, with Zelensky rebuffing the initial offer and insisting that he "would not sign something that will be paid by ten generations of Ukrainians."[1] Former British Prime Minister [[boris-johnson|Boris Johnson]], speaking at the Yalta European Strategy conference in Kiev, called the deal "extortionate" but urged Ukraine to accept it as Kyiv's best option for securing long-term support from Washington.[1] See [[us-ukraine-minerals-agreement|US–Ukraine minerals agreement]].[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands writer Burning Bright argued that the western globalist hegemon was not panicking because it had lost the war but because it had "lost the story of that war and of all wars to come," leaving its partisans in the untenable position of responding to overtures of peace by calling for war.[1] In a later item in the same brief he placed Ukraine within a broader "capitulation pattern" — Canada and Mexico backing down within hours of their trade confrontations, EU bureaucrats "shrieking about impending peace deals," and Zelenskyy playing hard against Trump's demands "right up until he doesn't."[1]

He contended that globalist interests could not afford to lose what he termed their "Prussian Proxy State," both for its strategic, economic, and criminal utility — laundering, saber rattling, and power projection — and for the precedent its loss would set, and that the true exposure of the Ukraine–Russia chapter lay not with Ukraine but with its handlers, who wield far less kinetic and economic power than most believed.[1] He asserted that the only thing that had kept Russia from taking the board by force earlier was that the U.S. military, before Trump, was controlled by those same interests.[1]
