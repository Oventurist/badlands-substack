# Garland v. Range

Garland v. Range is a Second Amendment case in which the [[department-of-justice|U.S. Department of Justice]] asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] to hold that a Pennsylvania man forfeited his firearms rights because of a decades-old nonviolent conviction.[1]

## Background

In 1995, Bryan David Range's wife understated the household's income on an application for public assistance. Range took responsibility and pleaded guilty to making a false statement to obtain food stamps in the Court of Common Pleas of Lancaster County, Pennsylvania.[1] He was sentenced to three years of probation, which court records show he completed without incident, and he paid $2,458 in restitution, $288.29 in costs and a $100 fine.[1]

## Litigation

The 3rd Circuit Court of Appeals ruled in Range's favor.[1] The DOJ nonetheless sought Supreme Court review, arguing that felons lose Second Amendment rights through disregard of the law whether or not their offenses were violent.[1] Range's own lawyers joined the government in asking the Court to take the case, seeking to probe the limits of the June 2022 decision in New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen.[1] As of the November 2023 reporting, the high court had not decided whether to hear the case.[1]
