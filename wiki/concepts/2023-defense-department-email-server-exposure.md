# 2023 Defense Department Email Server Exposure

The 2023 Defense Department email server exposure was a cybersecurity incident in which an internal U.S. military mailbox system was left accessible to the open internet for approximately two weeks before being secured on Monday, February 20, 2023.[1] The incident was reported by [[techcrunch|TechCrunch]] on February 21, 2023 and summarized the following day in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## The exposure

The affected server was hosted on [[microsoft|Microsoft]]'s Azure government cloud for [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] customers, an environment that runs on servers physically separated from other commercial customers so that sensitive but unclassified government data may be stored and shared on it.[1] The server was part of an internal mailbox system holding roughly three terabytes of internal military emails, many of them pertaining to [[united-states-special-operations-command|U.S. Special Operations Command]] (USSOCOM), the unit tasked with conducting special military operations.[1]

A misconfiguration left the server without any password protection. As a result, anyone on the internet who knew the server's IP address could access the sensitive mailbox data using nothing more than a web browser.[1] The [[pentagon|Department of Defense]] secured the server on Monday, ending the exposure window.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[just-human|Just Human]] treated the causes of the exposure as an open question, observing that it "could be the result of human error or ... not."[1] Two alternative readings were offered: that the lapse represented an attempt to compromise USSOCOM's civilian network, or that it constituted "a clever way of transferring information that was temporarily on that network."[1] The commentary concluded that the findings of any subsequent investigation would be of significant interest.[1]
