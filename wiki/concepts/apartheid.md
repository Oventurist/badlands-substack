# Apartheid

Apartheid was the codified system of racial segregation imposed in South Africa from 1948 by the Nationalist Party government of Hendrik Verwoerd.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] examines it as the political environment chosen by [[joshua-haldeman|Joshua Haldeman]] when he emigrated in 1949, and in which [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] was raised until the age of seventeen.[1]

## The legal architecture

Beginning in 1948, the apartheid government applied a racial segregation programme that classified the people of South Africa into four groups graded hierarchically from more to less fit: whites, Indians, coloured (mixed) and black.[1] Blacks were then "scientifically" sub-divided into ten additional sub-racial groups, while whites were divided into two.[1]

In 1949 the regime passed a law prohibiting mixed marriages, extending it in 1950 to criminalize interracial sexual intercourse.[1] A Population Registration Act in 1950 required every citizen to carry a racial identification card, and the Group Areas Act of the same year determined which racial groups could live in which districts, forcing the removal of thousands of citizens from their homes and splitting up families.[1] Entire homes and towns were demolished under this reasoning: when District Six in Cape Town was declared a whites-only area — despite whites making up only one per cent of its population when the law passed — nearly every building was demolished and an upper-class segregated community built in its place, displacing some 7,000 families.[1]

The 1950 Suppression of Communism Act labelled all anti-apartheid resistance communist, justifying the disappearance of thousands of activists and the creation of secret courts to punish resistance leaders, black or white.[1] With opposition suppressed, the 1951 Reservation of Separate Amenities Act imposed total segregation on beaches, benches, parks, schools, hospitals and building entrances, and the 1951 Bantu Authorities Act required blacks to obtain permission and special passes to leave their districts.[1]

## Repression

During the years the Haldeman family lived in Pretoria's richest neighbourhood, a 1960 march by 7,000 people protesting district passes ended in a massacre when police fired into the crowd, killing 69 and wounding hundreds.[1] In 1976 the Soweto Uprising, a protest by some 20,000 citizens — mostly schoolchildren — against school segregation, resulted in the deaths of nearly 700 children; the schoolboy Hector Pieterson, photographed by Sam Nzima being carried by Mbuyisa Makhubo after being shot by police, became its emblem.[1]

## The Musk and Haldeman family

Haldeman arrived in 1949 and began a new life in Pretoria as a chiropractor, an adventurer flying across Africa in search of the lost city of the Kalahari, and a writer promoting apartheid and Jewish conspiracy theories, declaring that "South Africa will become the leader of white civilization in the world."[1] Asked about the anti-black legislation, he said: "The natives are very primitive and must not be taken seriously... Some are quite clever in a routine job, but the best of them cannot assume responsibility and will abuse authority. The present government of South Africa knows how to handle the native question."[1]

Ehret writes that the South Africa which awarded Haldeman riches, his daughter [[maye-musk|Maye Musk]] national beauty queen status, and his son-in-law [[errol-musk|Errol Musk]] an emerald mine, was the only nation on earth to unapologetically embrace the full Nazi paradigm in practice after the Second World War.[1] He notes that no member of the family, from Joshua Haldeman to Maye and Errol Musk to Elon Musk himself, has ever offered a single critical word about the apartheid regime in any writing or interview.[1]
