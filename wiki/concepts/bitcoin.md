Bitcoin and blockchain technology are discussed on [[badlands-media]] as part of its coverage of cryptocurrency and criticism of traditional finance.[1] The network's Rugpull Radio show (Thursday, 10:30pm ET), hosted by GMoney and Patriots in Progress, "educate[s] viewers on both the benefits and dangers of Bitcoin/Blockchain technology and the cryptocurrency space," while also critiquing the [[central-banking]] system.[1]

## Trump's 2024 pro-crypto overture

Several Bitcoin miners met with former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at Mar-a-Lago on the night of Tuesday, June 11, 2024, according to Matthew Schultz, executive chairman of the crypto mining company CleanSpark Inc.[2] Trump told attendees that he loves and understands cryptocurrency, said Bitcoin miners help stabilize energy supply from the grid, and pledged to be an advocate for miners in the White House.[2] Jason Les, chief executive of Riot Platforms Inc., also met with Trump at Mar-a-Lago.[2]

Trump had increasingly highlighted Bitcoin and other digital assets on the campaign trail as a way to reach new voters, taking advice on the subject from [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], pledging at a Libertarian Party convention to commute the sentence of Silk Road founder Ross Ulbricht, and accepting crypto donations through his campaign.[2]

### Badlands price analysis

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that on the same day [[donald-trump-jr|Donald Trump Jr.]] boosted Bitcoin Magazine's endorsement of his father as the best choice for a Bitcoin future, Bitcoin crashed almost $2,000.[2] He argued this was a negative indicator not about Bitcoin but about an establishment attempting to suppress its true price discovery, as it had with gold and silver for decades, using futures contracts leveraged through the ballooning ETF funds launched in January to keep tests of all-time highs from breaking out sharply.[2] Such paper suppression, he contended, is far harder to sustain against a decentralized, finite resource once game theory kicks in among retail and institutional investors alike, and the Trump family's boosting appeared to be "baiting the final banking ammo into the light."[2]

## Long-term holder accumulation (March 2025)

In March 2025 Bitcoin's long-term holders resumed accumulation in what analysts described as a notable shift in investor sentiment despite turbulence in the market.[3] Data from the on-chain analytics platform Glassnode showed that the "BTC: Long-term holder net position change" metric had flipped positive for the first time that year, suggesting that long-term investors were capitalising on market conditions to add significant amounts of BTC to their holdings.[3]

Earlier that month Bitcoin's price had plunged from above $90,000 to around $80,000 during a rapid sell-off, a move that stunned traders and triggered a continuous wave of liquidations among short-term investors.[3] Long-term holders nonetheless treated the sub-$90,000 levels as a buying opportunity rather than a reason to capitulate.[3] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] paired the data with a remark from General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] ahead of his appearance on Bitcoin Rugpull Radio: "What I'm telling people is get on board and figure it out because it's here, it's going to be here in a bigger way and it's going to either control us or we are going to control it."[3]

## Trump and the 2024 Nashville conference

In July 2024 the organizers of the annual Bitcoin conference in Nashville, Tennessee announced that presumptive Republican presidential nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would speak at the event, which ran July 25–27.[4] Speculation had risen earlier that day when speakers told CoinDesk their slots had been reassigned to make room for what an email described as "a very special guest"; a conference spokesman confirmed Trump would speak for 30 minutes at 2 p.m. CT on the final day.[4]

Trump had leaned into Bitcoin and crypto rhetoric since coming out in favor of the industry at his NFT gala earlier that spring, and had baked pro-Bitcoin policies into the core Republican Party platform, drawing a sharp contrast with the Biden administration despite misgivings about the technology during his first term.[4]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the development as a front in the Info War against what he called the Globalist Controllers, arguing that all pillars of the "System of Systems" must be weakened before it collapses, and that Bitcoin represented sovereignty and free will against collectivism — a "First Principles codified into corporeal reality" watershed in the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]].[4]

## New all-time high (May 2025)

Bitcoin rose to a new all-time high on Wednesday, May 21, 2025, breaking its January record; the price was last higher by 2% at $108,955.10 according to Coin Metrics, after rising as high as $109,857 earlier in the session.[5] At one point the gains were erased as Treasury yields spiked and stocks fell to session lows.[5]

Antoni Trenchev, cofounder of crypto exchange Nexo, attributed the move to "an array of favorable ingredients in the macro cauldron, namely softer U.S. inflation numbers, a de-escalation in the U.S.-China trade war and the Moody's downgrade of U.S. sovereign debt, which has put the spotlight on alternative stores of value like bitcoin."[5] He contrasted the environment with early April, when global macro concerns peaked and bitcoin slumped to $74,000, and suggested a three-month window had opened for risk assets while a broader US-China trade agreement was negotiated.[5] After languishing for weeks amid tariff-related uncertainty, bitcoin had climbed steadily through May, up 15% for the month.[5]

Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] marked the milestone by asking whether his "Bitcoin frens" were tired of winning and noting he had forgotten Bitcoin Pizza Day.[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] added that he expected commentators who had not done the cognitive work to begin framing Bitcoin as part of the plan, after previously reposting claims that gold would destroy the Federal Reserve without considering the implications, and credited GMoney as having been on target.[5]

## Resilience during the 2024 CrowdStrike outage

During the [[2024-crowdstrike-global-outage|CrowdStrike global outage]] of July 19, 2024, the Bitcoin network was completely unaffected while banking networks failed, a contrast [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited as a practical demonstration of decentralization.[6] Bitcoin's price rose over the following weekend, and Badlands commentary framed the episode as an "orange pill" moment: repeated threats to the global banking, investing and cyber security system of systems would, in this reading, force those who believed the network could be "shut off" to confront its continued operation.[6] The outage coincided with speculation that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might declare Bitcoin a strategic reserve asset at a Nashville conference, an idea developed at [[us-bitcoin-strategic-reserve|US Bitcoin strategic reserve]].[6]

## Trump's 2024 embrace

At the [[bitcoin-2024-nashville-conference|Bitcoin 2024 conference]] in Nashville on July 27, 2024, Republican presidential nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] pledged to make the United States a "bitcoin superpower" and the "crypto capital of the planet," and to establish a bitcoin "strategic reserve" from the coins the federal government already holds.[7] He further promised to remove [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] chair [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]] and to create a crypto advisory council, telling the audience that future rules would be "written by people who love your industry, not hate your industry."[7] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the speech as an invitation to bitcoiners and anons alike to recognize that the monetary and political awakenings were "the same train."[7]


## Powell Speculation Spike and MicroStrategy Purchases (April 2025)

Bitcoin spiked suddenly in April 2025 alongside a record gold price and a three-year low in the dollar index, after remarks by [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]] revived speculation that [[donald-trump|President Trump]] would remove [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] from the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[8] Badlands commentary framed sound money backing — "whether you're talking about gold, Bitcoin or both" — as the endpoint of Trump's effort to pull the United States away from the fiat system after first milking it for all it was worth.[8] In the same period [[microstrategy|MicroStrategy]] disclosed in a Form 8-K to the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] that it had bought 6,556 bitcoin for $555.8 million in cash between April 14 and April 20, 2025, at roughly $84,785 per coin; analysts at TD Cowen argued the company's purchases had little effect on the price.[8]

## Trump's commitment to Bitcoin mining (June 2024)

In June 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met with Bitcoin Magazine CEO David Bailey and several prominent United States Bitcoin miners, committing at the meeting to champion Bitcoin mining both in Washington, D.C. and on the global stage.[9] Attendees included representatives of leading mining firms such as CleanSpark, Riot Platforms, and Marathon Digital, among them S Matthew Schultz of CleanSpark, Jason Les and Brian Morgenstern of Riot Platforms, Salman Khan of Marathon Digital, and Amanda Fabiano of Fabiano Consulting, previously Head of Mining at Galaxy and Director of Bitcoin Mining at Fidelity.[9] Afterward Trump posted on Truth Social that he wanted all "remaining Bitcoin to be made in the USA" and that doing so "would help us be energy dominant."[9]

Badlands commentary tied Trump's outreach to Bitcoin maximalists, alongside his promise of across-the-board tax cuts, to the establishment's economic bind following the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s June 2024 decision to hold rates steady while projecting only one cut for the year.[9]

## Trump's engagement with the mining industry (June 2024)

On June 11, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met at Mar-a-Lago with executives from the US Bitcoin mining industry, the latest step in his embrace of the cryptocurrency sector during the 2024 campaign.[10] Nine to eleven executives representing firms with roughly 30% of the Bitcoin network's total computing power attended, including [[marathon-digital|Marathon Digital]], Riot Platforms, Terawulf, CleanSpark, Core Scientific, Arkon Energy, Cholla Energy, and Exacore.[10] Marathon Digital's Jayson Browder described Trump as prepared and informed, said he grasped how Bitcoin mining would support workloads such as AI, and recounted his approving reaction to an American-made Auradine ASIC chip.[10] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the episode showed that the public narrative does not move Trump and patriots — they move the public narrative.[10] See [[2024-trump-mar-a-lago-bitcoin-mining-meeting]].

## JPMorgan reversal (May 2025)

On May 19, 2025, [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] chief executive [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] told the bank's annual investor day that the largest US bank would allow clients to buy bitcoin, though it would not custody the asset and would instead reflect holdings in client statements.[11] The announcement marked a reversal for Dimon, who had cited money-laundering risk and unclear ownership in calling bitcoin "worthless" in 2021 and a "pet rock" in 2024, and who had suggested in 2023 Senate testimony that the government shut it down.[11]

## Institutional retreat (November 2025)

By November 12, 2025, Bitcoin was struggling to climb out of a $330 billion hole, and the forces that had powered its ascent were in retreat.[12] After a bruising October, the digital currency staged only a halting recovery — climbing, dipping, and stalling above $100,000 — while the tailwind that had defined much of 2025, institutional conviction, went missing.[12] Over the preceding month many of the largest buyers, from exchange-traded fund allocators to corporate treasuries, quietly stepped back, depriving the market of flow-driven support; the retreat sparked no industry-wide panic but upended expectations.[12] For much of the year institutions had been the backbone of Bitcoin's legitimacy and price: ETFs as a cohort took in more than $25 billion according to Bloomberg data, pushing assets as high as roughly $169 billion and reframing the asset as a portfolio diversifier hedging inflation, monetary debasement, and political disarray — a narrative that was fraying and leaving the market exposed to quiet disengagement.[12]

[[gmoney|GMONEY]] remarked in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] on the abrupt reversal of sentiment in his X feed, which had shifted from relentlessly bullish all year to "pure bearish DOOM," summarizing the dynamic as "RUG ON. RUG OFF." and asking how many leveraged traders "got rekt."[12] He framed the episode as "banker panic" and argued that tokenization on Bitcoin Layer 2 ends Wall Street's rigged games.[12]

## May 2025 rally and the stablecoin bill

Bitcoin reclaimed the $100,000 level in May 2025 and surged to $106,000 on a Sunday, a level not seen since early February and close to its all-time high of almost $110,000.[13] Forbes reported that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] analysts had flipped from gold to bitcoin, predicting bitcoin would outperform gold over the remainder of 2025, even as BlackRock issued what the outlet called a stark bitcoin warning.[13] The rally coincided with a U.S. bill to regulate [[stablecoin|stablecoin]] issuers — formally introducing crypto into the established financial system — heading for debate in Congress that week, with some observers expecting it to pass the Senate.[13] Coverage tied the year's extreme price swings to [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s disruption of the global trade order and attendant predictions of dollar collapse.[13]

## August 2025 surge past $117,000

Having fallen below $112,000 days earlier, bitcoin returned above $117,000 late in U.S. trading on Thursday, August 7, 2025, retaking the range it had held for most of July.[14] The crypto market was first lifted overnight by news of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s intention to sign an executive order allowing crypto and other alternative assets in 401(k) retirement plans.[14] The later push above $117,000 followed the nomination of [[stephen-miran|Stephen Miran]] to the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Board of Governors, a figure presumed to share at least part of the president's dovish view of interest rates.[14]

## February 2024 rally and Coinbase outage

Bitcoin topped $60,000 on Wednesday, February 28, 2024 for the first time since 2021, reaching a collective market capitalization of nearly $1.2 trillion.[15] Shortly after noon Eastern that day it lost about $2,800, or over 4%, in a matter of minutes as reports spread on X of a [[coinbase|Coinbase]] glitch that showed users zero balances.[15] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that BTC was north of $62,000 later that day after passing $64,000 earlier, and doubted the narrative that investors had sold within minutes purely in reaction to the exchange outage.[15]

## Bitcoin as indictment and arrest

A January 3, 2024 Badlands Brief essay marking the fifteenth anniversary of the [[bitcoin-genesis-block|Genesis Block]] framed Bitcoin as an instrument of justice rather than an investment.[16] The essay described Bitcoin as "simply an uncorruptible spreadsheet" and, in a more relatable metaphor, as a decentralized history of bank statements that publishes transparent and accurate updates every ten minutes which no one can alter.[16] It contrasted this with all prior monetary ledgers — glass, beads, bronze, silver, gold, and [[fiat-currency|fiat]] — which the author characterized as centrally controlled and arbitrarily revisable.[16]

On that reading, each new block "unseals another indictment" against systemic corruption, with nearly 825,000 blocks produced in fifteen years, while the protocol's fixed issuance constitutes an ongoing "arrest" because it removes the ability of those closest to currency creation to seize purchasing power through [[inflation|inflation]].[16] The essay invoked [[jason-lowery|Jason Lowery]]'s [[softwar|Softwar]] thesis, a 2014 remark by [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] on cryptography's defensive power, and a 2023 NOSTR post by [[edward-snowden|Edward Snowden]] naming [[charlie-munger|Charlie Munger]], and grouped [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]] and [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] among the loudest critics.[16]

## Cryptographic and strategic framing

Bitcoin's mining and unlocking process uses SHA-256, one of the six hash functions in the Secure Hash Algorithm 2 family created by the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] in collaboration with the National Institute of Science and Technology.[18] The Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers" places this fact within a wider argument that [[cryptography|codes and ciphers]] are decisive in a civilization's rise, longevity, and fall.[18] The article further ties Bitcoin to the claim that the central control of currency (fiat) and money (gold) is the root cause of a great deal of evil, and cites Major [[jason-lowery|Jason Lowery]] on Bitcoin's national strategic significance in a new war-fighting domain.[18]

## Bitcoin as a filter against AI-generated data (April 2023)
In "Don't Bring Shiny Rocks to Digital Gunfights," Badlands contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] proposed Bitcoin as a mitigation for the hazard of [[artificial-intelligence|AI]]-generated text, audio and video overwhelming the public's ability to distinguish reality from fabrication.[17] The mechanism he described associates bits of data with an energy cost through a micro-transaction, creating an auditable, immutable record of a real-world physical cost and thereby a database of data that has "paid the cost."[17] Such payments would be trivial for individual humans but, he argued, cost-prohibitive to replicate across an effectively infinite set of AI-generated data.[17]
The author acknowledged that paying the cost does not itself guarantee that surviving data is truthful — only that a scarce resource was expended to gain entry to the record.[17] As a demonstration he pointed to an asset he had created on a Bitcoin Layer 2, the Counterparty protocol, carrying animation and an audio track, noting that observers can verify the price paid, the time of the action and the wallet that performed it.[17] He framed the approach through Major Jason Lowery's [[softwar|Softwar]] thesis and noted that [[michael-saylor|Michael Saylor]], [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had separately described using Bitcoin against bot farms.[17] The essay closed with the injunction "do not give your Bitcoin to AI."[17]

## Value, attributes and adoption (May 2023)

In a May 2023 feature, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] examined what makes a currency a currency, listing the six key attributes as scarcity, divisibility, acceptability, portability, durability, and uniformity or resistance to counterfeiting.[19] Citing Investopedia, the piece compared Bitcoin's value to that of precious metals — both limited in quantity with select use cases — and noted the estimate that at $514,000 per coin Bitcoin's market capitalization would reach roughly 15 percent of the global currency market.[19] Bitcoin's first transaction purchased two Papa John's pizzas; its initial value was less than a penny ($0.0008), reached $0.08 by the end of that month, peaked above $68,000, and stood at $29,163.70 when the article was written.[19]

The [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]] and the Great Recession were identified as the primary catalysts for the rise of digital asset classes, as awareness of central bank power drove innovators toward decentralization and digitization.[19] Since then more than 21,000 different cryptocurrencies have been created, with [[ethereum|Ethereum]] second to Bitcoin in both value and trust.[19] A 2021 Pew Research Center study found 16 percent of Americans had ever used cryptocurrency; the Financial Health Network reported that figure had fallen to 11 percent by early 2023 after the late-2022 market collapse.[19]

Bitcoin's [[proof-of-work]] design is criticized for slow transactions and energy use — Bankrate reported that mining accounted for 0.40 percent of world electricity consumption as of July 2022, with an estimated 22.9 million metric tons of annual carbon output.[19] Both [[gmoney|GMoney]] and [[ryan-matta|Ryan Matta]] nevertheless drew a hard line between Bitcoin and the wider token market, repeating the phrase "Bitcoin, not crypto."[19] The article framed the digitization of money as the most transformative monetary moment since the [[jekyll-island-meeting|1910 Jekyll Island meeting]], asking whether Bitcoin can free people from the private banking cartel or whether apathy and fraud will instead accelerate the [[great-reset|Great Reset]].[19]

## Bitcoin as trust-less money (July 2024)

In "TRUST: The Plan," [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] presented Bitcoin as the direct technical answer to the exploitation of trust, noting that the whitepaper *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*, released on October 31, 2008, contains the term "trust" fourteen times across its nine pages.[25] He quoted [[satoshi-nakamoto|Satoshi Nakamoto]]'s observation that commerce on the Internet has come to rely almost exclusively on financial institutions serving as trusted third parties, a model that "still suffers from the inherent weaknesses of the trust based model," and Satoshi's proposal of "an electronic payment system based on cryptographic proof instead of trust, allowing any two willing parties to transact directly with each other without the need for a trusted third party."[25]

The essay's reading is that Bitcoin removes the need to trust altogether — that Bitcoin is, in its formulation, trust-less.[25] Because until January 3, 2009 humanity had only trust-based monies such as seashells, glass beads, precious stones, precious metals and government-issued paper currencies, all of which could be exploited through dishonest scales, closed trade routes, sanctions, counterfeiting, double spending, theft and market manipulation, Bitcoin is described as revolutionary to money and as the means by which populations can regain their trust.[25] The author also cited [[michael-saylor|Michael Saylor]]'s remark that "Bitcoin insight is limited to those with a [need] to know," describing people who investigate solutions after a personal mortality event and discover Bitcoin.[25] The argument connects to [[softwar|Softwar]], the [[cantillon-effect|Cantillon effect]], and the reinterpretation of [[trust-the-plan|"TRUST the plan"]].[25]
