# One Country, Two Systems

"One country, two systems" was the slogan describing the constitutional framework under which [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]] returned to Chinese sovereignty on 1 July 1997 while retaining a separate political and economic system for 50 years.[1] Hong Kong was to be "the little exception in vast, Communist-ruled China," enjoying fifty years of democratic life and autonomy.[1]

## Skepticism and limits

Badlands Media noted that many observers were skeptical from the beginning that a single country could maintain two separate systems, especially when one system is communist and the other is supposed to be pseudo-democratic and free-market capitalist.[1] Hong Kong was in fact never fully democratic: from 1982 to 1997 it moved from having no elected government to having a fully elected Legislative Council, though not entirely by geographical constituencies.[1] Under Chinese sovereignty after 1 July 1997, Hong Kong regressed toward its pre-democracy condition through the establishment of appointed provisional bodies in place of elected ones.[1]

## What the framework preserved — and lost

Under the framework there remained press freedom, speech freedom, the right to a fair trial, and freedom of assembly — all of which ran counter to mainland practice and which largely made Hong Kong a prime financial hub for international business.[1] Those freedoms are nonexistent today.[1] The article characterized Hong Kong as an unprecedented case: never before had a city or state of more than 7 million people had its rights stripped away in such a remarkably short period.[1] The framework's guarantees derived from the [[sino-british-joint-declaration|Joint Declaration]] and the [[hong-kong-basic-law|Hong Kong Basic Law]], and were dismantled through instruments such as the [[hong-kong-national-security-law-2020|National Security Law]].[1]
