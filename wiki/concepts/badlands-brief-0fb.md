# Badlands Brief: Time is on Trump's Side ... But Which Regime is he Fighting?

"Time is on Trump's Side ... But Which Regime is he Fighting?" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily commentary series published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Substack, dated April 24, 2026. Following the standard format, each item pairs a blockquoted summary of mainstream reporting with signed commentary from a Badlands contributor — in this issue [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]. The edition carries the series' standing disclaimer that the opinions expressed are those of the tagged authors and do not necessarily reflect the attitudes or positions of Badlands Media.

## Iran, the Strait of Hormuz, and the inversion thesis

The lead item reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] stated that [[iran|Iran]]'s navy "lies at the bottom of the sea," its air force is demolished, its air defenses and radar are gone, and its leaders are no longer present, declaring that the United States has "all the time in the world" while time is not on Iran's side. A U.S. assessment cited in the item estimates it would take six months to clear Iranian mines from the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] if that became necessary. The shutdown of the waterway is said to have disrupted one-fifth of global oil and LNG flows, pushing Brent crude to $103 per barrel, with analysts warning of potential spikes above $120 if the stalemate persists beyond the following week or two. Tehran, for its part, claims it has begun collecting tolls from passing vessels, with the first revenue transferred to its central bank, and refuses to resume negotiations while the U.S. blockade remains in place.

Burning Bright's commentary observes that headlines "scream of fractured leadership in Tehran, of hardliners losing badly on the battlefield while moderates—whatever that word means in this hall of mirrors—gain respect." He quotes Trump's own characterizations that Iran "is having a very hard time figuring out who their leader is," that "the infighting is ... CRAZY," and that the United States has "total control" over Hormuz, which will remain "sealed up tight" until a deal is struck.

The commentary then advances what the author frames as an inversion of the surface narrative. The conventional premise, he argues — the one the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]] "has subsisted on for decades" — demands that the Islamic Republic be seen as the rogue actor that must be contained, sanctioned, or broken for the rules-based order to hold. Inverted, the picture changes: it is not Trump and the Global Collectorate cornering Iran, but Trump and Iran together putting the Global Collectorate in a bind. On energy grounds, he writes, the Hormuz disruptions ripple outward and hammer importers across Europe, Asia, and Australia while the United States, buoyed by domestic energy abundance, absorbs the shock and redirects capital inward; meanwhile the European Green Agenda "lies in ruins" as [[nato|NATO]] partners quietly beg for redirected flows. On financial grounds, sanctions that once weaponized scarcity are said to expose their own fragility as populations "from Berlin to Canberra" begin questioning why their sovereignty was outsourced to distant chokepoints. The true pincer, he concludes, is not against Iran but against the Invisible Enemy itself — "the faceless matrix that has long seeded wars for harvest," wearing the mask of "so-called Allies" while serving as the primary vector for globalist entanglement.

## Israel, Lebanon, and the ceasefire extension

The second item reports that Trump announced a three-week extension of the Israel–[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] ceasefire following a productive Oval Office meeting with Israeli and Lebanese representatives. He stated that the United States will work with [[lebanon|Lebanon]] to protect itself from Hezbollah and expressed plans to host Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]]. At the same time, the item states that [[israel|Israel]] is actively preparing to renew its war with Iran and awaits a U.S. green light to resume strikes. Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] declared the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] ready for both defense and offense with new targets marked, saying Israel seeks to eliminate the Khamenei dynasty — a reference to Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] — destroy Iran's major energy and power facilities, and deliver "more deadly blows" to collapse the regime's foundations. Israeli leadership anticipated a possible resumption by the end of the week while intensifying intelligence efforts and restocking missiles.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writes that Trump appears to have secured a pause in the Lebanese conflict while focusing on a sustainable peace with Iran. He notes that Israel's pattern of attacks on Lebanese journalists — including bombing their homes — has drawn the ire of the Lebanese government, which days earlier had met with Israel for the first time ever at the White House to negotiate a ceasefire and the fate of Hezbollah. He finds it notable that Israeli media portrayed the two sides afterward as a united front seeking to disarm and dismantle Hezbollah, "which is the more significant fighting force over the official Lebanese military," even as the Lebanese Prime Minister began accusing Israel of war crimes over the targeting of journalists.

The commentary cites [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]], described as Trump's best friend and ambassador to Turkey/Syria, who said in a Fox News interview that Hezbollah cannot be annihilated through military force given its deep integration into Lebanese society over the past several decades. It also notes that Israel has formally acknowledged an "Advanced Defense Zone" on the Lebanese side of the Israel–Lebanon border where the IDF says nobody is allowed and violators will be shot. Separately, Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] has called for Israel to formally expand its borders to include all of Palestine and parts of Lebanon and Syria, justifying the demand on the grounds that current borders lack defensible topography — an approach the author says would mean erasing entire villages and driving thousands from their homeland. The item reports that Israel has already destroyed over 62,000 structures, including apartments and single-family homes.

The author argues that Trump is "beginning to rug the Zionists" by declaring that no nuclear weapons will be used, noting that [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] and others had called for Trump to nuke Iran. Trump also reiterated that nobody will pressure him into a timeline on his peace negotiations with Iran, despite Israeli media reporting that the administration had told counterparts in Tel Aviv that the deadline was Sunday. Reviewing a widely circulated clip that many read as evidence of Trump's status as an Israeli puppet, the author instead hears Trump "feeding into the hubris of the Netanyahu regime," pointing to Trump's repeated line that "Israel will have to defend itself." On this reading Israel is being egged on to fight alone, without U.S. aid, while Trump negotiates lasting peace deals with the very entities Israel seeks to destroy — leaving the IDF, which failed to annihilate [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza, to face "the far more formidable dragon" in Hezbollah unaided. He closes by asking which will break first: the IDF, or Israeli public support for Netanyahu.

## Kalshi and insider trading in prediction markets

The third item reports that [[kalshi|Kalshi]] suspended three political candidates and issued its first-ever insider trading bans for betting on their own 2026 primary races. The candidates named are Mark Moran, an Independent running in the Virginia U.S. Senate Democratic primary; Matt Klein, a Democrat in a Minnesota primary race; and Zeke Enriquez, a Republican in a Texas primary race. All three had wagered on their own election outcomes on the platform, which allows trading on politics, elections, sports, and current events. Kalshi flagged the candidates under newly implemented safeguards blocking political candidates from trading on their own races. Moran stated on X that he bet on himself deliberately in order to get caught and draw attention to Kalshi, which he called destructive to young men, and vowed to impose a 25% "vice tax" on the platform as senator to reduce the national debt. Kalshi's head of enforcement said any trade by a candidate who can influence a market outcome violates the rules regardless of size, described the cases as political insider trading, and confirmed the matters were not referred to the [[commodity-futures-trading-commission|CFTC]] or the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]]. The actions come amid growing scrutiny of prediction markets such as Kalshi and [[polymarket|Polymarket]], with lawmakers warning that large stakes on elections could incentivize interference or undermine the electoral process.

Ashe in America comments that even with insider trading occurring inside the predictive gambling markets, "it's possible the results are more reliable and legitimate than the outcomes of US elections," and that they are certainly more auditable. She notes that weighting the predictions "has been a problem from the jump," since political predictions can swing 30 percentage points in a day as PACs flood the site, making them "not an indicator of anything except where the money is." Nevertheless, reported outcomes from the site can sway voters. Her conclusion: "Elections are fake at every level. But voter intent is real," and because voter intent is real, Americans should be livid about fake elections — perhaps manipulation of the betting markets will finally get them there.

## The Druzhba pipeline and the European pincer

The fourth item reports that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] has completed repairs to the southern branch of the [[druzhba-pipeline|Druzhba pipeline]], damaged by a Russian strike in January, and announced readiness to resume pumping Russian oil to [[hungary|Hungary]] and Slovakia just days after [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]]'s landslide election victory over [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]. President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] confirmed the pipeline can resume operation, with the timing expected to unlock a long-delayed €90 billion [[european-union|EU]] aid package to Ukraine that Orbán had vetoed. Magyar stated that his government will not block the loan but will refuse to contribute to it, will not support Ukraine's EU membership, will reject the EU Migration Pact, and will strengthen Hungary's borders.

Separately, Russia announced it will halt Kazakh crude oil shipments to [[germany|Germany]] through the northern branch of the Druzhba pipeline starting May 1, citing technical issues. The move affects the PCK Schwedt refinery, which supplies most fuel, jet fuel, and heating oil to Berlin, where Kazakh oil previously covered about 17% of needs with volumes reaching nearly 60,000 barrels per day in the first quarter of 2026. Germany's Economy Ministry stated that the suspension does not jeopardize overall petroleum product supply security, since northern pipeline sections and increased deliveries via Poland's Gdansk port can cover shortfalls even if Schwedt operates at reduced capacity.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry describes "an interesting tug-of-war." It began, he writes, with the "defeat" of Viktor Orbán, whose ardent support for Israel had begun to conflict with his ostensible pro-Russia position as tensions between Israel and Russia escalated amid the Iran war. In Orbán's place came Péter Magyar, who apart from a few key issues appears largely aligned with Orbán's platform: he will not support Ukraine's accession to the EU and vows to maintain the policy that Hungary not contribute directly to defending Ukraine. Where the two differ is on the EU itself — Orbán was prepared to leave the bloc, while Magyar strongly values Hungarian membership. That dynamic has led many to conclude Magyar is "an infiltrating snake," which the author allows may be true, but he suggests it is just as likely that Magyar serves a role similar to that of President [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Jolani]] in Syria: a figure designed to provide narrative catharsis and resolution to a conflict while allowing Trump to justify lifting sanctions and normalizing relations. He finds it odd that Ukraine suddenly completed the Druzhba repairs so shortly after Magyar was declared the winner, and notes the kicker — that after Zelensky announced completion, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] announced technical difficulties on the Russian side, meaning no energy access for Germany and much of the EU. Is Putin targeting Magyar as a Deep State puppet sent to replace Orbán, he asks, or is Magyar an earnest continuation of the Orbán arc and the Russian delay merely a ploy against higher powers in the EU? Time will tell, he concludes, but Europe's energy crisis appears set to worsen as the continent is forced to re-evaluate its political priorities.

## Rescheduling of medical marijuana

The final item reports that the Trump administration reclassified state-licensed medical marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III under federal drug policy. The move does not legalize marijuana federally but eases restrictions on a substance that had been in the most restrictive category since the 1937 Marijuana Tax Act. Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] stated that the rescheduling allows research on the safety and efficacy of medical marijuana, ultimately providing patients with better care and doctors with more reliable information. He credited Trump's "decisive leadership" and referenced a December 2025 executive order that removed federal hurdles to medical marijuana and CBD research while directing an expedited rescheduling process. Blanche announced plans for a new expedited hearing with set deadlines to fully reschedule marijuana in the near future. The change legitimizes medical marijuana programs operating in 40 states, allows tax deductions for state-licensed companies, and sets up a system for state-licensed producers to register with the [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]]. The reclassification follows years of stalled efforts under the prior administration and aligns with Trump's campaign promises on American healthcare.

Ashe in America notes that the action comes "on the heels of greenlighting psychedelic research," and that the scheduling change means tax deductions and financial access for THC-based businesses in the 40 states with marijuana programs. She predicts that states with marijuana industries will re-regulate, pointing to Colorado, where more than a decade of legal cannabis has brought steadily increasing regulation and taxation on consumers, producers, and retailers each year. "This never should have been a federal matter," she writes, calling the action "a strong indicator of our trajectory on both healthcare reform and literal liberty."
