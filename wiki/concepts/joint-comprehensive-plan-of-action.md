# Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action

The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), commonly called the Iran Nuclear Deal, is the 2015 agreement between [[iran]] and world powers that traded restrictions on the Iranian nuclear program for relief from international sanctions. In the Badlands Media essay "And Iran, Iran So Far Away" it is treated not primarily as an arms-control instrument but as "one of the most consequential geopolitical maneuvers in recent history" — the pivot on which a factional war inside the Western ruling class turned.

## The "cookie jar" framing

The source likens Iran under sanctions to a forbidden cookie jar: whoever opened it would be "dipping into a goldmine." For fifty years Iran was held under heavy sanctions and remained economically suppressed despite possessing the world's second-largest natural gas reserves and third-largest oil reserves, blocked from trading with other countries and private companies. The prevailing view, according to the article, was that Iran was better kept weak, since a thriving Iran comparable to [[saudi-arabia]] would transform Middle Eastern dynamics and might allow Iranian proxies, flush with funding, to threaten [[israel]]'s existence.

## Architects and opponents

The article attributes the deal to [[jake-sullivan]] at the policy level and [[william-burns]] as lead negotiator, with [[tom-donilon]] — technically Sullivan's superior at the time — playing a key policy role. Burns is said to have headed a secret negotiating team that met high-level Iranian representatives first in 2008 under George W. Bush and then in earnest in 2013, once the Obama and Rouhani governments revived talks. The agreement was signed during the second term of [[barack-obama]].

Resistance to American withdrawal came, on the article's account, from the foreign policy establishment clustered around the [[atlantic-council]], whose energy-industry funders — [[chevron]], [[exxonmobil]], [[bp]] and [[shell]] — stood to profit enormously from an opened Iran, in the same manner Chevron had pursued $10 billion agreements with [[naftogaz]] in Ukraine. [[blackrock]], holding large equity stakes in those firms, is placed at the centre of the financial interest.

## Consequences

The article argues that the deal was designed partly so that Western-partnered Iranian gas could supplant Russian gas in Europe while the Obama administration rallied European sanctions against [[russia]] over Ukraine; Russia consequently opposed sanctions relief. It also holds that the Saudi–Israeli coalition formed in reaction to the deal helped create the conditions for [[donald-trump]]'s rise, and that friction between Obama and [[benjamin-netanyahu]] after the 2015 signing produced reciprocal regime-change efforts in Washington and Jerusalem.

Trump's withdrawal from the agreement, accompanied by the "maximum pressure" campaign threatening "enormous consequences" for sanctions evasion, drew from Donilon the judgment that it was "the worst move in the Middle East since the Iraq War." Under [[joe-biden]] the posture reversed again, with [[china]] permitted to sign a $400 billion agreement with Iran within two months of the new administration taking office. The article concludes that the Iran Deal "split the ruling class factionally, more so than any other issue at the time."

## Zarif and Rouhani in the Badlands Brief (July 2026)

The July 14, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that although [[hassan-rouhani|Hassan Rouhani]] was president when the nuclear deal was signed with the Obama administration and authorized the meetings, Iranian Foreign Minister [[javad-zarif|Javad Zarif]] is credited as the prime contact and principal negotiator for the Iranian side. The same commentary claims Rouhani had central bankers arrested in 2018 — the same ones named by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in his sanctions package — for laundering US dollars through banks in [[iraq|Iraq]] and the UAE.
