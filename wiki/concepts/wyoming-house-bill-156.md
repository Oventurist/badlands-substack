# Wyoming House Bill 156

**Wyoming House Bill 156** is a Wyoming state law requiring voters to provide proof of U.S. citizenship and state residency in order to register to vote.[1]

## Legal challenge and dismissal

The law was challenged in federal court by the Equality State Policy Center, represented by Democratic election attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], in a lawsuit naming Wyoming Secretary of State [[chuck-gray|Chuck Gray]] as defendant.[1] On Tuesday, July 22, 2025, U.S. District Court Judge Scott Skavdahl dismissed the case.[1]

Gray celebrated the dismissal, claiming the state law was "common sense" and aligned with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s priorities on election integrity reform.[1] "We have successfully defended proof of citizenship for registering to vote and fought and defeated this lawsuit," Gray said. "This decision shows that Marc Elias and the radical left were no match for our vigorous defense. Today marks a huge victory for the people of Wyoming and for the truth."[1] The ruling was reported by [[just-the-news|Just the News]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "any day Marc Elias loses in court is a day to toast justice," and asked why anyone would advocate against proof of citizenship for voter registration — answering, "We know why."[1] The commentary linked the case to Elias's broader role in what Badlands writers describe as the grand election conspiracy, and to the subpoena of [[actblue|ActBlue]] over alleged foreign money laundering, noting that [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]] and later the [[elias-law-group|Elias Law Group]] had advised ActBlue on legal compliance.[1]
