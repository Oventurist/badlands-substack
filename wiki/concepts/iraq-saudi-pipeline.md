# Iraq–Saudi Pipeline (IPSA)

The Iraq–Saudi Pipeline, historically known as IPSA, is a long-idled crude oil export pipeline running from [[iraq|Iraq]] through [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to the Red Sea.[1] The line has been inactive for decades, largely because of political tensions following the Gulf War and strained Iraq–Saudi relations.[1]

## 2026 revival effort

In April 2026 Iraq was reported to be seeking to revive the pipeline in order to provide an alternative route for crude shipments outside the Persian Gulf.[1] The proposed route would allow Iraqi oil to bypass the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], one of the world's most critical and vulnerable energy chokepoints.[1] Iraqi officials framed the project as part of a broader effort to diversify export infrastructure and reduce dependence on Gulf shipping lanes amid rising regional instability.[1]

The initiative aligns with wider regional energy strategy trends, in which Middle Eastern producers are pursuing redundant export routes to mitigate disruption risks tied to conflict with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the story as a "full-circle moment of a redemption arc" for the Saudis and the Middle East, arguing that a triumvirate is forming in which Iraq runs oil from Basra in the Persian Gulf to the Mediterranean via Syria and Saudi Arabia.[1] The commentary linked Saudi elements to the historical funding and proliferation of Al Qaeda and later ISIS, and noted claims that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] backed the rise of Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa ("Al Jolani").[1] It further argued that the new Iraqi government elected by popular vote in February — aligned with Shia Islam and Iran — is being used to execute deals that could produce a public reconciliation between Arabs and Persians, and that the pipeline diplomacy cuts against [[israel|Israel]]'s attempt to route regional oil through its own territory.[1]
