# Seditious Six

"Seditious Six" is the label applied to a group of current and former military-affiliated lawmakers, including Senator [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]], whose public statements addressed to service members prompted disciplinary scrutiny by the Defense Department.[1]

## Legal aftermath

The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] attempted to demote Kelly and reduce his retirement benefits over his participation in the episode.[1] The effort drew judicial skepticism: in the US District Court for the District of Columbia, Judge [[richard-leon|Richard Leon]] pressed Justice Department lawyers to identify any Supreme Court or D.C. Circuit precedent authorizing punishment of a service member for off-duty speech.[1] The case is one strand of a broader early-2026 pattern in which federal district courts tested the limits of executive branch authority.[1]


## Hegseth censure of Mark Kelly (January 2026)

Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] formally censured Arizona Senator and retired Navy Capt. [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]] and launched [[pentagon|Pentagon]] proceedings that could demote Kelly's retired rank and reduce his military pension.[2] The action followed the November 2025 video in which Kelly and five other lawmakers urged U.S. service members to refuse what they described as "illegal orders."[2] Hegseth characterized Kelly's participation as "reckless misconduct" and "seditious," placing a letter of censure in Kelly's permanent military file and initiating a retirement grade review; Kelly had 30 days to respond and the Navy up to 45 days to complete its review.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that only Kelly faced consequences because he alone remained subject to military law, arguing that lawmakers had exempted themselves from accountability for sedition in the civilian world and quoting the Declaration of Independence on the right and duty to throw off despotic government.[2] She concluded that Kelly "isn't the test for whether we're under absolute Despotism — he's the proof that we have been for a while."[2]

## Origins of the episode (November 2025)

The underlying incident was a 90-second video first posted early on Tuesday, November 18, 2025 from Senator [[elissa-slotkin|Elissa Slotkin]]'s X account, in which six lawmakers — all veterans of the armed services or intelligence community — spoke directly to U.S. service members.[3] The participants were Slotkin, Arizona Senator [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]], and Representatives [[jason-crow|Jason Crow]], [[chris-deluzio|Chris Deluzio]], [[maggie-goodlander|Maggie Goodlander]] and [[chrissy-houlahan|Chrissy Houlahan]].[3] Slotkin acknowledged that service members were "under enormous stress and pressure right now" and wrote that "the American people need you to stand up for our laws and our Constitution."[3]

On Thursday, November 20, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] accused the six of sedition "punishable by DEATH," reposting and amplifying messages about the video with his own commentary.[3] The lawmakers responded in a joint statement that same morning: "Every American must unite and condemn the President's calls for our murder and political violence. This is a time for moral clarity."[3] Some Democrats accused Trump of acting like a king and of trying to distract from the soon-to-be-released [[epstein-files|Epstein files]].[3]

## Badlands commentary

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] framed the confrontation as mutual innuendo, writing that the Democrats "didn't actually call for a military coup, they just implied it" while "King Trump didn't actually call for them to be hung, he just implied it," and urging everyone to stop overreacting.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Crow's inability to name a specific illegal order, combined with his insistence on vague warnings that confuse and demoralize service members and degrade readiness, demonstrated a seditious conspiracy for which the lawmakers should be court-martialed.[3]

## Department of War investigation into Kelly

On November 24, 2025, the Department of War opened a formal investigation into "serious allegations of misconduct" by [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]], the only member of the group with clear exposure under the [[uniform-code-of-military-justice|Uniform Code of Military Justice]] as a retired Navy captain in the Regular component.[4] The department reminded all individuals that military retirees remain subject to the UCMJ and that federal statutes such as 18 U.S.C. § 2387 prohibit actions intended to interfere with the loyalty, morale, or good order and discipline of the armed forces.[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] remarked that it was striking that the "astronaut" was the only one of the six with military legal exposure, and observed that [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]]'s wife — [[maggie-goodlander|Maggie Goodlander]] — was among the group, speculating that the Justice Department might use her as leverage over Sullivan in the manner reportedly used with Michael Flynn Jr.[4]
