# 2025 Canadian Federal Election

The 2025 Canadian federal election was a snap general election held on April 28, 2025, called by Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] after weeks of speculation when he asked the Governor General to dissolve Parliament.[1] The campaign was widely expected to be a sprint dominated by U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s trade war and his annexation threats toward [[canada|Canada]].[1]

## Opening of the campaign

Party leaders began campaigning on the first Sunday of the race, each framing an opening pitch around the economic pain caused by Trump's on-again, off-again tariffs.[1] Carney, as [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal]] leader, launched in Ottawa with a pledge to pass a middle-class tax cut reducing the marginal rate on the lowest income bracket by one percentage point and to diversify Canada's trade, then formally kicked off the campaign at a rally in St. John's.[1] Conservative leader [[pierre-poilievre|Pierre Poilievre]] launched from Gatineau, acknowledging that Trump had become a new political rival, and pledged tax cuts and expanded natural-resource production before heading to Toronto.[1] New Democratic Party leader Jagmeet Singh cast his party as the underdog best positioned to take on Trump, framing the election as a fight to protect workers' livelihoods.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the election with open derision, describing it as a "ceremonial contest" staged by what the author called globalists in Canada to give the public the perception of choosing their leaders.[1] The commentary argued that U.S. trade inequities with Canada account for more than three percent of the Canadian economy, that the United States absorbs roughly 75 percent of Canadian exports, and that Canada's economy is therefore highly dependent on the American market — while both major parties campaigned, in the author's framing, on continuing those arrangements.[1] The piece further argued that meaningful change would require stripping the Canadian elite of power, putting "Canada First for Canadians," and declaring independence from the monarch depicted on Canadian currency, a reference to [[king-charles-iii|King Charles III]].[1]

## Result

[[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] was elected prime minister of [[canada|Canada]] on 28 April 2025, according to projections by the national broadcaster CBC/Radio-Canada, in what NPR called one of the country's most consequential elections in decades.[2] On election night it remained unclear whether his [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party]] would win the 172 seats needed for an outright majority in parliament, with full results not expected until late Monday evening or early Tuesday morning.[2] The vote was widely seen as a decision about which candidate could best handle President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who had helped spark a wave of nationalism across Canada by threatening to annex the country and placing stiff tariffs on it.[2]

The 60-year-old Carney, whose career in investment banking preceded terms as governor of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] during the Brexit turmoil and head of the Bank of Canada during the 2008 downturn, had never held political office before being named Liberal leader in March; his financial background and unflappable demeanor persuaded voters he could best tackle Trump's sometimes erratic policies.[2] He beat [[pierre-poilievre|Pierre Poilievre]], the 45-year-old career politician who led the Conservative Party — a stunning reversal for a rival who had ridden high in the polls for more than a year, at one point up 27 points over the Liberals.[2]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] quipped that "Canadian Ron! DeeSantis goes the way of the standard Ron! DeeSantis, minus the high heels."[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that "the City on the Hill template is going to wreak havoc on the mind of the collectivist west as the Golden Age gets going," writing that as Americans go all in on sovereignty, decentralization and small government, the regime from Canada to the EU was doubling down.[2]
