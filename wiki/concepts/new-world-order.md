# New World Order

The **New World Order (NWO)** is the term used across [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary for a projected system of unified world government held to be the strategic objective of a globalist [[cabal|cabal]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) In "AI Can Help Us Expose the NWO" (22 January 2024), [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] treats the NWO as the primary strategic objective against which every lower-order operation and tactic in the [[toronto-protocols|Toronto Protocols]] can be read.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## The stated objective

The Protocols passage Deschamps quotes describes the aim as constructing a World Government — "a New World Order Community" — in which all individuals without exception are subject to the "World State" of the "New Order."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Two preconditions are named: first the removal of the family, which is expected in turn to produce the loss of ancestral religious teachings; and second the levelling of all individuals by removing the social classes, in particular the middle classes.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Crucially, all of this must proceed so that it is seen as arising from the popular will and carries the appearance of democracy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## The AI's decomposition

Asked to respond as a general studying [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], ChatGPT broke this into layers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) The primary strategic objective was to establish a world government superseding national governments.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Secondary objectives were to eradicate the family structure — read as a means of weakening social bonds not tied to the World Government and making individuals more dependent on the central authority — to eliminate social classes and especially the middle class, whose members often possess resources and education that could challenge such a system, and to maintain the appearance of democracy while potentially concentrating power in an authoritarian manner.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

At the operational level the model listed psychological manipulation, legal and political maneuvering, and [[social-engineering|social engineering]]; at the tactical level, propaganda, legislation and economic policies redistributing wealth or altering economic structures.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) The model appended caveats to each response, characterising the scenario as hypothetical and noting that such a project would face opposition from those who value democracy, individual liberties and cultural diversity.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## The Global Harmony Consortium

When Deschamps reframed the exercise as material for a science-fiction dystopian film script, the model produced a fuller and, in his assessment, more accurate map, naming the fictional cabal the [[global-harmony-consortium|Global Harmony Consortium]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) In that final synthesis the establishment of the New World Order remained the primary strategic objective — a unified world government presented as promoting peace and prosperity while consolidating power and control.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Its supporting programme incorporated [[satanic-inversion|satanic inversion]], [[esg|ESG scores]], the [[climate-change-narrative|climate change narrative]], [[critical-race-theory|Critical Race Theory]], [[queer-theory|Queer Theory]] and the multigenerational [[ideological-subversion|ideological subversion]] model described by [[yuri-bezmenov|Yuri Bezmenov]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## The dying order and the "New World"

By 2026 Badlands commentary had shifted from describing the New World Order as an ascendant project to describing it as a collapsing one. The ninth episode of [[badlands-blitz|The Blitz]], published 7 March 2026, was titled "The New World Order is Dying … The New World is Beginning," presenting the NWO's dissolution and the emergence of a successor order as two faces of a single transition.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] took what the post called a "40k-foot view" in order to reconcile the seeming contradictions at the heart of that transition, arguing that the apparent chaos of the moment was better read as the mechanics of a handover than as simple disorder.[2]

That framing was set against a week in which, as the post put it, the world seemed to be on the brink of both kinetic and financial catastrophe — conditions Badlands writers treat as characteristic of the terminal phase of the old order rather than of the new one.[2]

## TIME Magazine's May 2026 framing

In May 2026 [[time-magazine|TIME Magazine]] published an analysis framing the recent summit between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] as part of an emerging "new world order," emphasizing shifting geopolitical alignments, direct US–China engagement, and the restructuring of global power relationships.[3] The piece covered cooperation and negotiation on trade, AI governance, Taiwan tensions and international security, arguing that the summit signaled movement away from the post–Cold War order dominated by US-led institutions.[3] TIME's social media promotion amplified the framing, explicitly using the phrase "new world order" while describing the Trump–Xi convergence as a defining realignment moment amid debates over multipolarity, the future of NATO, and changing economic blocs involving Russia, Europe and the Indo-Pacific.[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the article as "a panic piece masquerading as a feature" and noted that he had predicted both the pivot and the exact terminology twelve days earlier in "Collapse of the Global Cartel."[3] In that essay he argued that "the very coalition the Globalists will label the 'New World Order' (Trump, Putin, Xi and the emerging and rapidly-renormalized Iranian keystone) is the one actually dismantling the REAL New World Order — the faceless Collectorate that has fed on coerced unity, engineered scarcity and perpetual proxy war for decades," closing with the formulation: "The New World Order is dead. Long live the New World Order."[3] He compared the episode to his July 2025 prediction that the globalist regime would rebrand the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] as the "New Axis," codified two months later when the US, Russia and China were dubbed the "Axis of Upheaval."[3]

## Infrastructure attacks as a strategic objective

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] described the collapse of nation states and installation of a New World Order as the enemy's "Primary Strategic Objective," arguing in March 2024 that "accidents" advancing that objective — such as the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse]] — should be treated as opportunities to confront the cognitive battlefield with intention.[4] He suggested that crippling centralized infrastructure disempowers the public as a means of centralizing power, and asked which globalist operations would exploit the resulting pretext, from climate NGOs seizing on damage to oil operations to government intervention in farming after fuel-driven food distribution failures.[4] His counter-prescription was decentralization: each attack on centralized systems, he wrote, teaches the public how to build a parallel economy less vulnerable to network warfare.[4]


## The 2024 Hague rulings and the "rules-based order" (Badlands framing)

Badlands commentary in May 2024 treated the standoff between the Hague courts, Israel and the European Union as evidence of the New World Order fracturing from within.[5] The brief argued that the phrase "international rules-based order," which it dated to the presidency of George H. W. Bush, describes a deliberate deviation from objective international law toward a one-world government, and that [[josep-borrell|Josep Borrell]]'s public anguish over whether the EU should back the [[international-court-of-justice|ICJ]] or Israel showed globalists turning openly against one another.[5] Writers concluded that "you are witnessing the systematic destruction of the Old Guard," urging readers to focus on defeating the globalist oligarchy rather than being distracted by red herrings.[5]

The same framing was applied to the passage of foreign-agent transparency laws in Slovakia and [[georgia-country|Georgia]], which the brief read as smaller nations defying the global hegemony, and to the alleged EU threat against Georgian prime minister [[irakli-kobakhidze|Irakli Kobakhidze]].[5]

## Nuclear fear and the road to global decision rights

In her July 2023 review of Christopher Nolan's *Oppenheimer*, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described a conflict between national and global decision rights that "must be dealt with through the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] to the New World Order."[6] The film's three themes — sympathetic [[communism|communists]], nuclear weapons as the destroyer of worlds, and [[global-governance|global governance]] as the only solution — are read as narrative preparation for the sacrifice of national sovereignty, with the [[nuclear-arms-race|nuclear arms race]] serving as the metaphor now attached to [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[6]
