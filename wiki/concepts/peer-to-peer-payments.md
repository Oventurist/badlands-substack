# Peer-to-Peer Payments

A peer-to-peer (P2P) service is a decentralized platform on which two individuals interact directly with each other without intermediation by a third party; buyer and seller transact directly, while the platform may supply services such as search, screening, rating, payment processing or escrow.[1] Applied to money, P2P services allow people to send funds through their phones directly to another person in moments, using only an email address or phone number.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats P2P payments as central to the [[decentralization|decentralization]] of banking, because they let people interact and send money without permission from third-party intermediaries and thereby place power in the hands of ordinary people.[1] He notes that [[financial-technology|fintech]] firms offering P2P services are not merely bypassing legacy banks but also competing with credit card companies.[1]

[[paypal|PayPal]] is identified as one of the first companies to offer P2P payment services over the internet, helping to launch e-commerce by transferring payments securely instead of requiring mailed checks.[1] [[cash-app|Cash App]], built by [[square-inc|Square]] and carried into [[block-inc|Block]], is the corresponding P2P service in [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]]'s ecosystem.[1] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] has described payments as an exchange of information with "not a huge difference" between sending a direct message on [[twitter|Twitter]] and sending a payment, envisioning users able to send money to anyone, anywhere, "instantly and in real time."[1] The [[web5|Web5]] proposal likewise rests on a peer-to-peer infrastructure of personal servers.[1]
