# US-Europe Trade Tensions

US-Europe trade tensions describe the deterioration in transatlantic economic relations during the Biden presidency, in which European officials concluded that American policy had tilted permanently away from their interests regardless of which party held the White House.[1]

## Background

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s 2021 declaration that "America is back" was welcomed by European officials eager to move past their trade troubles with the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1] Instead of reversing policies driven by Trump's protectionist view, however, Biden advanced many of them: he kept trade barriers in place, left European companies out of subsidies designed to bolster U.S. manufacturing, and surprised allies with tighter restrictions on Chinese access to American technology.[1]

Many Europeans feared that Trump, seemingly en route to the Republican nomination, might abandon Ukraine and [[nato|NATO]] and inject chaos into global trade.[1] They nonetheless came to believe that regardless of the winner of the expected Biden-Trump rematch, U.S. economic policies had tilted away from their favor. "The honeymoon is over," one European diplomat said.[1] Diplomats and officials across Europe were left wondering whether the bloc could rely on the United States to continue backing the rules-based trading system, or whether they faced the possibility of economic conflict between longtime allies.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]]'s reporting as confirmation that the Biden administration was being seen externally by EU allies as a continuation of the Trump administration "in all the ways that actually matter," specifically in foreign relations and trade.[1] This, he wrote, echoed the long-standing Badlands contention that the Biden administration had continued the Trump-era "Actuals" while being bathed in narratives catastrophic to its public reputation.[1]

He cited as evidence that tariffs remained in place on [[china|China]] and other former trade partners, that Trump's proposed [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS Act]] was signed into law by Biden, that U.S. drilling had resumed in the Gulf of Mexico and elsewhere, and that LNG shipments overseas had been curtailed in some respects and cut off entirely in others.[1] That "allies" in what he called the Globalist Alliance — the [[european-union|European Union]] — were openly panicked that a second Trump administration would "inject chaos into global trade" meant, in his reading, that it would rebalance trade along fair, sovereign lines with a first-principles approach.[1] His conclusion: "It's almost like Biden is President in name only. War. Of. Stories."[1]
