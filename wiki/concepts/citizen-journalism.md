# Citizen Journalism

Citizen journalism is the practice of ordinary members of the public "playing an active role in the process of collecting, reporting, analyzing, and disseminating news and information."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) It is the organizing identity of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] and the movement that, in the outlet's framing, will "write the epitaph" of the collapsing corporate press.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Definition

Scholar Courtney C. Radsch defines citizen journalism as "an alternative and activist form of news gathering and reporting that functions outside mainstream media institutions, often as a response to shortcomings in the professional journalistic field, that uses similar journalistic practices but is driven by different objectives and ideals and relies on alternative sources of legitimacy than traditional or mainstream journalism."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The definition is drawn from her work *The Revolutions will be Blogged: Cyberactivism and the 4th Estate in Egypt*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Relationship to decentralized media

Badlands treats citizen journalism as the human component of the broader [[decentralized-media|decentralized media]] ecosystem — media existing outside the handful of conglomerates that own the mainstream, "where there are no emissaries of the think tank class telling journalists what to write and how to frame it."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Its practitioners have contended with demonetization, deplatforming, public ridicule and even accusations of treason for allegedly spreading foreign state disinformation, alongside opposition from Big Tech search platforms and major financial institutions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Despite this, the article argues, the sector continues to grow rapidly — evidence both of "the power of the truth" and of public thirst for it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Prediction and precedent

The article credits the author and psychonaut [[terence-mckenna|Terence McKenna]] with anticipating the shift in 1996, when he declared that "mass media is finished… mass media is one-to-many communication, and what the internet offers is any-to-any."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Establishment response

Badlands argues that nothing frightens the Western establishment more than a capable and honest decentralized media, because a self-organizing industry of citizen journalists operating on principle would render the mainstream obsolete.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The response has been the construction of an entire apparatus dedicated to crippling independent media and suppressing dissent — the [[censorship-industrial-complex|Censorship Industrial Complex]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The emergence of that apparatus is read as an indicator of panic rather than strength.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Rejection of professional mystique

A recurring argument is that mainstream commentators inflate journalism into a difficult craft beyond the reach of untrained citizens. The article rejects this: journalism "is not some fine art," and a good piece of honest reporting is not a work to be marveled at for centuries, but simply a means of conveying information accurately and without bias.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Because the corporate press has abandoned that function, "the American people are doing it themselves" — summarized in the movement slogan "We are the news now."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)
