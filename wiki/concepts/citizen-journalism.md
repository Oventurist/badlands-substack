# Citizen Journalism

Citizen journalism is the practice of ordinary members of the public "playing an active role in the process of collecting, reporting, analyzing, and disseminating news and information," outside the institutional structures of the professional press. It is the organizing identity of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] and the central subject of A Swan Song for the MSM, the February 2024 essay by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]].

## Definition

The Badlands corpus adopts the standard definition above and supplements it with the scholarly formulation of Courtney C. Radsch, who described citizen journalism in *The Revolutions will be Blogged: Cyberactivism and the 4th Estate in Egypt* as "an alternative and activist form of news gathering and reporting that functions outside mainstream media institutions, often as a response to shortcomings in the professional journalistic field, that uses similar journalistic practices but is driven by different objectives and ideals and relies on alternative sources of legitimacy than traditional or mainstream journalism."

## Relationship to decentralized media

In the Badlands framing, citizen journalism is the human component of what the corpus calls decentralized media — outlets and individuals operating outside the handful of conglomerates that constitute [[mainstream-media|centralized media]]. DeLarme argues that the decentralized space has "no emissaries of the think tank class telling journalists what to write and how to frame it," and that this independence is the source of both its credibility and the hostility it attracts.

## Obstacles

According to the source, citizen journalists have faced demonetization, deplatforming, public ridicule, and even accusations of treason for allegedly spreading foreign state disinformation. Opposition is said to come not only from government but from Big Tech platforms and major financial institutions, with the article citing reports of Google restricting independent media in search results and GoFundMe freezing donations to [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]]. The systematic character of this opposition is what the corpus names the [[censorship-industrial-complex|Censorship Industrial Complex]].

## The claim of succession

The essay argues that despite these obstacles the independent sector "continues to grow at a rapid pace—that is the power of the truth, and a testament to how thirsty the American public is for it." It rejects the idea that journalism is a specialized art beyond ordinary citizens, insisting that honest reporting "simply serves as a means to convey information to the public accurately and without bias." Because the corporate press has abandoned that function, the article concludes, "the citizen journalist will write its epitaph" — summarized in the Badlands slogan "We are the news now."
