# 2025 Rescissions Package

The 2025 rescissions package was a formal request from the White House asking Congress to claw back billions of dollars in previously appropriated funds, primarily foreign aid and public broadcasting money, in line with spending reductions identified by the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]].[1] A rescissions request is a statutory mechanism allowing the executive branch to propose cancelling enacted budget authority, subject to congressional approval by simple majority.

## Senate discharge vote

On Tuesday, July 15, 2025, the Senate voted 51–50 to discharge the rescissions request from the Appropriations Committee and bring it to the floor, with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] casting the tiebreaking vote.[1] The vote proceeded over the objections of centrists who argued the cuts would undermine American influence abroad and global public health.[1] Three Republicans — [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] of Alaska, [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] of Maine, and [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] of Kentucky — voted no.[1]

Collins said her opposition stemmed from a lack of clarity about which programs were included in the foreign aid cuts and from her unwillingness to strip funding from local public television and radio stations.[1] "The rescissions package has a big problem; nobody really knows what program reductions are in it," she said, attributing the gap not to insufficient review time but to the White House Office of Management and Budget having "never provided the details that would normally be part of this process."[1]

The vote was Vance's sixth tiebreaker in the Senate, following his deciding vote on July 1, 2025 to pass the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill Act]].[1] Debate was expected to continue the following morning, with no clear timetable for a final vote.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the outcome as clarifying: the [[doge]] cuts advanced and voters could now identify the three GOP senators who sided with Democrats against the package, concluding that Republicans "can lose those three losers and still win if JD Vance shows up to work."[1] The measure is closely related to the broader [[public-media-rescission-of-2025]] fight over federal support for public broadcasting.[1]
