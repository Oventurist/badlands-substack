# Quiet on Set: The Dark Side of Kids TV

"Quiet on Set: The Dark Side of Kids TV" is a Max/Investigation Discovery documentary that explored the exploitation of children in Nickelodeon programming.[1] Its release in 2024 prompted mainstream commentary on abuse within the children's entertainment industry.[1]

## Bill Maher commentary

On an April 2024 episode of "Real Time" on HBO, [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]] addressed the documentary in a "New Rule" segment titled "Kid 'n Prey," a nod to the hip-hop duo Kid 'N Play.[1] Maher opened by noting that as one of the few people in the public eye who never had children, he was nonetheless always defending them, adding that "it's every adult's job to protect them."[1] He told the audience: "Nickelodeon — it wasn't a studio, it was Neverland Ranch with craft services," describing the documentary as "scene after scene, clip after clip of the child stars of their day being subjected to obviously inappropriate, highly sexualized degradation."[1] Maher used the segment to launch a wider critique of modern progressive gender politics and children's exposure to nontraditional gender identities, and also called out Disney.[1]

Badlands writer Burning Bright characterized the moment as "the systematic destruction of the Old Guard" being implemented largely by the Old Guard's own hand, noting that Maher is Hollywood establishment and that Anons and Truthers reserve disdain for a class of "protectors desperately attempting to rebrand themselves as exposers."[1] The commentary argued Maher's public about-face was carefully crafted, timed and planned, and asked whether it represented rats fleeing a sinking ship ahead of the 2024 election or "proxy puppets dancing to the tune of different composers."[1] It attributed part of the cultural turn within the Entertainment Industrial Complex to the momentum [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] began in 2016 and the America First movement, concluding that "[They] are losing" and are attempting to get ahead of exposures to come — "very slowly ... and then all at once."[1]
