# Trump v. CNN Defamation Lawsuit

Trump v. CNN was a $475 million defamation suit brought by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against [[cnn|CNN]], dismissed by a federal judge on Friday, July 28, 2023.[1]

## The claim

Trump's legal team argued that the network's statements about him likened him to Adolf Hitler and were therefore defamatory.[1] The lawsuit cited five examples of CNN's use of the term "The Big Lie" that his attorneys said the network had refused to retract; the phrase has historically denoted a propaganda technique so powerful that believers could not accept that its message had been distorted, and has popular origins in Hitler's memoir *Mein Kampf*.[1]

## The ruling

Trump-appointed U.S. District Judge [[raag-singhal|Raag Singhal]] ruled that Trump could not sue the outlet for defamation.[1] "Being 'Hitler-like' is not a verifiable statement of fact that would support a defamation claim," Singhal wrote in the dismissal; "CNN's statements, while repugnant, were not, as a matter of law, defamatory."[1] The judge added that bad rhetoric does not equate to defamation.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that defamation is extremely hard to prove and must rest on verifiably false statements, and argued that although CNN had made numerous false statements about Trump, the ruling raised the defamation bar higher still.[1] She contended that the Nazi trope and the Hitler comparison had become ubiquitous in the mainstream narrative and were undeniably damaging to Trump's character and reputation among the public, yet were dismissed by the court as merely repugnant.[1]

Ashe called the outcome chilling, describing it as proof that the regime media would never be held accountable for election interference and could openly defame political opponents without fear of repercussion — evidence in her framing of two standards of justice in the United States and a reason it was "more important than ever to stand against the regime."[1]
