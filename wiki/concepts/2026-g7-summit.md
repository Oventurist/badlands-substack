# 2026 G7 Summit

The 2026 [[g7|G7]] summit was held in Évian-les-Bains, [[france|France]], with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arriving on Monday, June 15, 2026.[1]

## Context and agenda

Trump arrived after announcing a framework agreement aimed at ending the recent conflict with [[iran|Iran]], giving him a major foreign-policy development heading into meetings with allied leaders.[1] The agenda was expected to focus on Iran, [[ukraine|Ukraine]], trade disputes, energy security, artificial intelligence, and [[china|China]]'s economic influence.[1] European leaders sought continued US engagement on security and economic matters, while Trump was expected to press for progress on Ukraine.[1]

## The G2 shadow

The summit was overshadowed by concern among European officials and analysts that consequential global decisions are increasingly shaped by the Washington–Beijing relationship — the [[g2|"G2"]] — rather than by traditional multilateral institutions.[1] Reporting noted that China's economic power continued to shape most of the issues under discussion, including trade, critical minerals, and AI.[1]

## Trump's remarks at the summit

Speaking about the Iran peace deal while in France, Trump said that he and Iran "get along very well."[1] In the same meeting with [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], Trump said he wanted to talk to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in order to bring resolution to the conflict in Lebanon.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the phrasing as echoing Trump's rhetorical pivot on Venezuela and predicted it would alarm figures such as [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]].[1]

## Criticism of Israel's Lebanon campaign

During remarks at the summit, Trump publicly criticized [[israel|Israel]]'s military campaign against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in [[lebanon|Lebanon]], stating that the operation had lasted too long, resulted in excessive civilian deaths, and involved unnecessary destruction of apartment buildings when targeting militants.[2] He noted his displeasure with an Israeli strike in Beirut that occurred two hours before the announcement of progress on the U.S.–Iran agreement, saying it cast a negative light on the broader diplomatic effort.[2] Trump suggested Israel allow [[syria|Syria]] under President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] to take the lead against Hezbollah, describing the Syrian leader as capable and claiming Syrian forces could do the job more effectively and with fewer civilian casualties.[2] Badlands contributors treated the remarks as a landmark moment in which a U.S. president affirmed the long-standing accusation that Israel kills civilians indiscriminately while pursuing combatants.[2]
