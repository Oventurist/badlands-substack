# Montgomery County LGBTQ Books Case

The Montgomery County LGBTQ books case was a dispute argued before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] in April 2025 concerning a Maryland school district's withdrawal of a parental "opt-out" option for storybooks with LGBTQ+ themes.[1]

The plaintiffs — Christian, Jewish and Muslim parents — argued that their right to religious expression, and with it their First Amendment rights, had been violated when the district removed the ability of parents uncomfortable with the reading selections to withdraw their children from those classes.[1]

During oral argument on 22 April 2025 Justice [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]], a lifelong resident of Montgomery County, said he was "surprised" by the case, noting that the county had long been a "beacon" for religious freedom: "I guess I'm surprised that this is the hill we're going to die on, in terms of not respecting religious liberty."[1] He added: "I guess I am a bit mystified, as a lifelong resident of the county, how it came to this."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] focused on Kavanaugh's use of the word "we," arguing that he was not surprised that Americans generally would fight over parental rights but that his own community had done so — a formulation she said "nicely punctuates who has the power in this dynamic."[1] She connected the case to a wider argument about returning educational governance to local communities, recounting a friend's concern that dismantling the Department of Education would leave Colorado's education leadership unchecked, and noting accounts of predatory teachers in that state.[1] Accepting local control, she wrote, means accepting "the grave powers of governance," and change happens only when citizens demand it.[1]
