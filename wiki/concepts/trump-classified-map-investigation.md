# Trump Classified Map Investigation

The Trump classified map investigation refers to a strand of the Justice Department's inquiry into former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s handling of classified material after leaving the White House, focused on a map containing sensitive intelligence information.[1]

## Reported facts

According to reporting by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] in April 2023, federal investigators questioned witnesses about whether Trump showed aides and visitors a map he took with him on departing office.[1] Four people with knowledge of the matter described the map as one focus of the broader [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] investigation, overseen by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], who was weighing charges that could include obstruction of justice.[1]

The nature of the map and the information on it was not clear.[1] One person briefed on the matter said investigators asked about Trump showing the map aboard a plane; another said the questioning suggested investigators believed he showed it to at least one adviser after leaving office.[1] A third said the map might also have been shown to a journalist writing a book.[1] The Washington Post had previously reported that investigators asked about Trump showing classified material, including maps, to political donors.[1]

## Badlands commentary on timing

[[just-human|Just Human]] argued in the Badlands News Brief that the story's timing was conspicuous, appearing simultaneously with an actual leak of more than 100 genuinely classified documents — including maps and current intelligence — onto the internet via a Discord server.[1] Had the map story run two or three weeks earlier, he wrote, US media would have devoted multiple news cycles to the gravity of presidential map handling and possible Espionage Act violations, only to be confronted with a far larger real leak.[1] He concluded that the article read like an intentional narrative sync that had been mistimed, asking whether it was "Simulation winking at us, or an intentional sync?"[1]
