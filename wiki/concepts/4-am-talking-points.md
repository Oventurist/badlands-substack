# 4 AM Talking Points

**"4 AM talking points"** is a term used throughout the [[q-operation|Q]] drops and associated commentary to describe messages allegedly distributed each morning to news organizations to set the day's narrative.[1]

In Chapter 1 of [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]], [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] introduces the term while discussing [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]], which Q references in Drops #3 and #4 and repeatedly thereafter.[1] Fleuret describes the talking points as messages "ostensibly sent to and by those who control the popular narrative," delivered by various messaging services or even by fax to news stations, and timed for roughly 4:00 a.m. so that outlets know what "the news" will be that day.[1]

Fleuret frames the practice as one component of a broader mechanism of narrative control that also operates through entertainment.[1] He argues that Hollywood saturates film and television with whatever agenda is being pushed, citing LGBTQ and transgender themes in entertainment as his example, and characterizes the combined apparatus as a means of slowly conditioning society over a long period.[1] He tells readers they will encounter the phrase repeatedly as the series unfolds.[1]

Anticipating the objection that the persistence of Operation Mockingbird undercuts claims that patriots are in control, Fleuret says such questions are fair and should be encouraged, but defers answering them so that the series can unveil information in the order the drops present it.[1]

## Usage in Badlands commentary

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] invoked the term in his February 2023 essay on [[speculation|speculation]], noting that repeated complaints that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should "keep his mouth shut" reminded him of local news casts reciting the same message with the same words and inflection, from a script he said some refer to as the "4am talking points."[2] Carlson used the observation to argue that scripted messaging conditions audiences on the Right as well as the Left — in the Right's case, conditioning them to lose hope.[2]
