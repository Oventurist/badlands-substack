# 2024 US Measles Outbreaks

The 2024 US measles outbreaks were a cluster of measles cases reported in early 2024 in California, Arizona and Chicago, in which case counts in the first two months of the year nearly matched the total for all of 2023.[1]

## Reported spread

Hundreds of people were feared infected in California and Arizona after confirmed cases appeared among patients who had visited local hospitals.[1] A separate outbreak was feared at an 1,800-person migrant shelter in Chicago, where four cases were confirmed, two of them in children.[1] Physicians warned that many younger doctors had never seen a measles patient, and that the public no longer "remembers what it was like to live with the disease" because vaccination had been so successful.[1] Vaccination rates had declined for a third consecutive year.[1]

Reporting on the outbreaks linked the risk to immigration, noting that millions of migrants had entered the United States under President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s policies and that experts warned overcrowded shelters combined with low vaccination rates in migrants' countries of origin could leave the country vulnerable.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the coverage as narrative construction intended to rally majority public support for vaccination.[1] She noted that measles had been effectively eradicated in the United States, a result credited to the MMR vaccine, and argued that the addition of MMR to the childhood immunization schedule correlated with rising rates of autism and childhood cancers — while acknowledging that correlation is not causation and that raising the connection invites the label of science denier.[1] She contended that public reaction to the COVID-19 vaccines had introduced millions of Americans to skepticism of pharmaceutical products for the first time, creating a need for a narrative shift, and that the emphasis on migrants in the measles coverage was designed to keep Americans divided while providing racially framed narrative cover.[1]
