# Shadow Banking Empire

**Shadow banking**, in the context of the 2026 US pressure campaign against [[iran|Iran]], refers to networks of shell companies, exchange houses, and front operations used to move tens of billions of dollars globally, evade sanctions, and fund military and proxy operations.[1]

## The 2026 Treasury action

In April 2026 the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|US Department of the Treasury]] sanctioned 35 individuals and entities tied to Iran's shadow banking network under [[operation-economic-fury|Operation Economic Fury]], including nodes linked to the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]].[1]

## Badlands framing

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the architecture being dismantled is not fundamentally Iranian but "the ancient mechanism by which the Shadow Banking Empire has operated since its Prussian origins—fused with British imperial precedent and perfected across the 20th century into the invisible rails of the modern Deep State."[1] In this reading, shell companies are not a defect of the system but its central feature, providing the plausible-deniability layer that allows the hegemon to project power without exposure.[1] Squeezing so-called Iranian nodes is therefore described as severing the broader financial sinews sustaining the unipolar construct, with the "Iranian" label serving as narrative cover.[1]

## 2025 regulatory warnings

Badlands contributor Ashe in America noted the irony that the same institutions warning about shadow banking systems in 2025 were those whose systemic controls the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] was undermining through deregulation and changes to U.S. economic policy, all while public attention was consumed by the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] controversy.[2] The specific vector of concern was [[private-credit|private credit]], which the Federal Reserve Bank of Boston described as systemically important and a potential source of systemic risk, alongside the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]]'s simultaneous drain of bank reserves via heavy bill issuance into the [[treasury-general-account|Treasury General Account]].[2]
