# Lankford–Rosen Antisemitism Bill (2026)

The Lankford–Rosen antisemitism bill is sweeping bipartisan legislation unveiled in the U.S. Senate in May 2026 by Sens. [[james-lankford|James Lankford]] (R-Okla.) and [[jacky-rosen|Jacky Rosen]] (D-Nev.), intended to combat rising antisemitism through new federal requirements in education, online platforms, and community security funding.[1]

## Provisions

The bill would require the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] to appoint an antisemitism coordinator and would impose new regulations on colleges receiving federal funding, including mandatory non-discrimination policies and grievance processes.[1] It would raise nonprofit security grants to $1 billion for Jewish communities and create a dedicated grant program for places of worship.[1] It would also mandate that online platforms disclose how they moderate and respond to antisemitic content.[1]

## Development and prospects

The measure was more than a year in development and is backed by groups including the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] and the American Jewish Committee.[1] A House companion bill was expected soon, with sponsors hoping to advance the package through vehicles such as the annual defense authorization bill.[1] Rosen and Lankford co-chair the Senate's bipartisan antisemitism task force, and Rosen framed the bill as a coordinated federal response to protect Jewish communities.[1]

## Criticism from Badlands

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he had anticipated such a bill for three years and called it "the Trojan Horse that will dismantle the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]]," leading to the destruction of the Bill of Rights and the formal end of the American Experiment.[1] He predicted it would be the most widely supported bipartisan bill since the Patriot Act and argued that this breadth demonstrated Israeli influence over [[congress|Congress]].[1] He pointed to Rep. Tim Burchett's appearance on the Daily Herold, where Burchett said claims of disproportionate Israeli influence over Congress are exaggerated even after being pressed on the bill's First Amendment implications, as evidence that "nobody is coming to save you" from the Republican Party.[1] He further argued that [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]]'s primary defeat would pressure reluctant Republicans into supporting the agenda, and called for defiance and ridicule in response.[1]
