# Carter Page FISA Warrants

The Carter Page FISA warrants are the four Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act applications and renewals obtained by the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to conduct surveillance on [[carter-page|Carter Page]], a volunteer foreign-policy adviser to the 2016 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] campaign.[1] Badlands Media treats them as the documentary core of [[spygate|Spygate]] and as the "classified documents 1-4" whose release to Congress was allegedly blocked by Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]].[1]

## Contents and signatories

Citing Law & Crime's analysis of the released material, the article notes that the warrants "flatly declare Page is a Russian agent, over and over again."[1] [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] signed on page 389, Rosenstein on page 391, Acting Attorney General [[dana-boente|Dana Boente]] on page 271, and [[james-comey|James Comey]] on page 296, with [[sally-yates|Sally Yates]]'s signature also appearing.[1] Badlands describes Rosenstein's signature as a decisive conflict of interest given that he later appointed a Special Counsel to investigate the same matter.[1]

## Declassification and release

Rosenstein was scheduled to testify before the House in June 2018 and had, according to the article, offered excuses for withholding the applications from House investigators.[1] Acting Attorney General [[matthew-whitaker|Matthew Whitaker]], working with the Office of the Inspector General, is said to have approved the release of the documents after that testimony, with publication following in July 2018 — timing the author reads as designed to expose Rosenstein once his sworn account was already on the record.[1]

## The underlying deception

The applications rested on the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]], funded by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and compiled by [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]].[1] Rosenstein claimed under congressional testimony that he had not fully read the application.[1] Badlands further asserts that Page was a CIA asset rather than a Russian one and that FBI attorney [[kevin-clinesmith|Kevin Clinesmith]] admitted altering an email to insert the phrase "and not a source," a falsification exposed by the [[john-durham|Durham]] investigation.[1] Page was never indicted despite being the nominal target of the surveillance.[1]
