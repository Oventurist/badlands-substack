# Integrated Country Strategy for Ukraine

The Integrated Country Strategy for Ukraine is a long-term United States planning document for Ukraine that exists in both a public and a longer confidential version. In October 2023, POLITICO reported on a "sensitive but unclassified" confidential version of the strategy indicating that Biden administration officials were far more worried about corruption in Ukraine than they publicly admitted.[1]

## Contents

The confidential document lays out numerous steps Washington was taking to help Kyiv root out malfeasance and reform an array of Ukrainian sectors. It stresses that corruption could cause Western allies to abandon Ukraine's fight against Russia's invasion and that Kyiv could not put off the anti-graft effort.[1] The document warns that "perceptions of high-level corruption" could "undermine the Ukrainian public's and foreign leaders' confidence in the war-time government" — starker language than appears in the little-noticed 22-page public version that the [[state-department|State Department]] had posted on its website with no fanfare about a month earlier.[1]

The confidential version runs roughly three times as long as the public one and contains many more details about U.S. objectives in Ukraine, ranging from privatizing its banks to helping more schools teach English and encouraging its military to adopt [[nato|NATO]] protocols. Many of the stated goals are designed to reduce the corruption that bedevils the country.[1] According to POLITICO, the quiet release of the strategy and the retention of the toughest language in the confidential version underscored the messaging challenge facing the Biden team.[1]

## Truth Community reading

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the leak as an instance of [[overtons-goalpost|Overton's Goalpost]] — a deliberate repositioning of acceptable discourse on Ukrainian corruption by the Biden regime.[1] The commentary drew out four points: that the administration sees Ukrainian corruption as significantly more impactful than it lets on publicly; that it recognizes the conflict as a "perception war" more than an actual one and fears a public loss of confidence in Ukraine; that it blames [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] for bringing corruption to the fore through Defense Ministry firings and oligarch arrests during a funding campaign; and that it signals intent to create a "national level resistance plan," which could mean U.S. support for an insurgency if Russia gains more territory — a step the author characterized as effectively terrorism.[1] The commentary added the caveat that, in the author's view, the Biden administration is not actually in control of any of this, but that the story that it is remains revealing.[1]
