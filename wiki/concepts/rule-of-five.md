# Rule of Five

The "Rule of Five" is an obscure federal law that allows a minimum number of members of certain congressional committees to demand records from federal agencies.[1] It entered public discussion in July 2025 when Senate Democrats invoked it in an attempt to force disclosure of government files on [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## The 2025 Epstein invocation

On Wednesday, July 30, 2025, Senate Minority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] and other Senate Democrats announced they would use the rule to compel the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to release additional documents related to the Epstein case.[1] Axios reported that congressional Democrats were seeking to capitalize on an issue that had dogged President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and opened fissures between him and some of his most ardent supporters.[1]

"Today's letter matters," Schumer said. "It's not a stunt, it's not symbolic. It's a formal exercise of congressional power under federal law."[1] He said the request covered "all documents, files, evidence and other materials" in the possession of the Justice Department and the [[fbi|FBI]] related to the case.[1] The [[white-house|White House]] did not immediately respond to a request for comment and the Justice Department declined to comment.[1]
