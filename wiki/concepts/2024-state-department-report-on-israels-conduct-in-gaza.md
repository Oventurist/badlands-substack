# 2024 State Department Report on Israel's Conduct in Gaza

The **2024 State Department report on Israel's conduct in Gaza** was an assessment prepared by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration to determine whether Israel had violated U.S. and international humanitarian law during the war in Gaza.[1] Its delay in early May 2024 became a focal point of congressional and media attention.[1]

## Background and stakes

The [[state-department|State Department]] had been working on the report for months.[1] It was to issue a determination on whether Israel had violated international humanitarian law since the war in Gaza began; if the finding was affirmative, the United States would be expected to stop sending Israel military assistance.[1]

## Delay

Three Senate aides and a House aide told Politico that the report had been delayed and would no longer be released on the previously planned Wednesday.[1] In an email, the administration notified Capitol Hill that it would miss the deadline without providing a clear reason, saying only that the report was "briefly delayed" and offering no specific timeline.[1] The aides were granted anonymity to discuss internal communications.[1]

## Context

The delay came alongside the administration's decision to hold up shipments of [[boeing|Boeing]]-made precision munitions to Israel; see the [[2024-us-weapons-holdup-to-israel|2024 US weapons holdup to Israel]].[1] It also came as the Saudi Foreign Ministry issued a rare public statement singling out Israeli settlers — a non-state actor — for attacks on Jordanian humanitarian aid trucks carrying food bound for camps in Rafah, attacks documented in videos circulating online.[1]

## Commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the report, rather than the weapons pause, was the real story, and that no possible finding would spare the administration political damage: affirming the allegations would abandon [[benjamin-netanyahu|Bibi Netanyahu]] to his critics, while exonerating Israel would in his view provide the pretext for renewed domestic unrest.[1] He predicted the latter outcome and characterized the administration as facing "nothing but bad choices" while trying to time the report's release against deteriorating optics in Gaza.[1]
