# Pediatric Gender Transition Treatment

Pediatric gender transition treatment refers to the medical and psychosocial interventions offered to minors diagnosed with gender dysphoria, a practice that became a central controversy in American culture-war reporting during 2023.[1]

## North Carolina reporting

Investigative journalist [[sloan-rachmuth|Sloan Rachmuth]] reported that North Carolina's leading medical institutions — [[duke-university|Duke]], UNC and ECU — were treating children as young as two, three and four years old.[1] Duke Medicine launched its Gender Clinic in 2015 under director [[deanna-adkins|Dr. Deanna Adkins]] to provide a comprehensive range of treatments for children as young as two.[1] Adkins told the Charlotte Observer in 2016 that her pediatric patients were "not old enough to consciously just choose" their condition, and that the distress "is not a choice in any of my patients."[1]

## Critique

Clinics typically state that "before puberty, treatment does not include any drug or surgical intervention," instead encouraging social transition.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that this framing sugar-coats an eventual pipeline into medical intervention, describing social transitioning as "grooming future customers to fuel the industry" and predicting that affected children will begin hormones on reaching legal age.[1] He cited the cost of an average phalloplasty at $25,000–$35,000 and noted that demand for such surgeries — which he said almost always result in life-long complications — had skyrocketed amid a decade of cultural push and social validation.[1] DeLarme characterized the sector as a "multibillion-dollar industry" with population-control implications, argued that practitioners should lose their licenses, and suspected "an even darker agenda at play here beyond making a few bucks."[1]

## St. Louis clinic and the research record

In May 2023 the [[washington-university-transgender-center|Washington University Transgender Center]] at St. Louis Children's Hospital tacitly confirmed some whistleblower claims and pledged vague changes amid investigations by Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] and Senator [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]], while exonerating itself of "substandard care causing adverse outcomes for patients."[2] Reporting framed the episode as part of the shroud coming off practices associated with so-called gender-affirming care for minors, including puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones, and the surgical removal of healthy breasts and genitals.[2] A study published in the fall of 2022 in JAMA Pediatrics concluded that breast removal improves "chest dysphoria" in young women who identify as men or nonbinary, but inexplicably hid its results pertaining to gender dysphoria — suggesting those findings did not align with the researchers' hypothesis — while an accompanying editorial declared top surgery in adolescents and young adults "effective and medically necessary."[2]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the transgender agenda took hold because the medical system is an "extrinsically based orthodoxy" vulnerable to infiltration, in which compartmentalized expert boards leave most doctors executing treatments they neither developed nor can critically assess.[2] He held that the resulting exposure of that weakness was a "ray of hope," and that the remedy for citizens is to discern truth personally through critical thinking and epistemology.[2]

## HHS evidence review (December 2025)

The [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] published a report titled *Treatment for Pediatric Gender Dysphoria: Review of Evidence and Best Practices*, whose co-authors — writing in Newsweek and identifying themselves as liberals — urged fellow liberals to read it despite widespread distrust of HHS on issues such as vaccines.[3] They argued that those on the left needed to pay special attention because many of the organizations and individuals the left trusts had gotten the issue wrong.[3]

The review's central historical claim is that the approach currently favored in the United States — puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones, sometimes followed by surgery — was rapidly adopted internationally largely on the basis of a Dutch experiment involving just 70 adolescents who had been gender nonconforming since early childhood, 62 of whom reported being exclusively same-sex attracted.[3] During that study one patient died of surgical complications attributed to having been given puberty blockers years earlier; some developed diabetes and were excluded from the final data analysis; others were lost to follow-up.[3] The remaining 55 patients underwent surgical transition upon reaching legal adulthood and all were rendered sterile.[3] The authors concluded that the practice euphemistically known as pediatric "gender affirming care" rests on methodologically weak research that sterilized a group of gay or bisexual youth and left one of them dead.[3] They further alleged that some leading clinician-researchers have buried unfavorable research and minimized concerning findings, including completed suicides among minors started on hormones.[3]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] expressed surprise that such an opinion appeared in Newsweek and described gender-affirming care as "the sexualization, sterilization, mutilation, and psychological torture — of children."[3] She rejected in advance any claim of victimhood by parents who consented, invoking the figure of "Munchausen Moms with their fad children" (see [[munchausen-syndrome-by-proxy|Munchausen syndrome by proxy]]) and arguing that resistance to harming one's own children is "biologically wired into parents. Unless those parents are psychos."[3]
