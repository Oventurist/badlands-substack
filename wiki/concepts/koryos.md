# Koryos

The **Koryos** are described in the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — Agents from Russia" as an ancient cult, active from roughly 2000–1000 BC, whose home base lay in the territory occupied today by Ukraine, and which used the wolf as its central theme.[1]

## Description in the source

The essay states that the Koryos shared elements of language with other peoples including the Etruscans and the Scythians, and that traces of their influence can be found worldwide — including, the author claims, on the "so-called 'undiscovered'" American continent.[1] From ancient times until roughly 1000 AD they are said to have ridden into Asia and Europe to raid their more peaceful, agrarian neighbors, producing nothing themselves and spending much of the year using drugs between raids.[1] They are described as entering a "berserk" trance under the full moon and wearing wolf pelts on their heads while pillaging.[1]

## Continuity claim

The author argues the cult did not disappear, pointing to the coat of arms of the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] — which depicts a wolf in sheep's clothing — as surviving symbolism of the same tradition.[1] He connects the Koryos to the ancient kingdom of [[khazaria|Khazaria]], said to hail from the same region and to have pioneered nomadic horseback warfare, and suggests the Carpathian mountains were at one point a key base.[1]

## Linguistic and mythological arguments

The essay proposes that the titles *Caesar*, *Kaiser* and *Khazar* may be related, and notes that Romulus, the mythical founder of Rome, was said to have been suckled by a she-wolf alongside his twin Remus, whom he killed.[1] It also asks whether the shared "-aria" ending in Tartaria, Khazaria and Bulgaria reflects an "Are-an" wolf cult imposing its language on subject peoples.[1]

## Recommended sources cited in the article

The author recommends the books *Wicked Teeth: the Secret History of Werewolves* and *The Horse, the Wheel and Language* for detail on the wolf cult's history and influence.[1]

## Creation myth parallels

A later installment of the series, "Creation Myth Clues," describes the Koryos as a cult from the region known today as Ukraine, Romania and Western Russia that groomed children and wore wolf pelts on their heads while committing bizarre and berserk acts upon neighboring peoples.[2] The author states that evidence of these cultists can be found all over the world and throughout history, and argues that the "cultural revolution" of the 1960s signaled the ascendancy of their ancient practices within modern culture, since they were devoted to sex, drugs, occultism and grooming children while behaving as unproductive parasites.[2]

The same article holds that the Koryos arrived in Western and Southern Europe in waves over many centuries prior to the founding of Rome, bringing a culture of debauchery, drug use, demon worship and child grooming — a culture the author connects to the Roman aristocracy and to [[bacchus|Bacchus]], the god from whose name the word *debauchery* derives.[2] The she-wolf that suckled [[romulus-and-remus|Romulus and Remus]] and the wolf-woman of the Mayan [[popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]] are offered as surviving traces of the same tradition.[2]

## Subterranean and regional claims

The article ties the cult's homeland to the [[black-sea|Black Sea]], where an earlier installment proposed a portal to hell may be located, noting the sea's unusual sulphur content and the traditional association of sulphur with demons.[2] Combined with the [[moho-discontinuity|Moho Discontinuity]], the author suggests that something subterranean "surfaced" in that region, which would help explain why it produced the werewolf cult.[2] He further observes that the Koryos homeland in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] is widely described as a hotbed of cabal activity today, and argues that although the Koryos are not the only influence on the cabal, they have been conspicuously ignored by researchers even though their practices, symbols and beliefs are clearly at work in modern society.[2]

## Description as red-haired cannibals

The installment "On Languages and Cover-ups" identifies the Koryos as the original werewolf cult and states that its members were said to be red-haired albino cannibals, while noting that many of the peoples they conquered and assimilated into their ranks did not fit that description.[3] The essay treats this as highly significant in light of Maori origin stories, which speak of a time when red-haired, man-eating giants roamed the land of New Zealand, and promises to return to the parallel later in the series.[3]

The same piece restates the framing that the modern dark occultists who run the [[deep-state|Deep State]] belong to a hidden lineage going back many thousands of years, likely the origin of werewolf symbolism together with its closely related vampire and zombie symbolism, and that although the Koryos probably no longer exist in their original form their beliefs and practices have clearly influenced the Deep State of today.[3] The author argues that learning this material is useful for helping anons decode current events even though it reaches back into antiquity.[3]

## First introduction in the series

The Koryos were first introduced in the opening installment of [[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] series, published March 18, 2024, which states that the very archetype of the werewolf may come from an ancient death cult whose origins appear to lie in the Western Russia–Ukraine region.[4] That article describes the cult as going on killing sprees under every full moon while usually wearing wolf pelts on their heads, and presents this as evidence that fairy tales and mythology contain more clues about hidden history than people tend to assume.[4]

## Zombie-making and cyber-necromancy

Part 8 of the series, "Modern Day Cyber-Necromancy" (August 20, 2024), recalls the argument made in Part 3 that the Koryos werewolf cult — and similar cults whose influences the author regards as salient in the modern [[deep-state|Deep State]] — are intentionally trying to manifest hell on Earth.[5] It proposes that one of the key methods for accomplishing this is the practice of creating [[zombification|zombies]] through rituals, robots and ghosts used in combination, which the author terms [[cyber-necromancy|cyber-necromancy]].[5] The installment also restates the series' position that werewolf, vampire and zombie symbolism all point to this single cult.[5]

## Scythian aristocracy and worldwide elite transmission

Part 6 of the series, "Our Vast, Undiscovered History" (June 20, 2024), states that evidence of the cult can be found in Ukraine, Romania, among the [[aztecs|Aztecs]], and in places as geographically distant as Africa and New Zealand, that they existed as far back as 2000–3000 BC, and that their influence is observable in the music industry and Hollywood to this day.[6] Following *Wicked Teeth* by [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]], it identifies the Koryos as [[scythians|Scythian]] and specifically as the aristocracy of the Scythians, adding that the cult interbred with the aristocracies of other peoples — the article's stated explanation for why Koryos-style beliefs and rituals are practiced by "elites" the world over, producing a worldwide two-tier system of government based in dark occult practice.[6]

The same installment describes the cult as famous for using the lure of drugs and sex to seduce primarily young people into its ranks, and as famous for grooming children.[6] Everything the Koryos did is characterized as defying nature and inverting traditional morality: they worshipped hermaphroditism, abolished traditional family formation among themselves, paired men with men and women with women, and believed a person could switch sexes merely by imagining themselves to be the opposite sex.[6] The Koryos are said to have asserted that they were possessed by demons who commanded them to love only members of their own sex, which the author offers as an explanation for Scythian extinction.[6] Many ancient fairy tales are read as veiled warnings about the cult, with witches' "magic potions" interpreted as drug cocktails used to intoxicate and seduce recruits.[6]

## Demonic-origin and Aztec claims in Part 6

The author advances a personal theory that the cult's origin is non-human, comparing its influence to the cordyceps fungus that hijacks ants into self-destruction and arguing that a demonic force uses people as a host to wage war against mankind — the likely origin, in his account, of the Dark Cabal or [[deep-state|Deep State]].[6] He locates a "major outbreak of evil" around the Black Sea and suggests the Scythian Koryos, whom he puns as "the Sith," served as its vector.[6] Citing [[political-ponerology|Political Ponerology]], he adds that the Koryos, like most psychopaths, believed themselves a superior or more evolved species, and states his suspicion that the Q team and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] are battling a modern incarnation of the cult.[6] The article further holds that the cult's progenitors were red-haired albinos, noting Montezuma's depiction with red hair and the Aztecs' ritual blood sacrifice as blood-feeding of the "gods" in exchange for material abundance and worldly power.[6]

## Twelve essential features of the cult

Part 3 of the series, "Hell On" (April 22, 2024), sets out twelve essential indicators by which the cult's presence and activities may be detected, recommending *Wicked Teeth: the Secret History of Werewolves* for the full treatment.[7] The list holds that the Koryos engaged in ritualised extreme violence, historically slaughtering villages under the full moon while wearing wolf pelts on their heads — the likely true source of werewolf mythology; that they cannibalised children, which the author puns as "canni-BA'AL-ize"; that the spilling of blood was a key ritual element; and that they frequently linked sex and death, so that sacrifice rituals doubled as sex orgies.[7]

The remaining indicators are that they combined spirit possession with violence and drug use; that breaking civilizational taboos wherever they found them was an important part of their occult practice; that they saw themselves as apex predators removed from ordinary humanity; that they dressed as animals for rituals symbolising possession by "Earth-bound" spirits; that, much like communists, they treated perpetual revolution against all forms of tradition as their mission; that they used the colour red to symbolise their essence; that they worshipped demonic beings regarded as their ancestors and allowed themselves to be taken over by them; and that they groomed children to become their mercenaries.[7]

The author observes that this combination of sex, death, blood and possession, together with gore and animal motifs, recurs in contemporary popular music videos, and argues that linking sex — the means by which physical life is created — to death amounts to a deliberate insult to God's creation, making the cult, like the voices reported by [[jerry-marzinsky|Jerry Marzinsky]]'s patients, virulently anti-God.[7]

## Symbolic hierarchy and the hell thesis

"Hell On" identifies the Koryos as a primary vector through which [[hell|hell]] has been invading this realm since the beginning of recorded history, describing the cult as one of "hell's proxy armies" and a driving force in world politics from antiquity to the present.[7] Drawing on former satanist [[mark-passio|Mark Passio]], the author proposes that vampires represent the cult's royalty, werewolves its mercenaries and zombies the general population, as though an infernal hierarchy had been instantiated in this world.[7]

The same installment compares the Koryos — famous, in the author's account, for producing nothing of value while doing drugs between raids on neighbouring villages — to the drug-and-music culture that emerged in the West after the 1960s cultural revolution championed by [[timothy-leary|Timothy Leary]], holding that both the Koryos and the social engineers of the twentieth century hate traditional family values.[7] He notes that commenters on earlier installments pointed out that the Ukraine area is also where the Prussian army originated.[7]

## Possession, ghost-making and child weapons

Part 7 of the series, "Hell is Organized" (August 8, 2024), presents the Koryos as the original werewolf cult, hailing from the part of the world where Ukraine, Western Russia and Romania are located, and raises the possibility that they never left.[8] The author describes them as a distinct predator class with their own culture and history, whose psychology is for practical purposes alien to most human beings, so that mainstream models of anthropology and psychology are insufficient to understand them — normal people cannot fathom participating in satanic [[child-sacrifice|child sacrifices]], whereas these cultists built an entire culture around such activity.[8]

The installment's central mechanism is voluntary possession: the Koryos are said to have intentionally permitted [[earthbound-spirits|earthbound spirits]] to inhabit their bodies in the belief that this granted supernatural abilities including superhuman strength.[8] Combining this with the claim that sacrifice rituals manufacture controllable ghosts, the author proposes an ancient science of mind control in which tortured and groomed children become ferocious spirits who then enter the bodies of other cult members to imbue them with the same demonic ferocity.[8]

Following the anonymous author [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]]'s *Wicked Teeth: the Secret History of Werewolves*, the article states that the Koryos recruited children and groomed them into killers through drug-induced ritual means, likened to a "proto [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]] program for kids," and that they used children, corporeal or otherwise, as weapons.[8] The destruction of a person's will and its replacement with the will of the cult is symbolised by [[zombification|zombification]], with bands of young boys sent like mindless hordes to massacre nearby villages.[8] Because the cult prized homosexual over heterosexual bonds, the article argues, it had to recruit children rather than produce them.[8] The author speculates that the progenitors of MK Ultra may be direct inheritors of the werewolf tradition, and cautions that his account is one piece of the puzzle rather than a theory of everything.[8]

## Counterpart to a benevolent society

Part 5 of the series, "The Ancient Origin of Modern Conflict" (May 30, 2024), summarises the cult for newcomers as an ancient group hailing from the region known today as Ukraine, more likely than not still in operation, and behind the phenomenon many call the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[9] It states that the Koryos are almost certainly the original source of werewolf symbolism and have retained some degree of global influence over mankind for many thousands of years, naming [[wicked-teeth|*Wicked Teeth: the Secret History of Werewolves*]] as one of the few detailed sources on them.[9]

That installment adds that the Koryos are not the only group with worldwide influence, positing a second, benevolent globally influential society of which the [[donald-trump|Trump]] and [[john-f-kennedy|Kennedy]] families are said to be royal descendants and to which much [[tartaria|Tartarian]] architecture is attributed.[9] The red hair traditionally ascribed to the Koryos is invoked again alongside depictions of [[montezuma|Montezuma]] with red hair, prompting the author to ask whether the Saxons of the [[saxony|Saxony]] correspondences were an ancient counterbalance to the Koryos, whether the two groups are related, or whether both hold in part.[9] He concludes that the "good guys" and "bad guys" appear more closely related than one would assume, while insisting this implies no overlap in aim or practice.[9]

## Clown symbolism and the astral counterpart

Part 11 of the series, "What is Clown World?" (February 25, 2025), proposes a link between the Koryos and clown imagery, building on [[rogue-hypnotist|Rogue Hypnotist]]'s report that the early Koryos werewolf cult were described as giant red-haired albinos and suggesting that the powder-white, red-haired clown costume may be inspired by or otherwise connected to the cult.[10] It cites as supporting material the claims that [[genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]] and Montezuma were red-haired albinos and Maori traditions of red-haired albino giants who ate children, which the author speculates may have been done to obtain [[adrenochrome|adrenochrome]].[10]

The installment reframes the cult's [[ancestor-worship|ancestor worship]] as possession by the "first creators" of the werewolf bloodline rather than by literal forebears, exchanging access to a physical body for superhuman strength.[10] On this basis the author speculates that the Koryos are a "physicalized" version of ancient clown-demons, and that pale-faced, red-haired jesters reported by users of psychedelics in the [[astral-plane|astral realm]] are their non-physical correlate — "two branches of the same family," perhaps one and the same.[10] He notes that red, the dominant hue of the astral plane according to [[frederick-dodson|Fred Dodson]], was also a sacred color to the Koryos and a prominent circus color.[10] The article concludes that [[clown-world|Clown World]] conspicuously resembles the kind of world the Koryos werewolf cult would have humanity live in.[10]
