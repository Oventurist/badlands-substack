# Nazi Ratlines

The **"ratlines"** were unofficial escape routes used by thousands of Nazis and other fascist war criminals to flee Europe after the Second World War.[1] Up to 10,000 such figures — including Adolf Eichmann and Josef Mengele — escaped justice by fleeing to Argentina and other countries.[1]

## Argentine declassification (2025)

Argentine President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]] promised officials of the [[simon-wiesenthal-center|Simon Wiesenthal Center]] his full cooperation in granting access to documents related to the financing of the ratlines.[1] The commitment was made in Buenos Aires at the presidential palace, Casa Rosada, during a meeting with Milei and activists.[1]

For decades, organizations including the Simon Wiesenthal Center — named after the famed Nazi hunter — have sought records relating to these escape routes.[1] Rabbi Abraham Cooper, associate dean of the Center, told The Times of Israel that while some previous Argentine leaders had promised full cooperation, "Milei is the first to act with lightning speed to enable the SWC to uncover important pieces of the historic puzzle, especially as it related to involvement with Nazis before, during and after the Holocaust."[1]
