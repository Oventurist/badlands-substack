# Shield of the Americas

The "Shield of the Americas" is a regional coalition led by the United States that issues joint diplomatic statements on hemispheric political and electoral matters.[1] It is sometimes referred to in shorthand as the "nations of SHIELD."[1]

## July 2026 Colombia statement

On July 10, 2026, the United States and twelve regional partners — Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guyana, Honduras, Panama, Paraguay, and Trinidad and Tobago — issued a joint statement through the coalition voicing alarm over Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s attempts to challenge the outcome of [[colombia|Colombia]]'s June 21 election, won by Abelardo de la Espriella.[1] Petro reportedly claimed the true winner was Iván Cepeda and alleged the result had been manipulated using technology from California.[1]

The coalition stated that the sovereign will of citizens, expressed at the ballot box and certified by electoral authorities, is the sole basis of legitimate government, and rejected any effort to delegitimize the mandate, discredit electoral authorities, or obstruct Colombia's institutional transition process.[1] It urged Colombian officials to respect the certified results and to ensure a peaceful, orderly and transparent transition.[1] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] shared the statement publicly, and it was released through the [[state-department|State Department]] Office of the Spokesperson.[1]

The operative language read: "In every constitutional democracy, the sovereign will of the citizens — freely expressed at the ballot box and formalized by the competent electoral authorities — constitutes the sole foundation of the legitimacy of public power," and further: "We reject any action, statement, or decision that seeks to delegitimize the mandate conferred by the citizens, to discredit without basis the competent electoral authorities, or to obstruct the institutional transition."[1]

## Badlands critique

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the SHIELD language closely resembled the [[electoral-confidence-preservation-act|Electoral Confidence Preservation Act]], a fictitious statute invented for the Badlands Jury exercise debuted with [[jonathan-drake|Jonathan Drake]] at the Deadwood [[gart|GART]], which criminalized doubting the delivery mechanism of democracy.[1]

Her argument inverted the coalition's premise: if elections are the sole foundation of the legitimacy of public power, then fake elections are treason at the most foundational level, and those holding power under a fake election are illegitimate.[1] She further noted the escape hatch in the phrase "discredit without basis" — claiming mere incompetence is the plausibly deniable way to beat a treason charge, which should not be permissible if the public-private elections industry has knowingly enabled fake elections.[1] Discrediting authorities *with basis*, she wrote, requires only a public hearing, in America, Colombia and anywhere else that grounds legitimate power in the consent of the governed.[1]

The Brief tied the statement to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announced Speech to the Nation, widely speculated to address newly declassified intelligence on foreign interference in the 2020 election.[1]

## Special Envoy appointment (March 2026)

On March 6, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described The Shield of the Americas as "our new Security Initiative in the Western Hemisphere," to be announced that Saturday in Doral, Florida.[2] He named outgoing Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] as its Special Envoy, moving her out of [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] effective March 31, 2026, when Oklahoma Senator [[markwayne-mullin|Markwayne Mullin]] was to take over the department.[2]
