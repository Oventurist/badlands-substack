# Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP)

The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) is the federal food assistance program, created to fight hunger, which provides food-purchasing assistance to more than 42 million individuals.[1]

## Criticism over junk food subsidies

Critics argue that while SNAP successfully addresses food insecurity, it has become a driver of a new public health crisis of obesity and chronic disease by subsidizing junk food purchases with taxpayer dollars.[1] Studies cited in 2025 indicated that nearly one-quarter of SNAP benefits are spent on sodas, chips, snack cakes and desserts, contributing to worse diets and higher obesity rates among recipients than in the general population.[1]

Efforts by states including Minnesota, California and New York to limit junk food purchases with SNAP benefits have been rejected by the [[usda|U.S. Department of Agriculture]].[1]

## Reform legislation

In March 2025 Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] introduced the [[nutritious-snap-act|Nutritious SNAP Act of 2025]], which sought to restore personal responsibility and state flexibility to the program.[1] Badlands commentary placed the SNAP debate alongside Tenet, "Binders" and election-integrity controversies as issues on which a number of self-described MAGA and alt-media influencers had run afoul of their own constructed narratives.[1]

## Candy and soda restriction debate (2025)

In March 2025, proposals to bar the use of SNAP benefits to purchase candy and soda gained national attention.[2] Supporters framed the restrictions as a way to push recipients toward healthier choices and to bring the program back in line with its intended nutritional purpose, while opponents argued the effort was a first step toward eliminating the program altogether.[2] Karen Siebert, a public policy adviser for a Kansas food bank network, said momentum for changing SNAP had never been higher, in part because lobbying groups were drawing headwind from the [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] movement spearheaded by Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]].[2] Siebert said groups opposing food stamps had spent a decade coming into states like Kansas to chip away at the program, and that the 2025 push felt especially like a "David and Goliath" situation, adding that "restricting food choices within SNAP is not going to have the health outcomes that people are looking for."[2]

### Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary noted that a cluster of conservative influencers posted in defense of keeping soda eligible for SNAP purchases at roughly the same time, in apparent unison.[2] [[nick-sorter|Nick Sorter]] and others reported that such influencers were being offered upwards of $1,000 per post to advocate for soda companies, with [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] noting that Coca-Cola earns billions of dollars annually from soda bought with SNAP funds — a strong lobbying incentive — and arguing that taxpayer money should not fund what he called "shit soda and GMO poison products."[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] added that defending the use of welfare money for junk food was "totally unprincipled," not conservative, and amounted to a defense of the underlying spending itself.[2]

## Partial funding during the 2025 shutdown

On Monday, November 3, 2025, with the federal government shutdown in its 34th day, the Trump administration announced it would provide partial relief to food-stamp recipients, with the [[department-of-agriculture|U.S. Department of Agriculture]] using contingency funds to maintain SNAP benefits at 50 percent of usual funding levels.[3] The decision followed a court order from Judge John McConnell of the U.S. District Court in Rhode Island, who ruled that the administration could not deny SNAP funds during the shutdown.[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on Truth Social, "If we are given the appropriate legal direction by the Court, it will BE MY HONOR to provide the funding."[3]

The administration had previously argued that tapping the SNAP contingency fund was legally restricted to scenarios such as natural disasters, but Judge McConnell rejected that argument as "arbitrary" and instructed officials to begin releasing funds by midday Monday.[3] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said funds could start flowing by Wednesday, though some states might face delays in distributing benefits.[3]

## Restoration after the 2025 shutdown

Following the deal that ended the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|record-long government shutdown]], the Trump administration said millions of low-income Americans should receive full food aid benefits within a day of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signing the measure into law.[4] Once the government reopened, most states would receive the funds to distribute benefits "within 24 hours," USDA spokesperson Alec Varsamis said in a statement on Wednesday, November 12, 2025.[4] The deal restored SNAP, the nation's largest anti-hunger initiative, to its original spending levels, a relief for the nearly 42 million people who had experienced major disruptions to their typical monthly benefits during the lapse.[4] Reporting at the time nonetheless cautioned that the timing of full benefit delivery would vary by state.[4]

## Junk food waivers (August 2025)

In August 2025, six additional states received federal waivers allowing them to bar soda, candy and other high-sugar junk foods from being purchased with SNAP benefits, a development hailed as a win for [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] advocates.[5] The waivers, which amend the statutory definition of food eligible for purchase under the federally funded but state-operated program, went to West Virginia, Florida, Colorado, Louisiana, Oklahoma and Texas, with the new restrictions due to take effect in 2026.[5]

The six waivers brought to twelve the number of states that had sought to restrict SNAP junk-food purchases; the others — Nebraska, Iowa, Indiana, Arkansas, Idaho and Utah — had received waivers from the Trump administration earlier in the year.[5] Badlands commentator [[canncon|CannCon]] summarized the policy as "You can still get soda...you just have to use YOUR money!"[5]
