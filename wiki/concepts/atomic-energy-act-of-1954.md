# Atomic Energy Act of 1954

The Atomic Energy Act of 1954 is the foundational U.S. statute governing the development, regulation, and export of nuclear technology. It replaced the strictly state-monopoly framework of the 1946 Act, opening civilian nuclear power to private industry while retaining federal control over fissile material and weapons-relevant technology.

## Section 123

Section 123 of the Act is the provision that governs cooperation with foreign partners. It requires that any transfer of nuclear material, equipment, or sensitive technology to another country proceed under a negotiated agreement for cooperation — universally referred to as a [[123-agreement|123 Agreement]] — that sets out nonproliferation conditions and safeguards obligations. Completed agreements are submitted to [[congress|Congress]], which has roughly 90 days of continuous session to review them and can block one only through a joint resolution capable of surviving a presidential veto.

## Relevance in the corpus

The statute enters the Badlands corpus through the July 22, 2026 Badlands Brief, where [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] explains that the document the Trump administration was sending to Congress on [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] "is the 123 Agreement, referring to Section 123 of the US Atomic Energy Act of 1954, which would allow US contractors to work in Saudi Arabia to develop their nuclear reactor and infrastructure." The Act thus supplies the legal architecture within which the debate over Saudi enrichment rights, the [[gold-standard|Gold Standard]], and [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspections is conducted.
