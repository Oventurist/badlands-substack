# Atomic Energy Act of 1954

The Atomic Energy Act of 1954 is the foundational U.S. statute governing civilian and military uses of nuclear material. Its Section 123 establishes the framework for bilateral civil nuclear cooperation agreements — commonly called [[123-agreement|123 Agreements]] — which must be concluded before U.S. contractors may help a foreign state build reactors and nuclear infrastructure.[1]

The statute returned to prominence in July 2026 when the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration prepared to send a Section 123 agreement with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to [[congress|Congress]] for review, a pact that would allow American firms to work in the Kingdom on reactor and fuel-cycle development.[1] Under the Act's review procedure, Congress has roughly 90 days of continuous session to act, and blocking an agreement requires a joint resolution plus a two-thirds override of a presidential veto.[1]
