# BlackRock Panama Canal Ports Deal

The BlackRock Panama Canal ports deal is a $23 billion transaction announced on March 4, 2025, under which the Hong Kong-based conglomerate [[ck-hutchison|CK Hutchison Holdings]] agreed to sell the units operating ports near the [[panama-canal|Panama Canal]] to a consortium including [[blackrock|BlackRock Inc.]], after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] alleged Chinese interference with the operations of the critical shipping lane.[1]

## Structure of the transaction

In a regulatory filing, CK Hutchison said it would sell all shares in Hutchison Port Holdings and all shares in Hutchison Port Group Holdings.[1] The two units hold 80% of the Hutchison Ports group, which operates 43 ports in 23 countries, including two of the four major ports along the Panama Canal.[1] The deal gives the BlackRock consortium control over those 43 ports across 23 countries, including Mexico, the Netherlands, Egypt, Australia and Pakistan.[1] The consortium — comprising BlackRock, Global Infrastructure Partners and Terminal Investment Limited — will acquire 90% interests in Panama Ports Company, which owns and operates the ports of Balboa and Cristobal in Panama.[1]

BlackRock CEO [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]] said in a joint announcement that the agreement was "a powerful illustration of BlackRock and GIP's combined platform and our ability to deliver differentiated investments for clients," adding that "these world-class ports facilitate global growth."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the deal against Fink's long arc with Trump: his 2017 resignation from Trump's Strategic and Policy Forum after Charlottesville, his 2018 embrace of environmental, social and governance (ESG) investing in his annual letter to CEOs, his doubling down on ESG during the Biden administration, a softening of that stance by 2022, and BlackRock's 2024 withdrawal from the Net Zero Asset Managers alliance as Trump's return looked likely.[1] Since Trump assumed office, BlackRock has further distanced itself from DEI, including removing DEI-related language from its annual review process.[1] The open question posed was whether BlackRock had bent the knee or was infiltrating — with the commentary concluding that nothing in the deal made her trust BlackRock any more than before.[1]
