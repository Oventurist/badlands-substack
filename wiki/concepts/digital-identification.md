# Digital Identification

Digital identification refers to state- or institution-issued electronic credentials that authenticate a person's identity for access to services, payments, travel and health systems. In the Badlands Media account, digital ID is the keystone of an emerging "techno-fascist" architecture, because nearly every other initiative in that architecture depends on it.[1]

## Why it is treated as central

Badlands argues that the push for digital identification is "ramping up and gaining steam" because it is an essential component of the world the elites are constructing: most of the other dystopian initiatives — storing [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDCs]], tracking biometrics and vaccination status, unlocking the door to a smart home — are expected to run through a digital ID, making possession of one a requirement to participate in the system at all.[1]

The concept is not new. Badlands characterizes it as a decades-old "conspiracy theory" turned reality, pointing to filmmaker [[aaron-russo|Aaron Russo]]'s claim that Nicholas Rockefeller told him in the late 1990s that the elite intended to get everybody "chipped" with an RFID chip.[1] What was once ridiculed, the article argues, is now marketed as a lifestyle choice, citing a viral video in which a user praises the microchip implanted in her arm.[1]

## The institutional rollout

Badlands describes a "trifecta" of globalist institutions advancing digital ID in parallel.[1]

The [[european-union|European Union]] was reported by Forbes to be on track to make digital identity mainstream in 2023: under [[eidas-2-0|eIDAS 2.0]], effective September 2023, all Member States must offer a digital identity wallet to citizens and businesses, with the European Commission targeting at least 80% of citizens able to use a digital ID solution to access key public services by 2030.[1]

The [[united-nations|United Nations]] went further in a policy brief tied to its Global Digital Compact, arguing that digital IDs linked with bank or mobile money accounts can improve delivery of social protection coverage, better reach eligible beneficiaries, and reduce leakage, errors and costs.[1] Badlands reads this as a proposal for a global identification system tied directly to bank accounts.[1]

The [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] adopted the EU system of digital COVID-19 certification with the stated intent of transforming it into a global system — the first building block of the [[global-digital-health-certification-network|WHO Global Digital Health Certification Network]].[1]

## Criticism

The Badlands critique is that a universal, mandatory credential converts ordinary participation in economic and civic life into a permissioned activity, and that combining identity with payments and health status creates a single chokepoint for exclusion.[1] The article ties this concern to the broader themes of [[mass-surveillance|mass surveillance]] and the [[social-credit-score|social credit]] model.[1]

## Kenya's Maisha Namba

Badlands Media's November 2023 survey of technocratic developments treated digital ID as "the lynchpin that makes it all work": once populations have been coerced onto a digital control system, each new advancement arrives regardless of public sentiment until people are desensitized to the encroachment — compared to the ceaseless barrage of smartphone updates, except that what is being updated is society itself and one's ability to interact with it.[2]

The article's leading case study was [[maisha-namba|Maisha Namba]], the Kenyan biometric digital ID backed by [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] and announced after closed-door meetings with President [[william-ruto|William Ruto]], which includes a [[biometric-vaccination-system-for-newborns|biometric vaccination system for newborns]] intended to replace birth certificates.[2] Such systems, the article argues, allow ruling classes to monitor and manipulate populations, regulate information flow, restrict access to resources and enforce societal norms that perpetuate existing power dynamics.[2]

It also warned about the standard rhetorical pattern accompanying such rollouts — assurances that participation is not mandatory, which hold only until it becomes practically impossible to operate in the system without the new technology.[2] Related [[technocracy|technocratic]] developments include [[smart-clothing|smart clothing]] and the security failures of [[smart-homes|smart homes]], with [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]]'s notion of "surveillance under the skin" pointing toward an eventual implanted digital ID chip.[2]
