# European Democracy Shield

The European Democracy Shield is an initiative launched by the [[european-union|European Union]] to regulate speech and combat what the bloc characterizes as disinformation.[1] European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] framed the initiative as one that "protects democracy and civil society," while critics argued it restricts freedom of expression under the guise of battling so-called misinformation.[1]

## Structure

The Shield promotes a Monitoring Centre tasked with addressing "false content" and "disinformation," and establishes an EU-funded network of fact-checkers to verify information.[1] Critics contend that such a system stifles dissent and perpetuates the EU's own official narratives, raising the question of who is empowered to determine truth amid increasing propaganda.[1] Reporting on the initiative described it as signifying a concerning trend toward authoritarianism within the EU.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that when a government leans into policing speech it usually signals lost public support amid growing dissent, calling it a panicked and reactive move that assumes dissent can be neutralized by preventing people from speaking — an approach that almost always makes matters worse.[1] She noted that the EU has no equivalent of the First Amendment, called for the bloc's dissolution and for member-state exits such as "Frexit" and "Gerexit," and described the European Union as a failed globalist experiment.[1]
