# 2024 Pennsylvania Senate Race

The 2024 United States Senate election in Pennsylvania pitted Republican challenger [[dave-mccormick|Dave McCormick]] against Democratic incumbent [[bob-casey|Bob Casey]] and produced a margin narrow enough to require a statewide recount under Pennsylvania law.[1]

## Result and recount trigger

Unofficial returns gave McCormick 3,380,310 votes, or 48.93 percent, and Casey 3,350,972 votes, or 48.50 percent — a gap of roughly 29,000 ballots.[1] On November 13, 2024, Secretary of the Commonwealth Al Schmidt announced through the [[pennsylvania-department-of-state|Pennsylvania Department of State]] that the results had "triggered a legally required statewide recount."[1] The recount was to begin no later than November 20, 2024; counties were required to finish by noon on November 26, 2024 and report to the Secretary of the Commonwealth by noon the next day.[1]

## Badlands framing

Writing for the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the race should have been called on election night and that the Democratic Party's unwillingness to accept the loss amounted to "full blown election denier" behavior.[1] She welcomed the recount not because she expected it to change the outcome — in her experience recounts "raise more questions than they answer" — but because it would generate additional data useful for mapping what she described as a centralized web of election corruption.[1] She tied the exercise to an expectation that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], as the 47th president, intended to restore election integrity before the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]], and framed full transparency of secure and accurate elections as critical to the preservation of liberty.[1] The contest was contemporaneous with a Republican sweep of the presidency, Senate and House of Representatives.[1]
