# Conditional Acceptance

Conditional acceptance is a contract-law technique promoted by Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] as a citizen defense against regulatory enforcement, described by him as a "secret weapon that almost no one uses" capable of stopping the police state "in its tracks."[1] He presented it in the August 29, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] in response to the state seizure of an Amish farmer's livestock and meat-processing facility in Virginia.[1]

## Premises

Deschamps' argument rests on the claim that every relationship, including that between a citizen and their government, operates within the law of contracts, and that the justice system uses "honor and dishonor" with the law as its principal instrument for removing rights.[1] On this account, violating a statute is a dishonor of a contractual term that generates a charge in a legal jurisdiction; agencies from child protective services and health inspectors to school boards, the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] derive their compulsion from that contractual duty.[1]

## Method

The technique holds that when an official presents a citation, fine or compliance demand for signature, the official is in fact making an offer to contract, and since no party to a contract may be coerced into accepting it, the citizen may decline conditionally rather than refuse outright.[1] The citizen states willingness to accept and honor the duty upon the condition that the official first prove the facts asserted, demanding the highest available standard of proof.[1]

Deschamps' worked example involves a restaurant owner cited for violating a COVID-era social-distancing seating rule, who replies that before signing he requires the inspector to prove COVID is as deadly as claimed by citing epidemiological studies of death or injury, to demonstrate that the claimed incidence figures are valid and that live SARS-CoV-2 was present in those who died or fell ill, and to show where the owner agreed to the protocols either by wet-ink signature or through a duly elected legislature that debated the measures with adequate public evaluation.[1]

By remaining "in honor," the citizen is said to return the burden to the government, forcing it to substantiate its case.[1] Deschamps concedes the tactic will not work where facts are genuine and no fraud is present, but claims it succeeds "anytime fraud is present in an offer to contract," and that widespread use would rapidly shut down what he calls the machines of the globalists.[1] He lists silencing debt collectors, stopping foreclosures, combating health inspectors and winning court cases among its applications.[1]
