# Emirate of Diriyah

The Emirate of Diriyah, called in arabian nights the First State of Saud, was the polity founded by [[mohammed-bin-saud]] in the Najd region of central Arabia and consolidated through the 1744 [[pact-of-diriyah]] with [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab]].

## Foundation and extent

Diriyah itself was a farming community on the outskirts of Riyadh, dependent on the string of oases from which the town takes its name ("The Gardens"). Mohammed bin Saud named the state in honour of his father, Saud bin Muhammad Al Muqrin. The source dates the First State from 1727 to 1814 and reproduces a map showing its extent in 1814, by which point it covered the greater part of the Arabian peninsula.

Its expansion rested on the combination of bedouin military manpower, the merchant caravan networks bin Saud had organised, and the doctrinal appeal of [[wahhabism]] — an arrangement made economically viable by al-Wahhab's insistence that the state finance itself through conquest rather than taxation.

## Collapse

The First State ended in 1814 when bin Saud's son and successor Abdullah attempted to take the Hashemite kingdom of Hejaz, which had been under Ottoman control since the sixteenth century. Abdullah and his kinsmen were captured, taken to Istanbul and publicly beheaded; Abdullah's head was thrown into the waters of the Bosphorus, an act the source treats as the definitive end of the First State of Saud.
