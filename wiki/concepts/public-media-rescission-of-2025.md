# Public Media Rescission of 2025

The public media rescission of 2025 refers to the package approved by [[congress|Congress]] in July 2025 clawing back $1.1 billion in federal funding to public media, chiefly appropriations flowing through the [[corporation-for-public-broadcasting|Corporation for Public Broadcasting]].[1]

## Effects on affiliates

Staffers at local [[npr|NPR]] and [[pbs|PBS]] stations around the country were reported to be devastated by the vote, which could jeopardize the futures of dozens of stations.[1] Small and rural public media stations that rely heavily on federal funding to operate began bracing for possible staff cuts after the package passed. Some stations argued they were being punished over a fight between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the national public broadcasting organizations, a dispute they said bore little relationship to the service smaller outlets provide their communities.[1]

## Badlands response

Badlands writer Ashe in America rejected that framing, calling the coverage "little more than emotional blackmail" and summarizing its appeal as "Think of the children! (Or whatever.)"[1] She argued that local public radio stations and websites are themselves progressive propaganda, citing [[colorado-public-radio|Colorado Public Radio]]'s reporting on litigation involving Badlands — reporting that named two witnesses who were later cited by a judge in denying a summary judgment motion but who never materialized.[1] Her conclusion was: "Defund it all. This is exactly what I voted for."[1]
