# Kursk Incursion

The **Kursk incursion** was Ukraine's cross-border ground offensive into Russia's Kursk region, launched in early August 2024.[1] Described as brazen and high-risk, the operation was nearly a week old by August 12, 2024, with Russian forces still fighting the incoming units and Kyiv confirming that the "offensive operation" was continuing.[1]

## Scale and evacuations

Russian emergency authorities evacuated approximately 180,000 citizens from border regions affected by the fighting.[1] An official briefing delivered to President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] on August 12 indicated the offensive was larger, more expansive and more devastating than previously believed.[1] Per Bloomberg, Ukraine's military had taken control of 28 towns and villages in the Kursk border region, prompting a sixth of the region's population to flee, according to the acting regional governor.[1] At the same time, Russian forces continued advancing inside eastern Ukraine, with RIA reporting Russian control of the settlement of Lyscychne.[1]

## Putin's response

In his first detailed public statements since the incursion began, Putin declared that there would be no peace talks or settlement with Kyiv.[1] He argued it was "now clear why the Kiev regime refused our proposals to return to the plan for peaceful settlement," saying the enemy was doing the bidding of "its Western masters" and that "the West is waging war against us using Ukrainians" in order to improve its future negotiating position.[1] He further asked what negotiations were possible "with people who indiscriminately strike civilians, civilian infrastructure or try to create threats to nuclear power facilities."[1]

That last remark referenced a fire at the [[zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant]] the preceding Sunday, which President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] used to raise the specter of nuclear disaster; the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] subsequently said the incident — possibly involving tires set alight at the complex — had no impact on nuclear safety.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that "in order to make peace, you must first have the threat of war," presenting the escalation as a reversal of the Hegelian dialectic used to condition populations since the World Wars.[1] In this reading, the Russia–Ukraine conflict initiated the breakdown of the globalist order, so its resolution would trigger a corresponding "catharsis cascade," and the war "won't end until Trump and Putin end it" with a popular mandate.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] added that the adversary had "overplayed their hand in Kursk" and that Putin was "playing for keeps," posing the question of what "total victory" would look like for both Russia and the MAGA movement against the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1]

## Damage to churches and Putin's Kursk visit (May 2025)

Following the region's liberation, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said Ukraine's armed forces had used churches in the Kursk Region as strongholds during their failed incursion, and noted that many of the buildings suffered serious damage during the fighting to retake the area.[2] After meeting with the heads of the region's municipalities, Putin called on local authorities, including acting Kursk governor Aleksandr Khinshtein, to create a special program to assess the damage and to submit proposals for restoring the churches and repairing associated buildings.[2] During the trip — made public only the following morning — he emphasized the integral role of the Russian Orthodox Church, saying it had "been with its people throughout history."[2] The visit was the first by the Russian president to the region since it was fully cleared of Kiev's forces the previous month.[2] Ukraine had launched the incursion into the border region in August of the preceding year, claiming it sought to seize territory as leverage for eventual peace negotiations.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the account as evidence that Ukrainian forces converted Russian churches into bases specifically to force Russia to strike them, and wrote that Putin "really is up against a cabal of God-hating satanists."[2] He predicted no ceasefire or brokered peace, expecting instead that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would voice frustration that [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] and his allies refused the peace [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] had negotiated on their behalf, and then walk away.[2] He noted Putin's condition that the war's "root causes" be addressed — which he identified with those who took control of Kiev in the 2014 Maidan Coup — and observed that Putin and Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]] have said they have no interest in negotiating with a leadership that would simply regroup, re-arm and resume its campaign, which he read as signaling a "kill or capture" posture toward Ukrainian leadership.[2]

## Alleged objective of the Kursk nuclear plant (October 2024)

In commentary published October 4, 2024, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that during an in-depth Labor Day rant on Badlands Daily he had told [[canncon|CannCon]] that the intended objective of the Kursk invasion was to reach the [[kursk-nuclear-power-plant|Kursk nuclear power plant]] and destroy it, in the hope that the resulting fallout would render a vast region of Russia uninhabitable.[3] He pointed to reports that DC/NATO-aligned forces were by then shelling the plant, and circulated a map of the Ukrainian incursion alongside a satellite image showing miles of trenches the Russians had dug to defend the facility.[3]

## Russian retaliation and Zelensky's confirmation (August 2024)

On Sunday, August 11, 2024 — the sixth day of the incursion — Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] broke his silence and gave the first official confirmation of the cross-border ground operation, saying he had "received several reports from Commander-in-Chief Syrskyi regarding the front lines and our actions to push the war onto the aggressor's territory," and that "Ukraine is proving that it can indeed restore justice and ensure the necessary pressure on the aggressor."[4] The same day the [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]] vowed that "a tough response from the Russian army will not be long in coming."[4]

That response had already begun overnight, as Kyiv came under heavy missile attack; the missiles did not reach the city centre but struck the suburbs.[4] The Associated Press reported at least four ballistic missiles hitting the capital region along with 57 Shahed drones, of which Ukraine's air force said 53 were intercepted.[4] Zelensky alleged that Russia had used a North Korean ballistic missile in the assault, calling it "yet another deliberate terrorist strike against Ukraine" and saying pyrotechnic experts were still determining the exact data on the missile.[4] US Ambassador to Ukraine [[bridget-brink|Bridget Brink]] condemned the assault, saying it killed a man and his four-year-old son.[4]

## Historical analogy and Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the offensive through the Second World War, noting that he had argued in a May 30 news brief that the Third Reich's decision to attack Kursk was its ultimate undoing, and that "history doesn't repeat, but it often does rhyme."[4] He cited analysts quoted by ZeroHedge who viewed the cross-border offensive as either a last-ditch attempt to force US and NATO escalation or to gain leverage, or else a PR-driven operation in which Ukrainian troops were being sacrificed for propaganda and a temporary blow to Kremlin morale.[4] Learning that Ukraine had sent tanks into Kursk, which Russia was destroying by airstrike, he remarked that "maybe history does sometimes repeat itself."[4]

The alleged use of a North Korean missile prompted commentary on [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]]'s alignment with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], which the writer folded into his recurring thesis that events constitute pageantry produced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], invoking Trump's "Little Rocket Man" nickname as prophetic.[4]

## Assessment of failure (September 2024)

By early September 2024 the Ukrainian advance into Kursk had failed to achieve one of its main objectives: diverting Russian troops from the front lines of eastern Ukraine in order to reshape the battlefield.[5] The August incursion had achieved secondary goals — damaging Russia's image of strength, destroying military assets and taking territory and prisoners for negotiating leverage — but the failure of the more crucial diversion objective opened Kyiv's gamble to criticism as Russia advanced along the war's 600-mile eastern front.[5] Russian forces took vast swaths of territory over the preceding month and closed in on the key rail junction town of [[pokrovsk|Pokrovsk]] in the Donetsk region, prompting Kyiv to evacuate civilians.[5]

President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], who weeks earlier had expressed frustration at the incursion, touted his military's progress while visiting a school: "The enemy did not achieve the main task that they set themselves: to stop our offensive in the Donbas... We have not had such a pace of offensive in the Donbas for a long time."[5] President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] maintained that the Kursk offensive was a key part of Kyiv's strategy and would be folded into a plan for winning the war that he intended to present to the United States that month.[5]

## 1943 analogy and three-fold objective thesis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who had delivered an in-depth sitrep on Kursk to [[canncon|CannCon]] on Badlands Daily, explained the 19th-century Prussian origins of the Third Reich's blitzkrieg doctrine, the Russian "Shoulder Defense" method developed to defeat it, and argued that the 2024 invasion was playing out exactly as the German attack on Kursk did in 1943 — which proved the Wehrmacht's undoing.[5] He published side-by-side 1943 and 2024 Kursk battle maps to illustrate the near-identical strategy.[5]

In his reading, the "NATO Nazi" strategic intent was three-fold: draw Russian forces away from the east so Donetsk could be retaken; terrorize Russian civilians so that support for Putin's war would wane; and attack and destroy the [[kursk-nuclear-power-plant|Kurskaya nuclear power plant]] deep in Russian territory.[5] He argued the plan was foolish, noting that missile strikes on population centers killed over 30 and injured over 140 without producing unrest, instead securing Putin's wrath and ending the prospect of negotiation with Kiev.[5] He also cited Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]]'s remark to Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]] that the region is sparsely populated — pastures, forest and a few small towns — making the terror rationale incoherent.[5]

He further noted that the troops committed to the incursion left Ukraine's eastern front vulnerable, that Russians had dug massive trench lines to defend the Kurskaya plant, and that this was not the first attack on a nuclear facility, citing May drone launches at the Russian-controlled [[zaporizhzhia-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporizhzhia plant]] and allegations that NATO was threatening the families of Ukrainian plant workers to extract sensitive information.[5] He concluded that [[nato|NATO]] must be dismantled and war crime tribunals empaneled to prosecute those at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and [[state-department|State Department]] responsible.[5]

## Role in the Ukrainian "victory plan"

In late August 2024 Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] explicitly folded the operation into his [[ukraine-victory-plan|victory plan]], saying that "Kursk region is part of our plan—Ukraine's victory plan. It may sound overly ambitious to some, but for us, it's an important plan."[6] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterised the incursion as a possibly successful, possibly catastrophic operation whose real function was to set terms at a negotiating table Kyiv had refused to acknowledge existed six months earlier.[6]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Zelensky as "flexing" about the invasion of Kursk and promised a Badlands Daily deep dive on the region's historic significance, how the Russians developed the strategy that defeated blitzkrieg there, and why Kursk would likely once again become the doom of what he called the Nazi regime.[6]

## October 2024 Russian breakthrough

By mid-October 2024, Ukraine's Kursk offensive had visibly failed: its forces had stalled only a couple of weeks after the initial push and the Russians had been retaking pieces of territory ever since, a reality obvious to those tracking war maps and geolocation data but shocking to audiences of establishment news sources.[7] Russian troops made significant advances from the western flank of Ukrainian positions amid the heaviest fighting on Russian territory since the August cross-border incursion began, and reports indicated a threat that Ukrainian units would be encircled; The Telegraph, usually a platform for pro-Ukraine coverage, noted that sources on both sides confirmed the breakthrough.[7] Simultaneously, Ukraine evacuated the Sumy region.[7]

The Ukrainian military blogger DeepState said "the enemy pushed through the left flank of the Kursk grouping of Ukrainian defense forces" and that "we are on the verge of another mess due to the repetition of mistakes," though he later characterized the front lines as "difficult but under control."[7] Because DeepState had avoided updating his war maps to reflect significant Russian gains for many weeks — likely, ZeroHedge suggested, at the urging of officials in Kyiv seeking to present a facade of success — his admission was read as a sign that the situation had become dire.[7] Russian military bloggers reported that marines from the Pacific Fleet's 155th Guards Brigade were fighting around the Glushkovsky district of Kursk, where most Ukrainian forces had allegedly been overrun.[7]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry preferred the [[rt|RT]] account of the episode, which highlighted Major General [[apty-alaudinov|Apty Alaudinov]] and the [[akhmat-special-forces|Akhmat Special Forces]] from Russia's Chechen Republic, and used the occasion to disparage the [[state-department|State Department]] and adjacent corporate media as a "laughing stock" by comparison.[7] He framed the Chechen deployment as the force "crushing the NATO blitzkrieg" in Kursk, following Vladimir Putin's recent visit to Dagestan to bless the unit.[7]

## Russian protest over American journalists and mercenaries

On Tuesday, August 20, 2024, Russia's Foreign Ministry summoned a senior US Embassy official in Moscow to protest what it described as US interference in the Kursk region, which had seen heavy fighting since Ukraine's cross-border incursion began on August 6.[8] Moscow condemned the "provocative actions" of both American journalists and US mercenaries said to have been spotted on Russian territory in the context of the Kursk invasion.[8] In a meeting with US Embassy Chief of Mission Stephanie Holmes, the ministry issued a "strong protest" over "the provocative actions of American reporters who illegally entered the Kursk region to produce propaganda for covering up the crimes of the Kyiv regime," and said national law enforcement authorities planned to "carry out the necessary investigative measures" examining the American journalists' work.[8] The crew in question was from [[cnn|CNN]].[8]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry compared the episode to CNN's earlier employment of a photojournalist embedded with [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza, and argued that under the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] Law of War Manual the journalists' participation in what he called an unlawful incursion into Russian territory would classify them as "unprivileged belligerents."[8]

## Alleged atrocities in Sudzha

Also in August 2024, Major General [[apty-alaudinov|Apty Alaudinov]], commander of the [[akhmat-special-forces|Akhmat Special Forces]], claimed on Telegram that Ukrainian forces occupying part of Kursk Region had committed atrocities against civilians and used them as human shields, describing an incident in the border town of Sudzha in which troops confined children and teachers to an upper floor of an occupied residential building.[8] He said photographs showed civilians shot at point-blank range in the head and from behind, called the Ukrainians "fascists and Satanists," and vowed revenge.[8]

Badlands linked these allegations to earlier claims about the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] in Mariupol in March 2022 and cited an interview with a Russian soldier describing discoveries near Izyum, south of Kharkiv, which the outlet characterised in speculative terms as evidence of organ harvesting or adrenochrome farming.[8] The section closed by noting [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s statement, made after Ukrainian airstrikes on Kursk civilians and a drone strike that damaged the [[zaporozhye-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant]], that Russia was no longer interested in peace talks: "What is there to talk about with those who attack civilians and nuclear facilities?"[8]
