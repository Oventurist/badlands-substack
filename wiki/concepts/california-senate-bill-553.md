# California Senate Bill 553

**California Senate Bill 553 (SB 553)** is a workplace-violence measure authored by Democratic state senator [[david-cortese|David Cortese]] of San Jose. The bill passed the California Senate on May 31, 2023 by a vote of 29 to 8 and moved to review by state Assembly committees.[1]

## Provisions

SB 553 would require employers to maintain violent-incident logs, to provide active-shooter and shoplifter training, and to discard workplace policies requiring employees to confront suspected shoplifters.[1] Cortese described the bill's purpose as helping "employers keep employees safe at work."[1]

## Reception

Critics characterized the measure as legislation that would effectively make it harder for store employees to intervene against theft. The California Realtors Association warned that the bill would apply to all industries rather than retail alone; CRA president and chief executive Rachel Michelin told Fox2/KTVU that the bill "goes way too far," adding, "I think it will open the doors even wider for people to come in and steal from our stores."[1] The measure was reported by The Epoch Times and by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], the latter running it under the headline "California Advances Bill To Help Shoplifters Steal."[1]
