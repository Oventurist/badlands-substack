# Monroe Doctrine

The Monroe Doctrine is the American foreign-policy declaration, announced during the presidency of James Monroe, opposing further European colonization of the Western Hemisphere. In the Badlands Media corpus it is treated as the diplomatic companion of [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]] and, like it, as a principle whose original anti-imperial intent was later inverted.

## Authorship and original intent

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s essay Avoiding WWIII attributes the drafting of the doctrine to [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]], who is quoted as understanding that it could work only if America ventured "not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy." On this reading the doctrine was conceived as a shield for republican systems in the Americas against the hereditary empires of France, England, Russia and Spain, not as a licence for hemispheric domination.

## Abuse and inversion

The article acknowledges at the outset the objection that the world has "suffered from enough Monroe Doctrine and Manifest Destiny over the past two centuries," pointing to wars across the Middle East, the genocide against Native Americans, and imperial abuses of Latin American nations. Ehret does not deny these abuses, but argues that the doctrine's origins should not be discarded because "evil perverts justified evil things using them," and that great moral heroes acted against imperialism and the Anglo-American [[deep-state|Deep State]] in the doctrine's name.

## Contemporary relevance

Ehret concludes that when Russian "red lines" are viewed alongside the emerging Eurasian development programs of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], it is "a rich irony" that the spirit of John Quincy Adams's security doctrine is alive in the world — "just not in the U.S." The implication drawn in the article is that a sane, defensive sphere-of-interest doctrine, rather than global military primacy, is the practical means of avoiding a third world war.
