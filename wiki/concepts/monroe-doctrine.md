# Monroe Doctrine

The **Monroe Doctrine** is the American foreign-policy doctrine drafted by President [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]] declaring the Western Hemisphere off limits to further European colonization. In "Avoiding WWIII," [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that Adams knew the doctrine could work only if America ventured "not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy."[1]

## Origins and intent

Ehret situates the doctrine as an extension of the republican strand of [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]] articulated by [[george-washington|George Washington]] and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], who wanted America's institutions styled "Continental" rather than colonial.[1] Washington's warning against foreign entanglements and party politics forms the doctrine's moral backdrop: America's founding harmony with Natural Law did not immunize it against becoming another empire.[1]

## Abuse and inversion

Ehret concedes that wars across the Middle East, genocide against Native Americans and imperial abuses of Latin American nations have been justified under the banners of the Monroe Doctrine and Manifest Destiny, but insists that the origins of the interconnected philosophies should not be thrown away because "evil perverts justified evil things using them."[1] He rejects both the "Wokeist" reading of American history as wholly evil and the simple-minded patriotic reading of it as wholly good.[1]

## Contemporary relevance

Reading Russian "red lines" and the emergence of a Eurasian Manifest Destiny through [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Far Eastern Vision, the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]] and China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], Ehret concludes that the spirit of Adams' security doctrine survives in the world — "just not in the U.S."[1]

## Trump's 2025 reassertion

In December 2025, one of the central pillars of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s new [[2025-national-security-strategy|National Security Strategy]] was a plan to update the more than 200-year-old doctrine.[2] The strategy document stated that "after years of neglect, the United States will reassert and enforce the Monroe Doctrine to restore American preeminence in the Western Hemisphere, and to protect our homeland and our access to key geographies throughout the region."[2] It laid out a "[[trump-corollary-to-the-monroe-doctrine|Trump Corollary]] to the Monroe Doctrine" prioritizing American-led cooperative efforts against mass migration, drug trafficking and "hostile foreign incursion or ownership of key assets."[2] Reporting described this as the latest evolution of a doctrine that successive presidents have read either as an argument for peace and non-intervention or as a justification for American imperialism.[2] Badlands host [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that pairing the strategy with Trump's recognition of the doctrine's anniversary positioned the "American Restoration" as a callback to the Revolution, aimed at the same "Invisible Enemy" as before.[2]

## Adams' 1821 formulation and the McKinley era

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies the doctrine's architect as [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]], who laid out its principle on July 4, 1821 in describing a United States that "without a single exception, respected the independence of other nations while asserting and maintaining her own," and that "goes not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy."[3] Adams warned that by involving itself in the internal affairs of other nations the United States would destroy its own reason for existence and become "no longer the ruler of itself, but the dictator of the world," adding that "colonial establishments are engines of wrong."[3] The conception built on [[george-washington|Washington]]'s 1796 Farewell Address warning against permanent antipathies and passionate attachments toward particular nations.[3]

President [[william-mckinley|William McKinley]] is presented as the last chief executive to defend the doctrine as America's anti-imperial foreign policy since 1823, alongside his domestic commitment to the [[american-system|American System]].[3] [[alfred-thayer-mahan|Alfred Thayer Mahan]] argued the opposite — that the doctrine was an outdated habit unsuited to an age of world empires and sea power.[3] After McKinley's assassination, [[theodore-roosevelt|Theodore Roosevelt]] converted the doctrine into "Big Stick" diplomacy in Latin America, which Ehret characterizes as a perversion of its original anti-imperial meaning.[3]
