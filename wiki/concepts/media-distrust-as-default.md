# Media Distrust as Default

"Media distrust as default" — captured in the formula "fake news until proven real" — is the epistemic posture argued for by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in a May 10, 2024 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay: that everything reported by the media should be assumed to be a lie or a manipulation of the truth until it is proven true.[1]

## Origin of the argument

Carlson recounts overhearing two Badlanders debate how information from the media should be processed.[1] One held that everything should be assumed a lie until proven true; the other held that information should be received from a place of neutrality and then evaluated.[1] Notably, neither argued that media reporting should be assumed true until proven untrue.[1] Carlson sides with the former position, on the ground that the media has proven itself untrustworthy and that its purpose is to deceive.[1]

## Neutrality as a concession

The essay's central objection to the neutral posture is that neutrality amounts to extending the benefit of the doubt.[1] Carlson accepts that in a court of law a person should be presumed innocent until proven guilty — while noting this presumption appears not to apply when [[donald-trump|Trump]] is accused of anything — but asks why the same respect should be extended to an institution that has proved to be a liar time and again.[1] Because the media's lies are designed to elicit fear, granting neutrality means allowing oneself to be emotionally manipulated, and that damage is not undone when the story is later shown to be false; he compares it to stress, where the stressful event may pass but the harm is already done.[1] See [[fear-as-a-control-mechanism|fear as a control mechanism]].[1]

## Damage from a lifetime of lies

Carlson further argues that a genuine state of neutrality probably does not exist in a mind that has been manipulated by lies its whole life, even where the person feels they are beginning to see clearly.[1] He draws analogies: a recovering alcoholic leaving rehab for the fifth time cannot safely socialise in bars on the theory that he will not drink too much; an abused child cannot and probably should not trust the way a non-abused person trusts; and a spouse of forty or fifty years who discovers that everything they were told was a lie is not thereby ready to enter a new relationship.[1] Being awake to the lies, he concludes, does not mean being unaffected by them.[1]

He notes that many people who believed themselves awake before the Covid era nonetheless feared for their lives four years earlier, and argues that receiving news from a posture of neutrality could well have resulted in early vaccination under the [[covid-19-narrative|Covid narrative]] — a decision that fewer subsequent boosters would not undo, whereas beginning from distrust would have resulted in declining the injection altogether.[1]

## Practical stance

Carlson states that having believed media lies for most of his life, he now receives media information from a position of distrust, which makes him less likely to be fooled and prevents the emotional reaction of fear, without which, he writes, the mind control falls flat.[1] He characterises questioning reported claims of inhumanity as wise rather than insensitive, and offers examples of claims he regards as warranting proof before belief: babies placed in ovens, "flying go-carts" penetrating the most impenetrable borders in the world, an array of Iranian missiles fired at [[israel|Israel]] causing no apparent damage, and a man in a Toyota pickup truck returning hostages to [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]
