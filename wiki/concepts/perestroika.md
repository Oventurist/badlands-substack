# Perestroika

Perestroika, referred to by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as "Operation Hammer" — named in honour of Occidental Petroleum's Armand Hammer, who ran the 1920s New Economic Policy under Trotsky in Russia — describes the Western side of the process by which the former Soviet bloc was liberalized after 1991.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

The operation is said to have involved the IMF's Jeffrey Sachs, Vice President Al Gore, the Rhodes Scholar Strobe Talbott and the Oxford-trained Goldman Sachs investment banker [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) On the Russian side the direction came from monetarist "reformers" such as [[anatoly-chubais|Anatoly Chubais]] and Yegor Gaidar under Yeltsin.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Although the former Soviet bloc nations were already in tatters by 1992, Ehret argues their sovereign powers could only be undone by applying a liberalization process that had taken thirty years in the West within the space of a single decade.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

Similar privatization and liberalization reforms were applied viciously to Ukraine and other Warsaw Pact countries during the same period, producing a "nouveau riche" class including Oleg Deripaska, Boris Berezovsky, Mikhail Fridman and Roman Abramovich in Russia and Petro Poroshenko, Rinat Akhmetov, [[mikhail-khodorkovsky|Mikhail Khodorkovsky]] and [[viktor-pinchuk|Viktor Pinchuk]] in Ukraine.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Many of these oligarchs purchased houses in upmarket London, in the district that came to be known as "Moscow on Thames."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Parallel with the 1920s

Badlands Media coverage argues that the vast liberalization of the Russian economy during the "dark 1920s" under the [[new-economic-policy|New Economic Policy]] closely paralleled the Perestroika program of free trade and privatization in the 1990s.[2] The article notes that George Bush Sr. dubbed this later program of Balkanized looting of Russia "Operation Hammer," a reference tying it back to [[armand-hammer|Armand Hammer]], the magnate who had dominated Soviet-Western business dealings in the 1920s.[2]
