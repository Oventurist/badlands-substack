# Affordable Care Act

The Affordable Care Act (ACA), commonly known as ObamaCare, is the US health insurance law whose enhanced premium tax credits became a central fight in Congress at the end of 2025.[1]

## 2025 subsidy cliff

The enhanced ACA tax credits were set to sunset on December 31, 2025, creating a cost cliff expected to hit 22 million Americans.[1] House Republicans declined to extend the subsidies, instead passing the [[lower-health-care-premiums-for-all-americans-act|Lower Health Care Premiums for All Americans Act]] on December 17, 2025 as a conservative alternative, by a vote of 216–211.[1] The bill's failure to address the expiring credits infuriated centrist Republicans who had spent months pressing for an extension, though they ultimately supported the package's conservative policies.[1] The measure was given little chance of passing the [[us-senate|Senate]].[1]

## Abortion as a sticking point in the 2025 subsidy negotiations

In November 2025 the White House's silence on how its health care plan would deal with abortion created difficulties for Republicans on Capitol Hill, according to Politico.[2] For many GOP members an expansion of abortion restrictions within the ACA was a must-have, and the administration's decision to leave the issue out of its tentative framework caught them off guard, leaving aides unsure whether President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would take a public position.[2] The fight over the [[hyde-amendment|Hyde amendment]], which bars federal funding for abortion, was described as just one of many landmines to be cleared before any deal extending the expiring ACA subsidies could be reached.[2]
