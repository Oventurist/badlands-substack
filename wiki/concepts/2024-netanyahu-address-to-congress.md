# 2024 Netanyahu Address to Congress

On Wednesday, July 24, 2024, Israeli prime minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] addressed a joint meeting of the United States [[congress|Congress]] amid the Gaza war, delivering a defiant speech that dismissed war crimes allegations and pressed for accelerated American weapons transfers.[1] The visit drew thousands of demonstrators to Washington and became a focal point of Badlands Media's coverage of foreign influence over the U.S. legislature.[1]

## The speech

Netanyahu dismissed as "utter nonsense" criticism from the [[united-nations|United Nations]], human rights groups and the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] that Israel's government had committed war crimes in Gaza, and derided pro-Palestinian protesters as "idiots" and tools of Iran.[1] He told lawmakers Israel would settle for "nothing less" than total victory over [[hamas|Hamas]] and described a postwar vision for Gaza seemingly at odds with the ceasefire terms advocated by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration.[1] Asserting that the fates of Israel and the United States are inextricably linked, he argued that American investment in Israel's war aims also defends the United States and "all democracies."[1] "We help keep Americans' boots off the ground," he said, urging Congress to expedite billions of dollars in weapons: "Give us the tools faster, and we'll finish the job faster."[1]

## Standing-ovation record

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who hosted Badlands Media's special coverage of the address and a post-game analysis, recorded that the 52-minute speech received 58 standing ovations — a record for any congressional speech of any length — and that the ovations were bipartisan throughout.[1] The previous record was Netanyahu's own 2015 address: 28 bipartisan ovations in 48 minutes.[1] Third place belonged to [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s 2022 speech, with 21 bipartisan ovations in 23 minutes.[1] For GhostofBasedPatrickHenry the pattern made it "pretty evident" that Congress is bought and paid for by foreign interests, even if the identity of those interests remains a matter of debate.[1]

## Badlands critique

The Badlands analysis held that Netanyahu had effectively called for war with Iran, plainly stating that Iran would attack American soil if it were not stopped in the Middle East — language that sounded "more like a threat than a warning."[1] Much of the address was carried by surrogates: hostage victims, hostage families and wounded veterans recounting life during wartime, which the commentary described as leveraging genuine tragedy to justify cultivating more of it.[1] War, the piece argued, should never require persuasion; if truly necessary it would be self-evident, and peace remained an option.[1] Badlands further accused Netanyahu of repeating debunked claims, most prominently the "40 beheaded babies" narrative; citing [[haaretz|Haaretz]] reporting from the previous December based on life insurance records and police reports, it stated that the only baby killed on October 7 was ten-month-old Mila Cohen.[1] Both the beheaded-babies and "cooked-in-an-oven" narratives, the brief noted, had been debunked by investigative organisations across the political spectrum, and Netanyahu should not receive a free pass merely because the subject matter is tragic.[1]

## Protests in Washington

Thousands of Palestinian supporters and critics of the Israel–Gaza war descended on Washington before and during the address, marching around the city and drawing a massive police response.[1] Most demonstrators were peaceful, but there were clashes with law enforcement: D.C. police and Capitol Police arrested fifteen people in total and the U.S. Park Police arrested eight more.[1] Outside Union Station protesters set an American flag ablaze along with an effigy of Netanyahu, and spray-painted the Christopher Columbus fountain and an adjacent Liberty Bell reproduction with messages including "Free Gaza," "All zionists are bastards" and "Free Palestine."[1] Police struck some demonstrators with a chemical irritant at multiple points during the day.[1] Badlands urged readers to observe and distill rather than react, warning that powerful forces sought to exploit the moment to foment division and to psyop the public into supporting another foreign war, and that division — civil war — was "the enemy's only remaining play."[1]
