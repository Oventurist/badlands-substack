# Friendshoring

"Friendshoring" (also rendered "friend-shoring") is the practice of relocating manufacturing and supply-chain operations out of a geopolitical rival's territory and into countries considered politically friendly, rather than repatriating them entirely.[1] The term entered wide circulation as global supply chains fractured following the trade war that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] initiated against China during his first administration.[1]

## Apple and India

The clearest illustration cited in April 2024 was [[apple|Apple]], whose India-made iPhone output surpassed $14 billion in value.[1] Bloomberg reporting indicated that Apple was producing roughly 14 percent — about one in seven — of its iPhones in India, a rapid increase suggesting the company was accelerating efforts to reduce reliance on China amid deteriorating Sino-American relations.[1] In the fiscal year ended March 2024, [[foxconn|Foxconn Technology Group]] assembled nearly 67 percent and Pegatron Corporation about 17 percent of India-made iPhones, with the remainder produced at the Wistron plant in southern Karnataka state that the [[tata-group|Tata Group]] had taken over the previous year; Tata announced plans to build one of India's largest iPhone assembly plants.[1] China nonetheless continued to produce the largest share of iPhones.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands analysis

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the growing prevalence of the word "friendshoring" as both ironic and auspicious in light of [[devolution|Devolution]] theory and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] thesis.[1] On its face, mainstream and alternative media alike read the trend as an economic rift between the United States and China, driving both onshoring of domestic production and a shift of foreign production hubs from China to friendlier states such as India.[1]

Burning Bright argued that this outcome is not adverse to Chinese interests: [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] has been adversarial toward the American oligarchy represented by firms like Apple and Microsoft, instead prioritising domestic production by Chinese companies.[1] In that reading both [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] and Xi belong to the Sovereign Alliance, and the friendshoring trend therefore benefits multipolarity rather than any single bloc.[1]
