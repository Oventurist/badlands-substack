# Industrial Society and Its Future

*Industrial Society and Its Future* is the 1995 manifesto of [[ted-kaczynski|Theodore Kaczynski]], the Unabomber, and the text on which his growing reputation as a modern visionary critic of technology rests.[1] Badlands Media argues that the manifesto's author is much less a philosopher of technology than a disturbed product of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]] mind-control project of the Cold War.[1]

## Core assumption

The article isolates what it calls the core assumption of the manifesto: "The system does not and cannot exist to satisfy human needs. Instead, it is human behavior that has to be modified to fit the needs of the system. It is the fault of technology, because the system is guided not by ideology but by technical necessity."[1]

In that single statement, Badlands Media argues, Kaczynski denies two things: free will, since the system that shaped humanity is said to grow exploitatively under a systemic impulse to destroy until freedom and nature are gone; and the causal role of ideas, both right and wrong, in shaping history.[1]

## Convergence with the oligarchy

The article's central claim is that the manifesto's fundamental assumptions about humanity, technology and history are identical with those of figures it labels death cultists — [[prince-philip|Prince Philip]], [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] and [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]] — and are shared by eco-terrorists such as Andreas Malm, David Skribina, Roger Hallam and Derrick Jensen.[1] One side chooses to adapt to the supposed consequences of technological progress and become alphas in the emergent Brave New World; the other chooses to go "maximum John Savage" and burn it all down.[1] Both, the article says, are trapped in a [[double-bind-theory|double bind]] and arrive at the same techno-feudal dystopia.[1]
