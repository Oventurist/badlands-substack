# 2023 China Respiratory Illness Outbreak

The 2023 China respiratory illness outbreak was a surge of respiratory infections reported across Chinese schools and hospitals in November 2023, which drew international attention after the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] formally requested disease data from Beijing.[1]

## Course of the outbreak

China called for vigilance as the surge hit schools and hospitals during the country's first full winter season after it lifted its strict COVID-19 restrictions in December 2022.[1] Cases among children appeared especially high in northern areas such as Beijing and Liaoning province, where hospitals warned of long waiting times.[1] The State Council said influenza would peak during the winter and spring and that mycoplasma pneumoniae infection would remain high in some areas, while also warning of the risk of a rebound in COVID-19 infections; it instructed that "all localities should strengthen information reporting on infectious diseases to ensure information is reported in a timely and accurate manner."[1]

## WHO involvement

The situation came into international focus when the WHO asked China for more information, citing a report by the Program for Monitoring Emerging Diseases (ProMED) on clusters of undiagnosed pneumonia in children.[1] The WHO subsequently said that no unusual or novel pathogen had been detected.[1] Both China and the WHO had previously faced questions about the transparency of reporting on the earliest COVID-19 cases, which emerged in the central city of Wuhan in late 2019.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], mocked the terminology of the reporting, asking whether diagnosing pneumonia as "undiagnosed pneumonia" was not itself a diagnosis of pneumonia, and suggesting that "whoever is writing 'the science' these days needs a code update."[1] She noted that the outbreak arrived almost exactly four years after the first diagnosed case of COVID-19 in Wuhan on November 19, 2019 — itself one month after the October 2019 [[event-201|tabletop exercise]] that simulated a coronavirus pandemic.[1] Characterising the episode as "both a rerun and a remake," she argued that labelling a diagnosis "undiagnosed" eroded believability from the outset, observed that the "election variant" was bacterial this time, and predicted demands for billions in taxpayer funding for new pharmaceutical products moving through the FDA.[1]
