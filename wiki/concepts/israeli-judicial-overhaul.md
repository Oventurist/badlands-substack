# Israeli Judicial Overhaul

The Israeli judicial overhaul was a legislative program pushed by the government of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in 2023 to limit the powers of [[israel|Israel]]'s judiciary, and the subject of a landmark ruling by the country's highest court on January 1, 2024.[1]

## The reasonableness law

A central piece of the overhaul, passed in July 2023 and akin to a constitutional amendment, would have stripped the court of its power to abrogate government decisions it deems "unreasonable in the extreme."[1] Before the October 7 attack by [[hamas|Hamas]] on southern Israel, hundreds of thousands of Israelis turned out weekly to protest Netanyahu's push to curtail the court's powers and shift control toward the elected government.[1]

## Supreme Court ruling

Israel's highest court struck the law down, with eight justices voting in favor of striking it and seven against.[1] The ruling was expected to revive the deep political and social strife generated by the judicial reform effort the previous year, at a moment when the country was still reeling from October 7 and embroiled in a devastating war in [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## The March 2023 pause

The overhaul provoked a national crisis in March 2023. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] fired Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] a day after Gallant called on him to halt the plan, a dismissal read as a signal that the government would press ahead with the legislation that week.[2] Massive protests erupted across Israel in response, angering military and business leaders and raising concerns among Israel's allies.[2] Netanyahu and his allies argued the plan would restore balance between the judicial and executive branches and rein in what they saw as an interventionist court with liberal sympathies, while critics said the constellation of laws would remove checks and balances from Israel's democratic system and concentrate power in the hands of the governing coalition.[2]

In a much-anticipated 8pm televised address, delivered as hundreds of thousands of protesters filled the streets and rival protest groups clashed, Netanyahu confirmed he would pause the overhaul, framing the decision as necessary to avoid civil war.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators, following [[just-human|Just Human]], read the protest wave as a [[color-revolution|color revolution]] comparable to the 2014 Ukraine coup and the 2023 unrest in Brazil.[2]
