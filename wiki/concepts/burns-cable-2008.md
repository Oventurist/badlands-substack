# 2008 Burns Cable (08MOSCOW265)

The **2008 Burns cable**, catalogued as 08MOSCOW265, is a secret U.S. diplomatic cable written in February 2008 by [[william-burns|William Burns]], then serving in the intelligence and diplomatic community and later CIA director under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] The cable was never intended for public consumption but became publicly accessible through the efforts of [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]].[1] It spells out in clear terms how Ukraine's admission into [[nato|NATO]] would be viewed by Russia — not only by the Putin government but by the entire Russian population.[1]

## Contents

Burns wrote that Ukrainian and Georgian NATO aspirations "not only touch a raw nerve in Russia, they engender serious concerns about the consequences for stability in the region," with Russia perceiving encirclement and efforts to undermine its regional influence, while also fearing unpredictable and uncontrolled consequences affecting Russian security interests.[1] He relayed that experts warned Russia was particularly worried that strong divisions within Ukraine over NATO membership — with much of the ethnic-Russian community opposed — could lead to a major split involving violence or, at worst, civil war.[1]

The cable's most cited passage warns of the consequences of a premature Membership Action Plan offer, stating that "Ukrainian entry into NATO is the brightest of all redlines for the Russian elite (not just Putin)."[1] The phrase implies that nothing could provoke Russia more than this single action, which would leave Russia feeling insecure and threatened in the extreme; the article argued Burns made clear that such a step would essentially be seen as a declaration of war.[1]

Burns further wrote that in more than two and a half years of conversations with key Russian players, "from knuckle-draggers in the dark recesses of the Kremlin to Putin's sharpest liberal critics," he had yet to find anyone who viewed Ukraine in NATO as anything other than a direct challenge to Russian interests.[1] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited that line as evidence that the notion of a Russian constituency enamored of Western hegemony and rallying behind Alexei Navalny is a myth, and stressed that because these were internal documents never meant for public scrutiny they are void of typical propaganda and deal largely in geopolitical fact.[1]

A later passage predicted the operational consequences: a MAP offer "would be seen not as a technical step along a long road toward membership, but as throwing down the strategic gauntlet," that "today's Russia will respond," that Russian–Ukrainian relations would go into a deep freeze with Moscow contemplating economic measures from immediate gas-price increases to world market levels to a clampdown on Ukrainian workers in Russia, and that it would "create fertile soil for Russian meddling in Crimea and eastern Ukraine."[1]

## Significance

DeLarme characterized the cable as Burns's warning to an incoming administration that at least outwardly cultivated the appearance of desiring a tenable relationship with Russia.[1] He concluded that Washington, at the highest levels of government, has known for at least fifteen years exactly what would happen if it continued down this path, and that knowing this, officials chose to apply steady pressure until a breaking point was reached and the [[russia-ukraine-war|invasion of Ukraine]] began on 24 February 2022.[1]
