# 5GW Counter-Offensive – The Censorship Regime and the Road to 2024

## Background

In September 2023, Badlands Media contributor Ryan DeLarme published an article framing the period between the article's publication and the November 2024 U.S. presidential election as a deliberate, quadrennial escalation in what he terms a "5G war" — more commonly rendered in the corpus as **5th Generation Warfare (5GW)** — waged by what the source calls the "tech tyrants" and "Western hegemonic establishment" against alternative and dissident media voices. The article, dated **September 27, 2023**, opens by asserting that veterans of this conflict have become familiar with a recurring pattern: major elections are preceded by a systematic censorship offensive targeting individuals and platforms that challenge establishment narratives. The source claims that by September 2023, this offensive was already visible in the targeting of specific individuals, government-imposed platform access restrictions, the activation of political attack groups, and financial platform freezes on certain publications.

The article appeared on Badlands Media, a citizen-journalism outlet that describes itself as attempting to "take back the narrative from the MSM" and positions its contributors as "the news now." DeLarme also cross-promoted his work through the Underground Newswire Substack.

## The Online Safety Bill

The central legislative catalyst identified in the article is the United Kingdom's **Online Safety Bill (OSB)**, which the UK Parliament passed in **September 2023** and which the source characterizes as a sweeping censorship framework. The bill's full legislative title is *"A Bill to make provision for and in connection with the regulation by OFCOM of certain internet services; for and in connection with communications offences; and for connected purposes."*

The **Office of Communications (Ofcom)** was designated as the primary regulatory body responsible for implementing the bill. Ofcom's recently appointed Chief Executive, **Dame Melanie Dawes**, is quoted in the article as having declared at a press conference: *"Today is a major milestone in the mission to create a safer life online for children and adults in the UK. Everyone at Ofcom feels privileged to be entrusted with this important role, and we're ready to start implementing these new laws."*

The UK government publicly framed the legislation as a measure to make the UK "the safest place in the world to be online." The article argues that the actual effect will be a more heavily censored and locked-down internet for British users, and that the bill will establish a precedent for other nations within what the source calls the "Western hegemonic" sphere. According to DeLarme, the OSB compels tech platforms to comply with its rules or face "humongous fines," and that persistent non-compliance could result in a platform being banned outright. The article draws a parallel to **2022**, when the French government issued censorship demands against **Rumble** over its hosting of Russian news sources (Sputnik, RT), and Rumble responded by blocking access to France rather than complying.

The source highlights specific clauses within the bill as alarming. **Section 10** criminalizes the transmission of "harmful, false and threatening communications" without clearly defining the criteria for "harmful" or "false." **Clause 156** makes it a crime to "send a message" containing false information, while **Clause 157** grants immunity to every newspaper, television channel, and streaming service. The article asserts that the bill's public marketing — centered on "combating child pornography" — functions as pretextual window-dressing, noting that the source claims 99% of child pornography resides on the deep web outside the bill's purview. DeLarme compares the OSB to the **USA PATRIOT Act**, arguing that both use evocative framing to mask rights-curtailing provisions.

## The Targeting of Russell Brand

The article identifies the simultaneous treatment of comedian and activist **Russell Brand** as a prominent example of the censorship offensive in action. DeLarme explicitly states that his scrutiny of the Brand situation should not be read as dismissal of sexual abuse allegations, which he says should always be taken seriously. Rather, he frames the timing of the allegations as politically motivated: *"the only reason this is even happening right now is that there appears to be a coordinated effort to remove as many effective communicators from the 5GW chessboard as possible in the lead up to 2024."*

The article draws comparisons to previous targets of what DeLarme calls the "Deep-State playbook": **Julian Assange**, who faced sexual abuse allegations after public empathy shifted toward him, and responded by calling them a CIA smear campaign; **Matt Gaetz**, whom the Biden administration investigated but for which insufficient evidence was found to secure a conviction; and **Donald Trump**, who faced allegations from E. Jean Carroll and Stormy Daniels. The source contends that the pattern reveals a consistent strategy: accusations are deployed against effective dissident communicators while establishment-loyal figures accused of similar misconduct — including **Prince Andrew**, **Bill Clinton**, and **Joe Biden** — receive protection.

According to the article, Brand had transformed from a hedonistic lifestyle into a sober, family-oriented advocate who answers to "a higher power" and began podcasting to "tell the truth and expose the system and narrative." The source quotes a social media post (image reproduced in the article) contrasting Brand's personal transformation with the coordinated attack against him, his wife, and his children.

The British government, which had just passed the Online Safety Bill, became actively involved in the allegations. **Caroline Nokes**, a Conservative Party legislator, is cited as having urged police in both Britain and the United States to investigate the "incredibly shocking" allegations. **Prime Minister Rishi Sunak** issued a statement through his spokesman, **Max Blain**, who said the claims were *"very serious and concerning, and those making the allegations should be treated seriously and treated with sensitivity."* DeLarme questions whether Sunak's intervention was motivated by compassion or self-interest, noting that Brand is one of Sunak's biggest critics.

Following these developments, the British government requested that big-tech platforms censor Brand's online content, and some platforms complied. The article links this directly to the Online Safety Bill and the **Trusted News Initiative**.

## Rumble and X in the Crosshairs

The article identifies **Rumble** — described as "the premier free-speech platform advocating alternative YouTube" — as the censorship regime's primary target in the run-up to 2024. Rumble's CEO, **Chris Pavlovski**, is noted as lacking the financial resources of **Elon Musk**, while simultaneously challenging the censorship regime more intensely than Musk's platform, making Rumble both a high-priority target and more vulnerable.

The source recalls the **2020 election** as the previous major censorship event, when the Hunter Biden laptop story first leaked and platforms including Facebook, YouTube, and Google suppressed discussion. Since then, alternative infrastructure has emerged, including **Truth Social**, **Rumble**, and the **Brave browser**. The article asserts that Truth Social functions as an "echo chamber" and that the establishment's primary goal is to eliminate Rumble and revert **Twitter (X)** to its previous censored state.

The article cites a specific campaign in **September 2023** in which journalists from the **Associated Press** and the **Washington Post** targeted Rumble and its creators. The **Washington Post** is reported to have contacted sponsors of an upcoming event featuring **Dave Portnoy** of Barstool Sports, characterizing Portnoy as a "misogynic racist" in an effort to sabotage advertising deals. A sponsor is quoted as having received a message to the effect: *"Dave's a misogynic racist. Do you want to defend yourselves advertising at this event?"*

Rumble's position is described as precarious: if it continues rejecting government censorship demands, the end goal is a complete ban on the platform and its content creators through access restriction. The article argues that Badlands Media itself depends on Rumble's infrastructure and warns that Rumble's fall would set a precedent discouraging other platforms from challenging the regime.

## The Trusted News Initiative and the ADL

The **Trusted News Initiative (TNI)** is identified as a key institutional actor in the censorship regime. Founded in **2019** by the **BBC**, the TNI is described as *"a global coalition of news organizations and tech firms with the stated goal of combating fake news"* with a particular focus on disinformation around "moments of jeopardy, such as **elections**." The article asserts that the TNI has already attempted to interfere with **Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'**s 2024 presidential campaign, prompting a lawsuit while functioning as "gatekeepers of information during election cycles." A February 2023 Daily Sceptic article is cited as source for the RFK Jr. lawsuit claim.

The **Anti-Defamation League (ADL)** is highlighted as having conducted a similar campaign against **Elon Musk** after he acquired Twitter (X) and refused to satisfy the ADL's demands. DeLarme frames this as part of the same coordinated offensive that recurs every four years, directly referencing his own prior Badlands article, *"The Anti-Dissent League."* The article argues that Musk's transition from "revered" entrepreneur to pariah was triggered by his announced plan to buy Twitter, after which former watchdog organizations turned against him and formal investigations into Musk and his companies multiplied. The source asserts that **Rumble** and **X** are "the frontlines of this information war" and that the establishment needs both eliminated: Rumble because it challenges the regime more intensely despite fewer resources, and X because it must be returned to its previous censored state.

## Related Narratives

The article is situated within Badlands Media's broader coverage of what it terms the **5GW censorship regime** and the **censorship-industrial complex**. It references DeLarme's earlier Badlands article *"The Anti-Dissent League"* concerning the ADL's role in shaping online discourse, and frames the Online Safety Bill as the legal mechanism by which the 2023–2024 censorship offensive will be institutionalized in the UK, with global spillover effects.

The source's conceptual framework holds that major Western elections trigger predictable waves of platform de-platforming, financial censorship, and media-coordinated smear campaigns against dissident voices — a pattern the article argues has been deployed against figures ranging from Julian Assange to Matt Gaetz to Donald Trump to Russell Brand, while protecting establishment-aligned figures accused of comparable misconduct.
