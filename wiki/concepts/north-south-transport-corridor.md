# North-South Transport Corridor

The North-South Transport Corridor (NSTC) is a multimodal overland and maritime trade route developed under the Shanghai Cooperation Organization to compete with the Suez Canal.[1]

[[iran|Iran]] has become a geographic centerpiece of the corridor, a development cited by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] as evidence that the country — once geopolitically isolated — now maintains strong relations with major world powers, most notably Russia and China.[1] He argued that the deep economic entanglement created by such routes would keep Iran accountable, because economic pressure from inside and outside the country promotes peace and deters kinetic conflict.[1]

The argument was advanced in opposition to reported [[pentagon|Pentagon]] planning for a sustained military campaign against Iran, on the grounds that Iran has no incentive to provoke a war it knows would draw in the United States and Europe.[1]

## Completion of the Iranian link

The final piece needed to complete the corridor is the [[rasht-astara-railway|Rasht–Astara railway]], a 114-mile stretch of track in northern Iran linking Rasht in Gilan province to Astara on the Iran–[[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] border.[2] [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] signed a deal with Iran three years earlier to finance and build the line; [[russia|Russia]] and Iran signed the final portion of the agreement in February 2026, with construction reportedly beginning in April 2026.[2] Badlands analysis presented Putin's outsized investment in so short a segment as explicable only by the corridor's role in competing with the Suez Canal for freight moving between Asia and Europe.[2]

## US interest and adjacent networks

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement of the [[trump-route-for-international-peace-and-prosperity|Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity]] through [[armenia|Armenia]] — promising American energy companies access "from Central Asia all the way to the United States" — amounted to signaling that the US intends to join the NSTC.[2] The likely tie-in point would be [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]], site of a previously announced $4 billion US railroad equipment deal, connecting across eastern Russia to Alaska.[2] Related developments included the restoration of Baku–Tbilisi passenger rail service between Azerbaijan and Georgia after its COVID-era suspension, and [[china|China]]'s launch of a new container rail corridor linking Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, China, and Afghanistan.[2] The cumulative effect, the analysis held, is the assembly of the largest transportation system in the world across the Eurasian landmass, with Trump aligning himself with Putin and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] against the Paris-based [[suez-company|Suez Company]] and its [[city-of-london|City of London]] affiliates.[2]
