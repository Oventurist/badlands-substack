# Trump Allies' Plans to Blunt Federal Reserve Independence

In April 2024 the Wall Street Journal reported that allies of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were quietly drafting proposals that would attempt to erode the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s independence if the former president won a second term, amid a deepening divide among his advisers over how aggressively to challenge the central bank's authority.[1]

## The reported proposals

Former Trump administration officials and other supporters of the presumptive GOP nominee had in recent months discussed a range of proposals, from incremental policy changes to the long-shot assertion that the president himself should play a role in setting interest rates.[1] A small group — whose work was so secretive that even some prominent former Trump economic aides were unaware of it — produced a roughly ten-page document outlining a policy vision for the central bank.[1]

The group argued that Trump should be consulted on interest-rate decisions, and the draft recommended subjecting Fed regulations to White House review and more forcefully using the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] as a check on the central bank.[1] It further contended that a returning President Trump would have the authority to oust [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] as Fed chair before the end of his four-year term in 2026, though Powell would likely remain on the Fed's board of governors.[1]

It could not be determined whether Trump was aware of or had signed off on the effort, although some people close to the discussions believed the work had received his blessing.[1] Trump senior advisers [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] and [[chris-lacivita|Chris LaCivita]] issued a statement warning that "unless a message is coming directly from President Trump or an authorized member of his campaign team, no aspect of future presidential staffing or policy announcements should be deemed official."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the WSJ report as one of the establishment's prime narrative-setters hitting the panic button over a second Trump term, and suggested it would remind Anons of Trump's choice of [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]] as one of the presidential portraits adorning the Oval Office during his first administration.[1] Applying his practice of reading enemy panic patterns to identify what the adversary fears, he listed three fears: that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] fears Trump redefining presidential authority over money issuance and returning to the Kennedy stance of empowering the US Treasury rather than vesting authority in central banks; that they fear losing control over interest rates and therefore their ability to time recessions and the boom-bust cycle; and that they assume Trump will be unable to influence the Fed board, citing the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] as an example — a body Trump completely remade during his first term.[1]
