# March Against Monsanto

The March Against Monsanto was a 2013 wave of mass protests against corporate influence in agriculture, described by Badlands Media as "one of the largest mass protests that no one remembers, aside from those who attended."[1] The article uses it as a control case: an example of what happens to a genuinely grass-roots protest that threatens big business, in contrast to the heavily promoted and well-funded demonstrations produced by the [[plausible-deniability-network|plausible deniability network]].[1]

## Coverage

The protests received only brief mention on a handful of cable news outlets and zero live coverage.[1] What coverage did appear was skewed, framing the demonstrations as solely about genetically modified foods while omitting glyphosate — the primary herbicide used in industrial farming — and ignoring the dangers of corporate influence in agriculture, which was the actual purpose of the protests.[1]

## Glyphosate aftermath

Glyphosate's toxicity was known to [[monsanto|Monsanto]] but concealed for decades, and became increasingly exposed after the protests.[1] In 2013 the [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] determined glyphosate to be noncarcinogenic; in 2015 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified it as a probable carcinogen; and in 2018 a jury found that Monsanto's Roundup was carcinogenic after the company admitted it "has not conducted any 12-month or longer chronic toxicity studies" of current Roundup products.[1]

By August 2019, [[bayer-ag|Bayer]]/Monsanto was reported to be seeking to settle 18,000 US cancer lawsuits for $8 billion, and by June 2020 it had agreed to pay that amount to settle cancer claims.[1]

## Significance in the article

The author states the example is not meant as commentary on the ethics of Monsanto/Bayer or on herbicide choice, but purely to demonstrate the suppression and distortion that organic protest receives when it threatens an interest deeply embedded in the Western political establishment.[1]
