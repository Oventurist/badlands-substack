# Workplace Brain Monitoring

Workplace brain monitoring refers to the use of neurotechnology to read and record employees' brain activity, a set of applications showcased at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and reported in early February 2023.[1]

## Davos presentation

Breitbart reported on Friday, February 3, 2023 that during the WEF gathering in Davos, Switzerland the previous month, an attendee delivered a presentation depicting a world in which employers at major companies could spy on their workers' brain activity.[1] Under the scenario described, employers would have the power to fire workers and report them to government authorities simply for thinking the "wrong way."[1] The presentation further suggested that government could use brain wave data as evidence in a "crime," meaning a person's own thoughts could be used against them in a court of law.[1] In what The Gateway Pundit called an Orwellian twist, the presenter characterized giving business bosses and government power over thoughts as showing the "positive use cases" of brain monitoring technology.[1] The story was carried by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]], sourcing [[breitbart|Breitbart]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] asked what could possibly go wrong when "a close-knit fraternity of sociopaths" gains the ability to observe brain-wave patterns and punish people for their thoughts.[1] He observed that the same people who called [[donald-trump|Donald John Trump]] a dictator were likely enthusiastic about the technology, joked that the thoughts of the Davos ruling class could be predicted without any cutting-edge equipment, and challenged anyone to identify a single positive application.[1] DeLarme added that although he would normally doubt anyone would volunteer for such monitoring, the way people lined up for experimental injections in response to a virus with a 99% survival rate suggested otherwise — concluding that all it takes is a perceived threat and a pervasive marketing campaign.[1]
