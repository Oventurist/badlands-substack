# Fiat Currency

**Fiat currency** is money whose value is not backed by a physical commodity such as gold, but is instead declared legal tender by government decree. In the Badlands Media corpus, fiat currency is presented as the monetary regime the source opposes — the system it says was imposed on Americans through the [[Federal Reserve]] and the 1913 legislative package, displacing the earlier [[Gold Standard]] (1873–1933).

## The corpus's framing

The article "[[1913-the-year-the-cabal-enslaved-america|1913: The Year the Cabal Enslaved America]]" argues that prior to 1913 the United States operated under a "hard money" system in which "the value of money was worth astronomically more than it is today." It claims that bankers — who "hated the gold standard so intensely" — began calling as early as 1873 for "a permanent fiat currency issued by a central bank," and that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve Act]] realized this goal by removing citizens' ability to redeem currency for gold.

The source asserts that with the Fed and the income tax (administered by the [[Internal Revenue Service]]), "Wilson had successfully enslaved every American to the fiat monetary system." It contrasts fiat currency with the gold standard, which it claims "forced the bankers into a siloed depression while citizens flourished" by increasing the buying power of money.

## Relationship to other concepts

- **[[Gold Standard]]** — the commodity-backed system fiat currency replaced, per the source.
- **[[Federal Reserve]]** — the institution the article credits with entrenching fiat issuance.
- **Hard Money** — the Founders' coin-based alternative the source advocates restoring.
- **[[Cabal]]** — the force the article credits with imposing fiat currency in 1913.

## Fiat and the digital dollar

In "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", Joe Lange addresses the objection that a [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDC]] would merely be a digital fiat dollar. He concedes the point conditionally — "Yes, unless it's backed by gold, which is required by the constitution" — and argues that the litigation strategy he attributes to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is designed to have the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] remove fiat currency and the central bank altogether by revisiting the Legal Tender Cases, the Gold Clause decisions, and the [[federal-reserve-act|Federal Reserve Act of 1913]]. The article predicts the global fiat system will collapse under unpayable debt in a crash larger than 2008 and worse than the Depression.
