# Fiat Currency

Fiat currency — money issued by a central bank without backing by a physical commodity — is the monetary system the article says Americans have lived under since 1913.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Bankers who resented the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] after 1873 began calling for the government to permanently adopt a fiat currency issued by a central bank.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) With the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]] and the [[revenue-act-of-1913|Revenue Act of 1913]], President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] is said to have "successfully enslaved every American to the fiat monetary system," in which every American must pay income tax and watch the continued devaluation of their money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article describes the resulting gold-less monetary system as one in which citizens have less power, unable to demand their gold back from bankers and therefore subject to manipulation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) It argues that in light of the reckless money printing prompted by the Covid-19 crisis, every currency in the world is becoming less valuable.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The prescription offered is to end central banking and the fiat monetary system, replacing them with a system that brings back inherent, underlying value and trust in each trade — one that would eradicate the possibility of corruption entering the system in the future.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Gold, France and the closing of the window

*A Week to Remember* recounts that in the early 1970s France sought repayment of a loan to the United States and, aware that Washington intended simply to print the money, demanded payment in gold instead.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Because gold cannot be created out of thin air like fiat money, the account holds that the US government responded by making it illegal to pay foreign entities in gold, obliging everyone to accept dollars.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) That measure is said to have stood for fifty years before being quietly repealed a couple of years ago, at the moment the [[brics|BRICS]] nations began discussing a gold-backed currency for international settlement in place of the US dollar.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## The end of the fiat system

"Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?" argues that the fiat global financial system is coming to an end, collapsing under a weight of debt that was never going to be repaid.[3] Joe Lange writes that the fiat money system is deliberately being bankrupted while a replacement built by the [[brics|BRICS]] nations stands ready, and that most countries have repatriated gold to back their own currencies.[3]

He warns that a [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDC]] would be nothing more than a digital fiat dollar unless it is backed by gold, and frames the objective as a Supreme Court fight to remove fiat currencies and the central bank altogether in favor of redeemable, [[gold-standard|gold-backed]] money.[3]

## "Fiat has won" — the BIS declaration of 2023

In February 2023 [[agustin-carstens|Agustin Carstens]], head of the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]], declared that the battle between cryptocurrency and fiat currencies — money that has legal tender by government decree — had ended with fiat as the winner, telling Bloomberg TV that "that battle has been won … A technology doesn't make for trusted money" and that "only the legal, historical infrastructure behind central banks can give great credibility" to money.[4] Carstens anticipated a "strong statement" from the G20 nations calling for stronger regulation of digital assets.[4]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] responded that monetary policy is one of the few fields where a mere declaration can move an entire industry, and that the "self-declared and government endorsed international banking cartel" was repeating the strategy it used before the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] was pushed through in 1913.[4] He argued that the free-banking era functioned as a long-term psychological operation to strip public confidence in citizen-based currency systems, pointing to the debt-free and interest-free [[colonial-scrip|Colonial Scrip]] of the American colonies as a lost model of competent citizen money, and concluded that cryptocurrency represented a return to citizen-produced money that the central bankers were, for the moment, defeating.[4]

## "Fiat currency is DEAD" (December 2022)

In her December 2022 article on the [[trump-digital-trading-cards|Trump Digital Trading Cards]], [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] declared flatly that "fiat currency is DEAD," arguing the United States financial system was heading for collapse after decades of uncontrolled national debt and Federal Reserve money printing that devalued the dollar with every sheet issued.[5] She compared the expected outcome to the hyperinflation of the Weimar Republic in 1933, when a wheelbarrow of cash was needed to buy a loaf of bread.[5]

Gillespie read [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s remark in the NFT unveiling video that he would be "better than Lincoln, better than Washington" as monetary signalling — [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]] standing for U.S. coinage and [[george-washington|Washington]] for the U.S. dollar — indicating that a new currency associated with his presidency was coming.[5] She placed the replacement in the framework of asset-backed money held in [[digital-wallets|digital wallets]], as distinct from [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]], and linked it to the rise of [[brics|BRICS]] and the promise of [[nesara-gesara|NESARA/GESARA]].[5]
