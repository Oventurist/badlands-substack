# British Columbia Drug Decriminalization Pilot

The British Columbia drug decriminalization pilot is a three-year provincial program, begun in 2023, that decriminalized possession of small amounts of opioids, cocaine, methamphetamine and MDMA in the Canadian province.[1] Selling those substances remains prohibited under the pilot, a limit highlighted when [[jerry-martin|Jerry Martin]] opened [[the-drugs-store|The Drugs Store]] in Vancouver's Downtown Eastside in May 2023 and operated it completely illegally.[1]

The pilot sits against the backdrop of Canada's overdose epidemic, of which the Downtown Eastside has long been considered ground zero, and of fentanyl's role in driving record overdose deaths in both Canada and the United States.[1] Advocates such as Martin argue that decriminalizing possession without providing a tested, unadulterated supply leaves users exposed to fentanyl contamination.[1] Critics in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], including [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]], treated the province's "very progressive drug laws" as evidence of Canadian societal decline.[1]
