# Biden Student Debt Forgiveness Plan

The Biden student debt forgiveness plan was an initiative by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to cancel a portion of federal student loan debt, which the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] found unconstitutional in a ruling issued at the end of June 2023.[1]

## The "new path" under the Higher Education Act

On the Friday following the decision, Biden announced that he would pursue a "new path" to ease the burden of student loans, grounding the revised approach in a different statute — the Higher Education Act — rather than the authority underpinning his original plan.[1] He said the new approach was intended to provide "student debt relief to as many borrowers as possible as quickly as possible" while remaining "consistent with today's ruling."[1] The move was widely characterized as an effort to circumvent the Court's holding.[1]

## Reaction

Biden described the Supreme Court's decision as "unthinkable."[1] Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the decision was "completely expected" and that the premise of the plan was detached from American life, because it would require all taxpayers — including those who never attended college, those who chose trade school, and those who repaid their loans responsibly — to subsidize the debt of what she called the most privileged class of Americans.[1] She noted that a viable college degree confers higher earning potential than none, across all demographic combinations.[1]

In her framing, the ruling limited Biden's ability to force contributing members of society to pay for "gender studies and faux history degrees" of borrowers demanding tribute from auto mechanics, plumbers, and service industry professionals.[1] She concluded that what was actually unthinkable was that Biden remained in office, adding that "stolen elections have catastrophic consequences."[1]
