# Equal-Time Rule

The equal-time rule is a United States broadcast regulation, administered by the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]], requiring broadcast licensees that provide airtime to a political candidate to make comparable time available to other legally qualified candidates for the same office.[1]

## The Colbert–Talarico episode

In February 2026, [[stephen-colbert|Stephen Colbert]] used his program to criticize the FCC as "bullies," claiming that [[cbs|CBS]] lawyers had told him "in no uncertain terms … that we could not have [Talarico] on the broadcast," referring to Rep. [[james-talarico|James Talarico]].[1] CBS disputed the characterization, stating: "The Late Show was not prohibited by CBS from broadcasting the interview with Rep. James Talarico. The show was provided legal guidance that the broadcast could trigger the FCC equal-time rule for two other candidates, including Rep. Jasmine Crockett [D-Texas], and presented options for how the equal time for other candidates could be fulfilled."[1] CBS is owned by [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]], which was simultaneously the subject of merger talks requiring FCC approval of broadcast-license transfers.[1]

## Interpretation in the corpus

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that equal-time regulations "have always been a thing" and were simply not enforced by the FCC during the Trump era, adding that outlets seeking "the special access and protections from the government" must accept the accompanying rules.[1] She further contended that the rules were written for a different era, that broadcast networks survive only because of government protections and pharmaceutical advertising, and that neither is any longer necessary.[1]
