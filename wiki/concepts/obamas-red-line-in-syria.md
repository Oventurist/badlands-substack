# Obama's Red Line in Syria

The "red line" was President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2012–2013 declaration that the use of chemical weapons by the government of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in [[syria|Syria]] would trigger American military action. Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the episode as both a public humiliation for Obama and a piece of selective narrative management.[1]

## The episode

Obama threatened to bomb Syria if chemical weapons were used again in the country's war, and was then humiliated when they were used again and he took no action.[1] Citing *Politico*, the article notes that although [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was a private citizen when the red-line saga unfolded in late August and early September 2013, she played a supporting role: she had several conversations with Obama, his top aides and Secretary of State John Kerry, and issued several public statements supporting him, including unplanned remarks on the day of her White House visit.[1] *Politico* concluded that Clinton had put her stamp on one of the most consequential episodes of Obama's presidency, one she has sometimes defended and at other times tried to distance herself from.[1] Lange asks pointedly why Clinton was trying to distance herself from the debacle while running for president.[1]

## The selective-outrage argument

Lange's central objection is not to opposing chemical weapons but to the asymmetry of the red line: "Syria wasn't the only one using chemical weapons."[1] Assad was battling [[isis|ISIS]], which he says was constantly using chemical weapons in the war — the other "bad actors" referenced in [[qanon|Q]] drop #1306.[1] Obama, he notes, never declared a red line against chemical weapons use by ISIS; instead he described the group as the "junior varsity" team compared with Al-Qaeda while it conquered territory across the Middle East and publicly beheaded captives, which Lange reads as an attempt to distract Americans from the threat.[1]

## Relationship to the later strikes

The article connects the unenforced red line to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s later action: in April 2018 Trump, in conjunction with France and the United Kingdom, ordered [[2018-us-strikes-on-syria|strikes on Syria]] in retaliation for a suspected chemical weapons attack.[1] Lange argues the chemical-weapons rationale served as a "cover story" for destroying the satellite Iranian nuclear facility that Q said existed in Syria.[1] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
