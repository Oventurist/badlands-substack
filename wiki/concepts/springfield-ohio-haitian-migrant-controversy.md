# Springfield, Ohio Haitian Migrant Controversy

The Springfield, Ohio Haitian migrant controversy refers to the dispute that erupted in the summer and autumn of 2024 over the arrival of a large Haitian migrant population in the city of Springfield, Ohio, and over widely circulated local claims that some migrants were killing and eating pets and local wildlife.[1] The story was reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and carried in the September 10, 2024 edition of the Badlands News Brief.[1]

## Scale and municipal strain

Springfield, a city of roughly 60,000 residents, was reported to have been "overwhelmed" by approximately 20,000 Haitian illegal migrants who flocked to the city.[1] City officials described the influx as exacerbating a "significant housing crisis," a characterization documented in correspondence on housing and immigration associated with the office of Senator [[jd-vance|JD Vance]].[1]

## Claims about pets and wildlife

At an August 27 City Commission meeting, one local resident stated that Haitians were "in the park grabbing ducks, cutting the heads off, and eating them."[1] Another resident posted to a Facebook group that a neighbor's cat had gone missing, only for the animal to be seen "hanging from a branch, like you'd do a deer for butchering, & they [the Haitians] were carving it up to eat."[1] The cat claim was refuted publicly as racist fear-mongering, while others circulated material asserting that cats are indeed part of Haitian cuisine.[1] A further resident told the commission that she "can't take it anymore," describing littering on her yard and harassment of her and her husband.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as a test of the limits of American empathy and tolerance, asking whether residents of Springfield were "empathetic and tolerant enough to accommodate Haitians eating their pets."[1] The commentary drew a line at household animals — joking that seagulls and prairie dogs are nuisances but that pets are not negotiable in a country where "people have pet insurance, doggie day care, and monogrammed clothing for Fido."[1] Ashe also tied the food question to inflation, writing that "we're all eating things we didn't eat before as Kamalanomics turns our once usual grocery haul into a luxury wish list."[1]

The piece concluded that "the migrant madness is reaching a crescendo," predicting the behavior would be "explained away as a cultural difference… you know, like all the theft, rape, and murder," and that "weaponized empathy and tolerance of the intolerable was always going to end this way."[1]

## Later citation as evidence of systemic strain

In a November 12, 2024 essay, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cited Springfield as a concrete illustration of his broader argument about open borders, noting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had described a city of around 50,000 people receiving 30,000 Haitians.[2] Carlson wrote that the influx had put "huge strains on every aspect of life there," with the medical and educational systems, jobs, crime and housing prices all out of control, and that the media dismissed the account as a conspiracy theory.[2] He used the case to support his contention that migrants flown in and housed at taxpayer expense fill rental vacancies and drive up rents, benefiting investment companies such as [[blackrock|BlackRock]] while working-class Americans bear the cost.[2]

## Soft disclosure interpretation

Chapter 19 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series describes Haitian migrants in Springfield eating cats, dogs, ducks, geese and whatever else they could obtain in public, and argues that the story reached the mainstream because it functions as soft disclosure for sacrifices, human trafficking and darker activities the author declines to describe.[3] The author cites as supporting evidence the fact that [[john-legend|John Legend]] is from Springfield and released a video urging the public to leave the Haitian migrants alone, alongside Q Drop #1276, which names Legend directly.[3] The chapter closes by anticipating that the truth about Haiti has yet to be revealed.[3]
