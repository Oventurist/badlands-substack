# Morrison v. Olson

**Morrison v. Olson** (1988) is a United States Supreme Court decision that classified the independent counsel — a statutory predecessor to today's [[special-counsel|Special Counsel]] — as an inferior officer rather than a principal officer under the Appointments Clause.[1] The Court pointed to factors such as the office's limited jurisdiction, its performance of specific rather than general duties, and its temporary nature as hallmarks of inferior-officer status.[1]

## Significance

Because inferior officers may be appointed by the heads of departments without Senate confirmation, the ruling is central to the argument that an Attorney General may appoint a Special Counsel unilaterally.[1] The precedent was invoked in the 2024 debate over whether [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] could be installed as Special Counsel by a future Attorney General, a debate sharpened by Judge [[aileen-cannon|Eileen Cannon]]'s contrary ruling dismissing the classified-documents case brought by [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] on unconfirmed-appointment grounds.[1]
