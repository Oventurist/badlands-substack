# 2024 Republican Election Litigation Campaign

The 2024 Republican election litigation campaign was a coordinated pre-election legal effort by the [[republican-national-committee]] and allied groups, reported in late September 2024 as involving more than 120 lawsuits across 26 states, widely described as laying the groundwork for post-election challenges in November.[1]

## Scope and framing

The suits were concentrated in swing states such as Arizona, Michigan, Nevada and Pennsylvania — the jurisdictions expected to decide the contest between [[kamala-harris]] and [[donald-trump]].[1] Some legal experts and voting-rights groups characterized the blitz as an attempt to undercut public faith in the electoral system, and the Harris campaign accused Republicans of "scheming to sow distrust in our elections and undermine our democracy so they can cry foul when they lose."[1] Republicans countered that the litigation was aimed at restoring faith in elections by ensuring that people do not vote illegally.[1]

The effort was framed against the precedent of 2020, when Trump and his allies filed more than sixty hastily arranged lawsuits attempting to overturn his defeat to Joe Biden on claims of widespread fraud, all of which failed.[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands commentary situated the campaign within a broader analysis of election [[lawfare]], quoting attorney Robert Barnes's formulation that election claims are "Not Ripe in Spring, No Standing by Summer, Laches by Fall, and Moot by Winter."[1] The argument advanced was that election litigation is governed more by procedural precedent than by statutory text, that courts have repeatedly displayed an institutional bias toward trust in the system, and that judges and court officers generally lack the technological expertise to adjudicate complex election technology disputes.[1] The consequence is that election challenges are resolved on technicalities and procedural grounds rather than on the merits.[1]

The commentary further argued that public trust cannot be coerced — that once lost it is difficult to restore, and that the government's response to lost trust has been to silence and demonize those who name the loss rather than to repair it, with skeptics labeled dangerous or threatening.[1] On that basis the writer credited the RNC for preparing in advance of the election, in apparent contrast to its lack of preparation in 2020 and 2022, while expressing hope that the arguments filed would be sound and serve public trust.[1] The piece closed with an exhortation that there are no spectators in the [[information-war]] and that readers should vote, be vigilant, and be vocal.[1]
