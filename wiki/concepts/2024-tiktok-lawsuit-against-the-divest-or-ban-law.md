# 2024 TikTok Lawsuit Against the Divest-or-Ban Law

On Tuesday, May 7, 2024, [[tiktok|TikTok]] and its parent company [[bytedance|ByteDance]] sued to challenge the law President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had signed to force the sale or ban of the video sharing app.[1]

## The petition

The petition was filed with the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit.[1] It claimed that the law violates the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] rights of the platform's 170 million American users, and argued that the statute shuts down the platform based on "speculative and analytically flawed concerns about data security and content manipulation."[1] The filing stated: "For the first time in history, Congress has enacted a law that subjects a single, named speech platform to a permanent, nationwide ban."[1]

The companies asked the court to declare the law unconstitutional, to issue an order blocking Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] from enforcing it, and to grant "any further relief that may be appropriate."[1]

## The underlying statute

Biden signed the measure — see the [[protecting-americans-from-foreign-adversary-controlled-applications-act|Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act]] — as part of a foreign aid package passed in late April 2024.[1] It would force TikTok's sale within a year or subject the app to a ban in U.S. app stores.[1] The case was reported by [[politico|Politico]].[1]
