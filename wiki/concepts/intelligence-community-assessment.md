# 2017 Intelligence Community Assessment

The 2017 Intelligence Community Assessment (ICA) is the intelligence product on Russian interference whose drafting became the focus of a federal criminal investigation into former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]].[1]

The [[fbi|FBI]] prepared to question several witnesses in that investigation, concentrating on the ICA and how it was developed.[1] Investigators were expected to interview a small group of former intelligence officials involved in drafting the assessment, examining the origins of the inquiry.[1] The probe, led by the US Attorney's Office in Miami, had been underway for months and had already included initial witness interviews.[1]

The investigation centered in part on Brennan's 2023 congressional testimony about the creation of the assessment, and prosecutors had previously informed Brennan that he was a target of the inquiry, according to a letter from his attorney.[1]

## Authorship and 2026 witness disclosure

The January 2017 assessment was co-authored by [[gavin-wilde|Gavin Wilde]], who later served on the [[donald-trump|Trump]] National Security Council in 2018 and 2019 and worked on US intelligence assessments of alleged Russian influence efforts ahead of the 2020 election.[2] Badlands describes the assessment as flawed and notes that it was written at the direction of then-President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and largely overseen by then-FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], and former Director of National Intelligence [[james-clapper|James Clapper]].[2]

US intelligence agencies concluded through the assessment that [[russia|Russia]] interfered in the 2016 election primarily through cyber operations and influence campaigns aimed at undermining confidence in the electoral process and harming Democrat candidate [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[2] In May 2026 Wilde emerged as a newly disclosed witness before congressional investigators examining the origins and handling of the Trump–Russia probe, an inquiry tracing back to [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] and Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s review.[2]

## 2025 declassified tradecraft review

On July 2, 2025, CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] released a declassified "lessons-learned" review of the procedures and tradecraft employed in preparing the Intelligence Community Assessment conducted shortly after the 2016 election.[3] The review was published by the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] as an internal tradecraft examination.[3] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued that the more significant document remained classified — the report [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] "collected in July" 2016 — and tied its significance to the July 26, 2016 approval by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] of a plan to vilify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Brennan's July 28 meeting with [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] on "Russian malign influence" that produced the inter-agency Fusion Cell.[3]

## 2025 whistleblower testimony

A whistleblower who served as a Deputy National Intelligence Officer at the National Intelligence Council from 2015 to 2020 and was a key figure in producing the ICA on cyber threats to the 2016 U.S. presidential election told investigators he was pressured to alter his findings to support the narrative that Russia hacked cyber infrastructure to help Donald Trump win.[4] The testimony, contained in a 19-page report declassified by DNI Tulsi Gabbard in 2025, described a significant shift in the intelligence community's position after the November 2016 election.[4]
