# Shareholder Proxy Voting

Shareholder proxy voting is the annual process by which holders of a stock or fund vote on the composition of its board of directors and, in some cases, on how the entity is run.[1]

## Alleged manipulation

Badlands Media reported the claim — advanced by [[boaz-weinstein|Boaz Weinstein]] of [[saba-capital|Saba Capital]] in its 2024 campaign against ten [[blackrock|BlackRock]] closed-end funds — that BlackRock counted every non-vote as a vote in favor of management's recommendations, illegally insulating incumbent directors from challenge.[1] The article compares this to counting every non-voter in a presidential election as a vote for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Because the large majority of shareholders never vote, and those who do typically know nothing about the nominees and follow management's slate, results are systematically skewed toward the incumbent board and directors are effectively never replaced.[1]

## Use as a vehicle for anti-woke shareholder activism

The article notes that shareholders frustrated by [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]] policies have begun using the annual proxy vote to register objections and to propose alternative directors.[1] A prominent example was the attempted "mutiny" at [[disney|Disney]], where investors sought to seat directors focused on share price growth and possibly to remove chief executive [[bob-iger|Bob Iger]]; Disney's stock was down 15% over five years while the S&P 500 rose 74%.[1]
