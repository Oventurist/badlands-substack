# Gun Control Act of 1968

The Gun Control Act of 1968 is the principal federal statute governing the manufacture, sale and transfer of firearms in the United States, and it supplies the statutory definition of the term "firearm" that federal regulators apply.[1]

## Relevance to the ghost gun rule

In 2022 the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives]] issued a regulation that broadened the bureau's interpretation of the Act's definition of "firearm" in order to reach [[ghost-guns|ghost gun]] parts kits sold online.[1] The scope of that reinterpretation became the subject of litigation, and on August 8, 2023 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] voted 5-4 to leave the regulation in place provisionally while the challenge proceeded.[1] Critics in the Badlands orbit described the reinterpretation as unconstitutional and ambiguous, arguing that an agency had effectively rewritten the statutory definition rather than Congress amending it.[1]
