# Golden Triangle

The Golden Triangle is the opium-producing region that includes parts of Burma, China, Laos and Thailand.[1] Citing the Office of Justice Programs, Badlands Media noted that the region provides ideal conditions for opium cultivation, which began there during the 16th and 17th centuries.[1]

## Heroin economy

Heroin became a major component of the opium trade after the Second World War, and demand from United States troops during the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam War]] helped transform the opium economy of the Golden Triangle into a large and profitable heroin economy; drug trafficking now influences every aspect of politics in the region.[1]

## Role in the Badlands narrative

Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] identified the Golden Triangle as the source of the opium supply used by the Corsican mafia and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] during the [[french-connection|French Connection]] era.[1] When France lost the Indochina war and the French Connection lost control of that supply, the CIA — in this account — needed the U.S. military to step in and protect it, which is presented as the real reason for the Vietnam War and for the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war in Laos]].[1]

## CIA operations in the region

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] writes that by the 1950s the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] was arming Chiang Kai-shek's defeated generals in the Golden Triangle, trading guns for opium intelligence.[2] The arrangement forms one link in a chain he traces from [[operation-underworld|Operation Underworld]] and the [[french-connection|French Connection]] through Laos in the 1960s, where the agency allied with the warlord [[vang-pao|Vang Pao]] and its airline [[air-america|Air America]] allegedly became a conduit for opium and heroin precursors.[2] DeLarme presents the region as an early demonstration of the logic underpinning the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]]: narcotics tolerated, and profited from, in the service of anti-communist objectives.[2]

## Half of the CIA's opium map

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" pairs the Golden Triangle with the [[golden-crescent|Golden Crescent]] as the two areas in which the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] cultivated drug proxies.[3] According to United Nations figures cited in the article, in 2003 the two regions together accounted for 91 percent of the area devoted to illicit opium production and 95 percent of estimated product in metric tons; adding Colombia and Mexico brought the four areas to 96.6 percent of growing area and 97.8 percent of product.[3]

The article traces the escalation to the [[secret-war-in-laos|Secret War in Laos]]: when the CIA airline CAT began covert flights to Burma in the 1950s, the area produced about 80 tons of opium a year; within ten years production had perhaps quadrupled, and at one point during the Vietnam War output from the Golden Triangle reached 1,200 tons annually.[3] By 1971 there were at least seven heroin labs in the region, one of them near the CIA base at Ban Houei Sai in Laos producing an estimated 3.6 tons of heroin a year.[3]
