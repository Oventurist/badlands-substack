# 2025 Global Health Funding Decline

The **2025 global health funding decline** refers to the fall of worldwide global health financing to a 15-year low in 2025, driven by simultaneous cuts from the United States and major European donors.[1]

## Scope of the cuts

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took an axe to global health funding and cooperation after returning to the [[white-house|White House]] in January 2025, but reporting by [[politico|Politico]] emphasised that he was not the only leader chipping away at it.[1] In Europe, nine countries — including the biggest donors — were slashing foreign aid, with no other state stepping up to fill the void.[1] Although the Covid pandemic underscored the world's vulnerability to contagions, it had not yielded the new investments many global health advocates said were needed to do better next time; overall global health funding instead dropped precipitously as nations focused on other priorities.[1] Advocates warned this raised the prospect of an eventual repeat of the disorderly response to the coronavirus.[1]

Thomas Bollyky, director of the global health program at the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], was quoted saying there was "not a lot one can see geopolitically that would give people confidence that this is a great moment for cooperation or significant reinvestment in global health," setting aside the polarization that emerged around public health during the pandemic.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the Council on Foreign Relations was not lamenting the death of health cooperation and investment so much as the death of globalism, upon which its existence depends.[1] She noted that those providing expert analysis in the article were themselves impacted entities, wrote that the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]] is becoming visible in time to watch its demise, and closed with the single word "Accelerate."[1]
