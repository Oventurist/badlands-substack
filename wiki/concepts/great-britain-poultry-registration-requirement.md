# Great Britain Poultry Registration Requirement (2023)

In March 2023, the governments of England, Wales, and Scotland introduced a proposal requiring all poultry keepers in Great Britain to formally register their birds with the government as part of ongoing efforts to combat avian influenza.[1] The proposal was announced on Wednesday, March 8, 2023.[1]

## Terms of the proposal

Under existing law, only keepers of 50 birds or more were required to register.[1] The new rules would apply to all bird keepers regardless of flock size, including people who keep birds as pets or for food production such as eggs, and failure to register would constitute a violation of the law.[1] Registrants would also be required to update their information every year.[1]

Owners would be required to supply their contact information, the location where the birds are kept, and details on the species, quantity, and purpose of the birds.[1] In a joint statement, the chief veterinary officers from England, Scotland, and Wales said the proposals "will enable us to have a full picture of the number and location of birds kept across Great Britain and make it easier to track and manage the spread of avian disease."[1]

In an accompanying take, contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] wrote as a new chicken owner that the appeal of raising chickens is access to unpolluted food — birds free of hormones and steroids, roaming free and laying fresh eggs daily — which he described as freedom from the system, noting a dozen chickens can supply several dozen eggs a week.[1] He argued that government would naturally want to monitor such a detachment from the system, and warned that a registry could enable authorities to compel or harass keepers into administering vaccines to their flocks, raising unanswered questions about effects on egg-laying and meat quality.[1] Getz concluded that mandatory registration amounted to government overreach and was inherently suspicious.[1]
