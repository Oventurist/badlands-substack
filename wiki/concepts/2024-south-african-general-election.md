# 2024 South African General Election

The South African general election of May 29, 2024 produced a historic collapse in support for the ruling [[african-national-congress|African National Congress]], which captured less than 40 percent of the vote with almost all ballots counted.[1]

## Result and surprise

The ANC had governed continuously since Nelson Mandela led it to a sweeping victory in South Africa's first multiracial, multiparty poll in 1994, ending white minority apartheid rule.[1] The 2024 result reverberated internationally and had been predicted by only one pre-election survey; most polls had placed ANC support at about 45 to 48 percent.[1] The country, described as Africa's largest economy and most developed democracy, has a population of 62 million.[1]

## Public reaction

[[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] reported street-level reaction in Melville, Johannesburg, where residents Pieter Fourie and Sally Kruger expressed apprehension: Fourie said South Africa was "a new country today" but that he was afraid because "we don't know what that looks like," while Kruger, gesturing at a pothole filled with muddy water, said that after years of praying to be rid of the ANC she found it hard to celebrate because of what was rising to replace it.[1]

## Aftermath and Badlands interpretation

Former president [[jacob-zuma|Jacob Zuma]], who had evaded prison by claiming terminal illness, launched the [[umkhonto-we-sizwe-party|Umkhonto we Sizwe party]] and was credited with leading a successful insurgency against [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]], the ANC's current leader.[1] The MKP appeared to ally with the [[economic-freedom-fighters|Economic Freedom Fighters]] of [[julius-malema|Julius Malema]].[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the outcome as the "Globalist Empire" retaking control of South Africa through Zuma after earlier setbacks, linking it to a failed coup attempt in the Democratic Republic of Congo and to South Africa's genocide filing against Israel at the International Court of Justice.[1] He argued the Empire would not surrender Africa easily in a digital age where the continent's rare-earth mineral deposits are worth trillions, and that the MKP–EFF platform of nationalizing mines and banks and seizing white-owned land — despite pro-multipolar branding aligned with Beijing and Moscow — subverted the Sovereign Alliance's legal campaign to reclaim mining rights.[1]
