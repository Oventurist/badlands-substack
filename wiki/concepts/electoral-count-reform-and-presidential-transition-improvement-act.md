# Electoral Count Reform and Presidential Transition Improvement Act

The Electoral Count Reform and Presidential Transition Improvement Act is a federal statute passed by Congress in 2022 to overhaul the procedures governing the counting and certification of presidential electoral votes.[1]

## Purpose and 2024 context

The law was enacted expressly to avoid a reprise of January 6, 2021, when a pro-Trump mob marched on the Capitol during the certification of the 2020 election.[1] Ahead of the November 2024 vote, however, lawmakers in both parties braced for a messy aftermath, with polls showing the [[donald-trump]]–[[kamala-harris]] contest close enough in several battleground states that the winner might not be known for days.[1]

Democratic senators voiced fear that Trump and his allies would seize on initial uncertainty to claim fraud if Harris were projected the winner, and some lawmakers were already preparing for another fight on the Senate and House floors over certification despite the 2022 reforms.[1] Senator [[john-hickenlooper]] warned of deliberate efforts to slow the process down and of "chaos."[1] Separately, Democrats filed an ethics complaint in Georgia accusing members of the state election board of holding an illegal meeting and adopting rules exceeding their authority.[1]
