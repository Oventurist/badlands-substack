# Trump Transition Security Clearance Plan

The Trump transition security clearance plan was a November 2024 arrangement under which President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s transition team planned for all cabinet picks to receive sweeping security clearances directly from the president-elect, with [[fbi|FBI]] background checks conducted only after the incoming administration had taken over the bureau and installed its own officials in key positions.[1]

## Mechanics

The plan meant Trump's team would continue to skirt FBI vetting and might not receive classified briefings until Trump was sworn in on 20 January, at which point he would unilaterally grant sweeping clearances across the administration.[1] Under the putative process used in the first [[donald-trump|Trump administration]], the [[white-house|White House]] personnel security office relied on an FBI background check to decide whether to grant a clearance; the check looked for untrustworthiness or red flags exploitable by adversaries, and if initial law-enforcement database checks turned up nothing, applicants received an interim clearance while deeper investigations continued toward a permanent one.[1] The 2024 plan appeared set to bypass that initial stage entirely.[1]

## Motivation

According to people familiar with the matter, Trump's team had regarded the FBI background check process with contempt for months, a product of deep distrust of the bureau dating to officials turning over transition records to the Russia investigation during the first Trump presidency.[1] Delaying FBI vetting could also carry ancillary public-relations benefits for the team if political appointees ran into background-check problems that might upend [[us-senate|Senate]] confirmation, or struggled to obtain clearances once in the White House.[1]
