# January 6 Capitol Security Footage Release

The January 6 Capitol security footage release refers to the arrangement, reported in February 2023, under which House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] granted Fox News host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] access to thousands of hours of Capitol security footage recorded on January 6, 2021.[1] News of the deal circulated publicly on Monday, February 20, 2023 after being broken by [[axios|Axios]], and was covered the following day by [[politico|Politico]].[1]

## Security officials caught unaware

According to Politico's reporting, the apparent deal came as a surprise to at least one official with oversight responsibility over the files: [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] Chief [[thomas-manger|Thomas Manger]].[1] A person familiar with the matter said Manger told associates he did not learn of the arrangement between McCarthy and Carlson until it began publicly circulating on Monday.[1] House Sergeant at Arms [[william-mcfarland|William McFarland]] similarly told associates that he learned of the arrangement at around the same time the Axios story appeared.[1]

Capitol Police had been extremely reluctant to share large portions of their security footage, citing potential risks to lawmakers, aides and the officers tasked with protecting the building.[1]

## Access versus possession

In commentary published in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[just-human|Just Human]] drew a distinction between possession and access, arguing that Carlson did not appear to hold the January 6 footage himself but rather had access to it.[1] That distinction was described as important because airing any of the footage "may require approval from the Capitol Police Board."[1] The commentary further noted that the Capitol Police Chief and the Sergeant at Arms had not been aware of the arrangement until they saw it reported in the news, and suggested that someone should have informed them ahead of Monday's coverage.[1]

## March 2023 Chansley footage broadcast

On the evening of Monday, March 6, 2023, Carlson aired footage from the archive on his Fox News program that appeared to show Capitol Police escorting [[jacob-chansley|Jacob Chansley]], the demonstrator known as the "QAnon Shaman," through the Capitol's corridors and toward the Senate floor rather than attempting to stop him.[2] The two officers alongside Chansley had not subdued him and he did not appear to be handcuffed.[2] Carlson said the tapes showed that the police "never stopped Jacob Chansley. They helped him. They acted as his tour guides."[2] Chansley had received a 41-month sentence for his role in the events of that day.[2]

Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] by [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] argued that the broadcast proved the public had been shown only edited and chopped video for two years in service of a narrative against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his supporters, characterized the official account as "one of the largest psyops ever run against the American people," and predicted the January 6 narrative would keep crumbling as further video was released.[2]

## Reaction to the March 2023 broadcast

Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] reacted to the broadcast by declaring "Free at Last," casting the newly aired material as portraying a different picture from the one painted by former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]].[3] Coverage of the segment noted that the footage ran contrary to narratives advanced by figures such as former Rep. [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] and the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]], which had characterized the participants as having taken part in an "insurrection."[3] The most widely circulated clip, amplified by commentator [[greg-price|Greg Price]], showed Chansley being led through the building by police the entire time he was inside.[3]

Congressional leaders of both parties objected to the release. Senate Minority Leader [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] called it a "mistake" for Carlson and [[fox-news|Fox News]] to air the never-before-seen footage, while Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] said that [[rupert-murdoch|Rupert Murdoch]] "has a special obligation to stop Tucker Carlson from going on tonight," adding that "our democracy depends on it."[3] Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[patel-patriot|Patel Patriot]] argued that both leaders had promoted transparency in government only when it suited their political agendas, and asked what they were afraid of.[3]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that Democrats and RINOs had hidden the footage because it destroyed their January 6 narrative, faulted Carlson for not immediately releasing everything he was given, and called for the immediate release of defendants held in pretrial detention in Washington.[3] In his reading the evidence showed that participants were admitted and escorted by [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]], that they were peaceful, that police escorted Chansley around the Capitol for photo ops, that imposters were present, and that the protesters simply wanted Congress to confront claims of election fraud.[3]

## Access for January 6 defendants

On Tuesday, February 28, 2023, House Republicans agreed to permit January 6 defendants to use Capitol security footage in their legal defense, a decision with the potential to affect ongoing prosecutions arising from the 2021 riot.[4] Rep. [[barry-loudermilk|Barry Loudermilk]] said that Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] had granted the footage to defendants on a "case-by-case basis."[4] Loudermilk told Politico that "Everyone accused of a crime in this country deserves due process, which includes access to evidence which may be used to prove their guilt or innocence," adding that it was the intention of House Republicans to make available any relevant documents or videos, case by case, as requested by attorneys representing defendants.[4]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that after years of being held without charges and denied due process, defendants in what he called the "DC GITMO" were only now being allowed to use footage that could have exonerated them before their lives were destroyed, and contrasted their treatment with that of repeat offenders who firebomb pregnancy centers and burn businesses without consequence.[4] He argued it was not outside the realm of possibility that January 6 was always intended to entice peaceful Trump supporters so they could be accused of insurrection, made an example of, and used to stymie a Trump re-election, and said that if anyone should serve time it would be [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], the [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] who facilitated the breach, and the [[fbi|FBI]].[4] He asked whether a person charged with trespassing had truly trespassed if video showed they were allowed and even encouraged to enter, and called for all of the footage to be released to the public immediately.[4]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] called McCarthy's decision a moment of sanity but predicted it would not free all of the wrongfully incarcerated defendants, in part because in his view January 6 was not a genuine criminal investigation but a political witch hunt through which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] used grievous allegations to hold Americans in the "DC Gulag."[4] He argued that plea deals taken by some defendants to accelerate their release build a case against the remaining defendants by making allegations of sedition and insurrection appear more valid — the logic that if someone admits to insurrection, an insurrection must have occurred — and that the same effect operates on the general population, where one person's capitulation becomes a fact-in-law used to prove the event itself.[4] He described the practice as classic gulag-style political intimidation used by tyrannical governments, and suggested history would mark the episode either as proof of the fall of America or as the moment the people rose to take back their freedoms.[4]

## Political reading

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] framed the release as evidence that McCarthy was "beginning to silence the doubters," calling the turnover of January 6 footage — particularly to Carlson, described as "as big as it gets in mainstream media" — a significant development capable of rapidly disrupting the mainstream media narrative surrounding January 6.[1] The same commentary linked the footage decision to McCarthy's parallel maneuvering over [[ukraine-aid-funding-controversy|Ukraine aid]] in must-pass spending bills.[1]
