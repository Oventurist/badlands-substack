# Discharge Petition

A **discharge petition** is a rare procedural move in the U.S. House of Representatives that allows members to force a floor vote on legislation over the objection of the Speaker, requiring at least 218 signatures to succeed.[1]

## Use in the February 2024 foreign aid fight

After the Senate passed a $95 billion foreign aid package on Tuesday, February 13, 2024, Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-LA) refused to hold a vote on it before Congress secured the southern U.S. border first, saying: "We really do owe it to the American people to get control of that border."[1]

In response, House Democrats began laying the groundwork to force a vote anyway through a discharge petition, which would require the support of some Republicans to reach the 218-signature threshold.[1] The package allocated $60 billion to Ukraine support, $14 billion in military assistance to Israel, $9 billion in humanitarian aid to Gaza and elsewhere, and around $5 billion to defend Taiwan.[1]

In a letter to colleagues, House Democratic Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] (D-NY) said the caucus would use "every available legislative tool" to advance the bill, and called on "traditional Republicans" to support it.[1]

## Epstein files petition (November 2025)

On Wednesday, November 12, 2025, a discharge petition for a bill to release the [[epstein-files|Epstein files]] gathered enough signatures to force action, teeing up a long-sought floor vote on the matter.[2] Rep. [[adelita-grijalva|Adelita Grijalva]] of Arizona became the 218th and final signature shortly after she was sworn in, joining all other Democrats and four Republicans — [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]], [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]], [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]], and [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]].[2] She signed on the House floor as Democrats in the chamber cheered and two Epstein survivors looked on from the gallery.[2]
