# Durham House Judiciary Testimony (2023)

The Durham House Judiciary testimony was the public appearance of Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on Wednesday, June 21, 2023, at which he presented and defended the findings of the [[durham-report|Durham Report]] into the origins of the FBI's investigation of the 2016 [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign.[1]

## Durham's findings as presented

Durham told the committee that the [[cia|CIA]] knew that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] had approved a plan to smear then-candidate Donald Trump with Russia allegations in order to distract from her classified email scandal.[1] He testified that the [[fbi|FBI]] did not "sufficiently scrutinize information it received," nor "apply the same standards to allegations it received about the Clinton and Trump campaigns."[1]

Durham stated that "The FBI was too willing to accept and use politically funded and uncorroborated opposition research, such as the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]]," adding that "The FBI relied on the dossier and FISA applications, knowing there was likely material originating from a political campaign or political opponent."[1] He said it was "going to take time to rebuild the public's confidence in the institution," referring to the FBI's mishandling of the Russia collusion probe.[1]

## The withheld Brennan referral memo

Under questioning from Committee Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], Durham said former FBI Director [[james-comey|Jim Comey]] had withheld a referral memorandum from the agents working the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] case.[1] The memo contained intelligence from a high-level briefing with CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] related to Hillary Clinton's attempts to tie Trump to Russia.[1] Jordan responded: "They kept key intelligence from the investigators. This is how bad this investigation was but here's the scary part: I don't think anything has changed."[1]

## Partisan dynamics of the hearing

Commentary from [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] characterized the hearing as simultaneously encouraging and disappointing.[1] Ranking member [[jerrold-nadler|Jerrold Nadler]]'s opening remarks were described as delusional, and [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] was said to have continued pointing to the alleged legitimacy of the [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] report and the [[russiagate-hoax|Russia collusion]] narrative despite the Durham Report's destruction of the Steele dossier's validity.[1] Democratic members repeatedly asked why Durham's investigation had cost more than $6 million and produced fewer indictments than the Mueller investigation, and pressed him on matters he said he was not tasked to investigate.[1] Durham himself drew applause and laughter at points during the proceedings.[1]

A notable and, to Badlands commentators, uncharacteristic moment came when Republican Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] sharply attacked Durham during his questioning time.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that Durham "was fighting an uphill battle from the get go," noting the limited results of the Sussmann and Danchenko prosecutions, and laid out three competing interpretations: that Durham was "more rotten, spineless fruit from the [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]] tree" (a view attributed to Gaetz, [[peter-navarro|Steve Navarro]] and [[steve-bannon|Steven Bannon]]); that Durham's team tried in good faith but found the system too corrupt; or that the entire sequence is kayfabe designed to push the public toward welcoming military intervention.[1] DeLarme summarized the recurring formula as: Democrats fight dirty, there are no consequences, Republicans have no spine, and [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] sits down to pee — while still holding out that the report might prove instrumental later and noting "Durham isn't the only game in town."[1]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued the Durham investigation likely uncovered damning information about the [[deep-state|Deep State]] that was deliberately withheld, letting the hearing serve as a punching bag on which the left expended its ammunition, and that Gaetz's attack functioned within the narrative war by keeping alive the idea that the real crimes remain unexposed.[1]
