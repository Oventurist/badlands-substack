# WHO Pandemic Accord

The WHO Pandemic Accord is a proposed international instrument negotiated under the auspices of the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] to coordinate the prevention of, preparation for, and response to future pandemic emergencies.[1] In the United States it became a focus of sovereignty-based opposition after the administration of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] endorsed it.[1]

## United States commitment

In late February 2023 the [[state-department|Department of State]] announced that the United States was "committed to a #PandemicAccord," which it said would "vastly improve the global health architecture for preventing, preparing for, and responding to future pandemic emergencies."[1]

## Opposition

Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] rejected what he characterized as a globalist effort to rope the United States into the accord, asserting that American citizens would live under laws made according to the Constitution and by the representatives they elect rather than by globalists.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] endorsed Massie's position, writing that the WHO had "bamboozled us during COVID," covered up the virus's origins, and was beholden to Davos globalists and the Chinese Communist Party, leaving "no angle where this could possibly be seen as a good idea."[1] He argued the accord amounted to constructing an entity that supersedes the authority of nations — in effect an unspoken one-world government — able to violate rights in the name of public health, whether by treating speech or firearms as public-health dangers, and leaving parents unable to shield their children from the forces directing the organization.[1] DeLarme described Massie as "the kind of leader we need — one who will, at bare minimum not sell the entire country out to some control-hungry globalist apparatus."[1]

## Collapse of negotiations (May 2024)

After more than two years of negotiations, rich and poor countries failed — at least for the time being — to produce a plan for how the world might respond to the next pandemic, leaving the proposed treaty in limbo.[2] Member countries had asked the U.N. health agency in 2021 to oversee negotiations on how to better share scarce resources and stop future viruses from spreading globally, following COVID-19's once-unthinkable lockdowns, upended economies and millions of deaths.[2] On Friday, May 24, 2024, Roland Driece, co-chair of the WHO's negotiating board for the agreement, acknowledged that countries had been unable to come up with a draft, despite the organization's hope that a final text could be agreed at its yearly meeting of health ministers opening the following Monday in Geneva.[2] "We are not where we hoped we would be when we started this process," Driece said, adding that finalizing an international agreement on pandemic response was critical "for the sake of humanity."[2]
