# Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict

The Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict is the framework, endorsed by [[un-security-council-resolution-2803|United Nations Security Council Resolution 2803]], under which the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] serves as a transitional administration for [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## Objectives

The plan seeks to secure a permanent ceasefire, aid Gaza's reconstruction, and promote a lasting peace grounded in Palestinian self-determination and statehood, while advancing regional security and stability.[1]

## Accession of additional states

In January 2026 the foreign ministers of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], [[turkey|Turkey]], [[egypt|Egypt]], Jordan, Indonesia, Pakistan, [[qatar|Qatar]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] announced their countries' decision to join the Board of Peace following an invitation from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], pledging in a statement released through the Saudi Foreign Ministry's X account to help implement the Board's mission as a transitional administration under the plan.[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the pattern of acceptances and refusals — Arab states and Hungary joining while France, Norway, Sweden, [[denmark|Denmark]] and Slovenia refused — as a practical test of membership in what he calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[1]

## Phase Two (January 2026)

Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] announced on January 15, 2026 the launch of Phase Two of the 20-Point Plan, describing the transition as moving "from ceasefire to demilitarization, technocratic governance, and reconstruction."[2] Witkoff credited [[egypt|Egypt]], [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] with finalizing the necessary particulars.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized Phase Two as the pivotal stage, since it is the point at which Arab states assume control of Gaza from the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], and predicted Israeli resistance: most of the Israeli political establishment opposes any leadership role for Turkey or Qatar in the International Stabilization Force, while President [[donald-trump|Trump]] has insisted on including both.[2] He attributed Israeli hostility to Qatar to the internal Israeli scandal over funding of [[hamas|Hamas]] before October 7 and the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government's alleged involvement, and hostility to Turkey to two and a half years of mutual threats — noting that Turkey's [[nato|NATO]] membership means any Israeli action against it could trigger [[article-5|Article 5]].[2]

## First phase agreement, October 2025

On Wednesday, October 8, 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] announced that [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] had reached a breakthrough in their negotiations on the first phase of the Gaza peace plan.[3] The [[white-house|White House]] had released the 20-point plan the previous month, calling for an immediate ceasefire, a hostage-for-prisoner release, Hamas disarmament, and a withdrawal of Israeli forces from the Palestinian enclave.[3]

In a post on Truth Social, Trump said Hamas had consented to release all Israeli hostages while Israel had agreed to withdraw troops from Gaza "to an agreed upon line."[3] "I am very proud to announce that Israel and Hamas have both signed off on the first Phase of our Peace Plan," he wrote, calling the decisions the "first steps towards a strong, durable and everlasting peace."[3] Trump thanked mediators from [[qatar|Qatar]], [[egypt|Egypt]] and Türkiye for their role in the talks; the indirect negotiations between Israel and Hamas had been ongoing in Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt, since Monday, October 6.[3]

Speaking to Axios shortly after the announcement, Trump said, "It's a great day for Israel and for the world," and indicated he was "likely going to Israel in the coming days" to address the [[knesset|Knesset]].[3] He described a "great" call with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], who had invited him to speak before the Israeli parliament.[3] Axios characterized the agreement as the biggest diplomatic achievement of Trump's second term, given that ending the war in Gaza had been one of his key campaign promises.[3]

[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the deal as leverage, arguing that Trump was using the momentum of the peace plan to exert himself onto the Israeli political landscape and that no Israeli figure could credibly oppose peace in that moment.[3] The commentary predicted that Israeli religious-nationalist factions, animated by a "crusader complex" and the goal of establishing [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]], would not honor any pledge and would continue seeking war with Iran.[3]

## Trump 20-point Gaza plan, September 2025

Trump unveiled his Gaza peace plan at a White House press conference with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] on 29 September 2025, saying Washington was "beyond very close" to a deal after Israel accepted the proposal and Arab and Muslim states committed to disarming [[hamas|Hamas]].[4] The White House published the 20-point plan in full minutes before the press conference, trimming one point from an earlier version reported the previous week.[4] Egyptian and Qatari mediators presented the proposal to Hamas negotiators late that night, with an Arab diplomat estimating a response would take "several days, at least."[4]

Key provisions reported ahead of publication included safe passage out of [[gaza|Gaza]] for Hamas leaders if the 48 remaining hostages were freed within 48 hours of Israel's public acceptance, the release by Israel of hundreds of Palestinian prisoners serving life terms and over 1,000 Gazans detained since the war began, the return of the remains of several hundred Palestinians, amnesty for Hamas members committing to "peaceful coexistence," immediate humanitarian aid delivery, and an Israeli pledge not to launch further strikes on [[qatar|Qatar]].[4] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified the [[new-york-declaration|New York Declaration]] — the Saudi-French two-state proposal — as the explicit basis of the plan, while [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the 20-point framework looked "an AWFUL lot like a two-state solution" and therefore represented submission by the side least expected to submit.[4]
