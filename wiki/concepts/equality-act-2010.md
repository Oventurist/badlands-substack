# Equality Act 2010

The Equality Act 2010 is the United Kingdom's principal anti-discrimination statute, intended to prevent discrimination across protected characteristics including sex.[1] Its definition of "woman" became the central question in a landmark 2025 ruling by the UK Supreme Court.[1]

## 2025 Supreme Court ruling

On Wednesday, 16 April 2025, the Supreme Court in Britain ruled that trans women do not fall within the legal definition of women under the country's equality legislation, holding that the legal definition of a woman is based on biological sex.[1] The judgment was described as a landmark and a blow to campaigners for transgender rights.[1]

Reporting noted the decision could have far-reaching consequences for the operation of single-sex services such as domestic violence shelters, and for equal pay claims.[1] The five judges involved emphasised that they were not commenting more broadly on whether trans women are women, saying it was not the role of the court to adjudicate the meaning of gender or sex; the judgment was confined to the precise interpretation of language in the 2010 Act.[1]
