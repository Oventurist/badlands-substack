# Trump Second Term First 100 Days

The first 100 days of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second presidential term, running from January 20 to late April 2025, became the organizing frame for a round of competing assessments at the end of April 2025 — celebratory from the administration and its allies, and hostile from most of the mainstream press.[1]

## Competing assessments

A USA Today opinion column argued that Trump had done more good for the United States in his first 100 days than [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] did in four years, while complaining that much of the mainstream news media portrayed the presidency as a chaotic disaster and labeled Trump an authoritarian.[1] The columnist framed the period as the rapid implementation of policies that 77.3 million Americans voted for in November, and characterized Democrats as the party of the status quo — a status quo defended by "respected financial experts" forecasting economic disaster unless the deficit is cut and growth of the national debt slowed.[1]

Against that, top Democratic funding negotiators in the House and Senate published a tracker on April 29, 2025 accusing the administration of targeting at least $430 billion in federal funds across 114 programs frozen, cancelled or terminated during the first 100 days.[1] Newsweek, covering Trump's April 29 rally at Macomb Community College in Warren, Michigan — which he called "the most successful 100 days in the history of our nation" — tried to test whether MAGA morale was slipping amid a decline in his polling.[1] Before the speech he had arrived at Selfridge Air National Guard Base to tout the acquisition of 21 F-15EX Eagle II fighter jets, joined by Michigan Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] and Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]].[1]

## The "trap" thesis

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the first 100 days were "a multi-layered, perhaps even multi-dimensional TRAP for the Deep State regime, and they've fallen right into it."[1] He treated the 100-day mark as arbitrary but meaningful, setting the tone both for what the American people can expect from a president and what a president can expect from the people.[1]

Among the achievements he catalogued were an immediate crackdown on border security, the beginnings of an "Eisenhower model" purge of illegal criminal aliens, the outputs and narratives generated by the [[department-of-government-efficiency|Department of Government Efficiency]] — whose "many-colored genies can never be put back in their respective bottles" — and the opening of a "Sovereign Trade War" that he argued is merely a narrative veneer over a long-planned reset of the American and global financial system.[1]

The trade-war point, he acknowledged, was the most contentious, drawing opposition from establishment commentators, heads of state, "the entire western hegemon," and even some members of what he calls the Sovereign Alliance.[1] His argument was that Trump and his team know the reset is unfinished, know the decoupling period will be rocky, and know the end state is the "Golden Age" promised since the golden escalator announcement.[1] On this reading Trump's victory laps were premature only in appearance: he is "drawing a line of demarcation between the past and the future," and the moment calls on observers to pick a side.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] made a narrower point about the Newsweek rally coverage: the headline promised "what rallygoers say," and the rallygoers collectively said "THIS IS WHAT I VOTED FOR," with the only critical voice belonging to a protester escorted out by security — after which the outlet fell back on polling data and punditry to sustain the slipping-support thesis.[1]
