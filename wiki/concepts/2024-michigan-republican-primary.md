# 2024 Michigan Republican Primary

The 2024 Michigan Republican presidential primary, held on Tuesday, February 27, 2024, was won decisively by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who continued what reporting described as his march toward the Republican Party's presidential nomination.[1] Trump appeared on a path to take roughly 70 percent of the vote in the state's GOP contest.[1]

## Result and the Haley question

Despite the scale of Trump's victory, his sole remaining major rival, former U.N. ambassador [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]], continued to score a percentage of the vote significant enough to raise questions about Trump's standing in a general election.[1] Haley drew more than a quarter of the Michigan vote, but that share did not match her performance in the two preceding head-to-head contests with Trump — 40 percent in South Carolina and 43 percent in New Hampshire.[1] Analysts read the decline as a sign that, as the campaign nationalized ahead of the March 5 "Super Tuesday" round in which 15 states held nominating contests, Haley might struggle to replicate her stronger early-state showings.[1]

The result increased pressure on Haley to exit the race, though she had said she planned to remain in it at least through Super Tuesday.[1] By the time of the Michigan vote Trump had swept the primary calendar, notching clear wins in Iowa, New Hampshire, Nevada and South Carolina.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that legacy media coverage of the primary demonstrated a lack of coordination rather than the lock-step messaging typically observed from establishment outlets, contending that a "Deep State archipelago" had developed after Trump's departure from office along the lines of [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s [[devolution|Devolution]] research.[1] He characterized the Haley campaign as one of the growing divergences in that messaging: half of the corporate press lambasted her entire run while the other half sounded what he called "faux alarms" toward Trump on her behalf, which he interpreted as outlets hedging their narratives because they did not know where the race was heading.[1]
