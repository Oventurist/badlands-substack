# Israeli Buffer Zone in Syria

Following the fall of Syrian President Bashar al-Assad in December 2024, [[israel|Israel]] established a so-called "buffer zone" inside internationally recognised Syrian territory, a move criticised by Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]].[1]

## Lavrov's warning

At an online press conference in late December 2024, Lavrov cautioned that Israel should refrain from solving its geopolitical problems at the expense of war-torn [[syria|Syria]], warning that reckless actions by the Jewish state could erode the security framework of the Middle East.[1] He stressed that [[russia|Russia]] insists on Syria remaining an independent country after Assad's demise, reiterating that Moscow maintains contact with Damascus and other regional partners, and declaring that "the disintegration of Syria must not be allowed."[1]

Lavrov urged Israel "to understand its responsibility in these collective [stabilization] efforts and refrain from ensuring its security at the expense of others," adding: "One cannot expect to destroy all military facilities in a neighboring country and then live in peace and harmony forever. This is like sowing a storm that will inevitably come back to haunt those who engage in such actions."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read Lavrov's "sowing a storm" language as a boomerang metaphor and paired it with [[pepe-escobar|Pepe Escobar]]'s remarks on [[andrew-napolitano|Judge Andrew Napolitano]]'s programme about reports that Russia might abandon its two Cold War–era coastal bases in Syria and relocate operations to Libya.[1] He highlighted Escobar's comment about a negotiation between Vladimir Putin and Recep Tayyip Erdogan, predicting [[nato|NATO]] would work to subvert that conversation, and said the press conference moved the Overton window closer to his standing speculation that Russia would conduct a "Special Military Operation" against Benjamin Netanyahu.[1]
