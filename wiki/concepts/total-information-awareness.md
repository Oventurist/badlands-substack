# Total Information Awareness

Total Information Awareness was a Defense Department program directed by [[john-poindexter|John Poindexter]] in the years after the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]], aimed at large-scale collection and analysis of information about individuals.[1]

## Origins in the 1983 reorganization

The program's lineage is traced back to Poindexter's work in the Reagan White House. After the [[1983-beirut-barracks-bombings|1983 Beirut barracks bombing]] and the subsequent embassy bombing, Poindexter led a reorganization of the government's crisis management teams and took over an existing [[national-security-council|NSC]] outfit called the Crisis-Pre Planning Group, turning it into the engine of the government's antiterrorism campaign — powered for the first time by modern information technologies designed to capture information and get it to the people who needed it.[1]

Looking back, it is clear that Poindexter was planting the seeds of a much broader, pervasive and ultimately global system of information gathering. The government was first getting a handle on what was in its own databases; it would be another two decades, after 9/11, before the intelligence agencies turned their gaze toward the worlds of data held in private stores, on private individuals. Then, too, Poindexter would help lead those efforts, as director of Total Information Awareness.[1] The program bore a striking resemblance to the global surveillance conducted by the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], and that was no accident.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents Total Information Awareness as the mature form of a [[shadow-government|shadow government]] surveillance state entrenched by a suicide bombing that sacrificed 241 unarmed Marines.[1]
