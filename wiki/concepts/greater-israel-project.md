# Greater Israel Project

The "Greater Israel Project" is the term Badlands Media uses for a posited long-range Israeli territorial and strategic expansion agenda in the Middle East.[1]

The article raises it as one of the questions Western corporate media will not ask about the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]: whether it is "only a coincidence that this entire spectacle plays into the Greater Israel Project so nicely."[1] The framing sits alongside the article's other unasked questions — why the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and the [[cia|CIA]] fund militant proxy groups in [[syria|Syria]], whether all Syrians are rejoicing, and when al-Qaeda became "hip and chic."[1]

The article connects the concept to concrete regional stakes, noting that the sudden change in power dynamics carried implications for [[iran|Iran]]'s influence through Hezbollah and Syrian territory, and for [[israel|Israel]], which had been targeting Iranian assets in Syria.[1]

## Trump's red line over Lebanon and Syria (July 2026)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued in July 2026 that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s instruction to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to withdraw the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] from southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and southern [[syria|Syria]] — "They don't want you there. You should redeploy." — marked the beginning of a sequence in which Trump would be forced to choose a side, and would not choose Netanyahu.[2]

He predicted the red line would expose the project's objectives: whereas Zionist spokesmen had denied that any occupation of foreign nations was occurring and dismissed the allegations as antisemitism, Trump's public call to stand down would force them to defend the occupations as necessary security zones, and eventually to assert outright that the lands belong to Israel by divine grant and that the inhabitants must leave and surrender their property.[2] He characterized that endpoint as "the language of bandits," the logic of "might makes right."[2]

The commentary held that the project faced a hard material limit in the IDF's manpower crisis, deepened by the [[haredi-conscription-exemption-law|Haredi conscription exemption law]], and that Trump's strategy of letting the fire burn itself out — rather than merely halting it — was the only way to end the Greater Israel Project permanently.[2]

## Abraham Accords and the 2025 ceasefire framing

Badlands commentary in January 2025 argued that a genuine Israeli–Saudi normalization deal, folding Saudi Arabia into the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], would stabilize the region enough to make future plans regarding the Greater Israel Project "all but impossible" — and identified that prospect as the motive behind the [[october-7-attacks|October 7th attacks]].[3] Separately, contributor Burning Bright wrote that by advancing a Gaza peace deal Donald Trump had placed the [[israel-lobby|Israel lobby]] in a position where any overt move to restart kinetic action would be seen for what it was: an attempt to escalate and progress toward the Greater Israel Project.[3] If Israel declined the chance at peace, the argument ran, Trump would hold a bipartisan public mandate to break with it on a level never seen before.[3]
