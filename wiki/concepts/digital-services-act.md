# Digital Services Act

The Digital Services Act (DSA) is the European Union's online content regulation regime, characterized in Badlands Media coverage as "highly repressive."[1] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cites it as the clearest international example of a censorship framework justified by the pretext of protecting people from [[hate-speech|hate speech]].[1]

## Role in the censorship argument

DeLarme argues that the concept of protecting people from hate speech is the foundation of every censorship framework advanced by the interagency, international blob over the past decade, and that the DSA rests on the exact same reasoning later deployed domestically in the United States through the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]].[1] The identical logic across jurisdictions is presented as evidence that the campaign for [[government-sponsored-censorship|government-sponsored censorship]] is coordinated rather than organic.[1]
