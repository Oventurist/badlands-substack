# Digital Services Act

The Digital Services Act (DSA) is a European Union regulatory framework governing online content. Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterizes it as "highly repressive" and cites it as the paradigmatic example of a censorship regime justified by the pretext of protecting people from [[hate-speech|hate speech]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Significance in the essay

In "A Glaring Double Standard," the DSA functions as evidence that the same rationale advanced by American campus activists and, later, by post–October 7 conservatives has already been enacted into law abroad by what DeLarme calls the interagency, international blob.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He argues the reasoning is identical across jurisdictions and causes, differing only in which group is designated as requiring protection — "some snowflakes are more precious than others."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) The DSA thus stands as the international counterpart to the domestic [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]] in the broader project of [[government-sponsored-censorship|government-sponsored censorship]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Enforcement against X (October 2023)

Within days of the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]], the [[european-commission|European Commission]] used the DSA to give [[x-corp|X]] 24 hours to address illegal content and disinformation regarding the conflict or face penalties, then formally opened an investigation into the platform.[2] CEO [[linda-yaccarino|Linda Yaccarino]] replied to EU official [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] in a letter posted to X, saying the company had "redistributed resources and refocused internal teams who are working around the clock," had removed hundreds of Hamas-affiliated accounts and thousands of posts, and had assembled a leadership group to assess the situation.[2] EU officials asked the company to begin responding to investigators as early as October 18.[2]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the [[european-union|EU]] was already using the Israel–Palestine conflict as a pretense for ramping up censorship, describing the DSA as the bloc's escalation on the United Kingdom's [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]] and its unspoken doctrine as one in which "the only free speech you should need is the freedom to agree with the government and the corporatocracy."[2] In the context of [[fifth-generation-warfare|Fifth-Generational Warfare]], he wrote, the tight control of information flow is essential to Western centers of power, and the campaign amounts to an unabashedly totalitarian effort to root out dissent — resting on the assumption that the general population is stupid and that the undiscerning mass will police the fringe before dissenting information can spread.[2]

## Transition from voluntary commitments

When [[twitter|Twitter]] withdrew from the EU's voluntary [[eu-code-of-practice-on-disinformation|Code of Practice on Disinformation]] in late May 2023, Commissioner [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] warned that "beyond voluntary commitments, fighting disinformation will be legal obligation under #DSA as of August 25," and that enforcement teams would be ready.[3] The exchange illustrated the Act's function as the binding successor to the bloc's earlier voluntary content-moderation arrangements.[3]

## First penalties against X (2025)

By April 2025, [[european-union|European Union]] regulators were preparing major penalties against [[x-corp|X]] for breaking the landmark law, in what four people with knowledge of the plans described as a move likely to ratchet up tensions with the United States by targeting one of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s closest advisers, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[4] The penalties were set to include a fine and demands for product changes, were expected to be announced that summer, and would be the first issued under the Act.[4]

European authorities weighed how large a fine to issue as they considered the risks of further antagonizing Trump amid wider trans-Atlantic disputes over trade, tariffs, and the war in Ukraine; one person said the fine could surpass $1 billion as regulators sought to make an example of X and deter other companies from violating the law.[4] EU officials said the investigation into X was progressing independently from tariff negotiations after Trump announced major new levies that week.[4]

## First formal infringement proceedings (December 2023)

On December 18, 2023, the [[european-union|European Union]] opened infringement proceedings against [[x-corp|X]] over suspected failure to combat content disinformation and manipulation — the first such probe brought under the Digital Services Act.[5] Commissioner [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] said the action responded to suspected breaches of X's transparency obligations, its duties to counter illegal content and disinformation, and what the EU described as a "deceptive" user-interface design relating to the platform's blue check marks.[5]

The [[european-commission|European Commission]] said it acted on the basis of its preliminary investigation, including analysis of the risk assessment report X submitted in September, the platform's transparency report published on November 3, and X's replies to a formal request for information that concerned, among other matters, the dissemination of illegal content in the context of [[hamas|Hamas]]' attacks against Israel.[5] X responded that it "remains committed to complying with the Digital Services Act and is cooperating with the regulatory process," while urging that the process remain "free of political influence."[5] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the proceedings as an attempt by Brussels to suppress the most powerful information-dissemination platform in the world after establishment institutions lost control of it.[5]

## €120 million fine against X (December 2025)

On Friday, December 5, 2025, the [[european-commission|European Commission]] fined [[x-corp|X]] 120 million euros (about $140 million) following a two-year investigation under the Digital Services Act, citing the platform's "deceptive" blue checkmark and a lack of transparency in its advertising repository.[6] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] responded dismissively on X and the following day escalated, calling for the [[european-union|European Union]] to be abolished "and sovereignty returned to individual countries, so that governments can better represent their people."[6]

Senior U.S. officials joined the criticism: Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] called the fine "an attack on all American tech platforms and the American people by foreign governments."[6] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as part of a broader Trump–Musk effort to identify the EU explicitly as the adversary and to challenge its model of narrative control, censorship, and economic interdependence.[6]
