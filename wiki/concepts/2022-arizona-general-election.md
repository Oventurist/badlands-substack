# 2022 Arizona General Election

The 2022 Arizona general election, held on November 8, 2022, became the focus of sustained dispute over the administration of voting in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], the state's largest jurisdiction. Writing two weeks after election day, Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] described the state's election system as "appallingly broken" and framed her account as an SOS — "Save Our State" — on behalf of Arizona voters.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Election day failures

According to reporting summarized in the article, problems appeared within thirty minutes of polls opening on November 8, despite equipment having been tested the day before.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Maricopa County acknowledged that at least sixty voting locations experienced issues traced to [[ballot-on-demand-printers|ballot-on-demand printers]] whose configuration settings were non-uniform, producing ballots that on-site tabulators could not read.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Voters turned away from one location and directed to another frequently found that the check-in system still listed them as checked in at the first site, leaving them without a remedy; poll workers appeared inadequately trained in the provisional ballot process.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Ballots that could not be tabulated on site were deposited into the containers that became known as [[door-3-ballots|"Door 3"]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Statewide vote totals and the undervote anomaly

Gillespie drew attention to statewide totals published on the Arizona Secretary of State's "2022 Unofficial General Election Results" page as of November 21, 2022: 1,321,435 votes cast for U.S. Senate, 1,287,322 for Governor, 1,320,059 for Secretary of State, 1,254,096 for Attorney General, and 1,389,215 for State Treasurer.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) She argued it was implausible that the low-profile treasurer's race — for which she reported never having seen an advertisement or road sign — outdrew all four top-of-ticket contests, by margins of 67,780 votes over Senate, 101,893 over Governor, 69,156 over Secretary of State, and 135,119 over Attorney General.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) In her reading, the treasurer's race, won by Republican [[kimberly-yee|Kimberly Yee]] with 55.7 percent, represented an untouched baseline against which manipulation further up the ballot could be measured.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

The article recalled a warning Gillespie had published on November 5 in "Why Kari Lake Will Lose in Arizona": that flipping the governor's race alone would not be credible, and that "the left will need to flip the entire Republican down-ballot ticket in order for the Governor's race results to be believable."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Aftermath

Litigation and records requests proliferated in the weeks after the vote, with attorneys including [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] working on election-integrity claims and volunteers for [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] pressing the county for answers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) On November 19, [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] of the [[arizona-attorney-generals-office|Arizona Attorney General's Office]] Election Integrity Unit sent a formal demand letter to [[tom-liddy|Tom Liddy]] at the [[maricopa-county-attorneys-office|Maricopa County Attorney's Office]], seeking a full accounting before the county's statutory [[canvassing|canvass]] deadline of November 28, 2022.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Certification and contests

The general election was officially certified by Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] on Monday, December 5, 2022 — Hobbs, as the Democratic candidate for governor, thereby certifying her own election.[2] Arizona law gave candidates five days after certification to contest the results, and Friday, December 9 saw three separate Republican filings.[2] [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] filed a 70-page [[kari-lake-election-contest-2022|election contest]] naming Hobbs, [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]], [[scott-jarrett|Scott Jarrett]] and the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Board of Supervisors]] and alleging that illegal votes and machine failures far exceeded the 17,117-vote margin in the governor's race.[2] [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] and [[jeff-zink|Jeff Zink]] filed jointly against [[adrian-fontes|Adrian Fontes]], [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]] and Hobbs.[2] [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]], whose earlier suit with the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] had been dismissed as premature, refiled within the window.[2] [[blake-masters|Blake Masters]], the Trump-endorsed Senate candidate, filed nothing — a silence [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] found conspicuous.[2]

## Recounts

The attorney general contest was subject to an automatic recount: Democrat [[kris-mayes|Kris Mayes]] led Hamadeh by 511 votes out of some 2.5 million as of certification, under the 0.5-point threshold that triggers an automatic recount under Arizona law.[2] Recounts in the attorney general race, the state superintendent race and a state House race began on the Wednesday following certification.[2]

## Gubernatorial contest and its aftermath

The November 8, 2022 gubernatorial contest between Arizona Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] and journalist [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] was decided by 17,117 votes out of 2.59 million cast, a margin of 0.67 percent.[3] On election day, [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] voters waited hours in line only to be unable to cast ballots because of [[ballot-on-demand-printers|Ballot on Demand printer]] and tabulator failures, and were told that ballots placed in "Drawer Three" would be counted.[3]

The county's [[maricopa-county-2022-ballot-on-demand-printer-investigation|subsequent investigation]] confirmed that the ballot image had been lengthened from 19 to 20 inches before the election.[3] Litigation over the result continued into 2023, culminating in the remanded Count III trial in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]] on whether the county performed [[signature-verification|signature verification]] as required by [[arizona-revised-statutes-16-550|A.R.S. § 16-550]]; Judge [[peter-thompson|Peter Thompson]] dismissed the claim on May 22, 2023.[3]

## Certification delays and the "timing" argument

Writing on November 25, 2022, Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] reported that a second Republican-controlled Arizona county had delayed certifying the results of the election as a protest against voting problems in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] that some GOP officials blamed for their losses in top races, including the contest for governor.[4] The delay came as Maricopa, the state's most populous county, finished counting its last remaining ballots and the state attorney general demanded that officials explain the Election Day problems some voters had experienced.[4] The [[arizona-state-senate|state legislature]] separately demanded evidence from the county, requiring a response by the Friday before the certification date, and [[kelli-ward|Kelli Ward]] joined the effort.[4]

Lange argued that the fraud in this cycle had to be more blatant than in 2020 because [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] had promised, if elected governor, to expose election fraud in Arizona and would have gained prosecutorial power to do so — obliging her opponents to "go all in on the steal."[4] He predicted that the evidence would reach a courtroom through criminal indictment or lawsuit and enter the public record permanently, and that the [[arizona-attorney-generals-office|Arizona Attorney General's office]] Election Integrity Unit under [[mark-brnovich|Mark Brnovich]], acting through Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]], had been preparing for exactly this eventuality.[4]

## Contemporary account of the count

Two days after polls closed on November 8, 2022, only about 62,000 ballots had been released in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], a jurisdiction of roughly 4.5 million residents, drawing national and international media to Arizona.[5] [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] said on the morning of November 10 that the envelopes of the most committed Republican voters in the state had not yet been opened.[5]

[[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] attributed the pattern to Arizona's longstanding reliance on [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]], which she said most Arizonans had embraced for its convenience until 2020 convinced conservatives it was "fraught with corruption."[5] She credited the [[arizona-republican-party|AZ GOP]] and Republican candidates with persuading conservative voters not to mail their ballots, a shift she said produced a flood of Election Day voting in the August 2 primary and again in November, when most conservatives either dropped mailed ballots off at the polls or voted in person.[5] Her own polling place had no working tabulator, forcing her to use the "Box 3" receptacle.[5]
