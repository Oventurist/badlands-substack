# The Resistance

"The Resistance" is the label that entered corporate media vocabulary in 2017, following the election and inauguration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], to describe the loose coalition of celebrities, activists and non-governmental organization staff mobilized against his administration.[1]

## Character and origins

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that although the Resistance was publicly billed as a spontaneous, natural reaction to Trump's election, it was in fact organized and birthed from the NGO world.[1] He wrote that many well-meaning liberals were radicalized by a continuous barrage of anti-Trump messaging and drew into the movement believing they were fighting autocracy, while the underlying network consisted of tax-exempt entities and activist groups.[1] DeLarme stated that he could not definitively prove intelligence-agency coordination through cutouts but was confident it had occurred.[1]

## The Comedy Resistance

One affiliated project was [[the-comedy-resistance|The Comedy Resistance]] (TCR), co-founded by filmmaker Stephen Kessler and lobbyist [[heather-podesta|Heather Podesta]].[1] DeLarme reported personal proximity to the group, having lived in Hollywood with a roommate who worked as a video editor for it.[1] TCR recruited comedians including Bob Odenkirk, David Cross, Sarah Silverman, Mike Birbiglia, Patton Oswalt and Reggie Watts for tasks and photo opportunities such as registering voters at comedy shows and distributing masks during COVID.[1]

## Organizational infrastructure

DeLarme described TCR as an extension of the broader Resistance and a project of the left-of-center [[action-network|Action Network]], a digital organizing platform created in 2012 by [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] and Brian Young.[1] The Action Network, he wrote, was behind many of the protests against Trump's first-term policies — from the Dakota Access Pipeline demonstrations to the Women's March — using an infrastructure of apps and websites for rapid coordination.[1]

## Decline and succession

After Trump's November 2024 victory, commentators asked "where's the resistance?"[1] DeLarme's answer was that participants motivated by shallow social incentives had simply walked away or dissociated from politics, while professional operatives regrouped under a new banner, [[the-opposition|the Opposition]], articulated most fully by [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]].[1] Elias himself wrote that the resistance had been premised on the hope that holding Trump back for four years would restore normal politics, a premise he judged inadequate going forward.[1]
