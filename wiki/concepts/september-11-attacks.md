# September 11 Attacks

On September 11, 2001, [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] terrorists hijacked four passenger airliners and crashed two of them into the twin World Trade Center skyscrapers in Manhattan.[1] A third plane struck the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] building and a fourth crashed into a field in Pennsylvania.[1] Nearly 3,000 people were killed.[1]

## Renewed calls for disclosure (2025)

In April 2025, Hollywood actor and [[donald-trump|Trump]] supporter [[mel-gibson|Mel Gibson]] urged the American government to reveal the "truth" about the attacks.[1] Gibson shared a clip from [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s interview with former Congressman [[curt-weldon|Curt Weldon]], published Monday, April 14, 2025, and wrote on X that the tragedy "might be the biggest scandal in American history."[1]

Gibson raised the collapse of a third structure: "Three buildings collapsed as if they were programmed to do so. Not two – three. To this day, no one has explained how Building 7 fell, even though no plane hit it."[1] He also argued that the towers "were designed to withstand airplane impacts," and concluded: "What really happened that day? And why is no one allowed to talk about it? Enough with the fear. It's time for the truth. About 9/11. About history. About all of us."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] endorsed Gibson's post and asked when the public would learn who in the U.S. government was complicit in what he called an act of treason, asserting the attack did not result from a series of unfortunate mistakes but was allowed to happen by people in the Bush administration and the Republican Party — possibly including figures now advising President Trump on geopolitics.[1]

## 24th anniversary commemoration

For the 24th anniversary in September 2025, survivors and victims' families gathered at New York's [[9-11-memorial-and-museum|9/11 Memorial and Museum]] for the annual rite of remembrance, an hours-long recitation of nearly 3,000 names at the site known as ground zero.[2] The [[new-york-times|New York Times]] noted that students today see the events of September 11 as history, belonging to a post-Cold War generation for whom terrorism may be a more familiar threat than nuclear annihilation and whose defining event has been the coronavirus pandemic.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the anniversary landed the day after the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]], leaving her as angry and shaken as she had felt 24 years earlier, and pointed readers to [[codex-911|CODEX 9/11]].[2]

## Allegations of a CIA–FBI coverup (2023)

In April 2023 [[florida-bulldog|Florida Bulldog]] reported that ex-[[fbi|FBI]] agents had accused top [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and FBI officials of a 9/11 coverup, drawing on a sworn declaration by [[donald-canestraro|Donald Canestraro]], an investigator with the Department of Defense's [[military-commissions-defense-organization|Military Commissions Defense Organization]], dated July 20, 2021.[3] The declaration reports that weeks before the attacks an angry New York FBI counterterrorism agent nearly "came over the table" at CIA officials who were blocking him from obtaining intelligence about two al Qaeda terrorists who would soon help hijack an American Airlines jet and crash it into the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], writing afterward, "Someone is going to die."[3]

Canestraro interviewed eleven ex-FBI agents, two ex-CIA officers, a CNN investigative journalist, former deputy National Security Advisor Richard Clarke, and former Senator Bob Graham, co-chair of Congress's Joint Inquiry into 9/11, mostly in 2016 and 2018.[3] The accounts support allegations that CIA officials including Director [[george-tenet|George Tenet]] intentionally withheld from the FBI the fact that future hijackers [[nawaf-al-hazmi|Nawaf al Hazmi]] and [[khalid-al-mihdhar|Khalid al Mihdhar]] had entered the United States at Los Angeles after an [[al-qaeda|al Qaeda]] summit in Kuala Lumpur in early January 2000, and the theory — never fully explored by the 9/11 Commission or Congress — that the CIA kept silent because it was working with Saudi intelligence to recruit the pair as informants.[3]

The 22-page report states that the CIA "used their liaison relationship with the Saudi Intelligence Services to conduct an operation on U.S. soil" because the Agency is forbidden by law from running intelligence operations domestically, and that after the attacks FBI personnel in San Diego and at headquarters were told not to reveal to Congress the full extent of Saudi involvement with al Qaeda.[3] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that the investigation came from a DEA veteran working inside the Pentagon's own defense apparatus for Guantanamo detainee [[ammar-al-baluchi|Ammar al-Baluchi]], and concluded that while it is "not the full story of 9/11," it "does expose a significant strand of it."[3]
