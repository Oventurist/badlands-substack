# Steadfast Defender Exercise

Steadfast Defender is the name given to a [[nato|NATO]] live joint command exercise announced in September 2023 and scheduled for the following spring, described at the time as the alliance's biggest such drill since the Cold War.[1] The exercise was reported to assemble more than 40,000 troops to practise how the alliance would attempt to repel Russian aggression against one of its member states.[1]

## Scale and design

Planning figures cited by NATO officials included between 500 and 700 air combat missions, more than 50 ships, and roughly 41,000 troops.[1] The scenario was designed to model potential manoeuvres against an enemy based on a coalition led by [[russia|Russia]], given the notional name "Occasus" for the purposes of the drill.[1] Officials described the exercise as a technical first in that it would use real-world geographical data to generate more realistic training scenarios for participating troops.[1]

The drill was planned to take place across [[germany|Germany]], [[poland|Poland]] and the Baltic states in February and March, and formed part of a new NATO training strategy under which the alliance would conduct two large exercises each year rather than one.[1] NATO also indicated it would train to counter terrorist threats beyond its immediate borders.[1] Sweden, whose NATO accession bid had not yet been ratified by [[turkey|Turkey]] and [[hungary|Hungary]], was to be included, bringing the number of participating nations to 32.[1]

## Strategic context and commentary

The exercise was presented as part of NATO's rapid push to transform itself from a crisis-response organisation into a war-fighting alliance, a shift prompted by the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|Russian invasion of Ukraine]].[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterised the announcement as "NATO warmongers" preparing war drills as an open threat display or provocation directed at [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], and argued that Putin was unlikely to take the bait in a kinetic manner.[1] He assessed the headlines as producing two principal effects on what he termed the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]]: ratcheting tension amid concerns of a third world war, and doing so against a backdrop in which NATO — framed as the globalist, collectivist West — appears as the aggressor.[1]

## Steadfast Defender 24

The exercise formally kicked off in late January 2024 as Exercise Steadfast Defender 24, slated to run through the end of May and hailed as the largest NATO exercise since the Cold War, with some 90,000 troops from all 31 member states taking part.[2] Sweden — on the cusp of formally entering the alliance after the Turkish parliament belatedly signed off — was expected to send troops and equipment.[2] A NATO statement said the alliance "will demonstrate its ability to reinforce the Euro-Atlantic area via trans-Atlantic movement of forces from North America," and the focus of the drills was improving readiness to deploy NATO assets rapidly and at large scale from North America and elsewhere to defend Europe from an invasion.[2]

Burning Bright wrote that escalation and acceleration had defined the Biden era on the international stage, and that NATO chose to launch drills simulating an all-out boots-on-the-ground war with Russia precisely as the Collective Mind was being inundated with the failure of the Ukrainian proxy war and the increasingly hemmed-in position of the Establishment.[2] He argued this was the very escalatory aggression the Kremlin had accused the United States, the [[european-union|EU]] and the NATO bloc of pursuing for more than a generation of globalist policy.[2] While average people of all nations desire peace, he wrote, they were being shown that their own would-be masters seemed most hungry for a new world war; with proxies defeated across the game board and the U.S. military seemingly controlled by "the good guys" via [[devolution|Devolution]], the only remaining weapon was "inflammatory Kabuki theater" — in a word, desperation.[2]
