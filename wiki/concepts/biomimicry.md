# Biomimicry

Biomimicry, also known as biomimetics, is a design discipline that mimics nature in order to solve human limitations and challenges.[1]

## Historical practice

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified [[leonardo-da-vinci|Leonardo da Vinci]] as perhaps the most accomplished designer in the field.[1] Da Vinci concluded that flight was achievable after studying the skeletons and musculature of birds, recognizing that their bones were hollow and flexible and that the glide achieved by articulated wings mattered more than the flapping of wings.[1] Once he grasped that air had currents and properties analogous to water, he adapted Archimedes' screw — developed to move water uphill — into the design of the world's first helicopter in 1480.[1] Da Vinci also developed explosive cluster-bomb munitions after studying pomegranates and their seeds, and made obsessive comparisons between the human body and nature, noting the similarity between the branching of veins, particularly in the lungs, and the root systems of trees.[1] He concluded that God was the ultimate engineer who had perfected all of His designs, and that humans should learn from Him by studying and mimicking His work rather than competing with Him.[1]

## Applications in architecture and medicine

In an architecture-school thesis on hospital design, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry found research showing that exposure to nature and natural elements — such as sunlight and the sound of running water — reduced healing time by 30 percent and reduced requests for pain medication by 35 percent.[1] He lamented that this research is often implemented only superficially in hospital design, through mere images of the outdoors or faux-lighting systems meant to mimic sunlight.[1]

## Contemporary example

The [[garuda-squad|Garuda squad]] of eagles trained by the [[telangana-police|Telangana police]] to intercept drones was presented as a contemporary instance of biomimetic ingenuity in the security domain.[1]
