# Hate Speech

"Hate speech" is the category of expression that, in Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s account, functions as the foundational pretext for every censorship framework advocated by the interagency, international blob over the past decade.[1] The claim that hate speech poses such a danger to society that legal limits on expression become necessary is, he argues, the same reasoning used to justify the EU's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[1]

## Elastic application

What counts as "hatred" is described as a gray area determined largely by whichever political party holds power.[1] The article cites the labeling as hate speech of claims that COVID originated in a lab — despite the lab in question being U.S.-funded — and of the position that children should not undergo gender-affirming care.[1] Similarly, the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] recorded roughly 1,200 "antisemitic incidents" on U.S. college campuses between October 7, 2023 and September 24, 2024, the vast majority classified as harassment or hate speech; DeLarme contends many of these amount to differences of opinion about the conduct of the Israeli government.[1]

## Counterproductive effects

The article argues that aggressive policing of discourse around a topic makes that topic more charged, and that overbroad use of the term "antisemitism" has long been seen as helping antisemitism proliferate — a dynamic he says holds equally for COVID and for conspiracy theories.[1] Legal instruments built on this pretext include the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] and [[executive-order-14188|Executive Order 14188]].[1]
