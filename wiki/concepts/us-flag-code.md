# United States Flag Code

The **United States Flag Code** is the body of federal guidance governing the proper display and handling of the American flag. Among its provisions is the rule that "the flag of the United States of America should be at the center and at the highest point of the group when a number of flags of States or localities or pennants of societies are grouped and displayed from staffs."[1]

## 2023 White House Pride Month display controversy

In June 2023, the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] drew online criticism after a Pride Month display at the [[white-house|White House]] facing the South Lawn featured a rainbow-colored Pride flag in the center, flanked by two American flags.[1] Critics on Twitter argued that placing the Pride flag at the center of a multi-flag display contravened the Flag Code's centrality requirement.[1]

[[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] president [[tom-fitton|Tom Fitton]] wrote that in order "to advance revolutionary transgender agenda targeting children, Biden violates basic tenet of US Flag Code and disrespects every American service member buried under its colors."[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] posted that "the US flag DOES NOT FLANK *ANY* FLAG. Especially not on OUR White House."[1] Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that although reporting attributed the backlash generically to "Twitter users," much of the outrage came from active-duty military personnel and veterans.[1]

The episode occurred days before Flag Day, for which Biden had issued a proclamation on June 9, 2023 stating that "Old Glory stands for hope, pride, and progress" — a document published, in the Badlands reading, one day before the display itself allegedly violated the Code.[1] Badlands commentary framed the incident as part of a broader "degradation of American culture and values," connecting it to [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s contemporaneous commentary on the deliberate rejection of social taboos as a mechanism for transforming national culture.[1]

The Flag Code carries no penalties for violation; it is advisory in character, which is why disputes over displays such as the 2023 White House Pride event are argued in the register of symbolism and respect rather than enforcement.[1]
