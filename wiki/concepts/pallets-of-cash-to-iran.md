# Pallets of Cash to Iran

"Pallets of cash" refers to the transfer of approximately $1.7 billion in foreign banknotes from the United States government to [[iran|Iran]] in January 2016, delivered by unmarked aircraft at night and disclosed to the public only months afterward.[1] In the Badlands Media essay "Panic in DC," author [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that the episode has been persistently mischaracterized by both the press and the political class, and that it is in fact the single most important thread for unravelling what he calls the cabal's financing of global terrorism.[1]

## The two "media lies"

Lange identifies what he calls the first media lie as the claim that the cash shipments were part of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran nuclear deal]].[1] He argues they were a separate operation entirely, run by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], and that the nuclear-deal framing was adopted by the Obama administration and echoed uncritically by the media.[1]

The second lie, in his telling, was the "red herring" that the money was a ransom payment for American hostages.[1] He contends this framing was deliberately constructed to be argued about by both political camps, because a hostage-ransom debate — however damaging — is far less damaging than an admission of direct terrorism financing, and it drew sympathy from Democratic voters who would not otherwise defend funding terrorism.[1] Notably, he observes, there was never an actual investigation; there was only punditry.[1]

## Why cash, and why foreign currency

The essay stresses that the global financial system runs on the U.S. dollar, and that Iran, [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]], the [[quds-force|Quds Force]], the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]], [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and Hamas were all designated entities, making dollar transactions with them illegal for banks.[1] Delivering value in physical foreign banknotes was therefore, in Lange's reading, a deliberate mechanism for evading sanctions and avoiding wire-transfer logs that would have created an evidentiary trail.[1]

Citing Investor's Business Daily, the article notes a report by the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations alleging that Obama officials pressed the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] to let Iran convert roughly $5.7 billion held in Oman's Bank of Muscat from rials into dollars and then into euros — a conversion that required a special Treasury license granted in February 2016 and never disclosed to [[congress|Congress]] or the public.[1] This directly contradicted the administration's public assurance that Iran would be barred from the U.S. banking system.[1] The article observes that the [[state-department|State Department]] itself characterizes Iran as the world's foremost state sponsor of terrorism.[1]

## The $1.3 billion follow-on payment

Reporting from CBS News quoted in the article records that the administration paid Iran $1.3 billion in interest in January 2016, two days after allowing $400 million in cash to fly to Tehran, and that this was structured as thirteen separate payments of $99,999,999.99 plus a final payment of roughly $10 million.[1] State Department spokeswoman Elizabeth Trudeau declined to elaborate, citing diplomatic sensitivities.[1] Lange argues the sub-$100 million structuring was plainly intended to avoid a reporting threshold that would have alerted Congress or other agencies — a pattern indistinguishable from criminal structuring.[1]

The official justification was that the funds settled a dispute over a $400 million payment made in the 1970s by the U.S.-backed Shah's government for military equipment never delivered because of the 1979 Islamic Revolution.[1] President Barack Obama argued cash was necessary because the two countries lacked a banking relationship, an explanation the article notes did not rule out the use of intermediary banks.[1]

## The Judgment Fund and the 1991 precedent

The settlement money came from the [[judgment-fund|Judgment Fund]], a permanently appropriated Treasury account for settling litigation claims that allows a president to bypass direct congressional approval.[1] CBS noted that the United States had previously paid out $278 million in Iran-related claims from the same fund in 1991 — a data point Lange treats as the key to the whole story, since the president in 1991 was [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]], a former Director of Central Intelligence.[1] He describes this as "the same CIA playbook": cash to Iran, out of the Judgment Fund, justified by a supposed looming loss at the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]], with American hostages in the background.[1]

The article further argues the ICJ rationale was hollow, citing Reuters coverage of Iran's later suit against the Trump administration which noted that both countries have historically ignored ICJ rulings and that the court has no power to enforce them.[1] Secretary of State [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] called Iran's suit meritless and an attempt to interfere with lawful U.S. sanctions.[1]

## Alleged CIA operation

Lange's central claim is that the airlift was a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] operation — the "shadow agency" directing logistics and providing security — separate from the nuclear deal, intended both to fund proxy terrorism and to fund the agency's own off-books activity in promoting wars.[1] He asserts that Donald Trump knows the operational details, including plane counts, landing times, and how the cash was divided, and that the money was partially disguised as foreign aid routed through Europe.[1] The source of that knowledge, he argues, is the [[national-security-agency|NSA]], "the agency that collects all forms of data."[1]

## Traced to terrorist recipients

Quoting The Washington Times, the article states that the U.S. government traced portions of the $1.7 billion to Iranian-backed terrorists within two years, including [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], the [[quds-force|Quds Force]], and Houthi rebels in Yemen.[1] Trump is quoted describing Iran's access to more than $100 billion, including $1.8 billion in cash, as "a slush fund for weapons, terror, and oppression."[1]

## Reading in "Q for Dummies" Chapter 9

Chapter 9 of the Badlands series [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] offers a different explanation of the payment's purpose while working through Q drop #71.[2] Its author writes that the exact reason the money was sent remains up for debate, and rejects both the terrorism-funding and hostage-purchase readings in favour of the view that the transfer was repayment — compensation for the money [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] had given [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] on the certainty that she would win.[2]

On that reading, [[barack-obama|Obama]] sent billions in cash to [[iran|Iran]] to be funnelled back to the Brotherhood and the Saudis after the attempt to take full control of the U.S. government through a Clinton victory in 2016 failed, using American taxpayer dollars to do it.[2] The chapter situates this alongside its claim that Saudi Arabia donated more than $10 million to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] during 2015–16 and that Clinton's defeat cut off the donors' route into the [[white-house|White House]].[2]
