# Mind Control Programming

Mind control programming refers to the deliberate installation of conditioned responses in the dissociated "parts" or alters of an abuse victim, so that the victim can be triggered to act in ways the host identity neither chooses nor remembers.[1] The Badlands Media article "Gazing Into the Abyss pt.2: a Therapist's Tale" sets out to highlight how cults and military- and intelligence-affiliated groups have perfected this art.[1]

## Mechanism

Programming depends on the fragmentation described under [[dissociative-identity-disorder|dissociative identity disorder]]: trauma severe enough to split off compartmentalized parts that carry their own life and memory, leaving the host unaware.[1] Because the parts can be conditioned separately, handlers can address them directly.[1]

## Documented effects

The clinical notes of [[alison-miller|Alison Miller]] record "programs" being set off in her clients to counteract therapy: falling asleep in session, telephoning the group to report what had been discussed, a sudden inability to understand English, self-cutting, and attempts to harm the therapist.[1] A cult "pick-up system" outside a mental hospital relied on post-hypnotic triggering — patients felt an overwhelming urge to smoke at a precise time each night, at which point a handler approached to threaten them or activate further programs enabling later abductions.[1] The article cites G. H. Estabrooks' *Hypnotism* as the reference for this post-hypnotic triggering technique.[1]

Programming also enabled apparently inexplicable events among Miller's clients: a mother repeatedly appearing wherever her fostered daughter went, a young man coming to at midnight on a street corner waiting for a ride with no memory of arriving, and a mother abruptly surrendering a child she loved for adoption.[1]

## Wider context

The article treats programming as inseparable from [[ritual-abuse|ritual abuse]], and follows the earlier Badlands piece "The Deep State's Sinister Core" by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], which described Dr. James Randall Noblitt's initiation into the field.[1] It also points readers to a Ted Gunderson presentation on FBI files concerning alleged CIA involvement in satanic ritual abuse.[1]
