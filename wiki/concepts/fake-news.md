# Fake News

"Fake news" is described in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as a dismissal device — a label applied by mainstream outlets to information they do not wish to see examined, and simultaneously one of the brain's own defences against information that contradicts existing belief.[1]

## As a cognitive defence

In "The Aftermath of a Trump Victory" (October 2024), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] listed three tactics the mind uses to reject unwelcome information: excusing a wrong ("the people I support are bad, but the other side is worse"), justifying it ("it's bad, but it's for the greater good"), and blocking it outright with the assertion "it never happened, it's fake news."[1]

## As a media tactic

Carlson argued that most of the time the media can simply designate anything it does not want exposed as fake news, giving as examples the claims that children are not really being trafficked, that illegal immigrants are not really invading the borders, and that elections are not really stolen.[1] Where a phenomenon is too visible to deny — inflation being his primary example, since outlets cannot tell people that goods do not cost more — the tactic shifts from denial to deflection, blaming greedy businesses rather than the government money printing he identifies as the cause.[1]

He linked the label to the treatment of dissenters more broadly, placing media outlets that call people conspiracy theorists alongside Facebook and Twitter deplatforming, Snopes fact-checking and government threats of imprisonment for misinformation as the mechanisms that remove truth-tellers from public view, in the manner of the handlers in [[the-truman-show|*The Truman Show*]].[1] Carlson predicted that the shutting down of these mechanisms after a [[donald-trump|Trump]] victory would force a large part of the public out of its echo chamber and into a [[mass-awakening|mass awakening]].[1]

## Origin of mass skepticism (Carlson, 2024)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s September 17, 2024 essay credits Trump's use of the phrase "fake news" with being the point at which most Americans first considered that what they saw on television might not be the truth, calling it a stark realization to accept lifelong manipulation.[2]

## "Fake News" as part of the real war (November 2022)

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s essay "The Unreality Paradox" argued that the term is double-edged: the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]], dubbed the "Fake News" at turns affectionately and venomously by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], genuinely does spin webs of lies, but the label also invites a dismissal that is itself strategically costly.[3] In his formulation of the [[unreality-paradox|Unreality Paradox]], the fake news is a component of the real war, so treating it as unworthy of respect, attention, time or intellectual capacity forfeits any understanding of the intentions behind the narrative weaponization it carries.[3]

He wrote that the concept of "Fake News" suggests and even reveals the Men Behind the Curtain, and that rather than disengaging from their paradigm entirely the correct response is to "tear that curtain down" and read the scripts they type, so as to anticipate the next mass psychological deployment and work out how to thwart it.[3] The essay closes by restating the refrain in amended form: "The News is Fake, but [their] War on US is very real."[3]
