# Crown Temple

**Crown Temple** is the term used in sovereign-citizen and legal-mythology literature — and adopted in the Badlands Media essay *Throne of the Ivy Grid* — for the square-mile sovereign district of the City of London, described there as the legal citadel of the world rather than as an ordinary municipality.[1]

According to the essay, the Crown Temple houses the Inner and Middle Temple legal bars, the point at which maritime and Roman law are said to interface, and it is claimed that all barristers swear loyalty not to the British monarchy but to a distinct "Crown Corporation."[1] In this account, ownership of people, land, and nations is transacted in legalese within the Temple and enforced through international courts.[1]

## Language as legal instrument

The essay presents the Crown Temple as fusing with [[vatican|Vatican]] authority behind "marble veils," and attributes to Black's Law Dictionary a system of deliberate double meanings that "weaponize language itself" — the claim that "understand" means to "stand under," and that a summons constitutes a spiritual calling into bondage.[1] Words in this framing do not describe reality but control it.[1]

## Relationship to the soul-trust system

The Crown Temple is positioned as the secular-legal counterpart to the [[unam-sanctam|Unam Sanctam]] claim and the [[cestui-que-vie-trust|Cestui Que Vie]] trust system: the Vatican asserts the metaphysical claim, while the Temple's "legal technicians" merge with clerical immunity to render the resulting spiritual theft untouchable by secular courts.[1] [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] is described in the essay as having been mentored by "legal alchemists within the Crown Temple's American extensions."[1]

The essay further situates the Temple within the bloodline grid, calling it only one node in a wider structure in which sovereign authority migrated from monarchs to financiers, central banks, and covert compacts.[1]

These characterizations are not recognized by mainstream legal scholarship, which treats the Inner and Middle Temple as two of the four Inns of Court and the City of London Corporation as a local authority with historic privileges.[1]
