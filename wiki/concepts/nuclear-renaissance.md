# Nuclear Renaissance

The **Nuclear Renaissance** is the term used in Badlands macro commentary — principally by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] — for a long-seeded global pivot back to civil nuclear power, which contributors argue is being accelerated by engineered crises in fossil-fuel supply.[1]

## Thesis

Writing in the March 10, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], Burning Bright argued that an emergent macro narrative of energy weaponization was rising to compete with — and ultimately subsume — the engineered war story out of Tehran, and that the crisis was accelerating the "germination phase" of a Nuclear Renaissance seeded over many years.[1] On this reading the visible headlines about regime change and forever war conceal the real story: the deliberate targeting of energy infrastructure, including Israeli strikes on Iranian oil facilities, [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] responses against Gulf assets, production shut-ins, and the near-collapse of tanker traffic through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

The case for nuclear is framed as what survives the crisis intact: baseload reliability immune to geopolitics and weather, fuel stockpiled for years rather than dependent on daily tanker runs, and the capacity to meet exploding demand from artificial intelligence, data centers, manufacturing and electrification — without the "scarcity programming" Badlands attributes to decades of adversary messaging.[1]

## Winners and losers

Burning Bright contrasted two blocs. The "so-called Allies," embodied by [[israel|Israel]] and the NATO/European axis, are said to be escalating the energy crisis while being least equipped to weather it: Europe remains over 50% import-dependent after the engineered loss of Russian pipeline flows and what the piece calls suicidal nuclear phase-outs, a "severe strategic mistake" now openly admitted by Germany.[1] France's nuclear-heavy fleet is described as the lonely exception proving the rule, while Israel sits inside the region it is helping destabilize.[1]

Against them stand the "Sovereign Alliance" nations. [[russia|Russia]] continues flowing oil to [[india|India]], its largest buyer, under a US-issued temporary waiver; India has answered perceived pressure with the statement that it "has never depended on permission from any country to buy Russian oil"; and China-linked carriers transit the narrowest stretches of Hormuz without incident.[1] Burning Bright labels Russia, [[china|China]] and India the "Sovereign Trinity," with Iran as economic facilitator — sparring on the narrative level while cooperating on the Actual, rerouting supplies and insulating their populations from shocks the [[invisible-enemy|Invisible Enemy]] of neocons, globalists and warmongers requires to maintain control.[1]

## Mandate cultivation

The crisis is characterized as a feedback loop of "Mandate Cultivation and Actualization": by exposing the fragility of fossil-fuel dependence and the folly of the Green Agenda, it simultaneously discloses the need for an energy transition and accelerates the pathway toward it.[1] Badlands holds that the Iranian chaos, disruptive in the short term, grants President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] a stronger public mandate to sharpen a divergence he sent into overdrive from 2025 — executive orders accelerating NRC modernization and advanced reactor deployment, the quadrupling of domestic nuclear capacity, and the embedding of civil nuclear energy into the AI prosperity framework.[1]

## Davos 2026 and the AI power-demand pivot

The 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos featured extensive discussion of artificial intelligence's economic impact alongside infrastructure constraints — notably electricity supply and grid capacity needed for large-scale compute.[2] On Wednesday, January 21, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States was "going heavy into nuclear," explicitly linking nuclear expansion to rising power demand tied to AI and broader industrial growth; nuclear-linked equities rose in premarket trading after the remarks.[2] The US–Slovakia civil nuclear cooperation agreement was signed the same day, covering reactor technology, supply chains and safety and security best practices, according to a [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] press release, with Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] describing a shared strategic view of energy focused on "safety, sustainability, and technological maturity."[2]

In his Davos opening remarks, [[blackrock|BlackRock]] CEO [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]] warned that rapid AI growth could harm the prospects of working and professional classes and framed AI as a major stress test for capitalism.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the sequence as confirmation of his long-running nuclear thesis: Trump confirming an American nuclear pivot, Fico confirming the need for it, Germany admitting wind and solar were mistakes, and Fink pivoting BlackRock from climate alarmism to AI doom-mongering, with the AI transition ultimately powered by nuclear energy.[2] He argued the "Nuclear Nightmare" was an engineered [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian]] narrative designed to steer the United States away from the future Eisenhower and Kennedy envisioned, and that Trump's references to a "New Manhattan Project" reflect his view that the original Manhattan Project was a narrative deployment rather than an actual one.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] drew a parallel between the nuclear and AI fear campaigns, recalling the 2023 release of Christopher Nolan's *Oppenheimer* and quoting her own earlier writing that the regime's primary AI metaphor for driving hysteria and pushing global governance is the nuclear arms race — the same objective, themes and talking points, amounting to "Fear us and submit, for we have the power to destroy you."[2] She endorsed Burning Bright's formulation that nuclear has the power to accelerate the transition to the [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]], which is why the powers that be pivoted from demonizing nuclear to demonizing the technology it will power, and argued the fear is peddled to keep audiences paralyzed in the present.[2]


## As precondition of the AI buildout

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued in June 2026 that an AI infrastructure sprint demands abundant, reliable and ultimately decentralized power above every other resource, making a nuclear renaissance the non-negotiable precondition of the Age of Abundance the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] has been telegraphing for years.[3] He linked it to gargantuan data centers and the on-shoring of critical manufacturing, set against a disentanglement from scarcity-based control systems, and noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[trump-ai-action-plan|AI Action Plan]] and related executive orders advance the nuclear and permitting reforms that make AI-scale power possible.[3]

## Burning Bright's "Good Reset" framing (May 2025)

Writing in May 2025, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the surge of headlines about [[iran|Iranian]] nuclear talks, Chinese nuclear programs and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s repeated references to denuclearization mattered less as a war signal than as evidence that "NUCLEAR" had returned to the central narrative.[4] Beneath that noise, he contended, were repeated overtures to a global energy transition that could serve as the bedrock of a worldwide [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]].[4]

In this reading, Trump's nuclear rhetoric reflects an understanding that the winning story about nuclear technology for 80 years has been its use in mass casualty events, and that humanity has been "hard-coded" with a belief that nuclear energy is too unstable to explore.[4] Burning Bright pointed to the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] restarting nuclear energy projects at infamous sites such as Three Mile Island while Chernobyl re-entered international consciousness, and argued the timeline toward such an age was rug-pulled after the Eisenhower administration by the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] that [[dwight-eisenhower|Dwight Eisenhower]] warned of and [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] tried to resist.[4] He framed the push, staged in a region whose riches were built on the very energy systems nuclear power could render moot, as heading for a "'Good' Reset in the place of a Great one."[4]

## AI-driven restart wave (2024)

By September 2024 the nuclear revival had acquired a concrete driver: the enormous electricity appetite of the data centers powering the AI boom, with major stakeholders in Washington and Silicon Valley converging on nuclear as an answer after years of false starts.[5] On September 30, the [[department-of-energy]] announced a finalized $1.5 billion loan to stake the revival of the [[palisades-nuclear-plant|Holtec Palisades]] plant in Michigan, which would be the first American nuclear plant ever restarted.[5] A week earlier, [[microsoft]] and [[constellation-energy]] had unveiled a $1.6 billion power purchase deal to restart a dormant reactor at Pennsylvania's [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island]] plant in 2028.[5]

At Climate Week in New York, fourteen of the world's biggest banks and financial institutions pledged to support the COP28 goal of tripling global nuclear capacity by 2050, against a backdrop of U.S. electricity use soaring after fifteen flat years on the strength of new factories, data centers, electric vehicles and hotter summers.[5]

Badlands commentary described this as a "convergence of nuclear narratives," arguing that the establishment was too late to stop the American pivot from the [[green-new-deal]] back to the post-Eisenhower nuclear timeline abandoned after the assassination of JFK.[5] It distinguished "Actual Layer" developments — Department of Energy and Defense Department nuclear restoration initiatives proposed under Trump and actualized under Biden ahead of Trump's return — from "Narrative Layer" fear campaigns running from the film *Oppenheimer* to coverage of nuclear threats posed by the New Axis, framing the trend as reclaiming an American nuclear dream from a globalist nuclear nightmare.[5]

## May 2025 nuclear executive orders

On May 22, 2025, the [[white-house|White House]] promoted a package of four executive orders on nuclear power as a "HUGE Day for the Nuclear Industry," with Secretary [[doug-burgum|Doug Burgum]] saying the action was "going to turn the clock back on over 50 years of overregulation of an industry," each order attacking a separate constraint that had held the sector back.[6] The administration listed the nuclear renaissance among its accomplishments for the week alongside the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One, Big, Beautiful Bill]], the [[take-it-down-act|Take It Down Act]], and [[golden-dome|Golden Dome]].[6] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the signing as literal confirmation of a coming "Golden Age," tying it to a "Timeline War" begun in the 1950s and to remarks by Lt. Gen. [[steve-kwast|Steve Kwast]] that American energy independence and dominance is "a power card" giving Trump strategic room to usher in further changes.[6]
