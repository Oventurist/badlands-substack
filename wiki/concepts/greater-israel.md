# Greater Israel ("From the Nile to the Euphrates")

"Greater Israel" refers to the maximalist conception of Israeli territory, often invoked through the biblical phrase describing land "from the Nile to the Euphrates." The idea returned to international headlines in February 2026 after US Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] articulated it in a televised interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].[1]

## Huckabee's remarks and the diplomatic fallout

Huckabee said that [[israel|Israel]] has a biblical right to a large swath of the Middle East — referencing land "from the Nile to the Euphrates" — and added that "it would be fine if they took it all," while simultaneously stating that Israel is not currently seeking to expand its borders.[1]

Arab and Muslim governments and regional bodies condemned the remarks over the weekend, including [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Egypt, Jordan and others, together with the [[arab-league|League of Arab States]] and the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation|Organization of Islamic Cooperation]].[1] Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry "categorically" denounced the comments, called for clarification, and described them as contrary to international law, the UN Charter and diplomatic norms.[1] A US Embassy spokesperson said the comments did not reflect any change in US policy and that Huckabee's full remarks made clear Israel had no desire to change its current boundaries.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the Saudi statement was co-signed by countries from every continent, spanning NATO, BRICS, the EU, the Arab League, the 57-nation Organization of Islamic Cooperation, and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization — arguing that Huckabee had "succeeded not only in uniting the Muslim World, but what seems like the entire free world, against Israel."[1]

## Domestic Israeli resonance

The concept was not confined to the American ambassador. Opposition leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]], speaking in Hebrew, expressed empathy for the Greater Israel that Huckabee conceptualized in the Carlson interview, despite his positioning as a center-left politician.[1] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] separately said he expected [[hamas|Hamas]] to be given an ultimatum to disarm "in the coming days," and that "in the end" Israel will occupy Gaza and reestablish Jewish settlements there.[1] [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]], another leading contender in the October prime ministerial election, said "the world will never love Israel, so it should learn to fear it."[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands commentary rejected the apocalyptic reading of these events ("OH WOW JUST LIKE IN THE BOOK OF REVELATION!") in favor of the interpretation that the arrogance and hubris of Israel's ruling elite had provoked collective global revulsion.[1] The Brief tied the episode to a broader argument that the Israel Lobby and neoconservatives were undermining President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s peace efforts, including the rejection of his [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]].[1]

## April 2026 assessment

In April 2026, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] assessed that [[donald-trump|President Trump]] was diffusing the "Greater Israel Empire" before it could establish itself as the Middle East's hegemon, resolving the US–[[iran|Iran]] conflict while [[israel|Israel]] continued fighting both [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and the IRGC.[2] He identified two short-term objectives beneath the long-term Greater Israel goal: capturing [[mount-hermon|Mount Hermon]] to enable annexation of southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and southern Syria, and pushing the border north toward Tyre via a [[forward-defense-zone|Forward Defense Zone]] to shift the maritime boundary and annex the [[qana-gas-field|Qana Gas Field]].[2]

With no indication that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] planned to stop taking territory, he argued the campaign could only be halted by kinetic force — most plausibly by the emerging bloc of [[turkey|Turkey]], [[egypt|Egypt]], [[pakistan|Pakistan]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], and eventually Iran that met at the [[antalya-diplomacy-forum|Antalya Diplomacy Forum]], backed by Russia, China, and a Europe souring on Israel.[2]

## The 2026 formulation

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued in April 2026 that supporting Israel's wars to establish an empire in the Middle East had made it an international pariah and would leave America with no real friends, only vassals feigning allegiance while praying in secret for its demise so they could reclaim their sovereignty.[3] He framed the choice starkly: "You can have a Golden Age where all countries cooperate in tranquility to pursue prosperity, or you can have an empire where all countries are subjugated by the Israeli-American alliance," concluding that an alliance with Greater Israel and a Golden Age cannot both be had.[3]

## Revisionist genealogy of the Greater Israel map

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] traced the Greater Israel concept through the [[revisionist-zionism|Revisionist Movement]] founded by [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]], a form of [[zionism|Zionism]] calling for territorial maximalism on both sides of the Jordan River, with the ambition to annex both Palestine and Jordan as parts of Israel.[4] [[nathan-mileikowsky|Nathan Mileikowsky]], grandfather of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], became one of the movement's leading advocates after immigrating to Mandatory Palestine in 1920, and he and his sons affiliated with the [[irgun|Irgun]].[4]

Irgun propaganda posters prominently displayed the Greater Israel map, which the analysis identifies as identical to the map that appeared on the podium at which [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] spoke at an event in March 2023.[4] On this reading, Netanyahu is "the prodigal son of Ze'ev Jabotinsky and the Revisionist Movement," and the invasion of Rafah was designed to provoke the Arabs and [[iran|Iran]] into attacking Israel, creating conditions of war that could ultimately have permitted annexation expanding Israel's borders far beyond the Jordan River.[4]

## Rabbinical calls to conquer Lebanon (2024)

In September 2024, as Israel weighed a ground invasion of southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]], Rabbi [[yitzchak-ginsburgh|Yitzchak Ginsburgh]] of the [[chabad|Chabad]] movement published a letter calling for the conquest and annexation of southern Lebanon by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]].[5] Ginsburgh maintained that Lebanon was part of the Land of Israel and had been given to the Jewish people by God, citing verses describing the land as extending to the Euphrates River, and wrote that "after the conquest and expulsion of the hostile population, a Jewish settlement must be established, thus completing the victory."[5] Other rabbis echoed the call, with Rabbi Nir Ben Artzi Shalita saying that ending the war with Lebanon would open a new way for the Messiah and redemption of the people of Israel.[5]

Badlands commentary identified this program explicitly as Greater Israel and described it as a core tenet of the Chabad sect, noting that these figures expect the messiah before the end of the Jewish calendar in 2027 and concluding that the Lebanon campaign amounts to a religious conquest advanced by fanatics occupying the most powerful positions in the Israeli government.[5]


## Golan annexation after the fall of Assad

Following the December 2024 [[fall-of-the-assad-regime|fall of the Assad regime]], Badlands cited the Greater Israel thesis as one of three motives for the operation, noting that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had already publicly boasted of ordering the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to begin annexing land in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]], territory still legally part of Syria despite military occupation since the end of the Six Day War in 1967.[6] The commentary described the use of a nominal defense force to invade a foreign country and seize land as an Orwellian use of language.[6] It presented the move as fulfilling a statement by Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] in October, when he said in an interview that [[damascus|Damascus]] was part of Israel based on his interpretation of scripture, and summarised the whole episode as being about "sticking it to Putin, stifling prosperity, and fulfilling Talmudic prophecy."[6]

## Settler crossing into Syria (August 2025)

In August 2025 a group of settler activists, accompanied by their young children, crossed the border from Israel into Syria in an attempt to establish a new settlement.[7] The [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] said troops were dispatched after several civilian vehicles were seen crossing near the border town of Alonei Habashan, and that soldiers "returned the civilians safely to the territory of the State of Israel after a short time," with the participants called in for police questioning.[7] The military described the episode as "a grave incident that constitutes a criminal offense" endangering both the public and its troops.[7]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked the incident to remarks the previous week in which [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] publicly described himself as being on a "historic and spiritual mission" to create Greater Israel, a project he said would require annexing parts of Jordan and Egypt as well as Lebanon, Syria and Palestine.[7] He argued that Netanyahu had been raised to act as a "secular messiah" of Talmudic expectation preparing the way for the Mashiach, referenced reports that Israel had conducted a red heifer sacrifice in secret, and asserted that plans to build the [[third-temple|Third Temple]] would require destroying the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem.[7]

## British imperial origins of the "Greater Israel" idea

The Badlands series "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler" traces the Greater Israel conception to British imperial strategy rather than to Jewish nationalism alone.[8] [[theodor-herzl]] envisioned Israel's borders extending "from the Brook of Egypt to the Euphrates," and in *Der Judenstaat* described the projected state as "a portion of the rampart of Europe against Asia, an outpost of civilization as opposed to barbarism."[8] In 1914 [[chaim-weizmann]] pitched a Jewish Palestine to Britain as "a very effective guard for the Suez Canal."[8]

The article maps a lineage from Lord Shaftesbury's 1839 call for restoration of the Jews in Palestine, through [[lord-palmerston]]'s 1840 instruction to the British ambassador in Constantinople, the 1865 founding of the [[palestine-exploration-fund]], and the 1917 Balfour Declaration co-authored by [[arthur-balfour]], [[leo-amery]], [[alfred-milner|Lord Milner]] and Walter Rothschild.[8] It observes that the maps of Herzl's envisioned empire and of Babylon in 539 BCE are "eerily similar," and links the project to [[george-hawkins-pember]]'s prophecy that Babylon must be rebuilt to become "the capital of the Antichrist."[8] It also warns that American nuclear power may back Zionist ambitions to purge the land of Arabs, "starting with Palestine and followed by Syria, Iraq, Lebanon, Egypt, and possibly Saudi Arabia, as outlined by the neo-con Clean Break Doctrine submitted to Netanyahu in 1996."[8]
