# Odysseus Lunar Lander Mission

Odysseus was a robotic lunar lander built and operated by the Texas firm [[intuitive-machines|Intuitive Machines]] that on Thursday, February 22, 2024 became the first privately built and operated robot to complete a soft lunar touchdown.[1] The mission was reported as a historic milestone for commercial spaceflight even though the landing did not go entirely to plan.[1]

## Tipped landing

Shortly after touchdown it emerged that the spacecraft was likely lying on its side with its head resting against a rock, though otherwise in good condition.[1] Intuitive Machines said Odysseus had plenty of power and was communicating with Earth, and controllers were attempting to retrieve pictures from the robot.[1] Steve Altemus, the company's chief executive and co-founder, said it was not totally clear what had happened, but that the data suggested the robot caught a foot on the surface and then fell because it still had some lateral motion at the moment of landing.[1] Another possibility raised was that Odysseus broke a leg as it came down; inertial measurement sensors indicated the vehicle's body was in a horizontal pose.[1] The account was reported by [[bbc|BBC News]] and carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief.[1]
