# Operation Rising Lion

Operation Rising Lion is the name given to the large-scale Israeli air campaign against Iran that began overnight into June 13, 2025, aimed at Iran's nuclear program.[1]

## Course of the campaign

Israel struck several nuclear facilities inside Iran as part of the campaign, which Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said was necessary to "roll back the Iranian threat to Israel's very survival."[1] The first wave of attacks damaged the [[natanz-nuclear-facility|Natanz]] uranium enrichment plant and assassinated leading Iranian nuclear scientists.[1] Netanyahu justified the operation by asserting that "if not stopped, Iran could produce a nuclear weapon in a very short time," adding, "It could be a year. It could be within a few months."[1]

Iran's Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said Israel had "crossed a new red line in international law" by attacking nuclear sites, and that Iran was launching missiles and drones at Israel in response.[1] He insisted the Iranian nuclear program was peaceful and that Iranian doctrine was "rooted in our belief in the prohibition and illegitimacy of nuclear weapons."[1] Israel is widely believed to possess nuclear weapons but neither confirms nor denies this.[1]

## American posture

As the campaign continued, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said publicly that "nothing is too late" for talks with Iran and that the Iranians had suggested coming to the White House, while declining to say whether the United States would join the strikes: "I may do it. I may not do it."[1] He also asserted that Iran was "totally defenseless" with "no air defense whatsoever" and that the United States had "totally captured the air" over Iran.[1] Reporting attributed to anonymous sources said Trump had privately approved attack plans while withholding a final order, with the deeply buried [[fordow-fuel-enrichment-plant|Fordow]] enrichment facility a possible US target.[1] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] described the associated American build-up in the Middle East — a third destroyer in the eastern Mediterranean and a second carrier strike group heading to the Arabian Sea — as purely defensive.[1]

## Russian reaction

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said that despite the complexity of Iran's internal politics, Iranian society was consolidating around the country's political leadership in response to the campaign, remarking that "this is almost always and everywhere this way. Iran is not an exception here."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary treated the "Iran nukes" framing as a manufactured narrative, arguing that "when people are telling the same story in 30 different ways, the underlying premise of the story is fake," and that Iran "never threatened the world with fake nukes — the [[deep-state|Deep State]] did."[1] Other commentary accused the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] of trying to draw the United States into the war by publishing stories sourced to anonymous "people familiar with the deliberations."[1]
