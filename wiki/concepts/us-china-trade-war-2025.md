# US–China Trade War (2025)

The 2025 US–China trade war refers to the escalating exchange of tariffs between Washington and Beijing under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which by April 2025 threatened to disrupt trade between the world's two largest economies and push the two toward outright decoupling.[1]

## Escalation of April 9, 2025

Trump raised the tariff on Chinese imports to 125% on Wednesday, April 9, 2025, hours after [[china|China]] boosted its duty on American goods to 84%.[1] Citing a lack of respect, Trump set China apart from other countries, announcing in a social media post that he was pausing his so-called "reciprocal tariffs" on many other trading partners because they had responded by reaching out for talks rather than retaliating.[1] The Chinese government signaled determination not to bend: "If the U.S. insists on further escalating its economic and trade restrictions, China has the firm will and abundant means to take necessary countermeasures and fight to the end," its Ministry of Commerce said before announcing the hike.[1] [[white-house|White House]] press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] responded, "When you punch at the United States of America, President Trump is going to punch back further."[1]

## Decoupling and market impact

A year earlier, then-Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] had told an audience in Beijing that the US did not want to decouple from China, saying "our two economies are deeply integrated, and a wholesale separation would be disastrous for both."[1] Twelve months on, Trump's tariffs above 120% and Beijing's determination to match them meant the cleavage Yellen warned of was rapidly becoming reality.[1] About $19 trillion had been wiped off the world's equity markets since the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500]] closed at a record high on February 19, and that week's selloff in Treasuries was the worst since the pandemic, with economists rushing to price in a US recession as the two capitals engaged in economic brinkmanship.[1] The White House said Trump "has a spine of steel and will not break," while Beijing vowed to "fight to the end."[1]

## Goldman Sachs recession reversal

Economists at [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] walked back a recession forecast on the afternoon of April 9 after Trump announced a 90-day pause on tariffs, issuing a note calling for a 45% chance of recession within a year and retracting a call made barely an hour earlier that had put the odds at 65%.[1] The about-face highlighted the difficulty of forecasting an economy subject to frequent changes in trade policy; economists had flagged trade-policy uncertainty as a major risk because businesses may delay investment and hiring until they know what rules will apply long term.[1] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] noted that Goldman called a US recession at 12:57 p.m. and rescinded it 73 minutes later.[1]

## Bessent and the non-retaliation doctrine

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said the tariff pause had benefited over 75 countries that chose to negotiate rather than retaliate, adding that "it took great courage for [President Trump] to stay the course until this moment."[1] He restated the administration's message to the world — do not retaliate and you will be rewarded — and said, "Every country in the world that wants to come and negotiate: we are willing to hear you," singling out China for its "insistence on escalation."[1] Bessent then raised the possibility of special agreements with particular trading partners.[1] In remarks outside the White House alongside Leavitt he rejected the term "trade war" to describe shifting global trade arrangements, and rejected a reporter's suggestion that 125% tariffs on China amounted to a de facto embargo.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described him as a calming, competent presence who, like [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] in a different way, appeared humored by the drama, and framed the episode as the administration delivering on its campaign promises under an America First banner.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that a plethora of second-order effects remained latent in "Trump's Gambit," the biggest being an inversion of globalist propaganda: with Trump easing up on Western trade partners, any opposition to his trade agenda would now be read as de facto defense of China.[1] He juxtaposed the Chinese commerce minister's "China will fight to the end" with Trump's "I don't blame China for what happened. I blame the people sitting right at this desk" and Bessent's remark that "what gives me a lot of confidence is the relationship between President Trump and Chairman [[xi-jinping|Xi]]," asking who the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]] really is.[1] Commenting on the Goldman reversal, he wrote that critics were not angry that Trump could move the global stock market by narrative deployment alone, but that he was exposing the market as narrative: "He who wins the story wins the war."[1]

## The 104% tariff (April 8, 2025)

The day before the escalation to 125%, the United States hiked tariffs on all Chinese imports to 104%, wiping another $1.5 trillion from US stock markets on Tuesday, April 8, 2025.[2] China had originally been set to face a 34% tariff increase on Wednesday as part of Trump's so-called "reciprocal" measure targeting virtually all US trade partners, but after Beijing responded with a proportional 34% duty of its own, the president raised the blanket tariff to a total of 104%.[2] Speaking at a [[national-republican-congressional-committee|National Republican Congressional Committee]] dinner in Washington, Trump said, "After all of the abuses they've perpetrated, China is attempting to impose additional unjustified tariffs."[2]

The [[white-house|White House]] published an amendment to the April 2 executive order in which Trump had declared a "national emergency" over the US trade deficit and imposed a baseline tariff on all imports.[2] The administration said nearly 70 countries had sought negotiations to mitigate the impact of the tariffs as Trump pursued "tailored deals" with individual nations.[2] Trump also said late Tuesday that China was manipulating the yuan to offset the effect of the tariffs.[2]

## Market turbulence and business paralysis

After three trading sessions of heavy losses driven by fears that the tariff policy would push the US and global economies into recession, Dow futures rose 700 points, or 1.8%, on Tuesday, April 8, with S&P 500 futures up 1.3% and Nasdaq futures 1% higher as investors sought buying opportunities.[2] Multiple Wall Street banks, including [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] and [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], warned that any escalation of the trade war would probably cause a US and global recession that year.[2]

Analysts warned that the chaotic policymaking environment could paralyze business investment rather than spur it, since committing billions of dollars to years-long US projects requires stability and clarity.[2] John Graham, a finance professor at [[duke-university|Duke University]], said, "Without a doubt, this could freeze things rather than spur investment," adding that "there is a lot of dust in the air and it will have to settle before people can make predictions that will lead to an increase in manufacturing in the United States."[2]

## Badlands commentary on the April 8 phase

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that one of the more incredible features of the trade war was the transparency with which Trump's team communicated the plan to the American people and the world, which allowed the establishment to commit to pro-system takes, adding that "Trump doesn't bluff. He calls."[2] On the 104% rate he argued that Trump is waging war on the CCP and the globalist system it represents, which had been pillaging both east and west for forty years, and distinguished the CCP from China, the Soviet Union from Russia, and the Deep State from the USA.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the levy in terms of supply chains built on forced labor: "if you like your slave labor, you can keep your slave labor — it's just going to cost you a 104% mark-up."[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] suggested the market swings reflected narrative reactions in "fake and manipulated markets" rather than fundamentals.[2]

## Market turbulence and the cooking oil embargo threat (October 2025)

By mid-October 2025 the dispute was driving day-to-day volatility in U.S. equity markets. On Tuesday, October 14, the S&P 500 swung from up 0.4% to down as much as 1.5% before closing 0.2% lower after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened China with a cooking oil embargo late in the session, retaliation for Beijing's refusal to buy U.S. soybeans.[3] The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.8% after being down 2.1% at its lows, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average bucked the trend to rise 0.4%, or 202.88 points, having been down as much as 1.3% that morning.[3] Stock futures were little changed on Tuesday night.[3]

The session followed a rapid escalation: on Monday night China imposed new sanctions on five U.S. subsidiaries of South Korean shipbuilder Hanwha Ocean, itself a response to Trump's threat the previous Friday to place an additional 100% tariff on all Chinese goods after Beijing imposed strict export controls on rare earth minerals.[3] U.S. Trade Representative [[jamieson-greer|Jamieson Greer]] told CNBC that the tariffs could take effect on November 1 or sooner depending on Beijing's next move, saying "a lot depends on what the Chinese do. They are the ones who have chosen to make this major escalation."[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the market turbulence not as the onset of an adversary-driven "reset" but as the visible working-out of the administration's own plan, singling out Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] — whom he dubbed "the Wolf of Main Street" — as the central figure of the financial theater of the conflict.[3]

## London talks (June 2025)

As US–China trade talks in London entered a second day in June 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] warned that it was "not easy" to negotiate with [[china|China]], while insisting he was receiving only "good reports" about progress between the teams of officials from Washington and Beijing.[4] "We are doing well with China. China's not easy," Trump told reporters at the White House. "I'm only getting good reports."[4] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] called it a "good meeting," and Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] described the negotiations as "fruitful."[4] Stocks were mixed in Asia overnight, with European markets expected to open higher amid hopes the talks would ease tensions between the world's two largest economies.[4]

## Tariff walk-back and Xi meeting (October 2025)

On October 17, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signalled that his latest tariff broadside against Beijing was more negotiating ploy than firm policy, telling Fox Business of his threatened 145% tariffs on Chinese goods: "It's not sustainable ... It could stand, but they forced me to do that."[5] He also dialled back a veiled threat, made the previous week in a Truth Social post, to cancel a meeting with Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] at a gathering of Asia-Pacific leaders later that month, saying during a White House meeting with Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] that "we'd like to meet."[5] The remarks soothed Wall Street, where fears of a new tit-for-tat trade war between Washington and Beijing had triggered sharp market swings.[5] [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] framed the episode as one throughline in Trump's approach to his biggest foreign policy challenges — "let's make a deal" — alongside his expected trip to Budapest to meet Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[5]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump and Xi are not waging economic war against one another but against the [[deep-state|Deep State]], and that Trump is copying a playbook written by Putin.[5] On that reading, the two leaders are using the weapons of the enemy — sanctions, tariffs and supply chain chokepoints — repurposed from globalist tools of control into instruments of liberation, in a process he called the [[good-reset|Good Reset]].[5]

## Bessent on a Trump–Xi call (June 2025)

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said on Sunday, June 1, 2025, that he was "confident" the U.S.–China trade dispute "will be ironed out" once [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] spoke directly.[6] Asked on CBS's "Face the Nation with Margaret Brennan" about a call between the two leaders, Bessent said, "I believe we'll see something very soon."[6]

The remarks followed Trump's statement the previous Friday that China was violating its trade agreement with the United States, an accusation he did not substantiate with specifics, and came weeks after the two governments agreed to a temporary easing of the tariffs imposed earlier in the year.[6] Trump said in April that he had spoken with his Chinese counterpart about tariffs, but Beijing denied that any direct conversation took place; although Trump had vowed since taking office in January to speak with Xi, no formal plans had been announced.[6]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] grouped the episode with Trump's claim that his stance on Israel and Iran could change with "a phone call" and with a planned Trump–Putin call, arguing that backchannels already exist and that "the phone calls are vehicles for disclosure."[6]
