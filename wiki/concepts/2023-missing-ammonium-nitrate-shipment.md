# 2023 Missing Ammonium Nitrate Shipment

The 2023 missing ammonium nitrate shipment refers to the disappearance of approximately 61,000 pounds — 30 tons — of ammonium nitrate from a sealed railcar traveling from Wyoming to California in April 2023.[1] Ammonium nitrate is a chemical used both as fertilizer and as an ingredient in explosives; it was used in the 1995 [[oklahoma-city-bombing|attack on the Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City]] that killed 168 people and injured approximately 850.[1]

## Timeline

On April 12, 2023, a railcar loaded with 30 tons of the chemical left Cheyenne, Wyoming.[1] According to an incident report from [[dyno-nobel|Dyno Nobel]], the shipper, when the car arrived two weeks later at a rail stop in the Mojave Desert — Saltdale, California — it was completely empty, though its seals were still intact.[1] Dyno Nobel filed a report with the federal [[national-response-center|National Response Center]] on May 10, 2023.[1]

## Investigation

The [[federal-railroad-administration|Federal Railroad Administration]], the [[california-public-utilities-commission|California Public Utilities Commission]], [[union-pacific|Union Pacific]] and Dyno Nobel all looked into the disappearance, and the railcar was transported back to Wyoming for a thorough inspection.[1] Dyno Nobel's initial assessment, given to KQED News, was that "a leak through the bottom gate on the railcar may have developed in transit."[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] laid out the facts — 30 tons packed and sealed in Cheyenne, arrival on time in Saltdale, the car sealed but totally empty — and rejected the leak theory as implausible for a full 61,000-pound load.[1] She observed that the car made multiple stops during its two-week journey, providing opportunity for foul play, and argued that if this was a heist rather than a leak, it would have been sophisticated and must have involved insiders at the commercial explosives company, while acknowledging the point as speculation.[1] Noting that Dyno Nobel had "a team of investigators investigating themselves," she predicted the episode would follow the [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine, Ohio]] pattern of vague, unsatisfying answers before being memory-holed — "unless the chemicals reappear. And I sincerely hope that never happens."[1]
