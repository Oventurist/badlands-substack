# Manifest Destiny

Manifest Destiny is the nineteenth-century American doctrine holding that the United States was destined to expand across the North American continent. In the Badlands Media corpus the doctrine is treated not as a single idea but as two competing traditions: an original republican conception of America as a continental beacon of liberty, and a later imperial perversion imposed, in the corpus's account, by an Anglophile oligarchy.

## Republican origins

In [[avoiding-wwiii|Avoiding WWIII]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] traces the earliest expression of Manifest Destiny to John Winthrop's "City on a Hill" speech of 1630, which framed America's identity as a role model of liberty for other nations to be inspired by. The idea was later amplified by George Washington and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], who explicitly insisted that America's first army and congress be styled "Continental" rather than the "congress of the 13 colonies" or "army of the 13 colonies" — a deliberate signal, in this reading, of a continental republican purpose rather than a colonial one. At the time Washington led the Continental Congress, the Americas were dominated by the hereditary imperial systems of France, England, Russia and Spain, which controlled everything outside the thirteen colonies.

Washington nonetheless warned that harmony with Natural Law did not make the young republic immune from becoming another evil empire, and cautioned against the dual evils of foreign entanglements abroad and party politics at home. John Quincy Adams extended the doctrine by drafting the Monroe Doctrine, which he held could work only if America went "not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy."

## The imperial perversion

The article argues that during the first 125 years of the doctrine's greatest influence — as the United States grew from thirteen colonies in 1776 to forty-five industrially advanced states by 1900 — the anti-slavery voices of Franklin, John Jay, John Quincy Adams, [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], Charles Sumner, William Seward and William Gilpin were repeatedly subverted by a [[deep-state|Deep State]] parasite class running both Wall Street and the southern slave power. Where the abolitionist tradition of Franklin and Alexander Hamilton encouraged the sharing of knowledge, technical skills and technological progress with blacks and natives without forced religious conversion, the rival tradition of [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]], Jefferson Davis and Albert Pike envisioned expanded slavery and Native Americans cordoned into plantations and reservations under a claim of white racial superiority.

The article contrasts the legitimate expansion pursued through diplomacy — the Louisiana Purchase of 1804, the Oregon Territory in 1848 and the Alaska Purchase of 1867 — with the wars of conquest favored by Wall Street and the Virginia slave power, including the Mexican War of 1846–48 and the 1893 overthrow of Hawaii's monarchy.

## A Eurasian Manifest Destiny

Ehret's central thesis is that the authentic, development-oriented form of Manifest Destiny has migrated to Eurasia. He describes a "Russian Manifest Destiny" aimed at extending civilization into Siberia, the Far East and the Arctic, and a Chinese counterpart in the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] launched in 2013. Both are presented as continuations of the Lincoln-era rail and industrialization model rather than as threats to it.
