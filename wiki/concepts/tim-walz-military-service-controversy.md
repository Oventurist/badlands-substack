# Tim Walz Military Service Controversy

The Tim Walz military service controversy refers to allegations, amplified immediately after [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] was named [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s running mate in August 2024, that he abandoned his National Guard unit ahead of a combat deployment to Iraq and misrepresented aspects of his rank and record.[1]

## Allegations

Walz spent 24 years as an enlisted soldier in the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] and is fond of describing himself as the highest-ranking enlisted soldier to serve in [[congress|Congress]], having retired as a command sergeant major.[1] Critics noted that he deployed once to Italy and once to Norway, but that when his battalion was slated to deploy in the Global War on Terror he retired instead.[1]

In a letter posted to Facebook in 2018, veterans from his unit said Walz retired from the National Guard after learning his battalion would be deployed to Iraq, despite having assured his troops he would join them.[1] Walz would have served as Battalion Command Sergeant Major, the highest-ranking non-commissioned officer and, by the account of his critics, the most important leader in the unit; according to sources within the unit, three of his soldiers died on that deployment.[1] He also left the military before completing the school required to become a Command Sergeant Major, though he continued to use the title in his biography on the Minnesota government website.[1]

## Framing by critics

A Newsweek opinion piece by a combat veteran argued that the essential facts the mainstream media would not report were that "Tim Walz abandoned his men before a combat deployment and he abandoned his city when it was literally on fire," characterizing him as a man who uses others to advance his own ambitions and discards them when inconvenient.[1] The second charge referred to criticism that Walz was slow to activate the National Guard during the Minneapolis riots that followed the 2020 murder of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] by police.[1]

Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] folded related themes into his own attacks on the pick, raising the 2020 Minnesota riots and suggesting that period marked the only time the two men interacted while he was in office.[1]

## Minnesota National Guard statement on rank

On August 8, 2024, the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] itself disputed Walz's military biography, saying his long-repeated claim to have retired at the rank of command sergeant major was inaccurate.[2] Guard spokeswoman Army Lieutenant Colonel Kristen Augé, the organization's State Public Affairs Officer, told [[just-the-news|Just the News]] that Walz was demoted and did not retire as a command sergeant major because he failed to complete a 750-hour course at the U.S. Army Sergeants Major Academy, a mandatory requirement for E-9s, the Army's highest enlisted rank.[2] While Walz temporarily held the title, Augé said, he "retired as a master sergeant in 2005 for benefit purposes because he did not complete additional coursework at the U.S. Army Sergeants Major Academy."[2]

The statement reignited a controversy dating to Walz's 2018 campaign for governor, when a National Guardsman claimed on social media and in a paid advertisement that Walz declined to deploy to Iraq for combat duty in 2005 and forfeited his title of command sergeant major, choosing instead to run for [[congress|Congress]] that year.[2] Walz's gubernatorial biography nonetheless continued to state that "Command Sergeant Major Walz" retired from the Guard in 2005, at a time when he was serving as one of the highest-ranking members of the 1-125th Field Artillery Battalion.[2] Coverage noted that even [[cnn|CNN]] characterized the claim that Walz saw combat as false.[2]

Badlands Media commentary from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed misrepresentation of military service as one of the cardinal offenses in American political culture, arguing that the episode was rarely crossed so brazenly and that neither Walz, [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], nor the media apparatus covering for them would survive the resulting scrutiny.[2]
