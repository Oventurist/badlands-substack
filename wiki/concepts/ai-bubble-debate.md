# AI Bubble Debate

The AI bubble debate concerns whether the concentration of US stock market gains in artificial intelligence companies represents a durable boom or an inflating speculative bubble.[1]

## Market concentration

By late October 2025, the performance of the stock market depended almost entirely on artificial intelligence and the companies producing it.[1] Those firms, concentrated in Silicon Valley, were spending hundreds of billions of dollars, and investors — treating the technology as the future — were snapping up shares.[1] By one estimate, 80 percent of US stock gains that year came from AI companies.[1]

## Warnings

Prominent figures including Jeff Bezos of Amazon, Sam Altman of OpenAI, Jamie Dimon of JPMorgan, and David Solomon of Goldman Sachs voiced concern that a correction was near, warning that valuations were becoming too high and that reality might eventually bite — with a plunge in those companies dragging down the wider economy.[1]

## Countervailing behavior

Despite the warnings, market behavior showed no sign of alarm: the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500]] notched more than 30 record highs during the year.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary reframed the risk away from a Skynet-style machine takeover, arguing instead that "the Machine had you" already through the guidance of entertainment behavior and perceptions of reputable sources, and that current developments are "decentralizing the dangers of AI" rather than eliminating them.[1]
