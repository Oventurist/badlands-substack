# Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System

The Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) is a federal passive surveillance program that accepts reports of possible vaccine-related injuries and deaths from patients, doctors, and pharmacists.[1] Because submissions are open to the public and to clinicians alike, VAERS entries are signals for further investigation rather than confirmed causal findings.

## Role in the 2025 pediatric COVID-19 death review

In September 2025, Trump administration health officials prepared to link coronavirus vaccines to the deaths of 25 children in a presentation to the [[advisory-committee-on-immunization-practices|CDC Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]], according to reporting by the Washington Post citing four people familiar with confidential discussions.[1] The underlying findings appeared to stem from reports filed to VAERS.[1] Separately, [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] staff had already presented data in June showing at least 25 child deaths tied to COVID-related hospitalizations since July 2023, a figure the agency said was likely undercounted.[1]

[[marty-makary|FDA Commissioner Marty Makary]] confirmed that officials were investigating reports of possible vaccine-related child deaths, reviewing autopsy results and interviewing families; the review was expected to take months and the analysis prepared for ACIP was not final.[1] The ACIP meeting was considered critical because the committee's recommendations shape insurance coverage, pharmacy access, and physician willingness to administer the shots.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the reported 25 deaths likely undercount harms from what she characterized as mass experimentation on American children without informed consent, alleging that minors were pressured to take the shots to play sports, join clubs, and access schooling without being told of potential effects on fertility or heart function, and invoked the Nuremberg Code.[1]
