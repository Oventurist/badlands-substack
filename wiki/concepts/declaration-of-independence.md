# Declaration of Independence

The **Declaration of Independence** is treated in Badlands Media commentary as the foundational statement of American political authority. In a May 16, 2023 essay by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], the document's preamble is invoked as the clearest available definition of "true American values," offered in answer to readers who might ask what that phrase means.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-startling-contradiction-at-the)

## Source of governmental authority

The essay's chain-of-authority argument rests on the Declaration: according to it, the authority of the government of America ultimately comes from God, descending through the people to the states and only then to the federal government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-startling-contradiction-at-the) The article points readers to a companion Badlands piece, "God and the Declaration of Independence," for a fuller treatment of that claim.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-startling-contradiction-at-the)

This premise does the decisive work in the essay's critique of the [[united-states-federal-corporation|corporate "United States"]]: because no entity can exceed the authority of that which created it, and because corporations cannot exist prior to nations or body politics, any corporate United States must remain subordinate to the older foundation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-startling-contradiction-at-the) "If you were to remove the foundation of law—being God and the people—then none of the rest of it could stand," the author writes, adding that the existence of linguistic "shell games" does not negate "the eternal truths upon which true law rests."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-startling-contradiction-at-the) The Declaration thus functions in the argument as both a historical citation and a standard against which the present legal order is measured.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-startling-contradiction-at-the)

## Full text republication (July 4, 2025)

On July 4, 2025, the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] suspended its usual news roundup and instead republished the complete transcript of the Declaration of Independence, linking to the National Archives founding-documents transcript and telling readers to "enjoy American Liberty and kick off America's 250th year in style."[2] The brief, which normally curates "the stories dominating the info war," announced it would return the following Monday.[2]

The republished text opens "In Congress, July 4, 1776" and is styled "The unanimous Declaration of the thirteen united States of America," beginning with the assertion that when it becomes necessary for one people to dissolve the political bands connecting them with another and to assume the separate and equal station to which "the Laws of Nature and of Nature's God" entitle them, a decent respect to the opinions of mankind requires that they declare the causes of separation.[2] It states as self-evident truths that all men are created equal and are endowed by their Creator with unalienable Rights including Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness, that governments are instituted among men to secure those rights and derive their just powers from the consent of the governed, and that whenever a form of government becomes destructive of those ends it is the right of the people to alter or abolish it.[2]

### Enumerated grievances

The document tempers that right with the caution that prudence dictates long-established governments should not be changed for "light and transient causes," and that mankind is more disposed to suffer while evils are sufferable; but a long train of abuses and usurpations evincing a design to reduce a people under absolute Despotism creates both a right and a duty to throw off such government.[2] It then submits facts to a candid world against the King of Great Britain, charging him with refusing assent to wholesome laws, forbidding his governors to pass laws of pressing importance, dissolving representative houses repeatedly, obstructing naturalization and the administration of justice, making judges dependent on his will, erecting a multitude of new offices, keeping standing armies in peacetime without legislative consent, and rendering the military superior to civil power.[2]

Further grievances address his combination with others to subject the colonies to a foreign jurisdiction: quartering armed troops, protecting them by mock trial from punishment for murders, cutting off trade, imposing taxes without consent, depriving colonists of trial by jury, transporting them overseas for pretended offences, abolishing English law in a neighbouring province, taking away charters, and suspending colonial legislatures.[2] The text further accuses the King of abdicating government by waging war, plundering seas and burning towns, transporting armies of foreign mercenaries, constraining captive citizens to bear arms against their country, and exciting domestic insurrections.[2]

### Appeal and declaration

The Declaration records that every stage of oppression was met with petitions for redress answered only by repeated injury, and concludes that "a Prince, whose character is thus marked by every act which may define a Tyrant, is unfit to be the ruler of a free people."[2] It notes appeals to "our Brittish brethren" through ties of common kindred that went unheeded, obliging the colonies to hold them "Enemies in War, in Peace Friends."[2] The representatives, assembled in General Congress and appealing to the Supreme Judge of the world for the rectitude of their intentions, declare the United Colonies free and independent states, absolved from allegiance to the British Crown and possessing full power to levy war, conclude peace, contract alliances and establish commerce, pledging to each other their Lives, Fortunes and sacred Honor with firm reliance on divine Providence.[2] The brief reproduced an image of the signers alongside the text.[2]
