# West Bloomfield Synagogue Attack

The West Bloomfield synagogue attack refers to a shooting at a Jewish community site in West Bloomfield, Michigan, in March 2026, which became the focal point of a public dispute over [[antisemitism|antisemitism]], online rhetoric, and speech regulation.[1]

## Greenblatt response

Following the shooting, [[jonathan-greenblatt|Jonathan Greenblatt]] of the [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]] discussed confronting antisemitism and defending Jewish communities on X, writing that "What happened yesterday didn't happen in a vacuum. West Bloomfield now can be added to the long list of Jewish communities who have experienced the terror and trauma of a synagogue attack."[1] He added that Jewish people are "too often accused of conspiracies or scapegoated for the world's problems" and said he was "sick of the hypocrisy we're hearing from politicians to podcasters, candidates and commentators," arguing that one cannot claim to abhor antisemitism while blaming Jewish people or the Jewish state for the conflict in the Middle East.[1]

## Criticism

Greenblatt's post drew criticism from social media users who argued that his rhetoric or framing could escalate tensions or worsen online hostility.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that in the immediate aftermath of the shooting Greenblatt took to the airwaves to blame podcasters and social media influencers for inciting violence against the Michigan Jewish school, and argued that if podcasters are to be held responsible for inciting strangers to violence, the same logic should apply to those whose provocative statements stoke antisemitism.[1] He framed the sequence as an instance of the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] aimed at justifying restrictions on the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]
