# Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act

The Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA) is the federal statute governing absentee registration and voting by US citizens living abroad and by members of the uniformed services.[1]

## Colorado dispute

In 2026 the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], Douglas County Clerk [[sheri-davis|Sheri Davis]], and Representative [[jeff-crank|Jeff Crank]] sued [[colorado|Colorado]] Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] over state guidance interpreting UOCAVA.[1] That guidance permits some citizens abroad to register in Colorado even if they have never lived in the state, provided a parent, guardian, spouse, or domestic partner previously resided there.[1]

The lawsuit argues that this interpretation violates Colorado's constitutional residency requirements, on the theory that residency cannot be inherited through family ties and that a person who has never personally lived in the state is not a resident.[1] The plaintiffs seek to block the policy and to remove affected voters from the rolls.[1]
