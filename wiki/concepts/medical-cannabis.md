# Medical Cannabis

**Medical cannabis** refers to the use of cannabis and cannabinoids such as CBD to treat medical conditions including chronic pain, anxiety and insomnia. Its popularity has grown substantially: a 2018 survey found that 27% of people in the United States and Canada reported using cannabis for issues such as pain relief, anxiety and sleep difficulties.[1]

## The 2025 UCLA review

A large review led by [[ucla-health|UCLA Health]] and published in [[jama|JAMA]] reported that medical cannabis does not have strong scientific evidence supporting most of the conditions for which it is commonly used.[1] The analysis examined more than 2,500 studies published between January 2010 and September 2025, including randomized clinical trials, meta-analyses and clinical guidelines, and gave special attention to more than 120 studies selected for their large sample sizes, recent publication dates, relevance and range of topics.[1]

Dr. [[michael-hsu|Michael Hsu]] of UCLA Health, the review's first author, said many people believe cannabis provides broad medical benefits even though the latest research often does not support those beliefs, and that the review "highlights significant gaps between public perception and scientific evidence regarding its effectiveness for most medical conditions."[1]
