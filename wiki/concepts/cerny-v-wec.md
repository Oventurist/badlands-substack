# Cerny v. WEC

Cerny v. WEC is a Wisconsin state case in which a judge ruled that the [[wisconsin-elections-commission|Wisconsin Elections Commission]] must verify United States citizenship for every voter registration and audit the state's entire voter roll.[1] The decision was issued on Friday, October 3, 2025, and was characterized as a major victory for election integrity advocates.[1]

## The ruling

The court issued a writ of mandamus directing the commission to comply with state law and the state constitution.[1] Key elements of the ruling were that the commission must verify U.S. citizenship for every new voter registration; that it must review current voter rolls, cross-checking records with the Department of Transportation and other lawful sources to identify non-citizen registrants; that registration requests cannot be accepted without proof of citizenship; and that progress on the voter roll audit must be reported to the court within 60 days.[1]

Judge Maxwell criticized the elections commission for having previously claimed it had no duty to prevent non-citizens from registering, citing both the United States Constitution and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]].[1]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] predicted that the majority on Wisconsin's Supreme Court would propel the case toward the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|United States Supreme Court]], and welcomed that prospect.[1]
