# Automatic Voter Registration

Automatic Voter Registration (AVR) is a policy under which eligible citizens are enrolled on the voter rolls automatically through interactions with state agencies, rather than by affirmatively opting in. In an April 10, 2025 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], election analyst [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] treated AVR as one of the structural "house advantages" that tilt modern American elections, comparing the electoral system to a casino whose mathematics favor the operator over the player.[1]

## Keshel's analysis

Keshel described AVR as "the hallmark of a blue state," noting that AVR states broke 18-2 for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in the 2020 presidential election, delivering 243 electoral votes to nine.[1] He further argued that in 2024, [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] carried only five electoral votes from jurisdictions that did not operate under AVR.[1] In his framework, AVR functions alongside universal or excessive [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]], [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]], drop boxes and, in states such as Alaska and Maine, [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice voting]], as a weighted advantage that his election forecasting model now explicitly factors into predictions.[1]

Keshel attributed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 performance in Pennsylvania in part to AVR not having had "enough time to fully sink in" in that state, arguing that the policy's benefits to establishment candidates accrue gradually — the electoral equivalent, in his metaphor, of casino loyalty programs.[1]

## Criticism of the framing

Keshel's treatment of AVR is an advocacy argument rather than a neutral description of the policy; conventional accounts hold that AVR primarily reduces registration errors and administrative burden. He acknowledged that his reading differs from the harder position of Badlands colleague [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], who regards elections as outright fake, stating that he believes campaigning, vote-getting and fortune still played a real if declining role in the 2024 race.[1] Keshel concluded that without "major, full-fledged reforms" — full transparency, uniform federal rules and serious consequences for violations — Americans are "doomed to quasi-elections."[1]

## Role in the corruption sequence

In a February 13, 2025 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay, [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] placed AVR first in his sequence of manual election corruption, arguing that [[voter-registration|voter registration]] becomes corrupted most quickly once a state adopts it, because AVR reduces the risk of getting caught performing registration fraud — deliberate fraudulent registration being less crucial at that point.[2] He listed AVR among his [[eight-cardinal-sins|Eight Cardinal Sins]] of election administration, and argued that blue-wall states relying increasingly on AVR alongside expanding [[mail-in-voting|mail-in balloting]] offer the most achievable target for demonstrating voter-roll corruption to the public.[2]
