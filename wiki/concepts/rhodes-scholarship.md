# Rhodes Scholarship

The Rhodes Scholarship program was begun in 1902, following the will of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]], in order to advance a re-organized British Empire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) It has worked alongside the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] for over a century and has produced more than 7,000 scholars who have permeated across all fields of society, including media, education, government, military and corporate life.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

In [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, the conditioning received at Oxford defines the function its graduates perform afterward: [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]]'s role was described as doing "what every good Rhodes Scholar is conditioned to do upon their completion of their indoctrination at Oxford" — facilitating the transition of the pre-collapse world economy into a new "green post-collapse" operating system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Other figures named in the same tradition include [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], who managed Freeland's entry into politics, and Strobe Talbott, who participated in the Perestroika-era liberalization of the former Soviet bloc.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

Rhodes's 1877 will described the desired society as one with members in every part of the Empire, tested for endurance and eloquence, "elected and bound by oath to serve for the rest of his life," and "sent to that part of the Empire where it was felt he was needed."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Badlands characterization

Badlands commentary in June 2024 described the Rhodes Scholarship as an iconic moniker with instant cultural recognition, insinuating extreme intelligence and philanthropic sensibilities, while arguing that in reality the program serves to identify and cultivate talent for what the writer called the globalist mafia.[2] Bill Clinton and Pete Buttigieg were cited as examples of alumni.[2] The scholarship was discussed in the context of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]]'s colonial role in what became [[zimbabwe|Zimbabwe]].[2]

## Canadian pipeline into Washington

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that after the founding of the [[round-table-movement|Round Table]] in 1902, it was [[canada|Canada]] that incubated hundreds of Rhodes Scholars between 1907 and 1952, who then infiltrated the United States.[3] American journalist Eugene Griffin exposed the pattern in the 1951 *Chicago Tribune* article "Canada Offers Fine Field to Rhodes' Wards," one of sixteen articles published between July 15 and 31 of that year.[3]

Griffin wrote that "Scholars and other British educated Canadians are in a unique position to serve Britain through Canada's influence on Washington as a next door neighbor of the United States," and reported that the foreign office of [[lester-b-pearson|Lester B. Pearson]] contained 23 Rhodes Scholars among 183 staff officers — one in every eight.[3] He named Norman A. Robertson, a Rhodes Scholar of Balliol (1923) and Clerk of the Privy Council, as one of the most important figures in Canada's relations with Britain and the United States.[3] Griffin also recorded that when General MacArthur displeased Britain and Canada by his efforts to win the Korean War, Pearson complained that American-Canadian relations had become "difficult and delicate," and MacArthur was fired the next day.[3]

## The Fulton exposés and Cold War policy

A parallel 1951 *Chicago Tribune* series by journalist [[william-fulton|William Fulton]] documented what Ehret calls the cancerous penetration of hundreds of Oxford-trained Rhodes Scholars who had taken over American foreign policy and were directing the country toward a third world war.[4] Writing on July 14, 1951, Fulton reported that "key positions in the United States department of state are held by a network of American Rhodes scholars," educated and indoctrinated at Oxford with the bills paid by the estate of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil John Rhodes]], who had written of his ambition to cause "the ultimate recovery of the United States of America as an integral part of the British empire" and aimed at a world federation dominated by Anglo-Saxons.[4]

Ehret argues it is no exaggeration to say that many important Cold War events involved the top-down role of Rhodes Scholars, who occupied critical positions in the State Departments of the USA and Canada, the UN bureaucracy and the foundations, and operated with a discipline, enthusiasm and coherence unseen in any modern imperial civil service.[4] He traces this influence from Escott Reid's designs for [[nato|NATO]] two full years before the organization was brought online, to George McGhee's advance of the [[truman-doctrine|Truman Doctrine]], to [[dean-rusk|Dean Rusk]]'s guiding hand behind the Korean War and US support for the French in Vietnam and later American involvement there, to Senator J. William Fulbright's promotion of globalism and a European Union.[4] Wherever a bad idea was born in the postwar years, he writes, there is almost invariably a Rhodes Scholar or "Rhodie" behind it.[4] [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] entered this network as a star pupil of William Yandall Elliot, a leading Rhodes Scholar based at Harvard.[4]
