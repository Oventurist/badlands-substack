# Rhodes Scholarship

The Rhodes Scholarship program was begun in 1902, following the will of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]], in order to advance a re-organized British Empire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) It has worked alongside the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] for over a century and has produced more than 7,000 scholars who have permeated across all fields of society, including media, education, government, military and corporate life.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

In [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, the conditioning received at Oxford defines the function its graduates perform afterward: [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]]'s role was described as doing "what every good Rhodes Scholar is conditioned to do upon their completion of their indoctrination at Oxford" — facilitating the transition of the pre-collapse world economy into a new "green post-collapse" operating system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Other figures named in the same tradition include [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], who managed Freeland's entry into politics, and Strobe Talbott, who participated in the Perestroika-era liberalization of the former Soviet bloc.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

Rhodes's 1877 will described the desired society as one with members in every part of the Empire, tested for endurance and eloquence, "elected and bound by oath to serve for the rest of his life," and "sent to that part of the Empire where it was felt he was needed."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Badlands characterization

Badlands commentary in June 2024 described the Rhodes Scholarship as an iconic moniker with instant cultural recognition, insinuating extreme intelligence and philanthropic sensibilities, while arguing that in reality the program serves to identify and cultivate talent for what the writer called the globalist mafia.[2] Bill Clinton and Pete Buttigieg were cited as examples of alumni.[2] The scholarship was discussed in the context of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]]'s colonial role in what became [[zimbabwe|Zimbabwe]].[2]
