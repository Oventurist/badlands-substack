# Federalist Papers

The Federalist Papers are the series of essays written in support of ratifying the [[united-states-constitution|United States Constitution]].[1] The September 2, 2024 Badlands News Brief singled out Federalist No. 67, "The Executive Department," by [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]].[1]

## Federalist No. 67

Hamilton's argument in No. 67, as summarized in the brief, was that the new federal government could not become a monarchy or a dictatorship because power would be decentralized within the Executive Branch by appointing unlimited bureaucrats who would individually possess only a portion of power, so that no single person or agency alone wielded absolute control.[1] The Badlands writer rejected this reasoning outright, holding that the arrangement instead produced an unaccountable bureaucracy and a novel species of tyranny.[1]

## Anti-Federalist rebuttal

[[patrick-henry|Patrick Henry]] was cited as having warned at the time that the paper was an abomination, a judgment the brief endorsed emphatically.[1] The broader Anti-Federalist objection that the Constitution addressed only the rights of government led [[james-madison|James Madison]] to draft the [[bill-of-rights|Bill of Rights]], ratified in 1791.[1] The brief called for reviving the Federalist/Anti-Federalist debate, a topic it noted had been discussed at the GART gathering in Deadwood.[1]
