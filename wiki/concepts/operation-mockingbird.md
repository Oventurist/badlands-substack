# Operation Mockingbird

**Operation Mockingbird** was the [[cia|CIA]] program under which the agency placed assets inside media companies in order to shape American news coverage.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Disclosure

Its existence was revealed in 1975 when CIA Director William Colby testified under oath before the [[church-committee|Church Committee]], the congressional committee led by Frank Church. The committee established that the CIA had manipulated and controlled American media since at least 1960.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Legacy argument

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that before the 1990s controlling the media was straightforward — the CIA only had to control Hollywood, the music industry, television, radio, books, magazines, newspapers and broadcast news. The arrival of the internet, described as "the Wild West," broke that control at a moment when anti-government sentiment was rising, and by 2000 some 52% of Americans had internet access.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Carlson contends the CIA's solution was to create and control social media platforms and search engines so it could monitor what was said online and govern what people could find.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)


## Invoked in coverage of Syria

Badlands Media cited Operation Mockingbird, alongside the [[cia|CIA]]'s "Mighty Wurlitzer," as the origin point of the U.S. security state's transformation of the media into what it called "the ultimate psychological conditioning engine."[2] The article argued that this apparatus was refined especially after the United States emerged as the "police of the world" in pursuit of [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]], and that the same tactics used to sell the wars in Iraq, Iran, Libya, Egypt, and North Vietnam were deployed in reporting on the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[2]

## Esler's "ideological" successor thesis (2024)

Commenting in February 2024 on [[adam-rubenstein|Adam Rubenstein]]'s account of bias at [[new-york-times|The New York Times]], Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the transformation of the news media through what he called Neo-Marxist brainwashing represents the evolution of Operation Mockingbird into a much more dangerous form.[3] In its original form, he wrote, the program inserted agents into news and media organizations as conscious operators in a defined warfare operation and captured friendly assets willing to engage in contract work.[3] What replaced it, in his reading, is the result of generations of communist ideological subversion solidifying within the workforce: journalists who serve the same deep state that ran Mockingbird but act from ideology rather than orders, including a growing portion who are "fundamentally and philosophically against objective reality."[3]

## Origins, exposure and denial

In the "four pillars" framework of [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], Operation Mockingbird — launched by the CIA in the 1960s to fund media members who would promote agency talking points — marks the defining turning point in the capture of the mainstream media, the last of the four systems by which Americans are controlled.[4] Quoting Wikipedia, Carlson describes it as an alleged large-scale CIA program begun in the early years of the Cold War that attempted to manipulate domestic American news media organizations for propaganda purposes, recruiting leading American journalists into a propaganda network and influencing the operations of front groups; CIA support of such front groups was exposed when an April 1967 Ramparts article reported that the National Student Association received agency funding.[4]

The public unveiling came in stages. According to a December 1977 New York Times article, the closer-than-expected relationship between the CIA and the press was first made public in 1973, when CIA Director [[william-colby|William E. Colby]] shared some details of the practice with reporters and the Washington Star publicized the issue, leading to congressional investigations.[4] Four years later [[carl-bernstein|Carl Bernstein]] expanded on the relationship in a [[rolling-stone|Rolling Stone]] article, "The CIA and the Media," claiming that for 25 years some 400 American journalists had "secretly carried out assignments for the Central Intelligence Agency," involving organizations including The New York Times, CBS and Time Inc.[4] Bernstein's reporting focused mostly on journalists working abroad, but rumors soon emerged that the CIA was also using reporters to spread propaganda at home; by 1979 the alleged program had a name.[4] Facts about the operation remain murky — including whether it ever ended.[4]

Carlson notes the irony that Bernstein worked for the [[washington-post|Washington Post]], considered by some one of the outlets the CIA controlled at the time and still today, and argues the exposure came only after the cat was out of the bag, serving to lend the media borrowed credibility.[4] A mockingbird, he observes, mimics what it hears — as mainstream media personalities report what they are told to report.[4]

Despite the Church Committee's 1975 findings, the media still frames Operation Mockingbird as conspiracy theory: when [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] alleged in June 2024 that the program had secretly recruited journalists to help brainwash Americans, [[abc-news|ABC News]] called the claim "bonkers" and unsubstantiated, despite the acting CIA director having made the same claims under oath to Congress in 1975.[4] Carlson holds that CIA control of the media persists and has expanded, citing [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2012 repeal of the [[smith-mundt-act|Smith-Mundt Act]] as permitting the government to propagandize its citizens without repercussion, and notes that only 31% of Americans still say they trust the media.[4]

## Claimed pre-CIA origins

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] argues that Operation Mockingbird did not begin with the CIA, since the same media existed well before the Agency and was used for the same purpose.[5] She poses the question of when media deception actually started, and how much of accepted history is therefore a lie, as a necessary preliminary to understanding [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[5]
