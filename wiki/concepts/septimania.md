# Septimania

Septimania was a large territory in southern Gaul, hosting the strategic port city of Narbonne, which the Umayyad Caliphate in Spain lost in 751 to the new Carolingian Dynasty of [[pepin-the-short|Pepin the Short]].[1] Matthew Ehret describes it as Europe's key entry point for [[silk-road|Silk Road]] goods, making its survival essential to what he calls the humanist cause.[1]

## Tri-confessional governance

Septimania had a large Jewish and Muslim population, which Pepin and his son [[charlemagne|Charlemagne]] allied with against the intrigues of Venice rather than setting the communities against one another.[1] Pepin summoned Natronai al Makhir (725–765), a Jewish leader from Baghdad descended from the House of David, to become king of Septimania, and married his daughter Alda to him; al Makhir gave his own daughter to Charlemagne in marriage.[1] Charlemagne later divided the governance of Septimania into thirds — one under Archbishop Thomas of Normandy, one under an Islamic Viscount, and one under Jewish governance — with the article noting the irony of a formerly Muslim territory placed under Jewish and Christian protection.[1]

## Legacy and collapse

The region later became a leading renaissance zone, reviving the study of Greek classics, astronomy, poetry and medicine under the Andalusian Renaissance centuries afterward.[1] The ecumenical alliance centered on Septimania lasted some ninety years before collapsing amid the intrigues of Venice, which induced Charlemagne's grandchildren into civil war and broke the Carolingian Empire apart through the 842 Oath of Strasbourg into regions that later formed the borders of modern Europe.[1]
