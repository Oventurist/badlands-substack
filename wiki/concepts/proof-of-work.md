# Proof-of-Work

Proof-of-work (POW) is the original blockchain consensus mechanism, used by [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]], in which competitive miners — supercomputers designed to solve very complex mathematical problems — confirm transactions and add new blocks to the chain.[1] It contrasts with [[proof-of-stake]], which selects validators at random weighted by staked holdings.[1]

## Criticisms

The two biggest criticisms of proof-of-work chains are transaction speed and environmental impact.[1] Bitcoin transactions are notoriously slow, and according to Bankrate, "Bitcoin mining consumes so much electricity that it accounts for 0.40 percent of the entire world's electricity consumption as of July 2022."[1] Bitcoin's carbon footprint has been estimated at 22.9 million metric tons per year; if Bitcoin were a country it would rank among the top 30 energy users worldwide by widely accepted climate metrics — a framing the Badlands article treated with open sarcasm.[1]

## Decentralization advantage

Despite these criticisms, proponents interviewed for the article argued that proof-of-work preserves genuine decentralization in a way proof-of-stake does not, a view summarized in the refrain repeated by both [[gmoney|GMoney]] and [[ryan-matta|Ryan Matta]]: "Bitcoin, not crypto."[1]

## Theological reading

The October 2024 essay "Revolution Founded at the Crossroads" treats Proof of Work as more than a consensus mechanism, calling it the first effective mitigation humanity has ever had against the centralized capture of money and property.[2] The author argues that because "Cantillionaires" uncover asymmetric advantages in physical power projection by exploiting money- and property-related trust, every prior form of money — seashells, glass beads, silver, gold, paper currencies — and every property class eventually falls under centralized control; Proof of Work protocols like [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] are presented as the structural answer.[2]

The essay extends the metaphor to Christian theology, framing the life, crucifixion, and resurrection of [[jesus]] as the Proof of Work required to mint humanity's next block — the New Covenant — paying the debt in full on the ledger of the Old Covenant law.[2] [[satoshi-nakamoto|Satoshi Nakamoto]] is described in parallel terms as doing the Proof of Work required to mint the Genesis block.[2] The author also urges readers to "put the proof of work in to learn these tools," treating personal study as its own costly, verifiable effort.[2]
