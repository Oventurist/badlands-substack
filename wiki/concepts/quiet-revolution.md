# Quiet Revolution

The Quiet Revolution was the transformation of Quebec society during the 1960s. Badlands Media contributor Matthew Ehret characterizes it as a period that "gutted Christianity from Quebec" and replaced it with a new secular order built on systems control and a new technocratic elite — an elite sometimes bearing French names but, in his account, always loyal to British imperial influence.[1]

Ehret identifies Quebec Rhodes Scholars as central agents of this transformation, naming Jean Beetz and Paul Gérin-Lajoie, the creator of the Quebec Ministry of Education.[1] Both worked closely with [[pierre-trudeau|Pierre Elliott Trudeau]] at the Université Laval, where they oversaw the restructuring of Quebec governance and culture.[1]

The episode is presented as one stage in a longer diffusion of cybernetic and data-feedback governance ideas through Canadian political life across the 20th century, extending from [[technocracy-inc|Technocracy Inc.]] and the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] networks of the 1930s through to Trudeau's later cybernetic restructuring of the federal government.[1]
