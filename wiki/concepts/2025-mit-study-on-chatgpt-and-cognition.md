# 2025 MIT Study on ChatGPT and Cognition

The 2025 MIT study on ChatGPT and cognition was a preprint from researchers at the [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|Massachusetts Institute of Technology]] Media Lab that used EEG brain scans to compare the neural activity of people writing with [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] against those writing without it.[1] The paper, awaiting peer review at the time of reporting, was covered by Futurism and circulated widely as evidence that AI is having a serious and poorly understood effect on users' cognition even as adoption explodes worldwide.[1]

## Findings

The study documented what reporters called vast differences between the groups.[1] The ChatGPT group "consistently underperformed at neural, linguistic, and behavioral levels" and grew progressively lazier with each successive essay, with EEG readings showing "weaker neural connectivity and under-engagement of alpha and beta networks."[1] A Google-assisted group showed moderate neural engagement, while the "brain-only" group exhibited the strongest cognitive metrics throughout.[1] Coverage noted the results were novel but not entirely surprising given prior studies and anecdotes about how AI chatbot use appears to affect people's brains and minds.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] argued the findings only sound like doom at first glance and in fact confirm a long-standing Badlands position: "Ignore AI and it will ruin you; go all in into AI and it will ruin you."[1] The item ran in a brief whose title dubbed the phenomenon "MoronGPT."[1]
