# Days of Thunder

"Days of Thunder" is a phrase popularized by [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] to describe the opening period of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second presidential term, beginning with his January 2025 inauguration, during which the administration set about dismantling both the deep and administrative states.[1] The term supplied the title of a February 20, 2025 progress report by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], which surveyed the administration's first month and deliberately incorporated criticism from outside the pro-Trump commentariat.[1]

## Approval ratings

Despite sustained alarm from left-wing pundits, Trump's second term opened with a net approval rating stronger than at the outset of his first.[1] In 2020, during the initial stages of the coronavirus pandemic, Trump's approval reached 49% in some polls, considered the high point of his first term.[1] On February 9, 2025, [[cbs-news|CBS News]] published polling showing his popularity at an all-time high, a result even CNN acknowledged.[1] As of that date his net approval was up nearly ten points from the equivalent point in 2017 across the CBS, Ipsos, Pew, and Gallup polls, and he had been in positive territory for twenty consecutive days, compared with only eleven days in positive territory in total during his first term.[1] Arguments can be made about the accuracy and integrity of polling in both periods, but the optics suggested majority support for the administration's radical changes.[1]

## The USAID reckoning

The most conspicuous target of the period was [[usaid|USAID]], described in the report as a bloated agency finally being seen for what its critics claim it is.[1] Born in 1961 under [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], the agency was presented publicly as America's outstretched hand to the world, but critics characterize it as a cut-out and soft-power arm of empire — a slush fund for [[deep-state|deep state]] projects, color revolutions, and "democracy promotion."[1] [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] described it in lectures and interviews that month as "an angel that's lost its way."[1]

Revelations catalogued during the month included USAID's financial support for NGOs with contentious agendas, notably [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] and groups linked to the George Floyd riots, routed through the [[tides-foundation|Tides Foundation]].[1] The agency was also identified as a major funder of [[internews|Internews]], an intelligence-linked press-freedom and journalist-training organization, to the tune of roughly $473 million, and as the author of the [[usaid-disinformation-primer|Disinformation Primer]], an internal document endorsing censorship tactics.[1] USAID was further linked to funding groups that ran smear campaigns against anti-establishment American journalists and politicians including [[max-blumenthal|Max Blumenthal]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]].[1] Finally, through [[ecohealth-alliance|EcoHealth Alliance]], USAID was said to have indirectly funded [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]] at the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]].[1]

Benz cautioned that the dismantling carried civilizational risk, warning that "we are conducting open heart surgery on the vital organs of the American empire itself, and I'm not fully sure that the people who are conducting that open heart surgery understand the anatomy of the organs they are operating on."[1]

## The border

Since taking office in January 2025, Trump issued a torrent of executive actions reshaping border and immigration policy.[1] His first move was to declare a national emergency at the southern border, enabling the deployment of military personnel and framing immigration in terms of national security and invasion.[1] That framing justified a sweeping crackdown including the immediate shutdown of the [[cbp-one|CBP One app]], closing a legal pathway for asylum seekers.[1] Designating cartels as foreign terrorist organizations was read as addressing informational as well as physical threats, tying border policy to the administration's campaign against the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]].[1]

The reinstatement of [[remain-in-mexico|Remain in Mexico]], plans for mass deportations, and moves to end [[birthright-citizenship|birthright citizenship]] for children of undocumented immigrants were described as having already changed the threat picture.[1] DeLarme predicted that when border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] shifted from deporting criminals to deporting ordinary illegal residents, Democratic operatives would respond with "deportation porn" — emotionally manipulative media of crying children separated from parents — likely coinciding with organized riots in major cities.[1]

## The Department of Education

The [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] was described as being on the brink of closure, with Elon Musk's [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] at the helm of the effort.[1] The report framed the closure not merely as bureaucratic reduction or devolution to the states but as a strike against an entrenched globalist narrative that had used education for ideological dissemination, particularly around climate change, social justice, and global citizenship.[1] A "Benzian" caveat followed: fragmenting the educational landscape could make it more susceptible to localized ideological battles, producing a patchwork of mutually hostile systems and opening new fronts in the culture war rather than unifying national identity.[1]

## The Bad

The report's second half presented unfavorable views. The largest point of contention within the MAGA movement was Trump's stated plans for the [[gaza-relocation-proposal-2025|Gaza Strip]], which the same CBS poll indicated was his least popular move.[1] The idea of "owning" Gaza and transforming it into a "Riviera of the Middle East" was characterized as certain to provoke fury, and raised the question of whether it amounted to U.S. imperialism masquerading as benevolence.[1] DeLarme expressed hope that it formed part of a plan culminating in peace and justice for civilians, potentially through revelations about the roles of the [[cia|CIA]], [[mossad|Mossad]], the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], USAID, and deep state coalitions in fostering the conditions that preceded October 7 and in invoking it to justify subsequent military operations.[1]

Criticism also came from the anti-Trump, anti-establishment faction, abbreviated ATAEF, exemplified by [[michael-tracey|Michael Tracey]] and [[whitney-webb|Whitney Webb]].[1] These commentators argued that many of Trump's appointments and policies reinforced rather than dismantled entrenched structures, pointing to figures such as [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] and to [[blackrock|BlackRock]]'s policy influence, and raising surveillance fears about Thiel's [[palantir|Palantir Technologies]] and its contracts with [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and the Department of Defense.[1]

The piece closed by describing an America where the line between policy and performance art blurs, with the old order dying and a new one being born in blood and fire.[1]
