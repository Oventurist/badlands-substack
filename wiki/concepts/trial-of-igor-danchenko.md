# Trial of Igor Danchenko

The trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] was a federal criminal proceeding brought by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] in the Eastern District of Virginia in October 2022, charging Danchenko with false statements under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 arising from his work as the primary sub-source for the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]] and as an FBI confidential human source.[1] Judge [[anthony-trenga|Anthony Trenga]] presided.[1]

## Day two

The second day of trial was dominated, as the first had been, by the continued examination of FBI Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]], prompting the article's summary judgment that "Danchenko is in the courtroom, but the FBI is on trial."[2] Before the jury was brought in, counsel argued over emails and messages the defense wished to admit but which could realistically only come in if Danchenko testified; Judge [[anthony-trenga|Anthony Trenga]] framed the issue as "whether the present sense exception for forward-looking information as an exception to the hearsay rule trumps the rule against admissibility of exculpatory statements by a defendant absent his testifying."[2] It also emerged in these preliminaries that [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]], though willing to speak with the Special Counsel, would not travel to the United States to testify out of fear for his family's safety, and that [[john-durham|John Durham]] did not know where Millian actually was.[2]

On direct, Durham resumed questioning Auten about the three days of January 2017 interviews of Danchenko, using Exhibit 100 to refresh his memory.[2] Danchenko had first described two emails followed by a phone call, then the next day recast the sequence as one email, a phone call, and a second email much later in September — inconsistencies the article argued should have been glaring to investigators but which were never followed up.[2] Danchenko had produced a single Russian-language email concerning Millian; other emails existed which he never volunteered and which neither the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team nor the Mueller Special Counsel obtained from either man, and of which Auten first learned while preparing for trial.[2] Durham had those emails read into the record, including Danchenko's July 21, 2016 message to Millian, Millian's July 26, 2016 forwarding of it to [[dmitri-zlodorev|Dmitri Zlodorev]], Zlodorev's reply, an August 18, 2016 email, and August 24–25, 2016 Russian-language messages between Danchenko and Zlodorev containing the line "Sergei doesn't respond."[2] Millian never answered Danchenko's email, and the FBI never established whether Millian was even in the United States at the time of the supposed New York meeting.[2] Durham's repeated point was that the only means of contact Danchenko ever gave Millian were an email address and a telephone number — laying the groundwork against the anticipated defense theory that Millian had reached Danchenko by some other application.[2]

Durham then turned to [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]], establishing that Dolan's name had surfaced with [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] in October 2016 and that the FBI had "developed some information" on him between then and January 2017, yet nobody raised Dolan during the three days of Danchenko interviews even though Danchenko's immunity agreement obliged him to disclose such information.[2] Danchenko had described the June 2016 Moscow conference while omitting Dolan's name.[2] A binder of [[kglobal|KGlobal]] emails and business records followed, including an August 19, 2016 exchange between Danchenko and Dolan; the record the defense most wanted excluded was one in which Dolan speculated Danchenko might be KGB or a Russian agent.[2] The article's synthesis was that Dolan, a decades-long Democratic operative and Clinton associate, and Danchenko were working together to gather material against Donald Trump for use in Steele's dossier, and that the FBI never did the simple investigative work to discover it.[2] Durham closed his direct by introducing [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]], though he could not press the subject far because Auten, while he had travelled to Cyprus to interview her, was not present when Danchenko was questioned about her.[2]

Cross-examination by defense counsel [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] began with Auten's four interviews with the Department of Justice Inspector General, one lasting some twenty hours, and proceeded to a sustained attempt to show that Millian communicated through applications — Facebook, LinkedIn, Skype and FaceTime — whose traffic would not appear on phone records.[2] Onorato also elicited that the FBI could have sought Danchenko's emails and phone but did not, that Auten and Special Agent Somma had further questions but assumed later opportunities, and that the first meetings were about "breaking the ice."[2] Discussing the FBI's offer of one million dollars to Steele for corroboration, the article argued the offer was a defensible tactic: had Steele accepted he would necessarily have surrendered his sources, exposing Galkina, Danchenko and Dolan in early October 2016, before the FISA on [[carter-page|Carter Page]]; by refusing, Steele protected the network and undermined his own dossier's credibility.[2] Auten also confirmed he had not been subpoenaed by Durham to appear.[2]

On redirect Durham pressed Auten on what steps Crossfire Hurricane had taken regarding the records he had never seen, invoked Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]]'s report on Crossfire Hurricane, revisited the Papadopoulos material, clarified that the million-dollar offer was for any corroborating information rather than merely for a source, and noted that Danchenko had offered to "look back on phone" for evidence of the call but never provided anything.[2] Auten could not say whether investigators ever pulled Millian's phone records for the number he gave [[george-papadopoulos|George Papadopoulos]] on July 15, 2016, or his travel records.[2] Durham finished with Exhibit 207T, an August 18, 2016 email; the May 2016 Zlodorev email mentioning only phone and email; and a July 18, 2016 email from Danchenko to [[cenk-sidar|Cenk Sidar]] of Sidar Global Advisors.[2]

## Day four

The final day of testimony was, in the Badlands account, another strong day for the prosecution, in which the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] and [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller]] teams were unflattered while the investigative work of one FBI analyst and two FBI agents featured heavily.[1] Durham, Assistant Special Counsel Michael Keilty, and DOJ Trial Attorney Brittain Shaw concluded the government's case in chief.[1]

Durham first recalled Special Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] for further redirect; Helson testified that [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]]'s name had never come up in his early discussions with Danchenko, and that questions about Dolan supplied by Special Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] and posed on June 15, 2017 produced nothing.[1] Former FBI analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]], by then leading a global ethics team at [[nasdaq|NASDAQ]], described compiling a case file on [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]] that also contained her Dolan material, and serializing it in three places — the Washington Field Office, FBI Headquarters, and the Inspector General — even after the Special Counsel's Office instructed the team to take no further action on the Dolan–Danchenko relationship.[1] Anderson then testified about her assignment to "validate the Steele Dossier" and her memorandum urging investigative steps on Dolan, with hearsay portions of her answers struck.[1] The final witness, Supervisory Special Agent [[ryan-james|Ryan James]], introduced Amtrak, travel, phone, and Facebook records, including evidence that [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] was outside the United States from July 14 to July 27, 2016 and that no call in Danchenko's records matched the alleged ten-to-fifteen-minute anonymous call.[1]

## Verdict

After the lunch break the defense declined to cross examine James, and Durham rested.[1] The defense then rested without presenting a case in chief at all.[1] That Friday afternoon Trenga dismissed Count One of the indictment; Counts Two through Five went to the jury the following Monday, and by Tuesday evening the jury returned a not guilty verdict on all remaining counts.[1]

## Interpretation

The article's recurring question — "Was Danchenko ever really on trial?" — reflects its central thesis that the proceeding functioned less as a prosecution of Danchenko than as a public airing of [[fbi|FBI]] misconduct, with the Bureau, the Steele Dossier, and Dolan effectively in the dock.[1] Among the outcomes the author counted: the dossier's allegations were treated as discredited on the record, since Dolan admitted fabricating his contribution, Danchenko never met the manager of the Ritz-Carlton, and neither Danchenko over three and a half years as a source nor [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] himself — even offered $1,000,000 — could corroborate any element of it, which the author described as full exoneration for Donald Trump.[1] The FBI later turned Danchenko back against Steele to gather intelligence on him.[1] Dolan was referred for investigation on the strength of Danchenko's information, his email, phone and social media records were obtained by search warrant, and the matter was transferred to the [[durham-special-counsel-investigation|Durham Special Counsel]].[1] Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] endured two days of hostile questioning, is under Inspector General investigation, and has been recommended for suspension over his role in the [[carter-page|Carter Page]] FISA applications; Helson is likewise an OIG subject.[1]

The author compared the case to the prosecutions of [[kevin-clinesmith|Kevin Clinesmith]] and [[michael-sussmann|Michael Sussmann]], arguing that the durable value of each lay not in sentences but in the evidentiary record: the Sussmann trial destroyed the [[alfa-bank-hoax|Alfa Bank Hoax]], put Marc Elias, Robby Mook and FBI agents before a grand jury and on the stand, exposed Rodney Joffe and the researchers, and breached the attorney–client privilege wall.[1] The Danchenko case, in this reading, established the "real Russian collusion" among [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], the Democrats, their PR firms, Steele, Danchenko and Dolan, and supplied Durham with interviews, testimony and records for future prosecutions higher up the chain of the alleged [[spygate|Spygate]] conspiracy and the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1]
