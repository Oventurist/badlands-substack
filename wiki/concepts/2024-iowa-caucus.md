# 2024 Iowa Caucus

The 2024 Iowa Caucus, held on January 15, 2024, was the opening contest of the Republican presidential primary season and the first hard data set of the 2024 election cycle.[1] The Associated Press declared [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] the winner, with Trump defeating [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] by roughly 30 and 40 points respectively — a margin closely in line with the final NBC News/Des Moines Register/Mediacom Iowa poll.[1]

## Conditions and turnout

The caucuses took place amid extreme cold, with a "feels like" temperature of roughly 35 degrees below zero at the time the event got underway.[1] Despite the weather, images from caucus sites across the state circulated in the media and on X showing packed rooms.[1] Commentators nonetheless noted that the conditions may have suppressed turnout, observing that Trump's share stood at about 51 percent as of 9 p.m. Eastern.[1]

Ahead of the vote, DeSantis-aligned influencer Bill Mitchell drew accusations of attempted voter suppression after implying that elderly Trump supporters should skip the caucuses because of the cold and because Trump was already assured of victory; Mitchell subsequently deleted the post.[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted this with the seven-month sentence imposed on [[douglass-mackey|Douglass Mackey]] for conduct she characterized as less serious.[1]

## Results and aftermath

Media outlets called the race early for Trump.[1] The contest for second place was initially a dead heat between Haley and DeSantis, with Haley slightly ahead before DeSantis pulled ahead over the course of the evening.[1] Both candidates nonetheless secured four delegates apiece, effectively tying, and commentary noted that a hypothetical consolidated DeSantis–Haley ticket would still have lost Iowa to Trump by thousands of votes.[1]

On the same day, Bloomberg reported that [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] was ending his 2024 campaign and endorsing Trump; Trump credited Ramaswamy with having done a "hell of a job."[1]

Subsequent reporting placed Trump's total at more than 56,000 votes, more than double the tally of his nearest rival, DeSantis, and described the result as the biggest victory for an Iowa Republican challenger in modern history.[2] The scale of the win was widely read as confirmation that Trump would be the GOP nominee.[2] A day after the caucuses, Trump held a rally in Atkinson, New Hampshire, with Ramaswamy, who again endorsed him and told the crowd there was "not a better choice left in this race."[2]

### Early race calls

The [[associated-press|Associated Press]], [[cnn|CNN]], [[msnbc|MSNBC]] and [[fox-news|Fox News]] all called the race at 8:31 p.m., a little more than half an hour after caucusing began and before the caucuses had concluded, citing entrance polling and early returns from a handful of precincts.[2] The DeSantis campaign objected, with spokesman [[andrew-romeo|Andrew Romeo]] calling it "absolutely outrageous that the media would participate in election interference by calling the race before tens of thousands of Iowans even had a chance to vote."[2] CNN's [[jake-tapper|Jake Tapper]] said on air it was the earliest race call he could remember at the network, and CNN told [[the-hill|The Hill]] it had sufficient entrance-poll data to project as early as 8 p.m.[2] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] contrasted the complaints with the November 3, 2020 calling of Arizona for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] with a tiny share of the vote reported, arguing the episode exposed cognitive dissonance within [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] over what counts as election interference.[2]

### International reaction

Shortly before the caucuses, the Chinese state-run [[global-times|Global Times]] urged the world to "prepare for the possibility of Donald Trump being re-elected," a framing Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read as a signal that ran contrary to three years of narrative that Trump was finished.[2]

Ashe in America framed the outcome as evidence that "the people want their President back," arguing that Iowa's caucus-goers had again proven that when the people control the process, the outcome reflects their will, and calling to "make caucuses great again around the entire nation."[1] The result also became a reference point in Badlands commentary about [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] and the influencer apparatus that promoted alternatives to Trump.[1]
