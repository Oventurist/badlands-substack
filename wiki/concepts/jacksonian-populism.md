# Jacksonian Populism

Jacksonian Populism is the political tradition descended from [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]] that Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] identifies as the common ideological home of both [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Core commitments

As Carlson summarizes the tradition, Jacksonian Populists favor anti-establishment politics and non-career politicians, the strengthening of the Executive Branch, an end to the monopoly of government by elites, and greater rights for the common man in opposition to aristocracy.[1]

## Reagan and Trump as Jacksonians

Carlson notes that both presidents hung portraits of Andrew Jackson in their Oval Offices, treating the shared decor as a deliberate ideological signal rather than a coincidence.[1] Related common features he lists include: both men were former Democrats; both drew blue-collar support on their way to the presidency; and both brought minority voters into the Republican Party who had traditionally and almost exclusively voted Democrat.[1] Both also used deregulation and tax cuts as instruments of economic stimulus and financial relief, with Carlson arguing Trump is working toward the elimination of the income tax altogether.[1]

Related to the anti-elite strand, Carlson observes that both presidents sought to shrink the size of the federal government by an unusual method: staffing administration positions with officials who oppose the agencies they head — including [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]], installed with the purpose of eliminating her department, and [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], installed to gut and transform his.[1]
