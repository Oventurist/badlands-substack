# UN Charter

The Charter of the United Nations is the founding treaty of the [[united-nations|United Nations]], adopted in 1945. In the Badlands Media article [[a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation|A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats the Charter as the authoritative basis of international law and as a document standing in direct opposition to the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]].

## Content as characterised in the source

The article states that the Charter "premises international law on the sacredness of national sovereignty, non-military interference of one state into another, and mutual cooperation." Ehret argues that the discussions of the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|SCO]] summit and the broader [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]] — centred on the common good, common security and the improvement of the conditions of life of peoples and sovereign nations — are shaped by exactly these Charter principles.

## The stakes

According to the article, any acceptance by Russia, China, India and other multipolar states of a technocratic Great Reset and a world government above elected regimes would require "erasing or re-writing" the UN Charter. Ehret links the Charter's spirit to Franklin Roosevelt's Four Freedoms, the original Atlantic Charter, the original design of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]], the UN Declaration of Human Rights of 1947, and the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence from the Bandung Conference of 1955. He also emphasises that the UN as envisioned by Roosevelt and Henry Wallace was never intended to be a world government but a platform for dialogue and the harmonisation of security and economic interests among civilisations.
