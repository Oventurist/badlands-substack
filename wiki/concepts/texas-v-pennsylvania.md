# Texas v. Pennsylvania

*Texas v. Pennsylvania, et al.* was an original-jurisdiction action filed at the United States Supreme Court in December 2020 in which the State of Texas challenged the conduct of the 2020 presidential election in other states.[1]

## Disposition

The Court declined to hear the case, holding that "Texas has not demonstrated a judicially cognizable interest in the manner in which another State conducts its elections."[1] That standing holding has since been read as foreclosing interstate challenges to another state's election administration.[1]

## Invoked in the Proposition 50 debate

In October 2025, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that [[california-proposition-50|California Proposition 50]] could become "an inverted rerun" of the case.[1] Because Proposition 50's own text framed its temporary congressional maps as a response to partisan redistricting in Republican-led states such as Texas, Florida and Ohio, she contended that California was openly demonstrating the kind of judicially cognizable situation the Court found missing in 2020 — redrawing districts before the midterms with admittedly partisan intentions, and just after large numbers of migrants had been removed from the state.[1] On that reasoning she suggested Texas should sue California, as should voters, candidates and citizens.[1]

She described the 2020 Texas case as "a painful memory – but also a learning experience," noting that California holds the most electoral votes in the country and that Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] had said the manipulation part out loud.[1]
