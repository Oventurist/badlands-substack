# Long Depression

The **Long Depression** is the label bankers applied to the late-nineteenth-century period following the [[coinage-act-of-1873|Coinage Act of 1873]]. Badlands Media argues the term is a misnomer, contending that the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] only hurt banks, loaners and the corruption within the economic system, while ordinary citizens flourished.[1]

## Revisionist arguments

[[murray-rothbard|Murray Rothbard]], in *History of Money and Banking of the United States*, argued that the Long Depression was only a misunderstood recession, since real wages and production were increasing throughout the period. He attributed falling prices to the deflationary gold standard in the United States in the aftermath of the Civil War.[1]

[[david-ames-wells|David Ames Wells]] wrote of the technological advancements of 1870–90 and explained how deflation gave more power to the people by increasing productivity, observing that "the economic changes that have occurred during the last quarter of a century ... have unquestionably been more important and more varied than during any period of the world's history."[1]

## Political consequences

The article states that because the Long Depression was doing more damage to the rich and powerful, they needed something to permanently suppress the citizens — leading to the campaign for a federal income tax backed by the [[populist-party|Populist Party]] and culminating in the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]].[1]
