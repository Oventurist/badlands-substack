# Long Depression

The Long Depression is the name bankers gave, according to the article, to the deflationary era that followed the [[coinage-act-of-1873|Coinage Act of 1873]] — a label the article rejects as self-serving.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Bankers would call this period the Long Depression because the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] only hurt the banks, loaners, and the corruption within the economic system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) [[murray-rothbard|Murray Rothbard]], in *History of Money and Banking of the United States*, argues that the Long Depression was only a misunderstood recession, since real wages and production were increasing throughout the period, and attributes falling prices to the deflationary gold standard in the aftermath of the Civil War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

[[david-ames-wells|David Ames Wells]] similarly wrote of the technological advancements from 1870 to 1890 and how deflation gave more power to the people by increasing productivity.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article argues the Long Depression was doing more damage to the rich and powerful than to citizens, and that they consequently needed something to permanently suppress the citizens — motivating the push by the [[populist-party|Populist Party]] and others for a federal income tax.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)
