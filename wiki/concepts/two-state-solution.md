# Two-State Solution

The two-state solution is the diplomatic framework under which an independent Palestinian nation-state would exist alongside [[israel|Israel]], an outcome outlined in UN Security Council resolutions.[1]

## October 2023 revival

Following the [[october-7-attacks|Hamas attack on southern Israel]], Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] urged a two-state solution while blaming the United States for the crisis, arguing that Washington had "tried to monopolize the peace settlement" but "paid no attention to searching for compromises that would be acceptable for both parties."[1] Putin further alleged that American pressure on both sides amounted to an attempt to impose "unilateral solutions" without regard for the core interests of the Palestinian people, including the creation of an independent Palestinian state as contemplated by the Security Council.[1] He made the remarks in Moscow during a meeting with Iraqi Prime Minister [[mohammed-shia-al-sudani|Mohammed al-Sudani]], and paired them with an emphasis on minimizing harm to civilians on both sides.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted that a two-state solution is a position [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has also floated, and argued that what mattered more than the specific formula was Putin's repeated calls for peace and for avoiding civilian harm, in contrast to what the commentary called the arming, funding and bombing favored by "Uniparty Globalists on both sides of the Atlantic."[1] The piece suggested that the rhetoric of Trump and Putin had converged to the point of being difficult to tell apart, and urged readers to focus on those calling for an end to conflict rather than establishment figures proposing new ways of managing or stoking it.[1]

## Netanyahu's July 2025 rejection

On Monday, July 7, 2025, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said he wanted peace with the Palestinians but described any future independent Palestinian state as a platform to destroy Israel, arguing that for that reason sovereign power of security must remain with Israel.[2] Speaking at the White House, where he met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Netanyahu cited the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack launched from the Gaza Strip as evidence of what Palestinians would do with a state.[2] Asked by reporters whether a two-state solution was possible, Trump said "I don't know" and referred the question to Netanyahu.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked the remarks to a letter from 15 members of the [[likud|Likud]] party serving in Netanyahu's cabinet demanding immediate annexation of the West Bank, arguing that removing Netanyahu would not resolve the underlying wars because rival Israeli factions also seek wars of imperialism.[2] He read Trump's description of Netanyahu as the "greatest man in the world to answer the question of a Two-State Solution" not as a compliment but as an allusion to the 1995 assassination of Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin by a Netanyahu sycophant, which halted normalization efforts with the Palestinians, and noted Trump's December 2021 comments calling Netanyahu an untrustworthy partner while praising Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas as a "father-like figure."[2]

## Trump's 20-point Gaza plan, September 2025

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued in September 2025 that although Israel and the Western globalist hegemon had emphatically rejected any semblance of a two-state solution, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 20-point Gaza peace plan looked "an AWFUL lot like" one, making the episode a submission by the side least expected to submit.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] made the related point that the plan was explicitly based on the [[new-york-declaration|New York Declaration]], the Saudi-French two-state proposal.[3]

## General Assembly endorsement (September 2025)

On September 12, 2025, the [[united-nations-general-assembly|United Nations General Assembly]] voted overwhelmingly to endorse a declaration outlining "tangible, timebound, and irreversible steps" toward a two-state solution between [[israel|Israel]] and the Palestinians, ahead of a meeting of world leaders.[4] The seven-page text emerged from an international conference held at the UN in July and hosted by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[france|France]], an event the United States and Israel boycotted.[4] The resolution passed with 142 votes in favor, 10 against and 12 abstentions, ahead of a September 22 leaders' meeting at which Britain, France, Canada, Australia and Belgium were expected to formally recognize a Palestinian state.[4]

The declaration condemns the October 7, 2023 attacks on Israel by [[hamas]] that triggered the war in Gaza, and also condemns Israeli attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure in Gaza, along with siege and starvation, "which have resulted in a devastating humanitarian catastrophe and protection crisis."[4] The associated [[new-york-declaration|New York Declaration]] demanded that Hamas surrender to the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], disarm, release the hostages, and have its leadership leave Gaza permanently — terms that US Representative to the UN [[morgan-ortagus|Morgan Ortagus]] separately stipulated even while dismissing the declaration as a "publicity stunt."[4]

## Saudi advocacy at the United Nations (July 2025)

At the opening of the [[2025-un-two-state-solution-conference|high-level UN conference on the peaceful settlement of the Palestinian issue]] on July 28, 2025, Saudi Foreign Minister Prince [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] declared that "the Kingdom believes that the two-state solution is the key to regional stability."[5] He welcomed French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s intention to recognize a state of Palestine in September, while [[state-department|State Department]] spokeswoman [[tammy-bruce|Tammy Bruce]] condemned the conference on behalf of the United States.[5] Badlands commentary read the diplomatic momentum as evidence that the [[united-nations|UN]] and the European Union had grown increasingly sympathetic to Palestinian statehood under sustained Arab financial and diplomatic pressure.[5]
