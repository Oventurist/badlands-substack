# True Shutdown Fairness Act

The True Shutdown Fairness Act was a Democratic-sponsored bill introduced by Sen. [[chris-van-hollen|Chris Van Hollen]] of Maryland during the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]] that would have paid all federal workers, members of the military and federal contractors for the duration of the lapse in appropriations.[1]

## Provisions and blockage

In addition to authorizing pay, the bill would have prevented the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration from attempting mass layoffs of federal workers during the shutdown — the employee restructurings known as reductions in force.[1]

On Thursday, October 23, 2025, Sen. [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] of Wisconsin objected to Van Hollen's request that the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] immediately consider and pass the measure.[1] It was one of two Democratic-sponsored bills to pay federal workers during the shutdown that Johnson rejected on the floor that day.[1]

## Context

The same day, Senate Democrats blocked Johnson's competing [[shutdown-fairness-act-of-2025|Shutdown Fairness Act of 2025]], which covered only active-duty military and other essential workers, in a 54-45 vote that fell short of the 60-vote threshold.[1] The mirrored blockages were presented in the Badlands News Brief under the heading of "'Bipartisan' Bill Blockage."[1]
