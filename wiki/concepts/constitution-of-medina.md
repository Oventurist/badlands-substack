# Constitution of Medina

The Constitution of Medina was a historic document, corroborated by multiple sources, written by the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]] in the year 622.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Content

It was a formalized agreement between Muhammad's followers — the Muslims — and the Jewish clans that had historically ruled Medina.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) It established Medina as a haram (sanctuary), prohibited violence between clans, and guaranteed religious freedom for all non-Muslims, specifically Jews.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Significance

Muhammad later established similar laws in Jerusalem, where Jews had previously been banned from worship by the ruling Christian authorities.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The article presents the document as defying the modern narrative about Islam and its "rival" religions, adding that Muhammad's successors — especially under [[wahhabism|Wahhabism]] — would adopt a far less tolerant disposition.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) It sits alongside the article's broader argument that the idea Islam and Christianity cannot coexist peacefully is a devious deception propagated in the 20th century.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
