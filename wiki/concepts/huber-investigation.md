# Huber Investigation

The Huber investigation is the criminal inquiry that Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] assigned in November 2017 to [[john-huber|John Huber]], the United States Attorney for Utah, covering the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]] and other matters for which congressional Republicans were demanding a special counsel.[1]

## Origin

In March 2017 Sessions recused himself from the Russian collusion investigation under pressure from congressional Democrats, and [[donald-trump|Trump]] publicly criticised him for it.[1] In October 2017 Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]], chairman of the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], wrote to Sessions requesting a special counsel on Uranium One.[1] Sessions declined, and the media and Democrats treated the refusal as the end of the matter, labelling those who continued to question the deal conspiracy theorists.[1]

In a letter to Grassley, House Judiciary chairman [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]] and House Oversight chairman [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]], Sessions subsequently revealed that Huber had already been conducting a full criminal investigation into all of those matters for five months, since November 13, 2017, and that everything the chairmen sought investigated fell "fully within the scope of his existing mandate."[1]

## Structure and powers

Sessions paired Huber with [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]], whose staff of 470 investigators gave the effort investigative firepower exceeding that of any special counsel.[1] As a U.S. attorney, Huber holds full authority to empanel grand juries and file criminal charges, and grand juries can be seated anywhere — meaning citizens of Utah rather than a District of Columbia jury pool could decide on indictments.[1] The inspector general's jurisdiction extends beyond legal violations to breaches of established departmental practice, allowing accountability even where no statute was broken.[1]

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues this arrangement was far more dangerous to the targets than a special counsel would have been, and cites [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s difficulty obtaining convictions in D.C. courts as proof that venue matters, suggesting Durham deliberately reserved conspiracy charges for Huber to bring outside Washington.[1]

## Chain of command

[[qanon|Q]] drop #1711 of July 26, 2018 asked to whom Huber directly reports and whether it was normal that [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]] was side-stepped in Huber's chain of command.[1] Lange concludes that Sessions recused himself only from the Russian collusion investigation and not from the Huber inquiry, so Huber reported directly to Sessions rather than Rosenstein — evidence, in his reading, that Sessions was a participant in a sting operation while Rosenstein was a target.[1]

## Scope

Lange characterises the effort as a massive [[racketeer-influenced-and-corrupt-organizations-act|RICO]] investigation tying together Uranium One, the [[steele-dossier|Dossier]] used to frame Trump, and the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], generating [[sealed-indictments|sealed indictments]] while the public narrative focused on the [[russiagate-hoax|Russian collusion]] investigation of Trump.[1]
