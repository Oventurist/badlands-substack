# PREP Act

The Public Readiness and Emergency Preparedness Act (PREP Act) is a federal statute under which the Secretary of Health and Human Services may issue declarations shielding manufacturers and other "covered persons" from product-liability claims arising from "covered countermeasures" such as vaccines and therapeutics during a public health emergency.[1]

## Twelfth amendment to the COVID-19 declaration (December 2024)

In December 2024 the federal government quietly extended liability protection for manufacturers of COVID-19 and influenza vaccines for another five years, through 2029, via a twelfth amendment to the PREP Act declaration for "COVID-19 Medical Countermeasures" signed by outgoing [[department-of-health-and-human-services|HHS]] Secretary [[xavier-becerra|Xavier Becerra]].[1] Just the News reported that HHS did not appear to have publicized the change outside a December 11 Federal Register notice, read primarily by regulated entities, and a generic page buried within the agency's website.[1]

The 27-page notice, dated December 6, was issued 19 months after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] formally ended the COVID public health emergency, and offered a brief justification: "I have determined there is a credible risk that COVID-19 may in the future constitute such an emergency."[1] It also asserted that "Congress delegated to me the authority to strike the appropriate federal-state balance with respect to Covered Countermeasures through PREP Act Declarations."[1] According to the PREP Act questions-and-answers page, "the 12th Amendment generally extends the protections clarified in the 11th Amendment" from May 2023 — which had ended some liability provisions and was set to expire December 31 — through 2029.[1]

The timing drew attention because it came on the cusp of an incoming administration expected to aggressively examine vaccine injuries and to release federal vaccine-injury reporting data that Just the News had gone to court to obtain.[1]

## Scope of the COVID-19 liability shield

The PREP Act protections for COVID-19 vaccines rest on a 2020 declaration entered by then–Health Secretary Alex Azar during the administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which the administration of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] subsequently extended.[2] The declaration shields both manufacturers and the people who administer the vaccines from liability, with the only exception being cases of death or serious injury caused by "willful misconduct."[2] According to an opinion issued by the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|U.S. Department of Health and Human Services]], the protection extends even to people who "reasonably could have believed" they were covered when in fact they were not.[2]

## Repeal effort (March 2024)

Legislation introduced on March 5, 2024 by Representative [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]] (R-Texas) would retroactively remove PREP Act protections for COVID-19 vaccine manufacturers, allowing injured Americans to sue.[2] The bill provides that no federal law may make a COVID-19 vaccine manufacturer immune from suit or limit its liability for claims arising from administration or use of the vaccine.[2] Industry defenders warned that stripping the shield would place the ability to address future public health threats at risk, a line Badlands commentator [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] translated as an admission that accountability would end coercive vaccine development.[2]
