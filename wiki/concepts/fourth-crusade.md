# Fourth Crusade

The Fourth Crusade of 1204 is treated in "Shadows, Sovereigns and Civil Wars – Part 2" as a founding event for the Venetian oligarchical power that Matthew Ehret traces forward into the twentieth century.[1]

The crusade saw the strategically important sacking of Constantinople by hordes of Christian crusading mercenaries killing fellow Christians.[1] The victory was a veritable coup for the Venetian oligarchs, who promptly took control of Constantinople's vast maritime trade routes, strategic states, and the island of Crete.[1] Venice's takeover of the Byzantine possession of Crete brought the Kalergi bloodline to power; the clan went on to play a prominent role in the affairs of state of the Venetian and then Habsburg empires for the next eight centuries, culminating in Count [[richard-coudenhove-kalergi|Richard von Coudenhove-Kalergi]].[1]

The abbey at Trisulti, later the headquarters of the [[dignitatis-humanae-institute|Dignitatis Humanae Institute]], was constructed on the orders of [[pope-innocent-iii|Pope Innocent III]] in 1204 to celebrate the crusade.[1] Coudenhove-Kalergi also traced his paternal line to the crusading era, noting that "two brothers Coudenhove joined the first Crusade in 1099, when Jerusalem was conquered for the first time by the united armies of the Christian knights of Europe."[1]
