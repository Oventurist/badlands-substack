# Iran Continuity of Government Measures (2026)

The Iran continuity of government measures of 2026 were a set of emergency arrangements adopted by the Islamic Republic to preserve governmental function and essential supply lines amid escalating threats of American or Israeli attack.[1] Badlands commentators characterized the package as a Persian analogue of [[devolution|Devolution Theory]], nicknaming it "Persian Devolution."[1]

## Measures adopted

President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] delegated expanded powers to provincial governors to permit rapid local decision-making, accelerated imports, and barter trade conducted without foreign currency.[1] The [[supreme-national-security-council-iran|Supreme National Security Council]] designated successors for key military and civilian officials in order to prevent leadership gaps during a crisis, drawing on protocols developed in previous wartime periods.[1]

## Institutional background

In early August of the previous year, Iranian media quoting the Secretariat of the Supreme National Security Council reported that the leadership had approved the creation of a National Defense Council within the framework of Article 176 of the Iranian Constitution.[1] A September assessment reported that Deputy Interior Minister for Security and Law Enforcement and National Security Council Secretary Ali Akbar Pour Jamshidian had stated on September 27 that the Supreme National Security Council directed all Iranian military and civilian officials to designate several successors to ensure continuity of governance in the event of leadership disruption.[1] Iranian outlets around the same period reported prospective structural changes to the Supreme National Security Council centered on the "separation of defense and security affairs from each other," with emphasis on rebuilding intelligence and security institutions.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Iran had implemented not merely a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocol but one closely resembling [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s Devolution Theory, citing reporting from the Financial Times of London.[1] He speculated that the drive to rebuild intelligence and security institutions reflected an Iranian conclusion that the state had been compromised through the late General [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]] and alleged Mossad handler [[david-barnea|David Barnea]].[1]

He further argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appears to push nominally hostile governments into Continuity of Government protocols, beginning with [[venezuela|Venezuela]] in December 2024, and asked what benefit such protocols would serve for governments that Republican leadership would like to see overthrown and replaced by Israeli-aligned leaders.[1] The suggested answer was that COG could place those governments into a political lockdown that preemptively thwarts regime-change operations.[1]
