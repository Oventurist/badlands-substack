# Censure of Rashida Tlaib

The censure of Rashida Tlaib was a November 7, 2023 action by the United States House of Representatives formally rebuking Michigan Democrat [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] over comments critical of Israel and supportive of Palestinians made during Israel's war against [[hamas|Hamas]].[1]

## The vote

The GOP-led resolution, introduced by Georgia Republican [[rich-mccormick|Rich McCormick]], passed 234 to 188, with four Republicans voting against and 22 Democrats voting in favor.[1] It advanced earlier the same day after a Democratic-led effort to block the measure failed.[1] The action was described as a rare and significant rebuke of Tlaib, who is the first Palestinian-American woman to serve in Congress.[1]

## Tlaib's response

Tlaib argued that the censure attempts were an effort to silence her and that her colleagues had "resorted to distorting my positions in resolutions filled with obvious lies."[1] After the procedural vote, she delivered an emotional speech on the House floor contending that criticism of the Israeli government should not be conflated with antisemitism: "It is important to separate people and governments. No government is beyond criticism. The idea that criticizing the government of Israel is antisemitic sets a very dangerous precedent, and it's been used to silence diverse voices speaking up for human rights across our nation."[1] She grew emotional and had trouble speaking after saying, "I can't believe I have to say this, but Palestinian people are not disposable."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America characterized the censure as "another day, another meaningless virtue signal that doesn't move the needle," noting that Tlaib retained her committee assignments and that her standing among supporters would likely rise.[1] The commentary argued that the slogan "from the river to the sea" has always meant the eradication of the Jewish people from the land, but that silencing Tlaib — rather than spotlighting her words and framing them in historical context — gives those words more weight.[1] The episode was judged "a fail for House Republicans, a meaningless ceremony that prolongs the current narrative deployment," with the closing suggestion, "maybe that's the point."[1]
