# Arkansas Legal Tender Act

The Arkansas Legal Tender Act is a 2023 state law making gold and silver function as legal tender within Arkansas, described by Badlands Media as part of an accelerating state-level movement to restore constitutional money.[1]

## Provisions

Signed by Arkansas Governor [[sarah-huckabee-sanders|Sarah Huckabee Sanders]] on April 11, 2023, the act explicitly provides that gold and silver "specie" — meaning any kind of bullion or coin containing these materials — can be used to pay debts.[1] It further specifies that "specie or legal tender shall not be characterized as personal property for taxation or regulatory purposes," and that "the purchase, sale, or exchange of any type or form of specie shall not give rise to any tax liability."[1]

## Context

Arkansas became the fourth state to make gold and silver coins legal tender, after Utah, Wyoming and Oklahoma.[1] According to the [[tenth-amendment-center|Tenth Amendment Center]], 23 states were developing comparable regulations, an effort its communications director Michael Marrahey characterized as states "nullifying the Fed on a state-by-state level."[1]

## Rationale

Proponents argue that in a multicurrency environment the better currency prevails; constitutional tender expert professor William Greene has described a "reverse [[greshams-law|Gresham's Law]]" effect in which gold and silver coins, holding value better than Federal Reserve notes, drive the weaker money out.[1] Advocates ground the movement in the U.S. Constitution's clause that "no state shall … make anything but gold and silver coin a tender in payment of debts."[1] Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] frames such laws as a plan not merely to end the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] but to prevent any future central bank by restoring state authority over currency and moving the country back toward a [[gold-standard|gold standard]].[1]
