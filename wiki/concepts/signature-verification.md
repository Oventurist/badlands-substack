# Signature Verification

Signature verification is the process by which election officials confirm the identity of a mail-in voter by comparing the signature on the ballot affidavit envelope against signatures already held in the county's records.[1] Under Arizona law, the term means specifically that a human being looks at a signature on a mail-in ballot envelope and compares that signature to other signatures in the county record.[1]

## Central issue in Lake v. Hobbs

Signature verification was the narrow legal question at the heart of the second phase of [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]'s election challenge, [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]] — namely whether [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] conducted signature verification in accordance with the Arizona statute.[1]

On day two of the trial, held Thursday, May 18, 2023, Team Lake used the county's own data to argue that lawful verification did not occur.[1] According to testimony, 779,330 signatures were compared in less than six seconds each; 512,597 of those in less than four seconds; and 321,495 in less than three seconds.[1]

The data also showed a correlation between faster review speed and higher approval rates. Plaintiffs highlighted the example of the reviewer designated User 79, who processed 54,298 signatures at a 98.9 percent approval rate overall; of these, 45,217 were compared in under six seconds at a 99.91 percent approval rate, 37,524 in under four seconds at 99.97 percent, and 27,196 in under three seconds with a 100 percent approval rate.[1]

Plaintiffs' expert [[erich-speckin|Erich Speckin]] testified that the 45,670 signatures approved by User 26 and User 9 should be removed from the 321,495 figure because those comparisons were completed too quickly to have been performed by a human.[1] The plaintiffs' conclusion was that if machines were conducting signature verification, then verification under Arizona law and statute did not take place.[1]
