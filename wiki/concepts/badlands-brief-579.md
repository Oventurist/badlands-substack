# Badlands Brief: Two Cartels, Imminent Indictments, & A French Nun

"Two Cartels, Imminent Indictments, & A French Nun" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] published on May 1, 2026 by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. The daily Brief compiles news items with commentary from the outlet's contributors, and this installment carried five main segments plus a bonus item, with analysis from [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]. The post recorded 138 likes. As with all editions, the Brief carries a disclaimer that the opinions expressed belong to the tagged authors rather than to Badlands Media as an institution.

## US charges Mexican officials in expanding cartel corruption case

The Brief opens with the news that federal prosecutors in New York had charged multiple Mexican officials — including a sitting governor — with conspiring alongside the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]] to traffic narcotics into the United States. According to the [[department-of-justice|US Department of Justice]], the case alleges a network of current and former officials who used their positions to facilitate drug shipments, provide protection, and enable cartel operations across state institutions. The charges were filed by the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York, and the Brief describes the case as one of the most sweeping corruption prosecutions tied to Mexican governance in recent years. Reporting cited by the Brief noted that while the named officials deny wrongdoing, the indictment intensifies pressure on US–[[mexico|Mexico]] security cooperation and raises broader concerns about cartel influence inside political systems. Source links pointed to ZeroHedge, a Justice Department press release describing the "Governor of Sinaloa and nine other current and former Mexican officials" charged with drug offenses, and NBC News.

In commentary, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argues that the indictments confirm a long-standing thesis: that Mexico, and not [[venezuela|Venezuela]], is "the real target in Latin America." He notes that in the Venezuela operation no government officials other than [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] were targeted, no indictments were issued against the rest of the government, and once Maduro was removed the remaining Venezuelan government was recognized as "a great US ally." That the Sinaloa Cartel is the organization now being targeted, he writes, supports the standing Badlands theory that President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] of [[colombia|Colombia]] is a "white hat" working with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to dismantle the transnational cartel.

The commentary recalls that a year earlier, during a press conference with his generals, Petro accused the Sinaloa Cartel of running the [[national-liberation-army|National Liberation Army]] (ELN) and other armed groups in Colombia in order to secure drug routes for smuggling cocaine to Mexico. Petro described Sinaloa as the "head of the snake" of the Colombian drug trade and has since disclosed that he and Maduro had discussed a joint military operation against the cartels. According to the Brief, Petro shared this idea with Trump during a White House visit in February 2026, encouraging the United States to take the lead in targeting not only the Sinaloa Cartel and militant groups in the jungles but also the bankers helping launder their money.

With governors now indicted in Mexico, Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] publicly defended them and demanded that the United States produce proof of their crimes. The Brief situates this against Trump's earlier accusation that Sheinbaum is a "cartel puppet" and her public rejection of US offers to send American soldiers into Mexico to fight the cartels. The pattern of behavior, the commentary concludes, suggests that Petro was right about Mexico.

## DOJ weighs new classified leaks case against James Comey

The second item reports that the Justice Department was moving forward with a potential new round of charges against former [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], alleging that he leaked classified information. Bloomberg Law, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that federal prosecutors in the Eastern District of Virginia had revived a separate but connected investigation focused on Comey's alleged transmission of documents to Columbia Law professor [[daniel-richman|Daniel Richman]].

Sources told Bloomberg that a small group of EDVA prosecutors had been meeting with officials in the deputy attorney general's office since [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] was elevated to acting attorney general. The classified information probe had stalled after an initial review the previous year but was said to have regained traction under Blanche's leadership. No final decision had been made on venue, with options including Eastern Virginia, where Comey resides, or New York, where Richman is based; the timing likewise remained unclear.

Ashe in America's commentary recounts that on the previous day's Badlands Daily she and [[canncon|CannCon]] had discussed the "8647" indictment against Comey and agreed that it was "weak sauce," with Comey's post likely protected under [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] freedom of speech and expression. She notes that some in the audience were angered, with one person asking [[joshua-reid|Joshua Reid]] to reprimand them. Describing herself as "a First Amendment maxi," she observes that "having to defend your blogs and memes in federal court can be radicalizing." By contrast, she writes, a classified-leaks charge is a far more worthy use of the People's resources: as FBI Director, depending on what the information was, who Comey was leaking to, and why, the conduct "could arguably be treason." Her closing admonition — "Do it by the book. Make it stick. And leave the Bill of Rights alone" — is paired with the warning that "anyone cheering the government's erosion of protected rights is the enemy," regardless of "which jersey the government is wearing."

## Epic Fury targets OPEC's power as UAE exit fractures the oil cartel

The third segment covers the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]]' scheduled exit from [[opec|OPEC]] on May 1, described as a major strategic fracture in the global oil order that weakens the cartel's ability to control supply and defend price floors. [[russia|Russia]] publicly downplayed the risk of an immediate price war, arguing that [[iran|Iran]]-war disruptions and [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] constraints still dominate the market. Analysts quoted in the underlying reporting say the real impact comes after the war: once Gulf exports normalize, the UAE can ramp production outside quota limits, potentially forcing [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and others into a market-share fight. The Brief frames the "end of OPEC" as not merely an energy story but a geopolitical objective of [[operation-epic-fury|Epic Fury]] — to break Iran's leverage, weaken cartel coordination, and drive postwar oil prices lower.

Burning Bright's accompanying analysis places the development inside the Badlands framework of the [[global-cartel|Global Cartel]], described as "a centralized collective that has operated across borders for generations, weaponizing financial and energy apparatus against any sovereign nation that dared step out of line." War, regime change, and [[color-revolution|color revolutions]], he argues, were never ends in themselves but enforcement mechanisms deployed whenever a player threatened the scarcity model sustaining the collective. In this reading OPEC was "the crown jewel of said apparatus, the throttle ensuring artificial floors, engineered dependence and compliance through economic pain."

Burning Bright writes that the visible narrative war serves as cover for actual operations running beneath it — "Sovereign Disentanglement, supply line realignments and the quiet cultivation of force multipliers that will define the next era of multipolarity" — and that short-term disruptions are features rather than bugs of the realignment, each squeeze exposing the fragility of the old system while building public mandate. He insists the UAE's decision to formally exit OPEC after nearly six decades is "no impulsive wartime defection" but a deliberate sovereign policy decision, noting that Abu Dhabi has already invested heavily to expand capacity from roughly 3.4 million barrels per day toward 5 million by 2027. By stepping outside the quota "straitjacket," the Emirates signal the end of the enforced scarcity model they once helped perpetuate, intending to supply a world that will demand more energy "on their own terms." He predicts a cascade in which other Gulf producers follow, with Saudi spare capacity long held in reserve for cartel discipline now facing the logic of competition. Once the Hormuz theater resolves, he argues, the uncapping of production floors will unleash "a free-market race to the top in capacity and innovation" that becomes a race to the bottom in the only metric that matters to ordinary people: price.

## Congress passes short-term FISA extension

The fourth item reports that the House and Senate approved a 45-day extension of [[section-702|Section 702]] of the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act]], preserving the federal government's warrantless surveillance authority just hours before it was set to expire. The short-term fix followed the Senate's rejection of the House's three-year extension, which had included a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]] ban that drew bipartisan opposition. The measure passed 261–111 and headed to President Trump for signature.

Ashe in America comments that "the drama of the shutdown has lost its impact," saying she had forgotten the partial shutdown was even happening after sources in the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] told her that [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] were fully funded in the One Big Beautiful Bill. She dismisses concern for TSA and FEMA as "a burden on the people" that "appear to be fronts for money laundering based on the quality of the services," and calls CISA "an arguably treasonous entity that hoodwinked its non-technical overseers into funding a national coup." Turning to the CBDC provision, she notes that Trump said there would never be a central bank digital currency and asks why the Republican-controlled House tried to include one in its DHS funding package. Her proposed explanation is that "the [[congress|Congress]] are illegitimate usurpers, beneficiaries of stolen elections," whose lawmakers "continue to be owned and controlled by the [[invisible-enemy|invisible enemy]]." Voter intent, she argues, is real — the People voted for the agenda whether or not elections are real — yet lawmakers' actions "fly in the face of the will of their constituents."

## IDF warning highlights risk of escalation inside the West Bank

The fifth segment reports that a senior [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] general warned that rising extremist violence by Israeli civilians in the [[west-bank|West Bank]] could trigger a broader Palestinian uprising. The concerns followed a string of internal security incidents including assaults, trespassing cases, and a notable attack on a French nun at a Jerusalem biblical site. Israeli authorities have made arrests in several cases, but officials acknowledge gaps in enforcement capacity — particularly in regions such as the Negev, where a comptroller report attributed rising crime to police manpower shortages. The warning, the Brief says, signals growing concern within Israel's security establishment that internal instability, rather than external threats alone, could drive the next phase of conflict.

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry's commentary argues that [[jewish-terrorism|"Jewish terrorism"]] is "sweeping through the holy land," targeting not only Christians and Muslims in Palestine but Jews in Israel as well, and states that the murder rate in the Negev has tripled in recent months. He recounts that Israeli settlers stormed the home of the Chief of Military Prisons, angered that the IDF has been arresting Ultra-Orthodox students who refuse conscription orders. Their refusal, he writes, is not rooted in disagreement with the IDF's campaigns but in "a sense of entitlement," since for nearly 80 years they have enjoyed an exemption from required military service on the premise of rabbinical study. That exemption, he notes, was brought before the Supreme Court, which determined it had never been codified into law and was therefore not legitimate in practice.

The most recent episode in the saga, he continues, was the apparently random attack on a French nun in Jerusalem by a 36-year-old Israeli man; the nun was reportedly hospitalized with head injuries and Israeli police affirmed that video of the incident was genuine and that the man had been arrested. He criticizes US Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], who he says has for months been "trotted out by the Israelis" to deny that attacks against Christians are occurring, calling him "a tremendous coward who is devoid of any principles." He notes that Jewish terrorism has become a talking point in the upcoming Israeli elections, citing prime ministerial candidate [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] acknowledging a descent into lawlessness and vowing to restore law and order. As "the knives for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] come out," he argues that the world has turned against Israel not because of biblical prophecy or antisemitism but because "God gave people the natural ability of discernment," and closes by asking how much longer self-described [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionists]] will "forsake the teachings and spirit of Christ to advance the political interests of a foreign government."

## Bonus: asset managers pull back from the SPLC

The bonus item reports that [[fidelity-investments|Fidelity Investments]] and [[vanguard-group|Vanguard Group]] had paused charitable contributions to the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]] after a federal indictment involving an individual allegedly connected to the organization. The Brief characterizes the move as a signal of reputational-risk concern among major financial institutions amid increasing scrutiny of where corporate philanthropy is directed. While the SPLC itself has not been charged as an organization, the association prompted a reassessment by donors wary of legal and public-relations fallout, with the Brief suggesting broader implications as large asset managers face pressure to justify both their political neutrality and their charitable affiliations.
