# 2025 US-Iran Nuclear Negotiations

The 2025 US-Iran nuclear negotiations were talks opened by the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration with Tehran aimed at preventing Iran from developing nuclear weapons, pursued in preference to a military strike on Iranian nuclear sites.[1]

## Trump's decision against an Israeli strike

According to New York Times reporting cited in April 2025, Trump waved off an Israeli attack on Iranian nuclear sites that had been planned for the following month, choosing instead to negotiate a deal limiting Iran's nuclear program.[1] Trump informed Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] of the decision when the Israeli premier visited the White House earlier that month, and during the same meeting announced that the United States would begin negotiations with Iran.[1] The planned Israeli attack would have required US military support to carry out, and the report attributed Trump's refusal to internal disagreements within the administration; Netanyahu had been pushing for the strike to occur as soon as the following month.[1]

## Witkoff's enrichment framework

US special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] used a key component of the 2015 Iran nuclear deal — signed during the Obama administration and abandoned by Trump in 2018 — as a reference point for the talks, indicating that Washington sought to limit rather than dismantle Iran's nuclear program.[1] The 2015 framework barred Iran from enriching uranium beyond 3.67 percent.[1] "The president means what he says, which is: Iran cannot have a bomb," Witkoff told Fox News, saying the conversation with Iran would cover enrichment and weaponization with a requirement to verify any commitments.[1] He added that Iran "do[es] not need to enrich past 3.67%. In some circumstances, they're at 60%, in other circumstances 20%. That cannot be."[1] The posture fell short of the full dismantlement demanded by Israel, which regards a nuclear-armed Iran as an existential threat.[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that mainstream headlines suggesting Witkoff would simply allow Iran to keep its nuclear program were as provocative as they were misleading, and appeared mainly to have prompted Witkoff to engage the media and clarify his position.[1] He explained that under the deal being negotiated Iran would retain an enrichment program with stipulated limits, noting that a civil power reactor requires only 3.67 percent enrichment while "weapons grade" material is generally above 90 percent, with a grey area between 20 and 60 percent where a less powerful device could be built given sufficient quantity and the right system.[1] By this account Iran had enriched above 20 percent without producing officially weapons-grade material, and the negotiating concern was how quickly it could cross the 90 percent threshold.[1]

He also relayed rumors that [[russia|Russia]] — one of the world's original nuclear powers — had been suggested as a third party to provide facilities for safely storing more highly enriched uranium that could still be used in civil energy applications.[1] Nothing had been formalized, and he framed the open question as whether Netanyahu would proceed with attack plans without Trump's consent, and whether [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and Trump would then stand together to stop him.[1]
