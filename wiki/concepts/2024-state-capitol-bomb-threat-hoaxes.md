# 2024 State Capitol Bomb Threat Hoaxes

On Wednesday, January 3, 2024, a bomb threat emailed to officials in several U.S. states briefly disrupted government business and prompted evacuations and lockdowns at multiple statehouses.[1] No explosives were found, and federal officials quickly dismissed the threats as a hoax.[1]

## Scope

Connecticut, Georgia, Hawaii, Kentucky, Maine, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi and Montana were among the states that evacuated statehouse offices or buildings in response.[1] The [[fbi|FBI]] said it was aware of numerous hoax bomb threats at state capitol buildings but had "no information to indicate a specific and credible threat," and that it was working with state, local and federal law enforcement to gather, share and act on information.[1]

The threats followed a spate of false reports of shootings at the homes of public officials — [[swatting|swatting]] incidents — over the preceding days, including those targeting Marjorie Taylor Greene and Rick Scott.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the wave as deliberate narrative construction rather than random criminality, arguing that the incidents were "an obvious attempt to ratchet up the tension and spirit of fear (terror?) as the Presidential Election Year circus gets underway."[1] She contrasted the tactics and technology law enforcement deployed to identify and arrest January 6 defendants with the apparent inability to solve the swattings and bomb threats, contending that the U.S. law enforcement apparatus "has everything they need to know who is making bomb threats and making false reports to terrorize public officials."[1] Placing the episode alongside the January 6 pipe-bomb threats and the ongoing swatting of Marjorie Taylor Greene as "inexplicably unsolvable mysteries in modern America," she concluded that when the government cannot solve such a case, "it usually means the event is stage setting and narrative seeding — and it's only day four of the circus."[1]
