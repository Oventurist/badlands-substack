# 2025 Executive Orders on Flying Cars and Supersonic Flight

On 6 June 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed executive orders to deregulate and open research and development into flying cars and supersonic aviation technology, alongside further orders targeting American drone capabilities, technology and regulations.[1]

## Contents

One order instructs the [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]] to begin testing flying cars — electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, or eVTOL — establishing a pilot program working with both public and private stakeholders, according to a senior White House official and to [[michael-kratsios|Michael Kratsios]], director of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.[1] "Flying cars are not just for the Jetsons, they are also for the American people in the near term," Kratsios said during a White House press call, adding that "eVTOL promises to revolutionize transportation as well as cargo delivery and logistics ... blazing a trail to new frontiers as part of the golden age of American innovation."[1]

The supersonic order repeals regulations that hindered the technology's development and instructs the FAA to create a standard for supersonic aircraft noise certification.[1] It also advances research coordination between the FAA and the Office of Science and Technology Policy and promotes international engagement through the FAA and other agencies to "align global supersonic regulations and bilateral agreements for international operations."[1]

Kratsios said that together the orders "will accelerate American innovation in drones, flying cars, and supersonic aircraft, and chart the future of America's skies for years to come."[1] He argued that "Americans should be able to fly from New York to L.A. in under four hours," that advances in aerospace engineering, material science and noise reduction had made domestic supersonic flight safe, sustainable and commercially viable, and that "for the last 50 years, outdated and overly restricted regulations grounded supersonic passenger flight and weakened our global competitiveness in aviation," concluding: "The market is here and the technology is here."[1]
