# Palmyra Attack on Iowa National Guard Members

The Palmyra attack was a weekend ambush in December 2025 near the historic Syrian city of Palmyra in which two Iowa National Guard members and a civilian U.S. interpreter were killed and three other Guard members injured. The attack was attributed to the [[islamic-state|Islamic State]].[1]

## Casualties

The U.S. Army identified the fallen soldiers as Sergeant Edgar Brian Torres-Tovar, 25, of Des Moines, and Sergeant William Nathaniel Howard, 29, of Marshalltown.[1] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] confirmed that two of the injured Guard members were in stable condition and the third in good condition.[1] Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds ordered all flags in the state to fly at half-staff in honor of the fallen soldiers, saying, "We are grateful for their service and deeply mourn their loss."[1]

## Circumstances

A gunman suspected of being part of an ISIS affiliate opened fire during a meeting between U.S. and Syrian security officials. The assailant, who had recently been reassigned amid suspicions of ISIS affiliation, was killed after clashing with Syrian security officers.[1]

Syrian Interior Ministry spokesman Nour al-Din al-Baba described the attack as "a major security breach" but emphasized that Syrian security forces had achieved "many more successes than failures" since the fall of Bashar Assad's former government.[1] Coverage noted that [[syria|Syria]]'s new government is headed by [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Hussein al-Sharaa]], a former al-Qaeda terrorist.[1]
