# Climategate

Climategate is the name given to the November 2009 controversy in which East Anglia University researchers were, in the words of Badlands Media commentary, "publicly exposed as frauds" over their handling of climate data and models.[1]

## Significance

The scandal broke only weeks before the [[2009-copenhagen-climate-summit|December 2009 Copenhagen climate summit]], at which world leaders hoped to secure legally binding emissions reduction targets.[1] The source argues that the exposure strengthened the resolve of China, India and African governments such as Sudan not to sacrifice their industry and national sovereignty "on the altar of climate change models and technocrats" whose credibility had just collapsed.[1]

## Aftermath

Copenhagen failed, as did subsequent attempts to impose legally binding treaties at COP26 in 2021 and COP27 in 2022.[1] The source observes that few people retain the memory of this sequence, and fewer still connect it to the underlying fight over national sovereignty against the edicts of globalist institutions.[1] It is cited as part of the wider case that the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] definition of sustainability rests on contested science deployed for [[malthusianism|Malthusian]] political ends.[1]
