# Operation Lone Star

Operation Lone Star is the border-security initiative undertaken by the State of Texas under Governor [[greg-abbott]], under which the state erected its own physical barriers — including razor wire fencing — along the U.S.–Mexico border in response to what state officials characterised as a federal failure to secure it.[1]

## Razor wire and the federal conflict

Lawyers for Texas argued that Washington had not been able to secure the border and that Abbott's administration therefore set up razor wire fencing under the Operation Lone Star plan.[1] The [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration began removing those barriers, prompting Texas to go to the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-fifth-circuit|Fifth Circuit]] in October 2023 with the argument that [[united-states-border-patrol|Border Patrol]] agents were violating Texas law in doing so; the appeals court granted a stay pausing the removals.[1] The federal government countered that the wire blocked agents from reaching "the very border they are charged with patrolling and the individuals they are charged with apprehending and inspecting," and that Texas had positioned barriers so as to deny access to roughly 2.5 miles of border.[1]

## Supreme Court intervention

The dispute reached the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], which on January 22, 2024 voted 5–4 to vacate the Fifth Circuit's order and permit federal agents to remove the wire while litigation continued — an outcome detailed at [[2024-supreme-court-order-on-texas-border-razor-wire]].[1] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] framed the ruling as a reaffirmation that the federal government has the authority to enforce its own laws and that no state can prevent it from doing so, while flagging the unresolved question of state remedies when the federal government declines to enforce those laws.[1]
