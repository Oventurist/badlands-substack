# Siege of Leningrad

The Siege of Leningrad was the German blockade of the Soviet city of Leningrad — better known today as St. Petersburg — during the Second World War.[1] Conducted as part of Operation Barbarossa, it is considered the longest and most deadly siege in recorded history, lasting from September 1941 until January 1944, a span of 872 days.[1]

As a result of the German severing of the city's supply lines, it is estimated that over 1.5 million Russians perished, mostly from starvation.[1] Among the dead was the older brother of Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], who died of an illness related to malnutrition.[1]

## Invocation in the 2024 North Korea sanctions debate

In June 2024, following his summit with [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]], Putin held a press conference in which he explicitly compared the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]]'s long-standing sanctions regime against [[north-korea|North Korea]] to the Nazi siege of Leningrad.[1] The comparison framed indefinite economic restriction as a form of blockade warfare against a civilian population rather than as a neutral instrument of international law.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the personal dimension of the reference — the death of Putin's own brother — indicated that the Russian president was in earnest about helping the North Korean people avoid a similar fate, and speculated that Putin "may go down as one of the great humanitarians of our age" if he succeeds in alleviating that suffering.[1]
