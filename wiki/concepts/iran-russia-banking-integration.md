# Iran-Russia Banking System Integration

In late January 2023, a senior Iranian official announced that [[iran|Iran]] and [[russia|Russia]] had integrated their interbank communication and transfer systems in order to enhance bilateral trade and financial operations and to bypass strict economic sanctions imposed on their financial infrastructure.[1]

## Structure of the agreement

Under the signed agreement, 52 Iranian banks and 106 Russian banks were connected through the Russian Financial Message Transfer System, a domestic messaging network developed as an alternative to [[swift|SWIFT]].[1] The announcement was made by Mohsen Karimi, Deputy Governor of the [[central-bank-of-iran|Central Bank of Iran]], who said the linkage would facilitate economic relations between the two countries.[1] The development was reported by [[the-cradle|The Cradle]].[1]

## Commentary and significance

Badlands Media contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] argued that Russian and Iranian objections to SWIFT stemmed from the system's historical use as an instrument to control the political actions of nations or to force compliance.[1] In this reading, research into the origins of many sanctions regimes indicates that they serve a globalist agenda, and no nation or global organization should possess the power to restrict the flow of economic energy from any national, corporate, or individual bank account.[1]

The commentary also noted an effect typically unreported in mainstream outlets: sanctions frequently impose additional hardship on ordinary citizens, as demand for the targeted country's currency falls and domestic economic conditions worsen.[1]

The integration was placed in the context of [[brics|BRICS]] nations — together with several non-BRICS states — openly discussing the creation of their own basket of currencies for use outside the SWIFT system.[1] Patriots in Progress read these developments as evidence of a coordinated strategy aimed at eventually ending the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] system, warning that such an outcome would make existing economic troubles pale in comparison, since a significant drop in demand for the fiat currency could produce a hyperinflation event.[1]
