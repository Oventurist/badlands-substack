# Cybernetics

Cybernetics, founded by [[norbert-wiener|Norbert Wiener]], is presented in Badlands Media reporting as the intellectual source of the modern [[transhumanism|transhumanist religion]].[1]

According to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the "new wisdom" that emerged in the wake of the cybernetics revolution of the 1960s asserted that technology grows with a life all its own, acting as a synthetic and deterministic *élan vital* without any regard for human thought or free will.[1] This overturned the older understanding of technology as the consequence of the mind's exploration of the objective universe and the application of discoveries to improve subjective life, and it denied that "mind" is anything more than the sum total of the non-living atoms composing the physical brain.[1]

Ehret argues that the "cybernetics cult" abandoned the assumption that a moral direction shapes the flow of upward evolution, asserting instead that notions of better or worse have no meaning and that morality, God, patriotism, soul and freedom are human-made abstractions with no ontological existence.[1] Despite this professed randomness, its adherents hold to a deterministic set of laws in which each technological upshift irreversibly reduces human mental powers, with AI replacing organic life forms as mammals replaced dinosaurs.[1] The framework treats [[entropy|entropy]] as the only law governing all closed systems, from biosphere to brain to economy to universe.[1]

## The science of control and the reform of eugenics

Ehret describes cybernetics as "the science of control," developed by acolytes of Lord [[bertrand-russell|Bertrand Russell]] and led by Russell's disciple Norbert Wiener, and identifies it as the ideological underpinning of the social engineering programs meant to rewrite human nature and induce a global reset of civilization.[2] [[timothy-leary|Timothy Leary]] and [[gregory-bateson|Gregory Bateson]] were themselves devotees of cybernetics; Bateson took a leading role in the Macy Conferences on Cybernetics after the Second World War and infused the new system of control into anthropology, embracing its vocabulary because the interdisciplinary field offered a more rigorous formulation of concerns his work already addressed.[2]

In "The Revenge of the Malthusians and the Science of Limits," Ehret argued that cybernetics became the chosen conduit for the "reform" of [[eugenics|eugenics]] after 1945 — the same rotten wine, made unpalatable by Hitler, repackaged in new wine skins.[2] Under this new science, governing systems formerly associated with the growth of human creativity and the defense of freedom became instruments for control.[2] After the war, cyberneticists demanded that humanity be modeled on the analogue of a binary computing system, with feedback loops controlled from one center of central command, a sum of parts in which nothing metaphysical — souls, dignity, God or justice — was permitted a role, all such notions being dismissed as unscientific abstractions.[2]

Ehret traces this central command structure back to the "World Brain" fantasized by the [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]]'s [[h-g-wells|H.G. Wells]] in 1938, and forward to the system that [[julian-huxley|Sir Julian Huxley]] dubbed [[transhumanism|Transhumanism]] in 1954.[2] Institutions such as the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg group]] and its junior partner founded at Davos in 1971 became, in his account, the "controlling nodes" of cybernetic and transhumanist management of humanity over the ensuing seventy years.[2]
