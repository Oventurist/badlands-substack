# Unaccompanied Migrant Children

**Unaccompanied migrant children** are minors who cross the United States border without a parent or legal guardian. Their handling by the federal government became a central point of contention between President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in late 2024.[1]

## Trump's 300,000 claim

In his [[time-person-of-the-year|Time Person of the Year]] interview in December 2024, Trump claimed that the Biden administration had lost track of more than 300,000 migrant children who crossed the border unaccompanied, saying many were in danger or dead.[1] "We have 325,000 children here during Democrats — and this was done by Democrats — who are right now slaves, sex slaves or dead," Trump said, adding, "And what I will be doing will be trying to find where they are and get them back to their parents."[1]

Trump had repeated similar claims on the campaign trail, and Republicans — including his appointed "border czar" [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] — echoed similar figures in calling for increased border security.[1]

## Fact-check and dispute

Immigration experts told CBS News that these claims were misleading and misrepresented government data.[1] They said that while some unaccompanied minors have become victims of trafficking or other crimes, the government safely releases most of these children to vetted sponsors — often a family member — while their immigration cases proceed.[1] The same reporting noted that 32,000 unaccompanied children failed to show up for immigration court dates from fiscal year 2019 to 2023.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the CBS fact check fell "into the category of malinformation: Information that is true, but harmful to the narrative," pointing to the acknowledgment of 32,000 no-shows as substantiating rather than refuting the underlying concern.[1] She closed by asking what happens "when extreme declas meets human trafficking," writing that "the world holds its breath."[1]
