# Global Governance

Global governance, in the Badlands framing, is the transfer of decision rights from nations to supranational bodies — and the third and culminating theme identified in Christopher Nolan's *Oppenheimer*.[1]

## The formulation

The argument is stated compactly: governance is about decision rights; global governance is about global decision rights.[1] This raises a conflict between national and global decision rights that, in the review's reading, must be resolved through the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] to the [[new-world-order|New World Order]], because national interests and autonomy are a barrier to achieving global decision rights.[1]

## The film's role

As [[j-robert-oppenheimer|Oppenheimer]] struggles with the capability he has created, he raises sharing the science with the Soviets and engaging the [[united-nations|United Nations]] in an oversight capacity; the film highlights the nationalistic rejection and tees up globalism as the answer in hindsight.[1] Badlands treats this as intentional, noting that the film drew on *American Prometheus* by [[kai-bird|Kai Bird]] and Martin J. Sherwin, and that Bird — who consulted on the film — said he hoped it would stimulate a national and even global conversation about living in the atomic age, about McCarthyism, about patriotism, and about the role of scientists in a society drenched in technology.[1]

## Wider pattern

The review argues institutions are currently painting globalism as the answer to every societal problem — climate change, migration, global health and pandemic response, technology innovation and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] — and that with corporate [[esg|Environmental, Social and Governance]] initiatives added, a global corporate communist agenda is on display.[1] Nolan's film, on this reading, moves the needle toward accepting the nuclear threat as an imperative worth sacrificing national and individual liberty for.[1]
