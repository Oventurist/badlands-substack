# Badlands Brief: Arctic Escalation and Tropic Trouble for the Financial System

"Arctic Escalation and Tropic Trouble for the Financial System" is an edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], the daily news-and-commentary newsletter published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], dated January 19, 2026. The post recorded 163 likes. As with every Brief, each item pairs a summarised news report — presented in block quotation with links to mainstream outlets — with interpretive commentary from a named Badlands contributor. This edition carries commentary from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], and [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], and covers five main topics: the tariff confrontation between [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] and European governments over [[greenland]]; Trump's remark that the United States "shouldn't even have an election" in 2026; the aftermath of the American operation that removed Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]; Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]]'s trade opening to China; and developments in artificial intelligence policy, advertising, and electricity demand. A bonus item reports a fatal high-speed rail collision in Spain.

## Trump versus the European Union over Greenland

The Brief reports that Trump escalated tensions with close European allies by announcing a 10% tariff on imports from eight European countries that had opposed American efforts to assert greater influence over Greenland. According to the summarised Reuters and Associated Press reporting, the tariff was set to take effect on February 1 and targeted Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland. The measure was explicitly tied to Trump's long-standing objective of securing American control of the Arctic island, which he described as crucial to national security.

European leaders are described as responding with unified criticism, warning that linking trade policy to territorial ambitions undermines transatlantic relations and risks a "dangerous downward spiral." They emphasised support for the sovereignty of Greenland and [[denmark|Denmark]] and condemned the use of tariffs as coercive leverage; a Dutch minister characterised the threat as "blackmail." The United Kingdom's government described its position on Greenland's future as "non-negotiable," with senior politicians across parties rejecting the tariffs. [[european-union|European Union]] officials signalled readiness to coordinate trade counter-measures and to consider suspending parts of EU–U.S. trade agreements — a so-called "trade bazooka" — if Washington proceeded with additional tariffs.

Despite the political friction, the article notes that military cooperation appeared to persist. Denmark's [[joint-arctic-command|Joint Arctic Command]] stated that there were no immediate Chinese or Russian naval threats near Greenland waters and reaffirmed ongoing [[nato|NATO]] collaboration. The Brief frames the row as highlighting deepening friction between Washington and its NATO allies over Arctic security, trade policy, and the future status of Greenland, a semi-autonomous territory of the Kingdom of Denmark.

## Trump's suggestion of cancelling the midterm elections

The Brief reports that ahead of the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]] Trump drew national attention by suggesting in a January Reuters interview that the United States "shouldn't even have an election" that year because of what he described as the success of his administration — remarks his press secretary later characterised as jokingly made. In the same interview Trump acknowledged concerns within his party about potential losses in November, noting that the party of an incumbent president historically loses seats in [[congress|Congress]]. White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the president was speaking facetiously and was referring to his view that the country was "on the right track."

Polling data cited from RealClearPolitics showed Democrats holding a narrow lead on the generic congressional ballot — roughly 46 percent to 41 percent — with Trump's job approval below 50 percent and disapproval higher in several recent surveys. Trump had also emphasised policy initiatives ahead of the elections, including a $50 billion rural health plan. Separately, he told House Republicans in early January that the party must win the midterms or risk impeachment proceedings if Democrats retook control of Congress. Control of the House and Senate, the summary notes, will shape the final two years of Trump's presidency.

## Venezuela, Maduro's removal, and the banking system

The third item reports that U.S. officials engaged in months of covert discussions with Venezuelan Interior Minister [[diosdado-cabello|Diosdado Cabello]] before and after a January 3 U.S. military operation that resulted in the capture of President Nicolás Maduro. Sources told Reuters that American personnel warned Cabello — who remains under U.S. sanctions and indictment — to refrain from using security forces or militias against the political opposition.

On January 9, Trump signed an executive order titled ["Safeguarding Venezuelan Oil Revenue for the Good of the American and Venezuelan People"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue-for-the-good-of-the-american-and-venezuelan-people/), described in [[safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue|its own page]], which aims to shield Venezuelan oil revenues held in U.S. Treasury accounts from seizure or judicial attachment. The [[white-house|White House]] said the action was meant to protect the funds so they could support U.S. foreign policy goals and help stabilise Venezuela's economy. In a further development, [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] travelled to Caracas to meet acting Venezuelan President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] — the highest-ranking American visit since Maduro's ouster — in a two-hour meeting focused on bilateral cooperation, security, and curbing Venezuela's role as a haven for criminal networks. The Brief also relays an unverified political commentary claiming that opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] brought an advisor into the White House who had previously insulted Trump.

## Mark Carney, China, and "panic in London"

The fourth item reports that Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney initiated a significant shift in Canada's foreign and trade policy by forging a new strategic partnership with China during a state visit in mid-January 2026 — the first Canadian prime ministerial trip to Beijing in nearly a decade. The agreements covered trade, energy, agriculture, finance, and tourism, and were intended to revitalise ties strained by disputes dating to 2018. A central feature was a tariff reduction deal permitting up to 49,000 Chinese electric vehicles into Canada at a reduced 6.1% rate, reversing a previous 100% duty; in exchange China agreed to lower tariffs on Canadian agricultural exports such as canola seed, a move expected to unlock nearly $3 billion in trade. Carney framed the agreements as part of a broader effort to diversify export markets and reduce reliance on the United States. Preliminary pacts reopened ministerial talks in clean energy, forestry, culture, and tourism. The summary notes that China remains a far smaller export destination for Canada than the United States.

Ghost characterises Carney as "the central banker king" who "has taken the field to counteract Trump" as the president moves against the global banking system by lifting Venezuelan sanctions. Asked to clarify a comment about setting up a "new world order," Carney spoke of restoring "plural-lateralism," citing agreements such as the Trans-Pacific Partnership, and of expanding the global banking system to expand the "security architecture" of the financial world in order to prevent money-laundering by criminal organisations.

Ghost finds this ironic, arguing that the Trump–Maduro operation "is actually about exposing the widespread money-laundering occurring at the highest levels by the biggest banks." He traces this to the 2018 arrest by Trump's Justice Department of [[matthias-krull|Matthias Krull]], who laundered billions embezzled from Venezuela's state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] as well as money for drug cartels. Krull pleaded guilty to all charges and cooperated fully in exchange for clemency, having roughly 65% of his ten-year sentence removed. His testimony, Ghost writes, led to massive asset forfeitures at a number of banks and financial institutions, "namely [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]], which faced insolvency in 2023 and had to purchased in an acquisition by fellow Swiss bank [[ubs|UBS]] that was ostensibly forced by the Swiss government," with all money owed to the government of Venezuela.

With sanctions lifted, he predicts "the flood gates will likely open," as a civil action brought by Maduro against executives of banks, commodity trading houses, and oil companies — dismissed without prejudice in 2019 over national-security concerns tied to Trump's sanctions — can now resume. He asks whether discovery will reveal "that the entire global banking system is the same cartel that traffics drugs, weapons, and children (among other things) around the world," and connects this to the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]]'s sudden warning that financial markets could collapse if the U.S. government announced the existence of aliens. Noting that the article itself acknowledges the disclosure trend has been years in the making, he asks why senior analyst Helen McCaw would "decide to panic over this development at this exact moment in time," concluding: "It can't be a coincidence."

## Artificial intelligence: imagination and litigation

The fifth item summarises three AI developments. [[openai|OpenAI]] outlined a new approach to advertising and expanded access, saying it would begin testing clearly labelled advertisements in the free and ChatGPT Go tiers for logged-in adults in the United States within weeks. The company stressed that ads would be separate from the AI's responses and that it would not sell user conversations to advertisers, and set out guiding principles including mission alignment, answer independence, user control of data, and prioritising user experience over revenue. ChatGPT Go — offering messaging, image creation, file uploads, and memory — was noted as available worldwide for $8 per month.

Separately, the Office of the First Lady posted that [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] encouraged people to "use AI to unlock new parts of your imagination" while warning against allowing artificial intelligence to replace personal thinking, urging users not to "surrender your thinking to AI." In Washington, the Trump administration and a bipartisan coalition of governors announced efforts to confront rising electricity costs partly attributed to energy-intensive AI data centres. Officials pressed [[pjm-interconnection|PJM Interconnection]], operator of the nation's largest electric grid serving over 67 million people, to increase supply and slow price rises by making technology companies help fund new generation. The coalition urged PJM to hold an emergency energy auction requiring data centre operators to bid on long-term contracts financing up to $15 billion in new power plants, shifting cost from ratepayers to technology firms.

### Ashe in America's commentary

Ashe endorses the First Lady's message: "AI is a tool — be smart with it, turn your brain on and use the tool to unleash it, but don't outsource your critical thinking. Use the tool. Don't be used by the tool." She compares the present rush to legislate to the internet revolution of two decades earlier, when Napster-era file sharing produced mass legislation and litigation, which expanded piracy further and led to a showdown over internet censorship.

She points readers to [[the-starfish-and-the-spider|*The Starfish and the Spider: The Unstoppable Power of Leaderless Organizations*]] by Ori Brafman (2008), which she read a decade ago and whose methods of effective resistance she considers "more relevant than ever as we seek to build the Golden Age," even as her understanding of such transformational change programmes has shifted. She quotes the book: "The harder you fight a decentralized opponent, the stronger it gets. The labels had the power to annihilate Napster and destroy Kazaa. But waging that battle was possibly the worst strategic move the labels made... As the labels go after the Napsters and Kazaas of the world, little programs like eMule start popping up... What we've seen with the P2P companies is just the tip of the iceberg."

Calling this prophetic, she recounts the anti-censorship revolt in which Wikipedia shut down its English site, Reddit went dark, Google blacked out its logo and amplified opposition information, and thousands of smaller sites joined; millions contacted their representatives, lawmakers reversed positions within hours, and by day's end over 100 members of Congress had withdrawn support and the legislation died. She adds the caveat "again — we are told," then asks: "So did the CIA want the legislation to die? Or did they lose that round? My money is on the former." Applying the same scepticism to AI legislation, with states suing over Trump's position that they cannot regulate, she notes that "everything is fake and most people at least get some of that." She references her Saturday appearance with Burning Bright on [[badlands-blitz-episode-2|Badlands Blitz]], where they discussed "an alternative to begging the government to protect you from fake," and closes: "Turn your brain on. When you turn your brain on, you quickly see the underlying question is about decision rights to determine what's true. Never give those away."

## Bonus item: Spanish train collision

The Brief's bonus item reports that two high-speed passenger trains derailed and collided near Adamuz in the southern Spanish province of Córdoba on the evening of Sunday, January 18, killing at least 39 people and injuring more than 120. The crash occurred at about 7:45 p.m. local time when the rear of an Iryo service from Málaga to Madrid derailed and crossed onto the opposite track, striking an oncoming Renfe train travelling from Madrid to Huelva, according to rail operator Adif and government officials. Emergency responders including the Civil Guard and Spanish Red Cross worked through the night to evacuate survivors from overturned carriages. Spanish authorities suspended high-speed services between Madrid and Andalusian cities while investigators sought the cause of the derailment, which occurred on a straight, recently renovated section of track. National leaders including Prime Minister [[pedro-sanchez|Pedro Sánchez]] expressed condolences. The event is treated further at [[adamuz-train-collision]].
