# 2024 Trump Cabinet Selection Controversy

Following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s victory in the November 2024 presidential election, his announced cabinet nominations produced a visible split within the [[maga-coalition|MAGA]] base, with many supporters objecting that individual picks had promoted Covid-19 vaccines and mask mandates or were [[rino|RINO]]/[[neoconservatives|NeoCon]] figures.[1] The dispute was the subject of a December 3, 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]].[1]

## The reaction described

Carlson characterized the online reaction on X and Truth Social as a recurring pattern in which Trump is treated as having gone "from a genius to an idiot overnight," with supporters concluding he had lost all discernment and was being misled by advisers who claimed to be MAGA but were in fact [[deep-state|Deep State]] operatives.[1] The same people who had celebrated and wept at his victory weeks earlier, he wrote, had concluded that nothing would change.[1]

He offered a psychological explanation: a person who has developed a sense of hopelessness, on receiving hopeful information, is more likely to reject or distort that information to fit the hopelessness than to revise the attitude.[1] He compared the movement to an abused spouse who comes to believe she deserves the abuse.[1]

Carlson distinguished two groups pushing the narrative: those who sincerely believe Trump is being deceived, and those knowingly working against him to erode confidence and hope in the movement.[1] He argued Trump's position — incoming president with majorities in [[congress|Congress]] and the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] and a favorable [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] shaped during his first term — was not the product of luck.[1]

## The concessions thesis

Drawing on [[the-art-of-the-deal|The Art of the Deal]], Carlson argued that some picks were concessions required to secure cooperation from RINO and NeoCon legislators during the roughly two years in which Trump could count on congressional majorities.[1] He observed that several of Trump's most loyal and most persecuted supporters — including people who went to jail rather than turn state's witness against him — were absent from the announced administration, despite Trump's repeated emphasis on loyalty as the paramount trait.[1]

Rather than reading that absence as proof the loyalists were Deep State, Carlson proposed the reverse: that those making such claims were themselves Deep State actors sowing discord.[1] He suggested loyalists would eventually be rewarded with official or unofficial roles, that their exclusion might itself be a concession to RINOs and NeoCons, and that some current picks were placeholders who might be replaced near or shortly after inauguration.[1]

## The spotlight thesis

A second explanation Carlson advanced is that some picks exist to place a person or an office under public scrutiny, on the model of how [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] was made a daily public presence during the Covid pandemic and consequently lost public trust.[1] In some cases, he argued, nominees had been placed in positions in order to dismantle those positions — effectively firing themselves.[1]

## Conclusion

Carlson's summary held that some picks were concessions, some were spotlights on corrupt individuals or useless offices, some were placeholders, some were never intended to be confirmed, and some made straightforward sense.[1] While disclaiming any call for a "blank check," he urged the base to extend trust, arguing that Trump could not reveal the inner workings of a Deep State takedown without simultaneously revealing them to the Deep State — comparable to telling an NFL defense the next play.[1] The essay closed with an appeal to [[trust-the-plan|trust the plan]].[1]
