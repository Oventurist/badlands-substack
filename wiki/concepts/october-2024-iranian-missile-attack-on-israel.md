# October 2024 Iranian Missile Attack on Israel

On Tuesday, October 1, 2024, [[iran|Iran]] launched a large ballistic missile barrage at [[israel|Israel]], forcing millions of Israelis to seek shelter.[1] Israeli Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] cited a figure of 181 ballistic missiles, while U.S. reporting described roughly 200 missiles fired.[1]

## Diplomatic fallout

Katz barred UN Secretary-General [[antonio-guterres|António Guterres]] from Israel, declaring him persona non grata for failing to directly condemn Tehran over the attack.[1] Germany, which summoned Iran's envoy to condemn the barrage, said Katz's declaration was unhelpful.[1] Guterres wrote on X during the attack condemning "the broadening of the Middle East conflict with escalation after escalation" and calling for a ceasefire.[1]

## United States response

The Biden administration argued there was a significant difference between Israeli actions expanding its war against the Iranian-backed militant groups [[hamas|Hamas]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and Iran's retaliatory missile attack, which it condemned as escalatory.[1] In carefully calibrated remarks, officials across the administration defended the surge in Israeli attacks against Hezbollah leaders in Lebanon while still pressing for peace and vowing retribution for the missile strike.[1] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] praised the U.S. and Israeli militaries for defeating the barrage and warned, "Make no mistake, the United States is fully, fully supportive of Israel."[1] Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] called the Iranian missile attack "totally unacceptable, and the entire world should condemn it."[1]

## Course of the attack

Iran fired the salvo on Tuesday night, sending almost 10 million people into bomb shelters as projectiles and interceptors exploded in the skies above.[2] Israeli officials put the total at some 181 missiles, and the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] said it intercepted "a large number" of them.[2] The attack was described as largely unsuccessful; one Palestinian in the West Bank was reported killed and two Israelis were injured by falling shrapnel and debris, which caused damage and started fires.[2] Explosions were heard across much of Israel, from Jerusalem to the Jordan Valley, and reporters on state television lay flat on the ground during live broadcasts.[2] Israeli military spokesperson Rear Adm. Daniel Hagari said sirens had sounded across Israel and that air defense systems were fully operational.[2]

Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] warned Tehran soon after the strike that it had made "a big mistake" and "will pay for it."[2]

## Badlands commentary on Iranian capability

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he had speculated as early as June 2023, on Devolution Power Hour, that an Israel–Iran conflict was on the horizon, and had argued that Iran does not need a nuclear weapon to "destroy" Israel because its vast arsenal of hypersonic missiles can penetrate the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]].[2] A sufficiently large barrage aimed at power plants and other critical infrastructure, he argued, could render Israel uninhabitable.[2] Drawing on economic analysis by Brady Fuchs (War_Hamster) on Breaking History, the commentary held that Israel is not capable of self-reliance: it lacks an agriculture industry large enough to sustain its population and the manufacturing sector needed to operate autonomously, while [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] has repeatedly noted in veiled threats that Israel's power grid can be neutralized with relative ease.[2] The same was said of Haifa, Israel's only deep-water port, which holds large storehouses of ammonium nitrate and oil refineries vulnerable to explosion — targets Hezbollah documented with a drone video posted online months earlier.[2]

The commentary characterized the strike as a demonstration of Iranian missile technology, with carefully chosen targets, noting that the few deaths came from falling debris rather than the munitions themselves, likely from intercepted missiles.[2] It argued that video evidence circulating on social media contradicted IDF interception claims, and pointed to Iranian-posted footage of Netanyahu's post-strike address in which his hands appeared to be visibly shaking.[2] It concluded that further Israeli escalation would be foolish given that neither Russia nor China — critical allies of Iran — had become involved, and that it would be even more foolish for the United States to follow Israel into what it called a suicide mission that posed no threat to American national security.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that Israel's barring of the UN chief, while not technically a ban on the UN itself, would become the operative narrative and would reinforce a perception of the Israeli government as an international pariah.[1] The commentary pivoted to the roughly 50,000 Israelis who, according to the Ukrainian government, were making a Rosh Hashanah pilgrimage to the city of Uman in Ukraine to visit the grave of the late-18th-century Jewish mystic Nachman of Breslov, contrasting the celebrations with Ukrainian men being sent to the front lines.[1] The piece further cited 2017 reporting showing a Russian special operations commando in Aleppo, Syria, wearing a Hezbollah patch, concluding that Russia, Ukraine and Israel are all connected in one giant conflict.[1]
