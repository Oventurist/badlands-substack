# 2023 Family Leadership Summit Presidential Forum

The 2023 Family Leadership Summit presidential forum was the first Republican presidential forum of the 2024 primary season, hosted by former Fox News host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] and announced by [[blaze-media|Blaze TV]] on Monday, July 10, 2023.[1]

## Format and participants

The event was scheduled for the Family Leadership Summit in Des Moines, Iowa, organized by The Family Leader, later that month.[1] Blaze Media announced the partnership on social media, saying it would live-stream the event all day on Friday, July 14 on BlazeTV and the BlazeTV YouTube channel.[1] The participating candidates were Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], former South Carolina Governor [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]], businessman [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], U.S. Senator [[tim-scott|Tim Scott]] of South Carolina, and former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]].[1]

## Trump's absence and Badlands commentary

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was not attending.[1] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] noted that Trump had indicated both publicly and privately that he might also skip the later Milwaukee debate, in part because of his large lead over the rest of the field, though aides said no final decision had been made.[1]

Deschamps argued that from an optics perspective the absence of the overwhelming front-runner painted the debate as trivial and, by extension, devalued the legitimacy and pull of those attending — an effect he described as subtle, mainly reinforcing candidates' existing positions.[1] He allowed that the event could nonetheless surface previously unexposed conflicts, concerns or valid proposals from the participants.[1]

In his assessment of the field, Deschamps described Scott as the only candidate without "baggage," called Haley "a neocon that no one likes and never has," and suggested Pence "might be the most hated candidate in history."[1] He wrote that DeSantis, despite holding second place in the GOP spread, was similarly reviled over his campaign's conduct, citing the use of AI-generated photos of Trump and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] and the quiet alteration of Florida campaign laws to let him run while serving as governor.[1] He predicted Trump would address the event in a way that highlighted all of those blemishes in his usual style.[1]
