# Gold Repatriation

Gold repatriation refers to the return of national gold reserves from foreign vaults — chiefly New York, London and Paris — to central banks' home countries, a trend Badlands Media presents as a signal that a global monetary reset and a return to a [[gold-standard|gold standard]] are underway.[1]

## Germany's program

Germany's central bank announced in August 2017 that it had completed a program to repatriate gold bars worth nearly $31 billion from storage locations in New York and Paris.[1] The country had been bringing gold home to Frankfurt from the two cities since 2013, with the final 100 tons moved from Paris earlier that year; in total 743 tons were transferred, and the project was completed three years ahead of schedule.[1]

## Scope

Countries listed as having repatriated gold include Germany, France, Poland, Turkey, Austria, the Netherlands, Hungary, Italy, Venezuela, Romania, Iran, Serbia, Slovakia and Belgium.[1] Many countries repatriated their gold reserves and the pace accelerated while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was president, with reserves recovered from the New York Federal Reserve, London and Paris, where large numbers of countries had held gold for safekeeping and for use in currency exchange, leasing and collateral.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that the repatriations reflected public pressure and a loss of trust in the custodial central banks, and that decentralizing custody makes it harder for what he calls the cabal to manipulate the gold price.[1] The trend is presented alongside record central-bank gold buying and the Bank for International Settlements' 2019 reclassification of gold as a tier 1 asset as converging evidence that the fiat system is ending.[1]
