# Dispensationalism

Dispensationalism, in the article's account, is the doctrine that God relates to humanity differently within seven distinct phases of history, systematized by [[john-nelson-darby]] and propagated through the [[plymouth-brethren]].[1]

## The seven phases

Darby's phases were: Innocence (life in the Garden of Eden before the apple); Conscience (Adam to Noah); Government (Noah to Abraham); Patriarchal Rule (Abraham to Moses); Mosaic Law (Moses to Jesus); Grace (Jesus to the present); and finally the "Thousand Year Rule," preceded by a Rapture and followed by a seven-year period of absolute dominance by the Anti-Christ.[1]

## Jesuit roots

The article traces "Pre-Millennial Dispensationalism" back to the Futurist School of the Jesuit [[francisco-ribera]], who argued that the events of Revelation lay in the distant future rather than the present or past, and that the end times signs would occur only once the Temple of Solomon was rebuilt — implying the restoration of the Jews to their homeland.[1] Ribera's rediscovered writings inspired S.R. Maitland and, through him, the [[albury-conferences]] of 1826–1830 attended by [[henry-drummond]] and Lord Anthony Ashley Cooper.[1]

## Modern legacy

Dispensationalism became the theological engine of [[christian-zionism]], carried into American religion by Cyrus Scofield's 1909 reference Bible, Charles Fox Parham's Pentecostalism, Dwight L. Moody, and James Hall Brookes' Niagara Bible Conference.[1] The article notes that even [[aleister-crowley]], despite rejecting Christianity, retained dispensationalism in his religion of Thelema, replacing the imminent return of Christ with the Aeon of Horus inaugurated in 1904.[1]
