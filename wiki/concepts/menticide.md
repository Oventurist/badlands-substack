# Menticide

**Menticide** is a term coined by the Dutch-American psychoanalyst [[joost-meerloo|Joost Meerloo]] whose etymology means "a killing of the mind." Badlands Media described it as sounding like the title of a horror film, while arguing that it is in fact a tool of social control rather than fiction.[1]

## Definition

Meerloo defined menticide in his book *Rape of the Mind* as "an old crime against the human mind and spirit but systematized anew," describing it as "an organized system of psychological intervention and judicial perversion, through which a ruling class can imprint their opportunistic thoughts upon the minds they plan to use."[1] He further summarized the underlying method as "simply a question of reorganizing and manipulating collective feelings in a particular way."[1]

## Mechanisms

Badlands Media's treatment of menticide identifies several interlocking techniques.[1]

### Waves of terror

Priming a population for menticide begins with the sowing of misplaced fear and rage, most effectively through "waves of terror," in which spells of fear are staggered with periods of calm and each successive wave is more intense than the last.[1] Meerloo wrote that "each wave of terrorizing creates the effects more easily – after a breathing period – more easily than the one that preceded it because people are still disturbed by their previous experience," with morale sinking lower and each new propaganda campaign reaching "a public already softened up."[1]

### Technological saturation

Meerloo also addressed the technological dimension, observing that "modern technology teaches man to take for granted the world he is looking at," leaving "no rest, no meditation, no reflection or conversation" as the senses are overloaded with stimuli until "the screen provides all the answers."[1]

### Isolation and conditioning

A further step taken by would-be totalitarian rulers is to isolate victims and disrupt normal social interaction, since a person separated from friends, family and coworkers is far more susceptible to delusion.[1] Drawing on [[ivan-pavlov|Ivan Pavlov]]'s work on behavioral conditioning, Meerloo noted that the conditioned reflex develops most easily in a quiet laboratory with minimal disturbing stimuli, and that totalitarians follow the same rule by conditioning political victims in isolation.[1] The article raises the question of whether COVID-era lockdowns and quarantines, and the atomizing effect of cell phones and social media, served the same function.[1]

### Offering a "way out"

Under a sustained stream of propaganda, minds once capable of rational thought become "playhouses of irrational forces," and the masses, surrounded by chaos, crave a return to an ordered world.[1] At that point the would-be totalitarians offer a return to normalcy at a price: the population must relinquish its capacity for self-reliant, responsible individuality and become submissive, obedient subjects.[1]

## Consequences

Where menticide succeeds, reason and common decency become scarce, an atmosphere of pervasive terror sets in, and an imagined "enemy in our midst" is projected so that society turns on itself at the urging of ruling authorities.[1] The resulting order is described as pathological and psychopathic: by enforcing strict conformity and blind obedience, [[totalitarianism]] eliminates the spontaneity and creativity that drive societies forward, breeding stagnation and mass death regardless of whether the regime calls itself fascist, communist, or something future generations may invent.[1] The article offers architectural contrast as evidence, comparing the Winter Palace of the theistic Romanov era with the Great Hall of the People at Tiananmen Square.[1]

## Countermeasures

Because the menticidal attack is multipronged, the article argues, the counter-attack must be as well.[1] Proposed remedies include the [[carl-jung|Jungian]] step of ordering one's own mind and living as an example for others; disseminating counter-propaganda information widely, since censorship is essential to the controllers' success; using ridicule and humiliation to delegitimize demagogues, whom Meerloo considered nearly incapable of humor; and building [[parallel-structures|parallel structures]] as advocated by [[vaclav-havel|Václav Havel]].[1] The article stresses that such ridicule must target bad ideas, faulty logic and unethical methods rather than people, lest the victors adopt the weapons of the enemy and become "the tyrants of another age."[1]
