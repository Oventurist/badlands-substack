# Harris 2024 Economic Agenda

The Harris 2024 economic agenda refers to the platform Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] outlined in August 2024 as the Democratic presidential nominee, criticized both by the mainstream press and by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators as evasive and statistically dishonest.[1]

## Washington Post criticism

In a speech delivered on a Friday in mid-August 2024, Harris squandered what the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] editorial board called a golden opportunity to lay out a clear economic vision, leaving many wondering what her plan actually entailed.[1] With Americans still grappling with high costs for groceries, housing and even a Big Mac, her response — which the Post characterized as "populist gimmicks" — was to blame corporate "price gouging" rather than address underlying economic challenges.[1] The paper judged her pledge to crack down on businesses making "excessive" profits to be a recycled talking point rather than a real strategy, and drew comparisons to the failed price controls of the Nixon era.[1]

## The child poverty claim

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Harris's economic messaging amounted to a massive gaslight, focusing on her claim that she and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had reduced child poverty by roughly 50 percent in their administration's first year by expanding the Earned Income Tax Credit, which Ashe described as straightforward wealth redistribution that Harris now wanted to make a permanent entitlement.[1]

Ashe contended the claim was false: Harris was in fact citing the mid-pandemic impact of stimulus checks on child poverty, a 46 percent reduction in the *supplemental* poverty measure rather than the official metric.[1] The SPM counts entitlements, including government stimulus checks, as wealth creation, allowing Harris to present transfer payments as investments that lifted children out of poverty.[1]

Citing the [[united-states-census-bureau|United States Census Bureau]], Ashe noted that 2022 SPM estimates "reflect a return to the relationship that existed between official+ and SPM poverty prior to the COVID-19 pandemic," with the SPM rate having run 0.6 to 1.6 percentage points above the official+ rate from 2009 to 2019 and falling below it in 2020 and 2021 only because of pandemic policies such as stimulus payments and refundable tax credits captured by the SPM but not the official methodology.[1] By the official metric, which tracks people actually leaving poverty rather than temporary circumstances created by novel government payments, poverty decreased by 0.7 percent in 2021 — a considerable variance from 50 percent.[1]

Ashe further argued that in the wake of these Biden–Harris "investments," child poverty rose by both metrics beginning in 2022 and continued to climb, erasing what she judged to be historic and real gains from the Trump economy.[1] She called the resulting media push to expand the EITC asinine given that the strategy had measurably failed and, in her account, put more children into poverty — dismissing the whole exercise as gaslighting characteristic of what she called "Bidenomics 2: Finish the Job."[1]
