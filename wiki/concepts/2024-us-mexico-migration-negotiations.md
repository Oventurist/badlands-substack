# 2024 US–Mexico Migration Negotiations

The 2024 US–Mexico migration negotiations were talks in which the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] leaned increasingly on [[mexico|Mexico]] to curb a record flow of migrants crossing into the United States, while Mexico advanced its own list of ambitious demands in return.[1]

## Talks

Earlier measures taken by the Biden administration to stem the migrant surge had produced only temporary dips in crossing numbers.[1] In late December 2023, Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] and Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] traveled to Mexico to meet President [[andres-manuel-lopez-obrador|Andrés Manuel López Obrador]] and request greater assistance; officials from both governments described those conversations as preliminary, yielding no hard promises from either side.[1]

## Mexican demands

At a press conference in early January 2024, López Obrador called on the United States to approve a plan deploying $20 billion to Latin American and Caribbean countries, to suspend the US blockade of Cuba, to remove all sanctions against Venezuela, and to grant at least 10 million Hispanics living in the United States the right to remain and work legally.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the story as one of the clearest recent examples of media narratives turning against the Biden administration while the underlying "Actuals" set up permanent status changes heading into 2025.[1] He noted the broad media consensus that the Mexican president held the leverage in the negotiations, observed that the concessions sought — aid, an end to the Cuba blockade, sanctions relief for Venezuela, and work authorization for long-resident migrants — would be unpopular among conservatives even if they carried long-term benefits for US–Latin American relations and economic cohesion, and concluded that [[donald-trump|Trump]] would reap the rewards of Biden's failures.[1]
