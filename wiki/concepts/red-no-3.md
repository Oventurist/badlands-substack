# Red No. 3

**Red No. 3**, also known as E127 or erythrosine, is a synthetic food dye that gives some beverages, candies, cupcakes and icings their bright red appearance.[1] It was approved for use in food, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals in 1969.[1]

## Health concerns and regulatory history

Red No. 3 first caused concern in the 1980s when animal studies showed a link between the dye and thyroid tumors in male rats.[1] The [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] subsequently banned Red No. 3 in some external-use products, such as cosmetics, during the 1990s, but the dye remained permitted in certain foods.[1]

In January 2025 the FDA moved to remove the dye from the food supply entirely. Christi Evans, an Oklahoma State University Extension food safety specialist, explained that manufacturers have until 15 January 2027 to reformulate their products and remove Red No. 3 from their ingredients, and that products imported from outside the United States must also abide by the new regulations.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentary linked the Red No. 3 ban to the FDA's approval of the non-opioid painkiller [[journavx|Journavx]] on the same day, remarking that "all of a sudden the FDA is moving away from opioids and poisonous dyes" and asking what had prompted the shift.[1]
