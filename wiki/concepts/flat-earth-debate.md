# Flat Earth Debate

The "flat earth" debate is a recurring topic in dissident online communities and a staple subject of [[twitter-spaces|Twitter Spaces]] discussions described in a February 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].[1]

## Pattern of discussion

The author described a repeating cycle in such Spaces: a defender of the globe model — a "Glober" — enters a flat-earth-titled room and mocks the participants; the flat earth advocates respond by questioning his grasp of the heliocentric model; the Glober reveals he has accepted "settled science" without understanding it; the flat earth advocates mock him in turn; and the Glober finally appeals for decorum, complains of aggression, and asks his opponents to change their tone.[1] When the host is himself a Glober, the flat earth advocate is typically "moon-doored" — demoted from speaker — once the host realizes he cannot argue the science.[1] The author cited a participant known as Sacred as an example of a respectful, evidence-focused questioner who was nonetheless removed from every such debate.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] itself hosted a flat earth debate episode of "The Earth Chronicles" illustrating the same dynamic.[1]

## Framing

Ashe in America argued that most people labeled "flat earthers" do not assert that the earth is flat but rather that it is not what the public has been told; many raise inconsistencies without proposing an alternative model.[1] She compared the pejorative label to the way "conspiracy theorist" was applied to anyone questioning CIA involvement in the assassination of John F. Kennedy.[1] Stating her own position, she said she stands on scripture, citing passages on the firmament, the waters above and below, a fixed earth, and a moving sun, and rejected the common Christian globe-defender reply that God "didn't mean what he said."[1]
