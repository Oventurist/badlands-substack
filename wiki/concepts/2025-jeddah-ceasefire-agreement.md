# 2025 Jeddah Ceasefire Agreement

The 2025 Jeddah ceasefire agreement was a 30-day truce proposal that Washington pressed [[ukraine|Kyiv]] to accept on Tuesday, March 11, 2025, in talks held at [[jeddah|Jeddah]], Saudi Arabia. It was intended to pause the three-year-old war between Ukraine and Russia, although Moscow remained non-committal to the U.S. plan.[1]

## Background and leverage

The outcome of the Jeddah talks was widely seen as a foregone conclusion after the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] hardened its stance by cutting off military aid and intelligence sharing to Kyiv following a disastrous February 28 meeting at the White House between Ukrainian and U.S. officials, curtailing Ukraine's capacity to withstand the Russian assault.[1] Kyiv had little choice but to accept the transactional deal, since its defences had been substantially bankrolled by the United States.[1]

## Terms

The Jeddah package did not commit the United States to underwriting Ukraine's territorial sovereignty, though it did state that the two countries would work towards a deal on tapping and developing Ukraine's mineral resources.[1]

## Implications

Commentators noted the deal's implications extended beyond Russia and Ukraine. Washington faced the task of selling the arrangement to Moscow, where President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had previously said he did not favour a ceasefire.[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s seemingly pro-Russia posture was described as having alienated America's traditional allies and rocked the trans-Atlantic alliance that guaranteed Europe's security since World War II; recent UK and French summits to back Ukraine had failed to produce a concrete defence plan.[1]

The ceasefire also framed the March 11 phone call between [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] and [[sergey-naryshkin|Sergei Naryshkin]] of Russia's [[foreign-intelligence-service-of-russia|SVR]], in which the two intelligence chiefs endorsed the 30-day ceasefire.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the sequence as a "joint rope-a-dope," with Trump and Putin moving around the game board in sync and sowing narrative chaos that paralysed their adversaries with bewilderment and trepidation.[1] He judged Trump's resumption of intelligence sharing and weapons shipments to be smart — a signal that he was serious about resuming the war and therefore necessary leverage to force a reasonable Russian counter-proposal — while allowing that the entire exchange might be a performance rather than genuine negotiation.[1]
