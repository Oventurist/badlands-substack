# Deprivation of Rights Under Color of Law

"Deprivation of Rights Under Color of Law" is the federal criminal offense codified at Title 18, U.S. Code, Section 242, which penalizes officials who use the authority of their office to deny a person rights protected by the Constitution or laws of the United States.[1]

## Penalty structure

The statute's penalties escalate with the harm caused. In its most severe tier, the law provides that "if death results from the acts committed in violation of this section... [the offender] shall be fined under this title, or imprisoned for any term of years or for life, or both, or may be sentenced to death."[1]

## Relevance to RussiaGate prosecutions

In August 2025, journalist [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] said the overriding charge in the emerging [[russiagate-hoax|RussiaGate]] accountability effort would be deprivation of rights under color of law.[1] That framing gained weight after [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]] told Real America's Voice that the conspiracy had produced fatalities: "People have died. People have lost their livelihoods. People have lost their careers. People have not been promoted. Families have been destroyed. Finances have been wiped out. Millions of Americans were kicked off social media. News networks were not allowed onto the legacy media platforms."[1]

Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] connected the two statements directly, noting that if deaths resulted from the alleged scheme, Section 242 exposes defendants to sentences ranging up to life imprisonment or capital punishment.[1]
