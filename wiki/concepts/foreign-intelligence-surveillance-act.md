# Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act

The Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) is the statutory framework governing United States foreign intelligence surveillance, best known in contemporary politics for [[section-702|Section 702]], one of the most powerful and controversial post-9/11 surveillance authorities.[1]

Section 702 allows US intelligence agencies — including the [[fbi|FBI]] and the National Security Agency — to conduct warrantless surveillance targeting foreign individuals abroad, but the program can also incidentally collect communications involving Americans.[1]

## 2026 renewal fight

In 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] urged [[congress|Congress]] to pass an extension of Section 702, calling on Republican lawmakers to unify behind what he described as a critical national security tool ahead of its looming expiration deadline.[1] Trump backed a "clean" extension — potentially around eighteen months — arguing the program was essential to military and intelligence successes, including operations tied to countries such as [[iran|Iran]] and Venezuela.[1]

The push collided with bipartisan resistance in Congress, where civil liberties advocates and lawmakers from both parties demanded reforms such as requiring warrants before searching Americans' data and tightening oversight of intelligence agencies.[1] The debate reflected a sharp reversal from Trump's earlier criticism of FISA authorities, as the program faced a hard renewal deadline amid ongoing concerns about past misuse, including improper surveillance of Americans and political figures.[1]
