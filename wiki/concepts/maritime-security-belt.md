# Maritime Security Belt Exercises

"Maritime Security Belt" is the name of a recurring trilateral naval exercise series conducted by [[russia|Russia]], [[iran|Iran]], and [[china|China]], held since 2019.[1]

## February 2026 iteration

In February 2026, as tensions over possible United States military strikes on Iran escalated, the three countries launched joint naval drills in the Gulf of Oman and near the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] as part of the Maritime Security Belt series.[1] The drills involved warships and aircraft from all three countries operating off Iran's southern coast.[1]

Coverage in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] framed the exercises as underscoring expanded military coordination among Russia, Iran, and China in a strategically sensitive maritime corridor, occurring simultaneously with reports of a sudden U.S. military buildup in the region.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the Russian presence as a potential deterrent against an otherwise seemingly predetermined war, remarking that "Putin has entered the field."[1]

## Maritime Security Belt 2026 and the Geneva talks

A further iteration branded Maritime Security Belt 2026 was launched by Iran, Russia and China in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] as the second round of indirect United States–Iran nuclear negotiations concluded in Geneva.[2] The exercise followed shortly after separate Iranian Revolutionary Guard drills that included anti-ship missile tests and temporary closures of sections of the vital oil chokepoint.[2] The timing highlighted that tensions remained high despite diplomatic movement, coming as Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] warned President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that Iran possessed weapons capable of sending American warships to the bottom of the sea.[2]

## Security Belt-2025

In March 2025 the Chinese Defense Ministry announced on its official Weibo account that a trilateral naval exercise dubbed "Security Belt-2025" would be staged in the northern part of the Indian Ocean, in areas near Iran's southeastern Chabahar Port, in early and mid-March.[3] The drill involved large-scale maritime operations featuring various divisions of the Iranian, Russian and Chinese forces, with training courses including maritime target strike, VBSS (visit, board, search and seizure), damage control, and joint search and rescue.[3] The ministry said the exercise aimed to deepen military mutual trust and pragmatic cooperation among the participating countries' troops; it was reported by Press TV.[3]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry situated the drills within [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s 1998 book *The Grand Chessboard*, which he read as arguing that the only development capable of threatening the globalist agenda — and therefore to be stopped at all costs — would be an alliance between [[russia|Russia]], [[china|China]] and [[iran|Iran]].[3] He concluded that this "unholy alliance has now officially emerged" just as Washington and Tel Aviv were saber-rattling about war with Iran, with [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi]] presenting a united front against the West and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|MBS]] favored by both sides as a potential arbiter.[3]
