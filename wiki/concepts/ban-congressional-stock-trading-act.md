# Ban Congressional Stock Trading Act (S. 1879)

The Ban Congressional Stock Trading Act is a bill introduced in the [[united-states-senate|United States Senate]] during the 119th Congress as Senate Bill 1879, aimed at prohibiting congressional insider trading.[1]

## Provisions

The bill would require lawmakers, their spouses, and their dependent children to place certain assets, including individual stocks, into qualified blind trusts — effectively barring covered individuals from actively buying or selling individual stocks.[1] Covered individuals would be required either to divest prohibited assets or to place them in a qualified blind trust.[1] Investments in diversified vehicles such as mutual funds and exchange-traded funds would remain permitted.[1]

## Context

The measure was reported in the Badlands Brief as a bonus item alongside coverage of the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] probe of [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], within a broader theme of accountability for financial self-dealing among Washington officeholders and monetary authorities.[1]

## 2024 bipartisan Senate deal

The 2025 bill followed a long-running effort in [[congress|Congress]] to restrict lawmaker trading. In July 2024 a bipartisan group of senators — [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] of Missouri, [[jon-ossoff|Jon Ossoff]] of Georgia, [[jeff-merkley|Jeff Merkley]] of Oregon and [[gary-peters|Gary Peters]] of Michigan — announced a negotiated amendment to an existing stock trading ban bill.[2] The amendment would immediately forbid members of Congress, the president and the vice president from purchasing stocks and other covered investments, and give lawmakers 90 days to sell existing holdings.[2] It was the first proposal in the yearslong saga to receive formal consideration by a Senate committee, scheduled before the Homeland Security & Governmental Affairs Committee on July 24, 2024.[2]

"Congress should not be here to make a buck," Hawley said at a Capitol Hill press conference. "There is no reason why members of Congress ought to be profiting off of the information that only they get."[2] Ethics experts noted that lawmakers' access to nonpublic information gives them a potential unfair advantage over the investing public.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the absence of such a law already "tells you everything you need to know about our current predicament," drawing on fourteen years at the world's largest SEC audit firm where every employee — including secretaries — annually tested and attested on "Independence," an intensive treatment of insider trading, antitrust, money laundering and bribery.[2] Because capital markets run on public trust, violations in that domain lead to prison; yet legislative and executive branch officials with far greater access to market information face much more lenient oversight, which she argued explains the downward spiral in public trust.[2] She predicted lawmakers would not pass legislation that meaningfully held them accountable, and noted that only [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] and [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] had demanded a full comprehensive audit of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[2]

## July 2025 committee vote and Trump's reaction

On Wednesday, July 30, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly attacked [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]], one of his closest allies in [[congress|Congress]], over Hawley's committee vote to advance a bill banning stock trading by lawmakers, presidents and vice presidents.[3] The measure would bar congressional lawmakers and their spouses from trading stocks, and would extend the ban to presidents and vice presidents — though the latter provision would apply only to future administrations.[3]

Hawley was the lone Republican on the panel to vote with Democrats, advancing the bill out of committee by 8–7.[3] In a Truth Social post, Trump claimed Hawley was being used by Democrats to target him: "Why would one 'Republican,' Senator Josh Hawley from the Great State of Missouri, join with all of the Democrats to block a Review, sponsored by Senator [[rick-scott|Rick Scott]], and with the support of almost all other Republicans, of [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi's]] Stock Trading over the last 25 years. The information was inappropriately released just minutes before the Vote — Very much like SABOTAGE!"[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] pointed readers to a thread clipping the hearing and its substantive debate, remarking that legislators only reach that level of debate when the subject is themselves, and that the fact they were debating the question at all was "wild."[3]
