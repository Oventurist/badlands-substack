# EEOC Lawsuit Against Tesla (2023)

On September 28, 2023 the U.S. [[equal-employment-opportunity-commission|Equal Employment Opportunity Commission]] sued [[tesla|Tesla]] in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging racial harassment of Black employees and retaliation against workers who opposed that harassment.[1]

## Origins and legal basis

The agency stated that it had investigated Tesla after EEOC Chair [[charlotte-burrows|Charlotte A. Burrows]] filed a commissioner's charge alleging violations of [[title-vii-of-the-civil-rights-act-of-1964|Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964]], specifically the maintenance of an unlawful hostile work environment for Black employees and retaliation against those who complained.[1] The commission underscored that Title VII requires employers receiving harassment complaints to investigate promptly and take appropriate action to stop the conduct.[1]

## Reaction

Coverage in [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] characterised the filing as the "Biden Regime" again targeting [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that Musk had been "in the crosshairs" since defying establishment dictates, and observed that no public details of the underlying conduct had emerged, leading him to suspect the case might prove to be a "nothing burger."[1] He predicted the suit represented a template for future sanctions against those who fail to conform to state-sponsored social mores, comparing it to the deplatforming of [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]] on the strength of accusation alone, and read the tactic as a sign of establishment desperation.[1]
