# Ghost Guns

"Ghost guns" is the term applied to firearms assembled by an individual from parts kits that can be purchased online, producing a functional weapon that carries no serial number.[1] Because the resulting firearm is untraceable, it does not generate a transfer record, and kit purchases have not historically required a background check.[1] Critics of the kits argue that these features make them especially attractive to persons legally prohibited from purchasing conventional firearms, and officials in the Biden administration asserted that such weapons had risen sharply in popularity in recent years, particularly among criminals barred from buying ordinary guns.[1]

## Regulatory background

In 2022 the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives]] issued a regulation that broadened the bureau's interpretation of the statutory definition of "firearm" contained in the [[gun-control-act-of-1968|Gun Control Act of 1968]], sweeping the parts kits into the federal licensing, serialization and background-check framework.[1] The rule was described as a central component of President Biden's broader effort to address gun violence.[1]

## Supreme Court order of August 8, 2023

On Tuesday, August 8, 2023, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] temporarily revived the regulation by a vote of 5 to 4, allowing it to remain in effect while a legal challenge continued through the lower courts.[1] Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John G. Roberts Jr.]] and Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]] joined the Court's three liberal members — Justices Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan and Ketanji Brown Jackson — to form the majority.[1] Justices Clarence Thomas, Samuel A. Alito Jr., Neil M. Gorsuch and Brett M. Kavanaugh noted dissents.[1] The order was brief and gave no reasons, which is typical when the justices act on emergency applications, and it was expressly provisional.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that coverage of the order across outlets amounted to "pure hopium for the gun grabbers," because headlines implied that a conservative Supreme Court had ruled 5-4 in favor of disarmament when the order in fact merely permitted what she characterized as unconstitutional and ambiguous regulations to continue during appeal.[1] She contrasted official concern over "ghost guns" with what she described as indifference toward "ghost voters," writing that "tyrants need their fake elections to retain their power" and "also need disarmament to retain it for very long," closing with the exhortation "Keep your guns, America."[1]
