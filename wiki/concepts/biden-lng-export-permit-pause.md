# Biden LNG Export Permit Pause

The Biden LNG export permit pause was a January 2024 decision by the administration of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to delay approval of new liquefied natural gas export terminals in the United States.[1] Framed by the [[white-house|White House]] as a response to climate advocacy, the move was widely read by critics as retaliation against Texas — the world's third largest LNG exporter — for its confrontation with the federal government over the southern border.[1]

## Reaction

Energy analysts argued the pause benefited America's adversaries and harmed its allies.[1] The *Wall Street Journal* editorial board called it "an election-year gift to Russia and Iran."[1] [[american-petroleum-institute|American Petroleum Institute]] chief executive Mike Sommers said in a statement that "there is no review needed to understand the clear benefits of U.S. LNG for stabilizing global energy markets, supporting thousands of American jobs, and reducing emissions around the world by transitioning countries toward cleaner fuels," describing the decision as "nothing more than a broken promise to U.S. allies" and urging the administration to "stop playing politics with global energy security."[1]

Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Europe had turned to the United States to reduce its dependence on Moscow, producing a 141 percent increase in U.S. LNG exports in 2022 over the prior year and a modest further increase in 2023, according to the research firm Kpler; those imports, combined with mild winters, allowed Europe to avert shortages.[1] Rob Thummel, senior portfolio manager at Tortoise, told the *Epoch Times* that "U.S. LNG exports improve global energy security as U.S. natural gas is becoming Europe's primary energy supply source replacing Russia."[1] Texas Land Commissioner [[dawn-buckingham|Dawn Buckingham]] wrote on X that "this reckless move is nothing more than retaliation against Texas — for standing up to this administration over the border crisis."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode through the [[devolution|Devolution]] framework, highlighting *Wall Street Journal* reporting that instead of reversing policies driven by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s protectionist outlook, Biden had advanced many of them — keeping trade barriers in place, excluding European companies from subsidies designed to bolster U.S. manufacturing, and surprising allies with tighter restrictions on Chinese access to American technology.[1] The same reporting described European fears that Trump might abandon Ukraine and [[nato|NATO]] and inject chaos into global trade, and diplomats questioning whether the bloc could still rely on the United States to back the rules-based trading system.[1]

Burning Bright urged a "Bicameral mindset" toward the LNG crisis, noting that the near-term costs — reduced U.S. output and profits, and former buyers turning to Russia and Asia for supply — coexisted with what he saw as an America First signal.[1] Zoomed out, he argued, such policies push Europe and Asia toward multipolarity while forcing the United States to look inward for domestic production and profit, with Biden "rightly taking the Narrative fall."[1]
