# 2025 Los Angeles Immigration Enforcement Protests

The 2025 Los Angeles immigration enforcement protests were a series of demonstrations in the greater Los Angeles area in June 2025 against federal immigration enforcement raids, which prompted an unusual federal military mobilization.[1]

## Federal response

[[united-states-northern-command|U.S. Northern Command]] announced that roughly 2,000 [[california-army-national-guard|California Army National Guard]] soldiers had been placed under federal command to protect federal personnel and property in the greater Los Angeles area.[1] About 300 Guard members from the 78th Infantry Brigade Combat Team were deployed to Los Angeles, Paramount and Compton.[1] Separately, some 500 [[united-states-marine-corps|Marines]] from Twentynine Palms were placed in a prepared-to-deploy status in case they were needed.[1]

Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] said President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was escalating the situation in Los Angeles by threatening the deployment of Marines into the city.[1]

## Curfew, arrests and national spread

By Tuesday, June 10, 2025, a curfew was in place for pockets of downtown Los Angeles where demonstrations had concentrated, running from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Pacific Time.[2] Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] said it could remain in effect for several days; authorities noted it covered roughly one square mile and affected fewer than 100,000 of the city's 4 million residents.[2] The [[lapd|Los Angeles Police Department]] said late that evening it had begun making "mass arrests," with dozens detained for failing to disperse at an unlawful assembly area, and reported that at least 378 people had been arrested over the preceding four days.[2]

Similar protests against [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and the Trump administration spread to New York, Chicago, Seattle, Denver, San Francisco and Atlanta, arriving as ICE raced to meet the [[white-house|White House]]'s immigration arrest quotas.[2] Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] announced that the [[texas-national-guard|Texas National Guard]] would be deployed across the state ahead of planned demonstrations, including a [[no-kings-protests|"No Kings"]] protest in San Antonio.[2]

## Litigation over federalized troops

Newsom sued Trump, Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]], seeking a temporary restraining order against the federalization of the National Guard and the deployment of Marines, with nearly 5,000 troops on the ground in Los Angeles.[2] [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] lawyers called the motion "legally meritless" and warned it would "jeopardize the safety of [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] personnel and interfere with the Federal Government's ability to carry out operations."[2] U.S. District Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] granted the administration's request for additional time, denying Newsom's demanded same-day ruling and setting argument in open court for Thursday at 1:30 p.m. Pacific.[2] Rubber bullets were fired downtown the same day, while Trump addressed Army troops at Fort Bragg, North Carolina.[2]

## Competing official narratives

In a televised address, Newsom condemned what he called Trump's unlawful militarization of Los Angeles, saying, "What we're witnessing is not law enforcement — it's authoritarianism," and "Trump is pulling a military dragnet across Los Angeles. It's weakness masquerading as strength."[2] The [[white-house|White House]] countered that "radical left lunatics" were attacking law enforcement, burning vehicles and shutting down freeways because the administration was removing violent criminal illegal immigrants, naming detainees including Cuong Chanh Phan, Eswin Uriel Castro and Rolando Veneracion-Enriquez.[2]
