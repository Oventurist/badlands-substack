# Future of Work

"Future of work" is the term used inside global corporations and consultancies for the projected restructuring of employment by automation, robotics, and artificial intelligence. In Badlands Media's treatment it is not a forecast but a design: a deliberate program whose consequences — fewer jobs than people — were known to its architects in advance.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## The thesis

Global corporations have been investing in automation, robotics, and artificial intelligence for over a decade, and the displacement visible in 2023 is described as the return on that investment.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) A discussion of the "future of work" has been running in those corporations for almost ten years for a reason: there are going to be fewer jobs than people, and the globalists know this because they designed it.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

[[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]]'s July 2020 publication is offered as evidence, projecting that by 2030 up to 375 million workers — 14 percent of the global workforce — would have to switch occupational categories, a scenario the firm had modelled as early as 2017.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Mechanisms

Software can now replace entire skill sets that once required full-time employees; predictive analytics and generative AI have replaced whole disciplines, reaching output faster and more accurately than humans; and robots assemble where humans once stood.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The optimization of traditional work is not expected to stop, because the transformation is described as full steam ahead.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) In the automotive sector the same forces appear as optimization policies that streamlined and automated manufacturing and global sourcing policies that moved remaining jobs offshore — the process [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described as the auto industry being assassinated.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Consequences for organized labor

Because corporate back offices were streamlined a decade earlier without unionization or strikes — their redundancies celebrated, their workers given severance if they were lucky — the 2023 actions by the [[united-auto-workers|UAW]] and [[writers-guild-of-america|WGA]] represent both a first and a final stand against a transformation already largely complete.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The article concludes that the transformation of society is endgame, that it is here, and that the final stand is likely too late, closing with the ironic advice to "learn to code."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)
