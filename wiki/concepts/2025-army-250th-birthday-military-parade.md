# 2025 Army 250th Birthday Military Parade

The 2025 military parade in Washington, DC was staged to honor the 250th birthday of the [[united-states-army|United States Army]] and coincided with the 79th birthday of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] It followed immediately on the heels of World Pride in the capital, and drew one of the largest security mobilizations the District had seen.[1]

## Security preparations

Matt McCool of the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]]'s Washington Field Office said the agency was "preparing for an enormous turnout."[1] More than 18 miles of "anti-scale fencing" would be erected and "multiple drones" would be in the air, despite the District of Columbia normally being a no-fly zone for drones.[1] A total of 175 magnetometers were to be deployed at security checkpoints controlling access to both the daytime birthday festival and the nighttime parade.[1] Army officials estimated around 200,000 attendees, while McCool said he was prepared for "hundreds of thousands," noting: "We have a ton of magnetometers. If a million people show up, then we're going to have some lines."[1]

## The parade

President [[donald-trump|Trump]] presided over the parade on Saturday, June 14, 2025, celebrating American military power and history, though the event was shadowed by political violence at home — the [[2025-minnesota-lawmaker-shootings|targeted shootings of two Minnesota lawmakers]] that morning — and by escalating tensions abroad amid the [[june-2025-israeli-strikes-on-iran|Israeli strikes on Iran]].[2] "Every other country celebrates their victories," Trump said as he took the lectern after the parade ended. "It's about time America did, too. That's what we're doing tonight."[2]

Though prone to long, boastful speeches and warned beforehand by critics that he would politicize the event, Trump kept his remarks brief and made the military the focus, avoiding overt partisan messaging.[2] He appeared at one point to allude to his "Fight! Fight! Fight!" exhortation following the assassination attempt against him the previous year, telling the crowd: "Time and again, America's enemies have learned that if you threaten the American people, our soldiers are coming for you… They fight, fight, fight and they win, win, win."[2] The parade fell on Flag Day, which is also Trump's birthday, and drew accusations from critics that he was behaving like a king.[2]

Additional [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary described the day as one of "top tier pageantry and plenty of grandeur," cited [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]]'s reading of the parade as a bookend to Trump's 2017 inauguration speech, and noted the symbolic contrast of Trump watching fireworks while Tel Aviv was purportedly under Iranian attack.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump was not taking a premature victory lap because he believed the fight was won, but because he knew the outcome was coming — "on the back of the very deployments and First Principles, America First tactics the enemy is doubling down on opposing."[1] The commentary framed the parade as Trump drawing a line of demarcation between the past and the future, a line the commentator said had actually been drawn when Trump descended the golden escalator years earlier, with the public and the opposition both having already chosen sides.[1] Burning Bright tied the moment to his "Acceleratia" long-form series, describing opponents as entering "the final rings of Acceleratia" and wishing they could go back.[1]

## Veterans Affairs invitation

Ahead of the event, the Department of Veterans Affairs announced that veterans and the public in the National Capital Region were invited to attend a free festival and parade marking 250 years of the [[united-states-army|United States Army]] serving and defending the American people, with a livestream broadcast available nationally.[3] The Army hosted events throughout June in honor of its June 14, 1775 founding, culminating in the national birthday celebration on June 14 in Washington, D.C.[3] The celebration was held on the National Mall and featured a wreath-laying ceremony at Arlington National Cemetery, live music, a parade, fireworks, and appearances by senior military leaders and veterans; at 2:50 p.m. ET citizens across the country were encouraged to join a national moment of recognition reflecting on the Army's legacy.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] remarked that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is infamous for strong rhetoric but that his messaging around the anniversary "might take the cake," adding that if one believes he is part of a much larger plan and that the country faces a multi-dimensional, generational insurgency, his warlike posture "makes all the sense in the world."[3]
