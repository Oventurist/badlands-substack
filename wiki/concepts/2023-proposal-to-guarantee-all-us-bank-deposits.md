# 2023 Proposal to Guarantee All US Bank Deposits

In March 2023, amid the collapse of several American banks, US officials were reported to be studying ways to temporarily expand [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance Corp.]] coverage to all deposits — an estimated $18 trillion — a move sought by a coalition of banks that argued it was needed to head off a potential financial crisis.[1]

## Background

The idea had been pressed publicly by investor [[bill-ackman|Bill Ackman]], who said he continued "to believe that the best course of action is a temporary @FDICgov deposit guarantee until an updated insurance regime is introduced."[1] ZeroHedge noted that Ackman had flip-flopped from his 2022 call that the [[federal-reserve|Fed]] "must hike with shock and awe" to urging a hiking pause.[1] A Bloomberg weekend report had also described US mid-sized banks demanding a two-year total deposit insurance scheme from the FDIC, warning that without it there could be many more forced bank mergers.[1]

## Mechanics under study

According to the Bloomberg report, [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] staff were reviewing whether federal regulators possessed sufficient emergency authority to temporarily insure deposits above the existing $250,000 cap on most accounts without formal consent from a deeply divided [[us-congress|Congress]].[1] Officials did not yet view such a move as necessary, particularly after regulators had already acted that month to help banks meet withdrawal demands, but were said to be developing a strategy out of due diligence in case conditions worsened.[1]

## Commentary

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] treated the caveat that the move was not yet "necessary" as the same rhetorical device used by monetary hawks against a Fed pause, posing the question "what does the Treasury know that we don't," while conceding the likely answer was nothing, since bank crises are non-linear.[1] The outlet also noted its own March 12 tweet had anticipated the development.[1]
