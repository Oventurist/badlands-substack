# 2023 Ukrainian Counteroffensive

The 2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive was Kyiv's attempt to break through Russian defensive lines in southern Ukraine, with the stated aim of reaching the sea and splitting Russian forces in two.[1]

## Equipment losses and tactical shift

According to American and European officials cited by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]], as much as 20 percent of the weaponry Ukraine sent to the battlefield in the first two weeks of the counteroffensive was damaged or destroyed, including Western tanks and armored personnel carriers such as Leopards and Bradleys that Ukrainian forces had been counting on.[1] The rate of losses fell to roughly 10 percent in the following weeks, preserving troops and machines for a major offensive push Ukrainian officials said was still to come.[1]

Part of the improvement came from a change of tactics, with Ukraine focusing more on wearing down Russian forces with artillery and long-range missiles rather than charging into minefields and enemy fire.[1] The Times noted, however, that losses also slowed because the counteroffensive itself had slowed and even halted in places against Russia's formidable defenses; Ukrainian forces had taken only five of the 60 miles they hoped to cover.[1]

## Related developments

Contemporaneous items collected in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] included the US [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] describing the war as "at a bit of a stalemate"; reports that Ukraine was deploying US-supplied cluster munitions, which the US and [[nato|NATO]] consider a war crime; Ukraine's denial of NATO membership and EU funding; [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] escalating rhetoric and seeding World War III as an inevitability; and the apparent reconciliation of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]], which [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read as proof that the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] mutiny had been military deception.[1]

## Narrative framing

Burning Bright argued that the counteroffensive's difficulties were mirrored in Western media ceding narrative ground, and that "the winning side in a PsyWar is not the side that cedes Narrative ground."[1] He characterized the conflict as the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s latest attempt at a forever war, one "spinning out too quickly for sustained profits."[1]

## Russian claims of an early repulse

In early June 2023, the [[russian-defense-ministry|Russian Defense Ministry]] announced that Ukraine had launched a "large-scale offensive" across five sectors of the front in the South Donetsk direction on the morning of June 4, and claimed to have thwarted it within hours.[2] The ministry said the attack cost Ukraine roughly 250 troops killed, six tanks, three infantry vehicles, and 21 armored vehicles, and that Kyiv had committed six mechanized and two tank battalions against Russian-controlled southern Donetsk.[2] "The enemy has failed to reach its goals and was unsuccessful," the statement said, describing the Ukrainian objective as a breach of Russian defenses at what Kyiv assumed was the most vulnerable section of the line.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described Ukraine as having become "both a figurative and literal charnel house," and argued that Western reporting on the war and on the anticipated spring offensive had proved dramatically skewed.[2] Against expectations that the offensive would push Russia back to its original border, the commentary cited Ukrainian losses of 3,715 troops, 52 tanks, 207 armored vehicles, 143 motor vehicles, five aircraft, two helicopters, and 48 pieces of field artillery, with the expectation that losses would worsen.[2] DeLarme concluded that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was "the only person speaking sensibly about this war," namely that it needed to end.[2]
