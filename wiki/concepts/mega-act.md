# MEGA Act (Make Elections Great Again Act)

The MEGA Act, or Make Elections Great Again Act, is federal election legislation unveiled by House Republicans on January 29, 2026.[1] The proposal would establish nationwide standards for federal elections, including voter identification requirements, citizenship verification measures, changes to mail-in voting rules, and restrictions on ballot harvesting.[1]

## Effective dates and criticism

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] identified what he described as a "massive problem" with the bill: the effective date for most of its key provisions is 2027, after the 2026 midterm elections.[1] He cited the text of several sections directly:[1]

- Photo ID for voting: "This section and the amendments made by this section shall apply with respect to elections for Federal office held in 2027 or any succeeding year."[1]
- Computerized statewide voter registration list requirements: "...on and after January 1, 2027."[1]
- Requirements for applicants who register by mail: "...on and after January 1, 2027."[1]
- Receipt, processing, and counting of absentee and mail-in ballots: "This section shall apply with respect to elections for Federal office held in 2027 or any succeeding year."[1]

Herold framed the timing as a uniparty maneuver to "placate you ahead of the midterms with an election integrity solution using something that won't actually matter for the midterms," noting that the bill emerged just as major election-fraud news broke out of [[fulton-county|Fulton County]].[1] His broader skepticism rested on the argument that the officials who benefit from the existing system cannot be expected to reform it.[1]

## Trump endorsement (2026)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] endorsed the Make Elections Great Again Act in February 2026 alongside the [[save-america-act|Save America Act]], as part of his call for Republicans to "take over" and "nationalize" election administration in at least 15 states.[2] In that account the MEGA Act would prohibit ranked-choice voting and universal mail ballots and establish national election audits.[2]
