# Rumble and Trump Media v. Moraes

Rumble and Trump Media v. Moraes was the U.S. litigation in which [[rumble|Rumble]] and [[trump-media-and-technology-group|Trump Media & Technology Group]] challenged censorship directives issued by Brazilian Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]], resolved in the plaintiffs' favor in February 2025.[1]

## Filing and claims

The joint complaint was filed in a U.S. federal court on February 19, 2025.[2] Its immediate trigger was a Moraes order directing Rumble to close the account of the Brazilian conservative journalist [[allan-dos-santos|Allan dos Santos]] and to prohibit him from opening new ones; unlike earlier directives, it required a worldwide ban on his use and monetization of the platform rather than mere geo-blocking inside [[brazil|Brazil]], and allowed only two hours for compliance.[2]

Lawyers for [[trump-media-and-technology-group|Trump Media]] argued that interference with Rumble's operations would necessarily damage Trump Media as well, because [[truth-social|Truth Social]] relies on cloud services provided by Rumble — a dependency that supplied the legal foundation for Trump Media to contest directives aimed at Rumble.[2] The complaint sought a declaration that Moraes's order breaches American law and sovereignty, an order barring [[apple|Apple]] and [[google|Google]] from complying with any Moraes directive to remove Truth Social or Rumble from their app stores, and a jury trial to determine the legitimacy of the companies' allegations.[2]

[[chris-pavlovski|Chris Pavlovski]] called the case "a landmark battle for free speech in the digital age," noting that the U.S. government had already rejected Brazil's extradition request for dos Santos on the ground that the charges were "crimes of opinion."[2] Counsel [[e-martin-de-luca|E. Martin De Luca]] of Boies Schiller framed the suit as an effort to ensure "that no foreign court can unilaterally dictate what speech is allowed on American platforms without proper authorization from the U.S. government."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued the case carried political repercussions at least as significant as its legal ones, since numerous influential figures in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration had long-standing grudges against Moraes and Brazil's censorship system, and predicted the suit would exacerbate dormant tensions between the two governments.[2] See [[censorship-war|Censorship War]].[2]

## The ruling

The U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida confirmed that Moraes's censorship orders have no legal force in the United States, a result Rumble described as "a complete victory for free speech, digital sovereignty, and the right of American companies to operate without foreign judicial interference."[1] The ruling, dated February 25, 2025, held that TMTG and Rumble are under no obligation to comply with a Brazilian Supreme Court Justice's attempt to force the takedown of a U.S.-based user's accounts.[2]

The court explicitly ruled that Moraes's directives had never been properly served under U.S. or international law, finding that they were not delivered through the Hague Convention, the U.S.–Brazil Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty, or any other valid legal mechanism.[1] Consequently, Rumble and Trump Media are under no obligation to comply with the censorship demands, and no U.S. entity is required to enforce them.[1] The court further stated that if anyone attempts to enforce the orders on U.S. soil, it stands ready to intervene to protect American companies and free speech, a message directed at foreign governments seeking to bypass U.S. law to impose censorship on American platforms.[1]

## Reaction

Trump Media chief executive [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]]: "We won't be threatened by any foreign government trying to suppress #FreedomOfSpeech."[1] Badlands writer Ashe in America called the outcome not merely a win for Rumble but "a win for American liberty and US sovereignty," adding the pointed aside to the court that "the Hague is illegitimate."[1] Nunes also said the ruling "confirms that would-be dictators in any country can't force Trump Media or Rumble to censor their opponents," congratulating Rumble on "its principled stand for freedom."[2] Rumble said the case "was never just about Rumble or Trump Media—it was about stopping foreign judges from trying to silence speech in America," and pledged to continue fighting for free speech.[2]
