# 2022 Buffalo Supermarket Shooting

The 2022 Buffalo supermarket shooting was an attack in which a gunman killed ten people at a grocery store in Buffalo, New York. Its legal afterlife became a test case for the liability of social media platforms.[1]

## The attack and the lawsuit

The gunman, [[payton-gendron|Payton Gendron]], was 18 at the time; the [[great-replacement-theory|"white replacement theory"]] was alleged to have motivated the killings.[1] Victims' representatives sued [[meta-platforms|Meta]], [[google|Alphabet]], [[reddit|Reddit]] and [[4chan]], claiming the companies "profit from the racist, antisemitic, and violent material displayed on their platforms to maximize user engagement," including the material Gendron consumed.[1]

On March 18, 2024, Erie County Supreme Court Justice [[paula-feroleto|Paula Feroleto]] denied the defendants' motion to dismiss, ruling that the complaint sufficiently pleaded viable causes of action.[1] Defense counsel had argued the platforms were akin to message boards containing third-party content.[1]

## Commentary

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] warned that success for the plaintiffs would license big tech to crack down harder on ordinary conservative speech, and contrasted the suit with the absence of comparable litigation over social media's role in youth medical transition, citing [[chloe-cole|Chloe Cole]].[1] He framed the matter as an instance of [[lawfare]] unmoored from law itself — "nothing but lawlessness" — while predicting a "boomerang effect" in which public awareness of legal abuse cultivates renewed respect for the law.[1]
