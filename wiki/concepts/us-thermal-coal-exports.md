# US Thermal Coal Exports

**US thermal coal exports** became a point of contention in early 2024 as overseas shipments surged even while domestic utility coal burn fell to its lowest level of the century.[1]

## 2023 figures

US thermal coal exporters recorded more than $5 billion in overseas sales in 2023, shipping upwards of 32.5 million metric tons of the fuel, according to Reuters citing data from ship-tracking firm Kpler.[1] Those earnings were the second highest since 2017, trailing only 2022's $5.7 billion, even as US utility coal usage for electricity generation tumbled to the lowest level this century.[1] Reuters characterized the diverging trends — sliding domestic power-plant use alongside surging exports — as "hypocrisy, given the country's ambitions to become a global leader in energy transition and pollution reduction efforts."[1]

India was the largest destination for US coal shipments in 2023, taking 11.8 million tons, or 36.3% of total US thermal exports, a volume up 130% from 2022.[1] India is the world's largest coal producer and consumer after China, and Reuters reported that it "is expected to remain a keen buyer of international coal as the country's domestic reserves are being depleted and power firms rely on coal for about 75% of India's electricity."[1] The trend coincided with major US companies such as Apple shifting manufacturing supply chains from China to India, a move known as "friendshoring."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the export surge to [[bank-of-america|Bank of America]]'s retreat from its [[esg|ESG]] pledge against financing new coal projects, arguing that rising production and export of "archaic fossil fuels" under the Biden administration ranked among the most promising long-term economic and domestic trends in years and served as a directional proof of [[devolution|Devolution]].[1] He joked that "sometimes, it seems like there are patriot ghosts in the media machine" injecting confirmations into the narrative war, closing with "Winning is everywhere."[1]
