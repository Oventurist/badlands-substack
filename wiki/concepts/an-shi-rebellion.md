# An-Shi Rebellion

The An-Shi Rebellion was a crisis that struck the [[tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] in 755 CE, when the renegade general An Lushan declared himself emperor of the North, threatening both civil war and the dissolution of the newly revived [[silk-road|Silk Road]].[1]

Matthew Ehret presents the rebellion as a test of the era's ecumenical alliance. Caliph al-Mahdi of the [[abbasid-dynasty|Abbasid Dynasty]], grandfather of [[harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]], sent 4,000 Muslim soldiers to aid the Tang emperor in putting the rebellion down, thereby preserving the alliance between Islamic, Confucian, Jewish and Christian powers.[1]

The dynasty nonetheless never recovered its pre-civil-war prestige, and the Silk Road lost valuable vitality precisely as the Christian-Jewish-Muslim alliance was attaining its apex.[1]
