# Iowa State Capitol Satanic Display Controversy

The Iowa State Capitol Satanic display controversy was a December 2023 dispute over a holiday installation placed inside the Iowa State Capitol building by [[satanic-temple|The Satanic Temple]], featuring a Baphomet goat skull and a blood-red pentagram.[1]

## Background and dispute

Residents of Iowa expressed disbelief that the state's governor, [[kim-reynolds|Kim Reynolds]], had allowed the display to stand in a public building during the Christmas season.[1] Reynolds came under pressure to have it removed, with Iowa state representative [[brad-sherman-iowa|Brad Sherman]] leading the effort and describing the installation as "evil" and "disgusting."[1]

Sherman said in a statement that he was calling on the governor to remove the "blasphemous display" immediately "on the grounds that it is unconstitutional and offends God upon whom the State of Iowa depends for blessings," and demanded clarifying legislation prohibiting Satanic displays in the Capitol and on all state-owned property.[1] The episode was reported by [[modernity-news|Modernity News]].[1]

## Commentary

The controversy produced two divergent responses within [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued that the display's defenders were the same actors who had campaigned to tear down statues and monuments in prior years, and that their invocation of church–state separation was selective given the country's Judeo-Christian founding; he read the installation as "gregarious posturing" by a faction backed into a corner.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] took the opposing civil-liberties position, noting that the Temple had obtained a permit and that "freedom of speech is most important for speech you find detestable."[1] While stating that she was personally repulsed by the display, she warned that demanding its removal felt like "a trap to yield our own first amendment rights," and suggested Iowans should instead ask why their society had degraded to the point where open Satanists felt free to practice in the state house.[1] Her conclusion was that the decisive contest was cultural rather than legal: "Good people have tolerated evil for too long, and evil is taking its profits."[1]
