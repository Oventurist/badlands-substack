# Shadow Government

"Shadow government" is the term "A Family Affair" uses for the network the author says operates behind formal U.S. institutions, headed in his account by [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] as chief of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s parallel apparatus.[1]

Its defining property in the article is impunity. [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]] was found to hold millions in [[gulf-cartel|Gulf Cartel]] drug profits yet faced no legal consequence, being "well protected by the shadow government."[1] [[miguel-recarey|Miguel Recarey]], the largest Medicare fraud suspect in U.S. history at the time, was freed by a Spanish court that refused extradition — again attributed to shadow-government protection.[1] Bush himself is called "an untouchable of the highest order," his link to the [[bay-of-pigs-invasion|Bay of Pigs invasion]] never reported by mainstream media.[1]

The network's assets are described as valuable for specific reasons: [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] could be trusted and kept in close contact because he was the director's son, and his fluency in Spanish made him useful across Latin America.[1] Its operational culture is summed up by the family motto the article repeats — "Admit nothing and deny everything" — and by its gloss on the agency's initials, "Criminals In Action."[1]
