# Shadow Government

"Shadow government" is the term Badlands Media uses for an unaccountable governing structure said to have grown out of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s capture of the machinery of American oversight.[1]

## Infiltration of Congress

Drawing on QAnon drop 4164, Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] read the reference to "the Farm" as a reference to the CIA, which was working to elect agency members to Congress in order to take control of oversight and budget authority — thereby neutralising Congress's oversight powers through infiltration and creating a shadow government accountable to no one.[1] Lange noted that [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] had himself been a congressman, and suggested that this helped him persuade Congress to respond to the 1976 investigations with CIA reforms rather than criminal charges — described as Washington's customary way of sweeping matters under the rug.[1]

## Capture of the President's Daily Brief

The second mechanism identified is control of the [[presidents-daily-brief|President's Daily Brief]].[1] During his eleven months as Director of Central Intelligence, Bush secured direct access to President [[gerald-ford|Gerald Ford]] and CIA control over the brief, which elevated the agency within the executive branch and gave it influence over every administration since.[1] Control over the information reaching the president, Lange argued, accelerated the formation of the shadow government, which expanded rapidly while Bush served as [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s vice president.[1]

## Protection of criminal enterprises

Badlands extends the concept to the protection of global criminal networks, asking how [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] could operate what it calls the largest drug trafficking operation in the world for decades without public knowledge, and answering: "They were protected by a shadow government."[1] The same framework covers the CIA's alleged expansion of global drug trafficking through [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]] operating locations and the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war in Laos]].[1]

## Reversal

In the Badlands telling, the shadow government's own instrument was eventually turned against it: the President's Daily Brief that Bush had made "a cabal tool of control" over the presidency was used to expose treason, described as "the perfect boomerang."[1]

## Untouchables and expendables

In "Follow The Money," Joe Lange elaborated the shadow government's internal caste system: operatives fall into "untouchables," who are shielded from prosecution no matter how thoroughly they are exposed, and "expendables," who are sacrificed to public narratives once they become liabilities.[2] [[manuel-noriega]] and [[pablo-escobar]] were expendable — indicted, invaded and hunted so that [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]] could be cast as the hero of the war on drugs — while figures such as [[felix-rodriguez]], [[william-colby]], [[bernie-houghton]], [[paul-helliwell]] and [[michael-jon-hand]] were untouchable.[2] [[frank-nugan]] was expendable; his partner Hand escaped Australia on a false passport and lived for decades under an alias.[2] [[edwin-wilson]] is the borderline case: made expendable when his arms dealing with Libya became public, he served 25 years before FOIA documents proved he had been working for the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[2]

Lange also identified the shield that protects the untouchables. In the [[castle-bank-and-trust]] tax evasion case the CIA argued that prosecution would endanger "national security," and the [[justice-department]] dropped what could have been the largest tax evasion case in history; Lange writes that he repeatedly encounters this excuse wherever illegal CIA operations are exposed.[2] The financial engine of the enterprise, in his telling, is [[money-laundering]] carried out through complicit banks such as the [[nugan-hand-bank]] and by trained operatives such as [[ramon-milian-rodriguez]].[2]

## Exposure through Iran-Contra

In "Shadow Government Exposed," Lange argues that the structure was established and entrenched over many decades and grew so powerful that people inside and outside the country feared the consequences of bucking it — but that there were moments when it appeared vulnerable, the clearest being October 5, 1986, when a plane carrying [[eugene-hasenfus|Eugene Hasenfus]] was shot down over [[nicaragua]].[3] He contends that the resulting [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra scandal]] was not really about illegal arms sales at all: the arms-for-hostages framing was a purposeful "red herring" deployed to conceal the fact that the shadow government had long been operating outside congressional oversight and without presidential authorization, and had been building its own independent funding sources beyond congressional control.[3]

The scandal nonetheless exposed key players, chiefly through phone records.[3] [[felix-rodriguez|Felix Rodriguez]]'s calls on the night of the shootdown went not to the CIA or Defense Department but to [[donald-gregg|Donald Gregg]], national security adviser to Vice President [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]]; repeated calls from a safe house in El Salvador reached the home of [[richard-secord|Richard Secord]]; and the [[southern-air-transport|Southern Air Transport]] aircraft itself was a CIA-owned asset.[3] Lange links Rodriguez, Gregg and [[ted-shackley|Ted Shackley]] as three shadow-government figures with direct ties to Bush, connected to the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war in Laos]] and earlier to the Miami CIA station during the [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]], the Cuban Missile Crisis and the JFK assassination.[3]

## Funding beyond the power of the purse

For Lange the keystone is money: the CIA needed global funding sources of its own in order to eliminate the one constitutional restraint it feared most, the congressional "power of the purse."[3] He cites [[jimmy-carter|Carter]]'s cutoff of funding to Somoza over human rights abuses as the demonstration of that vulnerability, and identifies drug trafficking and the global arms trade as the twin revenue streams — propping up dictators, selling them overpriced weapons, or bribing them with advanced systems as in the 1981 [[awacs-sale-to-saudi-arabia|AWACS sale]] to Saudi Arabia.[3] Congressional investigators later found at least $280 million in Swiss accounts tied to Saudi donations for the Afghan guerrillas and the contras, could not trace the funds, and did nothing.[3]

Lange concludes that the whole Iran-Contra proceeding was a charade — a dog and pony show meant to persuade the public that accountability existed — noting that [[oliver-north|Oliver North]] and [[john-poindexter|John Poindexter]] had their convictions overturned after receiving immunity and that Secord escaped with two years' probation and a $50 fee.[3] He announced that his following article would trace the shadow government's global money laundering and connect it to Al Waleed bin Talal Al Saud.[3]

## The Cold War pretext

In "School of Assassins and Dictators," Joe Lange argues that the shadow government used "battling communism" as its excuse to start proxy wars whose real function was to grow and maintain global drug trafficking and money laundering operations, most of it covert activity hidden from the public and from congressional oversight.[4] He holds that the structure went into overdrive during the Reagan administration but had already been built by [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] before he became vice president, and that [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] himself was largely out of the loop.[4]

The mechanism of capture, on this account, was personnel: after Reagan lost the Iowa caucus in 1980 he replaced campaign director John P. Sears with [[william-casey|William J. Casey]], an [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]] intelligence veteran who brokered Bush's place on the ticket and then took the directorship of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], cementing shadow-government control over the executive branch.[4] The operational arm was the export of the [[phoenix-program|Phoenix Program]]'s assassination and torture methods to Latin America through the [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]], producing dictators and death squads across the cocaine corridor from Colombia to the U.S. border.[4] Lange's summary judgement is that the network cared about the resulting mass murder only when Congress threatened to cut off the money.[4]

## The "shadow game"

In "The Shadow Game," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] describes the [[cia|CIA]] — referred to by Q as the "shadow agency" — as having conducted a "shadow game" of war to create a shadow government that controls the entire country in order to control the world, all outside constitutional restrictions, with [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] at the center of it.[6] Self-preservation is presented as the agency's paramount concern: it will do whatever it takes to prevent public exposure of the truth, including resisting declassification, and will not tolerate any attempt to "buck the system" it has built.[6] To the global cabal of elites, Lange writes, everything is a game in which human life has no value and only wealth and power matter.[6]

[[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] is cast as the president who tried to buck that system — refusing to retain Bush as CIA director on the advice of [[walter-mondale|Walter Mondale]] and the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] reform agenda, ending the [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]] torture manuals, and defunding the [[anastasio-somoza-debayle|Somoza]] regime — and was removed from office through the [[october-surprise|October Surprise]] arrangement with Iran.[6]

Lange also traces the shadow government's consolidation of information control: after Bush placed the CIA in charge of the [[presidents-daily-brief|Presidential Daily Brief]], [[john-poindexter|John Poindexter]] used the [[1983-beirut-barracks-bombings|Beirut barracks bombing]] as the catalyst for a reorganization that routed intelligence from the [[state-department|State Department]], the [[national-security-agency|NSA]], the Defense Intelligence Agency and the [[national-security-council|NSC]] through a CIA-run hotline, extending agency control to the entire executive branch and seeding the surveillance apparatus that became [[total-information-awareness|Total Information Awareness]].[6] In this framing, American troops were pawns meant to be sacrificed for the enrichment of a global cabal, and the [[war-on-terror|war on terror]] was manufactured as the successor threat to communism because, as Q put it, peace does not sell but war and fear do.[6]
