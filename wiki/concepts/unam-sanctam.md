# Unam Sanctam

**Unam Sanctam** is the papal bull issued in 1302 by [[boniface-viii|Pope Boniface VIII]], best known for its declaration that "every human creature [is] subject to the Roman pontiff."[1]

In the Badlands Media essay [[soul-governance|Soul Governance]], the bull is treated as the foundational document of a centuries-long system of legal dominion masked as religious doctrine.[1] The essay argues that Boniface's declaration was not a symbolic or purely spiritual statement but a deliberate legal claim of jurisdiction over persons, and that the [[vatican|Vatican]] thereby "did not merely spread Christianity" but spread a framework of ownership over human beings.[1]

The essay links Unam Sanctam directly to the [[cestui-que-vie-trust|Cestui Que Vie trust]], describing the trust as the operational instrument by which the claim asserted in 1302 was later executed: individuals unable to prove they were alive "with spiritual sovereignty" were presumed lost at sea and treated as vessels, converting persons into cargo under maritime law with the Vatican functioning as the port.[1]

The article further contends that the claim did not lapse with the age of parchment but has been restated in contemporary form through biometric infrastructure — DNA registries, iris scans, neural interfaces, and behavior-prediction models — each checkpoint amounting to a digital restatement of the bull's premise: "you belong to the system unless you prove otherwise."[1] The essay holds that this claim operates only through silence and unexamined consent, and dissolves when the individual withdraws that consent.[1]

These readings of Unam Sanctam are characteristic of sovereign-citizen and related legal-mythology traditions rather than of mainstream canon-law or historical scholarship, which treats the bull as an assertion of papal supremacy over temporal rulers in a specific medieval dispute with Philip IV of France.[1]

## Cited as ruling-class delusion (January 2023)

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]]'s January 20, 2023 essay on [[mass-psychosis]] quotes the papal bull's declaration: "Furthermore, we declare, we proclaim, we define that it is absolutely necessary for salvation that every human creature be subject to the Roman Pontiff."[2] He presented the bull as an example of the delusion, attractive to the power-hungry, that a ruling elite can and should control and dominate an entire society, and stated that it helped inaugurate the conquest of foreign lands under the [[doctrine-of-discovery|Doctrine of Discovery]].[2]
