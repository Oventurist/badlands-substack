# Energy Consolidation

**Energy consolidation** is the term used by Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] to describe what he argues is a deliberate effort to funnel all American energy consumption into a single delivery channel — electricity — so that access to energy can be centrally switched on or off.[1] In a May 15, 2023 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] subtitled "It's About Control," Carlson argued that climate policy is a cover story and that the operative goal is control rather than emissions reduction.[1]

## The argument

Carlson observes that natural gas, gasoline, oil, coal, and nuclear power are being publicly demonized while electricity is promoted as "planet friendly," even though electricity in the United States is still generated largely from those same demonized fuels.[1] The distinction being drawn, in his reading, is not between clean and dirty energy but between direct consumer access to fuel and indirect access mediated by the grid.[1]

From this he infers that the objective is to create "one valve they can turn on or off."[1] If households retain multiple energy options, they retain leverage; if all energy arrives as electricity, prices can be raised without a substitution option, and physical mobility can be curtailed by restricting charging.[1] He offers the analogy of removing all but one emergency exit from a movie theater: consolidation removes redundancy and makes the whole system vulnerable to hackers or terrorists attacking the electrical grid.[1]

## Mobility and lockdown scenarios

Carlson asks readers to imagine the Covid-19 lockdowns in a world where every vehicle was electric: making charging illegal, or shutting charging stations, would confine people to their homes without any need for explicit house-arrest orders.[1] He notes that even high-end EVs achieve roughly 300 miles per charge, limiting how far a driver could travel from home if charging were curtailed.[1] He links this to proposals for "smart cities" in which residents live within a short radius of their daily needs, a theme also associated with [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]].[1]

He further speculates about a Black Mirror-style or Chinese-style [[social-credit-score|social credit score]] regime in which electricity rates vary with a citizen's political conduct: a controversial social media post raising rates, attendance at an anti-government rally disqualifying a person from charging, or a large carbon footprint capping an EV battery at 50 percent.[1]

## Supporting evidence claimed

Carlson's main empirical argument is drawn from stock prices. He reasons that if climate policy were genuinely intended to shift the economy toward electrification, the companies positioned to profit would be rising.[1] Instead, measuring from [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s January 20, 2021 inauguration to the time of writing, he reports [[tesla|Tesla]] down 42 percent, [[nikola|Nikola]] down 96 percent, [[rivian|Rivian]] down 90 percent, [[polestar|Polestar]] down 65 percent, and [[nio|Nio]] down 85 percent.[1] Among the largest US solar companies, he reports [[nextera-energy|NextEra]] down 7 percent, [[avangrid|Avangrid]] down 11 percent, and [[sunpower|SunPower]] down 76 percent — while [[first-solar|First Solar]], the one company he associates with prior political favoritism, was up 108 percent.[1] The pattern, he argues, is illogical on its face — comparable to "selling fewer cold drinks when the weather turns hot" — and therefore evidence that something other than the stated policy rationale is operating.[1]

## Hypocrisy claims

Carlson argues that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] has never cared about the environment, pointing to the exemption of India and China from carbon-reduction obligations imposed on the United States despite their being, in his description, the world's largest polluters.[1] He contends that if climate enforcement were sincere, its advocates would boycott those countries; instead, corporations he describes as Deep State-run manufacture in China precisely because it lacks environmental restrictions.[1] He concludes that carbon taxes, lost jobs, and higher prices deliver no demonstrated environmental benefit and that "when things make no sense, it should always be a red flag that the agenda is about control."[1]

## Related demonization campaigns

The essay treats the sudden vilification of [[gas-stoves|gas stoves]] as a component of the same consolidation program, arguing that gas appliances "became the new aerosol can" overnight and that attributing health harms to them conveniently displaces attention from vaccine injuries.[1]
