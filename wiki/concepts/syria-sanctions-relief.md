# Syria Sanctions Relief

Syria sanctions relief refers to the decision announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on May 13, 2025, that the United States would lift all sanctions on Syria and move to normalize relations with the country's post-Assad government.[1] Trump made the announcement from the Saudi-US Investment Forum in Riyadh during the [[2025-trump-middle-east-tour|2025 Trump Middle East tour]].[1]

## The decision

Trump framed the move as an opportunity for the country: "There is a new government that will hopefully succeed," he said, adding, "I say good luck, Syria. Show us something special," and describing the aim as giving Syria "a chance at peace."[1] He said the rapprochement followed discussions with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]], both of whom had urged the step.[1] The announcement drew a standing ovation in Riyadh, prompting Trump's remark, "Oh, what I do for the crown prince."[1]

Reporting described the shift as dramatic and contested, taken over objections from within Trump's own administration and against the opposition of close ally Israel.[1] Trump was scheduled to meet interim Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] in Riyadh the day after the announcement — a former Al Qaeda commander who helped topple [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in December and who renounced terrorism in 2016.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Trump explicitly cast the sanctions relief as a favor to the crown prince, and added a skeptical caveat about the durability of Syria's new leadership: "I still think this Jolani thing is a rug to be pulled and we will see Assad return as part of a maneuver warfare operation."[1]

## June 2025 executive order

On June 30, 2025, Trump signed an executive order lifting some financial sanctions on Syria, in a move the White House said would help stabilise the country after the ousting of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[2] A White House spokesperson said the order was designed to "terminate the United States' sanctions programme on Syria," cancelling a 2004 declaration that had frozen Syrian government property and limited exports to Syria over Damascus's chemical weapons programme.[2]

Some sanctions remained in force, including those mandated through Congress under the [[caesar-syria-civilian-protection-act|Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act]] of 2019, which targeted funds for reconstruction and natural gas development, as well as the U.S. designation of Syria as a state sponsor of terrorism.[2] The order primarily directed other members of the administration to consider broader sanctions relief, and was seen as potentially providing leverage in talks on normalising Syrian ties with Israel and attracting foreign investment in reconstruction.[2]

Badlands commentary tied the order to the parallel Israeli-Syrian security talks, arguing that lifting sanctions while Israel was pressed to accommodate the former rebel leadership left neoconservative advocates of the anti-Assad campaign without a coherent objection, since regime change had been their stated goal.[2] The same commentary linked the relief to a broader regional trend — a record Saudi arms package and rumoured easing of weapons sanctions on Turkey — that it argued was eroding Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|Qualitative Military Edge]].[2]
