# Russian Foreign Ministry

The Russian Foreign Ministry is the diplomatic arm of the Russian Federation, whose spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] issued Moscow's condemnation of Israeli operations in [[lebanon|Lebanon]] in September 2024.[1]

## Statement on Israeli strikes in Lebanon

In a commentary reported by TASS, Zakharova said Moscow "strongly condemns" Israel's large-scale military attacks against Lebanon and described the escalation as deeply worrying.[1] She noted that on September 23, 2024, the Israeli leadership had announced that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] launched an offensive operation dubbed "Northern Arrows," aimed at undermining the military infrastructure of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], with the Israeli Air Force carrying out massive airstrikes across Lebanon against dozens of settlements in the south and east; Hezbollah in turn intensified rocket attacks on military facilities in Israel.[1]

The ministry stressed a "principled position on the inadmissibility of indiscriminate attacks that target civilians" and called for an immediate cessation of hostilities to prevent further bloodshed and create conditions for a political and diplomatic settlement.[1] Zakharova warned that everything possible must be done to prevent the Middle East from plunging into a full-scale armed conflict whose consequences would affect the region and beyond, and said Russia was ready to coordinate efforts with international and regional partners to avert such a scenario, proceeding from the principle that the security of any state in the region should not be ensured at the expense of others.[1]

## Badlands Media commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted the ministry's posture favorably with that of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], writing that Russia appeared to be "the adult in the room," and restated his speculation that Russia might lead any offensive action taken by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] against Israel, describing Benjamin Netanyahu as "a cornered animal."[1]

## Russia–Iran treaty proposal, September 2024

In September 2024 the Russian Foreign Ministry submitted to President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] a proposal to sign the [[comprehensive-strategic-partnership-treaty|Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty]] with the Islamic Republic of [[iran|Iran]], a proposal the ministry had coordinated with interested federal government agencies and organizations.[2] Putin accepted it by decree published on the official website for legal information, directing that signing occur "at the highest level" and authorizing the ministry to make changes to the draft during negotiations that did not alter its general points.[2] The treaty itself had been finalized in June 2024 after about two and a half years of editorial work, with the ministry having publicly reaffirmed commitment to the strategic partnership earlier that year.[2]

## 2025 repudiation of the INF moratorium

In August 2025 the Russian Foreign Ministry issued a statement declaring that the conditions for maintaining the 1987 [[intermediate-range-nuclear-forces-treaty|Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty]] with the United States had "disappeared" and that Moscow "no longer considers itself bound" by it.[3] The ministry stated that it noted "the disappearance of conditions for maintaining the unilateral moratorium on the deployment of similar weapons" and was authorized to declare that Russia no longer considered itself bound by the corresponding self-imposed restrictions previously adopted.[3] The treaty, which banned ground-launched missiles with ranges of 500 to 5,500 kilometres, had collapsed in 2019 when Washington withdrew citing Russian violations, claims Moscow denied while accusing the United States of developing banned missiles itself.[3]
