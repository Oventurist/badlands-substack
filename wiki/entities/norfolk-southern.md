# Norfolk Southern

Norfolk Southern is a major American freight railroad company, known publicly since 2023 chiefly for the derailment of one of its trains in East Palestine, Ohio.[1]

## East Palestine derailment

On February 3, 2023, a Norfolk Southern freight train derailed near the Ohio–Pennsylvania border in the [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine train derailment]].[1] Thirty-eight cars left the tracks, twenty carrying hazardous materials including vinyl chloride, ethylene glycol, ethylhexyl acrylate, butyl acrylate and isobutylene.[1] Responders vented and burned vinyl chloride from some tank cars to avert a catastrophic explosion, and residents within about one mile were evacuated.[1]

The [[national-transportation-safety-board|National Transportation Safety Board]] and [[federal-railroad-administration|Federal Railroad Administration]] attributed the derailment to a failed and overheated wheel bearing on a railcar.[1]

## Litigation

Although no fatalities were recorded at the time, lawsuits filed in February 2025 alleged that at least seven people, including a one-week-old infant, died from exposure to toxic chemicals released by the derailment and its aftermath; those claims remain allegations in civil suits.[1]

## Safety record after East Palestine

Norfolk Southern operated the train and owned the track through East Palestine, Ohio, where a freight consist carrying hazardous chemicals derailed in February 2023 after an overheated wheel bearing failed on the 1.75-mile-long train.[2] In the year that followed, derailments rose across the top five freight railroads according to regulatory reports covering the first ten months of 2023, and the specific wheel-bearing failure mode increased steeply; Norfolk Southern was the only one of the five major carriers to report a decline in accidents over the period.[2] No federal rail-safety legislation was enacted in response, as the [[railway-safety-act-of-2023|Railway Safety Act]] stalled amid lobbying resistance.[2]

## Corporate response to East Palestine

Chief executive [[alan-shaw|Alan Shaw]] published a letter in February 2023 promising, "We will not walk away, East Palestine," but company officials had already declined to attend a town hall meeting with residents seeking answers about the derailment.[3] The train involved carried 150 cars, twenty of them loaded with hazardous materials, and the resulting chemical disaster and controlled burn of vinyl chloride generated increasing reports of short-term health impacts among residents and concern as far away as Pittsburgh.[3] Commentators observed that the railway was "not off to a great start" in handling the crisis, and a class action lawsuit alleged that residents might already be undergoing DNA mutations.[3] Critics within the Truth Community argued that industry-wide policy changes had made such derailments effectively inevitable.[3]

## Contribution trial over the East Palestine settlement (2025)

In the first week of April 2025, trial began on Norfolk Southern's effort to recover part of the $600 million class-action settlement it had accepted over the 2023 [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine, Ohio derailment]].[4] The railroad sought contributions from two businesses it argued shared responsibility: [[gatx-corporation|GATX Corp.]], a railcar lessor, and [[oxy-vinyls|Oxy Vinyls]], a chemical manufacturer.[4] The settlement, plus a 27 percent attorney fee, had been approved the previous year by US District Judge [[benita-pearson|Benita Pearson]], who also presided over the contribution trial.[4] A verdict for Norfolk Southern would not change the size of the compensation fund — the railroad remains liable for the payout — but would shift part of the cost onto the two firms.[4]
