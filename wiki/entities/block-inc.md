# Block, Inc.

Block, Inc. is the payments and financial technology company co-founded by [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]] as [[square-inc|Square, Inc.]] and later renamed Block.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents Block as one of the two principal vehicles — alongside [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s plans for [[twitter|Twitter]] — through which the banking system is being decentralized and financial power returned to individuals.[1]

## Business lines

According to Lange, Block's offerings include Square, a card-reader app for small businesses; Square Suite, a set of services allowing small businesses to manage finances and operations; [[cash-app|Cash App]], which lets consumers invest, spend, send or store money; a [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] portfolio comprising a hardware wallet, a mining business, the open-source developer effort TBD and an independent Bitcoin-focused business called Spiral; and Tidal, which helps musicians and artists control and monetize their own intellectual property using NFTs.[1]

Lange quotes Data Driven Investor's assessment that Dorsey "fully utilizes the platforms he has built — Twitter and Square to utilize Bitcoin's potential fully," and that this is what differentiates Square from other payment platforms such as Visa and [[paypal|PayPal]]: Twitter encourages communication, Square facilitates payments, and Bitcoin is the engine, so that anyone on the planet can earn money for their art through NFTs.[1] What began as a simple dongle, the Square Reader, is described as having become an entire ecosystem connecting and enabling people worldwide.[1]

## Lending and financial inclusion

Block has also moved into non-profit and community lending. Citing the Irish *Independent*, Lange reports that Block was looking to invest in a not-for-profit lender or social enterprise in Ireland, having earlier put £2m (€2.3m) into ART Business Loans, a Birmingham-based community development finance institution that lends £10,000–£150,000 to businesses unable to meet banks' full requirements.[1] Lange frames this as evidence that Dorsey "is on a mission to compete with banks" and to advance [[financial-inclusion|financial inclusion]].[1]

Like Musk, Dorsey open-sources Block's technology, which Lange attributes to a shared desire to invite other engineers to improve services and advance technology for the benefit of humanity.[1]
