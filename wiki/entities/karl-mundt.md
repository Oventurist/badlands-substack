# Karl E. Mundt

Karl E. Mundt was a Republican member of the United States [[congress|Congress]] from South Dakota and the original sponsor of the legislation that became the [[smith-mundt-act|Smith-Mundt Act]].[1] He first introduced the U.S. Information and Educational Exchange Act in January 1945 during the 79th Congress; the bill was subsequently passed by the 80th Congress and signed into law by President [[harry-truman|Harry S. Truman]] on January 27, 1948, as Public Law 80-402.[1]

The statute that carries Mundt's name regulated the broadcasting of programs for foreign audiences produced under the guidance of the [[state-department|State Department]], and prohibited the domestic dissemination of materials produced by such programs.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treats Mundt's law as the postwar firewall that kept the machinery of [[psychological-operations|psychological operations]] developed during the Second World War pointed outward rather than at Americans.[1]
