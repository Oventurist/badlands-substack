# Isidor Straus

Isidor Straus was the owner of Macy's and one of several prominent men who died aboard the [[rms-titanic|Titanic]] in 1912, one year prior to the signing of the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article notes that it has long been suggested that Straus and his fellow victims — American millionaire John Jacob Astor IV, industrialist Benjamin Guggenheim, and journalist and social reformer William Thomas Stead — all opposed the Federal Reserve Act to various degrees.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Straus in particular wrote an article published by the New York Times on October 25, 1911, titled "The Question of Reserves," in which he argued for the recognition of the Central Association's notes as reserve money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The article describes Straus as an opposing figure to the [[cabal]] because he was a Director of the Mutual Alliance Trust Company, one of its founding thirteen directors, a group that also included Emanuel Lehman, William Rockefeller, and Cornelius Vanderbilt.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)
