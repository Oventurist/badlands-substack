# George H. Simmons

**George H. Simmons** was the physician who in 1899 began a twenty-five-year tenure as head of the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]].[1]

## Reorganization of the AMA

At the time Simmons took charge, the AMA was a weak organization with little money and little respect from the general public. Chiropractic had just entered the mainstream, homeopathy was thriving, herbalists were flourishing, and conventional physicians struggled to profit from their practices while state governments remained reluctant to restrict the various healing arts.[1]

To change this, Simmons hired [[joseph-n-mccormack|Joseph McCormack]], secretary of the Kentucky State Board of Health, to "rouse the profession to lobby." Under McCormack's direction the AMA expanded its membership while preaching a code of ethics — which included not competing with other physicians and not publishing prices — and denouncing as "quackery" anything that competed with regular medicine.[1]

## Council on Medical Education

In 1904 Simmons had the AMA establish a Council on Medical Education, whose stated mission was to upgrade medical education. In practice, according to Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], the council devised a plan to rank the country's medical schools under questionable guidelines. The AMA ran out of money before the ranking project could be completed in 1910, and the Rockefeller and Carnegie foundations financed its completion — the work that became the [[flexner-report|Flexner Report]] and finished the restructuring Simmons had started.[1]
