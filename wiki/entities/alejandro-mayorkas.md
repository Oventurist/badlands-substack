# Alejandro Mayorkas

Alejandro Mayorkas served as Secretary of Homeland Security in the administration of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

In September 2024 Mayorkas designated the January 6, 2025 congressional certification of the presidential vote as a "national special security event," the first time the federal government had applied that status to the certification process.[1] The designation placed the [[congress|Congress]]ional session on the same security footing as events such as the Super Bowl or the U.N. General Assembly, and authorized measures intended to prevent a repeat of the 2021 Capitol riot by supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] seeking to overturn the 2020 election result.[1]

Eric Ranaghan, special agent in charge of the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]]'s Dignitary Protective Division, said in the announcement that "National Special Security Events are events of the highest national significance" and that the Service and its federal, state, and local partners would implement a comprehensive integrated security plan.[1] Reporting on the designation framed it as an acknowledgment that a once-routine democratic procedure now carried special risk amid a close race between Trump and Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[1]

## Impeachment effort

Mayorkas was the target of a sustained Republican campaign for removal over the Biden administration's border policies, culminating in the January 2024 [[impeachment-of-alejandro-mayorkas|release of two articles of impeachment]] accusing him of "willful and systemic refusal to comply with the law" and "breach of public trust."[2] House Homeland Security Chairman [[mark-green|Mark Green]] alleged that Mayorkas had willfully and systemically refused to comply with immigration laws enacted by Congress, knowingly made false statements to Congress and the American people, and obstructed congressional oversight of his department.[2] Mayorkas denied wrongdoing, and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] maintained that no high crimes or misdemeanors had been committed under the Biden administration, dismissing the House inquiry in a memo as unconstitutional and "evidence-free."[2] He had long been a focus of Republican criticism of White House border policy and had testified before Congress multiple times.[2]

## 2023 border wall waiver

In early October 2023 Mayorkas opted to waive a litany of laws and regulations in order to swiftly construct more border wall in Starr County, Texas, a key hotspot for illegal crossings — the action known as the [[2023-starr-county-border-wall-waiver|2023 Starr County border wall waiver]].[3] A notice posted to the U.S. Federal Register stated that the Secretary had determined it necessary to waive certain laws, regulations, and other legal requirements to ensure the expeditious construction of barriers and roads near the international land border there.[3] Citing the [[illegal-immigration-reform-and-immigrant-responsibility-act|Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act]] of 1996, Mayorkas noted that Congress had granted the Secretary authority to waive all legal requirements he, in his sole discretion, determines necessary for such construction, and he planned to waive 26 federal laws in total.[3] The notice cited 245,000 migrant encounters over the prior year in the Rio Grande Valley Sector and found an "acute and immediate need" for physical barriers and roads to prevent unlawful entries, against a backdrop of more than 7 million encounters nationwide since President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took office.[3]

## Impeachment (February 2024)

On the night of Tuesday, February 13, 2024, the Republican-led House narrowly impeached Mayorkas over what members described as his failure to enforce border laws amid a crisis of high illegal immigration, an accusation he called "baseless."[4] He became only the second Cabinet officer impeached in the history of the republic, after Secretary of War William Belknap in 1876, and the vote followed a failed attempt on identical charges the week before.[4] Critics in both parties argued the articles rested on policy disagreement rather than specific crimes, and the [[united-states-senate|Senate]]'s Democratic majority was expected to reject them.[4] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said afterward that Mayorkas had fueled "the worst border catastrophe in American history."[4] The episode is treated in detail at [[impeachment-of-alejandro-mayorkas|the impeachment of Alejandro Mayorkas]].[4]

## FEMA funding warning (October 2024)

On October 2, 2024, speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One, Mayorkas warned that the [[fema|Federal Emergency Management Agency]] could meet immediate needs but did "not have the funds to make it through the season," with hurricane season running to November 30 and another storm expected.[5] The agency was stretched assessing damage from [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]], which killed more than 160 people across the Southeast, and distributing meals, water and generators.[5] Mayorkas declined to specify how much additional money FEMA required, but his remarks reinforced calls from President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and lawmakers for a supplemental spending bill from [[congress|Congress]].[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the warning as an admission of cascading policy failure, tying the shortfall to border mismanagement and the diversion of disaster-relief money.[5]

## February 2024 impeachment and the ICE budget crisis

On Tuesday, February 13, 2024, House Republicans voted to impeach Mayorkas over his border record, the second such vote after an initial failure.[6] The impeachment coincided with reporting that [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] had drafted plans to release thousands of detainees and cut detention capacity from 38,000 beds to 22,000 following the collapse of a Senate border bill that would have closed a $700 million shortfall.[6] Immigration remained President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s worst-rated issue in polling at the time.[6] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] situated the impeachment alongside the ICE budget story as part of a broader narrative struggle over domestic versus foreign defense funding.[6] The proceedings are covered at [[impeachment-of-alejandro-mayorkas|the impeachment of Alejandro Mayorkas]].[6]

## 2024 Shelter and Services Program announcement

As Secretary of Homeland Security, Mayorkas announced on August 28, 2024 that the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] would distribute more than $380 million in Shelter and Services Program grants to sanctuary cities, state and local governments, and NGOs supporting migrants released from DHS custody pending immigration proceedings.[7] Badlands Media described the disbursement as a taxpayer-funded boondoggle rewarding organizations engaged in resettlement.[7]

## 2024 Shelter and Services Program announcement

As Secretary of Homeland Security, Mayorkas announced on August 28, 2024 that the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] would distribute more than $380 million in Shelter and Services Program grants to sanctuary cities, state and local governments, and NGOs supporting migrants released from DHS custody pending immigration proceedings.[7] Badlands Media described the disbursement as a taxpayer-funded boondoggle rewarding organizations engaged in resettlement.[7]

## Warnings on disaster funding (October 2024)

Remarks by Mayorkas, alongside those of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and insurance analysts, were cited in October 2024 as evidence that the main federal disaster programs — [[fema|FEMA]]'s disaster relief fund and flood insurance program and [[small-business-administration|Small Business Administration]] disaster loans — could be within weeks of running out of cash as [[hurricane-milton|Hurricane Milton]] bore down on Florida following [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]].[8]

## Impeachment timing and the Texas border fight (January 2024)

Badlands commentary on the January 2024 [[eagle-pass-border-standoff|Eagle Pass standoff]] highlighted that Mayorkas was set to undergo impeachment proceedings the week following January 24, 2024, with the Texas-federal border litigation scheduled to follow on February 7 — a convergence [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] flagged with the remark "Such timing."[9]

## Committee approval of impeachment articles

Early on Wednesday, January 31, 2024, the Republican-led [[house-homeland-security-committee|House Homeland Security Committee]] approved two articles of impeachment against Mayorkas for having "willfully and systematically" flouted federal immigration laws and breached the public trust.[10] The 18–15 party-line vote affirmed that Mayorkas failed to detain migrants crossing the border before deciding whether to grant asylum.[10] The articles also stated that he ignored records requests from the committee and misled [[congress|Congress]] about having "operational control" of the border and about his claim that it is "secure."[10] The hearing ran more than fifteen hours and included repeated procedural attempts by Democrats to derail the effort, while Republicans said their year-long investigation had established that Mayorkas violated his oath of office.[10]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the vote as one element in a cascade of border narratives converging in early 2024, alongside the Eagle Pass controversy and a Texas appeal before the Fifth Circuit over federal authority at the border, arguing the sequencing was too well-timed to be coincidence.[10]

## July 2024 assassination-attempt accountability

Badlands' July 2024 coverage grouped Mayorkas's handling of questions about the July 13 attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]]'s resignation and law enforcement's evasive congressional testimony, characterizing his contribution as "deflection" within a broader pattern of institutional non-answers.[11] The Department of Homeland Security, which he led, declined to appear before the [[house-homeland-security-committee|House Homeland Security Committee]] on July 23, 2024.[11]
