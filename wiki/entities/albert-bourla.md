# Albert Bourla

Albert Bourla is the Chief Executive Officer of [[pfizer|Pfizer]], the pharmaceutical company whose COVID-19 vaccine and antiviral treatment generated an enormous revenue windfall during the pandemic before falling sharply as demand collapsed.[1]

## 2024 guidance reset

On December 13, 2023, Bourla led Pfizer's reset of its 2024 expectations, forecasting sales that could come in as much as $5 billion below Wall Street estimates and driving the company's shares to a ten-year low.[1] Revenue from Pfizer's COVID-19 vaccine and treatment, which peaked at $57 billion in 2022, was projected at $8 billion for 2024 — below both the $13 billion analyst consensus and the company's own lowered $12.5 billion view for 2023.[1]

Bourla framed the downgrade as an exercise in credibility rather than pessimism. "We want to be conservative," he told investors on a conference call. "We want to be reliable so we won't create uncertainty (again), which was the case, unfortunately, this year."[1] The remark was an implicit acknowledgment that Pfizer's earlier 2023 guidance had proven unreliable.[1]

## Strategic context

Under Bourla, Pfizer deployed part of its COVID windfall into acquisitions, including a $43 billion purchase of the cancer drugmaker Seagen that was expected to close the same week as the guidance reset, and launched a new RSV vaccine whose rollout trailed a rival product.[1] Pfizer shares had fallen 44% over the course of 2023, and U.S. COVID vaccination uptake dropped sharply, with only about 17% of the eligible population receiving the most recent updated booster amid declining concern about the virus and vaccine fatigue.[1]
