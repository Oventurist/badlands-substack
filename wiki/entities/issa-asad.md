# Issa Asad

Issa Asad, 51, of Southwest Ranches, Florida, is the founder and chief executive of [[q-link-wireless|Q Link Wireless LLC]], a Dania Beach telecommunications company sentenced in July 2025 for a years-long scheme to defraud federal programs.[1]

## Federal conviction and sentence (July 2025)

Asad and Q Link Wireless were sentenced for conspiring to defraud and commit offenses against the United States in connection with a scheme to steal more than $100 million from a federal program providing discounted phone service to people in need — the [[lifeline-program|Lifeline program]], colloquially known as the "Obama phone" program.[1] Asad was also sentenced for laundering money obtained from a separate scheme defrauding a federal program intended to aid individuals and businesses harmed by the Covid-19 pandemic.[1]

Both defendants were sentenced on Count 1 of an Information charging them with conspiring to commit wire fraud and to steal government money, and with conspiring to defraud the United States; Asad was additionally sentenced on Count 2, charging money laundering.[1] He received a sentence of 60 months' imprisonment — five years.[1]

Q Link agreed to a forfeiture money judgment of more than $109 million, and Asad and the company jointly agreed to pay $109,637,057 in restitution to the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]].[1] Asad separately paid $1,758,339.25 in restitution to the [[small-business-administration|United States Small Business Administration]] and a forfeiture judgment against him of $17,484,118.00, bringing total financial penalties and restitution to over $128 million.[1]

## Badlands commentary and prior criminal history

Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] characterized the case as "MASSIVE Obama Phone fraud," noting that according to the Federal Register, Q Link Wireless made $618,736,494 from the Lifeline program between 2013 and 2019.[1] CannCon wrote that Asad and his company stole over $109,000,000 from the government through fraudulent billing and then fabricated records to conceal it once he realized he was under investigation, and that at least $50 million of his funds were sent to bank accounts controlled by his family in Jordan.[1]

The account added that this was not Asad's first encounter with law enforcement.[1] In 2014, Asad deliberately ran a man over in a parking lot in Dania Beach, Florida, over $65 the victim claimed he was owed for completed work; the victim, Mike Kramer, died of his injuries after several days in a coma.[1] Asad was originally charged with murder, but in 2019 he pleaded nolo contendere to misdemeanor culpable negligence and was ordered to pay a $225 fine and serve one year of probation.[1]
