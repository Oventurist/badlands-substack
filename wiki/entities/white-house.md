# The White House

In the article the White House functions less as a building than as the bottleneck in the military chain of command under President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Citing Q drop 2640, the article states that Obama placed restrictions on U.S. military command such that direct White House approval was required for each battlefield action.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) It characterizes this arrangement as effectively a [[stand-down-orders|stand down order]] protecting [[isis|ISIS]] and Al Qaeda, arguing the micromanagement existed to protect weapons sales and money laundering.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], running the drone program from the White House, is identified as the official who had to approve battlefield action and thus sat atop that chain.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) By contrast, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is said to have removed the White House from the battlefield chain of command, after which ISIS was destroyed in under a year.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

On the night of September 11, 2012, the President, Secretary of Defense, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and National Security Advisor convened at the White House by 5 p.m., where Obama directed that all resources be used to save lives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) A "Deputies Meeting" was then convened by the White House at 7:30 p.m. with [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and deputies from State, the Pentagon and the White House — the President and Secretary of Defense both absent.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Former CIA officers had for months tried to warn the White House that thousands of shoulder-fired missiles were reaching Al Qaeda, but their warnings fell on deaf ears.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article also claims Obama filled the White House with people who "hate America."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## PCAST appointments (March 2026)

On March 25, 2026 the White House announced new appointments to the [[presidents-council-of-advisors-on-science-and-technology|President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology]], a federal advisory body that provides the president with independent advice on science, technology, and innovation policy.[2] The council drew leaders from academia, industry, and research institutions, and appointees were tasked with advising on artificial intelligence, national security technology, economic competitiveness, and scientific research priorities.[2] Named appointees included [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]], Sergey Brin, Safra Catz, Michael Dell, Jacob DeWitte, Fred Ehrsam, [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], David Friedberg, Jensen Huang, John Martinis, Bob Mumgaard, Lisa Su, and [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].[2]

## Coverage of the 2026 election and Epstein stories

White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] addressed media coverage of the [[fbi|FBI]]'s seizure of 2020 election materials from [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], noting contrasts with prior reporting on claims of Russian election interference.[3] Separately, the White House continued to express support for Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] amid the February 2026 Epstein document release, dismissing demands for his resignation as absurd — a position shared by House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[3]


## 2026 Doha announcement
The White House announced in June 2026 that Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] would travel to Doha, [[qatar|Qatar]], for discussions aimed at advancing implementation of the US-[[iran|Iran]] memorandum of understanding.[4] The announcement followed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s statement that Iran had requested the meeting, a characterization Tehran publicly disputed.[4]

## March 2026: ABC News retraction demand

In March 2026 the White House publicly disputed [[abc-news|ABC News]] reporting that the [[fbi|FBI]] had warned of a potential Iranian drone attack on California in retaliation for US actions.[5] Press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the report was inaccurate and demanded a correction or retraction, arguing that the reporting misrepresented intelligence about potential Iranian retaliation.[5] No attack occurred, and officials said threat monitoring continued.[5] Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that the official White House response was consistent with his theory that a false-flag attack intended to be blamed on [[iran|Iran]] had been pre-empted, citing [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s statement that the American pre-emptive strike was meant to pre-empt an Israeli pre-emptive strike and so avoid a "9/11-style invasion dialectic."[5]

## Election integrity portal and the Netanyahu meeting dispute (July 2026)
On Thursday, July 16, 2026 the White House launched the [[white-house-election-integrity-portal|White House Election Integrity Portal]], publishing declassified intelligence assessments, investigative materials and other documents on election security, voter registration and foreign threats.[6] The administration said the documents showed Intelligence Community officials had known of vulnerabilities in electronic voting infrastructure, foreign cyber capabilities targeting election systems and blackmail schemes while withholding that information from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the public.[6]

The same week, senior White House officials told Axios that no meeting with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had been scheduled, with one official saying Israeli reports appeared to be an attempt to "will the meeting into existence"; the White House did not announce any rescheduled meeting after Netanyahu's office canceled the trip.[6] Badlands commentary argued that White House staff had deliberately let the press know of Netanyahu's second attempt to force a meeting.[6] Separately, Deputy Chief of Staff [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] announced an "action phase" in the administration's [[global-crackdown-on-far-left-political-terrorism|global crackdown on far-left political terrorism]].[6]

## June 2026 disavowals

Two separate White House sources confirmed to [[axios|Axios]] reporter [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] that the United States played no part in — and gave no blessing to — the Israeli strike on Beirut that preceded [[iran|Iran]]'s June 8, 2026 missile barrage against [[israel|Israel]].[7] A U.S. defense official likewise stated that American forces did not participate in the Israeli retaliatory strikes on Iranian territory.[7]

Separately, a White House official told NBC News that "the entire story is false" regarding reports that the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] had raised Israeli espionage to a "critical" threat level, even as the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] declined to comment.[7]

## Hosting UFC Freedom 250 (June 2026)

On June 14, 2026 the White House hosted "[[ufc-freedom-250|UFC Freedom 250]]" on the South Lawn, marking the nation's 250th anniversary and coinciding with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 80th birthday.[8] The mixed martial arts event was attended by Trump, Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], other administration officials, and thousands of guests, and proceeded after a federal judge declined to block it in response to a lawsuit filed by two Virginia residents challenging the government's decision to host the fights at the executive mansion.[8] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the event made young Americans pay attention to the White House and feel pride in the country "for maybe the first time ever."[8]

## 2020 election document task force (July 2026)

A White House task force has been gathering documents related to the 2020 presidential election for declassification from the [[cia|CIA]], the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], the [[fbi|FBI]], and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].[9] Operating in addition to ongoing Justice Department probes, the task force comprises *Just The News* journalist [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] and former [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration officials including former national security aide [[derek-harvey|Derek Harvey]], with lawyers reviewing the material for months and members encouraged to limit redactions.[9] The initiative involves thousands of pages, focused on ballot irregularities and potential voting machine issues, and Trump said acting DNI [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]] is authorized to "declassify almost everything."[9]

## Response to Machado's attempted return (July 2026)

A senior White House official described Venezuelan opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]]'s efforts to return to [[venezuela|Venezuela]] after the [[2026-venezuela-earthquakes|2026 earthquakes]] as "grotesque political opportunism."[9] Reports indicated that US officials support her eventual return but believe doing so during the humanitarian crisis could undermine relief operations, with the administration's stated priority being earthquake response including search-and-rescue and humanitarian assistance.[9]

## The "WTF" message to Israel (March 2026)

In March 2026 the White House was reportedly blindsided when [[israel|Israeli]] forces struck 30 fuel depots inside [[iran|Iran]] without notifying Washington in advance.[10] Officials were concerned that the strikes targeted infrastructure serving Iranian civilians, which they expected to cause unnecessary backlash while driving up the global price of oil.[10] According to reporting by [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] and [[marc-caputo|Marc Caputo]] in [[axios|Axios]], the precise message sent from the White House to its Israeli counterparts upon learning of the strike was, "WTF."[10]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the reporting tracked logically, since President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had repeatedly said he wanted oil flowing through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] — even lifting sanctions on Russian oil to that end — and had alluded to taking control of Iran's oil to stabilize global markets.[10] Destroying Iranian oil supplies, he wrote, would be counterintuitive to that objective and psychologically damaging among the very population Trump was trying to win over: "unnecessarily cruel and not at all strategic."[10]

## Quantum announcement and "Q" posts (June 2026)

In June 2026, the White House [[office-of-science-and-technology-policy|Office of Science and Technology Policy]] announced a national effort to develop a quantum computer capable of ushering in what it called the "era of quantum-enabled intelligence," framing [[quantum-computing|quantum computing]] as a strategic priority alongside artificial intelligence and biotechnology under the administration's "Golden Age of Innovation" theme.[11] Separately, two posts from the official White House social media account sparked extensive online activity about "Q" messaging, which commentators variously read as deliberate signaling or as shitpost-style marketing.[11]

## Kirby "handful of vets" email (September 2024)

On the anniversary of 9/11 in September 2024, [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] communications adviser [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] dismissed the concerns of veterans critical of the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's 2021 [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] withdrawal in a "reply all" email that inadvertently included [[fox-news|Fox News]] Digital: "Obviously no use in responding. A 'handful' of vets indeed and all of one stripe."[12] Fox had asked the White House for comment on criticism from four veterans, including Rep. [[cory-mills|Cory Mills]], R-Fla., who accused Kirby of providing "cover" for the administration; the chain was forwarded to White House staffers on the National Security Council before Kirby replied to all.[12]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode revealed the administration's private sentiment toward veterans and that, because Kirby speaks for the administration, the insult should attach to Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[12]

## Speaker system and the "Phantom of the Opera" incident (2025)

In November 2025 a newly installed speaker system at the White House became a news story in its own right.[13] On Friday, November 14, with nothing on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s public schedule except a trip to Mar-a-Lago, Politico correspondent Jake Taylor reported that music from "The Phantom of the Opera" was playing so loudly it could be heard outside the building; AFP correspondent Danny Kemp later reported hearing James Brown's "It's a Man's Man's Man's World."[13] Trump is described as a notorious fan of Broadway show tunes who enjoys playing DJ at events.[13]

Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] had recently told reporters on Trump's behalf that he was "testing the speakers for what will be the best event in the history of the White House," and said he could control the music from an iPad in the Oval Office.[13] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] interpreted the episode as narrative signaling tied to Bruce Wayne and the League of Shadows, while [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] joked about Trump taking song requests from the press pool.[13]

## Middle East de-escalation diplomacy (August 2024)

In August 2024, White House officials believed that intensive diplomatic efforts to temper [[iran|Iran]]'s retaliation for the killing of [[hamas|Hamas]] leader [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] in Tehran might be having an effect, according to Washington Post reporting, which also said the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration was enraged by the timing of the assassination.[14] The effort formed part of a broader push by the United States and its Western and Middle Eastern allies to press both Iran and [[israel|Israel]] to cool tensions and prevent the region from erupting into all-out war.[14] Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] said Washington had communicated directly to both governments that the conflict must not escalate.[14]

## June 2025 messaging on Los Angeles

During the [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-enforcement-protests|June 2025 Los Angeles immigration enforcement protests]], the White House published an article titled "President Trump is Stepping Up Where Democrats Won't," arguing that "radical left lunatics" were attacking law enforcement, hurling projectiles at police cruisers, burning vehicles and shutting down freeways because the administration was removing violent criminal illegal immigrants from their communities.[15] It said Democrats such as Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] should be thanking President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for "ridding their streets of criminal illegal immigrant killers, rapists, and gangbangers," and named detainees including Cuong Chanh Phan, Eswin Uriel Castro and Rolando Veneracion-Enriquez.[15]

In the same period, the White House was reported to be slashing military funding for Ukraine, a cut Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] confirmed before the [[house-appropriations-committee|House Appropriations Committee]], and [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] was racing to meet White House immigration arrest quotas as protests spread nationwide.[15]

## August 2023 statements

Following [[fitch-ratings|Fitch's]] downgrade of the United States credit rating on August 1, 2023, Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] said it "defies reality to downgrade the United States at a moment when President Biden has delivered the strongest recovery of any major economy in the world," and attributed the economic strain to Republican "extremism."[16] On [[niger|Niger]], the White House cited "over $140 million in development, food security, and humanitarian aid to Niger this year alone" as the scale of US assistance during the year of the [[2023-niger-coup|coup]].[16]

## July 2025 Gaza diplomacy

On July 8, 2025, the White House hosted [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] for a second consecutive day of talks with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in the Oval Office, ending without any public announcement of a breakthrough in the Gaza ceasefire and hostage release negotiations.[17] Earlier that day a team of senior Qatari officials met Trump and envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] at the White House for more than three hours to discuss the Gaza talks.[17] Badlands commentary noted that CBS's parent company settled a Trump lawsuit specifically to remain in the White House's good graces while seeking FCC approval of the Skydance–Paramount merger.[17]

## Cancellation of Biden's Jordan summit (October 2023)

On October 17, 2023, as Air Force One departed Washington for Israel, the White House canceled the entire Jordan leg of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s Middle East trip in the aftermath of the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|al-Ahli Baptist Hospital explosion]] in Gaza.[18] The confirmation was issued within the same hour that Arab leaders announced they were unwilling to meet Biden.[18] The official White House statement said that after consulting [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]] and in light of the days of mourning announced by [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], Biden would postpone travel to [[jordan|Jordan]] and the planned meeting with those leaders and Egyptian President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah el-Sisi]], and conveyed his "deepest condolences for the innocent lives lost in the hospital explosion in Gaza."[18]

## Trump–Ramaphosa meeting (May 2025)

On Wednesday, May 21, 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] hosted South African President [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]] at the White House for a meeting initially intended to focus on trade and bilateral relations.[19] The meeting shifted to the treatment of South Africa's white minority after a journalist asked what would convince Trump that there is "no white genocide in South Africa," and Trump had the lights dimmed to play a five-minute video montage alleging discrimination and violence against white farmers.[19] The White House separately announced a Presidential Delegation to Quito for the inauguration of [[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]], led by [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]][19]

## Response to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage

During the [[2024-crowdstrike-global-outage|CrowdStrike global outage]] of July 19, 2024, the White House began investigating the disruption between 6 and 7 a.m. Eastern, and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was briefed between 9 a.m. and noon as federal agencies became involved.[20]

## COVID.gov lab-leak redirect (2025)

In April 2025, the White House redirected COVID.gov to a new landing page titled "Lab Leak: True Origins of COVID-19," presenting a five-point argument that COVID-19 originated from a mistaken laboratory leak in Wuhan, China.[21] The page appeared to draw on the final report of the Republican-led Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic released in December 2024, replacing content that had, as recently as the prior week, listed resources for testing, treatment, vaccination, and Long COVID.[21] The five assertions were that the virus "possesses a biological characteristic that is not found in nature," that all cases "stem from a single introduction into humans," that "Wuhan is home to China's foremost SARS research lab," that researchers there "were sick with COVID-like symptoms in the fall of 2019," and that "if there was evidence of a natural origin it would have already surfaced."[21] See [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]].[21]

## Campus funding actions and Iran policy (2025)

Also in April 2025, the White House labelled the response of [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] and other elite schools to pro-Palestinian demonstrations a failure to control antisemitism on campus, and the administration withheld federal funding accordingly, froze $2.2 billion in multi-year grants, threatened Harvard's tax-exempt status, and weighed an additional $1 billion cut in health research funding.[21] In the same period, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters in the Oval Office that he wanted Iran "to be great and prosperous and terrific" but that it could not have a nuclear weapon, while his administration pursued talks in Rome and debated internally over diplomacy versus strikes.[21] Reporting cited by Badlands also highlighted tensions from Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s troubled meeting with Trump at the White House.[21]

## Criticism of Venezuela's 2024 ballot exclusion

On March 26, 2024, the Biden White House criticized Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] for barring the leading opposition candidate from that country's July elections.[22] Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]], asked about the matter during a briefing aboard Air Force One, said the administration was deeply concerned by the National Electoral Council's refusal to register Dr. [[corina-yoris|Corina Yoris]], the successor chosen by primary winner [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], and said Washington was working with the international community to secure inclusive and competitive elections while hinting that sanctions relief could be withdrawn.[22] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted this posture with the same administration's simultaneous legal campaign against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], noting also that Jean-Pierre conspicuously avoided the word "democracy."[22]

## Saudi–Israel Normalization Push (April 2024)

In April 2024 the White House pressed a long-shot diplomatic deal that would have required Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to accept a new commitment to Palestinian statehood in exchange for diplomatic recognition by Riyadh, according to US and Saudi officials.[23] As inducements for Saudi Arabia, the administration offered a more formal defense relationship with Washington, assistance in acquiring civil nuclear power, and a renewed push for a Palestinian state — a package officials said was in the final stages of negotiation.[23] Administration officials argued that the multicountry effort to shoot down Iranian missiles and drones that April demonstrated that Israeli security could be enhanced through closer integration with the kingdom.[23]

The same month, the official White House transcript of President Biden's April 17 Pittsburgh remarks carried his false claim that his uncle Ambrose Finnegan had been eaten by cannibals in New Guinea.[23] Badlands writers cast the accumulating headlines as evidence of an administration adrift, with Burning Bright describing Biden as a chaos agent whose failures were "table-setters" for a Trump restoration.[23]

## Bluesky account (October 2025)

The White House joined the social platform [[bluesky|Bluesky]] on a Friday evening in October 2025 and, in less than 48 hours, had been blocked by more than 92,000 users according to VQV, an app that tracks Bluesky statistics — making it the second most-blocked account on the platform.[24] Only Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], who had accumulated 166,000 blocks since joining in June, was ahead of it, and reporting noted that at the prevailing rate the White House would soon take the top spot.[24] Badlands host [[canncon|CannCon]] treated the mass blocking as a sign of advance into hostile territory rather than a setback.[24]

## Media strategy debate, October 2025

Following [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]]'s October 6, 2025 clash with [[cnn|CNN]] over immigration profiling allegations, [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the White House's continued provision of high-level guests to legacy outlets is "almost single handedly keeping many of these networks alive," and questioned why the administration legitimizes them by appearing at all.[25] In the same period Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] publicly attacked a federal court ruling blocking [[national-guard|National Guard]] deployments to Portland as "untethered in reality and in the law."[25]


## Mixed tariff messaging (April 2025)

The White House sent conflicting messages in the days after the April 2025 [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]] about whether the new duties were meant to be permanent or were a tactic to extract concessions, with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] saying they "give us great power to negotiate" even as other officials signaled permanence.[26] Trump used a press gaggle at the White House the same week to entertain the idea of a third term, and separately invited Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to visit — the second such visit of the term, after Netanyahu became the first foreign leader to meet Trump following his return to the building.[26]

## Shutdown meeting and Gaza press conference, September 2025

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the four top congressional leaders met privately at the White House on 29 September 2025 and emerged without an agreement to keep the federal government open, leaving a [[october-2025-government-shutdown|shutdown]] likely to begin at 12:01 a.m. on 1 October.[27] Later the same day Trump hosted Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in the Oval Office for over an hour before the pair appeared in the State Dining Room, where Trump presented his 20-point [[gaza|Gaza]] plan minutes after the White House published the document in full.[27] Separately, $22 million of [[youtube|YouTube]]'s $24.5 million settlement with Trump was directed to a trust for the National Mall, the entity handling construction of the $200 million [[white-house-ballroom|White House ballroom]].[27]

## Loss of trust in Israel (October 2024)

In early October 2024, four U.S. officials told Axios that the Biden administration had grown increasingly distrustful of what the Israeli government said about its military and diplomatic plans in its multi-front war, with one official saying, "Our trust of the Israelis is very low right now and for a good reason."[28] The trust crisis was magnified by Israel's planned retaliation against Iran for its missile attack, which required coordination with the United States in case Iran responded; the administration was not opposed to a response but wanted it to be measured.[28] During a Friday call, national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] told Israel's minister for strategic affairs [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] that Washington expected "clarity and transparency" about Israel's retaliation plans because of the implications for U.S. forces and interests in the region.[28]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that many in the West Wing likely were genuinely in the dark, not because they were uninvolved but because [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] effectively controlled [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] while [[joe-biden|Biden]] and [[kamala-harris|Harris]] were sidelined and Sullivan disregarded — though he judged that officials in the [[state-department|State Department]], some in the White House, and probably the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] did know.[28] He held that the more consequential story was the perception abroad that Washington had assured Iran, China, India and Middle Eastern states that a peace deal was nearly complete shortly before Hassan Nasrallah was killed, comparing the episode to the fates of Saddam Hussein and Muammar Gaddafi and arguing that a global breach of trust had occurred.[28]

## June 2025 disputes over the Iran strike assessment

The White House strongly disputed the leaked early Defense Intelligence Agency assessment suggesting U.S. strikes had set Iran's nuclear program back only a few months, and criticized leakers within the intelligence apparatus.[29] Press secretary Karoline Leavitt said the leak was "a clear attempt to demean President Trump, and discredit the brave fighter pilots who conducted a perfectly executed mission to obliterate Iran's nuclear program," adding that dropping "fourteen 30,000 pound bombs perfectly on their targets" produces "total obliteration."[29] Separately, Middle East envoy Steve Witkoff said the White House expected "big announcements on countries that are coming into the Abraham Accords."[29]

## 2024 repositioning on crime and the border

In May 2024 the defeat of a liberal Portland prosecutor by a tough-on-crime challenger hardened a view among top White House officials that Democrats needed to distance themselves further from the party's left flank on law-and-order issues.[30] Aides to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] argued that the Oregon results validated long-running internal concerns that crime and an immigration crisis at the southern border risked overwhelming the president's reelection case, particularly if the broader party were perceived as soft on both.[30] One Biden official, granted anonymity, said: "Particularly right now, Americans don't want to feel like things are out of control. Well-meaning ideas have gone too far, and we need a sensible approach."[30]

The White House was described as banking on voters rewarding public efforts to crack down on immigration and boost law-enforcement spending, and on the liberal forces that pushed the party leftward in 2020 not punishing the president in November.[30] Inside the West Wing, senior counselor [[steve-ricchetti|Steve Ricchetti]] was among the leading advocates of this approach and of greater toughness at the border.[30] Officials also contended that Biden had substantive accomplishments to run on, pointing to statistics showing steep drops in murder rates.[30] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the framing as "dangerous government propaganda," arguing that any drop in reported murders reflected changed tracking methodology and that no amount of late-term rhetoric could persuade Americans on the border.[30]

## June 2023 Pride Month display

In June 2023 the White House hosted a Pride Month celebration at which a rainbow Pride flag was hung facing the South Lawn, centered between two American flags — an arrangement critics said violated the [[us-flag-code|United States Flag Code]] requirement that the national flag occupy the center and highest point of a grouped display.[31] [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] president [[tom-fitton|Tom Fitton]] said the display disrespected "every American service member buried under its colors," and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] wrote that the US flag "DOES NOT FLANK *ANY* FLAG. Especially not on OUR White House."[31]

The event took place days before Flag Day and one day after [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] issued a Flag Day proclamation stating that "Old Glory stands for hope, pride, and progress."[31] At the celebration Biden said the United States is "the only nation that is founded on an idea — not geography, not religion, and not ethnicity — that all men and women are created equal, endowed by their Creator, et cetera," and closed with "Happy Pride Month. Happy Pride year. Happy Pride life."[31] He thanked [[tim-gill|Tim Gill]] of the [[gill-foundation|Gill Foundation]] and Gill's husband [[scott-miller|Scott Miller]], the administration's ambassador to Switzerland, for making the event possible; neither attended.[31]
