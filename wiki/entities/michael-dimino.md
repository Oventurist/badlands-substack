# Michael DiMino

Michael DiMino is a United States defense official serving as Deputy Assistant Secretary of War for the Middle East, a post in which he acts as principal policy advisor on all defense and security issues pertaining to the Middle East and is responsible for more than a dozen countries.[1] He was sworn in at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] on January 20, 2025.[1]

## Views on Israel

DiMino became a target of scrutiny in the Israeli press upon his hiring, owing to his past rhetoric and his position that [[israel|Israel]] is a security liability for the United States and that Washington should abandon it as an ally and withdraw all support.[1]

## Appointment and 2025 backlash

DiMino was the Trump administration's pick for deputy assistant secretary of defense for the Middle East, and his selection alarmed pro-Israel conservatives, who pointed to a record on Iran, the [[houthis|Houthis]] and the region that included describing Iran's missile attack on Israel as "fairly moderate" and urging the United States against bombing the Houthis while calling instead for American pressure on Israel.[2] Before the appointment he had been a military analyst at the [[cia|CIA]] and an official at the Defense Department during the first Trump administration, and a fellow at [[defense-priorities|Defense Priorities]], described as a Koch-funded isolationist think tank founded by allies of libertarian Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]].[2]

In a February 2024 Defense Priorities webinar he declared that the Middle East does "not really" matter for U.S. interests, that "vital or existential threats" there are "best characterized as minimal to nonexistent," and that the American role in the region had produced no benefits.[2] He was sworn in on Monday, January 20, 2025, reportedly alongside roughly thirty other [[pentagon|Pentagon]] appointees whose swearing-in went largely unreported outside a Sputnik item later circulated on X.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] welcomed the pick, summarizing DiMino's positions — that Israel's Gaza campaign was producing a resurgence of [[hamas|Hamas]], that President [[mahmoud-abbas|Abbas]] and the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] were being undermined, and that there is no military solution in Gaza or the Red Sea — and writing that "Mike Huckabee is noise, Michael DiMino is signal," having earlier speculated that Trump's appointment of [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] as ambassador to Israel was a feint to instill complacency in the Deep State and Tel Aviv.[2]

## Named in the DIA espionage assessment

In June 2026, reporting by NBC News and [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] on a [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] assessment elevating Israeli espionage to a "critical" threat level named DiMino — alongside [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[elbridge-colby|Elbridge Colby]] — among officials allegedly targeted by Israeli intelligence collection.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that DiMino's inclusion was what made the report "particularly compelling," since his publicly stated hostility to the alliance gives [[mossad|Mossad]] an obvious motive to surveil him: "So it makes perfect sense that Mossad would want to spy on this guy."[1]

## Establishment reaction to the appointment

Contemporaneous Badlands coverage of DiMino's appointment as chief Middle East policy advisor at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] reported that the move ruffled feathers among establishment Republicans such as [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]], who was incredulous that a figure like DiMino had "slipped through the cracks" during Senate vetting.[3] The commentary attributed to DiMino the positions that the United States has no national security interest in maintaining a military presence in the Middle East, that aid should be directed to Palestine rather than [[israel|Israel]], and that U.S. military support for the IDF should cease immediately because it encourages warmongering in Tel Aviv.[3] These views were invoked as context for the [[2025-patriot-interceptor-transfer-from-israel-to-ukraine|transfer of roughly 90 Patriot interceptors from Israel to Ukraine]] reported in late January 2025.[3]
