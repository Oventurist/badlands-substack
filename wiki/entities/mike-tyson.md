# Mike Tyson

Mike Tyson is an American boxing legend whose October 2025 visit to the Democratic Republic of the Congo placed him at the intersection of sport, celebrity diplomacy, and Badlands Media commentary on informal back-channel statecraft.[1]

## 2025 visit to the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Tyson met Congolese President [[felix-tshisekedi|Felix Tshisekedi]] on a Sunday in Kinshasa at the close of events commemorating the 50th anniversary of the "Rumble in the Jungle," the iconic Muhammad Ali–George Foreman fight.[1] Tyson described the encounter as a "beautiful experience" and said the two men had discussed life and several potential projects, adding that he would "return to the country in the coming days."[1] According to the press service of the Congolese presidency, Tyson publicly acknowledged his Congolese heritage during the trip.[1]

Tyson arrived in Kinshasa on the preceding Saturday and met with young boxers as part of the anniversary celebrations.[1] At a press conference held at the newly renamed Ali-Foreman Stadium — formerly Stade Tata Raphael — he offered an emotional reflection on the venue, saying, "I would love to be in the same room that Ali and Foreman were in. Yeah, I would like to do it, that'd be amazing."[1] The [[rumble-in-the-jungle|Rumble in the Jungle]] itself took place on October 30, 1974, in Kinshasa, and remains among the most iconic bouts in boxing history, with Ali knocking out Foreman in the eighth round.[1]

## 1992 conviction and the Trump connection

Badlands commentary recounted that Tyson experienced his "fall from grace" in 1992, when he was convicted of raping a woman and served three years in prison.[1] Three days after the conviction, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] famously came to Tyson's defense.[1] The conviction remains controversial in the view of the Badlands writer chiefly because three eye-witnesses from the night in question, who independently contradicted the claims made by the victim, were called to the stand by the defense but prevented from testifying by the judge; Tyson's attorneys raised the point on appeal, but the appeal was denied and the conviction upheld.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing for the brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the Kinshasa meeting as a "TrumpaMania" story reminiscent of the Dennis Rodman–[[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] plotline.[1] He noted that Tyson — like Muhammad Ali before him — has been represented by agent [[darren-prince|Darren Prince]], the same figure credited with placing Dennis Rodman on The Celebrity Apprentice and then sending him to North Korea, a sequence the author links to the eventual Trump–Kim summit.[1] Prince is also described as having negotiated the Trump and Melania NFT launches, a connection the author treats as significant for readers of The Book of Trump.[1]
