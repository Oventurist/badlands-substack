# Carl Nichols

Carl Nichols is a U.S. District Judge who, in May 2026, cleared President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order on voter citizenship checks and tighter mail-in voting rules to move forward.[1]

## Ruling on the voter-citizenship executive order

Nichols ruled that Democratic plaintiffs had failed to demonstrate legal standing or sufficient harm to justify a preliminary injunction blocking the [[voter-citizenship-executive-order|executive order]].[1] He found that the order does not directly require the plaintiffs to take any action, and that no federal agency had yet implemented steps under the order that could cause them harm.[1]

The judge rejected arguments that the order would disenfranchise millions of voters, calling those claims speculative, and noted that the order mandates procedures allowing individuals to access, review, and correct information in any state-by-state citizenship lists used to maintain voter rolls.[1] He also dismissed privacy concerns over the sharing of basic voter data — such as name, age, and residence — between government agencies, ruling that such sharing does not rise to the level of injury required for Article III standing.[1]

In the quoted passage of the opinion, Nichols wrote that "Plaintiffs have failed to show that they are likely to have Article III standing or that they will suffer imminent and irreparable harm absent an injunction," while acknowledging that the Postal Service might ultimately issue a final rule directly affecting the plaintiffs, or that the government might develop State Citizenship Lists omitting specific individuals due to particularized flaws — circumstances in which plaintiffs could renew their motions.[1]

## Effect

The ruling removed a key legal obstacle and allowed the Trump administration to proceed with implementing the executive order's provisions on policing voter rolls for citizenship and restricting mail-in voting; no specific timeline for rollout was detailed in the decision.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the decision as evidence that establishment efforts to obstruct the president's agenda through frivolous state litigation are "largely unsuccessful."[1]

## Bannon contempt sentence order (2024)

Earlier in his tenure, Nichols — a Trump appointee — presided over the [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] case against [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]].[2] On June 5, 2024, he agreed with prosecutors and ordered Bannon to self-surrender by July 1 to begin a four-month prison sentence, after a three-judge appeals panel rejected Bannon's bid to overturn his 2022 conviction.[2] "The government's motion is granted," Nichols said, over Bannon's objection that he intended to appeal to the Supreme Court and should remain free in the meantime.[2] Justice Department prosecutor [[john-crabb|John Crabb]] argued at the hearing that Bannon's chance of reversal was "unlikely."[2]
