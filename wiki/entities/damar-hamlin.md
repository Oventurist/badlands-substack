# Damar Hamlin

Damar Hamlin is an American professional football player who served as a safety for the Buffalo Bills of the National Football League. He became the center of intense national attention after collapsing in cardiac arrest during a nationally televised game and subsequently became a recurring subject in alternative-media commentary about vaccine safety, media narrative management, and public trust.[1]

## Cardiac arrest and recovery

Hamlin, then 24 years old, went into cardiac arrest on the field during a game in Cincinnati on January 2, 2023, requiring emergency CPR administered by on-field medical officials.[1] His collapse during a Monday Night Football broadcast was witnessed live by a national television audience, making it one of the most widely seen medical emergencies in American sports history.[1]

## Return to public view and the body-double claim

Hamlin attended the Bills–Bengals playoff game on the afternoon of Sunday, January 22, 2023, at Highmark Stadium, watching from a suite. It was his first appearance at an NFL game since the cardiac arrest.[1] His presence at the stadium immediately generated online speculation, with some commentators suggesting that the figure seen in the suite was a body double rather than Hamlin himself.[1] TMZ reported on January 23, 2023 that multiple sources had told TMZ Sports that the body-double claim was false, characterizing the theory as the product of "the tin foil hat-wearing crowd."[1]

## Treatment in Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the Hamlin episode to illustrate his methodological preference for analyzing narrative and potential events rather than attempting to adjudicate contested facts in what he termed the Fifth-Generation Information War.[1] He argued that it was effectively impossible for outside observers to determine what had actually happened — listing as open questions whether the on-field episode was staged, whether it was a vaccine injury being covered up, whether Hamlin had died, or whether he was alive and being withheld from public view in order to bait the "conspiracy" community into narrative false flags.[1] Rather than resolve those questions, Burning Bright directed readers to focus on what he called the net effects of the affair: that more Americans than ever were questioning not only vaccine efficacy and safety but the credibility of the media broadly, including sports media — an outcome he characterized as positive.[1]

## As a case study in "poisoning the well"

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s February 28, 2023 essay "The Road Without Struggle" made Hamlin its principal illustration of [[poisoning-the-well|poisoning the well]].[2] Carlson described Hamlin as the NFL player who suffered two heart attacks during a Monday Night Football game, and stated his own view that the collapse was not staged and was not an outcome the [[cabal|Cabal]] wanted.[2]

In his account, most of America privately concluded that the [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] might have caused or contributed to the episode, leaving the [[deep-state|Deep State]] a choice between denial and ridicule — and it chose both.[2] Media coverage first framed the initial cardiac arrest as an ordinary consequence of a direct hit to the chest, but the second episode undercut that explanation and the public did not accept it.[2]

Carlson then argued that the Buffalo Bills' staging of a public appearance by Hamlin at their stadium was engineered to look suspicious: the man wore sunglasses, a hood and a mask, camera operators never provided a clear image of him, and he gave no interviews.[2] Carlson called this "a set-up," designed so that observers would conclude Hamlin was dead, gravely ill in intensive care, or replaced by a double — and acknowledged that he himself fell for it.[2] The NFL's subsequent parading of Hamlin at the Super Bowl a month later then made everyone who had voiced those conclusions appear to lack credibility, and wavering vaccine skeptics fell back into line.[2]

## Immediate commentary: "You Are a Horrible Person" (January 2023)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s January 4, 2023 Badlands Media essay "You Are a Horrible Person" was written within days of the collapse and treated the episode primarily as a study in narrative management rather than as a medical case.[3] Carlson described Hamlin as an extremely fit 24-year-old safety for the [[buffalo-bills|Buffalo Bills]] who made a routine, mundane tackle on [[nfl|Monday Night Football]] that did not involve a helmet-to-helmet hit, stood up, collapsed seconds later, went into cardiac arrest, and remained in critical condition at the time of writing.[3] He opened and closed his remarks by saying he hoped and prayed for Hamlin's full recovery and stressed that nothing he wrote was proof of anything, only observation.[3]

Carlson noted that the collapse was immediately explained in public commentary as a rare condition in which blunt force trauma to the chest induces cardiac arrest, and that anyone raising [[covid-19-vaccines|vaccines]] as a possible contributing factor was at once demonized as insensitive and uncaring.[3] He found the mundane-tackle explanation hard to accept, writing that in a lifetime of watching football he had never seen or heard of a player going into cardiac arrest from a tackle, including thousands of far more violent collisions, and asking why — if blows to the chest occur on essentially every down, with shoulder pads protecting the area — such episodes were not more common.[3]

The essay pressed the asymmetry that speculation attributing the event to an extremely rare traumatic condition was treated as sensitive while speculation about vaccines was not, arguing that one speculation was permitted and the other forbidden.[3] Carlson explicitly declined to claim either that the cardiac arrest was staged or that the vaccine caused it, but described the media and social-media reaction as "less than genuine" and as the construction of a narrative.[3] He observed that the timing coincided with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s announcement that he would release [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] exposing [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] that same week, and pointed to the many recent cases of athletes collapsing on international soccer pitches and other sporting fields as context he and others could not ignore.[3]

By way of reductio, Carlson argued that if a routine, non-violent tackle really could stop a healthy 24-year-old's heart, then the remainder of the NFL season and playoffs — and indeed all contact sports, and ultimately all sports — would have to be cancelled, since blunt force trauma to the chest can occur anywhere; refusing that conclusion, he said, was supposedly what made one a horrible person.[3] He compared the selective focus on what did not cause the collapse, while ignoring what did, to a homicide detective investigating who had not committed a murder.[3] Carlson allowed that even if blunt force trauma was the proximate cause, that would not establish that vaccines had not contributed to the net effects.[3]
