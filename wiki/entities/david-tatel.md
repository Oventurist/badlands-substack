# David S. Tatel

David S. Tatel is a retired American federal judge, appointed by President Bill Clinton, who served for nearly 30 years on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit.[1] He stepped down from the court in January 2024.[1]

## Criticism of the Supreme Court

In a memoir titled *Vision: A Memoir of Blindness and Justice*, Tatel revealed that part of the reason he retired was that he had grown tired of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court's]] "low regard" for judicial principles.[1] Newsweek identified him as one of at least three judges who had recently delivered a strong rebuke of the high court, alongside Hawaii Supreme Court Justice [[todd-eddins|Todd Eddins]] and U.S. District Judge [[carlton-reeves|Carlton Reeves]].[1] Tatel told Newsweek: "My views, I think, are widely shared throughout the judicial system... there are, I can assure you, a large number of judges who will not find anything I've said in this book surprising."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing in the Badlands News Brief, argued that the wave of judicial criticism of the Supreme Court should be read as narrative positioning within an asymmetric information and shadow war rather than at face value.[1] He pointed out that all three judges most prominently quoted against the Court — Tatel (a Clinton appointee), Eddins (an appointee of Democratic Hawaii Governor David Ige), and Reeves (an Obama appointee) — shared a common partisan provenance, concluding: "Seeing a pattern? Relax."[1]
