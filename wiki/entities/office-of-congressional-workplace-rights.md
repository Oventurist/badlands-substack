# Office of Congressional Workplace Rights

The Office of Congressional Workplace Rights (OCWR) is the legislative-branch body responsible for administering workplace protections within [[congress|Congress]], including the handling of claims and settlements arising from allegations against members and their offices.[1]

## 2026 disclosure mandate

In July 2026 the OCWR became a central actor in a transparency fight over congressional sexual misconduct settlements. A resolution offered by Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] and passed by the [[house-of-representatives|House]] 420-0 directed the OCWR, alongside the [[house-ethics-committee|House Ethics Committee]], to release within 60 days all records identifying which current or former members had used taxpayer funds to settle sexual misconduct allegations, including the amounts paid.[1]

The disclosure demand followed high-profile 2026 resignations by Reps. [[tony-gonzales|Tony Gonzales]] and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] amid serious allegations, and built on an earlier House Oversight subpoena that had revealed more than $300,000 in federal payments to settle claims against House lawmakers or their offices.[1] Congress ended the practice of using taxpayer dollars for such settlements in 2018.[1]
