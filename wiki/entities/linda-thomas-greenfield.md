# Linda Thomas-Greenfield

Linda Thomas-Greenfield served as the United States Ambassador to the United Nations under the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], representing Washington in the [[united-nations-security-council|United Nations Security Council]] during the opening weeks of the war between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]].[1]

## Veto of the Brazilian-drafted Gaza resolution

On Wednesday, October 18, 2023, the United States vetoed a Security Council resolution that would have called for humanitarian pauses in the fighting between Israel and Hamas in order to allow aid access to the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]].[1] The vote on the Brazilian-drafted text had been delayed twice over the preceding days while Washington attempted to broker aid access to Gaza by other means; twelve of the fifteen council members voted in favor, while Russia and Britain abstained.[1]

Explaining the veto to the council after the vote, Thomas-Greenfield said, "We are on the ground doing the hard work of diplomacy," and added, "We believe we need to let that diplomacy play out."[1] She argued that although "resolutions are important" and the council "must speak out," the body's actions "must be informed by the facts on the ground and support direct diplomacy efforts," concluding that "the council needs to get this right."[1] Reporting on the vote noted that Washington has traditionally shielded its ally Israel from Security Council action.[1]
