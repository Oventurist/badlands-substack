# Canadian Armed Forces

The Canadian Armed Forces (CAF) are the military of [[canada|Canada]]. In early 2026 the CAF became the subject of international attention after reporting that it had quietly developed a conceptual war game simulating a United States military invasion of Canada and Ottawa's potential response — the first such planning exercise in more than a century.[1]

## 2026 invasion war game

The exercise was developed amid heightened tensions with the Trump administration, including tariff threats, the dispute over [[greenland|Greenland]], and [[donald-trump|President Trump's]] repeated framing of Canada as a potential 51st state.[1] The model envisions American forces rapidly overwhelming Canada's conventional defenses on land and at sea within days to a week, with planners assuming as much as three months of warning if joint NORAD operations collapse.[1] Additional scenarios contemplate missile attacks on Canadian cities and infrastructure by [[russia|Russia]] or [[china|China]].[1]

Unable to match U.S. conventional power, the simulated Canadian strategy relies on asymmetric insurgency: ambushes, sabotage, drone strikes, hit-and-run raids, and improvised explosive devices, drawing explicitly on the Afghan mujahedeen's resistance to Soviet forces, Taliban operations against NATO forces (including Canadian troops), and Ukraine's defense against Russia.[1] The stated goal is to inflict heavy casualties and prolong any conflict, potentially mobilizing a volunteer reserve force exceeding 400,000 while seeking reinforcements from allies such as Britain, France, Germany, Japan, and South Korea.[1]

Retired generals and outside experts quoted in the reporting concede that Canada could not repel a full invasion conventionally, but argue occupation would prove prohibitively costly given the limited U.S. force structure available to control vast Canadian territory and urban centers, and given anticipated global backlash against any aggressor.[1] The exercises are cited in support of urgent calls to bolster northern defense capabilities, deepen deterrence through rapid alliance-building, and maintain homeland resilience, even as Canada continues close military cooperation with the United States on shared continental defense systems.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the war-game story as part of a recurring "war with Canada" thread in the information war, noting Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney's]] earlier invocation of [[article-5|Article 5]] as another provocation, and dismissing Canadian statehood claims outright.[1]
