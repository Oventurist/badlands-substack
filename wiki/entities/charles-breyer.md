# Charles Breyer

Charles Breyer is a United States District Judge for the Northern District of California who presided over the June 2025 emergency litigation brought by California against the federal government over the deployment of military forces to Los Angeles.[1]

## Role in the 2025 Los Angeles troop deployment case

California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] sued President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]], seeking a temporary restraining order against the federalization of the California National Guard and the deployment of U.S. Marines during the anti-ICE unrest in Los Angeles.[1] Justice Department lawyers representing the administration argued that the plaintiffs' motion was "legally meritless" and sought "an extraordinary, unprecedented, and dangerous court order" that would "jeopardize the safety of Department of Homeland Security personnel and interfere with the Federal Government's ability to carry out operations."[1]

On Tuesday, June 10, 2025, Breyer granted the administration's request to push past Newsom's demanded 1 p.m. Pacific deadline for a TRO ruling, effectively denying immediate emergency relief and giving federal lawyers roughly 24 hours to file a response brief.[1] The judge instead set argument in open court for Thursday at 1:30 p.m. Pacific.[1] Badlands contributors noted that because federal courtrooms bar cameras, coverage of the hearing would depend on observers present in the Northern District of California.[1]

### June 12 ruling and appellate stay

On Thursday, June 12, 2025, Breyer ruled that Trump had broken the law when he mobilized thousands of Guard members amid the protests over immigration raids, and ordered that the troops be returned to state control by noon Friday.[2] In a 36-page decision, Breyer wrote that Trump's actions "were illegal — both exceeding the scope of his statutory authority and violating the 10th Amendment to the United States Constitution."[2] He added that he was "troubled by the implication" inherent in the administration's argument that "protest against the federal government, a core civil liberty protected by the First Amendment, can justify a finding of rebellion."[2] Hours later, the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|U.S. 9th Circuit Court of Appeals]] issued a late-night emergency stay pausing Breyer's order and scheduled a hearing for the following Tuesday, leaving the [[national-guard|National Guard]] federalized through the weekend.[2]

## 2025 National Guard rulings

Breyer, sitting in San Francisco, ruled in June 2025 that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federalization of roughly 4,000 [[california-army-national-guard|California National Guard]] troops for Los Angeles violated federal law — 10 U.S.C. § 12406, which authorizes such deployments only for invasion, rebellion, or inability to enforce the laws — as well as the Tenth Amendment's protection of state sovereignty, and he ordered control returned to Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[3] The [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|Ninth Circuit]] stayed his June 12 order almost immediately, and on June 19 a unanimous three-judge panel held that Trump likely had statutory authority to federalize the Guard, finding that the Los Angeles protests met the "danger of rebellion" threshold.[3]

On September 2, 2025, Breyer issued a further ruling enjoining the administration from using the remaining federalized California Guard members, or any military force, for domestic law enforcement in California, citing the [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus Act]].[3] The two orders left the administration holding federalized California troops it could not deploy within California, a situation that preceded the [[2025-portland-national-guard-deployment|2025 Portland National Guard deployment]].[3]
