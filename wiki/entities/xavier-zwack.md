# Xavier Zwack

Zwack was a lawyer and criminal court judge who served as one of the senior figures of the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]], operating under the code name Cato.[1] When the Order was exposed and [[adam-weishaupt|Adam Weishaupt]] was deprived of his professor's chair and banished from Bavaria, Zwack was banished as well, alongside two Italians, the Marquis Constanza and the Marquis Savioli, while some other members were imprisoned.[1]

## Correspondence and seized papers

Much of what is known of Zwack comes from the secret correspondence reproduced by [[john-robison|John Robison]]. A memo from Spartacus to Cato — Weishaupt to Zwack — dated March 17, 1778 sets down a blueprint for engaging in espionage.[1] Another letter, from Philo ([[baron-von-knigge|Knigge]]) to Cato, refers to religious authorities as cheats who bow to superstition and fanaticism and advocates a slow overthrow of religion so that the fanatics will not be alarmed or become aware of what is going on; in the same vein Knigge declared that "The Jewish Theosophy was a mystery like the Eleusinsian, or the Pythagorean, unfit for the vulgar."[1]

Zwack's own writings contained plans for a Women's Liberation movement, which he described as consisting of "two classes: the virtuous and the free-hearted (i.e., those who fly out of the common tract of prudish manners) ... Proper books must be put into their hands, and such as are flattering to their passions."[1] The article compares the methods of these operatives to those of modern CIA agents.[1]

Found with Zwack's papers were descriptions of a strong box rigged to blow up and destroy its contents if forced open; recipes for securing an abortion; a chemical composition which blinds or kills when spurted in the face; a recipe for invisible ink; a formula for a tea inducing abortion; a method for filling a bedroom with poisonous gas; methods for forging seals of state, including a collection of several hundred impressions with a list of their owners among princes, nobles, clergymen and prominent merchants; and a copy of a manuscript entitled *Better Than Horus*, printed and distributed at a Leipzig fair, containing a bitter satire on all religion.[1]

After the exposure, the Illuminati argued that these possessions properly belonged in Zwack's hands because he was a judge of the criminal court and it was his duty to know of such things — a defense, extended also to the collection of faux seals, that the article characterizes as a lie which nonetheless seemed to work, leaving unanswered the damning question of why such things were found among the papers of the Illuminati.[1] Confronted with the material, Weishaupt replied: "These things were not carried into effect, only spoken of, and were justifiable when taken in proper connection."[1]
