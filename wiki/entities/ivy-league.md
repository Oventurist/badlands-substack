# Ivy League

The **Ivy League** is the eight-member association of northeastern American universities — [[harvard-university|Harvard]], [[yale-university|Yale]], [[princeton-university|Princeton]], [[columbia-university|Columbia]], the [[university-of-pennsylvania|University of Pennsylvania]], [[cornell-university|Cornell]], [[brown-university|Brown]] and [[dartmouth-college|Dartmouth]] — that Badlands Media contributor Observing Consciousness identifies as the institutional backbone of the [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]].[1]

## Badlands framing

The article argues the eight schools "are not just universities" but "operating nodes of a supranational command system," each assigned a functional role across capital markets, intelligence agencies, central banks, media conglomerates, biotech firms and social engineering apparatuses.[1] The author rejects the premise that the schools "operate as academic equals pursuing truth," contending instead that their funding, alumni networks and intellectual exports converge on control.[1]

Graduates are described as populating presidential cabinets, multinational bank boards, media executive suites and the leadership of NGOs and intelligence bureaus, forming "a lattice of control that rivals any national government."[1] Each campus is said to maintain grooming portals in the form of secret societies and elite clubs — [[skull-and-bones|Skull & Bones]] and [[scroll-and-key|Scroll & Key]] at Yale, the [[porcellian-club|Porcellian]], Fly and A.D. Clubs at Harvard, the [[ivy-club|Ivy Club]] and [[whig-cliosophic-society|Whig-Cliosophic Society]] at Princeton, the [[philolexian-society|Philolexian Society]] at Columbia, [[zeta-psi|Zeta Psi]] and the Wharton Inner Circle at Penn, [[quill-and-dagger|Quill and Dagger]] at Cornell, the [[philermenian-society|Philermenian Society]] at Brown, and the [[sphinx-society|Sphinx Society]] at Dartmouth.[1]

## Endowment power

The article treats Ivy endowments as command nodes rather than financial cushions: multi-billion-dollar pools "wrapped in legal insulation," managed through quasi-private investment arms and interlocked with [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[vanguard|Vanguard]] and [[state-street|State Street]] portfolios.[1] Endowments are alleged to funnel capital into ESG-compliant startups, act as anchor clients for asset managers, co-invest with foreign sovereign funds and NGOs, and use philanthropic grants as ideological stipulations enforcing DEI mandates as "soft-law governance tools."[1] In the author's formulation, "the endowments do not serve the schools; the schools serve the endowments."[1]

## Alleged collapse

By 2025 the article describes the Ivy League as losing not only prestige but legal protection, with elite academic institutions placed under federal review for ideological coercion, anti-American programming and foreign influence violations.[1] It cites surging homeschooling, the rise of independent journalists and young people opting for apprenticeships and trades as evidence that the cultural shift away from Ivy legitimacy is already underway.[1]
