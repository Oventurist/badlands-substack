# SpartanNash

SpartanNash is the American food distribution and grocery retail company that owns the FamilyFare supermarket chain, used by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as the consumer-level anchor of his argument about concentrated institutional ownership.[1]

DeLarme's illustration imagines a shopper in Western Michigan visiting a FamilyFare store to buy orange juice, choosing between Minute Maid, owned by [[coca-cola|The Coca-Cola Company]], and Tropicana, largely owned by [[pepsico|PepsiCo]].[1] Having established that the two beverage brands share the same top institutional holders, he notes that the supermarket itself is owned by a parent company — SpartanNash — and that the top investors in SpartanNash are likewise [[blackrock|BlackRock, Inc.]] and [[vanguard-group|the Vanguard Group]].[1]

The example is intended to show that the pattern of common ownership is not limited to the largest S&P 500 firms but extends down through the retail chain to the store where the transaction actually occurs, so that every step of an ordinary grocery purchase runs through companies controlled by the same two asset managers — the structure DeLarme calls [[horizontal-shareholding|horizontal shareholding]].[1]
