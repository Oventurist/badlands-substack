# William Colby

William Colby was a former Director of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]. In the 1988 Frontline investigation into CIA links to the drug trade he stated: "CIA has had a solid rule against being involved in drug trafficking. That's not to say that some of the people who the CIA has used or been in touch with over the years may well have themselves been involved in drug traffic, but not the CIA."[1]

Joe Lange includes the quotation in order to rebut it, arguing that to accept Colby's denial one would have to ignore CIA-owned aircraft transporting drugs globally since the Vietnam war, the recruitment of American farmers to increase opium production during the [[secret-war-in-laos]], and the training of accountants such as [[ramon-milian-rodriguez]] who laundered billions for the [[medellin-cartel]].[1] Lange classes Colby among the network's "untouchables."[1]

Colby also had a direct connection to the [[nugan-hand-bank]]: he served as the bank's legal adviser, and his name was found on documents recovered from the body of co-founder [[frank-nugan]] after Nugan's suspicious death in 1980.[1]

## Appointment as CIA Director and death

[[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]] appointed Colby Director of the CIA in 1973, in Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s reading in order to clean up the agency, expose its atrocities, and rein in its out-of-control power.[2] In December 1974 Colby fired counterintelligence chief [[james-jesus-angleton|James Jesus Angleton]] after the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] revealed that Angleton had overseen a massive program of domestic spying on anti-war and black nationalist movements in violation of the CIA's charter.[2] Colby went on to air the agency's dirty laundry repeatedly before the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] in 1975, and Carlson wrote that the CIA felt he had volunteered too much information even though he testified under oath.[2]

Under President [[gerald-ford|Gerald Ford]], [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] replaced Colby as Director of the CIA in 1976.[2] Years later, Carlson recounts, Colby — having eaten half his meal that day — suddenly felt an overwhelming need to go canoeing; his body was found on a riverbank nine days later.[2]
