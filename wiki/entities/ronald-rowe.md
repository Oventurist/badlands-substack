# Ronald Rowe

Ronald Rowe Jr. is a career American law enforcement officer who served as Acting Director of the [[united-states-secret-service|United States Secret Service]] in the summer of 2024, taking over the agency in the immediate aftermath of the attempted assassination of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at his rally in Butler, Pennsylvania.[1] At the time of his elevation he described himself as a 25-year veteran of the Secret Service.[1]

## Senate testimony on the Butler shooting

On Tuesday, July 30, 2024, Rowe appeared before a Senate panel alongside Deputy FBI Director [[paul-abbate|Paul Abbate]] to answer for the protective failures of July 13 and to report on the status of the ongoing investigations into both the shooting itself and the days that preceded it.[1] The hearing surfaced new details, including a possible social media account belonging to the shooter and unresolved questions about precisely when the Secret Service first observed the gunman on the roof of the AGR building.[1] Officials also confirmed at the hearing — after ambiguous comments by the FBI director the previous week had muddied the question of what struck Trump — that the former president had been hit by a bullet.[1]

Rowe told lawmakers that he was "ashamed" of the agency's failures, and said that visiting the shooting site had been among his first acts as acting director.[1] In his opening statement he recounted: "I went to the roof of the AGR building where the assailant fired shots and laid in a prone position to evaluate his line of sight. What I saw made me ashamed. As a career law enforcement officer, and a 25-year veteran with the Secret Service, I cannot defend why that roof was not better secured."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media's coverage framed Rowe's testimony as an illustration of chronic inter-agency jurisdictional dysfunction — a familiar feature of American law enforcement made intolerable when it contributes to an assassination attempt on one of the highest-value protectees in federal care.[1] Communication breakdowns, radio interoperability failures, cellular problems and inadequate redundancies were described as the center point of the hearing.[1] Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] pressed hard on the communications failures, while Senator [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] demanded the FBI's 302 interview reports and criticized the Secret Service for failing to memorialize its encrypted communications from July 13.[1] The Badlands assessment was that after four congressional hearings a "chasm of uncertainty" remained, with the two officials retreating under oath into plausible deniability, "lessons learned" and a focus on the future — and that the greatest victim of the resulting bureaucratic dysfunction was public trust.[1]
