# Senate Finance Committee

The Senate Finance Committee is a standing committee of the United States Senate with jurisdiction over taxation, revenue measures, and a broad range of financial matters, and with the investigative and subpoena authority that accompanies that jurisdiction.[1] Its chairmanship carries substantial practical leverage, because a chair backed by the chamber's majority can secure votes for compulsory document production that minority-party investigators cannot obtain.[1]

## 2024 investigation of Affinity Partners

In June 2024, committee chairman Senator [[ron-wyden|Ron Wyden]] (D-Ore.) opened an investigation into [[affinity-partners|Affinity Partners]], the Saudi-backed private equity firm led by [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]].[1] Reporting on the inquiry noted that Wyden's position as chair mattered precisely because he could potentially marshal enough votes to subpoena documents that House Democrats had sought without success.[1] Wyden transmitted a letter to Affinity's financial management regarding payments to the firm, a document that circulated publicly through the committee's website.[1]

The investigation focused on whether Kushner had discussed his plans for the firm with foreign governments while still serving in the White House, and on the near-total foreign composition of the fund's capital — including a $2 billion commitment from Saudi Arabia's [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]].[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary treated the committee's inquiry as an exercise in selective enforcement, arguing that the conduct alleged against Kushner was conventional by Washington standards and that the outrage reflected institutional resentment of an outsider competitor rather than a genuine anti-corruption impulse.[1]
