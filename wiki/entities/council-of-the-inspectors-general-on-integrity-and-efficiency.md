# Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency

The Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency (CIGIE) is the umbrella body coordinating the work of federal inspectors general, whose funding the Trump administration moved to halt at the start of October 2025.[1]

## Role

CIGIE has served as a "watchdog of the watchdogs," coordinating cross-agency oversight, conducting peer reviews and training, and running oversight.gov — a platform that allows whistleblowers to report misconduct and makes inspector general findings publicly accessible.[1] Its activities supported 72 inspectors general across the federal government.[1]

## 2025 funding cutoff

The Trump administration moved to halt CIGIE's funding beginning Wednesday, October 1, 2025, suspending the group's oversight and training activities.[1] In a letter sent the preceding Saturday to four congressional committees and obtained by The Washington Post, CIGIE said it would "cease our statutorily mandated functions and furlough 25 permanent employees" because of the funding cutoff ordered by the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]], led by Director [[russ-vought|Russell Vought]].[1]

The decision followed the administration's removal of inspectors general at 19 agencies and the appointment of political allies to posts traditionally held by nonpartisan officials, and it remained unclear how several oversight programs required by law would continue without CIGIE's operations.[1] John Vecchione, an attorney with the [[new-civil-liberties-alliance|New Civil Liberties Alliance]] who represented Cuffari, argued that CIGIE lacks a clear chain of command to the president and that OMB has the legal authority to halt its funding because part of its budget comes from interagency transfers overseen by OMB's deputy director for management.[1]
