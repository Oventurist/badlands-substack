# Karim Khan

Karim Khan is the chief prosecutor of the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] (ICC) at The Hague.[1]

## Gaza war and the Rafah crossing warning

In late October 2023, amid the [[israel-palestine-conflict|Israel–Gaza conflict]], Khan visited the Rafah crossing between Egypt and [[gaza|Gaza]] and issued a warning to [[israel|Israel]] suggesting that obstructing the flow of humanitarian aid into Gaza could potentially constitute a criminal act.[1] "Impeding relief supplies as provided by the Geneva Conventions may constitute a crime within the court's jurisdiction," Khan stated.[1]

Khan's office featured in Turkish plans to bring a war crimes case against Israel before the ICC. Because [[turkey|Turkey]] is not a party to the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]], it cannot apply directly to the court; however, under Article 15 of the Statute the Prosecutor may open investigations *proprio motu* — on his own initiative — based on information received, meaning Ankara could notify Khan's office of alleged crimes through government agencies and non-governmental organizations.[1]

## British pressure over Israeli arrest warrants

Middle East Eye reported that former British Prime Minister [[david-cameron|David Cameron]], then Foreign Secretary in Rishi Sunak's government, threatened to defund the ICC and withdraw the United Kingdom from the Rome Statute if Khan sought arrest warrants for Israeli leaders.[2] Khan reportedly first contacted London in April of the preceding year to inform officials of his intentions, after which Cameron telephoned him and warned that seeking the warrants would be "like dropping a hydrogen bomb."[2] The ICC issued the warrants that November for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and then Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], along with three [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders, over alleged war crimes in Gaza.[2] Washington subsequently sanctioned Khan, accusing him of abuse of power for pursuing cases against US allies.[2]

## May 2024 warrant applications against Israeli and Hamas leaders

In May 2024, Khan announced that he was seeking arrest warrants in a step widely described as dramatic, prompting [[norway|Norway]] to declare it would arrest Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] should warrants be issued and the two enter Norwegian territory.[3] Britain criticized Khan's decision, singling out the moral equivalence it drew between the Israeli leaders and [[hamas|Hamas]] figures [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], Muhammad Deif and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[3] France also came forward to support the warrants.[3]
