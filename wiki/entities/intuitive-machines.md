# Intuitive Machines

Intuitive Machines is an American space exploration company that in February 2024 landed its Odysseus spacecraft on the Moon, the first successful U.S. lunar landing in roughly half a century.[1] The mission's troubled aftermath became a widely covered market story as the company's publicly traded shares collapsed.[1]

## Odysseus mission

Odysseus touched down late in the week of February 22, 2024, but tipped over on landing.[1] The resulting attitude meant the lander's solar array could not be properly deployed and its antennas were pointed toward the lunar surface, slowing communications with operators on Earth.[1] Without adequate power the lander's heaters would not function, making survival through the frigid lunar night unlikely.[1]

On February 27 the company announced that Odysseus was expected to lose communications capability, writing on X that "Flight controllers intend to collect data until the lander's solar panels are no longer exposed to light" and that they believed contact would continue until Tuesday morning.[1] Intuitive Machines also posted an image of the lander resting on the lunar surface.[1]

## Market reaction

Shares fell as much as 27 percent in premarket trading in New York following the announcement.[1] From a high of nearly $14 a share before the landing the previous Thursday, the stock had crashed 67 percent.[1]

## Odysseus landing outcome

Following the February 22, 2024 touchdown that made Odysseus the first privately built and operated robot to complete a soft lunar landing, the spacecraft was found to be lying on its side with its head resting against a rock, though otherwise in good condition with ample power and communications to Earth.[2] Controllers worked to retrieve pictures from the robot while chief executive and co-founder Steve Altemus said the data suggested the lander caught a foot on the surface and fell because it retained some lateral motion at the moment of landing; another possibility was that it broke a leg on descent, with inertial measurement sensors indicating a horizontal pose.[2] See [[odysseus-lunar-lander-mission|the Odysseus lunar lander mission]].[2]
