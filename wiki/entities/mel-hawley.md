# Mel Hawley

Mel Hawley is an independent investigative journalist covering the prosecution and detention of January 6, 2021 defendants, publishing at sovsouls.us and at 1776returns.com.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] consulted Hawley to rebut claims made by Colorado Congressman [[ken-buck|Ken Buck]] about the treatment of January 6 defendants.[1]

## Reporting on January 6 detention

Hawley reported that pre-trial January 6 defendants are held in many prisons and jails across the country, not only the [[dc-jail|DC jail]], and estimated that roughly 125 defendants — about 12.5% of the more than 1,000 charged — had been indefinitely detained for at least some period.[1] Of those, she estimated a third to half had some history of violent offenses while the remainder had none, that fully half of those arrested, charged with violence and indefinitely detained were military veterans, many highly decorated, and that at least a third of those indefinitely detained on felony charges had witnessed the death of Rosanne Boyland at the hands of Capitol Police.[1] She characterized the common "crime" of many detained veterans as membership in [[oath-keepers|The Oathkeepers]] or the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]].[1]

Hawley reported that DC detainees went two years without video or physical visitation with family and without religious services; when she telephoned the jail chaplain to ask about the denial, he hung up on her upon learning her inquiry concerned January 6 detainees.[1] She notified the legislative director for Representative [[troy-nehls|Troy Nehls]], who reportedly was also hung up on; after that office intervened, detainees began receiving some religious services.[1] She further reported that for the first year of detention, J6 detainees were given only Nation of Islam Final Call newsletters to read in their cells — no Bibles, newspapers or law books.[1]

Hawley documented the practice of [[diesel-therapy|diesel therapy]], in which defendants are continually transferred between jails without enough time at any location to establish commissary or gain access to discovery.[1] She also reported on the DC jail's deputy warden Kathleen Landerkin, who had posted vulgar anti-Trump material on social media before deleting the account and was later fired; Hawley said she was investigating the reasons for the dismissal.[1]

In a September 7, 2023 piece on vindictive prosecution, Hawley wrote that "we are at war, a seemingly civil one," and that "the casualties are there, if you have eyes to see," citing the August 28, 2023 suicide of 22-year-old Nejourde "Jord" Meacham two weeks after he was charged with four non-violent misdemeanors related to January 6.[1]
