# Sergei Witte

Sergei Witte (also rendered Sergey Witte) was a Russian statesman who served as Transport Minister and Prime Minister and is described by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the greatest of the Russian "American System economists" who studied, translated, and applied the political economy of [[friedrich-list|Friedrich List]] and, through him, of [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]].[1]

## Infrastructure program

Witte oversaw the completion of the Trans-Siberian Railway and envisioned a line that would eventually connect the Americas to Russia by way of the Bering Straits.[1] Ehret presents these projects as characteristic of the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System of Political Economy]], which prioritizes large-scale, long-term infrastructure that raises living standards, cognitive powers, and the productive powers of labor simultaneously.[1]

## Place in the anti-imperial tradition

Witte appears in Ehret's list of world leaders outside the United States who led their nations to break free of British imperial systems of control between the eighteenth and twentieth centuries and who were frequently students and practitioners of the Hamiltonian system.[1] That list also includes French President Sadi Carnot, Germany's Friedrich List, China's first president Sun Yat-sen, and Argentina's Foreign Minister of 1902–1903, Luis Marie Drago.[1]

## Advocacy of the Bering Strait tunnel

In 1905, acting on Witte's advice, Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] gave national support to the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] and funded a team of American engineers to carry out feasibility studies.[2] The proposal followed the completion of the [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] between 1890 and 1905, a project that had drawn heavily on Baldwin Locomotives made in Philadelphia and on hundreds of American technicians and engineers.[2]
