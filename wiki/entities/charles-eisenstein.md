# Charles Eisenstein

Charles Eisenstein is an author cited in Badlands Media's essay "Heroes of the Great Awakening" for his essay "The Conspiracy Myth," published by Science and Nonduality.[1] The article invokes him to defend a non-literal reading of popular beliefs circulating in the movement, specifically the widespread claim that [[jfk-jr|JFK Jr.]] is still alive.[1]

The author writes that while "JFK Jr. being among the living is a myth, that doesn't mean it's not true with the right perspective in mind," and then quotes Eisenstein at length.[1] In the quoted passage Eisenstein argues that "a myth is not the same thing as a fantasy or a delusion," that "myths are vehicles of truth, and that truth needn't be literal," and that classical Greek myths "seem like mere amusements until one decodes them by associating each god with psychosocial forces."[1] Myths, he continues, "bring light to the shadows and reveal what has been repressed," taking "a truth about the psyche or society" and forming it "into a story"; their truth "does not depend on whether it is objectively verifiable."[1]

Eisenstein's framework supplies the essay's interpretive turn. Having used it, the author proceeds to argue that the enduring meaning of the Kennedy story is that "his lineage is more than just a bloodline," and that victory will come not from a single charismatic figure but from ordinary people carrying on that spirit.[1]
