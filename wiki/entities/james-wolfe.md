# James Wolfe

James Wolfe was the English general who died at the [[battle-of-quebec|Battle of Quebec]] on the Plains of Abraham in 1759, at the moment the French lost their hold on North America.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He was a young man at the time of his death.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Depiction by Benjamin West

Wolfe is the subject of [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]]'s 1770 painting *The Death of General Wolfe*, which West executed in contemporary costume in defiance of the European convention requiring Greco-Roman dress in history painting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The realities of Wolfe's death — he was shot and died behind a bush with two people in attendance — were set aside in favour of a crowded composition including [[william-johnson|William Johnson]] and a central Mohawk warrior.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] reads the resulting contrast between the vigorous native figure and the weakness of the dying British aristocratic Wolfe as a subtle symbol of the dying British Empire itself, offering one message to American republicans and another to British aristocrats.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Two figures from the right side of this painting — a priest and a red-coated soldier — reappear on the rag in West's 1776 self-portrait.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
