# Heinrich Himmler

Heinrich Himmler was the chief of the Nazi [[schutzstaffel|SS]] and, in the account given by Matthew Ehret's series "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler," the head of the [[new-templar-order|New Templar Order]] within the Nazi high command.[1] He is presented not merely as a police-state administrator but as the principal organizer of the occult religion that Ehret argues bound the Nazi leadership together.[1]

## Thule Society milieu

Himmler is identified as part of the leading strata of the Nazi high command shaped by [[dietrich-eckart|Dietrich Eckart]]'s [[thule-society|Thule Society]], a group that also included Alfred Rosenberg, Hermann Göring and Hitler's personal physician Dr. Theodor Morel, and which took control of Germany in 1933.[1] Within that circle Himmler is described as the "chief of the New Templar Order."[1]

## Wewelsburg Castle

In 1934 Himmler took a 100-year lease on the dilapidated [[wewelsburg-castle|Wewelsburg Castle]] and renovated it to serve as the center of the projected new world Reich, embedding swastikas and pagan symbolism throughout and making it the headquarters of his New Templar high initiates before and during the Second World War.[1] On the floor of a central tower he commissioned a marble [[black-sun|Black Sun]] symbol.[1] Obsessed with the Grail myths of Parsifal and with King Arthur's Round Table, Himmler modelled his inner SS priesthood on the twelve Arthurian knights, who were to meet in a circular chamber known as "The Crypt" around an eternal flame; two further chambers were named "The Grail" and "King Arthur."[1] Himmler envisaged a huge complex with the castle at its centre, to be called the "Centre of the World," where victorious Nazis would rule their empire, SS "knights" would perform occult rituals, and the remains of fallen SS officers would be interred.[1]

## The Ahnenerbe and occult expeditions

By 1935 Himmler had taken control of all branches of Nazi science through the creation of the [[ahnenerbe|Ahnenerbe]], the Ancestral Heritage Research and Teaching Society, which he led until the end of the war and which reorganized anthropology, geology, agronomy and archaeology around a pagan/Nazi filter.[1] With nearly unlimited resources he despatched anthropologists to find the origins of the Aryan root race in Tibet, where SS officer [[ernst-schafer|Ernst Schäfer]] entered the country illegally, befriended and mentored the young Dalai Lama, and measured Tibetan skulls.[1] Himmler, obsessed with the Rosicrucian writings of [[edward-bulwer-lytton|Edward Bulwer-Lytton]], also assigned teams to seek the entrance to the hollow earth in Antarctica, the supposed Atlantean colonies in the South American Andes, and the Theosophists' mythical Shamballah in Tibet.[1] As a self-styled Grail knight and follower of the Templars, he allocated vast resources to the search for the Holy Grail, hiring the Wagner enthusiast [[otto-rahn|Otto Rahn]] to lead the expedition, and — like the Freemasonic Quatuor Coronati Research Lodge of Sir Charles Warren and King Edward VII — was devoted to finding the Lost Ark beneath Solomon's Temple.[1]

## End of the war

As the war drew to a close Himmler ordered the demolition of Wewelsburg Castle, which was carried out days before Allied forces could capture it, burying many of its secrets.[1] The castle was nonetheless swiftly reconstructed with Marshall Plan funding and by 1950 had become a museum and one of the largest boys' homes in Germany.[1]

## Ahnenerbe, Eichmann and the Jewish transfer policy

During his time managing the [[ahnenerbe|Ahnenerbe]], Reichsführer Himmler interfaced closely with [[adolf-eichmann|Adolf Eichmann]], the chief minister responsible for "Jewish Affairs" who oversaw the creation of the ghetto system in Germany.[2] Eichmann went on to lead the crafting of the 1933 [[haavara-agreement|Haavara Agreement]] with the British Empire, establishing Nazi support for the transfer of Jews to British-Mandate Palestine in preparation for the creation of a Jewish state.[2]

Himmler is also named by the historian William Laurence Shirer, alongside Alfred Rosenberg and [[martin-bormann|Martin Bormann]] and backed by [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]], as a leader of the Nazi regime's intention to destroy Christianity in Germany and substitute the old paganism of the early tribal Germanic gods and the new paganism of the Nazi extremists.[2]
