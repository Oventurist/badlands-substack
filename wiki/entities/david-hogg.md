# David Hogg

David Hogg is an American gun control activist and prominent progressive political figure.[1]

## Position on the New Mexico carry suspension

In September 2023 Hogg was cited, alongside Representative [[ted-lieu|Ted Lieu]], as one of the prominent leftists who affirmed that states do not possess the power to strike down the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] — a response to Governor [[michelle-lujan-grisham|Michelle Lujan Grisham]]'s emergency order suspending public firearms carry in Albuquerque.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized these statements as tactical rather than principled, arguing that such figures oppose states' rights generally and want gun restrictions enacted at the federal level via constitutional amendment, mirroring the establishment's insistence on federal legislation after abortion policy was returned to the states.[1]

## Exit from the DNC (2025)

Hogg's tenure as a [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] vice chair ended with what *The New York Times* described as an unceremonious exit in June 2025, following a protracted and public fight with chair [[ken-martin|Ken Martin]].[2] The 25-year-old activist had blindsided party officials with a plan to challenge incumbent Democrats in primaries; the resulting clash included the leak of embarrassing audio of Martin questioning his own role, and worsened perceptions that Martin had been badly distracted by internal battles at a time when the committee's finances were deteriorating sharply.[2]

## DNC vice chair re-vote (June 2025)

In June 2025 members of the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] voted 294–99 to redo the vice chair election, teeing up two separate votes for the positions held by Hogg and Pennsylvania state Representative [[malcolm-kenyatta|Malcolm Kenyatta]].[3] The challenge had been filed in late February by Oklahoma DNC member [[kalyn-free|Kalyn Free]], who had run for a vice chair position and lost and who alleged the election was conducted in a way that unfairly advantaged the male candidates.[3] The Credentials Committee advanced the challenge, and the remedy was two virtual ballots — one June 12–14 for a male vice chair and one June 15–17 open to any gender.[3] Hogg announced through his Leaders We Deserve group that he would forgo a run for his spot on the DNC.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked the participants as "election deniers," while [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] wrote that "the DNC is attempting to abort David Hogg."[3]
