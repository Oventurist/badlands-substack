# Joseph McCarthy

Joseph McCarthy is cited by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] as one of the early "Communist-fighting-Patriots" whose work he links to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a theme he had developed in an earlier essay on Trump, JFK, Patton and McCarthy.[1]

## Timing argument

Carlson's chief use of McCarthy is chronological. He notes that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] was formed in 1947 following World War II, though it already existed in substance as the [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]], and that by 1953 the agency was covertly overthrowing the Iranian government in the [[1953-iranian-coup|coup against Mohammad Mosaddegh]].[1] McCarthy was going after Communists in the United States at that same moment.[1] To Carlson, the coincidence of dates is evidence that the CIA had been strategically planning to overthrow America since at least the early 1950s, with the intent of replacing the US government with a Communist-like, totalitarian one — raising for him the question of whether that was the real reason the agency was created.[1]

## Senate career and the infiltration thesis

McCarthy was the former Senator from Wisconsin who, Carlson writes in an earlier essay, was "very aware in the 1950's that Communists had infiltrated D.C. and the media, and they were planning to take over America from the inside."[2] Carlson holds that McCarthy was "100% correct in his fears and understanding," and that pretty much everyone he accused of being a Communist was in fact a Communist.[2] McCarthy was nonetheless ridiculed and demonized by the media and by other politicians for voicing his concern, dismissed as a conspiracy theorist and a fascist, and left by the history books as a pariah and "a stain on America."[2] The reason he became the villain, Carlson argues, is that the media and rival politicians had louder megaphones and greater numbers — an illustration of his contention that perceived truth often means only that more people believe or espouse something.[2]

## Death at Walter Reed

McCarthy died mysteriously on 2 May 1957 at [[walter-reed-national-military-medical-center|Walter Reed National Military Medical Center]] in Bethesda, Maryland.[2] Carlson notes that eight years earlier, on 22 May 1949, [[james-forrestal|James Forrestal]] — Secretary of Defense, fellow anti-Communist and mentor to [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] — allegedly jumped from the sixteenth floor of the same hospital, and that Kennedy's body was later examined there after his assassination.[2] He groups McCarthy with Forrestal, [[george-s-patton|General George S. Patton]] and Kennedy as men who fought [[communism|Communism]] and died mysteriously, and with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as men subjected to character assassination by press and government.[2]

## Sanctimonious Stu and the Cohn connection

Among the enemies McCarthy made while pursuing suspected Communists in the government and military was [[stuart-symington|Stuart Symington]], Secretary of the Air Force from 1947 to 1950 and later a senator on the Armed Services and Foreign Relations Committees, who became a leading critic of McCarthy and of [[mccarthyism|McCarthyism]].[2] McCarthy repaid the hostility by calling him "Sanctimonious Stu," even during official Senate hearings — a usage Carlson links to Trump's nickname "De-Sanctimonious" for [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]].[2] McCarthy's chief counsel during the 1954 [[army-mccarthy-hearings|Army–McCarthy hearings]], and his assistant in the investigations of suspected Communists, was [[roy-cohn|Roy Cohn]], who decades later became Trump's mentor in New York real estate.[2] Carlson treats this chain of mentorships as evidence that there are "too many connections to dismiss things as being coincidence," concluding that Trump is a modern-day McCarthy, Forrestal, Patton and JFK "all rolled into one."[2]
