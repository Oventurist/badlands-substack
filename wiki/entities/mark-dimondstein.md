# Mark Dimondstein

Mark Dimondstein is the president of the [[american-postal-workers-union|American Postal Workers Union]] (APWU) and, in A Seismic Shift, the author of an opinion piece in The Hill advocating the expansion of [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]] banking. [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] describes him as making "a lot of great points" and as reminding readers that postal banking was a safeguard for many years in the United States and protected the American people's savings accounts.

## Argument in The Hill

Writing after the bank crisis set off by the failures of Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank, Dimondstein argued that the episode had exposed a reality about how U.S. banking really works: the system's very existence — insuring deposits, loan guarantees, setting interest rates — depends entirely on the federal government. Any debate about the future of banking, he wrote, should first take a page from the history of postal banking.

He recounted that during its 55-year existence, beginning in 1910 by an act of [[congress|Congress]], the [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]] became one of the largest depository institutions in the country, served as a safe harbour during the Great Depression, and provided simple and reliable basic banking services, especially in rural communities. Postal banking as an essential public service, he concluded, "is an old idea whose time has come again."

## FedAccounts

Dimondstein's column also introduced Lange's readers to the [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] concept, noting that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] currently serves as a bank for bankers — facilitating inter-bank payments, managing bank accounts and paying interest on those accounts — and that the same services could be extended to individuals and small businesses, "creating a 'FedAccount' for everyone, not just the banks." He observed that there is growing interest in and support for the concept. Lange treats this as the introduction of a second major safeguard for the American future.
