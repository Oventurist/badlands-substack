# Mark Dimondstein

Mark Dimondstein is the president of the [[american-postal-workers-union|American Postal Workers Union]] (APWU) and the author of an opinion piece in The Hill arguing for the expansion of [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]] banking.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Argument for postal banking

Writing in the wake of the bank crisis set off by the failures of Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank, Dimondstein argued that the episode exposed a reality about how U.S. banking really works: the system's very existence — insuring deposits, loan guarantees, setting interest rates — depends entirely on the federal government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Any debate about the future of banking in the United States, he wrote, should first take a page from the history of postal banking.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

He noted that during its 55-year existence, beginning in 1910 by an act of Congress, the [[postal-savings-system|U.S. Postal Savings System]] became one of the largest depository institutions in the country, served as a safe harbor during the Great Depression, and provided simple and reliable basic banking services especially in rural communities — concluding that postal banking as an essential public service is an old idea whose time has come again.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## FedAccounts

Dimondstein's piece also introduced the [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] concept to the article's argument, observing that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] currently serves as a bank for bankers — facilitating inter-bank payments, managing accounts and paying interest on them — and that the same service could be extended to individuals and small businesses, creating a "FedAccount for everyone."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media credits him with making a strong case that postal banking was a durable safeguard that protected Americans' savings for many years.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)
