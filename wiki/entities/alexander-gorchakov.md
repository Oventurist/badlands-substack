# Alexander Gorchakov

Gorchakov was the Russian Foreign Minister under Czar [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] and part of the circle of statesmen — with [[grand-duke-constantine|Grand Duke Constantine]], [[nikolai-muraviev|General Nikolai Muraviev]] and Ambassador [[eduard-de-stoeckl|Eduard de Stoeckl]] — that gained new influence after the [[crimean-war|Crimean War]] and formulated a foreign policy doctrine treating the United States as a strategic collaborator against the British Empire.[1]

During the American [[american-civil-war|Civil War]], Stoeckl wrote to Gorchakov that the disintegration of the United States "would from our point of view be something to be deplored," because the American union served as a counterpoise to British power and an important element in the balance of power, and that he desired "the preservation of the American Union as an indivisible nation."[1]
