# Dyno Nobel

Dyno Nobel is a company described as a leader in the commercial explosives business, and was the shipper responsible for packing and transporting a 30-ton railcar load of ammonium nitrate that vanished in transit between Wyoming and California in April 2023.[1]

## 2023 missing ammonium nitrate shipment

On April 12, 2023, a railcar loaded with 30 tons — approximately 61,000 pounds — of ammonium nitrate left Cheyenne, Wyoming.[1] According to an incident report filed by Dyno Nobel, when the car arrived roughly two weeks later at a rail stop in the Mojave Desert, at Saltdale, California, it was completely empty.[1] Dyno Nobel filed a report with the federal [[national-response-center|National Response Center]] on May 10, 2023.[1]

The company's stated theory was leakage. "The railcar was sealed when it left the Cheyenne facility, and the seals were still intact when it arrived in Saltdale. The initial assessment is that a leak through the bottom gate on the railcar may have developed in transit," Dyno Nobel told KQED News.[1] The [[federal-railroad-administration|Federal Railroad Administration]], the [[california-public-utilities-commission|California Public Utilities Commission]], [[union-pacific|Union Pacific]] and Dyno Nobel all opened inquiries, and the railcar was returned to Wyoming for inspection.[1]

Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the leak theory did not hold up given that all 61,000 pounds were gone and the seals were intact on arrival, suggesting that if the loss was a heist rather than a leak it would have been sophisticated and would have required insiders at the commercial explosives company.[1] She also noted that Dyno Nobel had "a team of investigators investigating themselves," and predicted the outcome would resemble [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine, Ohio]] — vague answers followed by the story being memory-holed.[1] Ammonium nitrate, a fertilizer also used as an explosives ingredient, was used in the 1995 [[oklahoma-city-bombing|attack on the Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City]], which killed 168 people and injured approximately 850.[1]
