# House Weaponization Committee

The House Weaponization Committee is the United States House of Representatives select subcommittee established to investigate the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of the federal government]], including government involvement in the censorship of American speech.[1]

## Hunter Biden laptop testimony

In July 2023 the committee heard testimony from [[emma-jo-morris|Emma-Jo Morris]], an editor at [[breitbart|Breitbart]] and former [[new-york-post|New York Post]] editor, concerning the censorship of the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] story.[1] Morris described the coordinated character of the social media blackout on the reporting and the potential collusion between federal agencies and media outlets such as [[politico|Politico]] in casting the story as "Russian disinformation" shortly before the 2020 election.[1]

## March 2023 Taibbi and Shellenberger hearing

On March 9, 2023, the subcommittee heard testimony from journalists [[matt-taibbi|Matt Taibbi]] and [[michael-shellenberger|Michael Shellenberger]] on the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]], in a session described as full of fireworks, facts, and ad hominem friction.[2] Ranking Member [[stacey-plaskett|Stacey E. Plaskett]] opened by calling the two witnesses "so-called journalists" and a "threat" to former Twitter employees, alleging that Republicans had brought in "two of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s 'public scribes'" to release cherry-picked, out-of-context emails and screenshots serving Musk's narrative.[2] Taibbi replied that he was not a so-called journalist, citing the National Magazine Award, the I.F. Stone Award for Independent Journalism, and ten books.[2]

Taibbi told the panel that the Files documented "a sweeping effort ... to turn the internet into an instrument of censorship and social control" in which the U.S. government "appears to be playing a lead role," such that "news media became an arm of a state-sponsored thought-policing system."[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the 2023 committees functioned less as kangaroo courts than as "massive exposure and disclosure operations" designed to keep the media industrial complex permanently on the defensive.[2]

## Significance

Coverage of the hearing by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] argued that the public, bombarded by successive crises, needed reminding of the scale of censorship between 2020 and 2022, and pointed to congressional investigations that established [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]]'s role in suppressing the [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]].[1] The committee's work is frequently grouped with the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]], the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]] and the disclosures in [[murthy-v-missouri|Missouri et al v. Biden]] as part of the documented record of the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]].[1]

## First hearing (February 2023)

The subcommittee held its first hearing in February 2023, at which a Democrat-called witness, [[elliot-williams|Elliot Williams]], defended the [[fbi|FBI]]'s collusion with Big Tech to censor conservatives.[3] Representative [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] used his questioning to establish that Williams was a partner at the [[raben-group|Raben Group]], a lobbying firm whose clients include [[google|Google]], [[george-soros|George Soros]]'s [[open-society-foundations|Open Society]], and [[pfizer|Pfizer]].[3] Badlands Media commentary held that although the legislature lacks prosecutorial powers, such hearings serve their designed function by placing the conduct of federal agencies and their private-sector allies on the public record.[3]

## July 2023 censorship hearing

A July 2023 hearing of the subcommittee, operating within the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], was convened to examine the federal government's role in censoring Americans, Big Tech's collusion with government agencies to silence speech, and the [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri v. Biden]] lawsuit.[4] Witnesses included [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], Louisiana special assistant attorney general [[d-john-sauer|D. John Sauer]], Breitbart journalist [[emma-jo-morris|Emma-Jo Morris]], and [[maya-wiley|Maya Wiley]], president and CEO of the Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights.[4] The proceedings were marked by repeated interruptions and by an attempt from House Democrats to remove Kennedy as a witness, which he characterised as "an attempt to censor a censorship hearing."[4]
