# Jared Moskowitz

Jared Moskowitz is a Democratic member of the U.S. House of Representatives from Florida who sits on the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]].[1]

## March 2024 impeachment-inquiry hearing

At the committee's March 20, 2024 hearing into the Biden family's alleged influence peddling, Moskowitz challenged Oversight Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] and Judiciary Committee Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] to hold an immediate vote on impeaching President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], arguing that they would do so if they had genuinely found evidence of high crimes and misdemeanors.[1]

Badlands Media commentary described Moskowitz as having "screeched at the committee chair to immediately hold an impeachment vote," listing his conduct alongside that of [[jamie-raskin|Jamie Raskin]], [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], and [[dan-goldman|Dan Goldman]] as examples of what the writer characterized as Democratic theatrics that drained the proceeding of credibility.[1] The commentary also argued that the Republican majority's reluctance to move to a vote reflected "uniparty" pressure rather than a lack of evidence.[1]

## Letter to the UN over Israel's status (October 2024)

In late October 2024, Moskowitz, a Democrat representing Florida, joined Republican [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] of New York in spearheading a bipartisan letter to United Nations Secretary-General [[antonio-guterres|António Guterres]] signed by more than one hundred members of [[congress|Congress]].[2] The letter warned that any downgrade in Israel's status or standing at the UN General Assembly would produce a corresponding downgrade in U.S. financial, material and political support for the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[2]

Its signatories declared that they had concluded "the UN is not a neutral party but one that has definitively taken sides against Israel," citing numerous UN actions "aimed to delegitimize Israel's right to self-defense," and noted that American contributions account for roughly one-third of the body's collective budget.[2] The letter was reported alongside the [[knesset|Knesset]]'s vote to ban [[unrwa|UNRWA]].[2]
