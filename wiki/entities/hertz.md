# Hertz Global Holdings

Hertz Global Holdings Inc. is an American rental car company that became a prominent symbol of corporate retreat from electric vehicles in early 2024.[1]

## Electrification push and reversal

In 2021 Hertz tapped quarterback [[tom-brady|Tom Brady]] to promote a massive push to electrify its fleet, and placed a huge order for 100,000 [[tesla|Tesla]] Model 3 vehicles.[1] By late October 2023, Hertz chief executive Stephen Scherr told investors on a third-quarter earnings call that the company would begin reducing its EV fleet because of high repair costs relative to the rest of the fleet, which had hurt its bottom line, saying "EV's will be slower than our prior expectations."[1] Scherr also told CNBC's Jim Cramer that EVs sustain more damage than combustion-engine cars and cost far more to repair.[1]

According to Bloomberg, Hertz began dumping 20,000 EVs on the used car market in December 2023 and would remain a seller through 2024, with the sales expected to produce a non-cash fourth-quarter charge of $245 million related to incremental net depreciation expense.[1] The company said it expected to reinvest a portion of the proceeds into internal combustion engine vehicles to meet customer demand and to "better balance supply against expected demand of EVs."[1] The story was reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and carried in the Badlands News Brief of January 12, 2024.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Brady had endured a rough run of promotional ventures, with the Hertz EV campaign following the collapse of FTX as another embarrassment to the quarterback's business portfolio.[1] She argued the episode vindicated [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s skepticism of electric vehicles, contending that EVs are more expensive, more breakable, less reliable and harder to power given existing infrastructure, and that customer demand does not lie.[1] She further speculated about whether Hertz would face government blowback for the decision, remarking that in "Biden's America" businesses no longer seem free to make their own decisions.[1]
