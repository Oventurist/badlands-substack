# Global Engagement Center

The Global Engagement Center (GEC) was an office established within the [[state-department|U.S. State Department]] under the Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs, created by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] in connection with the [[countering-foreign-propaganda-and-disinformation-act-of-2016|Countering Foreign Propaganda and Disinformation Act of 2016]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Mandate

Its stated purpose was "to direct, lead, synchronize, integrate, and coordinate U.S. Federal Government efforts to recognize, understand, expose, and counter foreign state and non-state propaganda and disinformation efforts aimed at undermining or influencing the policies, security, or stability of the United States, its allies, and partner nations."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Badlands critique

Badlands Media presents the GEC's creation as an admission that the [[centralized-media|mainstream media]] was already terminal before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s rise, and as a foundational institution of what independent journalists call the [[censorship-industrial-complex|Censorship Industrial Complex]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The article contends that the apparatus enabled a durable strategy of labeling any incriminating evidence of establishment corruption "Russian disinformation" — a tactic it says persists today.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Shutdown before the second Trump term

In a December 2024 court filing, the [[state-department|State Department]] revealed that it was "substantially likely" to shut down the $100 million Global Engagement Center, which had been revealed in early 2023 to have funded a "disinformation" tracking group that worked to pressure advertisers into demonetizing outlets it accused of spreading disinformation.[2] Critics noted, however, that the department was in practice merely "realigning" the center's staff and funding to other department offices and bureaus handling foreign information manipulation.[2]

The move came amid a lawsuit brought by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] and several conservative media outlets that had been listed on a GEC-funded "dynamic exclusion list" of disfavored websites, which was distributed to ad tech companies such as Microsoft's Xandr in order to "defund and downrank these worst offenders" and deprive the sites of ad revenue.[2] Headline USA's Ken Silva noted it was unclear how the closure would affect the lawsuit, with the December filing indicating that lawyers for all parties were still discussing the implications.[2] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had put the GEC on the map in March 2023 when he deemed it the "worst offender in U.S. government" for censorship and media manipulation.[2] According to revelations from the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]], the GEC funded groups such as the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]]'s [[digital-forensic-research-lab|Digital Forensic Research Lab]], which compiled blacklists of Twitter accounts supposedly tied to foreign disinformation campaigns and sent them to Twitter for deactivation.[2]

## Funding in the December 2024 continuing resolution

During the [[2024-december-continuing-resolution-fight|December 2024 continuing resolution fight]], Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the withdrawn stopgap bill would have continued funding the Global Engagement Center, which she described as "the censorship brain trust that is responsible for deplatforming, debanking, and dehumanizing Americans who have the wrong political ideas."[3] She argued that the case for continued funding was sold with "tiny violins and sad tales of government bureaucrats losing their phoney baloney jobs," while many Americans who had built livelihoods on Facebook, YouTube and Twitter had lost them overnight, often with no stated cause or ability to appeal.[3] Forcing Americans to fund what she called an unconstitutional ministry of truth, she wrote, was a central driver of public anger at the bill.[3]

## Closure in December 2024

The Global Engagement Center shut down in December 2024 after Republicans cut its funding, with the agency announcing on a Monday that it would cease operations by the end of that day and adding that "The State Department has consulted with Congress regarding next steps."[4] At the time of closure the organization employed around 120 people and had an annual budget of $61 million; established in 2016, its stated goal was to "recognize, understand, expose, and counter foreign state and non-state propaganda and disinformation efforts."[4]

In practice, reporting held that the GEC spearheaded complex propaganda campaigns of its own, including two campaigns funding video games aimed at teaching children about the supposed dangers of anti-American narratives, released in the UK, Ukraine, Latvia, Iraq and Saudi Arabia.[4] During the coronavirus pandemic the center funneled money to a range of NGOs that compiled lists of social media accounts supposedly spreading "disinformation" about the virus and its origins, which were then presented to platforms for banning or removal; many of the accounts belonged to what Twitter's former trust and safety chief Yoel Roth called "ordinary Americans," raising conservative concerns that the GEC was violating its prohibition on operating within the United States.[4]

In 2023 the GEC was forced to cut ties with George Soros' Global Disinformation Initiative after it emerged that the agency had been paying the Soros organization to compile lists of "high risk" news outlets for use in an advertiser boycott campaign, outlets that were predominantly right-leaning and American-based.[4] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] called the GEC a "threat to our democracy" and described it as the "worst offender in US government censorship [and] media manipulation," and was instrumental in its final shutdown: a 1,547-page spending bill put before the House of Representatives by Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] would have preserved the agency's funding until Musk threatened to fund primary election challenges against any Republican who voted for it.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified the GEC as the organization [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] frequently discusses, founded in 2016 to push back against the rising tide of populism, and noted that the center is connected to [[newsguard|NewsGuard]], the ratings organization that sent a threatening letter to [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] over Badlands Media content.[4] He concluded that the shutdown "couldn't have happened to a more deserving group."[4]

## Daily Wire v. State Department (2023)

The Global Engagement Center was the central target of the December 2023 lawsuit brought by [[daily-wire|The Daily Wire]], [[the-federalist|The Federalist]] and the state of Texas against the [[state-department|State Department]], filed by the [[new-civil-liberties-alliance|New Civil Liberties Alliance]] in the Eastern District of Texas.[5] Plaintiffs alleged that the government had taken an office created to counter foreign terrorist propaganda and "unleashed it against Americans" accused of disinformation, funding censorship technology intended to bankrupt disfavored domestic outlets.[5] The suit sought a declaration that the effort was illegal and a permanent injunction against promoting technology to de-amplify, shadow ban or restrict lawful American speech, naming Secretary [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and five other officials.[5] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the GEC is "a centralized control mechanism within the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]]" that independent researchers in the truth community had been exposing for over a year, and that with an election year arriving "the re-runs are in marathon mode."[5]
