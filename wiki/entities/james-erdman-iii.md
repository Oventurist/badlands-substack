# James Erdman III

James Erdman III is a Central Intelligence Agency whistleblower and, at the time of his testimony, a current agency employee who publicly accused Dr. Anthony Fauci of interfering in the intelligence community's assessment of the origins of COVID-19.[1]

## Senate testimony

Erdman testified before the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee on a Wednesday in May 2026, telling members that [[anthony-fauci|Dr. Anthony Fauci]] "injected" himself into the intelligence community's review of COVID-19's origins and influenced how the [[cia|CIA]] handled its assessment of a potential lab leak.[1]

According to Erdman, Fauci played an "intentional" role in the agency's move away from a 2021 plan to characterize the virus as having originated from a laboratory leak following a 90-day review ordered that year. He stated that the CIA was "considering calling this a lab leak" on August 12, 2021, before its position changed on August 17, and said the agency has not produced documentation explaining the reversal.[1]

"Dr. Fauci's role in the cover-up was intentional," Erdman testified, alleging that Fauci shaped the analytical process by steering the intelligence community toward "curated" lists of subject matter experts and scientists, including contacts in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia.[1]

## Conflict-of-interest allegations

Erdman further alleged that Fauci had contact with intelligence officials during 2020 and 2021, and that he raised concerns internally about potential conflicts of interest tied to research linked to Wuhan. He said those concerns were not escalated within the agency.[1]

## Reception

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Erdman's testimony as part of what she described as a steady accumulation of evidence and witnesses in "vast and various treasonous plots against the American people," spanning Russiagate, election fraud, and COVID-19, and argued that the central question is not only whether a justice phase arrives but what citizens themselves will do about entrenched public corruption.[1]
