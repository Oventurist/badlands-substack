# Ayman Joumaa

Ayman Joumaa is a Medellín-based Lebanese businessman identified by U.S. investigators as the head of one of the largest money-laundering and drug-smuggling networks the [[drug-enforcement-administration|Drug Enforcement Administration]] had ever encountered.[1]

## Network

According to Politico reporting quoted in the Badlands Media essay "Panic in DC," investigations in Colombia and Venezuela linked Joumaa's network to Hashem Safieddine, a senior [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] figure operating from Tehran whom investigators regarded as their key point of connection into the organization.[1]

Joumaa's operation raised alarm in Washington when agents discovered he was working with Mexico's Los Zetas cartel to move multi-ton cocaine loads directly into the United States.[1] The laundering side of the business washed roughly $200 million a month with the help of approximately 300 used-car dealerships: enormous sums were funnelled to the dealerships to purchase used vehicles, which were then shipped to Benin on Africa's west coast for resale.[1]

## Significance

The Joumaa network is central to the argument advanced in the article, both as evidence that [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] functioned as an international crime syndicate — the "Laundromat" — and as the mechanism connecting Hezbollah's finances to the American domestic economy.[1] It is the factual basis for the essay's further claim that the [[cash-for-clunkers|Cash for Clunkers]] program was designed to supply the network with used vehicles.[1] The network was a principal target of [[project-cassandra|Project Cassandra]].[1]
