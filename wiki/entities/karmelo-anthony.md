# Karmelo Anthony

Karmelo Anthony is a Texas teenager accused of fatally stabbing high school football player [[austin-metcalf|Austin Metcalf]] at a track meet in April 2025. He was 17 at the time of the reporting.[1]

## Bond and living arrangements

Anthony was released from jail on Monday, 14 April 2025 on a reduced $250,000 bond, after his defense had asked a judge to lower the original $1 million bond on grounds of financial hardship.[1] Subsequent reporting said he was staying with his family at a roughly $900,000 home inside the gated community of Richwoods in Frisco, Texas, where rent was estimated at $3,500 a month.[1]

Reporters observed a white Suburban, a black Acura and a third sedan in the driveway, and a neighbour said the family had recently purchased a new vehicle, noting its paper tag with a June 4 expiration.[1] The contrast between the claimed financial difficulty and the family's apparent circumstances drove much of the coverage, which Badlands carried as a bonus item in its 17 April 2025 news brief.[1]

## Indictment for first-degree murder

On Tuesday, June 24, 2025, Collin County District Attorney [[greg-willis|Greg Willis]] announced that a grand jury had indicted Karmelo Sincere Anthony for first-degree murder in the April 2, 2025 stabbing death of Austin Metcalf during a high school track meet in Frisco.[2] Both boys were 17 at the time of the incident, and the Texas criminal justice system considers 17-year-olds to be adults.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that since Metcalf's death the public had been "gaslit" into believing Anthony was the true victim and the affair an example of institutionalized racism, and asked readers to imagine how that framing felt to Metcalf's family, particularly the twin brother who held him as he died.[2] She noted that authorities had finally released CCTV footage of the event days earlier, which she said disputed the widespread narrative of the killing by showing the stabber as the aggressor, and that legal experts believed the video undercut Anthony's defenses.[2] She added that more than half a million dollars had been raised for Anthony since he became an alleged murderer, which she said fit "society's overall trend of lionizing dangerous criminals."[2]
