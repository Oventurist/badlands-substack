# Payton Gendron

Payton Gendron is the gunman who killed ten people at a grocery store in Buffalo, New York, in 2022, and whose online activity became the factual centerpiece of a novel civil lawsuit against major social media platforms.[1]

## Role in the social media litigation

Gendron was 18 years old at the time of the attack. A civil complaint brought on behalf of victims alleged that companies including [[meta-platforms|Meta]], [[google|Alphabet]] (parent of YouTube), [[reddit|Reddit]] and [[4chan]] "profit from the racist, antisemitic, and violent material displayed on their platforms to maximize user engagement," and pointed specifically to the time Gendron spent on those platforms consuming such material as the mechanism of his radicalization.[1]

On March 18, 2024, Erie County Supreme Court Justice [[paula-feroleto|Paula Feroleto]] denied the platforms' motion to dismiss, finding that "the complaint sufficiently pleads viable causes of action to go forward at this stage of the litigation."[1] Attorneys for the defendant companies had argued that the platforms were akin to message boards hosting third-party content, an argument grounded in longstanding intermediary-liability doctrine.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the case illustrated an asymmetry in how radicalization claims are litigated, contending that the same legal energy is not directed at the role of platforms such as TikTok and Instagram in promoting medical transition to minors, citing detransitioner [[chloe-cole|Chloe Cole]].[1] Esler linked the case to the broader theme of [[lawfare]] and to claims that "extremist" is now applied selectively to right-wing ideology, connecting it to the [[great-replacement-theory|Great Replacement]] motive attributed to the shooter.[1]
