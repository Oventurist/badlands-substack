# Kathleen Hicks

Kathleen Hicks is the United States Deputy Secretary of Defense, the second-ranking civilian official at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], who was sworn into that post on February 9, 2021.[1] As deputy, she is first in the Department of Defense line of succession and assumes the functions and duties of the Secretary of Defense whenever the office is vacant or its occupant is unable to perform them.[1]

## Assumption of duties during the Austin hospitalization

On Sunday, February 11, 2024, Hicks assumed the functions and duties of the top job at the Pentagon shortly before 5 p.m. after Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] was admitted to the critical care unit at [[walter-reed-national-military-medical-center|Walter Reed National Military Medical Center]] for symptoms "suggesting an emergent bladder issue."[1] Pentagon press secretary Maj. Gen. [[pat-ryder|Pat Ryder]] announced the transfer of duties in a statement, noting that the [[white-house|White House]], the chairman of the joint chiefs of staff and [[congress|Congress]] had been notified.[1] The rapid notification — the first Pentagon statement came within three hours — followed Austin's earlier acknowledgment of failures in notifying the administration and the public about his previous, secretive hospitalization for cancer treatment in December 2023.[1]

## Badlands commentary on command changes

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Hicks's temporary elevation was notable because she had presided over multiple senior command changes in the days immediately *before* the announcement of the Secretary of Defense's hospitalization: changes at [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence]] and [[us-cyber-command|Cyber Command]] on February 2, 2024, and at [[united-states-northern-command|Northern Command]] on February 5, 2024.[1] The commentary emphasized that Northern Command "plans, organizes and executes homeland defense and civil support missions," that Cyber Command's mission is to direct, synchronize and coordinate cyberspace planning and operations, and that the Defense Intelligence Agency provides intelligence on foreign militaries so the United States and its allies can prevent and decisively win wars.[1]

The piece linked Hicks's assumption of authority over the department to a broader cluster of critical-infrastructure warnings then circulating: House discussion of vulnerabilities in U.S. water infrastructure, scheduled hearings on securing communications networks from foreign adversaries, and an Ohio sheriff's public statement that current threat indicators were worse than before 9/11.[1] Ashe in America framed the sequence — puppets being removed such that the controllers are forced to be revealed — as part of the wider [[devolution|devolution]] thesis, closing with the sardonic remark, "I'm sure it's nothing."[1]

## Acting Pentagon chief, January 2024

During the [[lloyd-austin-hospitalization-controversy|secret January 2024 hospitalization]] of Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]], Hicks as Deputy Defense Secretary was required to assume his duties from a beach in Puerto Rico where she was on vacation, days before President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was informed of the situation.[2] According to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s own account of events, Hicks was among those kept in the dark about why she was carrying out Austin's duties, with only a small cadre of aides aware that he had been admitted to Walter Reed Military Medical Center.[2]
