# Lai Xiaomin

Lai Xiaomin was the chairman of [[china-huarong-asset-management|China Huarong Asset Management]], one of the country's largest financial institutions, and also served as the Communist Party chief inside the company.[1]

## Bribery confession

ABC News Australia, quoted in Badlands Media's "The Master Builder," reported in January 2020 that Lai had confessed to taking more than 200 million yuan — about $42 million — in cash bribes, stuffing the banknotes into cabinets in his apartment.[1] He confirmed the bribes during a confession broadcast in a documentary on the state broadcaster CCTV.[1]

## Context of the wider crackdown

The same report cited figures from China's [[central-commission-for-discipline-inspection|Central Commission for Discipline Inspection]] showing that more than 500,000 officials and party members were punished for corrupt behavior in 2019 alone, and noted that as the crackdown intensified, corrupt officials had been fleeing China for Western countries in increasing numbers.[1]

## Significance in Badlands Media commentary

Joe Lange's article uses the Lai Xiaomin case, together with that of [[hu-huaibang|Hu Huaibang]], to argue that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] is genuinely purging corruption inside the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] rather than merely consolidating power, and that this internal struggle parallels [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s alleged confrontation with the American [[deep-state|Deep State]].[1]
