# Mykhailo Podolyak

Mykhailo Podolyak is an adviser to the Ukrainian presidency who has served as a prominent public voice for Kyiv's appeals for Western military assistance.[1]

## Comparison of Western air defense for Israel and Ukraine

After the United States and its partners helped [[israel|Israel]] shoot down a wave of Iranian drones and missiles in April 2024, Podolyak publicly questioned why the same allies would not help protect [[ukraine|Ukraine]] from Russian attacks.[1] He told NBC News in an interview that the disparity "looks extremely strange," asking bluntly: "How does the civilian population of Ukraine or the civilian infrastructure of Ukraine differ from the civilian population of Israel from a humanistic point of view?"[1]

Podolyak also emphasized that the Kremlin had been deploying the same drones as Tehran in its assault on Ukrainian cities, stating that "Russia and [[iran|Iran]] use the same attack strategy and the same tools. They can be completely destroyed."[1] The episode was presented as illustrating both the possibilities and the limits of Western support as Ukraine awaited delayed U.S. aid.[1]

## Attacks on Elon Musk over Starlink and Crimea

In September 2023 Podolyak accused [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] of "committing" and "enabling evil" for having denied Kyiv the use of [[starlink]] to launch a mass drone attack on the Russian fleet at Crimea the previous year.[2] Writing — somewhat ironically — on Musk's own platform X, Podolyak said that "sometimes a mistake is much more than just a mistake," and that by "not allowing Ukrainian drones to destroy part of the Russian military (!) fleet via Starlink interference, Elon Musk allowed this fleet to fire Kalibr missiles at Ukrainian cities."[2] He framed the resulting civilian and child deaths as "the price of a cocktail of ignorance and big ego," and asked why "some people so desperately want to defend war criminals."[2] Observers noted the accusations were phrased as questions, likely reflecting fear that Musk could switch off Ukraine's Starlink communications entirely.[2] Musk replied that agreeing to the request would have made [[spacex|SpaceX]] "explicitly complicit in a major act of war and conflict escalation."[2]
