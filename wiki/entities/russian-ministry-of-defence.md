# Russian Ministry of Defence

The Russian Ministry of Defence is the government body that directs the armed forces of [[russia|Russia]] and issues official accounts of Russian military operations in the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|war in Ukraine]].[1]

## Retaliatory strike campaign, May–June 2026

Following the [[starobelsk-dormitory-attack|Starobelsk dormitory attack]] of May 22, 2026 — in which a Ukrainian drone struck a college dormitory in Russia's Lugansk People's Republic late at night, reportedly killing 21 people, mostly teenage girls, and injuring 70 others — Russia conducted a large-scale overnight missile and drone strike using precision weapons, including hypersonic missiles.[1] The targets included Ukrainian military airfields, defence industry facilities, fuel and transport infrastructure, and a drone production plant linked to President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], across the Kyiv, Zaporozhye, Kherson, Dnipropetrovsk, Poltava, Khmelnytskyi and Sumy regions.[1]

Ministry officials stated that all designated targets were hit and characterised the operation as a response to "terrorist attacks" by Kiev.[1] President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] described the Ukrainian actions as a new chapter in Ukraine's "crime spree" that would bring "well-deserved and inevitable punishment."[1]

## Denials of attributed incidents

Badlands commentary noted that when a drone struck Romania and Western media attributed it to Russia, Putin and Russian officials publicly denied responsibility and suggested the incident was more likely a false flag committed by [[ukraine|Ukraine]] or the West.[1]

## Black Sea drone incident (March 2023)

On March 14, 2023, the ministry issued Russia's account of the crash of a United States [[mq-9-reaper|MQ-9]] drone in the Black Sea, saying nothing about [[pentagon|Pentagon]] allegations that a Russian Su-27 had dumped fuel on the aircraft and instead attributing the loss to the drone's own maneuvering.[2] The ministry stated that at around 09:30 Moscow time the UAV "went into an uncontrolled flight with a loss of altitude and collided with the water surface," and that Russian fighters "did not use airborne weapons, did not come into contact with the unmanned aerial vehicle and returned safely to their home airfield."[2] It said Russian Aerospace Forces air control had recorded the drone flying over the Black Sea near the Crimean peninsula in the direction of the Russian state border with its transponders turned off, and the Kremlin accused the Pentagon of ignoring military-to-military protocols established to avoid inadvertent [[nato|NATO]]–Russia clashes during the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2]

## Warning of a Ukrainian false-flag before the Alaska summit (2025)

Ahead of the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Russian-American summit scheduled for August 15, 2025]], the Ministry of Defense alleged that the Ukrainian government was preparing a high-profile provocation intended to derail the talks, saying the plan involved staging an attack in a frontline city and attributing it to Russian forces in order to generate a damaging international media narrative.[3] The ministry asserted that Western journalists had already been brought into the Kharkov Region to produce civilian-focused reporting.[3] Russian officials linked the claim to the alleged March 2022 massacre in Bucha near Kiev, which Moscow maintains was a Ukrainian false-flag operation designed to derail the Istanbul peace talks, insisting the killings occurred after Russian forces had withdrawn and calling for a UN investigation.[3]
