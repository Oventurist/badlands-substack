# Janet Yellen

Janet Yellen served as chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] from 2014 to 2018, presiding over the beginning of the withdrawal of post-2008 emergency monetary policy, and later became Secretary of the Treasury.

## In the Badlands corpus

The Badlands Media essay [[a-week-to-remember|A Week to Remember]] describes Yellen dismissively, stating that when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won the 2016 election he "inherited shroom-tripping Janet Yellen as his Fed Chairman and had to keep her for two years before she could be replaced." The article dates the first interest rate hike of the post-crisis era to the period immediately following Trump's victory, contrasting it with the eight preceding years under [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] in which, the author argues, the Fed "never found an opportunity to raise interest rates back to a more normal level, or reduce the balance sheet."

In the essay's framing, that prolonged easy-money regime existed to allow companies such as Facebook, Amazon, Apple, Netflix, and Google to expand into mega-cap firms — an outcome the author attributes to preferential treatment. Yellen's replacement by [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] in 2018 marks, in the article's telling, the transition from "a fake, stimulated economy" toward "a real economy."
