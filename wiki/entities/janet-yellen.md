# Janet Yellen

Janet Yellen served as Chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] and was inherited in that role by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] upon his 2016 election victory.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Tenure under Trump

Badlands Media commentary states that Trump had to keep Yellen — described by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in derisive terms as "shroom-tripping" — for two years before she could be replaced by [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] in 2018.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The first interest rate hike of the post-2008 era occurred following Trump's 2016 win, in Carlson's account marking the moment when easy money began to disappear and "a fake, stimulated economy was being forced to become a real economy."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Warning against decoupling from China

In April 2024, then-Treasury Secretary Yellen stepped to a podium in Beijing to deliver a reassuring message that the United States did not want to decouple from [[china|China]], saying: "Our two economies are deeply integrated, and a wholesale separation would be disastrous for both."[2] Twelve months later, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariffs of more than 120% on Chinese goods and Beijing's determination to retaliate in kind meant the seismic cleavage Yellen had warned of was rapidly becoming reality, with roughly $19 trillion wiped off global equity markets since February 19 and the worst Treasury selloff since the pandemic.[2] Her remark was recalled as the framing device for the [[us-china-trade-war-2025|2025 US–China trade war]] and the decoupling it accelerated.[2]

## November 2023 talks with He Lifeng

In November 2023, as Treasury Secretary, Yellen was scheduled to hold economic talks with Chinese Vice Premier [[he-lifeng|He Lifeng]] on November 9 and 10, ahead of a potential meeting between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[3] The Treasury Department announced the meetings on November 6, and China's Foreign Ministry spokesman Wang Wenbin confirmed that He would visit the United States from November 8 to 12.[3] The talks preceded the [[asia-pacific-economic-cooperation|APEC]] summit in San Francisco, where Yellen was to host finance officials and ministers from the forum's 21 member states on November 11.[3] The announcement followed Chinese state media reports that He had taken charge of two Chinese Communist Party committees created by Xi in March to bring the financial system under direct Party control.[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the meeting to revisit the widely circulated account of Yellen's previous encounter with a Chinese economic official, in which she was said to have unknowingly eaten psychoactive mushroom soup, treating the episode as an example of "Ghosts in the Machine" — planted or absurd stories that make the establishment look cartoonishly inept — and as a memetic device for forcing otherwise-ignored economic stories into public awareness.[3]

## Response to the 2023 Fitch downgrade

After [[fitch-ratings|Fitch]] cut the United States credit rating from AAA to AA+ on August 1, 2023, Yellen said she disagreed with the decision, calling it "arbitrary and based on outdated data."[4] The [[2023-united-states-credit-rating-downgrade|downgrade]] followed the June 2023 bipartisan agreement that lifted the $31.4 trillion debt ceiling and suspended the debt limit until January 2025.[4]

## March 2023 testimony on the banking system

On Thursday, March 16, 2023, one week after the second-largest bank collapse in United States history, Treasury Secretary Yellen was set to tell the Senate Finance Committee that the U.S. banking system "remains sound" and that Americans could feel "confident" about their deposits, with no need to panic over recent events.[5] Her appearance made her the first Biden administration official to face lawmakers over the decision to protect uninsured money at two failed regional banks, a move critics labelled a bank "bailout."[5] In prepared testimony released before the hearing she said that "the government took decisive and forceful actions to strengthen public confidence" in the banking system, adding: "I can reassure the members of the Committee that our banking system remains sound, and that Americans can feel confident that their deposits will be there when they need them."[5]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded that there was "no reason to believe Janet Yellen," arguing she had "been wrong her entire tenure," and questioned how the banking system could be sound when [[silicon-valley-bank|SVB]] had, per the Claremont Institute, given $73 million to [[black-lives-matter|BLM]] and likely more to Democrat politicians.[5] He called for a task force to investigate recipients of bank "donations" — comparing the scale to the proposed 87,000 new IRS agents — contended that SVB failed because it issued huge unsecured loans to ESG start-ups and that "some might argue that it was designed to fall," and urged Yellen to address Congress on the fundamental drivers of inflation instead, hinting that the answer "isn't raising interest rates."[5]

## Response to the Silicon Valley Bank failure

On Sunday, March 12, 2023, Yellen — then Treasury Secretary in the Biden administration — appeared on *Face the Nation* and was asked whether a federal bailout was on the table for [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] following its collapse the previous Friday.[6] She answered that during the financial crisis "there were investors and owners of systemic large banks that were bailed out and we're certainly not looking — and the reforms that have been put in place means that we're not going to do that again," while stating that the government was "concerned about depositors and are focused on trying to meet their needs."[6] Badlands contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] responded that "the game is up" and that the [[fiat-currency|fiat]] system was in its "death rattle," predicting the destruction of the federal banking and fiat system as a precondition for the fall of the cabal.[6]

## China trade remarks (July 2023)

Speaking to Bloomberg television in July 2023, Yellen said China had failed to address the unfair trade practices that triggered US tariff hikes and that the "underlying concerns" remained.[7] Asked whether the US was prepared to de-escalate tensions with Beijing, she indicated that tariff reductions were not on the table, explaining that tariffs had been imposed because of concerns "particularly those affecting intellectual property and technology transfer."[7] She added that any US moves to curb outbound investment into China would be narrowly targeted and based solely on national security considerations.[7]

Her comments came hours after a raft of Chinese economic data came in weaker than expected.[7] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Yellen and the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration were taking credit for tariffs originally implemented by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], leaving the short-term costs of decoupling from China on their ledger while the longer-term benefits to US wage growth and production would accrue to a future Trump administration.[7]

## Reimposition of Gertler sanctions (March 2021)

In March 2021, Yellen reissued the sanctions against Israeli mining oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] that had been quietly reversed at the [[state-department|State Department]] on January 15, 2021, acting after the press finally reported the reversal.[8] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the move as coming from "Janet Yellen of all people," and noted that the Biden [[justice-department|Justice Department]] subsequently prosecuted Gertler and stripped him of his African mining rights in 2022.[8]

## Departure from Treasury (November 2024)

Reporting in late November 2024 described Yellen as "abruptly" exiting office and leaving a fiscal mess behind for the incoming Trump team.[9] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] juxtaposed her exit with NATO Admiral [[rob-bauer|Rob Bauer]]'s call for Western businesses to prepare for a "wartime scenario," implying the two developments were connected and urging readers to consider Bitcoin.[9]

## Considered for a return as Fed chair (2025)

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] disclosed on August 12, 2025 that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had considered re-appointing Yellen as [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] chair as part of a wide-ranging search for a successor to [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], whose term as chairman expires in May 2026.[10] Bessent said Trump had a "very open mind" on the choice and that the administration wanted "to see what everyone's thinking."[10]

## Carlson's "counter-Fed" thesis (April 2023)

In his April 2023 essay "Is Jerome Powell a Patriot?", [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] made Yellen's career trajectory central to his argument: [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "couldn't jettison Janet Yellen from the Federal Reserve fast enough," replacing her as Fed chair with [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] in February 2018, after which [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] installed her as Secretary of the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]].[11] Carlson presented this sequence — Trump hires Powell and [[john-c-williams|John C. Williams]]; Biden rehabilitates the person Trump removed — as evidence of two opposed factions inside American financial governance.[11]

Carlson further alleged that Yellen "has gone out of her way to undo any good that Powell has done," using the US Treasury to counter [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] moves and thereby slowing what he characterized as the deliberate weakening of offshore [[eurodollar|Eurodollar]] holdings through higher interest rates.[11]

## Rejection of the trillion-dollar coin

In January 2023, as Treasury Secretary, Yellen publicly dismissed the [[trillion-dollar-coin|trillion-dollar coin]] proposal — the idea that Treasury could mint a $1 trillion platinum coin to circumvent the [[debt-ceiling|debt ceiling]] — as a "gimmick."[12] Speaking to the Wall Street Journal, she said: "It truly is not by any means to be taken as a given that the Fed would do it, and I think especially with something that's a gimmick. The Fed is not required to accept it, there's no requirement on the part of the Fed. It's up to them what to do."[12] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Yellen's dismissal was largely irrelevant to the outcome, since the authority to accept and monetize such a coin rests with the Federal Reserve rather than with the Treasury Secretary.[12] The proposal was revived that May by economist [[paul-krugman|Paul Krugman]], who claimed it would not be inflationary.[12]
