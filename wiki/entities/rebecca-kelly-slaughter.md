# Rebecca Kelly Slaughter

**Rebecca Kelly Slaughter** is a former commissioner of the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]] whose dismissal by President Donald Trump became the vehicle for a landmark [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] test of presidential removal power over so-called independent agencies.[1]

## Removal and litigation

Trump dismissed Slaughter from the FTC, and her challenge to that removal reached the Supreme Court, where oral arguments were held in early December 2025.[1] The case squarely presented the question of whether the President may fire members of independent agencies at will.[1]

During argument, the Court's conservative justices appeared receptive to the President's position.[1] Justice Brett Kavanaugh questioned the accountability of independent agencies, while Chief Justice John Roberts challenged the continuing relevance of a 1935 precedent that has historically shielded such agencies from at-will removal, suggesting that the modern FTC wields significant power that should fall under presidential authority.[1] A ruling was anticipated by the end of June.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Chris Paul argued that there is no such thing as an "independent agency" within the Executive Branch, describing the concept as extraconstitutional and asserting that the President must hold plenary authority over the entire Executive Branch, including the power to remove any officer at will for any reason.[1] The commentary went further, contending that most executive agencies should not exist at all and that independent agencies are a creation of what Badlands calls the Edifice, with no legitimate legal standing.[1]
