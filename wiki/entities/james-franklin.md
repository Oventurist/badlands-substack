# James Franklin

James Franklin was a Boston printer and the eldest half-brother of [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], to whose printing business Benjamin had been unwillingly apprenticed in 1718 for the onerous term of nine years.[1]

According to historian [[graham-lowry|Graham Lowry]], as quoted in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin," James Franklin stood on the enemy side of the struggle his younger brother joined in 1721 against the occult networks then rising in the British Empire.[1] James had returned from London the previous year, where he acquired his press and type for printing along with a familiarity with the degenerate admirers of [[bernard-mandeville|Bernard Mandeville]].[1]

Having set up his printing business in Boston, James fell in with a circle of radical Anglicans attached to Boston's only Episcopal church, allied with the radical Whigs of Elisha Cooke, Jr.'s political machine in common cause against the republican leadership of the Mathers; at the centre of the Anglican operation stood [[john-checkley|John Checkley]].[1] By 1722 the Mathers were accusing James Franklin of promoting "an Hell-Fire club," a charge [[increase-mather|Increase Mather]] levelled on the grounds that James had published the claim that man cannot distinguish between the work of God and the work of the Devil.[1]
