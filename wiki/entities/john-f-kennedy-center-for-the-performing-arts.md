# John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts

The John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts is a congressionally chartered national cultural institution in Washington, D.C. Its name was established by [[congress|Congress]], a fact that became legally decisive in a 2026 dispute over the Trump administration's attempt to rename and rebuild the venue.[1]

## 2026 renaming and renovation litigation

On May 29, 2026, U.S. District Judge [[christopher-cooper|Christopher Cooper]] ordered the removal of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s name from the Kennedy Center and blocked a planned two-year closure of the venue for renovations, ruling that only Congress has authority to change the institution's name.[1] The litigation was brought by Rep. [[joyce-beatty|Joyce Beatty]] of Ohio.[1] The ruling halted a renovation plan approved by the center's Trump-appointed leadership and held that the center could not formally bear a name other than the one Congress established.[1]

## Trump's response

In a subsequent Truth Social post, Trump attacked Cooper as an appointee of former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and defended the renovation, saying construction materials had already been ordered or were about to be ordered and describing the project as a major structural and aesthetic rebuilding effort.[1] Trump said the building suffered from rust, aging mechanical systems, failing pipes, pest problems, and structural deterioration that made extensive renovation necessary.[1] He said he would work toward transferring responsibility for operating the Kennedy Center to Congress, arguing the court's decision prevented him from carrying out his plans.[1]

Trump's statement that "unless I am free to do what I do better than anyone else, bring this Institution back, physically, financially, and artistically, I have no interest in continuing what could only be a hopeless journey into 'NEVER NEVER LAND'" drew commentary from Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who speculated about whether the "Never Never Land" phrasing was a deliberate allusion to Michael Jackson, Jeffrey Epstein's island, or both, and noted that Trump's language implied he might be willing to shut the institution down entirely.[1]

## Return of the Stuttgart Ballet

In 2025 the Kennedy Center announced that the [[stuttgart-ballet|Stuttgart Ballet]] would return to the venue for the first time since 1992, performing John Cranko's *Onegin* — a balletic setting of Alexander Pushkin's 1825 verse novel *Eugene Onegin* — in the Opera House from October 8 to 12, 2025 to Tchaikovsky's score, opening the Center's 2025–2026 ballet season.[2] Jane Raleigh, the Center's Director of Dance Programming, said the company and Cranko's *Onegin* "were a staple of Kennedy Center ballet programming in the 1970's and 80's" and called the return "such a special moment for Washington audiences," coinciding with the work's 60th anniversary.[2]
