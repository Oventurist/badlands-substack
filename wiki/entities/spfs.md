# SPFS (System for Transfer of Financial Messages)

SPFS, the System for Transfer of Financial Messages, is the Russian financial messaging network created as a domestic substitute for [[swift|SWIFT]].[1]

## Creation

The Central Bank of Russia built SPFS in 2014, in the period following the Crimea referendum and the first rounds of Western [[sanctions]], specifically so that Russian banks could continue exchanging cross-border payment instructions if access to SWIFT were withdrawn.[1]

## Adoption

As of 2020, SPFS traffic stood at 13 million messages and more than 400 financial institutions had joined the system.[1] To attract new members, the central bank cut the system's tariffs to roughly half of SWIFT's charges, and in 2019 the Accounts Chamber proposed obliging all banks operating in Russia — including subsidiaries of foreign banks — to connect to the Russian analogue.[1]

## Role in sanctions evasion strategy

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] writes that Russia uses SPFS together with the [[mir-payment-system|MIR]] national payment card system to connect directly with other [[brics|BRICS]] nations and trading partners, bypassing the sanctions architecture that cut Russia off from Western financial infrastructure after the invasion of Ukraine.[1] He treats the preparation of these systems years in advance as evidence that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] anticipated the sanctions response.[1]
