# California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation

The California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) is the state financial regulator that ordered the closure of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] in March 2023 and documented the deposit flight that rendered the bank insolvent.[1]

## Order against Silicon Valley Bank

In an order dated March 10, 2023, the DFPI reported that shortly after the bank announced a loss of approximately $1.8 billion from a sale of investments and disclosed that it was conducting a capital raise — a raise that subsequently failed — investors and depositors reacted by initiating withdrawals of $42 billion in deposits on March 9, 2023, causing a run on the bank.[1] The department stated that the bank had been in sound financial condition prior to March 9, 2023.[1]

As a result of the withdrawals, the DFPI found that as of the close of business on Thursday, March 9, "the bank had a negative cash balance of approximately $958 million."[1] Despite attempts by the bank, with the assistance of regulators, to transfer collateral from various sources, the bank "did not meet its cash letter with the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]," and the regulator concluded that "the precipitous deposit withdrawal has caused the Bank to be incapable of paying its obligations as they come due, and the bank is now insolvent."[1]

## Significance

The DFPI's findings supplied the authoritative factual record for the [[2023-silicon-valley-bank-run|2023 Silicon Valley Bank run]], quantifying both the speed of the withdrawal — roughly a quarter of the deposit base drained in hours — and the resulting negative cash position.[1] The State of California took over the bank on Friday, March 10, 2023, in what was the largest US bank failure since the 2008 banking crisis.[1]
