# Marc Andreessen

Marc Andreessen is an American technology investor named in March 2026 to the [[presidents-council-of-advisors-on-science-and-technology|President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology]] (PCAST) by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## PCAST appointment

Andreessen appeared on the [[white-house|White House]] list of PCAST appointees announced in late March 2026, alongside figures including Sergey Brin, Safra Catz, Michael Dell, [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], Jensen Huang, Lisa Su, and [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].[1] The council's remit covers artificial intelligence, national security technology, economic competitiveness, and scientific research priorities.[1]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America grouped Andreessen among the appointees she described as "Thiel bros" — figures associated with the network of [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] — noting that critics who believe Thiel is shadow-running the government would read the appointment list as confirmation of that thesis.[1]

## Predictions on the end of corporate wokeness

Speaking with Erik Torenberg on the Moment of Zen podcast in December 2024, Andreessen — described as a billionaire investor and adviser to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — said the reign of extreme wokeness, particularly in corporate America and the media, was rapidly coming to an end.[2] He identified the catalyst as a combination of rising legal risks, the deflation of wokeness as a cultural force, and a change in leadership at the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[2] Andreessen highlighted the appointment of [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] to head the DOJ's Civil Rights Division, saying "every signal is being sent" that the federal government would soon begin to challenge and reverse the DEI-driven policies that had dominated corporations, universities, and other large institutions over the past decade — a shift he argued could trigger a major private-sector pullback as companies scrambled to comply with the law.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disputed the optimism, calling "wokeness" a cutesy term for global communism and arguing that communists fight until overexposed and then continue from the shadows.[2] She predicted that sustainability and climate initiatives would simply be reprioritized over social justice and DEI for a few years, and that making corporate culture less woke would merely force global communism to rebrand again.[2]

## 2024 donation to Donald Trump

In July 2024, Axios reported that Andreessen and his partner [[ben-horowitz|Ben Horowitz]] would each donate to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s re-election effort, part of a broader movement of tech industry money and power behind Trump.[3] Neither was believed to have previously donated to a presidential campaign.[3] Andreessen had publicly supported Mitt Romney in 2012, and in 2016 said he was struggling to decide between a candidate who "doesn't believe in science" (Trump) and one who "doesn't believe in economics" (Hillary Clinton).[3]

The donations followed [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s endorsement of Trump and formation of a supporting PAC, and were consistent with the late-2023 announcement by [[andreessen-horowitz|Andreessen Horowitz]] — the venture firm Andreessen and Horowitz co-founded in 2009 — that its partners would begin donating to political candidates seen as "advancing technology."[3] Axios described the pair as "techno-optimists" and noted that Silicon Valley's Republican camp was growing and throwing around its weight.[3]

## Reported role in DOGE candidate selection (December 2024)

In December 2024, the Financial Times reported, citing two unnamed sources with knowledge of the matter, that Andreessen was helping identify and interview candidates for President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s advisory commission on cutting government spending, [[doge|DOGE]].[4] An MSNBC opinion piece argued the reported role was hypocritical given Andreessen's recent criticism of unelected decision-makers in the federal government and specifically his complaints about the [[consumer-financial-protection-bureau|Consumer Financial Protection Bureau]], the agency created after the 2008 financial crisis to protect consumers from deceptive financial products.[4]
