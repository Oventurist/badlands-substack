# Canadian Broadcasting Corporation

The **Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC)** is Canada's national public broadcaster and is almost wholly funded by the Canadian government.[1]

## "Government funded" label dispute (April 2023)

In April 2023, [[twitter|Twitter]], under the ownership of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], applied a "government funded" label to the CBC's account.[1] The move prompted Canadian Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] to accuse Musk of "attacking foundational Canadian institutions," and Trudeau maintained that the CBC is an "independent media organization" despite its overwhelming reliance on public funds.[1] Trudeau also grouped Musk together with his domestic conservative opponents, charging that they had "run to American billionaire tech giants" in order to "constantly attack" Canadians and the journalists who "support our democracy."[1] The exchange was reported by [[summit-news|Summit News]].[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] and written by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the controversy revealed the importance of perceived independence to state-aligned media: labeling an outlet as government funded, when it factually is, was treated as a high crime precisely because the credibility of the outlet depends on the appearance of neutrality.[1] DeLarme further noted that Twitter under Musk was applying to mainstream outlets the same labeling standards the platform had previously applied to non-mainstream accounts.[1]

## Role in Trudeau's narrative operations

In a January 2024 essay, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described the CBC as one of the outlets through which [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] conducts paired [[narrative-warfare|narrative]] and [[asymmetric-warfare|asymmetric warfare]] operations, noting that the broadcaster receives most of its financing from his government.[2] He wrote that Trudeau also relies on Global and CTV to produce narrative assassinations of his dissenters, portraying them as a mounting force of hate, and then amplifies the resulting outrage among his radicalized base.[2]

Deschamps argued this media apparatus lets the government present the [[canadian-anti-hate-network|Canadian Anti-Hate Network]] as an independent authority whose findings justify harsh policy, and sustains the framing of majority opposition as a "fringe minority" of far-right extremists.[2]
