# Robert Fico

Robert Fico is the President of Slovakia as described in Badlands coverage of the January 2026 rupture in transatlantic relations. Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry referred to him approvingly as "Our Boy," noting that Fico had been very direct in stating that the US operation against Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] represented the end of the post-World War II international order.[1]

## Position on the postwar order

Where German President [[frank-walter-steinmeier|Frank-Walter Steinmeier]] and French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] framed the erosion of that order as a loss, Fico's characterization was cited by Badlands as confirmation of its own thesis: that the postwar apparatus was the machinery designed to deliver globalism, and that its collapse under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] amounted to a move away from consolidated power and toward the decentralization of authority.[1]

## Davos 2026: nuclear deal and EU criticism

At the January 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos, Fico — Prime Minister of [[slovakia|Slovakia]] — said after meeting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that the [[european-union|EU]] is "not taken completely seriously" by world leaders, attributing that assessment in part to EU policy choices, which Badlands rendered as suicidal migration policies and nonsense climate goals.[2] The US–Slovakia civil nuclear cooperation agreement was signed the same Wednesday, covering reactor technology, supply chains and safety and security best practices according to a [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] press release, and in public remarks Fico described a shared strategic view of energy focused on "safety, sustainability, and technological maturity."[2]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated Fico's bluntness as the signal that cut through the noise at Davos, since he stated openly what other European leaders only whisper, and listed "Robert Fico confirms the need for said pivot" among the confirmations of his [[nuclear-renaissance|nuclear renaissance]] thesis.[2]

## Cited in Badlands assassination commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the assassination attempt on Fico in September 2024 commentary alongside attempts on [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the successful assassination of [[shinzo-abe|Shinzo Abe]].[3] The list supported his argument that the roster of world leaders who would object to Russian retaliation against Western Deep State figures had grown short.[3]

## Halt to arms transfers to Ukraine (October 2023)

Shortly after taking office, Fico's new government in [[slovakia|Slovakia]] followed through on its campaign pledge to terminate arms transfers to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and called on the rest of Europe to adopt Bratislava's policy.[4] Fico urged the [[european-union|European Union]] to take on a peacekeeper role in the Ukraine war and signalled that Bratislava would not support further sanctions on Moscow.[4]

Addressing members of Slovakia's legislature on a Thursday, Fico said the country was abandoning the West's proxy war against [[russia|Russia]]: "I will support zero military aid to Ukraine… An immediate halt to military operations is the best solution we have for Ukraine. The EU should change from an arms supplier to a peacemaker," adding that Slovakia will "no longer supply weapons to Ukraine."[4] He also declined to back Washington's economic war on Moscow, stating: "I will not vote for any sanctions against Russia unless we see analyses of their impact on Slovakia," and, "If there are to be such sanctions that will harm us, like most sanctions have, I can see no reason to support them."[4] Fico further argued that "the people in Slovakia have bigger problems than (dealing with) Ukraine" and that "further killing [in Ukraine] will not help anyone."[4] His left-wing party Smer-SD had won Slovakia's election earlier that month on a platform that highlighted ending arms transfers to Ukraine.[4]

## 2024 assassination attempt

On Wednesday, May 15, 2024, Fico was shot several times in what Slovak officials described as a politically motivated [[2024-slovak-assassination-attempt|assassination attempt]], and underwent surgery for his wounds.[5] Deputy Prime Minister Tomas Taraba told the BBC the next day that Fico "is not in a life-threatening situation at this moment" and that "the operation went well – and I guess in the end he will survive."[5] Deputy Prime Minister Robert Kalinak said there was "no doubt" the attack was politically motivated, and the detained suspect, [[juraj-cintula|Juraj Cintula]], was recorded confessing that he "disagreed" with his government's policies.[5]

Western coverage of the shooting drew criticism from journalist [[glenn-greenwald|Glenn Greenwald]], who said a Sky News report came "close to justifying it because he opposes aid to Ukraine" while casually implying that Fico was being paid by the Kremlin.[5] Badlands commentary noted that Fico had voted against Ukraine joining [[nato|NATO]], voted against the World Health Organization pandemic treaty, and two days before the shooting had Slovakia abstain from the European Union's New Pact on Migration and Asylum governing how all 27 member states would receive future migration waves.[5] Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Fico as a close friend and confidant of Hungarian leader [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] and a known critic of NATO's anti-Russia sanctions and anti-China rhetoric.[5]

## 'Maidan' warning (January 2025)

Speaking at a joint press conference with Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] in Bratislava on Tuesday, January 21, 2025, Fico accused Slovakia's political opposition of preparing a "Maidan" in the country — a reference to the violent Western-backed coup in Kiev in 2014 that led to the overthrow of democratically elected Ukrainian President Viktor Yanukovich.[6] He based the accusation on a report prepared by the Slovak Information Service (SIS) intelligence agency, saying: "I cannot disclose the content of the report, but I can say in all seriousness that the opposition is preparing a 'Maidan'. It is gearing up to thwart the government from exercising its powers and it will do this in cooperation with foreign actors."[6] Fico added that his opponents had failed to notice that the "global state of affairs has changed significantly," apparently referring to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] replacing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as US president.[6]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry situated the warning within the outlet's reporting on Eastern European states such as [[slovakia|Slovakia]] and Georgia passing so-called "pro-Kremlin" legislation forcing foreign NGOs to disclose their funding sources, noting that prime ministers who advanced such laws often faced death threats and violence — as with Fico, who was shot five times in the chest but survived.[6] He said he would ask Trump whether the Eastern European leaders' allegations that these NGOs, tied to the [[state-department|State Department]], were attempting to topple their governments had merit, and why US tax dollars would be used that way, calling it a rhetorical question given [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[6] His prescription was to shut down the programs and defund the intelligence operators allegedly working in those countries.[6]

## Invoked as a warning to Georgia's leadership

Badlands commentary in December 2024 recalled that Fico was shot five times in the chest in May 2024, shortly after his newly seated government passed legislation requiring all foreign NGOs to publicly disclose the sources of their funding - legislation based on existing Russian law, which the commentary said sent Russophobes in the media into a frenzy.[7] In the immediate aftermath of the failed assassination attempt, and with comparable legislation pending in Georgia, newly elected Georgian Prime Minister [[irakli-kobakhidze|Irakli Kobakhidze]] said an MP from the European Union had contacted him and warned him to be careful, reminding him what had just happened to Fico - a message Badlands characterized as a thinly veiled threat.[7]

## Two-sexes constitutional amendment

Fico's government introduced the constitutional amendment passed by [[slovakia|Slovakia]]'s National Council on 26 September 2025 recognising only two sexes and restricting adoption rights to married couples, while guaranteeing equal pay and requiring parental consent for sexual education.[8] The [[slovak-two-sexes-constitutional-amendment|amendment]] passed with 90 of 150 votes, clearing the three-fifths threshold with support from twelve right-leaning opposition lawmakers, and Justice Minister Boris Susko framed it as strengthening traditional values.[8] [[amnesty-international|Amnesty International]] and the [[council-of-europe|Council of Europe]] both condemned the measure.[8]

## May 2024 shooting and the broader-plot investigation

Fico sustained life-threatening injuries on 15 May 2024 when a 71-year-old suspect fired five shots at close range as the prime minister approached a crowd after a government meeting in the town of Handlová, northeast of Bratislava.[9] Officials initially described a single perpetrator acting from political motive, but on the following Sunday Slovak police said cooperation with domestic and foreign intelligence services had broadened the probe to include the possibility that an unidentified group was linked to the crime.[9] Interior Minister [[matus-sutaj-estok|Matuš Šutaj Eštok]] said the broader-plot theory was supported by the erasure of the assailant's social media communications by another person about two hours after the shooting.[9] The attack was the first on a European leader in more than 20 years and sent shockwaves through one of the continent's most polarized countries.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] emphasized that Fico was the one NATO leader suing for peace between Russia and Ukraine, that he had been elected only the previous October, and that corporate media had rushed to the lone-wolf judgment before facts were established.[9] He noted CNN's framing of Fico receiving 'more votes than expected' despite being a two-time former prime minister, and its treatment of President [[zuzana-caputova|Zuzana Čaputová]]'s request that he form a government as a mere formality.[9] He speculated that 'Ukrainian puppet-masters from Langley, Virginia' had used [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] networks and tactics.[9]

## Letter to Elon Musk on USAID funding (February 2025)

In February 2025 Fico wrote to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] complaining about the subsidies and grants that [[usaid|USAID]] had provided to NGOs, media outlets and specific journalists in Slovakia, and congratulated Musk on his appointment and the decisions he was making.[10] In a Facebook post Fico wrote that "it is indisputable that financial resources from USAID were used in Slovakia for political purposes, with the aim of distorting the political system and favouring certain political parties," adding that "even from incomplete public sources, it is clear that the USAID agency supported these entities with subsidies amounting to several million dollars over a relatively short period."[10] He requested more detailed information about money transfers into Slovakia so as to "distinguish between useful and beneficial projects and blatant interference in Slovakia's internal affairs," at a time when the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration was seeking to abolish USAID and Musk led an agency tasked with improving federal efficiency.[10]

Badlands commentary argued that if Musk granted Fico's request, the disclosures would offer an opportunity to prove that the [[state-department|State Department]] uses proxy NGOs to destabilise foreign countries for regime-change purposes, and might further connect that funding to the assassination attempt on Fico in May of the previous year.[10] The writer held that such a finding would not only prove [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] but would justify the MAGA movement's desire to fundamentally transform the structure of the federal government.[10]
