# United States Border Patrol

The **United States Border Patrol** is the federal law enforcement agency responsible for immigration and border enforcement, operating as a component of the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]].[1] In early 2026 the agency's field conduct became the focus of state investigations, litigation, and public controversy.[1]

## Minnesota investigation

Hennepin County Attorney [[mary-moriarty|Mary Moriarty]] launched an investigation into the conduct of federal immigration agents, including the senior Border Patrol official [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]], tied to enforcement actions earlier in 2026.[1] DHS responded that enforcement is a federal responsibility and that state prosecutors lack authority to charge federal officers.[1]

## Death of Nurul Amin Shah Alam

In a separate incident, a nearly blind refugee from Myanmar who had been released by the Border Patrol after a misdemeanor plea deal was found dead in Buffalo, New York, five days after his release.[1] The Border Patrol said agents dropped the man, Nurul Amin Shah Alam, at a coffee shop near his last known address after determining that he could not be deported.[1] The circumstances of his death were placed under investigation.[1]

## Public opinion and outlook

A Reuters/Ipsos poll found that a majority of Americans support deporting unauthorized immigrants but disapprove of aggressive enforcement tactics.[1] Federal immigration operations continued to provoke debate, legal challenges, and investigations as cases involving deaths, enforcement authority, and agency conduct proceeded in courts and local jurisdictions.[1]

## Minneapolis shooting of Alex Pretti (January 2026)

On January 24, 2026, Border Patrol agents fatally shot 37-year-old former Minneapolis VA Medical Center ICU nurse [[alex-jeffrey-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] during chaotic anti-ICE protests in Minneapolis.[2] Body-worn camera footage showed Pretti approaching agents while filming, resisting dispersal orders, and reaching toward his waist, where he lawfully carried a concealed firearm.[2] The shooting was one of two fatal shootings of US citizens by federal agents in Minneapolis that drove national uproar over immigration enforcement tactics and helped precipitate the [[january-2026-government-shutdown-standoff|January 2026 government shutdown standoff]].[2] In response the administration removed CBP official [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]] from the Minneapolis operation, replacing him with border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]].[2]

## Nogales rancher call recordings (2023)

Recordings obtained by Fox News Digital and reported in March 2023 captured a Border Patrol staffer questioning whether Arizona rancher [[george-alan-kelly|George Alan Kelly]] was "crazy" before agents responded to his claims of armed drug traffickers on his property near Nogales.[3] The calls, exchanged with Kelly and the Santa Cruz County Sheriff's Office on January 30, 2023, included one in which someone from the Nogales Border Patrol Station told dispatch that agents had contacted the caller's spouse, who stated there were five people on the property and that the caller was pursuing them south, carrying a large backpack and possibly a rifle.[3] That evening the body of 48-year-old Mexican national Gabriel Cuen Buitimea was found on Kelly's ranch, and Kelly was arrested on a first-degree murder charge later downgraded to second-degree murder.[3] The Santa Cruz County Attorney's Office, releasing the recordings, stressed that Kelly never dialed 911 and that the calls were relays and contacts initiated by dispatch.[3]

## Yuma station detention death (2025)

A 52-year-old Chinese woman detained for overstaying her B1/B2 visitor visa died by suicide at the Border Patrol station in Yuma, Arizona, on 29 March 2025.[4] Representative [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]] alleged that officers failed to perform required welfare checks and falsely recorded them.[4] Officials also allegedly neglected internal policies on publicly acknowledging deaths in custody, publishing a notice only after the Tucson Sentinel reported the death on the following Friday.[4] Surveillance footage reportedly showed the woman making a noose and tying it around her neck, with no medical response for two hours.[4] The CBP Office of Professional Responsibility opened an investigation.[4]
