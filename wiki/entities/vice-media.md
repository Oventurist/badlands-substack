# Vice Media

Vice Media is an American-Canadian digital media and broadcasting company that, after years of financial difficulty and repeated departures of top executives, was reported in May 2023 to be preparing to file for bankruptcy.[1] The New York Times, citing two people with knowledge of the company's operations, reported the impending filing, which was recirculated by the outlet Mint and covered in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief.[1]

## Bankruptcy preparations

According to the reporting, Vice had received expressions of interest from five companies and might consider a sale in order to avoid bankruptcy.[1] In the event of a bankruptcy — which was described as possibly occurring within weeks — the company's debtholder [[fortress-investment-group|Fortress Investment Group]] could end up controlling the firm.[1]

The bankruptcy news came only days after the company laid off staff and canceled its flagship program *Vice News Tonight*.[1] The cancellation was part of a broader restructuring that produced job cuts across Vice's global news business, capping what observers described as years of financial trouble.[1]

## Valuation and investors

Vice, which operated a cable channel of the same name and produced documentaries and other video content both for its own outlets and for third parties, was once valued at $5.7 billion.[1] Its investors included Walt Disney Co. and Fox Corp., although the Times reported that their equity might by then be worthless.[1] Fortress Investment Group was identified as the company's largest debt holder.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] treated the collapse as evidence about the durability of progressive "infotainment," arguing that billions of dollars could not save "a company whose time has gone."[1] The commentary held that the dissolution of Vice News suggested the genre simply lacked the staying power and popularity it was said to have, and called into question the broader claim that the modern left had successfully "captured" modern culture.[1]

Invoking Walter Lippmann's book *Public Opinion*, the commentary argued that the mass proliferation of newspapers roughly a century earlier had not been an organic event but the product of powerful moneyed interests building a news industry from the ground up and then persuading the public that it had arisen from popular demand.[1] On that reading, outlets such as Vice may never have risen to prominence through genuine demand — the demand may instead have been engineered after their creation, sustained by "free" money that eventually ran dry.[1] The piece concluded that, whatever the explanation, the outcome was an undeniably positive development.[1]

## Fortress ownership and the Refinery29 sale (January 2024)

[[fortress-investment-group|Fortress Investment Group]], which took over Vice in bankruptcy in 2023, was by January 2024 seeking to sell the company's [[refinery29|Refinery29]] women's lifestyle site after a failed attempt to find a buyer for Vice in its entirety — a package that had included the namesake news brand, the production studio and the creative agency.[2] Vice had purchased Refinery29 for $400 million in 2019; by the time of the sale effort, Refinery29's revenue had fallen to roughly $30 million from around $50 million the prior year.[2]

The attempted divestiture was reported alongside [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed]]'s effort to sell Tasty and WeFeast, with both companies described as former darlings of digital media now siphoning off assets amid an industry-wide contraction.[2]

## Cessation of publishing (February 2024)

In February 2024, Vice Media — once valued at $5.7 billion in 2017 and acquired out of bankruptcy the previous year by a consortium including George Soros — announced plans to cut hundreds of jobs and stopped publishing on Vice.com.[3] Chief executive Bruce Dixon said, "It is no longer cost-effective for us to distribute our digital content the way we have done previously."[3] Modernity News reported that many observers greeted the demise with open celebration on the grounds that "nothing of value was lost," citing years of ridicule of the outlet's descent into what it called demented, vulgar and degenerate far-left content and pointing to headlines such as "Most Child Sex Abusers Are Not Pedophiles, Expert Says."[3]

Badlands contributor Simon Esler recalled a Vice documentary on a town where bestiality was normalized, in which the filmmakers framed men's sex with donkeys as preparation to be better lovers for their future wives.[3] He argued that societies still justifying sex with animals are "sociologically and morally behind us," that this is a case where what the left calls an imperialist, colonialist mindset is the correct view, and that Vice's normalization of such material formed part of a genuine conspiracy to inject Satanic values into the culture.[3]
