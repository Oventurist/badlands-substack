# Aleister Crowley

Aleister Crowley was an English occultist invoked in the Badlands Media feature "Legacy of the Bavarian Illuminati – Part 2" as an exemplar of moral relativism in the occult tradition.[1]

Discussing the Shakespearean line "There is nothing either good or bad, but thinking makes it so" — which the article notes may have been written by Francis Bacon — [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] observes that such platitudes are popular with modern liberals and occult practitioners alike, and that "Crowley's declaration of 'Do as thou wilt' echoes the same sentiment."[1] What appears to be a liberating and progressive proclamation, he argues, can equally serve as an excuse to indulge in "all manner of unhealthy, self-serving behavior" — self-service being, in his account, a predominant trait of the secret societies that scheme behind the public's back to shape civilization in their favor.[1]



## Nazi and Setian connections

In "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler - Part 4," William Ramsay is quoted describing Crowley and [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] as "the great black magicians of the 20th century," sharing worldviews shaped by European occultism, Freemasonry and Theosophy and endorsing the survival of the fittest, the primacy of the human will and a feudal slave state.[2] German [[new-templar-order|New Templars]] were closely associated with Crowley and with Dr. Franz Hartmann of the German Theosophical Society.[2] By 1915 the [[ordo-templi-orientis|Ordo Templi Orientis]] co-founded by Theodor Reuss had become a conduit of Crowley, who took control of the worldwide order after Reuss's death, extending it into New York, Los Angeles, Canada, Mexico and Germany.[2] Crowley's 1904 Cairo working, in which he encountered Aiwass, was later reinterpreted by [[michael-aquino|Michael Aquino]] as the opening of the Aeon of Horus preceding his own Aeon of Set; Aquino came to believe he was Crowley's reincarnation, imitating his appearance and promoting the Thelemic maxim "Do What Thou Wilt Shall be the Whole of the Law."[2]

## Plymouth Brethren roots and Templar symbolism

Part 6 of the "Real Patriots Don't Think Like Hitler" series states that Crowley — described there as a British intelligence agent and self-professed satanist — emerged out of [[john-nelson-darby|Darby's]] [[plymouth-brethren]] sect.[3] The occult researcher Henrik Bogdan observed that "Crowley's animosity toward Christianity was in part a reaction to the traumas of his upbringing in the cultic milieu of the Exclusive Branch of the Plymouth Brethren."[3]

Despite rejecting Christianity, Crowley retained core Brethren precepts, above all [[dispensationalism]].[3] Bogdan notes that in Crowley's new religion the Holy Scripture of the Bible was replaced by the Holy Books of Thelema, chief among them *The Book of the Law*, and that the new dispensation was not the period before the return of Christ but the Aeon of Horus, formally inaugurated at the vernal equinox in 1904.[3] Crowley himself wrote in his autobiography: "I accepted the theology of the Plymouth Brethren… I simply went over to Satan's side; and to this hour I cannot tell why."[3]

With his embrace of Satanism came an embrace of Templarism — he led the [[ordo-templi-orientis]] — and of the figure of Baphomet, which he used as his OTO pseudonym.[3] Crowley identified Baphomet, the androgynous goat-headed demon popularized by the [[knights-templar|Templars]] and the French occultist Eliphas Levi, as "the guardian of the key to the Temple" in black mass rituals he created for Thelema, Argentum Astrum and the Mysterium Mysticum Maximum.[3] Within the tenth degree of the OTO his Baphomet Cross takes the name "the elevenfold de Molay cross," referring to Templar Grand Master Jacques de Molay, burned at the stake in 1314; the symbol closely resembles the flag designed by Godfrey of Bouillon, first crusading king of Jerusalem.[3]

## Espionage in America, 1914–1919

Part 5 of the same series presents Crowley as one of two leading "black magicians" — the other being [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]] — who oversaw the occult espionage network built in the United States before and during the world wars.[4] According to Richard B. Spence's *Secret Agent 666*, one of Crowley's leading contacts and paymasters in New York before and during World War I was John Quinn, an Anglo-American agent of the private intelligence agency centered in the elite [[sleepy-hollow-club|Sleepy Hollow Club]].[4] Spence records that Crowley was stationed in the United States by British intelligence from 1914 to 1919, running anti-British propaganda while seeding occult cells across the country — including what became his [[ordo-templi-orientis|Ordo Templi Orientis]] bodies in New York and California, the latter later led by Jack Parsons and L. Ron Hubbard — and acting as "Honorous Magus" in the Societas Rosicruciana.[4]

From early 1915 Crowley collaborated with Viereck's German propaganda newspaper [[the-fatherland|The Fatherland]] and edited its companion journal The International, publishing violently anti-British articles and promoting his doctrine of [[thelema|Thelema]].[4] Marco Pasi notes that Crowley had already met Viereck in London in 1911, that he also contributed to Vanity Fair, and that in October 1915 he left New York to tour the West Coast, meeting his disciple Charles Stansfeld Jones in Vancouver.[4] Spence writes that through these connections Crowley reached the top of the German clandestine and diplomatic hierarchies in the United States, rubbing elbows with Dr. Albert, von Papen and Ambassador Bernstorff, and that the Germans placed great faith in "the Beast's" intuition and his uncanny ability to predict British actions, apparently never suspecting he was a British double agent plying them with disinformation.[4]

Crowley was made outer head of the Ordo Templi Orientis by [[theodor-reuss|Theodor Reuss]] in 1912 and became head of the Ancient and Primitive Rite of Memphis and Misraim in 1913.[4] In the same period he made contact with the leading Ariosophists led by Otto von List and Lanz von Liebenfels and with the German Theosophist Dr. Franz Hartmann, a member of Crowley's Argentum Astrum and president of the Indian Theosophical Society — networks whose collaboration gave rise in 1919 to the [[thule-society|Thule Society]].[4]

Spence's research is corroborated, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, by declassified documents from the United States Army's Military Intelligence Division demonstrating that Crowley was a British double agent throughout.[4] A declassified report from the chief intelligence officer at West Point dated 23 September 1918 noted that Crowley had been camping on Esopus Island on the Hudson River, came to attention through his connection with the actress Madeline George, and was determined to be "an employee of the British Government… in this country on official business, of which the British Counsel, New York City, has full cognizance," adding that the British government was fully aware he was connected with German propaganda and had received money for writing anti-British articles.[4] A US Office of Naval Intelligence report of 10 July 1917 stated that Crowley "maintained some mysterious means of communication with their chief in Berlin, [[rudolf-steiner|Rudolf Steiner]]" — a phrase Ehret speculates may refer to [[nikola-tesla|Tesla]]'s [[wardenclyffe-tower|Wardenclyffe Tower]] on Long Island.[4] Crowley's mastery of kundalini yoga, druidism and German occult ritual, Ehret argues, positioned him to win the appeal of the "anti-British" resistance movements of India, Ireland and Germany then active in neutral America.[4] Major General [[j-f-c-fuller|J.F.C. Fuller]], initiated into Crowley's Argentum Astrum in 1907, is described as acting as a back channel between Crowley and [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] before and during World War II.[4]
