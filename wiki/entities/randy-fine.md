# Randy Fine

Randy Fine is a Florida State Senator cited by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as an example of extreme rhetoric that nonetheless attracted no doxxing or termination campaign.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

Fine posted a series of tweets claiming there were no innocent Palestinian civilians, calling them "demons that live on Earth" who "deserve death," and demanding that "Gaza must be destroyed."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme sets these statements against the firings of [[maura-finkelstein|Maura Finkelstein]], Hesen Jabr, and Nikki Noor Aytoglu for anti-Zionist speech, asking readers who believe those individuals deserved termination to consider what has been said about Palestinian civilians without consequence.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) The contrast is central to his argument that enforcement of speech norms tracks political alignment rather than principle, and that the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] must protect repellent speech in both directions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## HB 269 and the campaign against Tucker Carlson

As a Florida state senator, Fine sponsored [[florida-hb-269|HB 269]], which created new offenses related to harassment, threats or intimidation targeting religious or ethnic groups.[2] Badlands writers argue that although the bill was presented as targeting graffiti and similar harassment, a key provision criminalized flyers and pamphlets critical of [[israel|Israel]], because the [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|IHRA definition of antisemitism]] equates antisemitism with anti-Zionism; the law also carries harsh prison sentences.[2] Fine and Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] made a "secret trip" to Jerusalem, where DeSantis signed the bill into law.[2]

At the Republican Jewish Coalition summit in November 2025, Fine labeled [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] "the most dangerous antisemite in America" — a designation Badlands attributes to the size and mainstream reach of Carlson's platform rather than to the content of his statements.[2] On March 5, 2026, Fine called Carlson a "cancer" after Carlson published a podcast episode about [[chabad-lubavitch|Chabad-Lubavitch]].[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] presents Fine's career as the clearest illustration of his thesis that antisemitism statutes function as a Trojan Horse against the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[2]

## Attack on Marjorie Taylor Greene (2026)

In March 2026 Fine claimed that [[iran|Iran]] was planning to detonate a nuclear weapon in the United States and, because Rep. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] opposed war with Iran, accused her of "wanting to see America get nuked."[3] The March 10, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] placed the remark within a broader pattern of what it called Christian Dispensationalists in Congress using X to call for societal revulsion against all Muslims, on the reasoning that Muslims intend to exterminate non-Muslims first.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the rhetoric as "deranged and violent" once the mask drops, arguing that its authors project onto Muslims what they themselves possess.[3]

## "Dogs or Muslims" tweet (February 2026)

By February 2026 Fine was serving as a Republican congressman from Florida, and he triggered widespread outrage after tweeting that if forced to choose between dogs and Muslims, the decision would be easy — a remark made in response to comments by a pro-Palestinian activist.[4] Jewish organizations, Democratic lawmakers including Sen. [[jacky-rosen|Jacky Rosen]] and Rep. [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], and other public figures denounced the comment as vile, Islamophobic and dehumanizing, with some calling for his censure or resignation; Fine stood by the statement.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] emphasized that Fine's target was not Hamas or Palestinians specifically but Muslims as a category, a group that would encompass leaders such as Mohammed bin Salman and Recep Tayyip Erdoğan.[4] He questioned whether such rhetoric was defensible at a moment when many Muslim-majority states had pledged trillions of dollars of investment into the US economy, and placed Fine's remark within his broader argument about a Jewish supremacist current in politics operating alongside [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s push to scrap the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]].[4]

## AIPAC withdrawal of endorsement (July 2025)

In late July 2025, the [[aipac|American Israel Public Affairs Committee]] removed Fine's name from its list of endorsed candidates after his repeated public calls to starve the population of Gaza.[5] Fine, elected to Congress in 2025 and described as perhaps the chamber's most overt Zionist, had used his position to promote unconditional US support for Israel and had repeatedly tweeted about his satisfaction at Palestinian suffering.[5] The American Jewish Committee had publicly condemned his comments on July 23, 2025, a week before AIPAC acted, yet Fine continued to post "starve them" messages on X.[5]

Commentators framed the break as evidence that the Israel lobby had reached a crossroads, obliged to distance itself from figures whose rhetoric inflamed the mounting public perception threatening its influence.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] paired the episode with [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]]'s characterization of the Gaza campaign as a "genocide" and with [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]]'s insistence that the starvation reports were Hamas propaganda.[5]

## 2025 special election campaign

Fine, then serving the Florida Senate's 19th District based in Brevard County, won the Republican primary for the April 2025 special election in [[florida-6th-congressional-district-special-election|Florida's 6th Congressional District]], the seat vacated by [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] on his appointment as national security adviser.[6] He faced Democrat [[joshua-weil|Joshua Weil]], a public school educator from Osceola County, along with Libertarian Andrew Parrott, non-party-affiliated candidate Randall Terry, and write-in candidate Chuck Sheridan.[6] The district includes all of Putnam and Flagler counties and parts of St. Johns County.[6]

## January 2025 terror-attack rhetoric

In "Hysteria and Error," Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described Fine as a Florida state senator running to fill the congressional seat vacated by [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]], and the overwhelming favorite in the special election to be held later that month.[7] DeLarme characterized him as so fanatical a supporter of Israel that his posture made [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and [[debbie-wasserman-schultz|Debbie Wasserman Schultz]] look like Hamas supporters.[7]

DeLarme cited a post by Fine responding to the New Year's 2025 attacks as an example of movement figures "who claim to hate the neocons sounding like rabid neocons."[7] He noted that [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]] publicly replied to Fine with what DeLarme called a display of nuance most partisan commentators were incapable of.[7]
