# Nicole Malliotakis

Nicole Malliotakis is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from New York.[1]

## Response to the Cannon fire alarm incident

Following the [[2023-cannon-building-fire-alarm-incident|2023 Cannon Building fire alarm incident]], in which fellow New York Representative [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] pulled a fire alarm during Republican efforts to pass a stopgap spending bill, Malliotakis pledged to introduce legislation to expel Bowman from the House.[1] Her move came alongside [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]]'s call for the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to prosecute Bowman under the obstruction statute applied to January 6 defendants, part of a broader House Republican push for expulsion and prosecution.[1]

## Questioning of federal health officials on vaccine safety

At a February 15, 2024 hearing of the House [[select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]], Malliotakis asked [[peter-marks|Peter Marks]], director of the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]]'s Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research, whether the agency was conducting active extended safety surveillance on recipients of early [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] and whether particular health markers were being studied for trends warranting further inquiry.[2] Marks described surveillance windows dating back to 2020 and deferred to [[daniel-jernigan|Daniel Jernigan]] of the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]], who answered narrowly on myocarditis; critics said the officials had side-stepped Malliotakis's question about long-term monitoring.[2]

## Migrant shelter voter registration allegations (December 2023)

On December 3, 2023, Malliotakis accused New York City of attempting to stack its voter rolls by illegally registering migrants housed at a Staten Island shelter.[3] Joined by four other Staten Island Republicans at a press conference near the former Island Shores Senior Residences on Father Capodanno Boulevard in Midland Beach, she said the city's contract with the nonprofit [[homes-for-the-homeless|Homes for the Homeless]] to run the shelter contained a stipulation requiring distribution of voter registration forms to asylum seekers.[3] "On page 50 of this contract, there is an entire section dedicated to voter registration," she said, calling it "the smoking gun that proves what we've been saying all along — that the city intends to register non-citizens to vote."[3] Malliotakis said the contract was obtained through a state Freedom of Information Law request, and that it required forms be made available in Spanish and Chinese in accordance with the New York City charter.[3]
