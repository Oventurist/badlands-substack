# Theodore Roosevelt

Theodore Roosevelt was the American president under whose "enthusiastic patronage" the United States began applying [[eugenics|eugenics]] laws in 1907.[1]

Matthew Ehret cites this origin point in describing how, by 1932, the [[third-eugenics-conference|Third Eugenics Conference]] in New York drew eugenicists from around the world specifically to study America's successful application of such laws.[1] By the depths of the Great Depression, eugenics statutes had been adopted in two Canadian provinces and in 30 U.S. states, which used them to sterilize those deemed unfit.[1]

## Rise to the presidency and the imperial turn

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Roosevelt as an Anglophilic racist who unilaterally launched the Spanish-American War in 1898, using the four-hour window he had as acting Undersecretary of the Navy — while the actual Secretary was out of Washington — to order Captain Dewey of the Pacific fleet to engage the Spanish over their Philippine territories.[2] President [[william-mckinley|William McKinley]] had resisted the war hawks until that point but ultimately bent to the momentum.[2]

McKinley's assassination on September 18, 1901 catapulted the Mahan-admiring Vice President into high office.[2] Roosevelt had earlier ordered copies of [[alfred-thayer-mahan|Alfred Thayer Mahan]]'s *[[the-influence-of-sea-power-upon-history|Influence of Sea Power Upon History]]* for every member of Congress, and with his ascension it was Mahan's worldview rather than [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]]'s that became the dominant American foreign policy doctrine for the next 120 years.[2]

Ehret writes that Roosevelt lost no time enmeshing America in a new epoch of Anglo-American imperialism abroad, perverting the [[monroe-doctrine|Monroe Doctrine]] into "Big Stick" diplomacy in Latin America, presiding over a growth of eugenics and segregation at home, and creating an independent police state agency in the [[fbi|FBI]].[2] Journalist Martin Sieff is quoted as writing that "Roosevelt devoted his next eight years in the presidency and the rest of his life to integrating the United States and the British Empire into a seamless web of racial imperialist oppression that dominated Latin America, sub-Saharan Africa and Asia."[2]
