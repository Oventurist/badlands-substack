# Jinchao Wei

Jinchao Wei, who also used the alias Patrick Wei, was an active-duty United States Navy sailor stationed at Naval Base San Diego who was convicted by a federal jury of espionage on behalf of the People's Republic of China.[1]

## The case

According to the announcement by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of California, Wei agreed to sell Navy secrets to a Chinese intelligence officer for $12,000 while serving on active duty.[1] Following a five-day trial and one day of deliberation, the jury convicted him of six crimes, including conspiracy to commit espionage, espionage, and unlawful export of — and conspiracy to export — technical data relating to defense articles in violation of the Arms Export Control Act and the International Traffic in Arms Regulations.[1] He was found not guilty of one count of naturalization fraud, making the verdict a conviction on six of seven charges.[1] Sentencing was scheduled for December 1 at 9 a.m., with Wei facing multiple life sentences and a fine of up to $2.5 million.[1]

## Official statements

U.S. Attorney Adam Gordon said the defendant's actions represented "an egregious betrayal of the trust placed in him as a member of the U.S. military," adding that "by trading military secrets to the People's Republic of China for cash, he jeopardized not only the lives of his fellow sailors but also the security of the entire nation and our allies," and that the verdict served as a reminder that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] would "vigorously prosecute traitors."[1] Special Agent in Charge Mark Dargis of the [[fbi|FBI]]'s San Diego Field Office said the conviction — "centered on the deliberate targeting and enticement of U.S. military personnel to betray their country" — demonstrated the lengths to which the People's Republic of China would go to undermine American security.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the case "a strange one," noting that Wei was convicted on August 20 and had openly admitted to the FBI during interview that he was knowingly engaging in espionage.[1] He reported that the Department of Justice appeared to have deleted the press release announcing the conviction from its website, forcing Badlands to link an archived copy, while stressing that the case and conviction were real and that the trial transcripts were sealed.[1] The commentary allowed that the deletion might have been an error that would be corrected, but said [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] had noticed the removal while the Brief was being assembled and that both writers found it notable and worth tracking.[1] The piece asked "what kind of a spy just flippantly admits to being a spy the moment that he is caught?", suggesting the episode felt like a psychological operation — possibly a demoralization campaign or a form of disclosure — and promised follow-up.[1]
