# International Longshoremen's Association

The International Longshoremen's Association (ILA) is the labor union representing dockworkers at ports along the United States East Coast and Gulf Coast.[1]

## 2024 threatened port strike

In September 2024 the ILA was negotiating with the [[united-states-maritime-alliance|United States Maritime Alliance]] (USMX) as a strike deadline approached, raising the prospect of the first dockworker strike in decades and a shutdown of all East and Gulf Coast ports roughly 36 days before the presidential election.[1] The CEO of logistics operator Flexport called the looming strike "the biggest wild card in the presidential election that nobody's talking about."[1]

The [[port-authority-of-new-york-and-new-jersey|Port Authority of New York and New Jersey]] said it was "coordinating with partners across the supply chain to prepare for any impacts" from a possible work stoppage, and urged both sides "to find common ground and keep the cargo flowing for the good of the national economy," noting that $240 billion in goods move through its two ports each year, supporting more than 600,000 local jobs.[1]

## Approach of the 2024 East and Gulf Coast strike

In late September 2024, authorities prepared for a threatened strike by dockworkers represented by the ILA at ports along the East Coast and Gulf Coast as the union negotiated with the [[united-states-maritime-alliance|United States Maritime Alliance]].[2] The [[port-authority-of-new-york-and-new-jersey|Port Authority of New York and New Jersey]] said it was "coordinating with partners across the supply chain to prepare for any impacts," urging both sides "to find common ground and keep the cargo flowing for the good of the national economy," and noting that $240 billion in goods move through its two ports annually, supporting more than 600,000 local jobs.[2] The CEO of [[flexport|Flexport]] called the prospective shutdown "the biggest wild card in the presidential election that nobody's talking about," landing 36 days before the vote.[2]

## September 2024 contract deadline

As of September 30, 2024, time was running out for the ILA and the [[united-states-maritime-alliance]] (USMX) — a coalition of port operators and carriers — to form a new labor contract before the existing one expired at midnight.[3] In a no-deal scenario, thousands of longshoremen at three dozen facilities across fourteen Gulf and East Coast ports would begin striking at 12:01 a.m. EST on Tuesday, October 1, opening what analysts expected to be a major supply chain disruption and inflation surge.[3]

[[goldman-sachs]] analyst Jordan Alliger estimated that upwards of $4.9 billion per day in international trade along the East and Gulf coasts was at risk, with congestion likely to make supply chains less fluid and revive transport price inflation.[3] [[flexport]] chief executive [[ryan-petersen]] described the looming walkout as the biggest unspoken wild card of the presidential election, coming just thirty-six days before the vote.[3] See [[2024-east-and-gulf-coast-dockworker-strike]].[3]
