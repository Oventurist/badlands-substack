# Diebold Election Systems

Diebold Election Systems supplied the paperless touchscreen voting machines used in Georgia for roughly two decades until the end of 2019.[1] The equipment was the subject of the original 2017 [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]] lawsuit, filed during [[brian-kemp|Brian Kemp]]'s tenure as Georgia Secretary of State over concerns about the machines' security.[1]

## Source code review and California decertification

[[j-alex-halderman|Dr. J. Alex Halderman]] helped co-author a "Source Code Review of the Diebold Voting System," which resulted in California de-certifying the Diebold machines.[1] According to Halderman, Georgia nevertheless continued to use the same machines for another decade, until the end of 2019, "without even patching the security flaws."[1]

## Replacement

In 2020, Judge [[amy-totenberg|Amy Totenberg]] ordered Georgia to replace the Diebold equipment.[1] The state settled on the [[dominion-imagecast-x|Dominion ImageCast X]] [[ballot-marking-device|ballot marking device]], a choice made against the advice of election security experts, including the only cybersecurity expert on the governor's commission tasked with recommending a new voting system.[1]
