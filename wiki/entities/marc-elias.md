# Marc Elias

**Marc Elias** is a prominent Democratic election attorney, described in Badlands coverage as a central legal operator in the party's election-litigation apparatus.[1]

## 2026 midterm planning

Elias was part of the slate of experts convened by Senate Minority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] in June 2026 to help Democratic senators prepare for potential election-disruption scenarios ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterms]], alongside former Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]], election-law expert [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]], and the heads of [[democracy-forward|Democracy Forward]] and [[protect-democracy|Protect Democracy]].[1] The group worked on litigation, communications, and response plans, and on coordination with the state and local officials who directly oversee election administration and turnout.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Elias was "strategizing election interference instead of preparing for his inevitable criminal trial, which is a bit discouraging," placing him within the recurring [[shadow-cabal|shadow cabal]] she traces from 2020 to 2026.[1]


## 2026 midterm election fight

In a May 2026 Truth Social post, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said Elias was involved in a Democrat-led "Election Integrity Group" being assembled with [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] under [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]], calling him "a terrible lawyer with a horrible track record" and holding him responsible for the "fake Russia dossier from a foreign nation" used to meddle in the 2016 election.[2] Trump said Republicans would respond with a bigger [[election-integrity-army|Election Integrity Army]].[2]

## February 2026 proposals and Badlands reaction

A blog post published February 13, 2026 on the Patheos site by Ted Peters, a pastor and emeritus theology professor, discussed predictions and recommendations associated with Elias for Election Day 2026, framing them in religious and political terms; the piece was opinion content rather than reporting.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing after discussing the matter with [[canncon|CannCon]] and Rachel Alexander on *Why We Vote*, said "Marc Elias is wigging."[3] She characterized his legal proposal as one in which the federal legislature bars the federal executive from investigating the state executive, after the state legislature bars all local authorities and their people from investigating or questioning the state — summarizing it as removing the checks and balances of democracy in order to save democracy.[3] She also noted his suggestion that local election certification should simply be done by Microsoft Excel, concluding that the founder of Democracy Docket treats both local and federal actors as threats to state election administration.[3]

Ashe argued that Elias appears to regard transparency in elections as an existential threat to his existence, which she found encouraging, and read his apparent derangement as a signal that justice is coming.[3] Responding to online claims that "nothing is happening" because "no one has been arrested yet," she wrote that arrests are occurring but that convictions, not arrests, are the goal, and that cutting corners would ensure the accused walk — since the U.S. justice system prioritizes the rights of the accused and every search, seizure, grand jury and prosecutorial step must be done meticulously by the book.[3]

## DNC separation (April 2023)

In April 2023 the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] parted ways with Elias after what sources described as a number of strategic disagreements, ending a relationship that had run since 2009.[4] He retained his other major clients, continuing to work for the DCCC, DSCC, DAGA and DLCC, and counted a slew of senators and members of Congress among those he represented through the [[elias-law-group|Elias Law Group]].[4] A former partner at [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]], Elias remained the driving force behind [[democracy-docket|Democracy Docket]], which as of April 2023 covered 45 voting-rights cases across 18 states — a record of wins that had raised Republican ire.[4] Federal Election Commission filings showed his firm had already collected roughly $100,000 from the DNC that year, after payments of just under $2 million during the 2022 cycle.[4]

Commentary by [[just-human|Just Human]] in the Badlands News Brief called the split unlikely on its face, given Elias's centrality to Democratic electioneering before and after Election Day across many cycles and the approach of the 2024 campaign.[4] Three possibilities were offered: that his 45-lawsuit caseload left him unable to serve the DNC effectively, that he would be more useful operating outside a formal DNC role, or that he was in legal jeopardy — noting that [[john-durham|John Durham]] had interviewed him and placed him before a grand jury — and that the party was distancing itself before that trouble surfaced.[4] Just Human identified the third explanation as both the preferred and possibly the most likely one.[4]

## Supreme Court argument before Clarence Thomas (December 2025)

In December 2025 Elias argued a case before the Supreme Court of the United States, an appearance conservative outlet Revolver characterized as a humiliation.[5] The account described Elias as a political fixer and "Clinton-world errand boy" whose reputation rests on cable-news appearances rather than courtroom performance, and who helped inject the Russia collusion narrative into American media by turning opposition research into a national preoccupation.[5]

According to the coverage, Elias — accustomed to friendly anchors who never cross-examine him — "ran straight into a brick wall called [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]]."[5] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Elias appears impaired whenever he must operate on the facts, the evidence, and the law, contrasting his confidence in the MSNBC echo chamber with his performance before the Court, and quoted Thomas telling him, "I still don't understand what you're saying."[5] She framed Elias's continued liberty as a "solid litmus test" for progress in restoring justice and the rule of law.[5]

## 2016 Clinton campaign post-mortem

Public records cited in newly declassified material show that Elias, as general counsel to the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] campaign, was among the senior figures who joined a coordinated effort to link then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Russian president [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] during the 2016 race, alongside campaign manager [[robby-mook|Robby Mook]], chairman [[john-podesta|John Podesta]], communications director [[jennifer-palmieri|Jennifer Palmieri]], and policy adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]].[6] The [[fbi|FBI]] was alerted in 2016 to intelligence describing this plan and then conducted a sweeping investigation into the resulting collusion claims.[6] The question now before the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] strike force created by Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] is whether the effort was a political dirty trick or a criminal conspiracy.[6]

## Wyoming proof-of-citizenship case (2025)

In July 2025 Elias represented the [[equality-state-policy-center|Equality State Policy Center]] in a federal lawsuit challenging [[wyoming-house-bill-156|Wyoming House Bill 156]], the state law requiring voters to provide proof of U.S. citizenship and state residency to register.[7] U.S. District Court Judge Scott Skavdahl dismissed the case on July 22, 2025, and Secretary of State [[chuck-gray|Chuck Gray]] declared that "Marc Elias and the radical left were no match for our vigorous defense."[7] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] marked the loss as "a day to toast justice" and linked Elias to the subpoena of [[actblue|ActBlue]] over alleged foreign money laundering, noting that [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]] and later the [[elias-law-group|Elias Law Group]] had advised ActBlue on legal compliance.[7]
