# Mike Lawler

Mike Lawler is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from New York (R-NY). He appears in the Badlands Media corpus as one of several congressional Republicans who publicly declined to endorse President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s maximalist position against mail-in voting during the 2026 election-reform debate.[1]

## Position on mail-in voting

In a February 2026 Politico report summarized by the Badlands Brief, Lawler was quoted saying, "I support the use of mail-in voting," while drawing a distinction between requested absentee ballots and universal mail distribution: "The idea that some states just mail out ballots without any requests is absurd, but the use of mail-in balloting, I do not have an objection."[1] The quotation was presented by the Brief as evidence that Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] were "riding the fence" on election reform rather than following Trump, who had characterized mail voting as "a corrupt system."[1]

## Significance in the corpus

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the resistance of members such as Lawler as self-interested: sitting legislators are "the beneficiaries of elections" and are therefore unlikely to change the rules during an election year.[1] In the Brief's reading, election-related legislation was passing the House but was expected to stall in the [[senate|Senate]], making individual House Republicans' hedging a leading indicator of the reform effort's likely failure before the midterms.[1]
