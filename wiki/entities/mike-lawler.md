# Mike Lawler

Mike Lawler is a Republican member of the U.S. House of Representatives from New York.[1]

## Position on mail-in voting

In February 2026, Politico reported that Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] were resisting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s calls for sweeping election reform, and quoted Lawler among the members distancing themselves from the President's position.[1] "I support the use of mail-in voting," Lawler said. "The idea that some states just mail out ballots without any requests is absurd, but the use of mail-in balloting, I do not have an objection."[1]

His remarks were cited in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] as evidence that congressional Republicans were "riding the fence" on the [[election-reform-campaign|election reform campaign]], with reform measures passing the House but expected to stall in the [[united-states-senate|Senate]].[1]

## Antisemitism Awareness Act

Lawler introduced the bipartisan [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]], which the House passed 320–91 on May 1, 2024 amid pro-Palestinian protests roiling college campuses.[2] The bill, which received backing from Democratic moderates supportive of [[israel|Israel]] amid its war with [[hamas]], would mandate that the [[education-department|Education Department]] adopt the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]]'s broad definition of antisemitism when enforcing anti-discrimination laws.[2] Its prospects in the Senate were unclear, with Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] saying "we haven't seen what the House is sending us yet."[2]

## Marijuana rescheduling

On Monday, December 15, 2025, Lawler said that it would be "wrong" for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to reclassify marijuana as a less dangerous drug, telling NewsNation's "The Hill" host Blake Burman: "I think rescheduling marijuana is wrong. The fact is, marijuana is a gateway drug. Most of the people who end up using hard substances start out on marijuana."[3] Trump had said the same day that he was "considering" reclassifying cannabis; the Biden administration had begun efforts to reschedule marijuana as a Schedule III drug the prior year but did not complete the process before leaving office.[3]


## IGO Anti-Boycott Act

Lawler introduced the [[igo-anti-boycott-act|IGO Anti-Boycott Act]], House Resolution 867, on January 31, 2025, together with New Jersey Democrat [[josh-gottheimer|Josh Gottheimer]].[4] The bill would expand a 2018 statute criminalizing participation in boycotts of Israel — which carries penalties of up to twenty years in prison and fines up to one million dollars — to cover international governmental organizations, and would permit prosecution of those who publicly shared information intending to connect individuals working for such IGOs with the Israeli or American governments.[4]

A vote scheduled for Monday in early May 2025 was cancelled after intense online pushback, including accusations from critics that Lawler was engaging in treason for endorsing the bill.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] endorsed that criticism and called for the entire [[congress|Congress]] to be investigated for potential foreign influence.[4]
