# European Food Safety Authority

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) is the European Union agency that provides scientific advice on food safety to EU institutions. Its opinions underpin authorization decisions taken by the [[european-commission|European Commission]].[1]

## Cricket powder authorization

In January 2023 the European Commission passed a ruling — citing the scientific opinion of the European Food Safety Authority — authorizing a food additive made from powdered, defatted house crickets for use across the European Union.[1] The decision permitted the additive in a broad range of products, "including but not limited to cereal bars, biscuits, pizza, pasta-based products, and whey powder."[1] Under the authorization, the crickets must first be made to "discard their bowel content" before being frozen.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] questioned the health basis of the approval, noting that several bacterial species have been associated with both farm-reared and wild-caught "edible" insects — including members of the genera Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, Bacillus, Pseudomonas, Micrococcus, Lactobacillus, Erwinia, Clostridium and Acinetobacter, as well as the family Enterobacteriaceae — and that insects can potentially serve as replicative vectors for viruses that infect vertebrates.[1] He asked whether a third-party organization could verify that bowel contents had in fact been discarded.[1] See also [[edible-insects|edible insects]].
