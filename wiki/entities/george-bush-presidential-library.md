# George Bush Presidential Library

The **George Bush Presidential Library**, housed at the George Bush Center at Texas A&M University, is the repository of the papers and materials of [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]].[1] It became a focus of controversy when [[don-w-wilson|Don W. Wilson]], then [[archivist-of-the-united-states|Archivist of the United States]], announced he would leave the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] on March 31 to become the center's executive director — three weeks after relinquishing control of White House computer files to Bush.[1]

## The bowling alley and Chinese restaurant

Before the permanent facility existed, the material was processed in improvised quarters. According to the Los Angeles Times, "the stuff of George Bush's life — an old infielder's mitt, the door of a Kuwaiti palace, even a huge likeness of Bush's head from a Republican convention — is being sorted in an old bowling alley," the Chimney Hill Bowl, with the overflow crammed next door into what used to be the kitchen of a Chinese restaurant.[1] The collection comprised 36 million pages of documents, a million photos and 40,000 objects.[1]

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] referenced this arrangement at a rally in Mesa, Arizona, saying his predecessor "took millions and millions of documents to a former bowling alley pieced together with what was then an old and broken Chinese restaurant... And it had a broken front door and broken windows. Other than that, it was quite secure."[1] [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] publicly defended his late father after Trump initiated calls to investigate the 41st president.[1] The media ridiculed Trump and the National Archives refuted his charges, but [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues the Los Angeles Times reporting vindicates the substance of his account.[1]

Crucially, the Times noted that the material was not confined to Bush's presidency: other items came from his eight years as vice president, his tenure as a Texas congressman, his time as [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] director and other government posts.[1] "Some printed material is classified and will remain so for years; it is open only to those with top-secret clearances."[1] Lange draws attention to the congressional-era records, noting that Bush travelled to Vietnam with a CIA frontman at the time the [[phoenix-program|Phoenix Program]] was being set up and the CIA was fighting a [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war in Laos]] to protect heroin trafficking.[1]

## Presidential libraries as control mechanisms

The article generalizes from this case to argue that presidential libraries function as instruments of concealment. A library is nominally a museum for the public, so Lange asks why classified documents viewable only by holders of top secret clearance should be stored at a former president's personal library rather than in a secure location in Washington, D.C.[1] His answer is that past presidents are afraid of the [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]], and that housing records at personal libraries lets them retain control.[1] Quoting QAnon material, he writes that "presidential libraries are put in place for past presidents to retain control over self-incriminating classified document releases."[1]
