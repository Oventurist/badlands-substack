# Silverado Savings and Loan Association

The Silverado Banking, Savings and Loan Association of Denver was the Colorado thrift on whose board [[neil-bush|Neil Bush]] sat from 1985, and whose collapse became the most notorious single failure of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]].[1]

Bush voted to approve $96 million in loans to [[william-walters|William Walters]] without disclosing that Walters had invested $150,000 in his energy company and that he owed Walters money.[1] In 1986 he asked the bank to approve a $900,000 line of credit for [[kenneth-good|Kenneth Good]], who had earlier "forgiven" a $100,000 loan to Bush, and abstained on votes approving a further $34 million in loans to Good.[1] The two men ultimately defaulted on Silverado loans totalling $106 million.[1]

In 1987 federal regulators in Topeka, Kansas accused Silverado of "self-dealing" and making preferential loans to favored customers, including its own officers.[1] The bank collapsed and the bailout of its federally insured deposits was expected to cost $1 billion.[1] The Orlando Sentinel described the internal circuit precisely: as a director Bush could and did approve million-dollar salaries for the bank's officers, who could and did recommend multimillion-dollar loans to Bush's friends, which Bush could and did vote to approve.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] emphasizes that regulators purposely delayed announcing their takeover of the bankrupt thrift until after [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was elected president in 1988, and that regulators who might have scrutinized Silverado saw the vice president's son and knew the vice president chaired the [[task-force-on-regulatory-relief|task force]] [[deregulation|deregulating]] banking — so they did nothing.[1]


## Reference in "Profiting From War"

"Profiting From War" describes [[neil-bush|Neil Bush]] as "most famous for the scandal surrounding the corrupt practices of Colorado's Silverado Savings & Loan," where he served as a director during the 1980s, before turning to business opportunities in the Persian Gulf and to his partnership with [[jamal-daniel|Jamal Daniel]].[2] The article groups the episode with the wider [[savings-and-loan-crisis|Savings and Loan scandal]], which it says transferred taxpayer money through a federal bailout while [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was president.[2]
