# Tim Walz

Tim Walz is the Governor of Minnesota. In January 2026 he authorized the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] "to be staged and ready to support local and state law enforcement if needed" in response to demonstrations in Minneapolis following a fatal shooting during a federal immigration enforcement operation.[1]

## Executive Order 26-01

Walz issued Executive Order 26-01, which authorizes the Adjutant General to place National Guard personnel, equipment, and facilities on state active duty to coordinate and support public safety and security services in Minnesota.[1] Under the order the Guard serves in a support role focused on protecting property, safeguarding critical infrastructure, and allowing local law enforcement to remain focused on community safety and investigative responsibilities.[1] The state additionally mobilized 85 members of the Minnesota State Patrol's Mobile Response Team to support law enforcement in the Twin Cities.[1]

The order's preamble stated that on January 7, 2026, federal immigration agents shot and killed Renee Nicole Good in south Minneapolis, and that the shooting immediately followed the federal government's surge of masked immigration officials in Minnesota.[1] It accused those agents of indiscriminately arresting people off the street, intimidating residents, and causing a loss of life, and said federal immigration officials had "increased tensions, caused chaos, and left our state less safe."[1]

Walz publicly stated: "Minnesotans have met this moment. Thousands of people have peacefully made their voices heard... We have every reason to believe that peace will hold."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor Ashe in America read the deployment as Walz "mobilizing the Minnesota National Guard to fight federal agents," framing the events as a rerun of the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] episode with a "winter protest premium," and describing the resistance as seizing its moment.[1]

## Whistleblower allegations in the Somali fraud scandal

Whistleblowers, including Minnesota state workers, provided affidavits during the January 2026 congressional investigations claiming they had warned Governor Walz and Attorney General [[keith-ellison|Keith Ellison]] about the welfare fraud involving the state's Somali immigrant community, but that no action was taken; both officials deny the allegations.[2] The [[minnesota-somali-fraud-hearings|investigations]] led by [[james-comer|James Comer]], [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] and [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] estimated that up to half of $18–20 billion in federally funded welfare spending had been lost to fraud.[2]

## The Pretti shooting, January 2026

After federal immigration agents fatally shot 37-year-old U.S. citizen [[alex-jeffrey-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] in Minneapolis on January 24, 2026, Walz joined Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] in sharply rebuking the federal operation as excessive and calling for the withdrawal of federal agents and for accountability.[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] responded on Truth Social by backing the agents, questioning the absence of local police, and accusing Minnesota officials of "inciting insurrection," while linking the unrest to alleged "theft and fraud" involving billions of dollars tied to state officials.[3]

## Apparent thaw with the White House (January 2026)

On Monday, January 26, 2026, Walz and President [[donald-trump|Trump]] spoke in a private call, after which Trump said the two were "on a similar wavelength" regarding federal immigration operations in [[minnesota|Minnesota]]; Walz's office described the call as "productive."[4] Trump also reportedly spoke with Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]].[4] Badlands contributor Ashe in America read Walz's conciliatory language as a sign either that conditions in Minnesota had become untenable or that the governor had reverted to performance.[4]

Despite the apparent thaw, protests continued in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] alongside federal immigration operations under [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]], and Trump announced he was sending Border Czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] to the state to take charge of enforcement.[4]

## Exit from the 2026 governor's race

On January 6, 2026, Walz announced that he would not seek a third term as governor of Minnesota, confirming that he plans to leave office at the end of his current term in January 2027.[5] Walz has served as Minnesota's governor since 2019 and was re-elected once; Minnesota imposes no term limits on governors.[5] He said the decision was not tied to immediate political plans, citing a desire to spend more time with his family and the need to focus fully on governing during an "enormously challenging" year.[5]

Central to that year was a massive welfare and child care fraud scandal involving hundreds of millions — and possibly billions — of dollars in misused federal funds, often tied to programs serving the Somali community.[5] Walz complained that "the political gamesmanship we're seeing from Republicans is only making that fight harder to win," and said that "every minute I spend defending my own political interests would be a minute I can't spend defending the people of Minnesota against the criminals who prey on our generosity and the cynics who prey on our differences."[5] He concluded: "So I've decided to step out of the race and let others worry about the election while I focus on the work."[5]

Democrats including [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]], and JB Pritzker praised his service and character, while Republicans characterized his departure as accountability for "failed leadership."[5] Senator [[amy-klobuchar|Amy Klobuchar]] was reported to be considering a run for the governorship.[5] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that whether Walz is in or out of office should be irrelevant to whether he is prosecuted, and asked how much of the fraud reached "the Americans charged with preventing it."[5]

## 2024 vice-presidential nomination

In August 2024, Walz was selected by [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] as her running mate on the Democratic presidential ticket, a choice announced shortly after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] withdrew from the race.[6] Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] welcomed the decision as a "shocking pick," telling Fox News's "Fox and Friends" on August 7 that "he's a very liberal man, and he's a shocking pick. I could not be more thrilled."[6] Trump characterized Walz as a "smarter version" of Harris and "about the same as [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]]," though Walz's voting record has been assessed as substantially more moderate than Sanders's.[6]

Trump attacked Walz as weak on immigration and "very heavy into transgender," a reference to legislation Walz signed as governor protecting access to gender-affirming care.[6] He also raised the Minnesota riots that followed the 2020 murder of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] by police, suggesting that episode was the only time the two men interacted during his presidency; Walz had faced Republican criticism for being slow to activate the National Guard to quell the demonstrations.[6] Trump argued that Pennsylvania Governor [[josh-shapiro|Josh Shapiro]] would have been the stronger choice.[6]

On their first full day campaigning together, Harris and Walz toured Wisconsin and Michigan, and Harris told a rally in Eau Claire, "As Tim Walz likes to point out, we are joyful warriors."[6] Their travel briefly overlapped on a Wisconsin tarmac with Republican vice-presidential nominee [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], narrowly avoiding a confrontation.[6] The Harris campaign reported raising $36 million in the first 24 hours after the announcement.[6]

His selection also revived scrutiny of his National Guard record, discussed at [[tim-walz-military-service-controversy|the Tim Walz military service controversy]], in which veterans of his unit alleged he retired ahead of an Iraq deployment after assuring his troops he would join them.[6]

## 2024 vice-presidential debate

Walz's October 1, 2024 debate against Senator [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] was widely judged a loss, with commentators contrasting Vance's "commanding performance" against what New York Times columnist Ross Douthat called "a nervous ramble."[7] Fellow Democrat [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] conceded on Jimmy Kimmel's program that Vance had been the better debater.[7] [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said Walz "embarrassed himself and the Democrat Party" and "proved to be a man that doesn't have it in any way shape or form for the office that he is seeking," though he added he would place Walz "a large number of steps above Kamala."[7] See [[2024-vice-presidential-debate|2024 vice-presidential debate]].[7]

## October 2024 allegations and satire

In an October 2024 Badlands commentary opposing early voting, Ashe in America referenced pedophilia allegations against Walz, stating that there were by then multiple accusers and documentation, and arguing that the roughly three million Americans who had already cast ballots would be unable to change their votes if the allegations proved true.[8] In the same brief, Walz appeared as a target of Saturday Night Live satire, portrayed in a Family Feud–style sketch as a knucklehead white guy.[8]

## DHS records and FBI counterintelligence probe (2025)

On Tuesday, July 29, 2025, [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] announced it had obtained 47 pages of [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] records through a FOIA lawsuit revealing that Walz's ties to the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] had prompted a counterintelligence probe involving both DHS and the [[fbi|FBI]].[9] According to internal communications, officials concluded that "China is happy" with Walz's selection as [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris's]] running mate on the 2024 Democratic ticket and viewed him as someone they could influence in Washington.[9]

The records were released in response to a March 2025 FOIA lawsuit seeking DHS communications and intelligence reports related to Walz.[9] An email dated August 6, 2024 — the day Walz was named the vice-presidential nominee — shows DHS officials discussing CCP influence operations targeting US governors, specifically Walz and Nevada Governor Joe Lombardo.[9] One redacted official wrote: "You all have no idea how this feeds into what PRC has been doing here with him and local gov. It's seriously a line of the intel. Target someone who is perceived they can get to DC."[9]

Officials cited raw intelligence, NGO links with prior CCP contacts, and coordination with [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] and the FBI's Counterintelligence Task Force.[9] An August 7 Microsoft Teams post included a BBC article highlighting Walz's 1989 teaching stint in Guangdong Province, which drew attention on Chinese social media for its timing with the Tiananmen Square massacre; the article trended on Weibo with 12 million views.[9]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] commented that Trump Derangement Syndrome had turned "the walls are closing in" into a meme rather than a claim about actual justice, but that the rapid pace of disclosure did appear to be building toward accountability for major figures in what she called the grand conspiracy to defraud the American people.[9] She noted that allegations of inappropriate and possibly illegal ties to China would normally have disappeared for an election loser, but that post-election norms had been destroyed and the campaign allegations against Walz were instead becoming better sourced over time.[9]

## Response to the 2025 lawmaker shootings

Following the [[2025-minnesota-lawmaker-shootings|targeted shootings]] of June 14, 2025, Walz announced in a news briefing that Minnesota House Speaker [[melissa-hortman|Melissa Hortman]] and her husband had been fatally shot at their Brooklyn Park home and that State Senator [[john-hoffman|John Hoffman]] and his wife had been wounded at their home in Champlin, calling the attacks "politically motivated."[10]

## Vice presidential debate scheduling (August 2024)

In mid-August 2024, while still the presumptive Democratic vice presidential nominee ahead of the Democratic National Convention, Walz accepted CBS's invitation to an October 1 debate in New York City against [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], writing on X: "See you on October 1, JD."[11] CBS had offered four possible dates — September 17, September 24, October 1 and October 8.[11] It was unclear at the time of reporting whether Walz would also accept a CNN-proposed debate on September 18 that Vance had agreed to.[11]

## Trump's Thanksgiving 2025 attack

In a Thanksgiving 2025 Truth Social statement on immigration, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called Walz the "seriously retarded Governor of Minnesota," accusing him of doing nothing "either through fear, incompetence, or both" about what Trump described as Somali gangs roving the streets of Minnesota while residents stayed locked in their homes.[12] Trump asserted that hundreds of thousands of Somali refugees were "completely taking over the once great State of Minnesota," pairing the attack on Walz with criticism of Representative [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] and an announcement that he would permanently pause migration from Third World countries.[12]

## 2028 presidential speculation

In early March 2025, Minnesota Governor Walz indicated openness to a potential presidential run in 2028, remarking, "If I feel like I can serve, I will," while acknowledging that "if, nationally, people are like, 'Dude, we tried you and look how that worked out,' I'm good with that."[13] Asked directly about a future bid, Walz cited advice from a friend — "Never turn down a job you haven't been offered" — and said he would consider running if he believed he possessed something valuable to contribute, adding, "I'm also, though, not arrogant enough to believe there's a lot of people that can do this."[13] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry mocked the trial balloon, calling Walz "the biggest goofball to ever be duped into running for office" and living proof that career politicians are puppets, while arguing that none of these figures should still be in government by 2028 if the movement succeeds.[13]
