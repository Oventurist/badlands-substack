# Bernie Sanders

Bernie Sanders is an American democratic socialist politician and United States senator, long associated with advocacy for public ownership stakes and government intervention in strategic industries.[1]

In commentary on the Senate's Fiscal Year 2027 [[national-defense-authorization-act|National Defense Authorization Act]] provision creating a federal strategic investment fund, Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's push to take direct equity stakes in private companies runs "along parallel rails" to policies Sanders has advocated for decades.[1] The commentary suggested that the convergence could place Trump in the good graces of American socialists and produce a period in which American conservatives and socialists find common cause in a "Golden Age transition" that bears the hallmarks of both but fully encapsulates neither.[1]

## Opposition to Israel military aid (2024)

On April 22, 2024, Sanders (I-Vt.) said he would put forth an amendment to remove offensive military funding for Israel from a House-passed aid package that the Senate was set to consider that week, cutting "billions in offensive military funding to Israel from the proposed national security supplemental package."[2] The package, approved by the Republican-controlled House over the preceding weekend, included $17 billion in unconditional military assistance to the Israeli government, which stood accused on the world stage of perpetrating genocide in [[gaza|Gaza]].[2]

Sanders said he would also offer an amendment to "protect essential humanitarian operations" in the Gaza Strip, where millions faced possible starvation under Israel's blockade and at least 28 children under the age of 12 had starved to death in recent weeks.[2] A further Sanders amendment would restore U.S. funding to [[unrwa|UNRWA]], the most important aid agency working in Gaza, after Washington suspended that aid in late January 2024 over Israeli allegations — allegations an independent report found unsupported by evidence.[2] The House-passed Israel legislation would prohibit funding for the organization.[2] Sanders argued the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] "should have a chance to debate and vote on the key components of such a massive package."[2]

## HELP Committee confrontation (November 2023)

As chairman of the Senate [[health-education-labor-and-pensions-committee|Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee]], Sanders intervened on November 14, 2023 to halt a confrontation in which Senator [[markwayne-mullin|Markwayne Mullin]] challenged [[teamsters|Teamsters]] president [[sean-obrien|Sean O'Brien]] to a physical fight during a hearing.[3] Both men had risen from their seats before Sanders stopped the exchange, a role Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted with irony in describing "Crazy Bernie" as the peacemaker.[3]

## "Establishment tool" critique (Badlands Media, 2023)

An October 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that Sanders, once seen as the left's leading anti-establishment insurgent, had been fully absorbed by the Democratic Party machine he once vowed to dismantle.[4] The piece contrasted the 2015–2016 moment, when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the right and Sanders on the left both appeared to be genuine outsiders polling strongly against [[uniparty|uniparty]] favorites, with the situation seven years later.[4] Sanders' original message, the essay noted, was not that the Democratic Party had minor internal differences but that it was fundamentally corrupted by a donor class that also funds establishment Republicans, is willing to destroy the middle class, and pursues trade deals that deindustrialize the United States; he campaigned on dismantling that establishment.[4]

The essay traced two capitulations. In 2016, the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]], firmly behind [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], was shown by leaked emails to have worked against Sanders' primary campaign, and Sanders declined to press the fight.[4] Asked by Chuck Todd whether his fellow Democrats had been fair to him, Sanders replied, "No... Look, we're taking on the establishment. That's pretty clear."[4] In 2020, after several lengthy phone calls from [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] — described in the essay as the de facto leader of the Democratic Party — Sanders again withdrew, persuaded that his mission had been accomplished by pushing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] further left.[4] From the perspective of his supporters, the essay argued, the movement's point was never to make establishment figures merely sound more left-wing, but to take the party back for its voters.[4]

By August 2023 Sanders had become one of the first members of Congress to endorse Biden for re-election, declining to acknowledge rival Democratic contenders including [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and [[marianne-williamson|Marianne Williamson]], and actively working against the independent campaign of his longtime ally [[cornel-west|Cornel West]].[4] The essay characterized this as the demolition of the notion that a genuine "oppositional faction" exists within the Democratic Party, and framed Sanders as the establishment's "attack dog" deployed against West precisely because West could otherwise win over disillusioned Sanders and [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Ocasio-Cortez]] supporters.[4]

## Early political career and Liberty Union years

Sanders entered politics in the 1970s in Vermont, remaining largely unemployed while running repeatedly for office, including two Senate campaigns on the ticket of the minor [[liberty-union-party|Liberty Union]] party.[4] His rhetoric in that period has been described as "downright apocalyptic and conspiratorial."[4] Announcing his 1974 Senate bid, Sanders declared: "I have the very frightened feeling that if fundamental and radical change does not come about in the very near future that our nation, and, in fact, our entire civilization could soon be entering an economic dark age."[4] Later that year he sent an open letter to President [[gerald-ford|Gerald Ford]] warning of a "virtual Rockefeller family dictatorship over the nation" if [[nelson-rockefeller|Nelson Rockefeller]] were named vice president, and called for the immediate disbanding of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] amid revelations of the agency's misdeeds.[4]

## Mayor of Burlington and the waterfront reversal

After losing his Senate bids, Sanders ran for mayor of [[burlington-vermont|Burlington, Vermont]] in 1980, entering a crowded race as a socialist and attacking local developer [[antonio-pomerleau|Tony Pomerleau]].[4] He won by ten votes out of 8,650 cast, upsetting the city's political establishment.[4] The former director of the Burlington Mall said of him at the time: "He has figured out that there is a cow to be milked. He wants to build a tx base. What he does and what he says are two different things."[4]

Sanders had campaigned on the slogan "Burlington is not for sale!", aimed at Pomerleau, and denounced Pomerleau's proposed lakefront condominium and shopping center as an "enclave for the rich."[4] Once in office he struck an alliance with the Pomerleau family that lasted until Pomerleau's death in 2018, eased his rhetoric, and began collaborating with Burlington's upper class.[4] Progressive journalist Greg Guma recalled that opposition to real estate development was largely why Sanders won, with local progressives protesting that "low-income people were being driven out of town... by condominium and office conversions and high rents," to be replaced by upwardly mobile young professionals.[4] As mayor, Sanders instead embraced a larger waterfront plan called "Aldean" that would "dwarf the $30 million Pomerleau project," bringing in outside investors including the heiress to the Dow Jones fortune.[4] Supporters later discovered he had been holding private meetings with those investors — precisely the behavior he had condemned in the establishment.[4] When the Citizens Waterfront Group demanded transparency and an end to closed-door meetings, Sanders "blasted the group," and green-movement figures accused him of "collusion with business interests."[4] Voters ultimately rejected the municipal bond referendum needed to finance the project.[4]

## Defense industry record

The Badlands essay also charged Sanders with hypocrisy on militarism, noting that he rails against defense corporations at rallies while omitting his own history with the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]].[4] After the bond referendum defeat, Sanders remained popular thanks to a booming Burlington economy propped up in part by a [[general-electric|General Electric]] plant — GE was then among the nation's largest defense contractors — and by the very military spending Sanders campaigned against.[4] Though he pledged to "challenge Reaganism and imperialism in its every manifestation," the plant employed three thousand workers and had nearly doubled its defense contracts by 1986, producing Vulcan Gatling guns mounted on attack helicopters and fighter jets — weapons used, the essay notes, to kill socialists in Central America.[4] When progressive protesters, many of them his own supporters, picketed the plant, Sanders sided with GE executives, denounced the protests, and had some demonstrators arrested.[4] He later persuaded [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] to place a research center in Burlington and secured the stationing of 18 Lockheed Martin [[f-35|F-35]] fighter jets at the city's airport for the Vermont National Guard.[4] The World Socialist Web Site called Sanders a "silent partner of American militarism," and the left-wing magazine Counterpunch has repeatedly criticized him as insufficiently pacifist.[4]

## Legacy on the anti-establishment left

"War Pigs" argues that a growing contingent of left-leaning pundits and journalists became increasingly anti-establishment after being "given hope and then duped" by figures such as Sanders and [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], and that those betrayals molded them into effective critics of establishment Democrats whom the populist left will actually listen to.[5]

The article cites [[briahna-joy-gray|Briahna Joy Gray]], Sanders' former National Press Secretary, as the prime example of this type of journalist, recalling her criticism on [[rising|Rising]] of Sanders' attacks against [[cornel-west|Cornel West]].[5]
