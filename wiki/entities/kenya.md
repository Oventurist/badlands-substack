# Kenya

Kenya is an East African republic that appears in the Badlands corpus principally in connection with its August 2023 decision to suspend the biometric cryptocurrency venture [[worldcoin|Worldcoin]] from operating within its borders.[1]

## Suspension of biometric data harvesting

The Kenyan Ministry of the Interior suspended Worldcoin and "any similar entities" in the first week of August 2023, after the company had been offering roughly $50 in free crypto tokens to Kenyans willing to have their eyeballs scanned by its "Orb" device.[1] The Ministry announced that security, financial services and data protection agencies had opened inquiries into the authenticity and legality of the activity, the safety of the data being harvested, and the harvesters' intentions for its use.[1] Cabinet Secretary [[alfred-mutua|Alfred Mutua]] framed the suspension in blunt terms, saying Kenyans should not be used as "guinea pigs" for data harvesting.[1]

## Place in the Badlands narrative

Badlands commentary treated Kenya's action as part of a wider African assertion of sovereignty in mid-2023, appearing alongside coverage of the [[2023-niger-coup|2023 Niger coup]] in the same brief.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "Africa is coming into focus in a big way this month," pairing the Niger events with "the people of Kenya now rejecting the global surveillance state," and argued that it falls to the population of each nation to halt what she termed the globalist agenda.[1]

## Finance Bill 2024 protests

In June 2024 Nairobi descended into violence as protests against new tax policies and an "Eco-Austerity" program culminated in the parliament building being set on fire and legislators evacuating.[2] Lawmakers had just passed the Finance Bill 2024, which imposed nationwide taxes including an eco-levy affecting diapers, batteries, smartphones, earphones, clocks, radios, televisions, cameras, staplers, printers, calculators, photocopiers, keyboards, mice, projectors and LCD monitors, with a hefty per-kilo fee on imported plastics.[2] Protesters entering parliament shouted "We're coming for every politician," and police were widely reported to have used live fire alongside tear gas, while President [[william-ruto|William Ruto]] attended an African Union event outside the capital.[2]
