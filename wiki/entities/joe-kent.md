# Joe Kent

Joe Kent is a former U.S. Army special operations soldier, political candidate and intelligence official who served as director of the National Counterterrorism Center and as a top aide to Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] in the second Trump administration.[1] In March 2026 he became the first senior official in that administration to resign in protest over the ongoing U.S. war in [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Military background

Kent is a combat veteran who deployed to combat eleven times.[1] His wife, Shannon Kent, was also serving in the Middle East and was killed during an attack in Syria; Kent has laid the blame for her death at the feet of the [[cia|CIA]].[1] His deployment history was repeatedly cited by allies as evidence of his standing to criticize U.S. Middle East policy.[1]

## Public profile

Kent became a nationally recognized figure after appearing on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s *War Room* program countless times throughout the Biden administration, where he was a leading voice of the non-interventionist wing of the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]].[1]

## Resignation over the Iran war

In a strongly worded resignation letter, Kent said he could not support the conflict in good conscience, declaring that "Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation" and asserting that the war was started because of pressure from [[israel|Israel]] and its powerful American lobby.[1] He accused high-ranking Israeli officials and influential American media outlets of running a misinformation campaign that undermined [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s "America First" foreign policy, comparing the tactics to those used to draw the United States into the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]].[1] Kent urged Trump to return to the non-interventionist approach of his 2016, 2020 and 2024 campaigns and of his first term.[1]

## Reaction

Trump called Kent "a nice guy" but "weak on security."[1] White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] rejected Kent's claims as "insulting and laughable," while figures in Trumpworld described him as a suspected leaker and a "crazed egomaniac."[1] Administration officials braced for further fallout, including a planned interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].[1]

Bannon defended Kent, citing his Middle East deployment history and calling for an investigation into the claims in the resignation letter that Israel controls U.S. foreign policy.[1] General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] also responded publicly to the affair.[1] In the hours following the resignation, Gabbard broke her long silence on the Iran war.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] compared the announcement to the reported death of [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] in Iran as another unexpected and strange development, arguing that plotlines of this kind "don't just fizzle out."[1] He described [[uniparty|Uniparty]] figures as participating in a "communist struggle session" against Kent, suggested Gabbard may be the next target, and asked why Israel dissidents inside the administration appear to be hunted down and removed from office.[1]

## Aftermath and Tucker interview

Later coverage tied Kent's resignation directly to the [[south-pars-gas-field|South Pars gas field]] strikes and the U.S. decision to attack [[iran|Iran]], describing him as the counterterrorism chief whose departure escalated the foreign-policy divide inside the MAGA coalition.[2] In his resignation letter Kent declared that the United States entered the conflict "due to pressure from Israel and its powerful American lobby" rather than vital American interests, and also blamed [[israel|Israel]] for several historical events.[2] Outlets including Politico and the Wall Street Journal treated the letter as evidence that MAGA was fracturing and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was losing his base.[2]

On [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s podcast, Kent did not attack or subvert Trump; he repeatedly said the situation was salvageable and that Trump is uniquely qualified to resolve it, laying out a path in which Washington tells Israel to back down or lose U.S. military support, then partners with the Gulf Arabs to negotiate a settlement requiring Iran to sell oil in U.S. dollars.[2] Kent also told Carlson that negotiations with Iran had been progressing and a deal was absolutely possible.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized Kent's "performative resignation" as arriving the same week the Iran theater ignited, arguing it functioned as a cognitive ranger sent to the perimeter of the Overton window to plant narrative seeds granting Trump's own measured shifts plausible deniability with his base.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] similarly read the sequence — Kent's letter, Trump's Truth Social post blaming Israel, [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s "acted alone" confirmation, and the Qatar–Turkey press conference — as a coordinated situation.[2]

## Syria disclosure (March 2026)

In an interview with MintPress News reported on March 27, 2026, Kent — identified as the former head of the U.S. [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]] who resigned over disagreements on Iran policy — said the United States "worked directly with Al-Qaeda" and Islamic State militants during the Syrian conflict.[3] He described U.S. efforts to support opposition forces in [[syria|Syria]], including groups he said were linked to [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] and later [[islamic-state|ISIS]], and characterized U.S. involvement as part of a broader pattern of military actions conducted on behalf of [[israel|Israel]], referencing both the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]] and Syria.[3] He said ISIS "got out of control," prompting subsequent U.S. military involvement under the stated aim of combating terrorism.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called this the long-awaited disclosure and stressed its significance as coming from a former top intelligence official of the Trump administration.[3] He noted the awkwardness of Israeli minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] calling for the assassination of Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] — formerly Mohammed Abu al-Jolani, once a leader in both Al Qaeda and ISIS — and speculated that Israel had backed al-Sharaa's rise expecting him to cede southern Syrian territory, only to find him aligned with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]].[3] He cited reports of [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] forces raising the Israeli flag over towns in southern Syria as evidence of a [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] land grab.[3]

## FBI leak probe (March 2026)

The [[fbi|FBI]] opened an investigation into Kent over allegations that he improperly disclosed classified information, according to sources cited by Semafor; the probe had reportedly been underway for months before his resignation on Tuesday, March 17, 2026.[4] Kent went on [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s podcast the following Wednesday to give his side of the story.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that screencaps circulating of Kent's X account showed him taking a pro-war posture toward [[iran|Iran]] in January 2020, days after the assassination of General Qassem Soleimani, and contrasted that with a January 2024 clip in which Kent answered Carlson's questions about an Iran war.[4] He argued the sequence sounded less like subversion than messaging intentionally deployed by the administration, observing that Kent had made no disparaging remarks about the President and had in fact been complimentary of [[donald-trump|Trump]] despite the optics.[4] He also flagged Kent's commentary on [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]]'s murder and on the death of [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] as notable.[4]

In that reading, Kent appears to have been deployed by Trump and [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] to carry White House messaging and shift the Overton window, with his resignation letter published the day before a major [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence]] hearing functioning as "free advertising."[4] The same commentary restated Kent's claim that his wife was killed by "subversive elements in the first Trump admin" who kept US forces in [[syria|Syria]] after Trump had formally ordered a withdrawal — a grievance he attributes to the [[cia|CIA]] and which contributors treated as strong motive.[4] After the Carlson interview, Trump posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] about [[israel|Israel]]'s subversive behavior while demanding that Iran refrain from retaliating against [[qatar|Qatar]], an omission GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read as tacit encouragement to strike Israel instead.[4]

## Guidance on exiting the Iran war (2026)

In April 2026, as U.S. intelligence agencies modeled how Tehran might react to a unilateral American victory declaration, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s signal that he may declare victory and exit the war matched "the exact guidance that Joe Kent has been repeating ever since leaving the DNI and going public with his experiences."[5] He suggested Kent had been waiting for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s political opponents to formally unite and merge their parties, which occurred that Sunday, leaving Netanyahu politically compelled to keep fighting.[5]
