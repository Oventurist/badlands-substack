# Joe Kent

Joe Kent is an American combat veteran, former congressional candidate and, as of March 2026, the director of the National Counterterrorism Center and a top aide to Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]. He became the first senior official of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration to resign over the U.S. war in [[iran|Iran]].

## Resignation over the Iran war

According to the March 18, 2026 Badlands Brief, Kent submitted a strongly worded resignation letter stating that he could not in good conscience support the conflict. He declared that "Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation" and asserted that the war had been started because of pressure from [[israel|Israel]] and its powerful American lobby. Kent accused high-ranking Israeli officials and influential American media outlets of conducting a misinformation campaign that undermined Trump's "[[america-first|America First]]" foreign policy, drawing an explicit comparison with the tactics used to draw the United States into the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]]. He urged Trump to return to the non-interventionist approach of his 2016, 2020 and 2024 campaigns and of his first term.

## Military background

Kent deployed to combat eleven times. His wife, Shannon Kent, was serving in the Middle East when she was killed in an attack in Syria; Kent lays blame for her death at the feet of the [[cia|CIA]]. His deployment record was cited by [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] in Kent's defense after the resignation.

## Reaction

Trump described Kent as "a nice guy" but "weak on security." White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] called the resignation letter's claims "insulting and laughable," while figures in Trump's orbit characterized Kent as a suspected leaker and a "crazed egomaniac." Administration officials were reported to be bracing for further fallout, including a planned Kent interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].

Bannon, on whose War Room program Kent became a nationally recognized figure during the Biden administration, defended him and called for an investigation into the letter's claim that Israel controls U.S. foreign policy. General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] responded publicly as well. The Brief's contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the episode as part of a pattern in which "all Israel dissidents in the administration are being hunted down and removed from office," described [[uniparty|Uniparty]] figures as conducting "a communist struggle session against Joe Kent," and suggested Gabbard could be the next target.

## 2026 Syria disclosure

In an interview with MintPress News reported on March 27, 2026, Kent — identified as the former head of the U.S. National Counterterrorism Center, who resigned over disagreements related to U.S. policy toward [[iran|Iran]] — said the United States "worked directly with Al-Qaeda" and Islamic State militants during the Syrian conflict. He described American efforts to support opposition forces in Syria, including groups he said were linked to [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] and later [[isis|ISIS]], and characterized U.S. involvement in Syria as part of a broader pattern of military actions conducted on behalf of [[israel|Israel]], referencing the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]] as well. He said ISIS "got out of control," prompting subsequent U.S. military deployment to Syria under the stated aim of combating terrorism.

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the significance lay less in the substance — long asserted by independent researchers — than in the source: a former top intelligence official of the Trump administration serving as counterterrorism chief under the Director of National Intelligence. He connected the admission to Israeli National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]]'s public calls for the assassination of Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]], formerly known as Mohammed Abu al-Jolani, who had himself been a leader in both Al-Qaeda and ISIS. In his reading, the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government welcomed al-Sharaa's December 2024 takeover expecting him to cede southern Syrian territory, and turned against him when he aligned instead with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Turkish President Erdogan — with reports of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] raising Israeli flags over southern Syrian towns cited as evidence of the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel]] ambition proceeding regardless.
