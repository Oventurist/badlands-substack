# League for Social Reconstruction

The League of Social Reconstruction was the organizational form taken by the self-professed "Fabian Society of Canada," set up at the height of the Depression by five [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholars]] in order, according to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], to create a Canadian fascist regime in 1932.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

The League established a political party called the Cooperative Commonwealth Federation (CCF), which later changed its name to the New Democratic Party (NDP) in 1961.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Ehret argues that while many decent people have belonged to the NDP and the Liberals over the years, the lineage retains a rotten core tied to the highest echelons of the British oligarchy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) The [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] proper is presented as having worked alongside the Rhodes network for more than a century.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Role in the technocracy lineage

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes the League for Social Reconstruction as the formal name of the Fabian Society of Canada, and records that when it established the [[cooperative-commonwealth-federation|Cooperative Commonwealth Federation]] as a political party in 1933, [[joshua-haldeman|Joshua Haldeman]] was assigned to set up the Saskatchewan branch in the Assiniboia federal constituency, where he would have interfaced with some or all of the five Oxford Rhodes Scholars who founded the organization.[2] Ehret's footnote names the Rhodes Trust network behind the League as [[escott-reid|Escott Reid]], Frank Underhill, Eugene Forsey, F. R. Scott and David Lewis, with Underhill an Oxford-trained Fabian tutored by Harold Laski and G. B. Shaw at Balliol College, and states that the group's avowed intention was to institute "scientific management of society" under Fabian precepts.[2] To head the CCF the group selected J. S. Woodsworth, another Oxford-trained Fabian and an avowed eugenicist who vigorously endorsed Alberta's 1927 sterilization laws.[2] Ehret argues that the League and [[technocracy-inc|Technocracy Inc.]] were run by the same transatlantic apparatus.[2]
