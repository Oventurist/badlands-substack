# Matthias Krull

Matthias Krull is a German national and former private banker who became the central cooperating witness in the U.S. money-laundering case built around Venezuela's state oil company, [[pdvsa|PDVSA]].[1]

## Arrest and prosecution

Three months after Swiss authorities raided the Geneva office of [[helsinge|Helsinge]], the firm's Miami office was raided as well, and Krull was arrested and charged with laundering $1.2 billion that had been stolen from PDVSA.[1] In August 2018, a month after the raid, Krull pled guilty and was sentenced to ten years in prison, with the sentencing judge advising that full cooperation with authorities could earn him a reduced sentence.[1]

## Cooperation and Operation Money Flight

Krull complied, and his cooperation launched [[operation-money-flight|Operation Money Flight]], spearheaded by the Southern District of Florida's International Narcotics and Money Laundering Section in cooperation with law enforcement in London, Rome, Madrid, and Malta.[1] The stated mission of the effort was to identify, investigate, and prosecute high-level members of drug trafficking enterprises by combining federal, state, and local law enforcement capabilities.[1]

Because Krull had personally handled the laundering of funds stolen from the Venezuelan government, he was able to map the entire financial network for investigators, demonstrating how embezzled funds moved from Venezuela into the United States and Europe.[1] He helped secure plea deals from two additional defendants, and the information he provided led Swiss regulators to sanction the private bank [[julius-baer|Julius Baer]].[1] For his cooperation his ten-year sentence was cut to three and a half years — described in the Badlands Brief as the largest sentence reduction ever granted by a U.S. federal judge.[1]

## Significance

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterizes Krull as "the key to unlocking the case" — the link that connected the white-collar cartel of oil, energy, and minerals interests to the traditional cartels trafficking drugs, guns, and humans.[1] Krull's roadmap is also credited with informing the layered sequence of Venezuela sanctions imposed by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], which were crafted to permit asset forfeiture and dismantle the surrounding corruption ecosystem.[1]
