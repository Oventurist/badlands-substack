# FTX

FTX was the cryptocurrency exchange founded by [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]], whose collapse produced federal fraud and conspiracy charges against its founder.[1]

## Criminal case

Prosecutors accused Bankman-Fried of misappropriating billions of dollars from the exchange he founded; he pleaded not guilty and was released on a $250 million bail package.[1] By February 2023 the presiding federal judge in New York was weighing revocation of that bail over possible witness tampering, including the defendant's contact with a witness in the criminal case and his use of encrypted communications.[1]

In Badlands coverage, the FTX prosecution was treated alongside the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]]–[[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] litigation as part of a broader pattern of financial-elite accountability stories moving through the courts in early 2023.[1]

## Criminal proceedings following the collapse

In a May 8, 2023 filing in Manhattan federal court, founder [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] — who had long denied stealing from FTX customers — asked a judge to dismiss all but three of the criminal charges arising from the exchange's bankruptcy.[2] His lawyers argued that many cryptocurrency exchanges collapsed during the 2022 market crash and that the government had improperly turned civil and regulatory issues into federal crimes.[2] FTX had been based in the Bahamas, where Bankman-Fried lived and was arrested in December 2022 before being extradited to the United States a week later.[2] Commentators placed the reported $8 billion shortfall alongside the political donations, regulatory relationships and celebrity endorsements FTX's wealth had purchased, arguing that the fraud could not have been carried out by one man alone.[2]

## Aftermath and sentencing

On March 28, 2024, founder [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] was sentenced to 25 years in prison and $11 billion in forfeiture for the fraud and conspiracy that doomed the exchange and its related hedge fund [[alameda-research|Alameda Research]].[3] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that other FTX executives had been indicted and flipped against Bankman-Fried, and that FTX, Alameda and related entities were taken from him and entered into bankruptcy by new owners who began a process of securing restitution for victims — with further restitution expected as a consequence of the conviction, though victims would never be made completely whole.[3]
