# Bitza

Bitza was a small Venezuelan technology company that, according to the account presented in Badlands Brief: Elections are Fake; as is Trump's Friendship with Bibi, wrote the software used in [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] voting machines while 28 percent of its shares were held by the government of [[venezuela|Venezuela]].

The New York Times reported that seven months before Smartmatic was awarded its 2004 Venezuelan voting contract, a Venezuelan government financing agency invested more than $200,000 in the smaller company — owned by some of the same people as Smartmatic — which then joined Smartmatic as a minor partner in the bid. In return the agency received a 28 percent stake and a seat on the board, occupied by a senior government official who had previously advised President [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] on elections technology. Venezuelan officials later insisted the money was a small-business loan repaid before the recall referendum.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] presents this arrangement as the origin point of what she describes as foreign-written source code embedded in the lineage of American election systems.
