# Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System

The Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) is the United States' passive post-marketing surveillance database for reports of adverse events following immunization. It is co-managed by the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]], which has historically relayed VAERS tallies of post-vaccination myocarditis and pericarditis cases to its vaccine advisory panels as updates to the COVID-19 vaccines were considered.[1]

## Role in the COVID-19 vaccine myocarditis dispute

Badlands Media's coverage of a September 2023 Epoch Times report highlighted VAERS as the central data source in a controversy over agency transparency. The CDC had previously confirmed that COVID-19 vaccines can cause the inflammatory heart conditions myocarditis and pericarditis, and had regularly conveyed VAERS case counts as part of its advisory process.[1] At a meeting on September 12, 2023, however, the agency did not mention VAERS data at all.[1]

When asked for the missing figures, a CDC spokesman pointed instead to a published CDC study covering data only through October 23, 2022 — a study that identified nine reports of myocarditis or pericarditis following vaccination with the bivalent COVID-19 vaccines introduced in September 2022, seven of which were verified by medical review.[1] Pressed for more current numbers, the spokesman acknowledged that the agency possessed the data but was not making it public.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued that the withholding was unsurprising, framing it as the CDC protecting the "safe and effective" messaging around the shots while, in his view, evidence of cardiac harm accumulated.[1] He noted that many people had been "cancelled, dismissed, or otherwise ostracized" for raising myocarditis risk in connection with the vaccines, and characterized the agency's posture as "mental gymnastics and obfuscation."[1]
