# Aliquippa Municipal Authority

The Aliquippa Municipal Authority is the water utility serving Aliquippa, Pennsylvania. In November 2023 it became the best-known American victim of an intrusion into water-sector [[operational-technology|operational technology]] attributed to Iranian-affiliated cyber actors.[1]

## The November 2023 intrusion

According to AP News reporting cited in the Badlands account, "Iranian hackers shut down a remotely controlled device that monitors and regulates water pressure at a pumping station" at the Aliquippa authority.[1] Customers were not affected because crews alerted by an alarm quickly switched to manual operation — but, as the AP noted, "not every water authority has a built-in manual backup system."[1] The compromise occurred through a device in the field, illustrating the [[internet-of-things|Internet of Things]] attack surface that the subsequent congressional hearing was convened to examine.[1]

## Congressional aftermath

Subcommittee chairman [[andrew-garbarino|Andrew Garbarino]] cited the incident in his opening remarks at the February 6, 2024 [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Homeland Security Committee]] hearing, noting that "in late 2023, we saw the latest … cyber activity against OT devices and multiple sectors, including water and wastewater systems, by Iranian affiliated cyber actors," and that recent incidents required Congress to revisit how it supports [[critical-infrastructure|critical infrastructure]] owners and operators.[1]

The federal response consisted primarily of sanctions on Iran. [[charles-clancy|Charles Clancy]] of [[mitre-corporation|MITRE]] criticized that approach at the hearing, saying it "does not scale to the strategic threat that we face" and that such attacks should be treated like major natural disasters, addressed through "procedures and people" rather than "technology band aids."[1]
