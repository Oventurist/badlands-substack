# Burkina Faso

Burkina Faso is a West African state under military government, described in Badlands coverage as a focal point of the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] and of accelerating great-power energy competition in Africa.[1]

## Nuclear and energy agreements

Burkina Faso acceded to the Vienna Convention on Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage, a concrete legal step reported alongside expanding nuclear cooperation with [[russia|Russia]].[1] A Dubai-based industrial group reported development of a 200-MW thermal power plant in the country with an estimated €180 million investment.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that both Russia and the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] are establishing power plants there — Russia preparing a nuclear plant, the UAE having just completed a thermal plant — and argued the country would become a case study in how quickly a nation where only 20% of the population has electricity can be brought into the "Golden Age."[1]

## Dissolution of political parties

On January 29, 2026, Burkina Faso's military government formally dissolved all political parties by decree, ordering party assets transferred to the state and repealing laws governing party status, financing, and the role of the opposition leader.[1] The order was issued by leader Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], who justified it on the grounds that the country's political parties had become subversive.[1] Commentary noted that over 100 political parties existed in Burkina Faso while only 15 held parliamentary seats.[1]

## Damiba arrest

Former Burkinabè President [[paul-henri-sandaogo-damiba|Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba]] was arrested in Togo and returned to Burkina Faso amid junta allegations of links to a coup and an assassination plot against Traoré.[1] Togo's justice ministry stated that the charges levelled by Burkina Faso's military government included "criminal embezzlement of public funds, illicit enrichment, corruption, aggravated receiving of stolen goods and money laundering."[1]

## 2022 coup and the Sahel rebellion

Badlands commentary states that the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] has been in rebellion against the UN-affiliated [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] bloc since 2022, when Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] led a military coup in Burkina Faso against his own commander, who had overthrown the government just eight months earlier.[2] The same analysis identifies Burkina Faso's gold — alongside the uranium of [[niger|Niger]] — as a reason the country was targeted for destabilization by militant proxies allegedly financed through funds [[congress|Congress]] appropriated for [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2]

## Foiled coup attempt (January 2026)

In early January 2026, Burkina Faso's government reported that it had foiled a coup attempt linked to supporters of former leaders, saying security forces had dismantled the networks involved in planning the takeover.[3] Authorities said the alleged plot involved both military personnel and civilians and that arrests had been made, though no independent evidence or detailed identities were released publicly.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reported that the plot was run out of neighboring [[ivory-coast|Ivory Coast]] with the intent of installing former president Lt. Colonel [[paul-henri-sandaogo-damiba|Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba]].[3] He recounted that Damiba first overthrew the Burkinabe government in early 2022 with the help of Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traore]] when the military judged the government complicit or indifferent to an ISIS advance, and that Traore led his own coup against Damiba nine months later after concluding Damiba was not organizing the counterinsurgency.[3] Damiba has since been accused repeatedly of plotting against Traore while allegedly operating from Ivorian territory.[3]

In this framing, which Badlands calls "Conflict A: The War for Sovereignty" in West Africa, [[nigeria|Nigeria]] and Ivory Coast are the two centers of power in [[ecowas|ECOWAS]], with Nigeria the political and economic center and Ivory Coast a paramilitary proxy hub used to launch insurgency operations against the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]].[3] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described Traore as the greatest figure to emerge from the African theater, calling him "the George Washington of Africa."[3]

## Russian nuclear power agreement

Burkina Faso's military leaders signed a deal with [[russia|Russia]] to build a nuclear power plant in order to increase electricity supplies — the junta's latest move to align with Moscow after falling out with most of its Western partners.[4] The country is among the least electrified in the world, with only 21% of the population connected to power, and the agreement was framed as part of a target of 95% electricity access in urban areas and 50% in rural areas by 2030.[4] The deal followed talks that military ruler Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] held with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in July at the Russia–Africa summit in Moscow, where Traoré said, "We have a critical need for energy... our position is rather strategic because we are in the heart of West Africa and we have an energy deficit in the sub-region."[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the coverage as inadvertently damning of the West, asking whether the "falling out" with Western partners might be connected to 80% of Burkinabe lacking electricity despite centuries of French imperial presence.[4] He noted parallel Russian nuclear projects — a $30 billion plant in Egypt agreed with President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah el-Sisi]] and Putin in 2017, a Nigerian agreement the same year that had not begun, Kenya's plan for a first plant by 2027, and Rwanda's September selection of Canadian-German firm Dual Fluid Energy — and argued that Western interest in African nuclear power materialized only once Russia moved first.[4]

## Defence of Niger, 2023

Under military rule itself and already suspended from [[ecowas|ECOWAS]], Burkina Faso joined [[mali|Mali]] on Monday, July 31, 2023, in declaring that an attack on [[niger|Niger]] would be an attack on them both, stating that "any military intervention against Niger would be tantamount to a declaration of war against Burkina Faso and Mali."[5] The warning came after ECOWAS threatened force to restore deposed President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]] in the wake of the [[2023-niger-coup|2023 Niger coup]], and a day before [[france|France]] began evacuating its citizens from the country.[5]

## 2024 condemnation of Ukraine over the Mali attack

In August 2024 the Foreign Ministry of Burkina Faso publicly called out Ukraine for praising a terrorist attack on Malian servicemen and Russian [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] contractors the previous month, saying Kiev should not set itself against a global community that unanimously condemns terrorism.[6] The convoy had been ambushed in northern [[mali|Mali]] in late July, killing dozens, after which [[main-directorate-of-intelligence|GUR]] spokesman [[andrey-yusov|Andrey Yusov]] said Ukrainian agents helped plan the operation and Ukrainian ambassador [[yury-pivovarov|Yury Pivovarov]] amplified the claim from Dakar.[6] Burkina Faso, itself plagued by insurgents, viewed those words as justification of terrorism and urged the international community to hold Ukraine to account for "subversive actions that threaten the stability of the continent," adding that Ukraine should stop interfering in other states' internal affairs and "come to its senses."[6]

## Traoré-era economic policy

Badlands commentary described [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], who took power in 2022 with Wagner's assistance and became at 34 the world's youngest national leader, as aligning Burkina Faso with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] against [[nato|NATO]] and Western financial interests.[6] In early August 2024 Traoré announced that the country's gold would no longer be exported for processing but retained domestically to back the national currency, following Russia's example; in May he had cut minister and politician salaries by 30% while raising ordinary workers' pay by 50%, and he takes no salary himself.[6]

## 2025 INSO espionage case

In October 2025 Burkina Faso's government arrested eight workers for the Netherlands-based [[international-ngo-safety-organisation|International NGO Safety Organisation]] (INSO) on charges of spying.[7] The organization's operations had already been suspended on July 31, 2025 for allegedly collecting sensitive data without authorization.[7]

At a press briefing on Tuesday, October 7, 2025, Security Minister [[mahamadou-sana|Mahamadou Sana]] said authorities had monitored the organization for some time and, thanks to the "contribution of citizens," had determined that the NGO "constituted an espionage network in Burkina Faso."[7] Sana stated that despite the earlier suspension, "some officials continued to carry out clandestine or disguised activities such as gathering information and holding meetings in person or online."[7] Those arrested comprised four foreign nationals — two French citizens, one Czech and one Malian — and four Burkinabè citizens; INSO had operated in the Sahel country since 2019.[7]

[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the case as another Western regime-change effort, writing that "the [[state-department|State Department]] ([[cia|CIA]]) has been busy, trying to overthrow yet another troublesome government."[7] The commentary suggested the motive lay in Burkina Faso's newly signed nuclear deal with Russia to construct a nuclear power plant, noting that 80 percent of the country's citizens lack access to electricity.[7] It also condemned [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and the [[republican-party|Republican Party]] for defending such intelligence networks as necessary to preserve American superpower status.[7]

## Russian military cooperation talks (September 2023)

A Russian delegation led by Deputy Defence Minister [[yunus-bek-yevkurov|Yunus-Bek Yevkurov]] travelled to Burkina Faso in late August 2023 to meet the country's military leader, [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], and discuss possible military cooperation, the Burkinabè presidency announced.[8] According to Ouagadougou, the meeting followed talks between Traoré and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] on the sidelines of the Russia-Africa summit in St. Petersburg in July 2023, and addressed "areas of cooperation that mainly concern the military sphere, including the training of Burkinabe cadet officers and officers at all levels, including pilots in Russia."[8] Ties with Moscow had deepened since the September 2022 coup that brought Traoré to power against Lieutenant Colonel [[paul-henri-sandaogo-damiba|Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba]], who had himself ousted President Roch Marc Christian Kaboré in January of that year.[8] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the visit as part of a Russian sweep through the Sahel — Bamako, then Ouagadougou, then Bangui in the [[central-african-republic|Central African Republic]] — as Western credibility in West Africa eroded.[8]
