# Hrant Bagratyan

**Hrant Bagratyan** is an Armenian politician and former Prime Minister of [[armenia|Armenia]] who, while serving as a member of parliament, publicly asserted that Armenia possesses nuclear weapons.[1] His statements form a central evidentiary pillar of the Badlands Media argument that Armenia is an unacknowledged "rogue nuclear state."[1]

## The 2016 nuclear claim

At a press conference in Armenia on April 29, Bagratyan told journalists, "We have the ability to create nuclear weapons," adding, "We have nuclear weapons."[1] A recording of the remarks was later released by the Media Center, an Armenian NGO.[1] The press conference followed an earlier speech to parliament in which Bagratyan urged the creation of nuclear weapons to prevent "further attacks and aggression" from neighboring Turkey and [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]].[1]

The remarks came in response to a recent escalation of the Karabakh conflict that killed hundreds of soldiers and civilians, and the Jerusalem Post commentary cited in the article acknowledges they could be dismissed as angry rhetoric.[1] That same commentary nevertheless asks why so few news sources, particularly outside the immediate region, reported the statements at all, arguing the world should not be so eager to let them slip.[1]

## Context

The article treats the silence surrounding Bagratyan's claim as part of a broader pattern: while Iran and North Korea absorbed the world's non-proliferation attention, Armenia — described by the Jerusalem Post as "[[iran|Iran]]'s neighbor and long-time regional buddy" — went unexamined.[1] Days before Bagratyan spoke, on April 18, the Georgian State Security Service arrested three Armenian nationals and three Georgian citizens for attempting to illegally sell roughly $200 million of weapons-grade uranium 238.[1] The author argues that a former head of government announcing possession of nuclear weapons, with no subsequent investigation or diplomatic consequence, is explicable only if the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] wanted Armenia to remain under the radar.[1]
