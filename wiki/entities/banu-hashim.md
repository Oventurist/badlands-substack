# Banu Hashim

The Banu Hashim — *banu* meaning clan — are the descendants of [[hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]] and [[prophet-muhammad|Muhammad]], known in English as the Hashemites. The Badlands Media essay [[arabian-nights]] introduces them as a dynasty that will be central to its promised sequel on the First World War.

## Position in Arabian society

The Banu Hashim were a clan within the tribe of [[quraysh]], which held Mecca. The source notes that [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] Muslims extend the phrase *Ahl al-Bayt*, "People of the House [of the Prophet]," to all members of the Banu Hashim, whereas [[shia-islam|Shia]] Muslims restrict it to Muhammad's cousin, daughter, son-in-law, two grandsons and eleven wives. Most caliphs of Islam have historically been either *sharifs* — descendants of Muhammad — or members of the Banu Hashim; the source emphasises that the modern "throne" of the Muslim world, held by [[saudi-arabia]], belongs to neither.

## Arabia and Hejaz

The essay identifies Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Syria as the geographic area the Hashemites regarded as the true Arabia, and for which they sought sovereignty from both the [[ottoman-empire]] and the British Crown during the First World War. The 1916 Arab Revolt was led by King Hussein and his son Prince Faisal, whose definition of Arabia the author uses in constructing his own map of the region. Today, the source notes, the Hashemites control only Jordan.

The Hashemite kingdom of Hejaz, under Ottoman control since the sixteenth century, was the target of the 1814 campaign by Abdullah, successor to [[mohammed-bin-saud]], which ended in Abdullah's capture and public beheading in Istanbul and the collapse of the [[emirate-of-diriyah]]. The promised sequel is to treat what the author calls the British Cabal's betrayal of the Hashemites and the usurpation of their stewardship over the Islamic holy lands by Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman Al Saud, acting through [[british-intelligence]].
