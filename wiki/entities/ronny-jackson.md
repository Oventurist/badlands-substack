# Ronny Jackson

Ronny Jackson is a Republican member of the United States [[house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] from Texas and a former [[white-house|White House]] physician, a background that has made him a leading congressional voice questioning the cognitive fitness of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

## Letter demanding a Biden cognitive test

In May 2023, Jackson led a letter signed by 61 House Republicans demanding that Biden either submit to a mental competency test or withdraw from the 2024 presidential race, framing the request as a matter of public accountability rather than partisanship.[1] The letter stated that "regardless of gender, age, or political party, all Presidents should document and demonstrate sound mental abilities," and noted that while Biden had undergone physical examinations on November 19, 2021 and February 16, 2023, there was "no indication you have had any cognitive assessment, or if you have, such results were concealed from the public."[1] The February 2023 physical, conducted by White House physician [[kevin-oconnor|Kevin O'Connor]], had described Biden as a "healthy, vigorous, 80-year-old male" fit to execute the duties of the presidency, but Jackson and his co-signers argued that the exam explicitly excluded evaluation of cognitive and mental abilities.[1]

Jackson framed the proposed test in explicitly political terms, writing that failure of such a test "will allow you to come to terms with the many failures of your administration over the past two years and allow a mentally fit leader to emerge."[1] The May 2023 letter followed earlier letters Jackson had sent to the president in June 2021, February 2022 and July 2022 raising similar concerns.[1] The letter was sent after Biden announced his reelection campaign on April 25, 2023.[1]

## Commentary and reception

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the letter as evidence of insufficient Republican urgency rather than as a decisive action, observing that only about 27 percent of the 222 House Republicans — roughly 60 of them — had signed on, and asking where the remaining members were on invoking the [[twenty-fifth-amendment|25th Amendment]] or filing articles of impeachment.[1] The commentary quoted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s January 12, 2021 remark that "the 25th Amendment is of zero risk to me but will come back to haunt Joe Biden and the Biden administration," and argued that Jackson's framing appeared "overtly political" while the underlying concern was widely shared.[1]
