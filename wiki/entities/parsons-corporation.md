# Parsons Corporation

The Parsons company is the American engineering firm credited in Badlands Media commentary with sponsoring the [[north-american-water-and-power-alliance|North American Water and Power Alliance]] (NAWAPA), the continental water and power scheme that emerged in the early 1960s and was endorsed by leading politicians of both parties, including [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert Kennedy]].[1]

## The 1964 feasibility studies

Parsons engineers conducted the 1964 feasibility studies that defined the NAWAPA design and presented it to the public in a fifteen-minute promotional film.[1] The studies proposed the construction of 95 dams, 8 lifts, 39 major tunnels and 4,515 miles (7,266 km) of canals to collect and move between 10 and 20 percent of the fresh water runoff of northern Alaska and Canada into the southwestern United States and northern Mexico.[1] Imagery from the Parsons film illustrating the northern collection zones and the U.S. distribution zones remains the standard visual reference for the concept.[1]

The Parsons plan foresaw water being pumped up from the Susitna, Copper, Yukon and Taku rivers into the Stikine reservoir 2,400 feet above sea level, then into the [[rocky-mountain-trench|Rocky Mountain Trench]], and finally lifted over the Sawtooth lifts in Idaho and Oregon before transiting Utah, Nevada, California, Arizona, Texas and northern Mexico by canal and tunnel.[1] The scheme became federal legislation by 1965 before drifting into obscurity amid the Vietnam War.[1]
