# Gaza Humanitarian Foundation

The Gaza Humanitarian Foundation (GHF) is a US- and Israeli-backed initiative created to feed Gazans through a network of distribution hubs operating outside the established United Nations relief system.[1]

## Troubled launch

The foundation struggled during its first week of operations in late May and early June 2025, marked by the resignation of two top executives, allegations that the Israeli military had shot into crowds of civilians rushing to pick up aid packages, and the continuing refusal of the United Nations and its humanitarian partners to join the effort.[1]

Its executive director, Jake Woods, resigned days before the program was set to launch, stating that "it is clear that it is not possible to implement this plan while also strictly adhering to the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence."[1]

## Relationship with Boston Consulting Group

[[boston-consulting-group|Boston Consulting Group]] was hired in the autumn of 2024 to help design the program and run its business operations, and in October 2024 agreed to provide what it described as pro bono support to establish the organisation.[1] BCG developed the initiative in close coordination with Israel and set the prices for paying and equipping the contractors who built four distribution hubs in southern Gaza.[1]

BCG withdrew its team from Tel Aviv, terminated its contract with GHF, and placed the senior partner leading the project on administrative leave pending an internal review; the firm said unapproved follow-on work relating to Gaza had been stopped on May 30.[1] Three people connected to both organisations said anonymously that it would be difficult for the foundation to continue to function without the consultants who helped create it.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the episode as "a dumpster fire," arguing that the contract termination and partner suspension implied possible legal or criminal liability, and pointing to the contradiction between BCG's pro bono characterisation and reports of million-dollar invoices.[1]

## Suspension of distribution, June 2025

On Wednesday, June 4, 2025, the foundation announced the temporary closure of its aid sites in the Gaza Strip, with the Israeli army warning that roads leading to the distribution centres were "considered combat zones."[2] The announcement followed a string of deadly incidents near the sites it operates, which had drawn condemnation from the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[2] Israeli bombardment the same day killed at least 16 people in Gaza, including 12 in a single strike on a tent housing displaced people, according to the Palestinian territory's civil defence agency, and on the preceding Tuesday 27 people were killed in southern Gaza when Israeli troops opened fire near a GHF aid site, an incident the military said was under investigation.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] remarked "well that lasted long," arguing that setting aside the corruption allegations, aid was no longer being delivered into Gaza and the NGO had wasted untold sums promoting itself as a savior — and that, given the storylines around it, the organisation may merely have been used to launder and steal money.[2] He predicted the humanitarian crisis would continue, further increasing the likelihood of real intervention by a [[donald-trump|Trump]]/[[vladimir-putin|Putin]]-led coalition, and noted the concurrent return of [[greta-thunberg|Greta Thunberg]] to the public sphere aboard a Gaza-bound boat.[2]
