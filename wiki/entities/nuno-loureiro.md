# Nuno F. G. Loureiro

Nuno F. G. Loureiro was a physics professor at the [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|Massachusetts Institute of Technology]] who was fatally shot at his home in Brookline, Massachusetts, on Monday, December 15, 2025.[1]

## Killing

Leah Foley, the US Attorney for the District of Massachusetts, said that the man believed to have killed Loureiro was [[claudio-manuel-neves-valente|Claudio Manuel Neves Valente]], the same suspect sought in the [[brown-university-shooting|Brown University shooting]] days earlier.[1] Valente was found dead by suicide in a storage facility in Salem, New Hampshire, on the evening of Thursday, December 18, 2025, with firearms beside him.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Because Loureiro's field placed him within plasma and fusion research, Badlands contributors treated his death as "fusion-adjacent" and linked it thematically to the same day's announcement of the [[trump-media-and-technology-group|Trump Media]]–[[tae-technologies|TAE Technologies]] fusion merger.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that closing the Brown case with a dead suspect also conveniently closed the professor's killing, and — with heavy irony — urged readers not to ask questions about the late professor's research or about who benefited most from his death.[1]

## Investigation and Iranian-connection speculation

By Wednesday, December 17, 2025, authorities had ramped up the search for Loureiro's killer but had disclosed no motive, and no suspects were in custody, according to the Norfolk District Attorney's Office.[2] An upstairs neighbor, Louise Cohen, recalled lighting a menorah candle on Monday night when three loud gunshots came from the floor below; investigating the noise, she found Loureiro lying on his back in the apartment building's entrance and called 911.[2] Loureiro's wife was present during the incident. "I can't sleep now," Cohen told The Boston Globe. "This family is so amazing. I can't imagine anyone wanting to kill him."[2]

Israeli officials were reported to be examining intelligence from recent days suggesting an Iranian connection to the killing — an assessment that had not been verified and was not supported at that stage by official findings from US investigative authorities.[2] The Jerusalem Post noted that Loureiro, Portuguese-born and 47, had previously spoken out in favor of Israel.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the Iran theory as unsupported internet speculation propagated by "a certain lobby," pointing to hedge fund manager [[bill-ackman|Bill Ackman]]'s deleted post asking "Did the Iranians kill one of our nuclear scientists?"[2]

## Research and its stakes

Hired by [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|MIT]] in 2016, Loureiro led the university's [[plasma-science-and-fusion-center|Plasma Science and Fusion Center]] — a laboratory of more than 250 researchers across seven buildings — from 2024.[2] A leading theoretical physicist specializing in plasma physics with a focus on magnetic confinement fusion, his work advanced understanding of plasma turbulence, instabilities, magnetic reconnection, and confinement in fusion devices, research described as essential to clean, effectively limitless energy that produces no carbon emissions, avoids meltdown risk, and generates minimal long-lived waste.[2] Ashe in America argued that such a technology "sounds like a massive threat to a lot of interests."[2]
