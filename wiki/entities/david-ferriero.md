# David Ferriero

**David S. Ferriero** served as the 10th [[archivist-of-the-united-states|Archivist of the United States]]. He was nominated by President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] on July 28, 2009, confirmed by the U.S. Senate on November 6, 2009, and sworn into office on November 13, 2009.[1] He was the first librarian to lead the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]].[1]

Obama also appointed Ferriero to simultaneously head the newly created [[national-declassification-center|National Declassification Center]], which had been given four years to review 400 million pages of federal documents that remained top secret, some dating to World War I.[1] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that this dual appointment gave Ferriero access not only to Obama's own classified documents but to hundreds of millions of top secret records spanning multiple administrations, making him a uniquely valuable resource or witness for any future criminal investigation.[1]

## Transparency mission

Ferriero described his post as a "little-known job" that placed him at the helm of the United States' 10-billion-item trove of documents and at the forefront of efforts to make the U.S. government as transparent as possible to its citizens.[1] "One of our missions is to ensure people of the United States have access to the records," he said.[1] He characterized his responsibilities as an oversight role extending beyond the White House to the government in general, saying he needed to make sure each agency was doing what it was supposed to be doing in the area of records.[1]

His tenure followed a difficult period for the National Archives: the agency had fought the [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] administration over access to records following the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks and was accused by The New York Times of needing "spine-stiffening" in its dealings with the White House.[1] In 2003, [[sandy-berger|Sandy Berger]], former national security adviser to President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]], removed classified memos on terrorist plots from the National Archives and destroyed them, later pleading guilty to unauthorized removal of classified documents.[1]

## Role in the Badlands narrative

Ferriero remained Archivist throughout the entire [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration before retiring in April 2022.[1] Lange contends that Trump's decision to leave him in place is evidence Ferriero chose "the right side" of what Lange frames as an ongoing information war, and that Ferriero forms part of a sting operation designed to entrap presidents of the past rather than to catch Trump.[1]

As supporting evidence, Lange cites a 2020 Washington Post report that the National Archives altered a large photograph of the January 21, 2017 Women's March displayed in an exhibit celebrating the centennial of women's suffrage, blurring signs critical of Trump and words referencing women's anatomy — a placard reading "God Hates Trump" was edited to read "God Hates," and "Trump & GOP — Hands Off Women" had "Trump" blurred out.[1] The Archives said Ferriero participated in talks regarding the exhibit and supported the decision to edit the photo.[1] Lange treats this as a signal that Ferriero was not acting on Obama's behalf.[1]
