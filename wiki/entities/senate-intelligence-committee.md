# Senate Intelligence Committee

The **U.S. Senate Select Committee on Intelligence** oversees the American intelligence community and receives some of the most closely held classified material handled by [[congress|Congress]]. In May 2026 it became the focus of an [[fbi|FBI]] leak investigation.[1]

## 2026 FBI leak investigation

According to a May 7, 2026 report by Just the News citing unnamed sources, the FBI opened an investigation into possible leaks of classified information involving Democrats or staff members on the committee.[1] The inquiry was prompted in part by a criminal referral from the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] concerning disclosure of information from one of its overseas intelligence intercepts.[1]

The referral stemmed from media coverage published during Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s confirmation process that referenced an intercepted conversation between two [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] figures discussing Gabbard's 2017 visit to Syria.[1] The NSA concluded the published information accurately reflected the intercept's contents but found that Gabbard had not met Hezbollah leaders.[1] The agency identified potential leakers among Democratic staff members on the committee who had access to the intercept before publication.[1] The referral reportedly sat pending for months until FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] became aware of it, after which FBI counterintelligence and criminal investigators broadened the inquiry to examine other possible leaks and contacts with reporters.[1]

## Prior leak cases

The reporting also referenced earlier leak investigations touching the committee, including the 2018 case in which former committee security director [[james-wolfe|James Wolfe]] pleaded guilty to lying to the FBI about contacts with a reporter.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized the framing that the probe would center on rogue staffers leaking NSA cables, calling it "silly" and suggesting it read as plausible deniability for the committee's members.[1] She noted that the Speech and Debate Clause protects members for legitimate legislative activity but not, in theory, for bribery, corruption, fraud, espionage, unauthorized disclosure of classified information, conspiracy or obstruction — several of which leaks aimed at undermining the President's ODNI pick could implicate.[1] The long dormancy of the referral, she argued, implied conspiracy and obstruction were on the table and looped the executive branch into an apparent legislative conspiracy, closing with the observation that [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] sits on the committee.[1]

## Clayton confirmation hearing (July 2026)

On Wednesday, July 15, 2026, the committee questioned President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee for Director of National Intelligence, [[jay-clayton|Walter "Jay" Clayton III]], with Democratic lawmakers repeatedly pressing him on the 2020 presidential election; Clayton acknowledged the election was certified and that Joe Biden was certified as president while rejecting the "election denier" label.[2] Senators also examined his authorization, as US Attorney for the Southern District of New York, of subpoenas targeting *New York Times* reporters in a leak investigation, and asked about election-related investigations involving former DNI [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]].[2]

## March 2026 annual threats hearing

On Wednesday, March 18, 2026, the committee held its annual hearing on national security and foreign policy, with testimony from Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and other intelligence chiefs.[3] The proceeding was dominated by the previous day's departure of [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]] Director [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]].[3] Senators pressed the panel on US policy toward [[iran|Iran]], intelligence assessments underpinning it, US military posture in the Middle East, escalation risks and involvement in other foreign conflicts, and the panel went into a classified session after the public portion concluded.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the questioning as slanderous nonsense staged for outrage cycles and campaign sound bites, praising the panelists as calm and patient.[3] She singled out an exchange in which Senator [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]] (D-CO), a candidate for governor of Colorado, clashed with Ratcliffe and asked the chairman to make the director stop talking.[3] A separate Badlands item noted that a senator at the hearing invoked how angry "the People" are, which Ashe read as lawmakers invoking a popular mandate to justify their own conduct.[3]
