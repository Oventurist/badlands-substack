# Power Forward Communities

Power Forward Communities is a nonprofit organization that received a $2 billion federal award for residential decarbonization projects under Biden-era green energy programs funded by the [[inflation-reduction-act|Inflation Reduction Act]].[1]

## EPA scrutiny

The award became a focal point of criminal referrals issued in 2026 by [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] to the agency's inspector general and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], which alleged self-dealing in the distribution of the grants.[1] Reporting cited by the referrals noted that Power Forward Communities is tied to [[stacey-abrams|Stacey Abrams]] and had reported only $100 in revenue in the year before receiving the $2 billion grant.[1]

The Power Forward Communities grant was among roughly $29 billion in green energy awards that Zeldin canceled or paused, a total that also included $20 billion from the [[greenhouse-gas-reduction-fund|Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund]] routed through eight nonprofit "cutouts."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that programs of this type functioned less as climate policy than as a mechanism for routing large flows of public capital through networks of aligned nonprofits and politically connected entities, part of what he calls the Scarcity Paradigm.[1]

### The $2 billion EPA award
In February 2025 the Environmental Protection Agency, under Administrator Lee Zeldin, announced its discovery that the Biden administration had awarded $2 billion to Power Forward Communities, described as a nonprofit partnered with multiple left-wing groups founded by Stacey Abrams, who said she was "thrilled" to be part of it.[2] The funds had been set aside at Citibank, an outside financial institution, before Biden left office, and were part of a larger $20 billion pot the EPA received through the Inflation Reduction Act to distribute to climate groups.[2] Zeldin noted that the organization reported just $100 in revenue in its 2023 tax filings — "that's 20 million times the organization's reported revenue."[2] Badlands commentary described the group as a vast network of state, local, tribal and federal governments, nonprofits, corporate partners and other entities, and reported that the $2 billion was discovered by DOGE.[2]
