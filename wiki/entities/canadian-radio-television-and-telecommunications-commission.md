# Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission

The Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC) is [[canada|Canada]]'s broadcasting and telecommunications regulator, whose authority was extended over online streaming platforms by the [[online-streaming-act|Online Streaming Act]] (Bill C-11).[1]

C-11 amended Canada's Broadcasting Act to bring services such as Netflix, YouTube and Spotify under the CRTC's regulatory purview.[1] The bill was introduced in 2022 by the Liberal government under [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] and is now in force.[1]

Critics argue that the statute grants the CRTC excessive power to dictate which content is prioritized online, and that the requirement for streaming platforms and social media services to promote "Canadian content" (CanCon) amounts to government overreach capable of limiting user choice and suppressing diverse voices.[1] Complaints focus on unclear definitions — of "Canadian content" and "online undertaking" — and on the breadth of the commission's discretion, which invites inconsistent or excessive regulation and leaves platforms and creators in uncertainty.[1] The CRTC's own process to define CanCon began with consultations in May 2023, with a draft due in mid-2025, leaving open questions such as whether a creator must be Canadian-born or whether filming in Canada suffices — vagueness that Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued can be weaponized.[1]

DeLarme characterized C-11 as a regulatory mess designed, behind window dressing and jargon, as a pretense for state-sponsored censorship, and grouped it with the UK's Online Safety Act and the EU's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] as fronts in the same [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship]] campaign.[1]
