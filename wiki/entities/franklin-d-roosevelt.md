# Franklin D. Roosevelt

Franklin Delano Roosevelt was the 32nd President of the United States, serving from 1933 until his death in 1945. In the Badlands Media article [[a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation|A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] invokes Roosevelt as the author of the political and economic vision he believes the world should return to.

## The Four Freedoms and the post-war design

The article centres on Roosevelt's [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]] of 1941 — freedom from want, freedom from fear of war, freedom of speech and freedom of conscience — describing them as universal and as the backbone of the original [[atlantic-charter|Atlantic Charter]], the [[un-charter|UN Charter]], and the original design of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]]. Ehret writes that the [[united-nations|United Nations]] as envisioned by Roosevelt or [[henry-wallace|Henry Wallace]] "was also never meant to be a world government, but rather a platform for dialogue and harmonization of security and economic interests among civilizations," and links to material on Roosevelt's anti-colonial vision for the post-war world.

The article's closing irony is that Roosevelt's Four Freedoms are, in the author's judgment, now being advanced by the Eurasian powers of the [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]] rather than by the United States.

## The rejection of fascism in the 1930s

The essay [[a-re-alignment-of-world-systems|A Re-Alignment of World Systems]] argues that the plan to convert a deliberately engineered financial collapse into a fascist restructuring "failed 80 years ago due to the American rejection of fascism under President Roosevelt," referring to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s earlier piece "How to Crush a Bankers' Dictatorship: How the Great Reset of 1933 Was Thwarted by FDR." The article presents the [[new-deal|New Deal]] — its protectionism, bank regulation, state credit and central authority — as the model of a policy that empowers sovereign nations, in contrast to the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] framework it criticizes. The [[glass-steagall-act|Glass-Steagall Act]] of 1933 is described as the bedrock of financial regulation established in this period.
