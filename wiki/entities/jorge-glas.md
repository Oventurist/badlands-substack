# Jorge Glas

Jorge Glas is a former Vice-President of [[ecuador|Ecuador]] whose arrest inside
the Mexican embassy in Quito in April 2024 triggered
[[mexico|Mexico]]'s severing of diplomatic relations with Ecuador.[1]

## Career and convictions

Glas served as Ecuador's vice-president between 2013 and 2017 and was relieved
of his duties amid mounting corruption allegations.[1] Later in 2017 he was
sentenced to six years in jail in connection with corruption at the Brazilian
construction giant Odebrecht, with prosecutors saying he took $13.5 million
(£10.2 million) in bribes.[1] A separate eight-year prison sentence followed in
2020 for using money from contractors to finance campaigns.[1] Although
Ecuador's legislature declined on 21 December 2023 to authorize a trial over
the Manabí case, the attorney general's office said it was insisting on
charging him.[1]

## Asylum and embassy raid

Glas was released from prison in November 2023, but Ecuadorian authorities
issued a further arrest warrant alleging additional corruption, prompting him
to seek refuge in the Mexican embassy in Quito the following month.[1] On
Friday, 5 April 2024, Mexico announced it had granted him political asylum
"after a thorough analysis" of the situation — an action Ecuador viewed as
illegal.[1] Ecuadorian police then stormed the embassy and arrested him;
Mexican President [[andres-manuel-lopez-obrador|Andrés Manuel López Obrador]]
said they had "forcibly entered" the mission in a "flagrant violation of
international law."[1] Glas's lawyer maintained that he was innocent.[1] He was
flown under police guard to Guayaquil and was expected to await trial in a
maximum security prison.[1] Ecuador's Presidency responded that "Ecuador is a
sovereign nation and we are not going to allow any criminal to stay free."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she did not
know whether Glas was "a corrupt scumbag, a political prisoner, or (likely) a
bit of both," but that the justice-department playbook on display was
familiar, given that the Biden administration had been training Ecuadorian
police and prosecutors under President
[[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]].[1]
