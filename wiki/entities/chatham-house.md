# Chatham House

Chatham House, the Royal Institute of International Affairs, is a British foreign-policy think tank frequently cited in legacy media analysis of international crises.[1]

## Role in Iran protest coverage

Sanam Vakil, director of Chatham House's Middle East and North Africa program, was quoted by NBC News on the [[2026-iran-protests|2026 Iran protests]], arguing that Iranians had united in the face of external bombardment without abandoning their grievances against the regime: "Did [Iranians] coming together mean that they suddenly abandoned all of their contentious feelings towards the regime or its leadership? Absolutely not. They're not mutually exclusive feelings."[1] She added that Iranians remain united amid challenges "from their governance system and their leadership that isn't willing to reform or change."[1]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] flagged Chatham House's prominence with suspicion, noting that the institution appeared in the Iranian "color revolution" article at the same time analyst Susan Kokinda was describing British imperial influence losing control in the face of [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] moves — "Hey! Chatham House is also cited in that Iranian color revolution article. Weird."[1]

## Ehret's account of Chatham House and the Round Table

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Chatham House as "the queen of all think tanks," whose American branch was set up in 1921 as the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], and notes that [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] was made its president.[2] In his account the institution sits at the head of an international web of [[round-table-movement|Round Table]] think tanks that coordinated several generations of Oxford [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholars]] and followed [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]]'s mandate to create a "church of the British Empire" and recapture the American colonies that declared independence in 1776.[2]

Ehret further names Farhana Yamin and Sam Gaell of Chatham House among the British intelligence figures he says managed the [[extinction-rebellion|Extinction Rebellion]] movement, describing Chatham House as the controlling institution behind the New York-based Council on Foreign Relations overseen by Carney.[2]
