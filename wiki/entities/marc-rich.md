# Marc Rich

Marc Rich, born Marcel Reich, was a commodities trader and international financier whom the article places at the center of a decades-long scheme in which economic [[sanctions|sanctions]] functioned not as punishment but as a profit-generating and money-laundering mechanism for the [[cia|CIA]] and what the author calls the shadow government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Indictment and background

Citing The American Liberty Report, the article describes Rich as a notorious commodities trader convicted of tax fraud in the United States in the wake of oil purchases from [[iran|Iran]] during the 1979 American hostage crisis.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) He was indicted by the Department of Justice in 1983 for tax evasion, racketeering and wire fraud, and was described as the biggest tax evader in American history at the time.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) He owed nearly $50 million in back taxes, fled the country, and was placed on the FBI's ten most wanted list.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Sanctions-busting business model

The article argues that sanctions were the engine of Rich's fortune: by limiting his competition and leaving desperate dictators with no other buyers, sanctions allowed him to purchase oil far below market price and resell it far above market value to other sanctioned states, pocketing the difference and quickly becoming a billionaire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The signature example given is his 1979 purchase of Iranian oil during the hostage-crisis embargo and its onward sale to apartheid-era [[south-africa|South Africa]], itself under sanctions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The American Liberty Report is quoted describing Rich's career of negotiating metals and commodities deals for "vilified dictators and despots," including Libya's Muammar Gadhafi, North Korea's Kim Il Sung, Yugoslavia's Slobodan Milosevic and the Philippines' Ferdinand Marcos.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Washington Post similarly lists his counterparties as a compendium of rogue states: Iran during the hostage crisis, apartheid-era South Africa, Milosevic's Yugoslavia, [[north-korea|North Korea]], Gaddafi's Libya and the Soviet Union under Leonid Brezhnev.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Citing ForeignPolicy.com, the article notes that Rich was an "inveterate sanctions-buster" who used offshore front companies and corporate cutouts to stay below the radar, and that he pioneered the practice of commodity swaps — specifically the uranium-for-oil deals he brokered in the 1980s between apartheid South Africa, Islamic Iran and Soviet Russia.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The author uses this to ask how Iran and North Korea obtained uranium for their nuclear weapons programs, answering that the CIA and shadow government stood behind the nuclear programs of these "rogue states."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Zug, Switzerland

The Washington Post is quoted describing Rich as "the uncrowned king of Zug," the Swiss lakeside town whose stringent privacy laws, low tax rates and guarantees of corporate anonymity host roughly 10,000 international companies — about one corporation for every two residents.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Rich operated from Zug for a quarter-century, seventeen of those years hiding from U.S. marshals, perfecting there the practices cited in his 1983 indictment: trading with pariah states, manipulating markets for personal gain, and hiding profits in a thicket of offshore companies.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Former U.S. marshal Howard Safir, who waited outside Rich's Swiss residence in 1985 in one of several futile arrest attempts, said Rich "sees himself as a citizen of the world, unencumbered by the laws of sovereign nations" and believed "everything and everyone can be bought and sold, and government is irrelevant."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The article asserts that repeated arrest attempts failed because someone in the [[state-department|State Department]] always tipped Rich off, which it treats as unsurprising given his deep ties to the CIA and Mossad — ties that made him an "untouchable."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Switzerland is characterized as a hub of corruption protecting such figures through banking privacy laws and front companies.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## The Clinton pardon

Rich received a presidential pardon from [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] on Clinton's last day in office, January 20, 2001, one of 140 pardons and 36 commutations issued that day.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article argues the volume of pardons was a deliberate distraction meant to reduce scrutiny of this single controversial act, which nonetheless triggered the [[pardongate|Pardongate]] scandal and congressional and criminal investigations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The author concludes the pardon was about one thing — money — and speculates that Rich helped the Clintons sell Iranian oil to North Korea for a large profit under the 1994 [[agreed-framework|Agreed Framework]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Associates tied to the Clintons

Rich died in 2013, but the New York Post is quoted reporting that his business partners, lawyers, advisers and friends showered millions of dollars on the Clintons in the decade and a half after the pardon scandal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Two are singled out: Nigerian businessman [[gilbert-chagoury|Gilbert Chagoury]], a close ally with whom Rich sold oil on international markets, and Russian investor [[sergei-kurzin|Sergei Kurzin]], who worked for Rich in the 1990s scouting investments across the former Soviet Union and later participated in the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article treats Rich's South African commodity-trading ties and uranium-for-oil expertise as no coincidence given that [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] was originally a South African company.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
