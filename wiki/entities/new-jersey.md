# New Jersey

**New Jersey** is a U.S. state whose enforcement actions against [[crisis-pregnancy-centers|crisis pregnancy centers]] produced a 2026 [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruling on access to federal courts.[1]

## First Choice case

The state, through Attorney General [[matthew-platkin|Matthew Platkin]], issued a subpoena to [[first-choice-womens-resource-centers|First Choice Women's Resource Centers]] seeking donor and operational records.[1] The Supreme Court held that the center could pursue its constitutional claims in federal court rather than being limited to state proceedings, describing the demand for private donor information as an injury to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] associational rights.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that New Jersey "has been harassing women's reproductive centers" and framed the ruling as part of the dismantling of the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]].[1]

## Noncitizen voter registration dispute (2026)

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] and New Jersey became locked in a legal and political dispute after the state disclosed that approximately 6,600 noncitizens were mistakenly registered to vote between June 2023 and June 2024, which the state attributed to an alleged software error in its Motor Vehicle Commission system.[2] The software vendor disputed that account, claiming the state knowingly approved the noncitizen registrations.[2] Governor [[mikie-sherrill|Mikie Sherrill]] said fewer than 400 of those individuals ultimately cast ballots and that the affected registrations were being removed from the voter rolls.[2]

The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] launched an investigation and demanded that New Jersey provide detailed records — names, nationalities, addresses, registration information, and voting records for those who cast ballots — within five business days, with Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] saying the information was necessary to determine whether federal voting laws were violated and to support the department's broader election integrity efforts.[2] State officials rejected the demands, arguing the federal government lacks legal authority to compel production of the requested voter data and characterizing the investigation as politically motivated, amid a broader administration push to obtain voter registration information from multiple states.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] published a satirical code snippet illustrating what a "data coding mismatch" looks like, arguing that failures of voter eligibility verification occur in software and affect tens of thousands of records at minimum.[2] She contended that states do not discover such failures when systems fail, but only when noncitizens vote or when mass mailings expose the problem — citing an incident in [[colorado|Colorado]] in which roughly 29,997 noncitizens were sent invitations to vote — and suggested the systems may be working exactly as intended, with "plausible deniability" as the operative defense.[2]

## DOJ sanctuary lawsuit (February 2026)

On Monday, February 23, 2026, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed suit against New Jersey and Gov. [[mikie-sherrill|Mikie Sherrill]] challenging [[new-jersey-executive-order-12|Executive Order No. 12]], which restricts federal immigration enforcement inside certain state-controlled facilities by barring [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] from conducting secure arrests of criminal illegal aliens in nonpublic areas of state property, including correctional facilities.[3] Federal officials argued the policy obstructs immigration enforcement and results in the release of removable individuals, including illegal aliens convicted of aggravated assault, burglary, and drug and human trafficking offenses.[3]
