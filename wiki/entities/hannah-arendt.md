# Hannah Arendt

**Hannah Arendt** was, in Badlands Media's description, one of the twentieth century's preeminent scholars, cited for her analysis of totalitarian rule in *The Origins of Totalitarianism*.[1]

## Cited characterization

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]]'s January 20, 2023 essay quotes Arendt as calling [[totalitarianism]] "an attempted transformation of human nature itself."[1] Deschamps argued that this matches the cult-like features observable in totalitarian regimes, listing Nazi Germany and Soviet Russia alongside what he characterized as radical left groups of the present day.[1]

## Function in the argument

Arendt's formulation is used in tandem with Arthur Versluis's definition of totalitarianism as total centralized state power coupled with the obliteration of individual rights, in which the masses become psychologically regressed dependents of pathological rulers.[1] From the pairing, the essay concludes that once an individual and their in-group lose a clear and effective moral compass, conditions are ripe for triggering a [[mass-psychosis]].[1] The transformation-of-human-nature framing also supports the essay's claim that the specific manner in which a mass psychosis unfolds varies with the historical and cultural context of the affected society, and that in the modern era the totalitarian form is the greatest threat.[1]
