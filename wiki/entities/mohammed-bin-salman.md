# Mohammed bin Salman

Mohammed bin Salman, commonly abbreviated MBS, is the Crown Prince of [[saudi-arabia]] and the central figure of the Badlands Media essay [[arabian-nights]]. Prior to his elevation he served for five years as Saudi defence minister, with responsibility for the military. He is the son of king salman and the grandson of Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman Al Saud, founder of the modern kingdom.

## The 2017 consolidation

The source dates the crown prince's formal coronation to Saturday, November 4, 2017. On that day he detained roughly forty relatives and officials at the Riyadh Ritz-Carlton on accusations of corruption and treason. The article notes that "40" functions in Arabian idiom as an indefinite large number, comparable to the Western "dozens" — forty years in the desert, forty days and forty nights, "Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves" — and that the figure should not be read as an exact count. Among those displaced or detained were muhammad bin nayef, the would-be crown prince charged with treason and later accused of plotting to murder King Salman, and the investor alwaleed bin talal.

The [[q]] drops of the period placed unusual emphasis on Saudi Arabia, including a post asserting that martial law had been declared there, which the author of the source says he confirmed against contemporaneous news reports. Contemporary online speculation also linked the crown prince to the October 2017 Las Vegas shooting, suggesting he may have been the intended target.

## Formation and character

Drawing on reporting by ben hubbard of the [[new-york-times]], the source records that two of MBS's half-brothers died almost exactly a year apart between 2001 and 2002, and argues that this bereavement brought him closer to his father and instilled a strong attachment to his country and a sense of altruism toward its people. It portrays him as having rejected the globetrotting lifestyle of his young relatives in favour of shadowing his father and learning about Arabia directly, while absorbing 1980s and 1990s American popular culture in the manner of his Western millennial contemporaries.

The article gives particular weight to the claim that MBS, then sixteen, watched the September 11, 2001 attacks in real time and reacted with shock, fear, and then anger and a desire for retribution — a response the source compares to that later described by Noor bin Ladin. It presents his subsequent trajectory as having begun at that moment.

## Interpretation in the corpus

The source rejects both the mainstream portrait of MBS as a malevolent autocrat — it characterises Hubbard's analysis on this point as "hot garbage" — and the assumption, which the author says he once held himself, that the 2017 purge was carried out at [[donald-trump]]'s direction. Its position is that MBS "is no agent of Donald Trump," that he was already on his path when Trump took office, and that the two are best understood as "two warriors meeting on the road, each on their own quest to slay the same dragon." An image caption in the article identifies him as "The Punisher," and the legend of Alexander and the Gordian Knot is invoked as the model for his approach to intractable problems. The essay concludes by describing him as "the Prodigal Son of the Islamic world," on a redemption arc intended to liberate the Middle East from what the corpus calls the Puppet Masters.
