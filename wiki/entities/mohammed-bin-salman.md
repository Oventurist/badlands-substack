# Mohammed bin Salman

Mohammed bin Salman (commonly abbreviated MBS) is the Crown Prince of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and, in the Badlands Media telling, the central figure of a "redemption arc of the House of Saud" that the author argues has been systematically misread by Western media.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Rise to power

Before his elevation, MBS served for roughly five years as Saudi Defense Minister, placing him in charge of the kingdom's military.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He was formally coronated as Crown Prince in a royal ceremony on Saturday, November 4, 2017.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Upon his coronation he ordered the arrest of some "40" of his relatives, imprisoning them in the Ritz-Carlton hotel in Riyadh on accusations of corruption and treason — an episode accompanied by reports that martial law had been declared in the kingdom.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The author notes that "40" functions in Arabian usage as a colloquialism roughly equivalent to the Western "dozens," citing forty years in the desert, forty days and forty nights, and the fable of Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) See [[ritz-carlton-purge]].

The consolidation also involved deposing the would-be Crown Prince, [[muhammad-bin-nayef|Muhammad bin Nayef]], and the prince then in charge of the Saudi military — both cousins of MBS, arrested and stripped of all powers by MBS and his father, [[king-salman|King Salman]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Nayef was charged with treason and later accused of plotting to murder the King.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Formation and character

The article argues that MBS's trajectory did not begin with American influence but with personal events around age 16, the year in which two of his half-brothers died almost exactly a year apart between 2001 and 2002, and specifically with the morning of September 11, 2001.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Drawing on New York Times journalist Ben Hubbard — whose data-gathering the author credits while dismissing his portrait of MBS as malevolent — the piece describes a young prince who rejected the elitist, globetrotting lifestyle embraced by his young relatives, instead shadowing his father to learn about Arabia, and who grew up consuming 1980s and 1990s American culture.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Death, the author writes, brought MBS and his father closer and inspired a deep love of country and altruism toward his people.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The article likens him to "The Punisher" and invokes Alexander and the Gordian Knot as the model for cutting through an intractable mess.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

MBS's reaction to 9/11 is described as mirroring that of most Americans — shock, then fear, then anger and a desire for retribution — an effect the article compares to that on Noor bin Ladin.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Relationship with Donald Trump

The article emphasizes a special dynamic between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and MBS, noting that even the New York Times acknowledged it, and contrasts it with Trump's relationship with [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]], whom the Times sought to distinguish from the Saudi royal family.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The author explicitly rejects the notion that MBS is an agent of Trump, describing the two instead as "two warriors meeting on the road, each on their own quest to slay the same dragon."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The 2017 Riyadh "Sword Dance" ([[sword-dance]]) is treated as the iconic emblem of that convergence, and was highlighted in [[qanon|Q]] drops emphasizing Saudi Arabia.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

In February 2026, former [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] commander [[hussein-kanani|Hussein Kanani]] claimed on RT that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] possesses a nuclear bomb.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that nobody in the West is genuinely worried about such an arsenal with MBS in charge, describing him as a benevolent strongman who brought the hammer down on corrupt cousins and showed the clout to drag Saudi culture away from radical Islam toward the Golden Age — and suggesting a Saudi deterrent under his control could make the region safer.[2] The commentary noted the cultivated partnership between MBS and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the reason Riyadh enjoys a reputation as a reliable ally, in contrast to [[iran|Iran]]'s diminished credibility in the West.[2]

In March 2026 MBS met with Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] amid discussions about [[iran|Iran]] and regional security, as Graham urged stronger action against Tehran.[6] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Graham's decision to air his proposals on Fox News and social media was unlikely to succeed, because the Saudi royal family values its privacy and prefers to conduct diplomacy behind closed doors.[6]

## Meeting with the US Senate delegation (October 2023)

On Saturday, October 21, 2023, the Crown Prince met a bipartisan US Senate delegation of five Republicans and five Democrats led by [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], who urged him to offer funds to sustain the Gazan people until [[hamas|Hamas]] was destroyed.[25] The delegation had originally intended the trip to promote Israeli-Saudi normalization, an initiative Badlands and the visiting senators alike suggested the October 7 attack had been designed to derail.[25] Badlands commentary also noted that the USS Eisenhower carrier group's re-routing into the Persian Gulf left US carrier groups flanking Saudi Arabia.[25]

## Assassination fears over the Israel normalization deal (August 2024)

The conversations were described to Politico by a former U.S. official briefed on them and two other people with knowledge of them, all granted anonymity given the sensitivity of the topic; the takeaway reported was that MBS appeared intent on striking the mega-deal despite the risks, seeing it as crucial to his country's future.[26] Badlands commentary responded by listing assassinated peacemakers — [[john-f-kennedy|Kennedy]], [[king-faisal|King Faisal]], [[yitzhak-rabin|Yitzhak Rabin]] and [[yasser-arafat|Yasser Arafat]] of the 1993 [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] — and the attempt on [[donald-trump|Trump]], concluding "they kill peacemakers."[26]

## November 2025 White House visit and F-35 sale

Mohammed bin Salman was scheduled to meet President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the White House on November 18, 2025, with an [[f-35|F-35]] deal, a U.S.–Saudi security agreement, and possible normalization with [[israel|Israel]] on the agenda.[30] The day before the meeting, Trump said he would approve the sale of F-35s to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], which would make the kingdom the first Middle Eastern country other than Israel to obtain the jets and would affect Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|qualitative military edge]].[30] Israel did not oppose the sale but asked the Trump administration to condition it on Saudi normalization with Israel.[30]

## Al-Ula and the Atlantic account (2024)

A September 2024 Atlantic essay by Franklin Foer on the year-long American effort to free hostages, end the fighting in Gaza, and bring peace to the Middle East opened with a January 8 meeting at al-Ula, described as the realization of the crown prince's dreams — a remote oasis representing his theory of modernization, filled with five-star resorts and luxurious spas, with a planned satellite branch of the Centre Pompidou.[32] MBS maintained winter quarters at al-Ula and took meetings there in a tent lined with thick rugs and plush cushions, where he received [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]].[32] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that the rapper Ye had also wintered at an MBS-owned resort at al-Ula while recording the album Vultures, whose double-headed eagle cover art he linked to Rothschild iconography, and observed that MBS had likewise hosted Shinzo Abe there.[32]

## Disputed account of the crown prince's motives

The Atlantic reported that MBS told Blinken the Biden administration represented his best chance of realizing his plans, since two-thirds of the Senate was needed to ratify any Saudi-U.S. defense pact and he believed that could happen only under a Democratic administration able to deliver progressive votes by building a Palestinian state into the deal — and that he had to move before the November election risked returning [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to power.[32] The piece also quoted the 38-year-old ruler saying that "seventy percent of my population is younger than me," that most of them "never really knew much about the Palestinian issue," and that while he did not personally care about it, "my people do, so I need to make sure this is meaningful"; a Saudi official described this account of the conversation as "incorrect."[32] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry dismissed the Atlantic's framing as "crackpot nonsense," pointing to the Trump-era Sword Dance in Riyadh, and noted that Scott Ritter had relayed a similar account to Judge Napolitano sourced to intelligence-community contacts, potentially the same channel.[32]

## Bedouin roots and the Khashoggi framing

In Badlands' account, King Salman selected MBS as crown prince over Mohammed bin Nayef because he wanted a "true Saudi national" able to win the devotion of the people and make Saudi Arabia great by making its people prosperous — an idea attributed to President Kennedy's 1961 advice to Salman's brother, the future King Faisal, who implemented it after becoming king in 1964 and was assassinated in 1975.[32] Salman is quoted as saying, "To deal with a Bedouin, I need a Bedouin," referring to the 2.5 million nomads still living in the Arabian deserts, whom the Saudi government under Western influence had sought to marginalize and domesticate; MBS, who declined the European boarding-school and university path taken by his older half-brothers, is described as having always had a strong bond with the Bedouin.[32] Badlands further argued that [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]]'s 2018 murder was used to frame MBS, with the CIA hostile to him because he had usurped Mohammed bin Nayef, and that Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] introduced a Senate resolution holding MBS responsible despite what Badlands characterized as an absence of evidence beyond anonymous CIA sources.[32]

Badlands attributed two motives to MBS in the Israel-Gaza-Hezbollah conflict: revenge and justice against those responsible for 9/11, for framing the Saudi royal family and bringing the U.S. military into the region — a significant factor in the [[saudi-purge|Saudi royal purge]] — and stability and prosperity, in service of his ambition to transform the Middle East into "the next Europe."[32] On this reading, MBS has long accepted that there is space for [[israel|Israel]] in the future Middle East, and his post-October 7 "enabling" of Israel amounts to holding back the Muslim world so that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] would show his true colors, while providing logistics for food and supplies as Houthi action threatened shipping.[32]

## The 2017 Qatar embargo and the Salwa Canal

Badlands Media dates MBS's rise to June 2017 — one month after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] visited Riyadh and received the sword dance — when King [[salman-bin-abdulaziz|Salman]] named him Crown Prince over longtime CIA ally [[mohammed-bin-nayef|Mohammed bin Nayef]], described as a dramatic display of power projection by the emerging Sovereign Alliance.[33] One of his first acts was to launch an embargo against [[qatar|Qatar]], backed by Trump and Emirati President [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]] as well as Jordan, Egypt, Yemen, Bahrain, and a number of African nations.[33]

MBS cut all diplomatic and economic ties with Qatar, ordered Saudi and Emirati ports to refuse Qatari ships, and closed the land border, effectively making the peninsula an island; he then sought to make it a literal island by announcing the [[salwa-canal|Salwa Canal]] project and issuing a Request for Proposal to regional contractors.[33] His stated motive — shared, per Badlands, with Trump — was that Qatar was a terrorist haven allowing the [[cia|CIA]] and ISIS to conduct psychological warfare operations through outlets such as [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]].[33] The net effect was to drive Qatar deeper into its relationship with [[iran|Iran]].[33] MBS abandoned the canal, but the embargo endured until January 4, 2021, when Trump negotiated a reconciliation between MBS and Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim]] two weeks before leaving office.[33]

## Role in the 2024 US–Russia prisoner swap

On September 5, 2024, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] publicly thanked the crown prince and prime minister for helping to arrange the largest prisoner exchange between the United States and Russia since the Cold War.[34] Addressing the [[eastern-economic-forum|Eastern Economic Forum]] in Vladivostok, Putin said, "Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince played an active role in the initial stages of this work. We are very grateful to him, as it led to the return of our citizens to the homeland."[34] Putin also thanked Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] for providing the venue and mentioned that several other Arab countries facilitated the swap without naming them.[34] The exchange, worked on in complete secrecy for more than a year, freed American journalist [[evan-gershkovich|Evan Gershkovich]] and former US Marine [[paul-whelan|Paul Whelan]] on August 1, and involved 24 prisoners in total — 16 returned to the West and eight moved to Russia.[34]

Coverage noted that Putin and Mohammed bin Salman have maintained a close personal relationship since 2015, when the latter first visited Russia, and that the rapport helped the leaders of the world's two largest oil exporters conclude and sustain the OPEC+ energy deal.[34] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] traced the 2015 meeting — held when MBS was Saudi defence minister and not yet a household name — to a Guardian report on Syria talks, arguing it contradicted the mainstream narrative that the Saudis opposed [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] and were aiding NATO's regime-change attempt.[34] He linked this to MBS's readmission of Syria to the [[arab-league|Arab League]] on May 19, 2023, eleven years after Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] used her clout to have it expelled in 2012, and concluded that "the Middle East is healing" through a [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] of leaders foiling the globalist camp.[34]

## Postponed Japan visit, May 2024

In May 2024 the Crown Prince, the de facto Saudi leader, postponed a visit to Japan scheduled to begin that Monday because of the health of his father, [[king-salman|King Salman]], who was being treated for lung inflammation at Al Salam Palace in Jeddah; Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary Yoshimasa Hayashi confirmed the postponement in Tokyo.[35] Badlands commentary noted that MBS nonetheless met with Japan by video conference, describing it as an important meeting given Japan's status as the only Asian country to have consistently supported Israel since 1948.[35] The same commentary traces MBS's "taste for justice" to his father's record as governor of Riyadh, and predicts that he will transform Arabia into a great world power.[35]

### Trump's 2025 Miami address and the FII
When President Donald Trump addressed the Future Investment Initiative Institute's Priority Summit in Miami Beach on February 19, 2025 — the first American president to do so — Badlands commentary characterized the speech as Trump paying reverence to the Saudi crown prince and to the emerging multipolar world order.[36] The FII, created by the Saudi Public Investment Fund in October 2018 and branded "Davos in the Desert," was described as the Sovereign Alliance's answer to the World Economic Forum, a framing said to have angered Klaus Schwab and his circle.[36] Its inaugural edition was overshadowed by the Jamal Khashoggi affair, launched weeks earlier and characterized in the Badlands reading as a psychological operation against the crown prince intended to make would-be attendees doubt whether the young leader represented a new chapter in Arab history.[36] In that account, Trump's political embrace of the crown prince put to rest remaining doubts, casting him as the personification of the Arab Revolt of 1916 and finishing what the Hashemites and Saudis began in fighting the Ottomans for their sovereignty.[36]

## White House meeting on Israel normalization (November 2025)

Israeli Channel 12 news reported in late November 2025, citing two senior American officials and an additional source familiar with the conversation, that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was left "disappointed and angry" after a tense exchange with the crown prince over normalization with [[israel|Israel]] during their White House meeting the previous week.[37] Trump reportedly raised the issue himself and urged bin Salman to move forward immediately.[37]

Bin Salman pushed back, saying that while he was not opposed to normalization in principle, widespread anti-Israel sentiment in Saudi Arabia following the war in Gaza did not allow for the step at that time.[37] A senior U.S. official said the conversation nonetheless remained polite, and added that bin Salman "did not say 'never'" regarding normalization.[37] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he believed the Trump administration had indeed briefed the story to reporter Barak Ravid but doubted the underlying characterization, arguing that Trump knew in advance the crown prince would not move on the Palestinian statehood demand and in fact agreed with him — which is why Trump had built a pathway to statehood into his 20-point peace plan.[37]

## Meeting with Sergey Lavrov, September 2024

On Monday, September 9, 2024, the Crown Prince met in Riyadh with Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]], according to the Saudi Press Agency.[38] The two discussed strengthening bilateral relations, identifying areas for cooperation, and enhancing collaboration across various fields, along with recent regional and international developments and efforts to bolster security and stability.[38] Lavrov had earlier that day taken part in the seventh [[gulf-cooperation-council|GCC]]–Russia Joint Ministerial Meeting of Strategic Dialogue, and at a press conference he announced that Russia had invited Mohammed bin Salman to the [[brics|BRICS]] summit in Kazan on October 22–24.[38] Badlands commentary noted that visiting foreign ministers rarely meet the Crown Prince directly, since he typically receives only heads of state, and read the exception as a measure of the standing Lavrov commands.[38]

## 2024 Arab Summit and bilateral strategy

In May 2024, MBS hosted the [[arab-summit-2024-bahrain|33rd Arab Summit]] in Bahrain, with UN Secretary-General [[antonio-guterres|António Guterres]] attending as special guest of honor, and the gathering issued a joint statement demanding a permanent Israeli withdrawal from Palestine.[39] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Saudis had spent years courting players across the geopolitical board through bilateral agreements — 'an art long lost to the globalists' — building a network of relationships that could now be leveraged to turn globalist institutions against the globalists themselves, concluding that MBS had 'made himself the new gamemaster.'[39]

## Tense November 2025 White House meeting with Trump

According to an Axios report published November 25, 2025 and sourced to two U.S. officials and one source with knowledge, the crown prince's November 18 meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] became heated when the two discussed Saudi Arabia joining the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and normalizing relations with [[israel|Israel]].[40] White House officials had told MBS ahead of time that Trump expected progress on normalization, and Trump himself raised the issue and pressed hard during the closed session; MBS pushed back, and Trump was reportedly disappointed.[40] MBS explained that although he wants to move forward with normalization eventually, he cannot do so now because Saudi public opinion is highly anti-Israel in the aftermath of the Gaza war and Saudi society "isn't ready."[40] Publicly the two men praised each other and showed no daylight between them.[40]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that he had expected MBS not to relent on the Palestinian statehood condition, questioned whether the leak to Barak Ravid was accurate, and suggested its purpose was to seed a narrative of friction; he argued MBS "is holding all of the cards" given unfavorable global views of Israel and the crown prince's own rising popularity among world leaders and publics.[40]
