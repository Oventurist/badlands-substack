# José "Pepe" Figueres

José "Pepe" Figueres, known as "Don Pepe," was a Costa Rican leader whom [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents across his Badlands Media writing as a model statesman who protected his country from capture by a global cabal. He is introduced in Lange's earlier article "The Illusion of Democracy" and returns as the organising example of A Seismic Shift.

## The two safeguards

According to Lange, Figueres placed constitutional safeguards in Costa Rican law to prevent an insurgency and protect the rights of the people, and in doing so "gave the power back to the people." Two measures are singled out.

The first was electoral: he permanently protected elections with an identification document issued only to citizens, which prevented anybody else from voting. Lange connects this directly to his expectation of a U.S.-owned voter ID law and a cleansing of the American voter rolls, potentially secured by the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]] blockchain voting patent.

The second was financial: he created a permanent **"public banking option"** that prevented private banks from controlling the government. These publicly owned banks, Lange writes, protect the people's savings from being stolen or controlled by private bankers. This is the model against which he measures the [[bank-of-north-dakota|Bank of North Dakota]], the historical [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]], and the proposed revival of [[public-banking|public banking]] through postal banking and [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]].

## Role in the article's argument

Lange argues these safeguards have gone a long way toward preventing another Costa Rican government controlled by a global cabal, and frames the postal banking and FedAccounts agenda he attributes to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as an American version of the same design. He closes the article by crediting the coming transformation, rhetorically, to "'Don Pepe' himself."
