# José "Pepe" Figueres

José "Pepe" Figueres was a Costa Rican leader whom Badlands Media presents as the model for constitutional safeguards capable of protecting a nation from capture by a global cabal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Known as "Don Pepe," he is credited with putting constitutional safeguards in place to prevent an insurgency and protect the rights of the people of Costa Rica, giving power back to the people and going a long way toward preventing another government controlled by a global cabal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## The two safeguards

Figueres is credited with two specific reforms. First, he permanently protected elections with an identification document issued only to citizens, which prevented anyone else from voting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Second, he created a permanent [[public-banking-option|public banking option]] that prevented private banks from controlling the government, with publicly owned banks protecting the people's savings from being stolen or controlled by private bankers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Relevance to the United States

Badlands Media argues that the same two safeguards are being built in the United States: election protection through a citizen-verified voter identity system tied to the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]]'s blockchain voting patent, and banking protection through a revived [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]] and [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Turning the Post Office into a public banking option is described as similar to what Figueres did in Costa Rica, and the article closes its argument by attributing the whole program, rhetorically, to "Don Pepe" himself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The only existing American analogue to his public bank is the [[bank-of-north-dakota|Bank of North Dakota]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)
