# Alice Weidel

Alice Weidel is a German politician and co-leader of the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD) party.[1]

## October 2023 polling record

In October 2023 Weidel publicly celebrated a new record high for her party in the ARD Deutschlandtrend poll, writing: "New record also in the ARD Germany trend: The AfD is now at 23% here too! In addition, 50% of citizens would like the AfD to be in government where it has performed strongly. A great confirmation of our work!"[1] The result placed the AfD above the governing [[social-democratic-party-of-germany|Social Democratic Party]] and the [[free-democratic-party|Free Democratic Party]] combined and gave the party an unprecedented eight-point lead over the SPD.[1]

## 'Make Europe Great Again' (January 2025)

On January 15, 2025, Weidel, co-leader of the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD), adapted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s MAGA slogan for her own continent, calling to "Make Europe Great Again" in a post on X.[2] She was responding to a post drawing parallels between her party's program and policies advocated by Trump and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[2] Musk, who owns X and had endorsed the AfD, interviewed Weidel live on the platform the previous week; during that conversation she described the AfD as a "libertarian conservative party" seeking to reduce bureaucracy and "free the people from the state," while Musk repeatedly asserted that "only AfD can save Germany" ahead of the snap parliamentary election scheduled for late February 2025.[2] Badlands commentary welcomed the slogan as evidence that MAGA had become a global movement, but objected that "Europe is not a nation" and that making Europe great again would require making the individual European nations great again by stripping the European Union of much of its power.[2]

## February 2025 federal election

In the German federal election of February 2025, Weidel's [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] took second place with a record 20.8% of the vote, behind the conservatives led by [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]], who finished well ahead of rivals but short of the 30% vote share they had expected.[3] Weidel, the AfD's candidate for chancellor, did a victory lap of her supporters, though even her own party had hoped for a stronger result and the mood at AfD headquarters was subdued.[3]

As results came in during the early hours of Monday, the AfD was shown far ahead of all other parties in the east of the country, with a projected 34% according to a survey for the public broadcaster ZDF.[3] "Germans have voted for change," Weidel said, predicting that Merz's attempt to form a coalition would ultimately fail: "We'll have fresh elections — I don't think we'll have to wait another four years."[3] Badlands commentator Chris Paul reacted by questioning whether German elections are genuine at all, suggesting they may be "fake like ours are."[3]
