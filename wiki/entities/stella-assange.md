# Stella Assange

**Stella Assange** is a lawyer and the wife of imprisoned WikiLeaks publisher [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], and a leading public advocate against his extradition to the United States.[1]

In December 2023 she publicly confirmed that her husband's appeal hearing would take place on February 20–21, 2024, before two High Court judges at the Royal Courts of Justice in London — likely his final opportunity to resist extradition on [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]] charges as part of the [[assange-extradition-case|Assange extradition case]].[1]

## Tucker Carlson interview (2024)

Erik Carlson's "The CIA is Being Thrown Under the Bus" cites [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s interview with Stella Assange as one of several early-2024 broadcasts that put the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] under an unprecedented spotlight.[2] In the interview she discussed the agency's plan, attributed to [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]], to assassinate her husband [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]].[2]

For Carlson the interview belongs to a sequence — alongside the [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] interviews — that he reads as the Cabal abandoning the agency and preparing to scapegoat it.[2]

## Criticism in "The Mystery of Julian Assange" (March 2024)

In his March 4, 2024 essay "The Mystery of Julian Assange," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that after watching Stella Assange's interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] he did not trust her "at all," saying his instincts told him something was not right about her presentation.[3] Asked why Britain was holding her husband essentially without domestic criminal charges, she reportedly smirked and answered that the United Kingdom views itself as a lapdog of the United States — a framing Carlson rejected, arguing the relationship runs the other way.[3]

Carlson also noted that Stella disclosed that Tucker Carlson's December 2023 visit to [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] in prison had not been permitted to be recorded, meaning the journalist had merely a private conversation rather than an interview, and that no footage of Assange was produced.[3] He further criticised her comparison of her husband's case to that of [[alexei-navalny|Alexei Navalny]], the Russian opposition figure who died in prison while serving a three-decade sentence, asking whether she was truly ignorant of the disputed circumstances of that death.[3] Carlson observed that she had said her husband's lawyers would seek an injunction from the [[european-court-of-human-rights|European Court of Human Rights]] if the UK appeal failed, and that she described his mind and body as fading fast in inhumane prison conditions even while his legal team fought to keep him in Britain.[3]
