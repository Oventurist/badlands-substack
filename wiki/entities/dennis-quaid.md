# Dennis Quaid

Dennis Quaid is an American actor who, at age 70 in 2024, publicly declared his intention to vote for former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in that year's presidential election.[1] Quaid stars as [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] in the biopic *Reagan*, which was scheduled for release on August 30, 2024.[1]

## 2024 endorsement of Trump

Speaking on *[[piers-morgan|Piers Morgan]] Uncensored* on May 28, 2024, Quaid said of Trump, "I think I'm gonna vote for him," adding that a vote for the presumptive GOP nominee "just makes sense."[1] He explained that he had not initially planned to vote for Trump, but that the Manhattan "hush money" prosecution — which began on April 15, 2024 and was under jury deliberation at the time of the interview — changed his mind.[1] The case, part of the [[trump-hush-money-case|Trump hush-money case]], marked the first criminal trial of a former U.S. president; Trump faced 88 charges across four criminal cases in total, pleading not guilty to all.[1]

"I see a weaponization of our justice system and, uh, a challenge to our constitution," Quaid said, adding that Trump "is the most investigated person, probably in the history of the world, and they haven't been able to really get him on anything."[1] He asked rhetorically, "What is the crime? I still can't figure it out."[1]

Quaid also offered a favorable personal assessment of Trump: "I tell you one true thing about him is that I really feel that he is working for the American people. That's what he's all about. And I do believe that to be true and sincere."[1] By contrast he said of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], "I feel that he says things to get votes not that he truly believes in them."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that although Quaid framed the politicized trial as his deciding factor, his remarks revealed a deeper conviction, calling the contrast between his statements on Trump and Biden "stark" coming from a Hollywood actor.[1] She also noted the timeliness of Quaid's forthcoming portrayal of Reagan.[1] Quaid's remarks were reported by [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]].[1] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the endorsement within a wider argument that populist sentiment was beginning to surface in popular culture as "the counter culture becomes the culture."[1]
