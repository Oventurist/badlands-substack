# Metropolitan Police Department of the District of Columbia

The Metropolitan Police Department (MPD) is the municipal police force of Washington, D.C.[1]

## Federalization in August 2025

In August 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued an executive action declaring a crime emergency in the District — framed publicly as "[[liberation-day|Liberation Day]]" for the capital — that imposed federal control over the MPD and deployed the [[national-guard|National Guard]] to patrol city streets.[1] Mayor [[muriel-bowser|Muriel Bowser]] responded by leading a renewed campaign for [[dc-statehood|statehood]], while protests emerged over fears that [[congress|Congress]] might rescind the [[dc-home-rule-act|Home Rule Act]].[1]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] defended the federalization, arguing that the police of the federal city should be accountable to the federal government rather than to local elected officials.[1]

## 2025 crime data manipulation investigation

In August 2025, federal prosecutors opened an investigation into whether MPD officials manipulated crime statistics, amid an escalating political showdown between the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and the District of Columbia over control of the department.[2] It was not immediately clear what federal laws could have been violated by the possible manipulation of crime data.[2]

U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]] led the inquiry, which was first reported by The New York Times and later confirmed by the Associated Press.[2] The probe followed reporting by NBC Washington that MPD commander [[michael-pulliam|Michael Pulliam]] had been placed on administrative leave over allegations that he manipulated crime statistics; Pulliam denied wrongdoing.[2] MPD said it does not comment on "active internal investigations and personnel matters," referring instead to Chief [[pamela-smith|Pamela A. Smith]]'s statement that the department "is committed to upholding the trust and the confidence of the public."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied the D.C. allegations to similar reporting about crime-statistic gaming in Colorado, calling manipulation of public-safety data "the most viscous form of gaslighting" and part of a longer pattern of falsified economic, health, climate, and election data.[2]
