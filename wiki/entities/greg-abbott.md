# Greg Abbott

Greg Abbott is the Governor of Texas who, on May 16, 2024, pardoned former U.S. Army Sgt. [[daniel-perry|Daniel Perry]], who had been convicted of murder and sentenced to 25 years in prison for shooting an armed [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] protester who wielded an AK-47.[1]

Abbott issued a proclamation granting Perry a full pardon and restoration of full civil rights of citizenship, while taking aim at Travis County District Attorney [[jose-garza|Jose Garza]] for allegedly directing the lead investigator to withhold exculpatory evidence and for demonstrating "unethical and biased misuse" of his office in prosecuting Perry.[1]

"Texas has one of the strongest 'Stand Your Ground' laws of self-defense that cannot be nullified by a jury or a progressive District Attorney," Abbott said in the statement announcing the pardon. "I thank the Board for its thorough investigation, and I approve their pardon recommendation."[1] The pardon followed a unanimous recommendation issued earlier the same day by the [[texas-board-of-pardons-and-paroles|Texas Board of Pardons and Paroles]].[1]

### Criticism from Tucker Carlson (September 2023)

[[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] singled out Abbott in a September 2023 clip as a "liar, and betrayer of your own people," accusing him of failing to take decisive action against illegal immigration despite possessing the means to do so.[2] "Don't you have a National Guard? Why don't you seal the Border? Oh, it's very complicated. No, it's not," Carlson said, presenting Abbott alongside [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] as evidence of governance detached from majority will.[2]

## Dominion and the Tucker Carlson firing (May 2023)

On Saturday, May 13, 2023, Abbott called on [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] to "answer" questions about its alleged role in the firing of [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] from [[fox-news|Fox News]], after reports surfaced that the network had removed Carlson as part of its $787.5 million defamation settlement with the voting-machine company.[3] Abbott wrote on social media: "If Dominion wants to do business with Texas in the future, they should first answer questions about what role, if any, they played in silencing a prominent conservative journalist. The answers to those questions, and other factors, should guide whether we want them to operate here."[3] The remarks were reported by [[breitbart|Breitbart]].[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized the response as inadequate for an officeholder who "swore an oath," arguing that upon learning a settlement agreement had included the removal of the most-watched cable news host in the 8pm slot, Abbott's reaction was merely to seek answers before considering future business with the company.[3] In her reading, free speech, First Amendment journalistic protections and "even 'our democracy'" had become negotiation levers for a governor who remained open to dealing with Dominion.[3] She added that Carlson's replacement program, Fox News Tonight, had reclaimed the top prime-time spot with 1.4 million viewers while being down nearly 1.2 million from both its debut and Carlson's roughly 2.6 million average, concluding that "Abbott and the other establishment RINOs are the only ones still watching — and their number is shrinking."[3]

## Defiance of the Supreme Court razor-wire ruling (January 2024)

On Monday, January 22, 2024, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] determined in a 5-4 ruling that the Biden administration could remove concertina wire that Texas had installed at various points along its border with Mexico, wire that had been linked to numerous injuries among undocumented migrants.[4] Abbott, who had spent months escalating his feud with the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration over border security, responded on Wednesday, January 24 with a statement declaring that "the federal government has broken the compact between the United States and the States."[4]

In that statement Abbott wrote: "I have already declared an invasion under Article I, § 10, Clause 3 to invoke Texas's constitutional authority to defend and protect itself. That authority is the supreme law of the land and supersedes any federal statutes to the contrary."[4] He further argued that "the failure of the Biden administration to fulfill the duties imposed by Article IV section 4 has triggered Article 1, section 10, Clause 3, which reserves to this State the right of self-defense," and vowed that authorities under his jurisdiction would not back down.[4] The [[texas-national-guard|Texas National Guard]] continued building and installing the barriers notwithstanding the ruling.[4]

House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] threw his support behind Abbott, joining other high-profile Republicans urging Texas to defy the ruling, while Democratic Representatives [[joaquin-castro|Joaquin Castro]] and [[greg-casar|Greg Casar]] called on Biden to seize and federalize the Texas Guard.[4] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the practical stakes of this particular patch of border were inconsequential and Texas's legal case far from certain, setting up "a fascinating bit of Federal vs. State lawfare" in February, while the overstatement of the actual facts served a narrative escalation between federal and state forces that he judged a net win for Trumpism.[4]
