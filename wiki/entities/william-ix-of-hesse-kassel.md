# William IX of Hesse-Kassel

**William IX** (Wilhelm IX, later Prince Elector Wilhelm I) was the royal administrator of the German Hesse-Kassel region and, in Badlands Media's telling, the patron whose patronage launched the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]] banking dynasty.[1] Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" describes him as a prominent Freemason and identifies [[mayer-amschel-rothschild|Mayer Amschel Bauer]] as his court financial agent.[1]

## Patron of Mayer Amschel Rothschild

DeLarme writes that Mayer, only a year younger than William, "ingratiated himself" to the prince by joining his interests in [[freemasonry|Freemasonry]] and antiquities.[1] Through this relationship, the article speculates, Mayer was initiated into the "High Cabal," a network said to have operated for years out of Venice and eventually Geneva, Switzerland.[1] The period is also described as the moment when the metaphysics of the Jewish Cabala began to fuse with the traditions of Freemasonry, a synthesis to which Mayer's rabbinical training and antiquarian searches are said to have given him access.[1]

## The Hessian soldier money

The most consequential claim attached to William IX concerns the enormous sum paid to him by the British government for supplying Hessian soldiers to fight American colonists during the Revolutionary War.[1] According to various authors dissenting from the mainstream view of the Rothschilds, William handed this money to Mayer for investment, but it was instead used to establish his son [[nathan-rothschild|Nathan]] as head of the London branch of the family banking house.[1] Mayer eventually repaid the money, but Nathan is said to have manipulated the situation in such a way that the episode became the origin of the enormous Rothschild fortune.[1]

Even the Rothschild-friendly biographer [[derek-wilson|Derek Wilson]] acknowledged the point, writing that "it was the temporary diversion of the immense sums of money originating in Hesse-Kassel which enabled N. M. to launch his banking operation, providing him with both liquidity and prestige."[1]
