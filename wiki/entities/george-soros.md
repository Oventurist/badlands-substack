# George Soros

George Soros is a financier counted by [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] among her "close friends" in the West, alongside Larry Summers and Al Gore, and among the figures she was happy to bring into Canada during the re-organization of the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party of Canada]] between 2011 and 2014 under the [[canada-2020|Canada 2020]] banner.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

In Freeland's taxonomy of the new plutocracy, Soros belongs with Bill Gates and Warren Buffett to the category of technocratically friendly plutocrats who are, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s characterization, ideologically compatible with a New World Order of depopulation — as distinct from the "bad" plutocrats such as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] who do not conform to the British Empire's program of global governance under the green agenda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Polish media acquisition (2023)

In August 2023 a company backed by the Soros Economic Development Fund took control of two major Polish newspapers, raising concerns over media bias attributed to the billionaire's progressive politics.[2] The Dutch company [[pluralis|Pluralis]] completed a deal first struck in 2021, buying additional shares from Polish owner Grzegorz Hajdarowicz to reach 57 percent of [[gremi-media|Gremi Media]] — publisher of the daily Rzeczpospolita and the financial daily Parkiet — and thereby taking control of the company.[2] The transaction was reported by [[remix-news|Remix News]] as evidence that Soros remained active in European affairs despite claims of his withdrawal.[2]

## Romanian ban pledge (2025)

On February 17, 2025, Romanian political figure [[calin-georgescu|Călin Georgescu]] declared that he would ban the entire Soros network in [[romania|Romania]] if elected president, saying, "We have ways to do this. We know who they are, it's very clear."[3] Coverage framed the pledge as reflecting a growing sentiment among Romanian political factions that view the Soros network as a detrimental force undermining national sovereignty, amid disputes over identity, European Union membership and foreign influence.[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] observed that such bans have succeeded in small countries around the world but argued that Americans, "cucked to the television," would not attempt the same.[3]

## Soros-backed prosecutors (2023)

Badlands commentary in March 2023 framed the Manhattan prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the work of a "blatantly corrupt Soros-backed DA," referring to [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], whose campaign had benefited from Soros-linked funding and who presides over what the commentary called the most crime-ridden areas in the nation.[4] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] urged that protest against the prosecution be massive, peaceful and highly energized, and be directed specifically at drawing attention to Soros as the financier behind the wave of progressive prosecutors.[4]

## Texas Spending (2023–2024)

State campaign finance records reviewed by Fox News Digital and reported in January 2024 showed Soros pouring extensive sums into Texas in an attempt to flip the traditionally Republican state to Democrats.[5] Over the preceding year he contributed more than $3 million to at least five left-wing groups in the state to build political infrastructure and help Democrats make gains.[5] Among the recipients was Texas Majority PAC, an under-the-radar group positioning itself to turn the state blue, which Fox News Digital had reported in August 2023 received hundreds of thousands of dollars from Soros' Democracy PAC II; the financier was described as single-handedly propelling that organization.[5]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the story to revisit claims that Soros has been "banned" from multiple countries, noting fact-checkers' distinction that it was the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] rather than Soros personally that ceased operations in Hungary in 2018 after passage of the so-called "Stop Soros" law targeting its work with illegal immigrants.[5] She argued the Hungarian precedent was directly relevant because Soros holds dual U.S. and Hungarian citizenship and because the banned Hungarian activities concerned immigration, much like the Texas spending — with the difference being the degree of influence his money buys over American decision-makers.[5]

## Capital Research Center terror-funding report (September 2025)

On 17 September 2025 the [[capital-research-center|Capital Research Center]] published an exclusive report alleging that Soros's [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]], run with his son [[alex-soros|Alexander Soros]], had directed over $80 million since 2016 to organizations tied to terrorism or extremist violence.[6] The report cited grants to the [[center-for-third-world-organizing|Center for Third World Organizing]] and the [[ruckus-society|Ruckus Society]], the [[sunrise-movement|Sunrise Movement]]'s endorsement of the [[stop-cop-city|Stop Cop City]] campaign, $18 million to the [[movement-for-black-lives|Movement for Black Lives]], and more than $2.3 million to the West Bank NGO [[al-haq|Al-Haq]].[6]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that the report — published the same day President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced the [[antifa|Antifa]] terror designation — could function as a predicate for that designation and as a fulcrum for the RICO charges against Soros that Trump had been signaling all summer, and noted the presence of [[edwin-meese-iii|Edwin Meese III]] on the Capital Research Center board.[6]

## Succession and the OSF retreat from Europe

Soros handed control of the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] — which holds most of the assets of his roughly $25 billion family office — to his son [[alexander-soros|Alexander Soros]] in June 2023.[7] Two months later, OSF announced it would terminate most of its European Union operations amid a "radical shift of strategic direction," closing all regional and global programs and cutting headcount by at least 40%.[7] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the handover as bequeathing "a somewhat unceremonious inheritance that may be more of an albatross," and read the retreat as a sign of shifting global narrative terrain.[7]

## Indian raids and the "Sovereign Alliance" squeeze (March 2025)

On March 18, 2025, India's [[enforcement-directorate|Enforcement Directorate]] conducted extensive searches at the Bengaluru offices of the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] and linked entities, in an inquiry into foreign direct investment allegedly routed in violation of Foreign Exchange Management Act guidelines; Indian coverage described Soros as a "US billionaire currency speculator and hedge fund operator" accused by the Bharatiya Janata Party of acting against India's interests.[8] Investigators reportedly traced funding from the [[soros-economic-development-fund|Soros Economic Development Fund]] to three Indian companies while the Open Society Institute had been under the home ministry's prior reference category since 2016.[8]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the Indian action coincided with heat Soros was facing at home following the election of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], whom Soros had openly opposed while aligning with hardline liberals, and concluded that "Soros is getting pinched and pincered by the Sovereign Alliance."[8] He noted that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had effectively defeated Soros within a few years of taking office in 2000 and that Russia banned the foundation outright in 2015 as a threat to state security, and compared Trump's campaign against [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] to [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]]'s 2018 drive to expel Soros from Hungary.[8]

The same commentary advanced a speculative claim that Soros himself has been dead for years — which the writer argued would explain why his son had been "rolled out as the heir apparent" — while contending that the elder Soros "has come to symbolize the globalist oligarchy," making preservation of his caricature useful both to allies keeping money flowing and to opponents seeking to destroy the symbol.[8]

## KCBS broadcast of ICE agent locations, 2025

In January 2025 a radio station reported to be owned by Soros, [[kcbs-740-am|KCBS 740 AM]], publicly revealed the locations of undercover [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|US Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] agents operating in San Jose, California.[9] Host [[bret-burkhart|Bret Burkhart]] detailed the unmarked vehicles used and the specific locations where agents were present on January 26, citing social media updates from the [[rapid-response-network|Rapid Response Network]], an organization opposing deportation efforts.[9] Reporting noted the broadcast might have endangered the agents' safety by exposing their covert operations.[9]

## 2024 Biden campaign donations

Federal filings released in July 2023 showed that George Soros and his son Alexander each wrote maximum $6,600 checks to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s re-election committee on June 30, 2023, marking their first financial entry into the 2024 presidential race.[10] Neither had yet contributed to the Biden Victory Fund, which carries far higher contribution limits, though reporting suggested that was likely to change as the election approached.[10] Badlands commentary characterized campaign finance, like foreign aid, as a money-laundering mechanism, and read the donations as an early signal that establishment funders intended to keep Biden on the ticket despite media speculation about a Bidenless primary.[10]

## District attorney funding (2023 reporting)

In January 2023, [[breitbart|Breitbart]] reported that Soros had spent more than $40 million getting far-left district attorneys — prosecutors described as having agendas to decriminalize a range of crimes — elected across the United States.[13] The figure came from author Matt Palumbo, writing in the [[new-york-post|New York Post]], who concluded that "Soros-backed DAs (and their ideological allies) now represent at least one-fifth of Americans."[13] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] commented sarcastically on Soros's "completely harmless habit of installing district attorneys across the U.S.," noting that Politico had documented the practice as early as August 2016.[13]

## June 2023 succession

In June 2023, reporting by [[breitbart|Breitbart]] and other outlets confirmed that Soros, then 92, was handing control of his $25 billion financial empire to his younger son, 37-year-old [[alexander-soros|Alexander "Alex" Soros]], who told the Wall Street Journal he would broaden his father's interests to include voting and abortion rights as well as "gender equality."[14] The elder Soros said of his successor, "We think alike," and separately that "he's earned it."[14] Alex Soros had already visited the [[white-house|White House]] 17 times in little over a year and met with prominent Democrats.[14]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that what had been suspected in the wake of those White House visits was now confirmed, and that the most striking feature of the handover was how public it was: Soros, he argued, is a figure who understands the value of discretion and has conducted most of his "philanthropy" quietly, so the sudden visibility of the operation was itself notable.[14] DeLarme also observed that despite Soros being an admitted atheist, merely naming him had previously been enough to attract accusations of antisemitism.[14] Contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] framed the succession as generational continuity — "like father, like son" — made more concerning by the elder Soros's endorsement, and called for influential globalists in league with what he termed the cabal to be exposed.[14]

## Audacy and media consolidation (2024)

In February 2024, Bloomberg reported that [[soros-fund-management|Soros Fund Management]], the investment firm Soros founded, was positioned to become the biggest shareholder of the radio and podcast company [[audacy|Audacy Inc.]] upon its emergence from bankruptcy, having accumulated more than $400 million of the company's highest-ranking debt for conversion into equity.[16] The transaction was described as the latest in a series of media investments by the fund, which had joined the lender group that bought [[vice-media|Vice Media]] out of bankruptcy in 2023 and had earlier taken a minority stake in the podcast company [[crooked-media|Crooked Media]].[16] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the debt-for-equity swap would give Soros interests direct influence over the conglomerate's business planning and narrative output across demographics ahead of the 2024 US election.[16]
