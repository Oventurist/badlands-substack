# United States Capitol Police

The United States Capitol Police is the federal law enforcement agency responsible for protecting the United States Capitol complex and members of [[congress|Congress]].[1]

## 2026 State of the Union arrest

Following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2026 State of the Union address, Capitol Police arrested a guest whom Rep. [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] had invited to the event.[1] Omar publicly stated that the detention was tied to immigration enforcement concerns and complained about the guest's treatment.[1]

## January 6 excessive force litigation

Participants in the January 6, 2021 Capitol breach sued the federal government in Florida for tens of millions of dollars, alleging that the Capitol Police and the [[metropolitan-police-department-of-the-district-of-columbia|Metropolitan Police Department]] of Washington, DC used rubber bullets, chemical spray, flashbangs, and other crowd-control measures recklessly and against people not engaged in violence.[2] The proposed class action, which would represent dozens of individuals present on the Capitol's West Front, describes a "wanton barrage" of non-lethal munitions and was assigned to US District Judge [[paul-byron|Paul Byron]]; it is detailed at [[jan-6-excessive-force-lawsuit|Jan. 6 excessive force lawsuit]].[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she was gassed by Capitol Police on January 6 while protesting peacefully, asserting that gas was launched away from the violence and toward peaceful protesters with no riot declaration issued.[2]

## 2023 Capitol security footage dispute

In February 2023 the Capitol Police were reported to have been left out of the arrangement under which Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] granted [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] access to thousands of hours of January 6 Capitol security footage.[3] Capitol Police Chief [[thomas-manger|Thomas Manger]] reportedly told associates that he did not learn of the deal until it began circulating publicly on Monday, February 20, 2023.[3] The department had been extremely reluctant to release large swaths of its security footage, citing potential risks to lawmakers, aides and the officers protecting the building.[3] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] noted that airing any of the footage might require approval from the Capitol Police Board, making the distinction between possession and access material; the episode is covered at [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|January 6 Capitol security footage release]].[3]

In footage aired by [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] on Monday, March 6, 2023, Capitol Police officers appeared not to stop leading demonstrator [[jacob-chansley|Jacob Chansley]] on January 6, 2021, but instead to escort him through the Capitol corridors toward the Senate floor, with two officers walking alongside him while he remained unrestrained and un-handcuffed.[4] Carlson said the tapes showed the officers "acted as his tour guides," a characterization Badlands commentary cited as undermining the official insurrection narrative.[4]

## Rushingbrook Children's Choir incident (2023)

In June 2023, Capitol Police, along with a congressional staffer, stopped the [[rushingbrook-childrens-choir|Rushingbrook Children's Choir]] of Greenville, South Carolina from performing a capella renditions of patriotic songs, including the national anthem, inside the Capitol Building.[5] The choir's director, Rasbach, said he was told the performance of national songs constituted a "demonstration" and was therefore not permitted.[5] Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the prohibition "a shame" and invited the choir to perform at his upcoming South Carolina rally, while [[human-events|Human Events]] described the shutdown as "a stain on our national character and the people's House."[5] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] called the Capitol Police account absurd and contradictory and argued the refusal was contrived on political grounds.[5]

## Security posture ahead of the anticipated Trump indictment (March 2023)

In a statement released on the night of Monday, March 20, 2023, the [[senate-sergeant-at-arms|Sergeant at Arms]] said the United States Capitol Police anticipated demonstration activity across the country related to the possible indictment of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[6] While stating that law enforcement was not tracking any specific, credible threats against Capitol or state offices, the statement said there was potential for demonstration activity and that USCP was working with law enforcement partners, so observers might see a greater law enforcement presence on Capitol Hill.[6] The statement listed streets where bike racks would remain open to allow pedestrian transit and noted that the Senate Operations Center and the State Office Readiness Program were monitoring the situation and prepared to share information with the Senate community.[6] Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the scale of the preparations, alongside NYPD barricades in New York, indicated authorities genuinely expected an event, while comparing the visual staging to that surrounding January 6.[6]
