# Michael T. Keilty

Michael T. Keilty is an Assistant Special Counsel who served on Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s team and delivered the prosecution's opening statement at the 2022 trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] in Alexandria, Virginia.[1] He appeared alongside Durham and Department of Justice trial attorney Brittain Shaw, with paralegal specialist Kori Arsenault and [[fbi|FBI]] case agent Ryan James seated at the rear table.[1]

## Opening statement

Keilty framed the case around the materiality of Danchenko's alleged lies, explaining to the jury why the false statements mattered to the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|FISA warrants on Carter Page]] and to the Bureau's investigative decisions.[1] He described the [[danchenko-immunity-agreement|immunity agreement]] presented to Danchenko at the January 2017 meeting, under which Danchenko needed only to be truthful and forthcoming about the information he had supplied for the [[steele-dossier|Steele reports]] and about where he obtained it, and noted that Danchenko was made a Confidential Human Source after those three days of interviews.[1] Keilty acknowledged that Danchenko did provide useful information to the FBI, but said he could not corroborate what he had given [[christopher-steele|Steele]] and lied about his sourcing.[1]

Keilty told the jury that Danchenko had described himself to [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] as "working on a very important project against Donald Trump," that Dolan would testify, and that four FBI agents would give evidence concerning Dolan.[1] He said that had Danchenko not lied about Dolan, the Bureau might have taken steps to interview him — Agent [[kevin-helson|Helson]] had wanted to — but Keilty did not use that observation to excuse the FBI, following it immediately with criticism of the Bureau's own conduct.[1]

## Dispute over the opening

After opening statements, defence counsel [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] accused Keilty of having lied in his opening statement.[1] Durham raised the accusation with [[anthony-trenga|Judge Trenga]] once the jury had been excused; the judge did not accept Onorato's position and refused to entertain it further when pressed.[1]
