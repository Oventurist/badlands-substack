# Meir Kahane

Meir Kahane was an Israeli Knesset member and founder of the militant political movement that bears his name, Kahanism.[1] Badlands commentary describes him as having advocated for the murder of Christians and the burning of churches until his own murder in New York City in 1990.[1]

## Legacy and the Knesset ban

In 1994, in response to Kahane's rhetoric, the Knesset officially banned any member from serving whose political platform championed racial (Jewish) supremacy.[1] According to Badlands commentary, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was forced to repeal that prohibition in 2022 in order to gain enough Knesset votes to remain in office.[1]

## Contemporary disciples

Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] is described as a disciple of Kahane, and Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that Smotrich's March 2025 visit to Washington represented an opportunity for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to expose the Kahanist movement to the American public.[1]

## Criminal record and Knesset career

Kahane was convicted of conducting or planning several terrorist attacks in the 1970s, including a plot to blow up the Iraqi embassy in Washington, D.C.[2] Arrested in Israel in the 1980s, he was convicted of plotting to fly a small plane loaded with explosives into the Dome of the Rock and served six months in jail.[2] The activist group he founded in New York City, the [[jewish-defense-league|Jewish Defense League]], was accused of planning many violent terrorist attacks — including some that were carried out — and was classified as a terrorist organization by law enforcement throughout America before ceasing operations in 2015.[2]

After his release from prison, Kahane was elected to the [[knesset|Knesset]], where he served four years and pushed for Halachic Law, the forced deportation of Arabs, and the banning of marriage and sexual intercourse between Jews and Gentiles.[2] He advocated Halachic Law as effectively the Jewish equivalent of Shariah Law, imbuing rabbinical leaders with lawmaking powers akin to those of Shariah clerics.[2] This body of ideas is treated in the wiki under [[kahanism|Kahanism]].[2]

## Rehabilitation in Israeli politics (2025)

On November 12, 2025, [[likud|Likud]] MK [[nissim-vaturi|Nissim Vaturi]] told the Knesset plenum that "Kahane was right — about many things" and that "Absolutely not, he was not a terrorist," adding that his party had erred in ostracizing him.[2] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] observed that the zealots occupying [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s Security Cabinet are all Kahanists, noting that [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] had served as defense counsel for [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]], Kahane's protégé and political heir, after Gopstein's 2015 arrest for advocating the burning of all churches and the expulsion of Christians from Israel.[2]

## Legacy and the Kahanist movement

Badlands coverage identifies Kahane as the founding leader of what it calls the Israeli "Messianic Movement," succeeded by [[benzion-gopstein|Benzion Gopstein]].[3] Both men have said that Christians are their ancient enemy and have condoned the burning of churches.[3]

Kahane was arrested by the Shin Bet in the 1980s for plotting to blow up the Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem, hoping to start a war with the Arabs, and his political party was banned from the [[knesset|Knesset]] in 1994 for a platform explicitly proclaiming Jewish supremacy over Christians, Muslims, and all others as the foundation of its ideology.[3] To solidify the alliance between [[likud|Likud]] and the [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] ("Jewish Power") party, [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had to support reversing that ban in 2022.[3]

The movement associated with Kahane's legacy is described as seeking to expand Israel's borders under [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]], codify a formal theocracy of radical Jewish supremacy under the [[smotrich-doctrine|Smotrich Doctrine]], and summon the Jewish messiah through [[red-heifer|red heifer]] sacrifice and construction of the [[third-temple|Third Temple]] on the Al-Aqsa site, a project associated with [[temple-institute|The Temple Institute]].[3]
