# Persian Empire

The Persian Empire appears in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s November 2025 essay "Don't Be Symbol-Minded" as the imperial power against which the Ionian city states rebelled in the [[ionian-revolt|Ionian Revolt]] of 499–493 BC.[1] Ehret characterized the empire as the "Marcher Lord of Babylon," the entity which managed the global [[mystery-cults|mystery cults]] of the ancient world.[1]

After the revolt was sabotaged and Anatolia reabsorbed under Persian control, according to Ehret, the [[phrygian-cap|Phrygian cap]] — until then a symbol of freedom and honor — was rebranded through the deities Mithras and Cybele-Attis, which were infused into the governance of the defeated Anatolian and Ionian people.[1] He emphasized that earlier Persian renditions of Mithra, dating from before the cult's infusion into Phrygia roughly a century after 493 BC, featured no Phrygian cap, and that the caps appear on cult iconography only after the republican revolts had been put down.[1] The reworked cults were subsequently imported to Rome via Phrygia during the Second Punic War of 218–201 BC.[1]
