# Elon Musk

**Elon Musk** is the entrepreneur who acquired Twitter, now [[x-twitter|X]], and in Badlands Media's account a figure whose public reputation was inverted by the establishment the moment he moved against the censorship regime.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Reversal of establishment favour

Musk had been revered as a brilliant, quirky entrepreneur and the great tycoon of the modern age; the article describes the establishment turning on him overnight once he announced his plan to buy Twitter.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Beyond attacks from what the author calls former watchdog organisations, a number of formal investigations into Musk and his companies materialised that had not existed before he challenged the censorship regime.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## The ADL campaign

The [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]] mounted an advertiser- and reputation-pressure effort against Musk after he refused to satisfy its demands, a campaign the article treats as part of the same coordinated four-year offensive that produced the [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]] and the [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]] affair.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Position in the information war

The article places X and [[rumble]] together as the frontlines of the information war, arguing that Truth Social, while useful, functions largely as an echo chamber.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The establishment's objectives are described as taking Rumble out and reverting Twitter to its previous state.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Musk's comparatively vast resources are contrasted with those of Rumble's [[chris-pavlovski|Chris Pavlovski]], who is said to be challenging the regime more intensely with far less protection.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## TikTok proposal

In "A New Hollywood," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] notes that while rolling out [[project-stargate|Project Stargate]], [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] suggested that either Musk or [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]] buy [[tiktok|TikTok]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Carlson uses the pairing to argue that Ellison, like Musk, is a major part of Trump's forward plan, and rejects the framing that past tech-sector ties to DARPA or the [[cia|CIA]] make such figures compromised.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## "Prosecute/Fauci"

In December 2022, Musk announced by tweet that his pronouns were "Prosecute/Fauci," a declaration that [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[j-b-white|J.B. White]] read as a signal that accountability for [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] was near.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/anthony-fauci) White paired the remark with the ongoing [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] releases published under Musk's ownership of [[x-twitter|Twitter]], which he described as exposing remarkable wrongdoing by a collection of officials he considered unworthy of American public service.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/anthony-fauci)

## Population collapse

In "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?", the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] cites Musk as being concerned about and highlighting data on population collapse.[4] The reference is offered as one of the essay's "unique" data points in support of the argument that the present era could satisfy the demographic scale of the fourth-seal and sixth-trumpet judgements of [[the-tribulation|the Tribulation]], in which a quarter and then a third of humanity die.[4]

## AI pause letter

Musk was among the signatories of the [[ai-pause-open-letter|open letter published March 22, 2023]] that called on all [[artificial-intelligence|AI]] labs to immediately pause for at least six months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4, alongside [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]], [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]], and [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]].[5] Badlands Media's July 2023 essay on artificial intelligence argues the pause was unworkable and, given the research caveats written into the letter, functionally a restriction on public access rather than on state and defense development.[5]

## Alignment with Trump in 2025

In "America Has a CIA-Run Economy," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] singles out Musk as the most supportive of the big-tech chief executives who have endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] or at least stopped working against him, "both vocally and financially."[6] Carlson groups him with [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], who agreed to open his platforms to more free speech, and [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]], who said the *[[washington-post|Washington Post]]* should be less biased and more trustworthy.[6]

Closing the article, Carlson writes that although he does not expect figures such as Zuckerberg and Bezos to walk away from their alleged crimes entirely unscathed, he will not let his blood boil at the sight of them sitting with Musk at the inauguration — accepting their continued presence as the price of not destroying the American economy.[6]

## DOGE findings and departure

As a special government employee leading [[doge|DOGE]], Musk and his team catalogued what [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] later summarized as systemic federal dysfunction: Treasury payment approval officers instructed to always approve payments with zero career denials, even for known fraudulent or terrorist-linked recipients; budget codes optional or blank for nearly 70% of federal spending (~$4.7 trillion); agencies holding 37,000 software licenses for 13,000 employees; 47 redundant financial systems across 24 major agencies and 111 instances of 59 HR products; retirements hand-processed in a Pennsylvania limestone mine by 700+ workers at roughly 10,000 applications a month; and a website modernization project that consumed over $5 million with no output.[7] Musk remarked that most government systems were "shockingly primitive."[7]

Musk left government service in May 2025 and the temporary DOGE organization was shut down that November, after which the modernization tooling he installed was presumed to continue operating — with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] supplying the human follow-through through the [[war-on-fraud|War on Fraud]].[7]

## Wisconsin election bribery referral (2026)

In July 2026 a panel of the [[wisconsin-elections-commission|Wisconsin Elections Commission]] found probable cause that Musk likely violated Wisconsin's election bribery law by offering $1 million to voters during the 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court race.[8] In a 5-1 vote the commission referred two confidential complaints to the Brown County District Attorney's Office, which will decide whether to pursue criminal charges and has 40 days to report back.[8]

The referral centers on a social media post in which Musk offered $1 million to people who voted in the election, which the commission said was intended to induce voting.[8] Three Wisconsin voters ultimately received $1 million checks, including two presented the money by Musk at a Green Bay rally days before the election.[8] Musk played a major role in the race, with him and affiliated groups spending at least $20 million supporting Republican-backed candidate [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]]; earlier in the campaign his [[america-pac|America PAC]] offered $100 to voters who signed a petition opposing "activist judges" or referred others to sign it.[8]

## Loss in the OpenAI lawsuit (2026)

In May 2026 a federal jury rejected Musk's claims against [[openai|OpenAI]] and chief executive [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] after less than two hours of deliberation.[9] Musk had filed the suit in 2024, alleging that Altman and OpenAI abandoned their founding nonprofit mission after Musk co-founded the company in 2015 and left its board in 2018, and had sought as much as $134 billion in ill-gotten gains, the removal of Altman and president [[greg-brockman|Greg Brockman]], and reversal of OpenAI's 2025 restructuring, arguing any funds should go to "the OpenAI charity."[9] U.S. District Judge [[yvonne-gonzalez-rogers|Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers]] adopted the advisory jury's finding of no liability, dismissed the breach-of-charitable-trust and unjust-enrichment claims as untimely, and dismissed the related claims against [[microsoft|Microsoft]]; the case is covered at [[musk-v-openai|Musk v. OpenAI]].[9]

## January 2026 reconciliation with Trump

Writing in mid-January 2026, Badlands analyst [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the 2025 Trump–Musk "feud" — punctuated by public resignations, pointed social-media posts and a period of short-lived distance — as a deliberate "Narrative Pincer" designed to provoke enemy overreach and seed controlled tension rather than a genuine rupture.[10] In that reading, the falling-out was always intended to end in a "performative reconciliation" that would amplify the [[doge|DOGE]] deployment which had defined the two men's convergence in the first place.[10] By January 2026 the re-convergence was unfolding publicly through Mar-a-Lago dinners and viral photo opportunities, with Musk describing a "lovely evening" spent with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the First Lady and proclaiming that "2026 is going to be amazing."[10]

## Space Force contract and the Musk–Trump convergence (May 2026)

In May 2026 the [[united-states-space-force|US Space Force]] awarded Musk's [[spacex|SpaceX]] a $2.29 billion contract to build the [[space-data-network-backbone|Space Data Network Backbone]], a laser-linked low-Earth orbit constellation for secure, high-speed military data movement.[11] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] presented the award as the latest confirmation that the domestic alliance between Musk and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lives in contracts, integrations and partnerships between seemingly unrelated entities.[11]

In that analysis, Musk's projects viewed holistically constitute the practical load-bearing structure for the Golden Age transition Trump has described: emergent energy systems designed to move beyond engineered scarcity toward genuine abundance, and autonomy in vehicles, robotics and logistics that multiplies productive capacity rather than displacing it.[11] Transportation, communications and computing power are named as the material foundation without which rhetoric about American renewal remains aspirational.[11] Musk's recently detailed Master Plan — centered on sustainable abundance through integrated AI, energy density, automation and next-generation platforms — is said to mirror Trump's Golden Age promises directly, which Burning Bright offers as an explanation for why both men have been demonized and mocked.[11] He further argued that speculation about eventual consolidation between Musk's two core entities, Tesla and SpaceX, is not merely financial rumor but the logical extension of synergies compounding for years across balance sheets, technology, personnel and mission statements.[11]

## Gender medicine litigation comments (2026)

In February 2026 Musk said on X that U.S. doctors who perform gender transition procedures on minors could face "thousands" of lawsuits, equating such doctors to "modern day Mengeles."[12] His remarks followed a jury verdict in a malpractice suit brought by [[fox-varian|Fox Varian]], who underwent a double mastectomy at age 16 and later said she was compelled to have the surgery while still a minor; the jury found both a psychologist and a surgeon liable, awarding $1.6 million for pain and suffering and $400,000 for future medical costs.[12] Musk also criticized schools, mental-health professionals and state officials for facilitating such procedures, in a context where the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had rolled back federal support for gender transition procedures for those under 19 and several states had moved to restrict them.[12]

## Tucker Carlson interview on Google and AI (April 2023)

In a long-anticipated sit-down interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] aired in April 2023, Musk said that [[google|Google]] co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]] had once told him he planned to build a "[[digital-god|digital god]]."[13] Musk recounted that he used to stay at Page's house in Palo Alto, California, and frequently warned him about the dangers of [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]], but that Page did not heed the warnings and instead called Musk a "speciesist."[13] Musk characterized Google's end game as creating a "digital super intelligence."[13]

## DOGE campaign, February 2025

In February 2025 Musk led the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] through a rapid campaign across federal agencies that drew a lawsuit from Democrat-led states seeking to bar him and his allies from accessing data or causing firings.[14] Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]] appeared unwilling to grant that immediate restriction, though she called DOGE's secrecy troubling and its pace unpredictable.[14]

Musk turned next to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], welcomed by Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] into a department whose accounting system has never passed an audit and whose $850 billion budget conceals decades of contractor price gouging.[14] On February 17 he signaled DOGE would look for the gold at [[fort-knox|Fort Knox]], writing that "that gold is owned by the American public! We want to know if it's still there," and drawing support from Senators [[mike-lee|Mike Lee]] and [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]].[14] He also posted that 400 million Americans appeared eligible for [[social-security-administration|Social Security]] despite a national population near 340 million.[14]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cast Musk as a cypher through which an inverted [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] is run in accelerated form, with Musk and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as "the two biggest Mind-Movers on the game board" taking alternating swings at seizing the narrative as the exposure op becomes indistinguishable from a disclosure op.[14]

## Defense of Trump in the "bloodbath" media episode (March 2024)

In March 2024, after corporate outlets and Biden/Harris campaign accounts recast a passage of a [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] speech as a promise of a "bloodbath," Musk publicly responded that "Legacy media lies."[15] MSNBC's Joe Scarborough subsequently deleted his own "Bloodbath" post on X after being called out by Musk, one of a series of walkbacks that followed the story's collapse.[15] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the exchange as evidence both that exposure of media operations had accelerated to a matter of minutes and that Trump and Musk were coordinating their messaging.[15]

## Ukrainian oligarch sanctions proposal (March 2025)

In March 2025 Musk suggested on X that sanctioning Ukraine's top ten oligarchs could bring a swift resolution to the conflict with Russia, writing: "Place sanctions on the top 10 Ukrainian oligarchs, especially the ones with mansions in Monaco, and this will stop immediately. That is the key to the puzzle."[16] The post responded to Senator [[mike-lee|Mike Lee]]'s call for the United States to halt financial aid to Kiev, and Musk did not elaborate on how such a move would affect the war.[16] Musk, who leads the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]], has frequently criticized US support for Kiev while advocating reduced federal spending on foreign assistance generally.[16] The oligarchs named in the reporting included [[rinat-akhmetov|Rinat Akhmetov]], [[viktor-pinchuk|Viktor Pinchuk]], [[petro-poroshenko|Petro Poroshenko]], [[igor-kolomoisky|Igor Kolomoisky]] and [[konstantin-zhevago|Konstantin Zhevago]].[16]

## X cyberattack (March 2025)

Musk pointed the finger at Ukraine over a major cyberattack on [[x-corp|X]] that caused at least three outages reported by tens of thousands of users across a Monday in March 2025.[16] The pro-Palestinian hacker group [[dark-storm-team|Dark Storm Team]] claimed responsibility for a DDoS attack in a public Telegram post, but Musk suggested a "large, coordinated group and/or a country is involved," noting that "we get attacked every day, but this was done with a lot of resources," and later told Larry Kudlow on Fox News that the hackers had "IP addresses originating in the Ukraine area."[16]

## DOGE and USAID cancellations (March 2025)

Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] publicly thanked DOGE, headed by Musk, for implementing "overdue and historic reform" when announcing the cancellation of 83% of US foreign aid contracts in March 2025.[16]

## BBC interview (April 2023)

Less than a week after Twitter applied a [[government-funded-media-label|"government funded media" label]] to the [[bbc|BBC]], Musk gave an impromptu interview to the broadcaster at Twitter headquarters in April 2023, discussing layoffs, misinformation, his work habits, and the return of advertisers who had left the platform.[17] The exchange became widely circulated when reporter [[james-clayton|James Clayton]] claimed there was far more [[hate-speech|hate speech]] on Twitter since the takeover and Musk demanded a single example.[17] Clayton conceded he had not used the relevant feed in three or four weeks, drawing Musk's response: "You said you've seen more hateful content but you can't name a single example. Not even one ... That's false. You just lied!"[17]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that Musk had again "schooled" fake news media and that the interview modeled critical thinking — entertaining a claim on its face, requesting supporting evidence, and judging the mismatch.[17] Deschamps raised the recurring question of whether Musk should be trusted, and answered it by shifting from character to observable effect: in this case Musk exposed a reporter's unsupported claim, demonstrated what benevolent confrontation looks like, and highlighted that no clear definition of hate speech exists.[17]

## Zuckerberg cage match challenge (2023)

In June 2023, replying to a user discussing [[meta-platforms|Meta]]'s plans to build a [[twitter|Twitter]] rival, Musk challenged Meta founder [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] to a "cage match," and Zuckerberg responded on Instagram with "Send me location."[18] Musk suggested the "Vegas Octagon" — apparently the UFC Apex in Las Vegas — as the venue, joked that he had a move called "the Walrus" in which he lies on top of an opponent and does nothing, and wrote that he would go through with it "if this is for real."[18] A Meta spokesperson told CBS News only that "the story speaks for itself."[18]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] contrasted the two men, describing Musk as a builder of successful businesses, a rabble-rouser and memester who spent billions buying Twitter as a self-described "free speech absolutist," helped expose collusion between big tech and the shadow government, and carried a sink into Twitter headquarters as a gesture of defiance.[18]

## Snopes Starlink fact check (2023)

On June 20, 2023, the fact-checking site [[snopes|Snopes]] published an article implying that the missing [[oceangate|OceanGate]] Titanic submersible had lost contact because of a failure of Musk's [[starlink|Starlink]] service.[18] A Twitter community note corrected the claim, noting that OceanGate used Starlink for its surface vessels and that such radio frequencies do not work underwater; Snopes revised the piece, downgraded its rating to "Unproven" and then to "False."[18] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the episode as a failed attempt to demean Musk.[18]

## "Thermonuclear lawsuit" against Media Matters (November 2023)

Early on Saturday, November 18, 2023, Musk posted that [[x-corp|X]] would be "filing a thermonuclear lawsuit" against the left-leaning non-profit [[media-matters|Media Matters]] and "all those who colluded" for "completely misrepresenting" the real user experience on the platform.[19] He said the suit would be filed the following Monday, prompting widespread skepticism among pro-censorship critics, including one CNBC reporter who suggested "Musk may have been lying, like he has done before."[19] The suit was in fact filed on November 20 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Fort Worth Division, alleging that Media Matters' tactics were manipulative and deceptive.[19] Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] separately opened an investigation into the organization for potential fraudulent activity the same day.[19]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the filings as a "lawsuit cascade" in the current stage of the [[information-war|Info War]] and called Musk "an enigmatic figure among Anons and the public at large" who was nonetheless "standing in the breach against the Deep State's Narrative power projection arm."[19] He argued the suits were the beginning of a template-setting campaign to go "scorched earth" against those who had assaulted the constitutional right to free speech, fought simultaneously in court and in the court of public opinion ahead of the 2024 election.[19]

## H-1B controversy (December 2024)

At the end of December 2024, Musk — one of the twin heads of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] alongside [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] — appeared to call for expansion of the [[h-1b-visa-program|H-1B visa program]], touching off a week of public infighting within Trump World that columnist David Marcus characterized as a MAGA "civil war."[20] Ranged against them were [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and prominent America Firsters who all but demanded an end to the skilled foreign worker program.[20] Tensions eased within days, with President-elect [[donald-trump|Trump]] saying he likes the program and uses it himself while declining to endorse expansion.[20] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] concluded that "globalists don’t stop being globalists when they ‘win’ an ‘election’" and that "you can’t have ‘America First Globalism.’"[20]

## Proposed as recipient of the January 6 footage

## Banking ambitions: X.com, PayPal and the "people's financial institution"

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] devoted a March 2023 article to Musk's role in what he called a coming revolution in banking, tracing it to Musk's earliest venture in financial services.[160] Before [[paypal|PayPal]], Musk in 1999 created an online bank called [[x-com|X.com]], described by CBS Market Watch as marrying banking and mutual fund management and taking just six months to develop, with a no-load, no-cost S&P 500 index fund that actually paid shareholders a small amount to participate.[160] Musk told the outlet that after selling his previous company to Compaq in April 1999 he judged the internet had reached a "third stage" in which "people are ready to use the internet as their main financial repository."[160]

## The Brazilian ban on X and Musk's capitulation (2024)

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] recounted that in August 2024 Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] issued censorship orders to Elon Musk's [[x-corp|X]], warning that non-compliance would result in the arrest of X's Brazilian executives; Musk, unable to guarantee their safety, closed all of the company's offices in the country.[169] Moraes secured the votes of four fellow justices of the [[supremo-tribunal-federal|Supremo Tribunal Federal]] to ban X from [[brazil|Brazil]] entirely and to criminalize use of the platform, fining citizens who accessed it via VPN $8,900 per day — a sum exceeding the median annual income of most Brazilians.[169]

Musk was livid, publicly criticizing Moraes and suggesting he deserved prison time, and the quarrel prompted a major debate in Brazil over free speech and the limits of online discourse.[169] DeLarme noted that Musk had acquired the platform for $44 billion using credit from major banks and financial institutions rather than personal funds alone, and that X's valuation had since fallen by perhaps 50% or more, partly because of his commitment to free speech and the resulting advertiser exodus driven by fear of the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] and similar organizations.[169] Moraes also imposed millions of dollars in fines and froze the bank accounts of [[starlink|Starlink]] — an entity under [[spacex|SpaceX]], legally separate from X with different shareholders — seizing funds into Brazilian coffers despite Starlink's popularity for providing free internet service in the Amazon and other unconnected regions.[169]

Under pressure from institutional investors in both companies, Musk removed his employees and assets from Brazil and then, over the following weeks, capitulated — complying with every requirement he had denounced and censoring the accounts Moraes had ordered banned, including those of elected officials.[169] DeLarme argued that Musk's capitulation, coming after Durov's, would embolden the international censorship regime to encroach further, while still describing X as an indispensable tool where the bulk of online debate, activism and journalism take place.[169]
