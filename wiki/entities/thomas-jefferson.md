# Thomas Jefferson

Thomas Jefferson was the third president of the United States and, in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the leader of the southern party then called the Democratic Party, which Ehret identifies with the interests of the slaveholding states.[1]

## Role in the article's argument

[[robert-livingston|Robert Livingston]] served as US ambassador to France during Jefferson's first term while acting as a leader of the Federalist Party.[1] Ehret argues in his book *The Clash of the Two Americas: The Unfinished Symphony* that the Anglophile Federalist faction and the Jeffersonian Democratic Party functioned as a controlled gang-counter-gang operation: both shared anti-constitutional views, both denied the proposition that "All Men Are Created Equal," and both were used by British intelligence to undermine the better interests of the young nation.[1]

## Warning about a paper money system

Badlands Media's March 2023 analysis of the American monetary system cites Jefferson's writings as a warning that agents of the Crown plotted to recapture the United States after the Revolutionary War by means of a paper system of "money," the effect of which would "unite the destinies" of the United States and Great Britain once again under a [[bastard-system-of-laws|"bastard system of laws"]].[2] Jefferson specifically warned, according to the article, that Crown agents had infiltrated the federalists and would use federalism to assimilate the governments of the United States and England.[2]

The article treats Jefferson's choice of the word "bastard" as deliberate, taking it to denote a blending of previously separate systems of law, and argues the plot largely succeeded — noting that four of the first five [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] justices were trained at [[middle-temple|the Middle Temple]] in London and that Delaware still practices eighteenth-century English equity law.[2] On this reading, the Jeffersonian warning is the key to understanding how [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]], a private credit issuer, became America's central bank.[2]
