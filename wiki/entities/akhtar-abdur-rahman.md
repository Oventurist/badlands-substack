# Akhtar Abdur Rahman

Lieutenant General Akhtar Abdur Rahman was the director of Pakistan's [[inter-services-intelligence|Inter-Services Intelligence]] (ISI) during the [[soviet-afghan-war|Soviet–Afghan War]].[1]

## Handling of CIA and Saudi cash

According to WhoWhatWhy material quoted by Badlands Media, [[turki-al-faisal|Prince Turki al-Faisal]] distributed more than $1 billion in cash to Afghan guerrillas, matched by another billion from the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]; because the Saudis provided funding, the administration was able to bypass Congress.[1]

Accessing the CIA money was relatively easy. Bags of dollar bills were flown into [[pakistan|Pakistan]] and handed over to Rahman, who banked the cash in ISI accounts held by the National Bank of Pakistan, the Pakistan-controlled [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]], and the Bank of Oman, one-third owned by BCCI.[1]

## Significance

Rahman's position made him the operational hinge of the arms and money pipeline to the [[mujahideen|mujahideen]]. Badlands Media, citing Fair.org, notes that BCCI was also a major channel for drug money laundered by corrupt Pakistani military officers who administered the CIA's arms pipeline to the Afghan rebels while supplying much of the heroin that inundated the United States and Europe during the 1980s.[1]
