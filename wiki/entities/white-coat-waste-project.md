# White Coat Waste Project

The White Coat Waste Project is an American taxpayer watchdog and animal-advocacy organization that campaigns against government-funded animal experimentation. It is described in Badlands Media coverage as one of the two principal groups — alongside People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals — whose long-running opposition to federally financed animal testing was vindicated by a series of grant cancellations under the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Role in the 2025 research-grant cancellations

In late May 2025, Badlands Media relayed reporting from the *Washington Examiner* describing a "Making America Greater for Animals" movement crediting President Trump with ending a set of experiments on monkeys, dogs, and cats.[1] Among the actions cited, the U.S. Navy ended testing on dogs and cats following a sustained campaign by the White Coat Waste Project; the specific target of that campaign was a $10 million Navy contract for experiments on cats.[1]

The same round of cancellations included the termination of funding for research grants at [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] that supported testing on infant monkeys taken from their mothers, including experiments in which the eyes of young monkeys were sewn shut to study the effects on developing brains.[1] [[peta|PETA]] senior vice president Kathy Guillermo publicly applauded the decision.[1]

## Significance

The White Coat Waste Project's success in the Navy case is presented in the Badlands framing as an example of a fiscally oriented watchdog operating outside conventional partisan animal-rights politics: its argument is framed in terms of wasteful taxpayer spending rather than purely ethical objection, which allowed it to find purchase with an administration otherwise focused on cutting federal expenditures.[1] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] treated the resulting news cycle — headlined "Trump Cheered as the Best Friend of Animals" — as a comic artifact of the era.[1]
