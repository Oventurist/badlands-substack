# Department of War

The Department of War (DOW) is the renamed United States military department under the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, successor in function to the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] and headquartered at the Pentagon.[1]

## Cartel drone interdiction near El Paso (February 2026)

In February 2026 the Trump Administration confirmed that cartel-linked drones had entered US airspace near El Paso, Texas, and that they were disabled through joint action by the Department of War and the [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]].[1] Transportation Secretary [[sean-duffy|Sean Duffy]] announced that "The FAA and DOW acted swiftly to address a cartel drone incursion. The threat has been neutralized."[1]

*Business Insider* cited anonymous officials saying counter-drone measures were used to disable the aircraft, but the department declined to publicly specify the technology involved.[1] Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] denied that any drone incursion had occurred, while Representative Tony Gonzales, whose district spans the border, said that "For those of us who live and work along the border, daily drone incursions by criminal organizations are part of everyday life. It's a Wednesday for us."[1]

Badlands commentary linked the episode to the wider question of whether the concurrent military buildup in the eastern Mediterranean was a "head-fake" masking a Western Hemisphere focus on cartels, noting Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s recent visit to Washington and his pledge to work with Trump against cartels and the bankers financing them.[1]

## 260th Special Purpose Brigade directive

A directive from the Department of War, issued alongside [[executive-order-14339|Executive Order 14339]], called for a trained and equipped National Guard unit to support public safety in the nation's capital and integrate with federal and local security partners.[2] The resulting [[260th-special-purpose-brigade|260th Special Purpose Brigade]] was activated by the [[district-of-columbia-national-guard|District of Columbia National Guard]] in March 2026 as the Army's only brigade dedicated to coordinating military support to civil authorities and protecting critical infrastructure in Washington, DC.[2]

## Dispute with Anthropic over military AI (2026)

The Department of War has been expanding its use of artificial intelligence in intelligence analysis, cyber operations, logistics, and battlefield planning, creating a growing market for firms that can supply advanced software to military and national-security agencies.[3] In March 2026 its dispute with AI developer [[anthropic|Anthropic]] intensified after a leaked internal memo from chief executive [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] detailed the company's objections to certain military uses of its systems, including "red lines" against autonomous weapons and certain surveillance functions.[3]

Anthropic had previously worked with the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] under an AI contract valued at roughly $200 million, and Pentagon officials allegedly threatened to cancel or reconsider it if the restrictions were maintained.[3] The DoW subsequently designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk," a classification that can restrict a company's ability to participate in government contracts, after which Amodei said his company was attempting to "de-escalate."[3] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the department was demanding unchecked war applications of AI and treating dissent as a threat to national security.[3]

## UAP disclosure portal (May 2026)

On May 8, 2026 the Department of War announced the initial release of new files on unidentified anomalous phenomena through the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters, known as [[pursue|PURSUE]].[4] The department said the effort includes the White House, the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|ODNI]], the Department of Energy, the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, [[nasa|NASA]], the [[fbi|FBI]] and other intelligence components, and that the records would be housed at WAR.GOV/UFO with additional files released on a rolling basis.[4] It said the files were reviewed for security purposes while many materials had not yet been analyzed for resolution of anomalies; the portal listed Release 01 as cleared for release May 8, 2026, and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] reported the launch the same day.[4]

## Military pay during the October 2025 appropriations lapse

During the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 lapse in annual appropriations]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued [[national-security-presidential-memorandum-8|National Security Presidential Memorandum 8]], directing the Secretary of War, in coordination with the Director of the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]], to use any funds appropriated by [[congress|Congress]] that remained available for expenditure in Fiscal Year 2026 to pay active-duty personnel and Reserve component personnel who had performed active service during the relevant pay period.[5] The memorandum specified that funds used should be those the Secretary of War determined were provided for purposes bearing "a reasonable, logical relationship" to military pay and allowances, consistent with applicable law including 31 U.S.C. 1301(a).[5] It further instructed that at the conclusion of the lapse every effort be made, as authorized by law, to adjust funding accounts within the department so that operations and activities could continue consistent with pre-lapse planned expenditures.[5]

## Nigeria contingency preparations (November 2025)

In a Truth Social post on November 1, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] instructed the Department of War to prepare for possible military action in [[nigeria|Nigeria]], accusing the West African country of allowing Islamist militants to kill Christians and warning that any attack would be "fast, vicious, and sweet."[6] Department head [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] responded on X that the department "is preparing for action."[6]

## September 2025 rebrand friction

Officials at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] grappled in early September 2025 with implementing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order remolding the department into the Department of War, an undertaking [[politico|Politico]] described as Herculean.[7] Many expressed frustration, anger and confusion, arguing the effort could cost billions of dollars for a cosmetic change that would do little to address the military's most pressing challenges, such as countering a more aggressive alliance of authoritarian nations.[7] Details of the order remained vague, but officials anticipated changing [[department-of-defense|Defense Department]] seals at more than 700,000 facilities in 40 countries and all 50 states, covering letterhead for six military branches and dozens of agencies, embossed napkins in chow halls, embroidered jackets for Senate-confirmed officials, and merchandise in the Pentagon store.[7] "This is purely for domestic political audiences," a former defense official said, arguing it would have zero impact on Chinese or Russian calculations while being used by adversaries to portray the United States as warmongering.[7]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] countered that the Department of War was established by [[george-washington|Washington]] in 1789, during an era in which the United States did not fight forever wars or act as the world's police, and became the Department of Defense in 1949 when Truman, in her framing, "enabled the Prussian infiltration of our nation."[7] She suggested Colorado senator and former governor [[john-hickenlooper|John Hickenlooper]] either did not know that history or was angered that the infiltration was ending, adding simply: "Accelerate."[7]

## Internet content blocking instruction and Scouting America memo (November 2025)

On November 18, 2025 — the same day six Democratic lawmakers released the [[seditious-six-video|"Seditious Six" video]] — the department issued [[dodi-8025-01|DoDI 8025.01, "DoD Internet Content Blocking"]], whose details were not publicly available; Badlands writer Jon Herold suggested the timing linked it to the video.[8] Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] denounced the video as a "politically-motivated influence operation" that injected doubt into the armed forces.[8] In the same period, a reported Hegseth memo sought to cut the military's ties with [[scouting-america|Scouting America]] over its diversity, equity and inclusion programming, drawing a statement from the organization noting the military's support for the Scouts since 1937.[8]
