# Associated Press

The Associated Press (AP) is an American wire service whose coverage is frequently cited — and criticized — in the *Badlands News Brief*.[1]

In August 2024 the AP published a retrospective reporting that a year after a Georgia grand jury accused [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and others of illegally trying to overturn the 2020 presidential election in the state, the case had stalled with no chance of reaching trial before the end of the year.[1] The AP recounted that Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] had secured the indictment using Georgia's anti-racketeering law against Trump and 18 others, that it was the fourth and most sprawling of the criminal cases against the former president, and that allegations raised by a co-defendant had caused delays that could derail the prosecution.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the piece as the AP "lamenting the impending death of Fani's RICO," arguing that the absence of actual news in the story — the anniversary itself being the hook — demonstrated that the case was stalled indefinitely.[1]

## Coverage of Trump's Nashville bitcoin speech (July 2024)

The Associated Press reported [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s July 27, 2024 keynote at the [[bitcoin-2024-nashville-conference|Bitcoin 2024 conference]] in Nashville, in which he told hundreds of cheering cryptocurrency supporters that he wanted the United States to be a "bitcoin superpower" and the "crypto capital of the planet" under his leadership.[2] The AP account documented his promises to create a bitcoin "strategic reserve" from government holdings, to remove [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] chair [[gary-gensler|Gary Gensler]], and to implement a crypto advisory council, along with his line that "the rules will be written by people who love your industry, not hate your industry."[2]

## New Mexico firearms order coverage

AP News supplied the September 2023 report on New Mexico Governor [[michelle-lujan-grisham|Michelle Lujan Grisham]]'s emergency order suspending public firearms carry in Albuquerque and Bernalillo County, including her acknowledgment that she expected legal challenges, the statement that state police would enforce what amounted to civil violations, Albuquerque police chief [[harold-medina|Harold Medina]]'s refusal to enforce, and Bernalillo County Sheriff [[john-allen-sheriff|John Allen]]'s stated unease about constitutional rights.[3]

## Coverage of Trump's June 2024 Capitol Hill visit

The AP's report on [[2024-trump-return-to-capitol-hill|Donald Trump's return to Capitol Hill]] on June 13, 2024 became a case study for Badlands critics of the wire service.[4] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the piece opened by calling the visit a "triumphant return" while describing it in the same sentence as Trump's first since sending the mob to "fight like hell," a juxtaposition she called schizophrenic paranoia rather than snark.[4] She noted the sourcing imbalance — several supportive Republicans quoted against two Democrats, only one of them a relevant legislator — and disputed the article's assertions that Trump had purged the GOP of critics and silenced most skeptics, arguing voters removed the critics and that the AP itself has never stopped publishing warnings about him.[4] She emphasized that the article ran under the "Politics" category rather than "Opinion," a few paragraphs above the wire service's claim to be "the most trusted source of information on election night, with a history of accuracy dating to 1848."[4]
