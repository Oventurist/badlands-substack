# Colorado Department of Corrections

The Colorado Department of Corrections (CDOC) is the state agency responsible for the custody of persons convicted under Colorado state law. In December 2025 the agency became a focal point of the dispute over the imprisonment of former Mesa County clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1]

## Redacted transfer order

A November 12 order concerning the transfer of Peters was released by the CDOC to news organizations in heavily redacted form.[1] An unredacted version that later circulated showed what commentators described as a benign document, with the redacted passage covering the paragraph citing the federal government's stated legal authority for the transfer request.[1] Kyle Clark of Colorado NBC affiliate 9News confirmed that the paragraph in question had been redacted in the copy his newsroom received from the department.[1]

The pollster [[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] amplified the discrepancy on X, asking what federal officials had told Colorado officials about Colorado elections that "suddenly now has to be kept secret."[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] filed open-records requests seeking the cited authority after being unable to locate it publicly, arguing that the decision to redact precisely that citation suggested the underlying federal claim had merit.[1]

## Federal investigation

Earlier the same week, the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] announced it was investigating complaints about conditions within the Colorado correctional system, adding a second front to what Badlands commentary described as a "Rocky Mountain Showdown" between federal authorities and Colorado's Democratic state government.[1]

## DEIB office and denial of media access (December 2025)

In December 2025 [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the head of "diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging" at the department - a title she noted includes the word "belonging" - denied her request to see [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], and doubled down after she pushed back on her treatment as a member of the media.[2] She argued the episode showed [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]] was not only alive and well in Colorado but "mutating again" into DEIB, and questioned why "belonging" was being pushed inside a prison system.[2]

## Federal investigation, December 2025

On December 8, 2025 the [[united-states-department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] announced an investigation into 33 facilities operated by the Colorado Department of Corrections and the state's Department of Youth Services.[3] [[disability-law-colorado|Disability Law Colorado]] had spent years urging the DOJ to examine the system over alleged "systematic failures," though *Westword* reported the probe appeared politically motivated, tied to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to free former Mesa County Clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]; Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]], who announced the probe, reshared posts calling it "how we FREE TINA PETERS."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that sources close to the matter described staff at the La Vista Correctional Facility, where Peters is held, hurrying to clean the place up, and circulated a photograph of degraded air handlers at the CDOC mental health hospital in the same Pueblo government complex, alleging similar conditions across most buildings on the campus.[3] Local disability advocates nonetheless called the federal intervention "an important step toward accountability."[3]
