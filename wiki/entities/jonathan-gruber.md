# Jonathan Gruber

Jonathan Gruber is an MIT economist who served as a major technical consultant to the Obama administration and [[us-congress|Congress]] during the drafting of the [[affordable-care-act|Affordable Care Act]], and who has been referred to as "the architect of Obamacare."[1]

## The transparency remarks

Gruber became a focus of criticism after recorded remarks in which he described how the law was deliberately structured to obscure its true operation.[1] According to *Medical Economics*, Gruber said: "[The ACA] was written in a tortured way to make sure [the Congressional Budget Office] did not score the mandate as taxes. If CBO scores the mandate as taxes, the bill dies. OK? So it's written to do that. In terms of risk-rated subsidies, if you had a law which said healthy people are going to pay in—you made [it] explicit that healthy people pay in and sick people get money; it would not have passed. OK? Lack of transparency is a huge political advantage. And basically, call it the stupidity of the American voter or whatever, but basically that was really, really critical to get the thing to pass."[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentary treats the Gruber remarks as an admission that the ACA was a fraud from the beginning, made while bragging to a left-wing audience by a speaker who never expected the recording to reach the public.[1] The article argues the quotation reveals a governing assumption among the law's designers that the American people are stupid and voters are easily fooled — the same assumption it attributes to [[john-mccain|John McCain]] when he voted against repealing the law after campaigning on repeal.[1] The same account links the law's design to subsequent large-scale fraud, citing a Paragon Health Institute estimate of $15–20 billion in fraudulent exchange enrollment in 2024.[1]
