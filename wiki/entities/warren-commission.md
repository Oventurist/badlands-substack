# Warren Commission

The Warren Commission was the federal body that investigated the 1963 assassination of President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]]. It established that two shots fired by a lone gunman, [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]], from the sixth floor of the Texas School Book Depository struck the president from behind as his motorcade passed through Dallas.[1]

## Official findings

According to the commission, one bullet entered Kennedy's upper back and exited near his larynx, while a second entered the right side of his head and exited through his forehead.[1] Kennedy was still breathing when he arrived at Parkland Hospital and was pronounced dead roughly half an hour later.[1]

## Challenges from Parkland physicians

The commission's conclusions were publicly disputed by several of the emergency-room doctors who treated Kennedy at Parkland. In interviews conducted in 2013 and released in the documentary "JFK: What the Doctors Saw," seven Parkland physicians said the president's appearance at the hospital did not match the autopsy photographs later taken at Bethesda.[1] Those who observed the neck wound said they believed it was an entrance wound — a finding inconsistent with the commission's shots-from-behind account — and several described a gaping hole in the back of Kennedy's head.[1] Executive producer Jacquelynn Lueth said the doctors' recollections "were precise and clear, as if the intervening decades had melted away," and that they "had no agenda other than trying to save the president's life."[1]

## Commentary
