# Mike Lindell

Mike Lindell is an American businessman and prominent advocate of claims that the 2020 US presidential election was compromised by fraud. In Badlands coverage he is discussed chiefly through his long association with attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]], who served as Lindell's lawyer during the years between the two [[donald-trump|Trump]] administrations and pursued election-fraud evidence on his behalf across multiple states.[1]

## Role in the election-integrity movement

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Olsen "spent the time between Trump Administrations as Mike Lindell's attorney, and he went everywhere the evidence was," framing Lindell as the financier and organizer behind much of the private investigative effort into fake voters, fake ballots and fake counts.[1]

## January 2021 photo op

Ashe noted that she had long assumed Olsen's entry into the election fight was marked by Lindell's "unfortunate photo op" of January 15, 2021, in which documents visible under Lindell's arm outside the White House listed Olsen's name. That assumption was complicated by [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]]'s account that Byrne and Olsen had already agreed in December 2020 to take election fraud "to the firing squad," predating the photograph. Ashe added that she does not recall where Lindell obtained the papers or who placed Olsen's name on them, calling the question "important."[1]

## Conclusion of the Dominion litigation (2026)

The defamation litigation between Lindell and [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] ended in June 2026 when the case was dismissed with prejudice following the company's acquisition and rebranding.[2] Dominion had sued Lindell and [[mypillow|My Pillow, Inc.]] in February 2021 for $1.3 billion, accusing him of repeatedly and falsely claiming that the company rigged the 2020 presidential election.[2] Lindell countersued Dominion and added [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] as a third-party defendant, but those counterclaims were largely unsuccessful; Smartmatic obtained sanctions against him for frivolous filings, with fines exceeding $92,000 by early June 2026 after he failed to pay.[2]

After former Republican election official [[scott-leiendecker|Scott Leiendecker]] acquired Dominion in October 2025 and rebranded it as [[liberty-vote|Liberty Vote]], he chose not to pursue further recovery from Lindell.[2] Liberty Vote Holdings, My Pillow, a Dominion subsidiary, and Lindell filed a joint stipulation in federal court in Washington, D.C., dismissing all claims with prejudice, with each party bearing its own attorneys' fees and costs; Smartmatic was not a party to the agreement.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the most apparent change was that the company would no longer pursue a cent from Lindell or his once-profitable bedding company.[2]

## Coomer defamation trial (2025)

Lindell, [[mypillow|MyPillow]] and [[frankspeech|FrankSpeech]] were defendants in the defamation trial brought by former Dominion Voting Systems executive [[eric-coomer|Eric Coomer]] in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado, which entered its second week in June 2025 before Judge Nina Y. Wang.[3] Lindell was scheduled to testify on the Monday opening that week, alongside his former employee Brannon Howse and video-deposition clips from Dennis Montgomery covering PCAPs, China, and Hammer and Scorecard; the Clerk of the Court reopened the courthouse overflow room in anticipation of public interest.[3] Separately, Lindell was served in the Colorado courtroom on the preceding Friday in connection with his long-running litigation with FedEx, with the process server asking to serve him outside the view of the media.[3] See [[coomer-v-lindell|Coomer v. Lindell]].[3]

Taking the stand in the trial's third week, Lindell testified that he had called "50 to 100 people traitors," a moment recorded in courtroom sketches aired by Denver station 9News.[4] The Wednesday session moved in fits and starts as his defense proffered video clips from *Absolute Proof* and *Kill Chain*, which the court said it would rule on individually the following morning, when Lindell was expected to testify for a third consecutive day.[4]

By Thursday, June 12, 2025, Coomer had rested his case and Lindell's defense came close to resting, with his attorneys asking to call one additional witness before the case went to the jury.[7] In an interview that day, [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] told Lindell that mainstream coverage of him was "universally horrible" and that reporters said he was "getting blown out" and that the jury "will not be out long to rule against you."[7] Lindell replied that "If we lose this, Steve, I really believe it's how, you know, anyone could get sued at any time with talking about anything that they decide that we don't want to hear from you," and said he hoped God and prayers would produce a favorable verdict.[7] Jury instructions and closing arguments were expected the following morning.[7]

## Reaction to the Totenberg ruling (2023)

In November 2023, Lindell promised that a new ruling in Georgia would "expose everything" and vindicate those who had made claims about election fraud.[6] Speaking to [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] on Bannon's *War Room* podcast, he said, "This is going to expose everything. The judge has opened the door that no man can shut."[6] The ruling in question came from District Judge [[amy-totenberg|Amy Totenberg]], an Obama appointee, who granted a bench trial in the long-running suit seeking to replace Georgia's electronic voting machines with hand-marked paper ballots and denied the state's request for a ruling without trial.[6] Lindell was especially enthusiastic about a footnote stating that the evidence "does not suggest that the Plaintiffs are conspiracy theorists of any variety," and noting that leading cybersecurity experts and computer scientists had provided evidence that Georgia's voting system threatens voters' constitutional rights.[6] The case, [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]], was set for a bench trial in January 2024.[6]

## Eighth Circuit voids the $5 million Zeidman award (July 2025)

On July 23, 2025, the 8th Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that Lindell does not have to pay a $5 million award to software engineer [[robert-zeidman|Robert Zeidman]] of Las Vegas, who had taken up Lindell's "Prove Mike Wrong Challenge" and disputed the data Lindell says proves Chinese interference in the 2020 United States presidential election.[8] The appeals court held that the arbitration panel that granted the award in 2023 had overstepped its authority.[8] "It's a great day for our country," Lindell said. "This is a big win. It opens the door to getting rid of these electronic voting machines and getting paper ballots, hand-counted."[8]

Badlands commentary recalled that during Lindell's [[coomer-v-lindell|trial]] the previous month his attorneys had sought to exclude testimony about the $5 million dispute because the award was on appeal, warning of jury prejudice; the judge denied the motion but preserved the issue for appeal, so the reversal may bolster any challenge to the Coomer verdict.[8]

## Testimony in the Denver defamation trial (June 2025)

Lindell took the stand on Monday, June 9, 2025 — day six of [[coomer-v-lindell|Coomer v. Lindell]] — and remained committed to his campaign against voting machines and his claim that the 2020 presidential election was stolen.[9] He continued attacking [[eric-coomer|Eric Coomer]], the former [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] executive, while distancing himself from claims that he coordinated attacks on Coomer at a 2021 convention he organized, and characterized Coomer as motivated by money: "(I) cost Dr. Coomer what? He's out there suing people for money."[9]

Coomer's attorneys sought to tie Lindell's election-fraud broadcasts on [[frankspeech|FrankSpeech]] to [[mypillow|MyPillow]] promo codes underwriting the effort, as the plaintiffs must show that Lindell, FrankSpeech and MyPillow are all liable.[9] Clips from FrankSpeech, RSBN and NewsMax were played for the jury, along with a CNN segment from Anderson Cooper's program that Lindell admitted having watched when it aired.[9] Because Lindell's testimony made clear he believes his own claims, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] assessed that Coomer's route to "actual malice" would be reckless disregard for the truth — hence a day spent asking Lindell about all the ways he should have known his statements were false.[9] His own attorneys later focused on his modest upbringing, business history and unexpected success, with questioning set to resume Tuesday morning ahead of the charge conference.[9]

## Defense strategy and the media order (June 2025)

At the outset of the Colorado trial Lindell's counsel [[chris-kachouroff|Chris Kachouroff]] told the jury it was not necessary to prove Lindell's voting-machine claims true, only that Lindell believed them substantially true when he made them — a position [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted matched the affirmative defenses filed in the April pretrial order rather than representing any change of strategy.[10] Lindell had nonetheless promised his followers that he would use the trial to banish election computers.[10]

On Tuesday, June 3, 2025, the court declined to hold Lindell in contempt but issued a warning, and he agreed not to discuss witness testimony with the press while the trial was underway; the dispute concerned his tweeting about testimony in real time rather than any restriction on reporters, and no gag order or media blackout was imposed.[10] Badlands coverage argued that if Lindell believed his claims — and still does — then he was not speaking lies to harm [[eric-coomer|Coomer]] but stating what he believed, a posture that could not meet Coomer's burden of willful negligence and actual malice.[10] See [[coomer-v-lindell|Coomer v. Lindell]].[10]
