# Antony Sutton

Antony Sutton is described in Badlands Media commentary as a researcher and author who, during the 1970s, published three books that are treated as essential reading for understanding the financial architecture behind the twentieth century's major political upheavals.[1] The author of the Badlands piece "On Infiltration and Israel" calls him "a brilliant man" and presents his work as foundational evidence that ostensibly opposed ideological movements were funded from a common source.[1]

## The Wall Street Trilogy

Sutton's three principal works, collectively known as the [[wall-street-trilogy|Wall Street Trilogy]], are *Wall Street and the Bolshevik Revolution*, *Wall Street and Hitler*, and *Wall Street and FDR*.[1] Badlands summarizes their combined thesis as showing that Wall Street provided substantial funding to communists, Nazis, and other parties subsequently instrumental in subverting Western civilization.[1] From this the author draws the conclusion that much of the twentieth century amounted to "a theatrical production staged by Wall Street," with Wall Street itself functioning as a front for a deeper cabal.[1]

## Interpretive use in Badlands commentary

Sutton's research is used to support the maxim that "all wars are bankers wars" — a formulation the author concedes oversimplifies matters but regards as largely true.[1] The practical implication drawn for readers is that adherents of communism, Nazism, capitalism, and socialism alike may sincerely believe themselves to be in genuine opposition while all being manipulated by the same financial interests.[1] Within the article's broader argument, Sutton's documentation of cross-ideological financing serves as the bridge to claims about the financiers the author terms "ziobanksters," including [[max-warburg|Max Warburg]] and the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild family]].[1]

## Influence on the Gladio primer

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] describes her research into [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] as beginning with Sutton's work on how Wall Street interfaced with foreign affairs in the first half of the twentieth century, starting with the Russian — or Bolshevik — revolution.[2] From Sutton she draws the account of forces operating out of New York City, centred at 120 Broadway, home of the New York Federal Reserve Bank among other financial institutions, and out of the City of London, which constantly interfered in the inner workings of foreign governments through both nonprofit "expeditions" such as the Red Cross and for-profit banking interests.[2]

Watkins credits Sutton with documenting that the same group of people funded the Bolshevik revolution, [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] and [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]], noting that Sutton did not address [[benito-mussolini|Mussolini]] but that the same pattern appears in independent accounts of his rise.[2] She names the coalition Sutton described the [[international-syndicate|International Syndicate]].[2]

## America's Secret Establishment

In his book *America's Secret Establishment*, cited at length in "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 8," Sutton outlined the ability of the Order of [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]] to establish vertical and horizontal "chains of influence" that ensured the continuity of its conspiratorial schemes.[3] He treated the [[william-collins-whitney|Whitney]]–[[henry-stimson|Stimson]]–Bundy links as the exemplary "vertical chain," running from Whitney's attorney Elihu Root through Stimson's successive War, State and Philippine appointments to [[hollister-bundy|Hollister Bundy]] and his sons [[william-bundy|William]] and [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]].[3]
