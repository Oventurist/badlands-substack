# Rumble

**Rumble** is a video-hosting platform positioned as the premier free-speech alternative to YouTube, and in Badlands Media's assessment the censorship regime's primary target in the run-up to the 2024 election.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) It is part of the parallel media and communication system built out after the suppression events of 2020, alongside Truth Social and Brave.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Pressure campaigns

Rumble is described as under simultaneous pressure from governments, media outlets and "watchdog" organisations the article characterises as attack dogs.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The Associated Press was reported to be probing the company for weaknesses and establishing narratives about it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Journalists demanded that Rumble deplatform [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]], one of its biggest channels, over an alleged crime for which he had not been convicted, and also worked to make the platform commercially toxic by calling advertisers directly to sabotage existing deals and deter prospective ones.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) A comparable advertiser-pressure tactic was documented when Barstool Sports' Dave Portnoy learned that the Washington Post had contacted sponsors of an upcoming event, characterising him to them as "a misogynic racist."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## The France precedent

In 2022 the French government, angered that Rumble continued to allow Russian news sources such as Sputnik and RT on the platform, demanded the accounts be removed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Rumble rejected the demands and instead switched off access to the site for France entirely.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The article uses this episode to argue that the endgame of the [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]] and the Brand affair is the complete removal of Rumble from the United Kingdom, achieved by restricting the whole population's access rather than by negotiating content removals.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Strategic significance

Rumble's chief executive [[chris-pavlovski|Chris Pavlovski]] is described as challenging the regime more intensely than [[x-twitter|X]] does, while commanding a fraction of the resources available to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] — leaving Rumble in a more vulnerable and possibly higher-priority position.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Badlands Media notes that its own operations depend on Rumble's hard stance against censorship, and argues that if Rumble falls the loss would extend beyond one platform: it would set a standard and serve as a warning to any other company hoping to fill the void.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The article closes with the injunction that Rumble must be protected and supported at all costs.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Targeted by Brazil's censorship regime (2026)

An interim staff report by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] released in April 2026 stated that when platforms such as [[x-corp|X]] and Rumble refused to fully comply with takedown orders issued under [[brazil|Brazil]]'s censorship system, Brazilian authorities fined them and threatened or ordered them to cease operations in the country.[2] The report warned that forcing American companies to censor US residents' speech creates a broader threat to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[2]

## Badlands Media broadcasting (2023)

As of January 2023 Rumble hosted the entire [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] broadcast operation across two channels: all shows aired live on the [[patel-patriot-rumble-channel|Patel Patriot channel]], while replays were made available in library format on the Badlands Media channel.[3]

A second Badlands Media schedule post, published January 3, 2023 to open the 2023 broadcast year, carried the same listing and description, and reiterated that all network shows air live on the Patel Patriot Rumble channel with replays archived on the Badlands Media library channel.[4]

## Tether and Bitcoin integration, 2025

Following [[tether|Tether]]'s February 2025 announcement that USDt would be integrated into Bitcoin's base layer and Lightning Network via the Taproot Assets protocol built by [[lightning-labs|Lightning Labs]], Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] noted that Rumble's integration of Tether and Bitcoin would give the platform lightning rails, enabling instant micro USD payments supported by the Bitcoin network.[5]

## Antitrust suit against Google (2024)

In May 2024 Rumble initiated legal proceedings against [[google|Google]] and its parent [[alphabet|Alphabet]] in the Northern District of California, alleging that Google's digital advertising practices violate the [[sherman-antitrust-act|Sherman Antitrust Act]].[6] The complaint argued that "Google exploits significant conflicts of interest that stem from its multiple roles in this electronically traded marketplace," allowing it to "pocket a supra-competitive portion of every advertising dollar that passes through the Ad Tech markets it controls," revenue Rumble says should have reached publishers and creators.[6] Rumble accused Google of unlawful tying arrangements and a pattern of exclusionary conduct against actual and potential rivals, and sought injunctive relief and damages in excess of $1 billion.[6]

Separately, Rumble is suing [[check-my-ads|Check My Ads]], a tax-exempt organization it accuses of defamation and of "routinely target[ing] news outlets and platforms that do not adhere to their political worldview."[6]

### Co-plaintiff in the de Moraes lawsuit
In February 2025 Rumble joined Trump Media and Technology Group as a plaintiff in a lawsuit filed in U.S. federal court in Florida against Brazilian Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes, accusing the judge of illegally censoring political discourse.[7] Badlands hosts also used the Badlands Rumble Channel to livestream coverage of President Donald Trump's February 19, 2025 address to the Future Investment Initiative summit in Miami Beach.[7]

### December 2023 cyber attack after the J6 footage release
In December 2023 Rumble was hit with what was described as an "unprecedented" cyber attack shortly after new January 6 security camera footage was posted to the platform.[8] The GOP-controlled Committee on House Administration's Subcommittee on Oversight had created a Rumble channel to publish hours of United States Capitol Police security footage recorded on January 6, 2021; the first videos went up on December 5, the collection grew to 135 clips of about ten minutes each by the following day, and the channel had nearly 700 followers by December 7, according to the Epoch Times.[8] Modernity News, whose report was carried in the Badlands news brief, concluded that the release "upset some people."[8] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the attacks reflected an unprecedented assault on free speech across multiple domains, and credited Rumble with taking a stand for the First Amendment both by hosting the J6 footage and by allowing Badlands creators to publish without compromise.[8]

## Tether investment

In December 2024 Rumble disclosed a $775 million strategic investment from the digital assets company [[tether|Tether]], after which the company's stock rose 76% to trade at $12.66.[9] Under the deal Tether agreed to buy 103.3 million shares of Rumble Class A common stock at $7.50 each; Rumble planned to deploy $250 million to support its balance sheet and growth objectives and to use the remainder to finance a self-tender offer for up to 70 million Class A shares, providing liquidity to existing holders.[9] CEO [[chris-pavlovski|Chris Pavlovski]] would retain his majority stake while Tether took a minority role without board participation, with closing scheduled for the first quarter of 2025 subject to regulatory clearances.[9] Both companies framed the alliance around decentralization and free expression, and Tether indicated interest in exploring cloud services, crypto payment solutions and advertising with Rumble.[9] See [[tether-investment-in-rumble|Tether Investment in Rumble]].[9]
