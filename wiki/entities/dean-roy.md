# Dean Roy

Dean Roy is a 14-year-old high school freshman from [[vermont|Vermont]] who launched a campaign for governor of the state, framing his bid as a challenge to "elder rule" and declaring that "we are a dying state."[1]

Roy cannot vote, cannot drive a car, and still requires a permission slip for school field trips.[1] If elected, he would become the first U.S. governor under the age of 18.[1] He told the Daily Mail, speaking from his bedroom with an American flag hanging vertically behind him, that he hopes his campaign "leads to more youth getting involved."[1]

The campaign began with an offhand remark: at his 8th-grade graduation, a teacher joked that he wanted the campaign manager job if Roy ever ran for office. Roy went home, looked up the eligibility requirements, and found no barrier — Vermont is the only U.S. state with no age restriction on candidates for chief executive.[1]

## Precedents

In 2018, 13-year-old Ethan Sonneborn ran unsuccessfully in Vermont's Democratic gubernatorial primary.[1] The youngest person ever to serve as a U.S. governor was Democrat Stevens Mason, who had just turned 24 when he began his term in Michigan in 1835.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Vermont's lack of an age restriction makes the candidacy legally viable and argued Roy "couldn't possibly do worse than those who came before him," adding that Vermont's corruption is such that it "makes the nation fund [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]]' commie fever dreams." Her verdict: "Give the kid a chance."[1]
