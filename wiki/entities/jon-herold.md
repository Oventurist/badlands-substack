Jon Herold is the founder of [[badlands-media]] and one of its most frequent on-air hosts.[1] He is described as the host of the Devolution Power Hour, "the show that started it all on the Badlands Media Network," through which he analyzes recent events via [[devolution]] and Continuity of Government theories.[1]

On the network's weekly schedule, Herold appears across several programs. He co-hosts Badlands Daily (Monday–Friday, 10am ET) with [[canncon]] in a traditional news-broadcast format "without the Mainstream Media spin and corporate BS."[1] He is the subject of Baseless Conspiracies (Monday, 10:30pm ET), in which [[zak-paine]] "takes Jon Herold to conspiracy theory school" on a new topic each week.[1] He hosts the Devolution Power Hour on Wednesday and Saturday nights at 10:30pm ET, joined by co-host [[burning-bright]] on Wednesdays.[1] He also co-hosts The Liberty Den (Friday, 10:30pm ET) with The Kate Awakening, a casual week-in-review conversation often featuring a special guest.[1]

## NPR coverage and Epstein document response (November 2025)

Herold was quoted in a November 2025 [[npr|NPR]] article headlined "Right-Wing Media Shrugs Off Latest Epstein Document Release," which described him as a pro-Trump podcaster who gained an audience after the 2020 election by spreading QAnon-adjacent conspiracy theories.[2] On his [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] Rumble livestream that week he said of the newly released [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] material, "To me, these are nothingburgers. If they're even real."[2]

Responding in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] under the heading "NPR Melts Down Over Badlands Shrugging," Herold reiterated that spending time analyzing the material was "the dumb thing to do," said millions of people online had spent days on "something that's probably fake," and noted it was likely the first time Badlands had ever been mentioned by NPR.[2] He argued the outlet's headline confirmed that Badlands was "the only one not taking the bait" and that critics were "mad we didn't go hook, line, and sinker like literally everybody else on the internet."[2] He rebutted the "QAnon-adjacent" label by referring to a post number that does not exist.[2] Elsewhere in the same brief, reacting to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s endorsement of a congressional vote to release the Epstein files, Herold quipped: "Can whoever removed a chromosome from 'MAGA' last week put it back already?"[2]
