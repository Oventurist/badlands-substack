# Anastasio Somoza Debayle

Anastasio Somoza Debayle was the last dictator of Nicaragua's Somoza dynasty, overthrown by the [[sandinista-national-liberation-front|Sandinistas]] in 1979.[1] The source article presents him as a central but largely forgotten piece of Cold War history in Central America: the corruption and brutality of his rule created the very oppression that produced the Sandinista revolution.[1]

## The Somoza dynasty

The Somozas — the father, then his elder son Luis, and finally Anastasio — formed a family dynasty that ruled Nicaragua for 43 years and dominated the country as if it were their personal fiefdom.[1] They owned large tracts of land; when General Somoza fled into exile he personally controlled 22 percent of the country's agricultural land.[1] The family also owned much of Nicaragua's business and industry, including the national airline, two steamship companies, banks, and insurance firms.[1]

The dynasty was propped up by a 12,000-member National Guard that was "part soldier, part bully boy."[1] When Anastasio came to power on Luis's death in 1967, he strengthened the hand of government in ways that made his father's era look like a kindergarten exercise: National Guard brutality became notorious and opponents were jailed and tortured.[1] Somoza himself lived ostentatiously — lavish parties, an enormous wine cellar, a personal life of excess — while his people lived in poverty.[1]

## Aftermath

After the Sandinistas took power, the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] built up a rebel army, the [[nicaraguan-contras|Contras]], to try to take back control, fighting a proxy war justified publicly as anti-communism.[1] The article notes that contemporary American coverage dwelt on the Sandinistas' communism while saying nothing about the dictator they had overthrown or about the CIA's role in sustaining him.[1]
