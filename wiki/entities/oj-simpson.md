# O. J. Simpson

O. J. Simpson is cited by Erik Carlson as the subject of the 1994 murder case that, in his reading, functioned as a mass mind-control event coinciding with the passage of the [[crime-bill-1994|1994 Crime Bill]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## The case as described

Simpson allegedly killed his ex-wife and a man he believed to be her lover.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) America watched the white Ford Bronco during its slow-speed police chase through the Los Angeles freeways; Carlson adds the aside that more pizzas were delivered that day than any other day in American history.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) Americans were glued to their televisions through 24-hour coverage of the incident and the ensuing trial, an appetite Carlson describes as being "like mind control" before correcting himself to say it literally was mind control.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Racial polarization

Many in the black community supported Simpson and believed him innocent, while most in the white community believed him guilty. When a jury containing a single white juror found him not guilty, riots did not follow, but a deep-seated anger set in.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) The verdict thus serves in Carlson's argument as another instrument of engineered racial division alongside the [[la-riots-1992|1992 Los Angeles riots]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Death and reactions, April 2024

Simpson died of prostate cancer in Las Vegas on April 10, 2024 at the age of 76, with his family announcing the death on his official X account.[2] He had earned fame and fortune through football and show business, but his legacy was permanently altered by the June 1994 knife slayings of his former wife [[nicole-brown-simpson|Nicole Brown Simpson]] and her friend [[ronald-goldman|Ronald Goldman]] in Los Angeles; he was acquitted at a criminal trial that mesmerized the public and exposed divisions over race and policing, later found liable for the deaths in a civil case, and subsequently served nine years in prison on unrelated charges.[2] Goldman's father Fred and sister Kim said in a statement that "the hope for true accountability has ended" and that news of "Ron's killer passing away is a mixed bag of complicated emotions."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted the case's entanglement with the Kardashian family: [[caitlyn-jenner|Caitlyn Jenner]], who had vacationed with Simpson and was married to [[kris-jenner|Kris Jenner]] at the time of the killings, reacted to the death by posting "Good Riddance," while Kris Jenner — reportedly Nicole Brown Simpson's best friend — stayed silent, and Kris's ex-husband [[robert-kardashian|Robert Kardashian]] had served on Simpson's defense team, leaving the family "kind of on both sides of the OJ saga."[2]
