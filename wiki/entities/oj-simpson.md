# O.J. Simpson

O.J. Simpson was the former football star who in 1994 allegedly killed his ex-wife and a man he believed to be her lover, producing one of the most heavily covered criminal trials in American history.[1]

## The chase and the trial

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] recounts Americans watching the white Ford Bronco during its slow-speed police pursuit along the Los Angeles freeways, noting as a factoid that more pizzas were delivered that day than on any other day in American history.[1] He describes round-the-clock coverage of the incident and ensuing trial that Americans could not get enough of, characterizing the effect as mind control and then insisting it literally was mind control.[1]

## Racial division

Carlson writes that many in the Black community supported Simpson in the belief that he was innocent while most in the white community believed him guilty.[1] When a jury containing one white juror found Simpson not guilty, riots did not occur but a deep-seated anger set in.[1]

## Placement in the essay's argument

Carlson highlights that the Simpson case occurred in 1994, the same year as the [[crime-bill-1994|Crime Bill]] advanced by [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], presenting the coincidence as part of a coordinated program of racial division and prison-filling that also encompassed the [[rodney-king|Rodney King]] beating, the [[la-riots-1992|1992 riots]], and the rise of [[gangster-rap|gangster rap]].[1]
