# Lloyd Jowers

Lloyd Jowers was the defendant in the 1999 [[memphis|Memphis]] civil trial that produced a jury finding of conspiracy in the assassination of Dr. [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]][1] The Badlands Media essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI" cites the case as documented evidence that an officially designated lone assassin — [[james-earl-ray|James Earl Ray]] — was in fact a substitute for other actors.[1]

## Testimony

Jowers named Memphis police officer Lt. Earl Clark as King's actual assassin.[1] He testified that a Memphis produce dealer involved with the Mafia gave him $100,000 to hire an assassin, and assured him that the police would not be present at the scene of the shooting.[1]

After four weeks of testimony and more than seventy witnesses, twelve jurors returned a unanimous verdict on 8 December 1999 that King's death resulted from a conspiracy.[1] King's widow said the jury had been convinced that, in addition to Jowers, "the conspiracy of the Mafia, local, state and federal government agencies, were deeply involved in the assassination of my husband," and that someone other than Ray had been the shooter.[1]

## Role in the article's argument

For author [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], the Jowers verdict serves as a legal anchor for a thesis otherwise built on circumstantial patterns: a jury, presented with extensive evidence in open court, concluded that federal agencies participated in the killing of a major civil-rights leader and that the man imprisoned for it had been set up.[1] The article then moves from that finding to allegations of Ray's own prior dealings with [[cia|CIA]] agents and mobsters.[1]
