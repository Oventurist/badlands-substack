# Colleen Kollar-Kotelly

Colleen Kollar-Kotelly is a United States District Judge in Washington, D.C., who in April 2025 issued a partial injunction against [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s executive order overhauling the administration of federal elections.[1]

## April 2025 elections ruling

On Thursday, April 24, 2025, Kollar-Kotelly blocked the Trump administration from immediately enacting certain changes to how federal elections are run, most prominently a requirement that documentary proof of citizenship be added to the federal voter registration form.[1] She did not enjoin the order in its entirety: other provisions, including a directive to tighten mail ballot receipt deadlines nationwide, were permitted to proceed for the time being.[1]

The judge sided with voting rights organizations and Democratic Party plaintiffs, reasoning that the Constitution assigns the power to regulate federal elections to the states and to Congress rather than to the president.[1] She further observed that federal lawmakers were at that time working on their own legislation to impose a proof-of-citizenship requirement for voting, underscoring that the subject lay within the legislative sphere.[1]

The suit had been brought by the [[league-of-united-latin-american-citizens|League of United Latin American Citizens]], the [[league-of-women-voters-education-fund|League of Women Voters Education Fund]], the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] and others, who characterized the March executive order as unconstitutional.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the roughly 120-page opinion as "clarifying and confirming authorities," arguing that while the executive branch's ability to alter election procedure directly is constitutionally limited, it is "limited, but not powerless."[1] In that reading, conditional federal funding remains an available lever, since — in the commentary's framing — no voting system is fully compliant with the letter of all applicable laws and therefore none within a state plan can properly be certified.[1]
