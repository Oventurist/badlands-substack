# Susan Crawford

Susan Crawford is a Dane County, Wisconsin judge who ran as the liberal-aligned candidate in the April 2025 election for a seat on the Wisconsin Supreme Court, facing Waukesha County Judge [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]].[1]

## 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court election

Crawford's candidacy became the focus of what Wisconsin Public Radio described as an unprecedented flood of out-of-state money into a state judicial contest. Both Crawford and Schimel received grassroots contributions from ordinary citizens, but the bulk of their backing came from prominent political groups and megadonors, with at least $81 million spent on the race to date on the eve of the vote.[1] Wisconsin caps direct campaign contributions to individual state Supreme Court candidates at $20,000, but donors with more to spend may give unlimited sums through political action committees or the state political parties — the mechanism through which the largest out-of-state sums reached the race.[1]

Badlands coverage of the contest noted an interview between [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] on [[the-narrative|The Narrative]] which suggested there was a solid chance the race would break for Crawford, while stressing the outlet's broader position that nothing had been done to fix American elections.[1] See [[2025-wisconsin-supreme-court-election]].

## April 2025 victory

Crawford won the Wisconsin Supreme Court election on Tuesday, April 1, 2025, NBC News projected, preserving a liberal majority on the swing state's highest court.[2] The Dane County circuit court judge defeated [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]], a Waukesha County circuit court judge and former Republican state attorney general, in the race to replace 73-year-old Justice Ann Walsh Bradley, who had announced her retirement nearly a year earlier; the winner serves a 10-year term.[2] The contest was cast in part as a referendum on billionaire [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who poured millions into the race to help Schimel.[2] Although officially nonpartisan, the race featured clear ideological divides over labor rights, abortion access and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s January 6 pardons, with prominent Democrats including former President Barack Obama backing Crawford and Republicans, including Trump, largely endorsing Schimel.[2] Badlands contributors [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the outcome — a liberal court seat lost alongside a passed voter ID ballot measure — as evidence that the electoral system itself is fraudulent.[2]

## Margin and turnout analysis

Election analyst [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] had predicted Crawford would win by at least five points; she instead won by ten in what he described as an otherwise competitive state, exceeding [[janet-protasiewicz|Janet Protasiewicz]]'s previous record for a Wisconsin Supreme Court seat in a spring election by almost 300,000 votes while [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]] concurrently set a new record on the losing side.[3] Keshel attributed the scale of the margin to Democratic mastery of mail-ballot turnout in off-year elections, noting that roughly 600,000 votes had sufficed to win comparable Wisconsin races in 2018 and 2019.[3] He argued that structural advantages including [[automatic-voter-registration|Automatic Voter Registration]], universal [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]] and [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]] should be treated as weighted factors in forecasting such races.[3]
