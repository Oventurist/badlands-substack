# Consortium for Elections and Political Process Strengthening

The Consortium for Elections and Political Process Strengthening (CEPPS) is a joint venture created in 1995 by three nonprofit organizations linked to the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]]: the [[international-foundation-for-electoral-systems|International Foundation for Electoral Systems]] (IFES), the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]] (IRI), and the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]] (NDI).[1] Its stated mission is "advancing democratic practices and institutions globally," and it is funded primarily by the [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]].[1]

## Function and criticism

Badlands Media coverage describes CEPPS as having been created as part of a broader strategy to influence foreign political processes, but argues it also came to play a central role in coordinating internet censorship — characterized as the primary vector by which the foreign-policy establishment attacks the rising tide of national populism.[1] Through its USAID partnership, CEPPS has been linked to programs aimed at countering "disinformation," which critics argue in practice means censoring narratives that challenge U.S. foreign policy or particular political agendas.[1] Reporting cited in the article alleges USAID funneled as much as $799 million to CEPPS for initiatives including global censorship platforms.[1]

[[internews|Internews]], described as a "senior technical partner" of CEPPS, is said to have shifted from supporting journalists abroad to suppressing content labeled as "disinformation" under the banner of protecting democratic processes.[1]

## Global footprint

CEPPS operates in more than 140 countries.[1] Its work is alleged to include building censorship infrastructure in nations such as Brazil, where it reportedly collaborated with USAID and the [[state-department|State Department]] to influence digital media environments.[1] Critics contend that this counter-disinformation work prioritizes certain political narratives and results in suppression of free speech.[1] Combined with the NED's grantmaking powers, the article argues, the CEPPS censorship network constitutes one of the most effective weapons available against the MAGA movement and against a second Trump administration.[1]
