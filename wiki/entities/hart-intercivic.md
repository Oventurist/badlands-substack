# Hart InterCivic

Hart InterCivic is an American election technology vendor. In the Badlands Media essay "US Elections Must Be Independent," it is listed among the election technology companies that have always been private entities, alongside [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] and [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck]] — in contrast to [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]], which was publicly traded before going private in 2008, and [[election-systems-software|Election Systems & Software]], which was public prior to 2010.[1]

The distinction matters to the essay's argument: public companies are required to disclose financial information and business operations to the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]], while private entities are not.[1] For private election companies, disclosures and audits fall to industry regulators, with requirements varying by industry; oversight and audits of US election vendors are largely the responsibility of state and local election authorities, focused on whether equipment and services meet legal and regulatory standards set by federal agencies such as the [[election-assistance-commission|Election Assistance Commission]].[1] The author characterizes the resulting arrangement as a mashup of governmental, pseudo-governmental, non-governmental and private corporate entities in which plausible deniability reigns.[1]
