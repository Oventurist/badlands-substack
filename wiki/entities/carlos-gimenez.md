# Carlos Gimenez

Carlos Gimenez is a Republican member of the U.S. House of Representatives from Florida who participated in the February 6, 2024 hearing of the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Committee on Homeland Security]]'s Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee on the water sector.[1]

## Exchange on digitization and quantum computing

Gimenez used his time to press the witnesses on the implications of [[quantum-computing|quantum computing]] and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] for [[critical-infrastructure|critical infrastructure]] security, warning: "you've got this threat coming. It's called quantum computing attached to AI. It's going to make all your efforts – it could make all your efforts – fruitless."[1] He asked whether it would be "smarter for us to rely more on intranet" or to return to manual operation instead of internet-connected systems.[1]

[[robert-m-lee|Robert M. Lee]] of [[dragos|Dragos]] resisted, saying he preferred American infrastructure services to those the Chinese provide their citizens precisely because of digitization and connectivity, and that "you can't go back."[1] Gimenez replied that he was "giving you Murphy's law" and that resourcing was not a viable answer against an adversary with far greater manpower: "for every one person that we have working on the Chinese issue or the CCP, they have 50. You'll never be able to out-resource them."[1] He pressed the core point that "the vulnerability comes in the fact that you're tied to the internet. Anybody can attack you from anywhere in the world. If you have a closed system … they can't attack you."[1] Lee responded that a closed system could not be operated at all given how original equipment manufacturers build modern systems. Gimenez closed by saying, "I guess I'm a little bit more pessimistic knowing what's coming."[1]

The Badlands account described Gimenez as "representing all us proud luddites" and treated the exchange as a revealing illustration of how deeply centralization and digitization are entrenched in U.S. infrastructure policy.[1]
