# United Nations Security Council

The United Nations Security Council is the organ of the [[united-nations|United Nations]] charged with maintaining international peace and security, and serves as the principal forum in which the permanent members — including [[russia|Russia]], the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]], and [[france|France]] — press competing claims about armed conflict.[1]

In February 2026 the Council was the venue for Russian ambassador [[vasily-nebenzya|Vasily Nebenzya]]'s claim that the United Kingdom and France were considering transferring nuclear weapons capabilities to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and were exploring ways to "circumvent" nonproliferation restrictions in order to arm Kyiv. Nebenzya presented no publicly verifiable evidence during the session, and neither Western government had announced any such plan.[1]

## Nord Stream investigation resolution (2023)

In February 2023 Russia circulated a draft Security Council resolution calling for an international independent investigation commission into the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream pipeline sabotage]], and began the Council's "silence procedure" on the text.[2] The United States and some other Western members of the UNSC broke silence and objected to the creation of such a commission, blocking the draft's adoption.[2] In March 2023 [[china|China]]'s foreign ministry publicly announced its support for the Russian draft, with a spokesperson making the remarks at a regular press briefing carried by state-run Xinhua.[2] Moscow and Beijing treated Washington's resistance to the resolution as a sign of guilt and suggested that Western allies were obfuscating the investigation.[2]

## Russian allegations against Ukraine in Africa (2025)

At an August 2025 Security Council briefing on threats to international peace and security, Russia's deputy representative [[dmitry-polyansky|Dmitry Polyansky]] alleged that [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s special services, including the Defense Intelligence of Ukraine, were supplying weapons, drones and training to militants staging coordinated attacks on governments in Africa's Sahel region, and called for an investigation.[3] Polyansky commended the work of the UN Office of Counter-Terrorism and the Counter-Terrorism Committee Executive Directorate while criticizing the UN Secretary-General's latest report on the threat posed by [[isis|Islamic State]] in Africa.[3]

## Sixth U.S. Gaza veto (September 2025)

On Thursday, 18 September 2025, the United States vetoed a Security Council resolution demanding an immediate and permanent ceasefire in Gaza and the lifting of all restrictions on humanitarian aid deliveries to the enclave.[4] The draft was tabled by the ten elected members of the fifteen-member council and received fourteen votes in favor, failing only because of the American veto — the sixth such veto since the beginning of the war between [[israel|Israel]] and Hamas nearly two years earlier.[4] The veto was delivered by U.S. representative [[morgan-ortagus|Morgan Ortagus]].[4]

Washington argued that UN ceasefire resolutions risk undermining peace negotiations on the ground as well as Israel's ability to act against Hamas and its "right to self-defense," while critics accused the United States of shielding Israel from international accountability.[4] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the episode as a humiliation ritual for both the United States and the [[united-nations|United Nations]], contending that the council's loss of authority made the emergence of a one-world government from the existing UN framework remote.[4]

## March 2024 Gaza ceasefire resolution and Israeli response

In March 2024 the United States refrained from vetoing a Security Council proposal calling for a ceasefire in Gaza.[5] Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] responded by cancelling a planned delegation to Washington, calling the abstention a "clear retreat" from the previous American position, while the [[white-house|White House]] denied any change in policy.[5]

## Putin's 2024 challenge to the North Korea sanctions regime

In June 2024, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] publicly called for a revision of the Security Council's sanctions regime against [[north-korea|North Korea]], which he described as having been "inspired by the United States and its allies" and characterized as an indefinite restrictive regime that "must be revised."[6] He made the statement following negotiations with North Korean leader [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] in Pyongyang, and argued that Western propaganda cliches were no longer able to disguise aggressive geopolitical plans in the North-East Asia region.[6] Putin further pledged that Moscow and Pyongyang would continue to oppose "the practice of sanctions strangulation as a tool that the West is accustomed to using to maintain its hegemony in politics, economy and other spheres."[6]

At an accompanying press conference, Putin compared the sanctions to the Nazi [[siege-of-leningrad|siege of Leningrad]].[6] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary anticipated that the challenge would not go unanswered, and framed it as a potential new front in what its writers term the War of Sovereignty, with Russia positioning itself as North Korea's champion against the Council's regime.[6]

## Gaza stabilization force resolution (November 2025)

On November 17, 2025, the Security Council approved a U.S.-led resolution establishing an [[international-stabilization-force|international stabilization force]] (ISF) to deploy to [[gaza|Gaza]] for at least two years, giving President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s peace plan additional momentum and international legitimacy.[7] Thirteen Council members supported the resolution, Russia and China abstained, and no member opposed it.[7] In addition to authorizing the ISF, the resolution endorsed the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]], which will oversee governance of Gaza and which Trump has said he will chair.[7] The text granted the United States and other participating countries a broad mandate to govern Gaza, demilitarize it, and begin reconstruction, with deployment sought as early as January 2026 and troops drawn from several Arab and Muslim countries.[7]

## October 2023 Gaza resolution veto

On October 18, 2023, the United States vetoed a Brazilian-drafted Security Council resolution calling for humanitarian pauses in the [[israel|Israel]]–[[hamas|Hamas]] conflict to allow aid access to the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]]; twelve of the fifteen members voted in favor while Russia and Britain abstained.[8] US Ambassador [[linda-thomas-greenfield|Linda Thomas-Greenfield]] told the council that Washington was "doing the hard work of diplomacy" and needed "to let that diplomacy play out," while acknowledging that the council "must speak out."[8] Reuters noted that the United States traditionally shields its ally Israel from Security Council action.[8]

## Gaza aid convoy statement, 2024

Amid American opposition, Arab nations failed overnight on Thursday, February 29, 2024 to win immediate support for a Security Council statement that would have blamed Israeli forces for the more than 100 reported deaths that occurred as Palestinians in northern Gaza swarmed an aid convoy.[9] Riyad Mansour, the Palestinian UN ambassador, told reporters after an emergency closed council meeting that 14 of the 15 council members supported the statement, which had been put forward by Algeria, the Arab representative on the body.[9] Algeria's draft declaration expressed "deep concern" and stated that the situation was "due to opening fire by Israel forces."[9]

The United States did not support the statement, and US deputy ambassador Robert Wood told reporters: "The parties are working on some language to see if we can get to a statement."[9] He added: "The problem is that we don't have all the facts here," saying he wanted the wording to reflect "the necessary due diligence with regards to culpability."[9]

## Third US veto of a Gaza ceasefire resolution (February 2024)

On February 20, 2024, the United States cast the lone veto against a Security Council resolution calling for a ceasefire in Gaza, the third time Washington had blocked such a measure.[10] The vote on the Algerian-drafted resolution was 13–1 on the 15-member council, with the United Kingdom abstaining.[10] The veto came a day after Washington floated its own rival resolution calling for a temporary ceasefire, and left the US increasingly isolated as it faced criticism for backing Israel's campaign against [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza, launched after the October 7 assault that killed some 1,200 people.[10] The United States argued that a complete ceasefire would jeopardise negotiations involving Egypt and Qatar to release the hostages Hamas abducted in the attack.[10]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the veto as part of an "Ouroborosian death spiral" of the System of Systems and the Central Narrative, noting that Washington publicly refused a Gaza ceasefire days after stumping for more taxpayer funding for its proxy war in the Donbas.[10] He argued that the awakening public increasingly perceives that the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] establishment will stop at nothing to perpetuate its endless war machine, and asked, in light of [[devolution|Devolution]] theory, which globalist-aligned proxies were threatened by events in Gaza sufficiently to make the entire EU bloc call for a cessation of violence there while demanding more of it to the east.[10]

## US veto of the 2023 Gaza humanitarian pause resolution

"War Pigs" notes that in October 2023 the United States was the only nation on the Security Council to veto a resolution calling for "humanitarian pauses" to deliver lifesaving aid to millions in Gaza.[12] Badlands Media observed this was not the first such veto — the pattern had held regularly for several decades — and argued it had produced a perception throughout the Muslim world that the US is Israel's partner and enabler.[12] Mass protests in Lebanon, Turkey, Yemen, Saudi Arabia and Iran, all countries the article describes as having formidable militaries, blamed the United States as much as Israel.[12]

## Veto power and calls for abolition

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes the Security Council as among the few institutions of the [[united-nations|United Nations]] that gave an equal voice to leading members on both sides of the Iron Curtain, noting that especially since 2011 its veto power has been vital in blocking unilateral acts of imperialism, since any official military intervention required unanimity among the five permanent members.[11] He argues the Council's existence has unarguably saved the lives of millions by blocking countless attempts to destroy Syria and continues to serve as a wedge against unipolar ambitions.[11]

Against this, [[mark-malloch-brown|Lord Mark Malloch Brown]] — who integrated the [[responsibility-to-protect|Responsibility to Protect]] doctrine into the UN while serving as Undersecretary General — has spent recent years calling for the dissolution of the Security Council in order to remove "authoritarian nations" such as Russia and China from any role in global war-making decisions.[11]
