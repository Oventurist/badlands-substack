# Walmart

Walmart is the largest retailer in the United States and one of the import-dependent corporations most exposed to the tariff program pursued by the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

On 21 April 2025 Walmart chief executive Doug McMillon joined [[target|Target]] chief executive Brian Cornell and [[home-depot|Home Depot]] chief executive Ted Decker at the [[white-house|White House]] to discuss how the administration's sweeping tariff plans could affect their import-heavy business models.[1] A White House official had earlier told CNBC that a representative of Lowe's would also attend, though after the meeting an official told NBC News that no one from Lowe's had been present.[1] The three companies issued nearly identical statements afterwards, saying they "had a productive meeting with President Trump and his team and appreciated the opportunity to share our insights."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued on the Badlands program *The Narrative* that major corporations such as Apple, Walmart and Amazon should be understood as nation states rather than companies, and that observing the net effects of the trade war reveals who its real targets are: "Capitulation comes in many forms."[1]

## Comparison with Target, May 2024

In the week before [[target-corporation|Target]]'s May 2024 earnings miss, Walmart — Target's larger rival — reported better-than-expected results and raised its annual outlook as shoppers prioritized food and essentials such as toilet paper and detergent.[2] Analysts used the divergence to argue that Target was losing market share, though Charles Sizemore of Sizemore Capital Management cautioned that it was "not accurate to compare" the two, since Walmart is the biggest U.S. grocery retailer.[2]
