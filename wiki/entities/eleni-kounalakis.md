# Eleni Kounalakis

Eleni Kounalakis is the Lieutenant Governor of California, a Democrat, who in December 2023 sought to have former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] removed from the state's 2024 presidential primary ballot.[1]

## Ballot removal campaign

On 20 December 2023, one day after the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s 4–3 decision disqualifying Trump in that state, Kounalakis wrote to Secretary of State [[shirley-weber|Shirley Weber]] urging her to explore any and all legal options to ensure California voters could not cast a ballot for Trump.[1] Citing the Colorado ruling, she accused Trump of insurrection during the 6 January 2021 breach of the Capitol and concluded that Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] disqualified him from holding office.[1]

Weber rejected the request and certified Trump on the primary ballot on 28 December 2023.[1] Badlands commentary framed Kounalakis's effort as part of a wider pattern of officials attempting to remove political opposition administratively rather than at the polls, contrasting it unfavourably with the stance taken by Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[1]

## December 2023 letter on Trump's ballot eligibility

A day after the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] disqualified former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from that state's 2024 ballots, Kounalakis sent a letter dated Wednesday, December 20, 2023 to California Secretary of State [[shirley-weber|Shirley Weber]] asking her to "explore every legal option" to do the same in California.[2] She wrote that "this decision is about honoring the rule of law in our country and protecting the fundamental pillars of our democracy," citing the holding in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]] (2023 CO 63) that Trump's insurrection disqualifies him under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] and that it would be a "wrongful act" for a secretary of state to list an ineligible candidate.[2] Kounalakis, who had launched a campaign for governor of California in 2026, told Weber that the state "must stand on the right side of history" and was "obligated to determine" whether the former president was ineligible on the same grounds.[2]
