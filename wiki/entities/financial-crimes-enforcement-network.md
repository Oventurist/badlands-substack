# Financial Crimes Enforcement Network

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is the financial crimes intelligence unit of the United States government, operating as a bureau of the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]].[1] It collects, analyzes and disseminates financial intelligence used to detect money laundering, sanctions evasion and fraud.[1]

## Whistleblower portal (2026)

On February 13, 2026, the Treasury announced a new website to accept confidential whistleblower tips concerning fraud, money laundering and sanctions violations, expanding federal efforts against financial crime.[1] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said the portal, operated through FinCEN's Office of the Whistleblower, would allow individuals to report suspected misconduct involving violations of the [[bank-secrecy-act|Bank Secrecy Act]], U.S. sanctions programs and other financial laws.[1] Tipsters whose information leads to successful enforcement actions may be eligible for financial awards, and the Treasury encouraged submitters to provide detailed documentation supporting their claims.[1]

The initiative was framed as a way to strengthen enforcement tools and encourage reporting by offering a confidential channel backed by monetary incentives, coming amid broader federal scrutiny of financial abuses and fraud schemes.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the FinCEN program improved on earlier proposals for privately funded political "bounty" schemes, which suffered from subjective metrics and corruptible incentive structures, because it is financed from fines imposed on guilty parties and focuses on objective, traceable money flows.[1] He described forensic accounting as the ideal method of mapping criminal networks and characterized the program as "weaponizing the financial system against itself" by paying insiders, bureaucrats and accountants to report misconduct.[1]

FinCEN's international counterpart network, the [[egmont-group|Egmont Group]], has featured in Badlands' *Breaking History* discussions of the same subject.[1]
