# Defense Council (Iran)

The Defense Council is the Iranian body reported in 2026 to have been created to organize a restructured national command hierarchy.[1] Badlands coverage treats it as the specific apparatus cited in the Iranian constitution as the mechanism for reorganizing command infrastructure in the event of a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] (COG) protocol.[1]

## Formation and significance

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[iran|Iran]] had implemented a Continuity of Government protocol the previous August that fundamentally transformed the command hierarchy of the Iranian government and military, according to news reports and public statements by regime figures.[1] Under this account the traditional intelligence and [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] hierarchies were significantly restructured, with the Defense Council formed to organize the new command structure, and the Ayatollah ostensibly no longer wielding much power.[1]

He argued that mainstream outlets reporting on the creation of the Defense Council were implicitly acknowledging that a COG protocol was underway, since the Council is the constitutional mechanism for such a reorganization — while allowing that mainstream reporters might simply not understand the concept's implications.[1] The related package of Iranian emergency arrangements is covered at [[iran-continuity-of-government-measures-2026|Iran Continuity of Government Measures (2026)]].[1]

## Context

The reporting emerged as U.S. intelligence agencies assessed how Tehran might react if President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] unilaterally declared victory and began drawing down forces after the two-month U.S.–Israel war with Iran entered a fragile ceasefire phase.[1] Trump had rejected Iran's latest peace proposal, delivered via Pakistan, which called for staged talks addressing hostilities and the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] blockade before any discussion of the nuclear program.[1] In this framing, the changed leadership structure that the Defense Council represents was one of the two main developments finally being acknowledged by mainstream reporting.[1]
