# Daniel Perry

Daniel Perry is a former U.S. Army sergeant who was convicted of murder and sentenced to 25 years in prison for shooting an armed [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] protester who wielded an AK-47, and who was granted a full pardon by Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] on May 16, 2024.[1]

Abbott's proclamation granted Perry a full pardon and restoration of full civil rights of citizenship, and accused Travis County District Attorney [[jose-garza|Jose Garza]] of directing the lead investigator to withhold exculpatory evidence and of "unethical and biased misuse" of his office in the prosecution.[1]

The [[texas-board-of-pardons-and-paroles|Texas Board of Pardons and Paroles]] had earlier that day issued a pardon recommendation, stating that it had "delved into the intricacies" of Perry's case, reviewing police reports, court records, witness statements, and interviews with people linked to the case, and that it "voted unanimously to recommend a full pardon and restoration of firearm rights."[1]

Garza's office called the board's decision and the governor's pardon a "mockery of our legal system," saying, "Their actions are contrary to the law and demonstrate that there are two classes of people in this state where some lives matter and some lives do not."[1]
