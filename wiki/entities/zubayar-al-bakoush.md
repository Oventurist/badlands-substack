# Zubayar Al-Bakoush

Zubayar Al-Bakoush is a Libyan national accused of participating in the September 11, 2012 attack on the U.S. diplomatic mission in Benghazi, Libya. On February 6, 2026, U.S. authorities transported him to Virginia and he appeared in federal court in Washington, D.C., where he was ordered detained pending further proceedings.[1]

He is charged in an unsealed eight-count indictment filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia that includes murder, attempted murder, arson, terrorism, conspiracy, and conspiracy to provide material support to terrorism for his alleged role in the assault, which killed four Americans.[1] Al-Bakoush had been sought by the United States for more than a decade before his transfer into U.S. custody, and his arrest was the first Benghazi-related arrest in over eight years.[1]

## Context within the Benghazi prosecutions

Al-Bakoush is the third individual charged in U.S. courts in connection with the [[benghazi-attack|Benghazi attack]]; the Libyan militants [[ahmed-abu-khatallah|Ahmed Abu Khatallah]] and [[mustafa-al-imam|Mustafa al-Imam]] were convicted in earlier prosecutions, while another suspect, Ali Awni al-Harzi, was killed in a 2015 airstrike in Iraq.[1] Justice Department officials, including Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and U.S. Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]], used public remarks on the extradition to emphasize the government's continuing commitment to accountability for the 2012 deaths.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the arrest arrives "too little too late" for survivors and that genuine accountability would require scrutiny of American officials rather than a single foreign defendant.[1] Benghazi annex contractor [[john-tiegen|John "Tig" Tiegen]] told her the operation was "a waste of time and resources," questioning the cost of tracking, transporting and prosecuting a suspect he described as "maybe number 36, not even top 20," while allowing that Al-Bakoush might have been "captured him for interrogation on more recent stuff."[1]
