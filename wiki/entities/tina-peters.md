# Tina Peters

Tina Peters is a former county clerk in Colorado who became a prominent figure in American election-integrity activism after being prosecuted in connection with the copying of voting-system data from equipment under her official custody. Within the circles covered by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] she is widely regarded as a political prisoner rather than an offender, and her case has served as a rallying point for commentators who dispute the security and auditability of electronic voting systems.

## Commutation

According to the announcement for [[badlands-blitz-episode-18|Badlands Blitz Episode 18]], published May 23, 2026, the commutation of Peters' sentence was a leading subject of that week's discussion, with the episode's subtitle rendering the development as "Tina Peters Coming Out." The source treats the commutation as a significant and welcome turn in a case its authors had followed closely, and pairs it in the same episode with commentary on what the post calls "the coming ban on antisemitism and other speech laws" — an implicit framing of both matters as questions of state power over dissenting citizens. The source does not specify the authority that granted the commutation, its date, or its terms.

## March 2026 clemency fight

The Badlands Brief of March 5, 2026 reported a sudden shift in Peters's situation. On Monday, March 2, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] to "Free Tina Peters," and the following day Colorado Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] signaled on X that he was open to commuting her sentence. Polis drew an explicit comparison to former state Senator [[sonya-jaquez-lewis|Sonya Jaquez Lewis]], convicted of four felonies including attempt to influence a public official and sentenced only to probation and community service, writing that Peters "as a non-violent first time offender got a nine year sentence" for "the exact same felony charge," and that "justice in Colorado and America needs to be applied evenly." He extended the deadline for clemency applications to April 3, 2026.

Peters's attorney [[john-case|John Case]] responded with a statement to Badlands Media's Ashe Epp thanking the governor, contrasting Peters's imprisonment with Jaquez Lewis's fine and 150 hours of public service, and naming Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]], Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]] and [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]]-associated county clerk [[matt-crane|Matt Crane]] as "political enemies" who had publicly demanded that Polis show no mercy. Asked whether a clemency application had been filed, a member of Peters's team said only, "There is no missing paperwork" — which [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] speculated might reflect the legal team's theory that a presidential pardon would suffice. Her commentary also reported, without confirmation, that Peters was or would be charged over a prison altercation the previous month, and characterized Peters as the Colorado legal establishment's "most valuable political scalp" and the last support for the [[election-denial|election denialism]] narrative.
