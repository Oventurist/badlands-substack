# Zhao Lijian

Zhao Lijian (rendered "Lijian Zhou" in some accounts) is a Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman who became one of the earliest official voices to publicly question the claim that SARS-CoV-2 emerged as a naturally occurring evolutionary event.[1] In March 2020 he made international waves by circulating two articles by writer Larry Romanov arguing for the possibility of "gene targeting" in the virus, a hypothesis advanced to explain the disproportionately severe early impact of the outbreak on Iranians, Italians and various Asian genotypes.[1]

Coverage of his remarks noted that in the first months of the declared pandemic, hundreds of Chinese and Iranian figures — including high-level Iranian military officers — collapsed and died with symptoms bearing little apparent similarity to what was being encountered in the broader western population, a pattern that Zhao's shared material treated as evidence against a purely natural origin.[1] His intervention amounted to a suggestion that the US Army might have brought the epidemic to Wuhan, and it placed the Chinese government early on the record as rejecting the framing that would later dominate western discussion.[1]

Zhao was subsequently joined in questioning the natural-origin consensus by bioweapons scholar [[francis-boyle|Francis Boyle]], virologists [[luc-montagnier|Luc Montagnier]] and [[judy-mikovits|Judy Mikovits]], and a growing array of scientists and academics who assessed that the virus's apparent gene sequencing implied human handiwork.[1] Later Chinese official commentary in the same vein came from epidemiologist [[zeng-guang|Zeng Guang]] and fellow Foreign Ministry spokesperson [[hua-chunying|Hua Chunying]], both of whom redirected attention toward the [[pentagon|Pentagon's]] globally extended bioweapons laboratory network rather than the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]].[1]
