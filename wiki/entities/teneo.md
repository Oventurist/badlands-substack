# Teneo

Teneo, formally Teneo Holdings, is a multinational C-suite advisory firm and investment bank founded in June 2011 by [[doug-band|Doug Band]], [[declan-kelly|Declan Kelly]] and [[paul-keary|Paul Keary]]. In the Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 2, [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats Teneo as the commercial hinge of an alleged pay-for-play network connecting the [[state-department|State Department]] under Secretary [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]].

## Founding

According to *Politico*, planning for the firm began in 2009 — the year Hillary Clinton became Secretary of State. Weeks before taking his unpaid State Department post as economic envoy to Northern Ireland, Kelly created his own private consulting firm, while Keary created two others; all three were later merged under the Teneo umbrella. The official launch was delayed until June 2011 because Kelly, on leaving [[fti-consulting|FTI Consulting]] in July 2009, had signed a two-year noncompete agreement that nonetheless permitted him to keep consulting part-time for [[dow-chemical|Dow Chemical]] and Coca-Cola. The article argues that the delay was used productively: Kelly built corporate relationships from inside the State Department while Keary assembled a client book and Band cultivated the Clinton Foundation.

Several employees of Kelly's envoy office simultaneously listed Declan Kelly Consulting on their résumés and became among Teneo's first employees; because they were not on the State Department payroll, *Politico* reported, their work escaped the oversight applied to department staff.

## Relationship with the Clintons

*Politico* reported that Teneo would go on to employ numerous Hillary Clinton associates, including [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], and for a period retained [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] as "honorary chairman," giving clients rare access to the Clintons and their network of world leaders. The article states that Teneo paid Bill Clinton $2.5 million per year as a client and paid adviser, and that the arrangement received State Department approval while Hillary Clinton led the department.

Abedin was paid a $15,000-a-month consulting fee by Teneo over a seven-month period while simultaneously serving as a "special government employee" at the State Department, according to *The Washington Times*. Among the projects she worked on was a September 20, 2012 reception at the Essex House in New York City — days after the [[benghazi|Benghazi]] attack — at which Bill Clinton, [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and [[tony-blair|Tony Blair]] spoke before potential Teneo clients.

## Clients and events

Teneo's early accounts included Dow Chemical and Coca-Cola, both carried over from Kelly's FTI relationships. Keary won the business of the USA bid committee for the [[fifa|FIFA]] World Cup in 2010 and of the Swiss bank [[ubs|UBS]]. A former employee told *Politico* that Teneo helped arrange the "Revitalizing America" event series beginning in May 2011, at which UBS executive Bob McCann appeared on stage with Bill Clinton and George W. Bush; McClatchy reported that Clinton received $1.5 million for his participation.

## Interpretation in the corpus

The Badlands account emphasizes that Teneo was not merely a consulting firm but an investment bank, which the author says "come in real handy when you're trying to launder large sums of money." In this reading, Abedin served as liaison between Clinton Foundation donors seeking favors and Hillary Clinton's State Department, with Teneo functioning as the intermediary, and [[prince-alwaleed|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] gaining access to all three institutions through Abedin. These interpretive claims are contested; the underlying employment, payment and client relationships derive from mainstream reporting.
