# Bennie Thompson

Bennie Thompson is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from Mississippi who chaired the select committee investigating the events of January 6, 2021.[1]

## Role on the January 6 Committee

Thompson chaired the [[january-6-committee|January 6th Select Committee]] during the previous Congress. The panel referred evidence to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] concerning what it described as an extensive plot by the former president to overturn the 2020 presidential election and prevent the peaceful transfer of power.[1]

## Response to the August 2023 Trump indictment

Shortly after a Washington, D.C. grand jury returned a four-count indictment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on August 1, 2023 in connection with the 2021 riot at the U.S. Capitol, Thompson posted on X (formerly Twitter) claiming credit for the now-defunct committee and describing it as "bipartisan."[1]

"And, at the end of its investigation, the bipartisan January 6th Select Committee turned over to the Justice Department its evidence of an extensive plot by the ex-President to overturn the Presidential election on January 6th and prevent the peaceful transfer of power," Thompson wrote.[1] He added that "Today's charges are consistent with those the Select Committee referred to the Special Counsel last year, and successful prosecutions will not only bring accountability but also help prevent something like January 6th from ever happening again."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated Thompson's victory lap as one more iteration of what he called an aging formula, arguing that indicting Trump had become "the Fast and Furious of Deep State ploys with endless iterations of the same cheap, thoughtless stratagem."[1] DeLarme situated the committee's framing within a longer institutional pattern in which the security state seeks to convince Americans that the gravest threat is domestic rather than foreign, drawing a line from [[cointelpro|COINTELPRO]] through the [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]] informant networks to the handling of January 6.[1]

## Fallout from the 2024 oversight findings

Preliminary findings released in March 2024 by the [[house-administration-subcommittee-on-oversight|House Administration Subcommittee on Oversight]] concluded that the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] Thompson chaired had refused to adopt rules, hidden transcribed interviews contradicting its narrative, failed to turn over evidence used in its prime-time hearings, and colluded with [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] on her prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called for accountability beginning with [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], Thompson, and Yogananda Pittman, as well as [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] and [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]].[2]

## Deleted encrypted files

In January 2024 the Republican-led House Oversight subcommittee led by [[barry-loudermilk|Barry Loudermilk]] disclosed that the [[january-6-committee|January 6 select committee]] Thompson had chaired deleted more than 100 encrypted files days before Republicans retook the House majority.[3] House rules required the select committee to turn over all documents from its investigation, and Thompson reportedly said he would transmit four terabytes of archived data; the material actually handed over came to less than three terabytes.[3] A digital forensics team hired to scrape the hard drives determined that 117 files had been encrypted and deleted on January 1, 2023, days before the transfer deadline, and recovered all 117.[3]
