# Warner Bros. Discovery

Warner Bros. Discovery is an American media and entertainment conglomerate whose 2026 merger and takeover maneuvering placed it at the center of a consolidation battle among Hollywood studios and streaming platforms.[1]

## 2026 Paramount and Netflix talks

In February 2026, Warner Bros. Discovery reopened talks with [[paramount-global|Paramount]] about potential business arrangements after receiving a waiver from [[netflix|Netflix]].[1] The development was disclosed in a new regulatory filing from Warner Bros. Discovery on Tuesday, February 17, 2026, and AP News reported that Warner was reopening takeover talks with Skydance-owned Paramount while still backing its existing merger agreement with Netflix.[1]

Any deal among these players would require regulatory approval, because it would involve broadcast assets owned by Paramount — including [[cbs|CBS]] — and the licenses attached to those assets.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] that "Paramount and Warner are wigging all the way out," linking the renewed consolidation talk to studio anxiety over AI-driven content generation and intellectual-property disputes.[1] Drawing on the "spider and starfish" metaphor for centralized versus decentralized organizations, she wrote that "a 'spider' centralizes when it perceives a new threat," and that by merging, the studios were consolidating into a single vulnerable head: "If you cut off a spider's head, it dies."[1] She noted the talks between Paramount and Warner had previously been dead, as discussed in a December brief, and expressed surprise that "the movie studios didn't learn from the music studios," arguing they should be transforming their business models rather than defending a dying one.[1]

## Paramount acquisition (2026)

In 2026 Warner Bros. Discovery became the target of a $111 billion acquisition by Paramount, financed in large part by Gulf sovereign wealth funds including approximately $12 billion from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund as part of a $24 billion equity package with Qatari and Abu Dhabi investors.[2] More than 1,000 Hollywood writers, actors, and directors — among them Bryan Cranston, Joaquin Phoenix, Tiffany Haddish, Lily Gladstone, Denis Villeneuve, and J.J. Abrams — signed an open letter on April 13, 2026 opposing the deal, organized by the Committee for the First Amendment and the Future Film Coalition.[2]

## December 2023 merger talks

The first publicly reported round of merger discussions between the two companies surfaced in December 2023, when Warner Bros. Discovery chief executive [[david-zaslav|David Zaslav]] met Paramount Global chief executive [[bob-bakish|Bob Bakish]] for a lunch meeting in New York on Tuesday, December 19, 2023 to discuss a possible combination.[3] Zaslav also spoke with Shari Redstone, whose National Amusements Inc. holds a controlling stake in Paramount Global, about a potential tie-up; representatives for both companies declined to comment, and [[axios|Axios]] first reported the talks.[3]

Terms could not be learned and the status of the discussions was described as very preliminary, though both companies had enlisted bankers.[3] As of the end of the third quarter of 2023, Paramount Global reported long-term debt of $15.6 billion, considerably less than Warner Bros. Discovery's debt load of $43.5 billion; by market value, however, Warner Bros. Discovery was the larger company, with a market capitalization of $28.4 billion as of the close of trading on December 20, 2023 against $10.3 billion for Paramount Global.[3] The two firms were reported to be seeking to pool assets spanning television, film, sports, and streaming for greater scale and operational efficiency, with particular interest in combining the Max and Paramount+ premium streaming services to compete with [[netflix|Netflix]], [[disney|Disney]]'s Disney+, and Hulu.[3]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the talks in a macroeconomic frame, repeating his maxim that "consolidation precedes collapse" and arguing that the trend was accelerating.[3] He cautioned readers not to read blockbuster deal headlines as evidence of economic viability: in his view a smaller company seeking to merge with a larger one does so "because it HAS to," reflecting an accounting shell game in which firms nominally worth tens of billions operate on single-digit margins before facing market backlash to sub-par offerings.[3] He predicted many more such headlines heading into 2024.[3]

## $9.1 billion writedown (August 2024)

In August 2024 shares of Warner Bros. Discovery, parent of [[cnn|CNN]] and TNT, crashed 10% to their lowest level on record after the company reported results that missed across the board and plunged across every income statement category.[4] The biggest hit, and the surprise of the report, was a $9.1 billion charge written down against the value of its traditional TV networks such as CNN and TNT, which had been acquired in 2022 when Warner Bros. Discovery was created through its acquisition of WarnerMedia.[4] The writedown confirmed that legacy cable channels were no longer worth what they had been when the $42 billion merger was completed, and commentary argued that, judging by ongoing mass layoffs at CNN, the network's value was effectively negative and would remain so until it stopped hemorrhaging cash.[4]

For the quarter, Warner Bros. Discovery reported a net loss of $10 billion, including additional charges of $2.1 billion related to the merger, while revenue fell 6.2% to $9.71 billion; the revenue collapse in the Networks segment that includes CNN continued unabated.[4]

## Netflix agreement and Paramount's hostile bid (December 2025)

On Friday, December 5, 2025, [[netflix|Netflix]] announced an $83 billion deal to buy a large part of Warner Bros. Discovery, an agreement approved by the boards of both companies that excluded the company's portfolio of cable channels.[5] The following Monday, [[paramount-global|Paramount]] went around the Warner Bros. Discovery board directly to shareholders with a competing all-cash offer of $30 per share, valuing the company at around $108 billion including debt and covering the entire business — the Warner Bros. movie studio, the HBO Max streaming service and cable channels including CNN.[5]

Paramount said it bypassed the board because it was "pursuing an inferior proposal" that would lead to "a challenging regulatory approval process," while chief executive [[david-ellison|David Ellison]] argued the bid would "create a stronger Hollywood."[5]

## Ellison acquisition interest (September 2025)

In September 2025, [[david-ellison|David Ellison]] was reported to be mulling a deal then worth more than $70 billion to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery in its entirety, only five weeks after his [[skydance-media|Skydance Media]] completed an $8 billion takeover of [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] and while the merged company was still cutting upwards of 2,000 jobs.[6] Variety framed the timing as an attempt to beat rival suitors to the punch.[6]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that an Ellison-backed bid would place cultural assets including [[hbo|HBO]], [[cnn|CNN]], Cartoon Network, DC, Harry Potter and Game of Thrones under sovereign-aligned ownership, describing the establishment's hysteria over the prospective acquisitions as evidence that incumbents feared losing their grip on American storytelling and narrative control.[6]
