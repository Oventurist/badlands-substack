# Commonwealth Fusion Systems

Commonwealth Fusion Systems (CFS) is a fusion power company founded in 2018 in Cambridge, Massachusetts, which in December 2024 unveiled plans to build what state officials and company leaders described as the world's first grid-scale commercial fusion power plant, in Virginia.[1]

## Chesterfield County project

The company announced plans for a 100-acre site at the James River Industrial Park in Chesterfield County, with the plant itself occupying about 25 acres.[1] The facility is expected to generate 400 megawatts of electricity — enough to power roughly 150,000 homes — and could be operational by the early 2030s.[1] Governor [[glenn-youngkin|Glenn Youngkin]] announced the project at Richmond's Patrick Henry Building, declaring that CFS "plans on building the world's first grid scale commercial fusion power plant in the world, full stop, and it's going to be right here in the commonwealth of Virginia."[1] The land is leased from [[dominion-energy|Dominion Energy]].[1]

The announcement came as Virginia's energy demand surged, driven by rapid growth in data centers serving big-technology operations, which consume large amounts of electricity and water for processing and cooling.[1]

## Technology

CFS relies on a donut-shaped device called a tokamak to confine and fuse molecules.[1] Co-founder and chief executive officer Bob Mumgaard said "you won't need a pipeline to bring the fuel in, or a smokestack for the exhaust."[1] Alex Creely, the company's director of tokamak operations, explained that unlike fission — which splits heavy atoms such as uranium and leaves radioactive waste — fusion creates energy by fusing light atoms: "One of the big advantages of fusion is that it doesn't produce any long lived waste material, and there's no risk of some kind of meltdown event... It's a very safe energy source — something that you can live right next to and feel very comfortable with."[1]

By contrast, conventional light-water reactors generate electricity through steam: the reactor's heat flash-boils water, and the resulting steam spins a turbine whose kinetic energy is converted to electricity, a process fundamentally similar to a hydroelectric dam except for the irradiated water requiring deep underground storage.[1] Badlands commentary noted that the visible plumes from conventional nuclear cooling towers are water vapor rather than smoke.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the project as a rebuke to what he characterized as decades of deliberate obstruction of Mid-Atlantic energy infrastructure, referencing the [[mountain-valley-pipeline|Mountain Valley Pipeline]] and the cancelled [[atlantic-coast-pipeline|Atlantic Coast Pipeline]], and calling the fusion plant "a huge middle finger to oligarchs like [[george-soros|George Soros]]."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] was cited as having written extensively about emerging technologies expected to usher in a new epoch.[1]
