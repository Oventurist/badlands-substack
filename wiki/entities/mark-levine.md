# Mark Levine

Mark Levine is the Comptroller of the City of New York, the municipality's independently elected fiscal watchdog.[1]

## Fiscal year 2027 budget dispute

Levine responded critically to the preliminary $127 billion fiscal year 2027 budget released on February 17, 2026 by Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]], which proposed a 9.5 percent increase in property tax rates as a means of closing an inherited $5.4 billion deficit.[1] The comptroller characterized the property tax route as "a pretty extreme option," signaling institutional resistance to what would be the city's first such rate increase in more than two decades.[1] As comptroller, Levine's assessment carries weight in the city's budget negotiations because his office audits agency spending and certifies the assumptions underlying the mayor's revenue projections.[1]
