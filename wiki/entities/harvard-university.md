# Harvard University

Harvard University is an American private research university whose leadership became the subject of national controversy in December 2023 when its president, Dr. [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]], was accused of plagiarism in her academic work.[1]

## 2023 plagiarism scandal

[[new-york-times|The New York Times]] published excerpts comparing Gay's scholarship with that of other researchers, documenting passages lifted verbatim and instances of minor rewording such as substituting "popular saying" for "adage."[1] The episode raised institutional questions about whether Harvard applied different standards of conduct to faculty than to students, and whether the university would permit its president to revise research dating from 1992 in response to the allegations.[1]

Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] speculated that the public plagiarism story might be functioning as cover for a different, private crisis at the university, drawing an analogy to the way earlier scandals had displaced one another in the news cycle.[1] She also questioned why the Times had chosen to cover the matter rather than bury it.[1]

## Harvard/Harris polling criticism

In June 2023 the Harvard/Harris poll reported that 55 percent of Americans considered the indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] politically motivated and 56 percent considered it election interference.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the survey as useful chiefly for reading how the regime interprets shifting voter sentiment rather than as a reliable measurement.[2] She observed that the 60-page results presentation contained no mention of a margin of error — a standard indicator of polling reliability — and that although the methodology summary described weighting for age within gender, region, race/ethnicity, marital status, household size, income, employment, education, political party and political ideology, plus propensity score weighting for online respondents, Harvard supplied no specific data on the weighting methods used.[2]

Ashe in America characterized this as a "trust us, bro" posture, argued that the true share of voters viewing the indictment as political and as 2024 election interference is likely higher than reported, and pointed to a misleading use of colors in the side-by-side charts, concluding that Harvard "is not a reliable source of truth."[2] See [[harvard-harris-poll-on-the-trump-indictment|Harvard/Harris poll on the Trump indictment]].[2]

## Revocation of international student certification (2025)

In May 2025 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] said it was halting Harvard University's ability to enroll international students and ordered existing international students at the university to transfer or lose their legal status.[3] The administration notified Harvard of the decision following ongoing correspondence over the "legality of a sprawling records request," according to reporting citing three people familiar with the matter.[3] The records request formed part of an investigation by the homeland security department in which federal officials threatened the university's international student admissions.[3]

Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] posted a copy of the letter on X, writing: "I am writing to inform you that effective immediately, Harvard University's Student and Exchange Visitor Program certification is revoked."[3]

## 2025 federal funding confrontation

In April 2025, the Wall Street Journal reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was planning to cut an additional $1 billion in health research funding for Harvard as the administration's row with elite schools escalated.[4] The administration had already withheld government funding from Harvard, Columbia, and other universities in response to pro-Palestinian demonstrations, in what the [[white-house|White House]] labelled a failure to control antisemitism on campus.[4]

Harvard pushed back the previous week, rejecting demands for control over its student body, faculty, and curriculum on the grounds that acceding would cede control of the university to the government.[4] Soon after that response, the administration announced it would freeze $2.2 billion in multi-year grants to the school and threatened to strip Harvard of its tax-exempt status.[4] Trump also publicly denounced the university on two separate occasions, calling Harvard a "joke" and a "disgrace."[4]

## Governing board deliberations over Claudine Gay (December 2023)

In December 2023, Harvard's governing board held a marathon closed-door meeting over the future of university president [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]], whose congressional testimony on campus antisemitism had provoked a flurry of criticism.[5] Two people familiar with the matter told *The New York Times* on Monday night, December 11, that the board was close to a decision to keep Gay in her job, though the meeting remained ongoing and no announcement was expected before Tuesday.[5] Gay's remarks — including her statement that whether calling for the "genocide of Jews" violated Harvard's code of conduct "can be, depending on the context" — had drawn calls for her resignation and threats from hundreds of alumni to withdraw donations, and she later apologized.[5]

Countervailing support emerged from within the university community: the Executive Committee of the Harvard Alumni Association declared that it "unanimously and unequivocally supports" Gay, and more than 600 faculty members signed an open letter demanding that the governing board not bend to political pressure to fire her.[5]


## Lawsuit Over the 2025 Federal Funding Freeze

Harvard recruited law firms and attorneys with ties to allies of [[donald-trump|President Trump]] to sue his administration over the freezing of billions of dollars in federal grants and contracts.[6] The suit, filed in federal court in Massachusetts, listed lawyers from four firms and named a long roster of executive agency officials and Trump appointees as defendants, including Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]].[6] The complaint argued the freeze would disrupt research developing drugs for Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases, nanofibers to protect servicemembers and first responders, support for astronauts, and an artificial intelligence system for diagnosing and treating cancer.[6] Among the firms was Quinn Emanuel, a Los Angeles-headquartered practice that has long represented [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who donated $250 million to Trump's campaign and became the public face of the White House [[doge|DOGE]] office.[6]

## Morgue body-parts case (2025)

In May 2025, [[cedric-lodge|Cedric Lodge]], the manager of the [[harvard-medical-school|Harvard Medical School]] morgue, pleaded guilty to a federal charge of interstate transport of human remains after stealing and selling heads, brains, skins, bones, and other remains over a five-year period.[7] Federal prosecutors in the Middle District of Pennsylvania said a Pennsylvania buyer paid Lodge's wife Denise $37,355.16 over three years via PayPal, with memos including "head number 7" and "braiiiiiiins."[7] Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] remarked on the coincidence of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posting about Harvard the same night the case was being discussed on air.[7]

## Court ruling voiding the $2.2 billion funding freeze (2025)

On Wednesday, September 3, 2025, a federal judge ruled that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's freeze of $2.2 billion in grant funds for Harvard over concerns about campus antisemitism and other issues was illegal, calling it "a targeted, ideologically-motivated assault on this country's premier universities."[8] Judge [[allison-burroughs|Allison Burroughs]] agreed with Harvard's arguments that the freeze had been imposed in retaliation for the university's refusal to capitulate to demands for reforms that violated First Amendment protections under the Constitution.[8]

Burroughs' ruling in U.S. District Court in Massachusetts vacated the freezing orders affecting Harvard and barred anyone in the Trump administration from enforcing them; the [[white-house|White House]] said it would appeal.[8] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] objected that the decision compelled taxpayer grants to an institution holding a roughly $55 billion endowment.[8]

## April 2025 push to revoke tax-exempt status

In April 2025 the Trump administration sought to revoke Harvard's tax-exempt status, a move reported as potentially undercutting the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]]'s efforts to assure the public that politics plays no part in tax enforcement.[9] Republicans had for years contended that the IRS was weaponized against conservatives, making it notable that it was President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] who publicly raised the prospect of the agency stripping a valuable tax benefit from a perceived opponent.[9]

Speaking in the Oval Office on Thursday, April 17, 2025, Trump alleged that the Biden administration had engaged in "very, very illegal" use of the IRS for political ends.[9] Asked why his own moves against Harvard differed from what he accused Democrats of doing, he replied: "Because I think Harvard's a disgrace. I think what they did was a disgrace," describing the school as anti-Semitic.[9] He also hinted at future actions against other universities and nonprofits, naming [[citizens-for-responsibility-and-ethics-in-washington|Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington]] (CREW), a watchdog group suing the administration over records-law violations.[9]

The request that the IRS cancel Harvard's exemption — which allows the university to receive tax-deductible donations and avoid income taxes on net earnings — formed part of a multipronged administration battle against the institution.[9]

## March 2025 federal contract review

On March 31, 2025, the Trump administration announced it would initiate a review of federal contracts with Harvard, describing it as an inquiry similar to the one conducted at [[columbia-university|Columbia University]], which had $400 million in funding paused and had yet to see it restored despite agreeing to all of the administration's stated demands.[10] The Departments of Education and Health and Human Services and the US General Services Administration were to assess $255.6 million in contracts between the federal government and Harvard and its affiliates, along with more than $8.7 billion in multiyear grant commitments.[10] The review was to determine whether any funding needed to be paused and was described as part of the efforts of the [[joint-task-force-to-combat-anti-semitism|Joint Task Force to Combat Anti-Semitism]].[10]

## December 2023 early-application decline

In December 2023, ZeroHedge, citing new Bloomberg data, reported that Harvard College received 17 percent fewer early-admission applications from high school seniors than the prior year — 7,921 non-binding early applications versus 9,553.[11] The outlet framed the drop as blowback against president [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]], whom it said had done more damage to the school's reputation than anyone in its nearly 500-year history, citing her handling of campus antisemitism and a perceived shift away from educational excellence toward "toxic woke narratives and political indoctrination."[11] Applications had been due on November 1, however, more than a month before Gay's appearance before lawmakers on Capitol Hill at which she declined to state plainly whether "calling for the genocide of Jews" constitutes bullying and harassment under the school's code of conduct.[11]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] highlighted that chronology, noting that the application data predated the plagiarism and antisemitism scandals and was obscured by a sensational headline.[11] He described Harvard as the epicenter of a "social circular firing squad" pattern in academia and as the symbolic heart of what he termed the Woke Industrial Complex, arguing that American universities were being allowed to destroy themselves in full public view.[11]

## Board defense of Claudine Gay (December 2023)

On December 12, 2023, Harvard's governing board issued a full-throated defense of President [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]], a week after her congressional testimony on campus antisemitism and amid allegations that she had plagiarized portions of her dissertation, including from the work of political scientist [[carol-swain|Carol Swain]].[12] Badlands commentary argued that the Ivy League's "facade of intellectual superiority" collapses when the head of such an institution appears incapable of original thought, and read the establishment's decision to protect Gay at the expense of institutional credibility as "a deviation" and "panic."[12]

## Affirmative action ruling (June 2023)

Harvard College, described as the Ivy League titan whose name has symbolized achievement and power for centuries, was one of the two institutions whose admissions practices were before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in the June 2023 [[affirmative-action|affirmative action]] cases, alongside the [[university-of-north-carolina|University of North Carolina]].[14] Harvard maintained that, consistent with decades of Supreme Court precedent, a minority applicant's race could serve as an unenumerated plus factor raising the chances of admission, and lower courts agreed, rejecting the lawsuits organized by [[edward-blum|Edward Blum]].[14] On June 29, 2023 the Court ruled 6–3 that considering race in university admissions is unconstitutional, forcing Harvard and peer institutions to rework their admissions criteria.[14]

## MKUltra and Cold War intelligence research

Harvard was a key site for the CIA's [[mk-ultra|MKUltra]] program, which conducted psychological experiments to develop mind-control techniques and often used universities as fronts to obscure agency involvement.[18] Harvard psychologists funded by CIA front organizations such as the Human Ecology Fund conducted experiments on unwitting subjects, including students and foreign nationals, aiming to develop interrogation and propaganda techniques that were shared with allied governments through backchannels.[18]

[[henry-a-murray|Dr. Henry Murray]]'s experiments subjected students to stress tests designed to probe their resilience and mental breaking points; among them was [[ted-kaczynski|Ted Kaczynski]], then a 16-year-old Harvard student, who endured over 200 hours of the experiments between 1959 and 1962 and experienced severe psychological abuse.[18] The CIA suppressed public knowledge of MKUltra by classifying research and using academic cover to deflect scrutiny; documents declassified in the 1970s revealed that Harvard personnel played a significant role, though initial censorship delayed exposure.[18] Harvard's prestigious reputation provided legitimacy and shielded the agency, and faculty such as Murray were likely unaware of the full extent of CIA involvement, believing they were conducting pure science.[18]

Harvard was also the setting for the recruitment of [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], founder of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], by [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] through a CIA program.[18]

## Harvard Yard encampment (April 2024)

## The "Wilted Ivy" collapse thesis

Badlands Media's May 2025 essay "Wilted Ivy" opens with the claim that Harvard, long "a monument to untouchable prestige," is scrambling to liquidate over $1 billion in private equity assets "not to innovate, but to survive."[24] Because private equity holdings are illiquid by design and universities rely on them for long-term returns insulated from market volatility, the essay argues that dumping them en masse is "the equivalent of throwing furniture overboard on a sinking ship" and constitutes a distress signal rather than strategic rebalancing.[24]

The article describes Harvard as historically "an initiation into the ruling class" whose seal was worn by presidents, bankers, media moguls and bureaucrats, and, following [[carroll-quigley|Carroll Quigley]]'s [[tragedy-and-hope|Tragedy and Hope]], as "less a neutral educator and more a strategic hub" that produced policymakers and lawyers who "reshaped America's middle class and expanded multinational control."[24] It identifies the [[harvard-management-company|Harvard Management Company]], overseeing a $53 billion endowment, as the true machinery of Harvard's power and a "single point of failure" whose destabilization ripples through the financial, political and cultural layers of the old order.[24]

The essay dates the visible unraveling to the forced resignation of president [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]] in January 2024, followed within weeks by public financial panic: the $1 billion liquidation, massive donor withdrawals, rising calls for endowment transparency, and a break in alumni loyalty.[24] It attributes the endowment's weakness not to bad financial management but to the dismantling of the captured financial system propping it up, arguing that as [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[vanguard|Vanguard]] and [[state-street|State Street]] were "neutralized or redirected under White Hat influence," Harvard lost its invisible financial safety nets.[24] Harvard, in this telling, "was never just Harvard" but a conduit for Rothschild and Rockefeller ideologies, a funnel for BlackRock, [[goldman-sachs|Goldman]] and State Street capital, and "a nerve center for globalist consensus enforcement, disguised as education" — and therefore "the first domino in the takedown."[24]
