# American Civil Liberties Union

The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) is a United States non-governmental legal advocacy organization that litigates civil-liberties claims in the federal courts. In April 2025 the organization was at the center of the litigation challenging the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's use of the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]] of 1798 to accelerate the removal of Venezuelan migrants from the United States.[1]

## Role in the Alien Enemies Act deportation litigation

The ACLU's suits produced parallel rulings on 22 April 2025 in which two federal judges extended temporary blocks on some deportations of Venezuelan migrants and signaled that the invocation of the 1798 wartime statute might not survive judicial review.[1] Denver-based District Judge [[charlotte-sweeney|Charlotte Sweeney]] ordered that migrants detained in Colorado receive 21 days' advance notice of removal and be informed of their right to challenge it, while Judge [[alvin-hellerstein|Alvin Hellerstein]] in Manhattan appeared inclined to impose a 10-day notice requirement.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributors treated the episode as an illustration of asymmetric enforcement of legal ethics. Citing reporting by [[julie-kelly|Julie Kelly]], the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] argued that the ACLU had "violated the rules and abused the legal process, repeatedly" and should be held accountable.[1] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that non-governmental organizations such as the ACLU and the [[naacp|NAACP]] frequently breach procedural rules and are excused because of their "solemn civil rights mission," while the same rules are enforced with severity against figures such as [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] and John Eastman — a pattern she described as "not justice. It's politics."[1] She stated that plaintiffs in her own case had fabricated claims, coerced witnesses and violated discovery rules without consequence.[1]

## April 2025 Alien Enemies Act emergency application

On the evening of Good Friday, April 18, 2025, the ACLU filed an emergency application with the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] seeking immediate protection for a group of Venezuelan detainees facing removal under the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]], framed as a proposed class action.[2] Its lawyers told the court they feared their clients could be deported within hours, saying some had already been loaded onto buses presumably bound for the airport.[2] The court acted around 1 a.m. Saturday, ordering the government not to remove any member of the putative class until further order.[2]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the ACLU had essentially skipped the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|Fifth Circuit]] by giving the court an ultimatum to rule within 42 minutes, calling that "not really a thing that parties can do in court" and suggesting the organization enjoys special privileges.[2] She wrote that the Supreme Court "inexplicably" rewarded the breach, and questioned why the ACLU could make such demands and why the Court would grant unprecedented relief in response.[2] The government's response argued the ACLU sought to deprive the courts of the critical fact-finding that due process demands, since no lower court had made findings about the notice given to detainees or the adequacy of the government's notice procedures.[2]

## Badlands criticism over immigration litigation (July 2025)

Commenting on [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]]'s announcement that it had arrested 214 migrants charged with or convicted of child sex offenses in the Houston area over six months, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] identified the ACLU as "the coordinating entity" behind the immigration-related legal challenges to the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s removal operations.[3] The commentary framed those challenges as obstructing the deportation of convicted child sex offenders.[3]
