# Plymouth Brethren

The Plymouth Brethren are described in the article as "a gnostic sect of pseudo-Christians" founded in 1829 by Anthony Norris Groves, characterized as an agent of the British East India Company.[1]

## Origins

Groves was sent to the Ottoman Empire and then India in 1830 as an orientalist engaged in recruiting young elites to train in British universities while carrying out espionage under the banner of Christian missionary work.[1] He was soon joined by [[john-nelson-darby]], godson of Admiral Horatio Nelson and father of modern rapture theology.[1] The sect emerged in tandem with a network of Anglican and Jesuit intelligence operatives led by [[henry-drummond]], Lord Anthony Ashley Cooper (7th Earl of Shaftesbury), and Darby.[1]

## Doctrine and reach

The Brethren propagated [[dispensationalism]] and rapture theology, which the article says required the restoration of the Jews to Palestine and, in most versions, the rebuilding of Solomon's Temple as preconditions of the End Times.[1] The article traces the "dual origins of Christian Zionism and End Times rapture theology" to this milieu rather than to scripture.[1]

## Operatives and offshoots

Brethren figures appear repeatedly in the article's imperial narrative: Sir Robert Anderson, head of Scotland Yard, worked with [[charles-warren]] to sabotage the Jack the Ripper investigation; Colonel Charles Wingate was a leading figure in the sect and raised his son [[orde-wingate]] as a Christian Zionist imperialist; [[george-hawkins-pember]] served as one of its grand strategists; and [[aleister-crowley]] emerged out of the Exclusive Branch of the sect, retaining its dispensationalist framework in his religion of Thelema.[1] William Eugene Blackstone, a Darby devotee, drafted the 1891 "Palestine for the Jews" memorandum.[1]
