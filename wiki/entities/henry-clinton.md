# Henry Clinton

Sir Henry Clinton was the British commander-in-chief in North America who launched the 1780 invasion of South Carolina, the campaign that produced the partisan war waged by [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]].[1]

## The 1780 southern invasion

In the spring of 1780, rumor reached Charleston society that Clinton had landed an enormous army thirty miles south of the city, in the direction of Savannah — the South's only deep-water port, which the British had captured six months earlier.[1] The British had spent five years in a virtual stalemate with [[george-washington|George Washington]] in the north, and the southern strategy was crafted on the assumption that the many families of noble birth in the southern colonies would remain loyal to their ancestral homeland and rise to subdue the rebellion if called upon.[1]

Clinton's [[siege-of-charleston|siege of Charleston]] succeeded in trapping and capturing an entire Continental Army inside the city.[1] As the siege began, Clinton unleashed [[banastre-tarleton|Banastre Tarleton]], ordering him to cut off all escape routes from Charleston and ride down any fleeing military units — orders that led directly to the [[waxhaws-massacre|Waxhaws massacre]].[1] Clinton had earlier rewarded Tarleton's capture of General [[charles-lee|Charles Lee]] by promoting him to lieutenant colonel and giving him a six-hundred-man unit to shape as he pleased.[1]
