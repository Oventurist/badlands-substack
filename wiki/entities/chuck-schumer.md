# Chuck Schumer

**Chuck Schumer**, then Senate Majority Leader, appears in Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s account as one of the American officials involved in efforts to remove Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

DeLarme cites Schumer's March 2024 call for new elections in [[israel|Israel]] as evidence of the Atlanticist regime change effort against Netanyahu, reading it as essentially declaring the Israeli prime minister illegitimate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) The call is listed alongside the ongoing corruption probe against Netanyahu and the [[usaid|USAID]]-funded judicial-reform protests of 2023 as part of the same campaign, which the article says ended abruptly after October 7th.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## 2026 midterm scenario-planning sessions

In June 2026, Politico reported that Schumer had convened a slate of experts to prepare Senate Democrats for potential disruptions around the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[2] The group included former Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]], Democratic election attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], election-law expert [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]], and the heads of the legal group [[democracy-forward|Democracy Forward]] and the nonprofit [[protect-democracy|Protect Democracy]].[2] They helped senators prepare for a trio of possible scenarios and boost coordination with the state and local leaders who directly oversee election administration and voter-turnout operations.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read Schumer's convening as the reconstitution of the [[shadow-cabal|shadow cabal]] described in Time magazine's February 2021 account of the 2020 election, noting that the same principals were involved and that this time the participants were announcing their plans in advance rather than waiting three months to describe them.[2]


## Democratic "Election Integrity Group" (2026)

In a May 2026 Truth Social post, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] attacked Schumer for hiring former Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] as part of a Democrat-led "Election Integrity Group" that Trump said would attempt to suppress Republican voters and interfere in the elections, and noted that Democratic lawyer [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]] was also involved.[3] Trump announced in the same post that Republicans would field a larger [[election-integrity-army|Election Integrity Army]] for the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]] in response.[3]

## Iran MOU transparency demands (June 2026)

As Senate Democratic Leader, Schumer joined some Republican senators in demanding immediate details and classified briefings on the June 14, 2026 U.S.–Iran memorandum of understanding, noting the unusual lack of transparency around an agreement with significant national security implications.[4] No text or classified briefing had been provided to congressional leaders at the time, despite [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s statement that he would send the MOU to [[congress|Congress]] for review after the formal signing.[4]

## DHS funding demands (February 2026)

In February 2026 Schumer joined House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] in sending Republican leaders a formal list of ten demands as part of congressional [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] funding negotiations.[5] The demands covered judicial warrant requirements, a ban on masks for [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agents, officer identification requirements, protection of sensitive locations, a prohibition on racial profiling, use-of-force standards, state and local oversight, detention safeguards, body-worn cameras, and standardized non-paramilitary uniforms and equipment.[5] The two leaders also called for Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] to be removed from her position.[5]

## Response to the January 6 footage release (2023)

In March 2023, after [[tucker-carlson]] aired Capitol surveillance footage contradicting elements of the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]]'s account, Schumer went to the Senate floor and denounced Carlson and [[fox-news]] for broadcasting the video.[6] [[mitch-mcconnell]] joined him in criticizing the release.[6] The [[badlands-news-brief]] framed the response as Senate leadership acting to protect an official narrative from disruption by primary-source evidence.[6]

## Record-length shutdown endgame (November 2025)

On November 4, 2025, hours before the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|government shutdown]] became the longest in U.S. history, Senate Democrats met privately for more than two hours to discuss an endgame to the 35-day standoff and ended without a clear consensus, even after senators involved in increasingly serious bipartisan negotiations laid out their thinking.[7] Emerging from the meeting, Schumer, the Senate minority leader, signaled that his party was not yet ready to surrender; a few hours later the shutdown surpassed the 34-day, 20-hour record set in January 2019.[7]

## 2024 border bill negotiations

As Senate Majority Leader, Schumer negotiated the [[bipartisan-border-bill-2024|bipartisan border bill]] of early 2024 over a period of months with Minority Leader [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]].[8] Confirming the closeness of that partnership, Schumer told reporters on Sunday, February 4, 2024, "I have never worked more closely with Leader McConnell on any piece of legislation as we did on this."[8] Badlands commentary observed that the two leaders had frequently worked in lockstep while heading their respective conferences, most recently on large spending bills and on American taxpayer subsidization of Ukraine's war effort, and treated Schumer's remark as damaging to McConnell's standing with Senate Republicans.[8]

## Linking Grok's "MechaHitler" episode to the Pentagon deal (2025)

In July 2025 Schumer publicly connected the [[grok|Grok]] chatbot's antisemitic "MechaHitler" outputs to the Department of Defense contract awarded to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[xai|xAI]] days later.[9] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] seized on the post, writing that "Chuck Schumer is just giving the game away" and arguing that Grok was turned into MechaHitler for one night precisely in order to frame the deal — with Schumer effectively announcing the connection himself.[9]

## Drone detection push (December 2024)

On Sunday, December 15, 2024, Schumer called for the federal government to deploy recently declassified radar technology to identify the mysterious drone swarms alarming the tri-state region, addressing mounting frustration soon after Governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]] announced federal deployment of a "state-of-the-art drone detection system" to New York.[10] He specifically urged the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to send the Robin, a 360-degree radar capable of quickly identifying drones and tracking them to their landing zones, noting it can be mounted on a drone or airplane and "can go anywhere."[10] "New Yorkers have tremendous questions about [the drones]. We are going to get the answers," he said, adding that he had told Homeland Security and the FBI that answers were needed quickly.[10]

## March 2025 shutdown standoff

In March 2025 Schumer, as Senate Democratic leader, managed his caucus through a funding fight over a GOP-crafted six-month stopgap spending bill passed by the House.[11] Senate Democratic sources said privately that the party would not allow the government to shut down that Saturday despite growing pressure from activists and liberal lawmakers who wanted the [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] killed, and that Schumer was giving centrists in his caucus room to vote for the House-passed measure if that was the only way to avoid a shutdown at week's end.[11] One Democratic senator familiar with internal deliberations said Senate Democrats would ultimately vote to keep the government open, while allowing that unexpected developments could still change the political calculus.[11]

Publicly, Schumer said on Wednesday, March 12, 2025, that Democrats would insist on a vote for a monthlong continuing resolution to give congressional negotiators more time to reach an omnibus spending deal, and warned that there were not enough votes to pass the House-approved funding bill.[11] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued there was no evident downside to a shutdown, welcoming the suspension of non-essential services as the downsizing he had voted for.[11] Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the Democratic posture as capitulation rather than negotiation, writing that a party which refuses an opponent's demands for three years and then accedes to them has submitted rather than bargained.[11]

## "Schumer Shutdown" and WIC funding lapse (October 2025)

Conservative coverage of the funding lapse that began on October 1, 2025 branded it the "Schumer Shutdown," attributing the impasse to Senate Democrats under Schumer's leadership.[12] Rep. [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]], R-Fla., said the shutdown had cut aid to 6.7 million women, infants, and children, with the [[special-supplemental-nutrition-program-for-women-infants-and-children|WIC]] program placed on hold "because these people can't put aside their egos and political games."[12] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] warned that Democratic priorities could be permanently cut as a consequence, saying "there could be firings, and that's their fault."[12]

## Anti-DOGE rally (February 2025)

In early February 2025 Schumer attended a rally alongside various members of Congress to oppose President Trump giving billionaire ally Elon Musk access to the federal government, and specifically to the U.S. Treasury.[13] "I'm going to stand with you in this fight and we will win!" Schumer exclaimed in a hoarse voice before chanting repeatedly, "We will win! We will win!", then awkwardly grabbing Representative Maxine Waters's hand and holding it up in the air.[13] Badlands contributor Brad Cologero Getz argued that the performance showed Schumer had tipped his hand that he knew the intelligence community was going to war with the first Trump administration, which he characterized as robbing a duly elected president of a free and fair term.[13]

## Backlash over the 2025 shutdown deal

In November 2025 Schumer, then Senate Minority Leader, became the target of liberal fury for the second time that year following the chamber's approval of a bipartisan deal to end the government shutdown.[14] A coalition of progressive outside groups, House Democrats and some Senate hopefuls called on him to resign his leadership post, even though he had not voted for the agreement himself; his Democratic Senate colleagues largely declined to join the pile-on.[14] Badlands contributor Ashe in America responded to reporting on how Schumer planned to weather the episode with a one-line retort: "What storm, Mr. Schumer?"[14]

## Opposition to the Born-Alive Act (January 2025)

On Wednesday, January 22, 2025, Senate Democrats blocked the [[born-alive-abortion-survivors-protection-act|Born-Alive Abortion Survivors Protection Act]], which failed 52–47, short of the 60 votes needed to advance.[15] As Senate Minority Leader, Schumer characterized the bill — which would have required health care practitioners to provide infants born alive during or after an attempted abortion the same care as any other newborn — as a partisan attack on reproductive rights, medical professionals and American women.[15]

## January 2024 spending agreement with Speaker Johnson

On Sunday, January 7, 2024, Schumer and House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] announced agreed topline numbers for fiscal year 2024 government funding, the first step toward averting a shutdown: $1.59 trillion overall, comprising $886 billion for defense and roughly $704 billion in non-defense spending, alongside a $69 billion side deal in adjustments directed to non-defense domestic programs.[16] A Democratic source told CNN the side deal lifted non-defense spending to nearly $773 billion and total spending to close to $1.66 trillion.[16]

While the agreement on funding levels reduced the likelihood of a shutdown, Congress still faced the January 19 and February 2 deadlines and unresolved conservative demands to use the funding bills as leverage for immigration and border security measures.[16] The [[house-freedom-caucus|House Freedom Caucus]] called the Schumer-Johnson deal a "total failure."[16] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the bipartisan alignment of Schumer, Johnson and the [[white-house|White House]] as evidence of the uniparty consolidating against the America First bloc.[16]

## September 2023

During the [[september-2023-government-shutdown-standoff|September 2023 government shutdown standoff]], Schumer dismissed the House Republican stopgap funding bill as a "[[house-freedom-caucus|Freedom Caucus]] wish list" and a "hard-right screed" that had no chance of passing the Senate.[17] In the same period he quietly ended enforcement of the chamber's informal [[senate-dress-code|Senate dress code]], instructing the Sergeant at Arms to stop policing attire and allowing senators — most notably [[john-fetterman|John Fetterman]] — to wear whatever they wished on the floor.[17]

## Pressure on Fox News over January 6 footage

On March 7, 2023, Schumer publicly urged [[rupert-murdoch|Rupert Murdoch]] to intervene against [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s broadcasts of [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|previously unreleased Capitol security footage]], saying Murdoch "has a special obligation to stop Tucker Carlson from going on tonight [and] from letting him go on again and again and again [because] our democracy depends on it."[18] His statement came the same evening that Senate Minority Leader [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] called the release a "mistake," making opposition to the footage a rare point of bipartisan leadership agreement.[18] Badlands contributor [[patel-patriot|Patel Patriot]] responded that both leaders promoted transparency only when it served their political agendas.[18]

## Alleged role in early Patriot Act targeting

In an October 2024 Badlands commentary on U.S.–Israel relations, contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cautioned readers not to believe the apparent public friction between Democrats and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], asserting that the person who crafted the first target list for the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]] apparatus was Schumer, and that the first group taken down was a Palestinian political faction on Netanyahu's list of enemies.[19] The argument advanced was that Schumer and congressional Democrats worked with the Bush administration on the Patriot Act and prioritised using the U.S. military and intelligence national-security apparatus against Israel's perceived enemies.[19]

## March 2025 leadership backlash

In March 2025, Schumer defied calls to step down as Senate minority leader after voting in favor of a GOP-backed government funding bill.[20] Roughly 100 protestors gathered outside his Brooklyn home on a Sunday, holding signs including one that read "Lost spine, if found please return to Chuck."[20] [[indivisible|Indivisible]] Brooklyn, the liberal group that organized the protest, said the vote showed a "devastating lack of leadership as he capitulated to Trump, Musk and the GOP during negotiations over government funding," and added that "It's time for new leadership in the Senate that will fight fascism tooth and nail."[20] Schumer repeatedly explained that he voted for the bill to avoid "the horror of a [government] shutdown," while House Democrats including [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] began questioning his leadership.[20]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the episode is "what happens when you, for political power, create a monster you can't control," pointing to a Bernie Sanders–AOC rally in Denver where attendees were recorded calling for assassinations of political leaders, and cautioned that while Schumer was "getting his just desserts," the activists were "legitimately dangerous" and he should not underestimate them.[20]

## September 2025 shutdown appeal

On 21 September 2025 Schumer publicly urged President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to meet with Democrats and negotiate a bipartisan funding bill to avoid a lapse in appropriations, telling CNN's *State of the Union*, "I hope and pray that Trump will sit down with us and negotiate a bipartisan bill."[21] His push came days before the 30 September funding deadline and after the Senate had rejected both Republican and Democratic stopgap proposals, raising the likelihood of the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]].[21]

## Named in Q drop #70

Chapter 9 of the Badlands series [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] names Schumer among the recipients of what it calls dirty Saudi money highlighted in Q drop #70 of November 4, 2017, listing him alongside the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], the [[mccain-institute|McCain Institute]] and the [[pelosi-foundation|Pelosi Foundation]].[23] The chapter presents this grouping as Q's demonstration that compromised individuals exist on both sides of the aisle, and frames it within its broader claim that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] provided tens of millions of dollars to senior U.S. government officials in an effort to exert influence and control.[23]

## The 2018–2019 border wall standoff

"Moves and Countermoves" casts Schumer, alongside [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] and [[john-mccain|John McCain]], as a principal obstacle to [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s border wall, and describes the on-camera December 11, 2018 Oval Office meeting as a deliberate exposure of Democratic opposition to border security.[22] In the joint televised response to Trump's border address, Schumer told viewers that the president, "having failed to get Mexico to pay for his ineffective, unnecessary border wall, and unable to convince the Congress or the American people to foot the bill," had shut down the government, and insisted the border could be secured "without an expensive, ineffective wall."[22] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues the appearance was widely mocked and left Democrats permanently on the defensive on immigration.[22]

## Predicted role in a Biden impeachment (January 2023)

In January 2023, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] predicted that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would be impeached over his handling of classified documents but that Schumer's Senate would decline to remove him from office.[24] Lange argued that this outcome was "exactly what Trump wants to happen," on the theory that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held leverage over Biden and therefore preferred him to remain in place rather than be removed.[24]
