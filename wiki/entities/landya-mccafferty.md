# Landya McCafferty

Landya McCafferty is a United States District Judge who in April 2025 ruled against the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]]'s policy restricting diversity, equity and inclusion practices in federally funded schools.[1]

## April 2025 anti-DEI ruling

On Thursday, April 24, 2025, McCafferty was one of three separate judges — two of them appointed by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] — to rule against the Department of Education policy that threatened to withhold federal funding from schools engaging in [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]] programming or incorporating race into aspects of student life.[1]

In what was described as a scathing opinion, McCafferty called the administration's policy "textbook viewpoint discrimination," likely in violation of the First Amendment's free speech protections.[1] She also concluded that the [[national-education-association|National Education Association]], the administration's opponent in the case, was likely to succeed in arguing that the policy was unconstitutionally vague and that the agency had failed to follow procedural steps required by law in implementing it.[1]

The policy at issue had first been set out in a so-called Dear Colleague letter sent to schools in February 2025; beginning in April, schools receiving federal funding would have been subject to certification mandates requiring them to disclose information about compliance with the administration's prohibitions.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] cited Sean Davis of [[the-federalist|The Federalist]] likening the judiciary to fiat currency, arguing that judicial power is abstract and exists only so long as people consent and comply with the system, since a court can do nothing beyond state its opinion.[1]
