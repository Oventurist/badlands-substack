# Brown Brothers Harriman

Brown Brothers Harriman is the Wall Street private banking house described by Matthew Ehret as the institutional vehicle through which American financiers funded the German Nazi movement in the 1930s and early 1940s.[1]

## Formation

The bank was the product of a 1931 merger between the family bank of [[montagu-norman|Montagu Norman]] — Brown Brothers — and Harriman, Bush and Co.[1] Its directors included [[prescott-bush|Prescott Bush]], [[averell-harriman|Averell Harriman]] and E. Roland Harriman.[1]

## Nazi finance

Acting as a director of the firm, Prescott Bush provided loans that kept the bankrupt Nazi party afloat during Hitler's loss of support in 1932, when German voters instead installed the anti-fascist General Kurt von Schleicher as Chancellor.[1] Bush was subsequently found guilty of "trading with the enemy" as a director of the [[union-banking-corporation|Union Banking Corporation]] in 1942, an institution deeply enmeshed with [[fritz-thyssen|Fritz Thyssen]]'s Bank voor Handel en Scheepvaart of the Netherlands.[1]

Norman, the bank's co-progenitor, simultaneously served as Governor of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] from 1920 to 1944 and as the primary controller of the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]], giving the Harriman network direct access to the central-banking architecture of interwar Europe.[1]
