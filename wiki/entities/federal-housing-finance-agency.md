# Federal Housing Finance Agency

The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) is the United States regulator of the housing finance system. Under Director [[william-pulte|William Pulte]], the agency became a recurring source of criminal referrals to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] concerning alleged misrepresentations in mortgage and insurance filings by prominent political figures.[1]

## Referral of Letitia James

In March 2026, Pulte again referred New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] to the Department of Justice for potential criminal prosecution, alleging she may have provided false information on a homeowner's insurance application.[1] Pulte sent letters to United States Attorneys in Florida and Illinois asking them to "authenticate and investigate" the claims, citing analysis by attorney Mike Davis, who pointed to court filings he said showed discrepancies in James' insurance representations.[1]

According to the letters, an application submitted to a Florida-based insurer stated that James' Norfolk, Virginia property would remain unoccupied for five months each year, while testimony referenced in the referral indicated her niece, Nakia Thompson, had lived in the home for several years.[1] A separate filing cited in the Illinois referral indicated the home would be occupied by a single adult with no children, whereas public reporting cited by Pulte stated the property was occupied by the niece and three children.[1]

## Dual role with ODNI (June 2026)

In June 2026 FHFA director [[william-pulte|William J. Pulte]] was appointed Acting Director of National Intelligence by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and the announcement confirmed he would remain FHFA director and chairman of [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] while holding the intelligence post.[2] Trump cited Pulte's oversight of sensitive financial matters, including the safety and soundness of U.S. housing markets, as qualification for the intelligence role.[2]

## Pulte's campaign against the Federal Reserve (2025)

In July 2025 FHFA Director [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]] used the agency's platform to call for a congressional investigation of [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], alleging deceptive Senate testimony about the Fed's headquarters renovation and urging his resignation.[3] _The Hill_ characterized the effort as an unprecedented campaign by the head of one independent financial regulatory agency to oust the head of another, noting that the FHFA is responsible for overseeing the financial stability of [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] and for working with fellow regulators such as Powell to protect Americans from major financial shocks.[3] Pulte also pressed the Fed to lower interest rates, citing housing affordability data showing that, for the first time since 2006, buying an entry-level home cost more than twice as much per month as renting.[3]
