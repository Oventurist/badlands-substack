# Xiangguo Qiu

**Dr. Xiangguo Qiu** is a virologist formerly employed at Canada's top biolaboratory under the Public Health Agency of Canada, whose abrupt departure — long attributed by the government to a private "personnel issue" — was revealed in 2024 to have followed a security assessment accusing her of clandestine collaboration with the People's Republic of China.[1]

## CSIS assessment

Documents authored by the [[canadian-security-intelligence-service|Canadian Security Intelligence Service]] (CSIS), viewed and published by The Counter Signal, set out the explanation behind the departure of Qiu and a second virologist from the laboratory.[1] Despite the Liberal government citing the departure as a private personnel matter, CSIS accused Qiu of a long history of clandestine actions that put Canadians' health and security at risk, and of directly assisting military research in China.[1]

The documents alleged that Qiu sold deadly pathogens to Chinese authorities at the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] for just $75, and that she hid a Chinese bank account from CSIS.[1] The assessment stated: "Further to our security assessment […], the Service assesses that Ms. Qiu developed deep, cooperative relationships with a variety of People's Republic of China (PRC) institutions and has intentionally transferred scientific knowledge and materials to China in order to benefit the PRC Government, and herself, without regard for the implications to her employer or to Canada's interests."[1]

## Political significance

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued the significance of the case lay less in the virus narrative than in the timeline of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]'s collaboration with the Chinese Communist Party.[1] Esler rejected the framing, common in Canadian media and among the conservative opposition, that the breach represented a national-security "failure," contending instead that Canada had been handed to the CCP intentionally and that "the real issue is one of treason."[1] He noted that immediately after Canada's most secure and classified lab was breached and Trudeau was informed, the prime minister attempted to purchase a vaccine from China, then called a snap election later shown to have been influenced by Beijing in his favor.[1]
