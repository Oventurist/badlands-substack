# Stephen Miran

Stephen Miran is an American economist described as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s chief economist and former chairman of the Council of Economic Advisers, whose nomination to fill a vacancy on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Board was being fast-tracked in August 2025.[1]

## Fed board nomination

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] listed the fast-tracking of Miran's appointment among the "moves on the edges" signaling a coming monetary reset, arguing that his arrival on the Fed board could challenge the status quo on central bank independence and policy.[1] The nomination was framed as part of Trump's broader "pincer move" on the Fed, running alongside the [[lisa-cook|Lisa Cook]] controversy and Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s anticipation of a "global economic reordering," at a moment when Chairman [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] was preparing his final Jackson Hole speech.[1]

## August 2025 nomination to the Kugler seat

On Thursday, August 7, 2025, President Trump announced that he had selected Miran, then chair of the [[council-of-economic-advisers|Council of Economic Advisers]], for a pivotal seat on the Federal Reserve Board of Governors, replacing [[adriana-kugler|Adriana Kugler]], who had resigned the preceding Friday.[2] The nominee was to serve out Kugler's unexpired term, which ran to January 31, 2026.[2] In a Truth Social post Trump said the search for a permanent replacement would continue, indicating that the nominee for the full fourteen-year term could be someone else and that Miran might occupy the seat only in a caretaker role.[2]

As sitting chairman of the White House council, Miran was presumed to be an ally of the president and at least partially aligned with his dovish views on the level of interest rates.[2] Financial coverage credited the nomination with helping push [[bitcoin|bitcoin]] back above $117,000 late in Thursday's U.S. trading session, after the cryptocurrency had dipped below $112,000 days earlier; the crypto market had already been lifted overnight by news of Trump's intention to sign an executive order allowing crypto and other assets in 401(k) retirement plans.[2] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] noted that "the author of the Mar-a-Lago Accords will be the next Fed governor," and joked about [[michael-saylor|Michael Saylor]] for Fed chairman.[2]

The Miran nomination coincided with a shift in prediction-market expectations for the Fed chairmanship, where Governor [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]] had just become the favorite.[2]

## Discussion paper behind the "Mar-a-Lago Accord"

While a nominee to chair the Council of Economic Advisers, Miran authored a detailed discussion paper that became the intellectual basis for what commentators dubbed the [[mar-a-lago-accord|Mar-a-Lago Accord]].[3] As described by the [[financial-times|Financial Times]], the scheme would have America's trading partners help weaken the dollar and provide low-cost, long-term financing to the US government, enforced by the threat of higher tariffs or the removal of security guarantees — potentially including the purchase of 100-year zero-coupon Treasury bonds.[3] Although neither the administration nor President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced any such initiative, Miran's paper together with remarks by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] convinced Wall Street observers that it was in the offing.[3]
