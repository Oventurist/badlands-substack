# Brian Driscoll

Brian Driscoll is a career official of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] who was named acting director of the bureau by the White House on Monday, January 20, 2025, the day of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second inauguration.[1] His appointment came amid a rapid reshuffling of the bureau's senior ranks that began before the new administration formally took office.[1]

## Appointment and context

Driscoll's elevation followed the departure of two predecessors in short order. Former FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]], who had originally been nominated by Trump during his first administration, announced in December 2024 that he would step down from his post rather than be removed.[1] Acting director Paul Abbate similarly stepped down hours before Trump was sworn in, leaving the top job vacant on Inauguration Day.[1]

Driscoll's tenure as acting director was expected to be a caretaker one, ending when Kashyap "Kash" Patel — Trump's nominee for a full ten-year term as director — was confirmed by the [[us-senate|U.S. Senate]].[1]

## The bureau shakeup

Driscoll took over as day-one personnel changes were implemented across the bureau and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]. The Associated Press reported that the longtime head of the Justice Department's office of international affairs, Bruce Swartz, was reassigned along with as many as twenty other staffers.[1] Fox News framed the reorganization as putting "untouchable" federal agents on notice, quoting a former agent to that effect.[1]

Throughout [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s term the FBI had been entangled in repeated scandals, prompting Trump to campaign on rooting out corruption at both the bureau and the Justice Department; the personnel moves overseen during Driscoll's brief acting tenure were presented as the first installment of that promise.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentator Burning Bright characterized Trump's "common sense" agenda across gender, borders, crime and the FBI overhaul as "a game theory masterstroke," arguing that systemic institutional resistance to the agenda would itself codify intent and accelerate public awakening.[1]
