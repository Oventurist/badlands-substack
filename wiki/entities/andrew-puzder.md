# Andrew Puzder

Andrew Puzder is the United States Ambassador to the [[european-union|European Union]] under the second Trump administration.[1] In March 2026 he became the public face of an American pressure campaign urging Brussels to ratify the transatlantic trade agreement signed the previous summer.[1]

## Energy leverage over the EU

Speaking to the Financial Times, Puzder warned that while Washington would continue doing business with the bloc, the terms of American energy supply "may not be as favorable" if the agreement were not approved.[1] He emphasized that the EU "needs energy" to survive economically and that the United States is positioned to supply it, but only on terms that encourage ratification.[1] The [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] was scheduled to vote on ratification that week.[1]

Under the deal, the EU accepted a baseline 15% tariff on all of its goods entering the US market while eliminating its own tariffs on US industrial goods and certain agricultural products, and committed to purchasing $750 billion worth of American energy by 2028.[1]

## Context

The warning came as the bloc had grown heavily dependent on US liquefied natural gas after sharply reducing imports from Russia; Germany sourced 96% of its LNG imports from the United States in 2025, according to the German Environmental Aid Association.[1] The [[european-commission|European Commission]] has maintained its plan to fully phase out Russian fossil fuels by 2027 even as global energy prices rose amid the Middle East conflict.[1] Badlands commentary framed the episode as [[donald-trump|President Trump]] deliberately exploiting European energy dependency as diplomatic leverage, and speculated that the same lever would be pulled again in negotiations over peace between Ukraine and Russia.[1]
