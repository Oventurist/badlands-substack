# Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi

Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi was the leader of the Islamic State, declared caliph and "leader for Muslims everywhere" when [[isis|ISIS]] seized large swathes of territory in Iraq and Syria and announced the establishment of a caliphate on June 29, 2014.[1] That same year marked the beginning of Operation Inherent Resolve, the US-led mission in the Middle East to destroy the group.[1]

## Death and legacy

Al-Baghdadi died in a 2019 US special operations raid, cornered in a tunnel before detonating a suicide vest; President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said of him, "He died like a dog. Like a coward."[1] The military working dog Conan, injured in the raid, was honored at the [[white-house|White House]] and became the subject of widespread memes; Conan died in November 2023.[1] Badlands commentary contrasted Trump's characterization with the Washington Post's much-mocked headline describing al-Baghdadi as an "austere religious scholar," and criticized former CIA official [[michael-morell|Michael Morell]] for invoking al-Baghdadi's name while warning of an ISIS resurgence in 2024.[1]

## Presence in Syria during the 2013 McCain meeting

The Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" argues that al-Baghdadi is the man circled in the background of photographs from Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]]'s May 28, 2013 meeting with purported Syrian rebel leaders.[2] McCain's allies in the media dismissed the identification as a conspiracy theory and claimed the figure was a [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]] commander, but declined to name or verify him.[2]

The article assembles a supporting timeline. Al-Baghdadi became leader of the [[islamic-state-of-iraq|Islamic State of Iraq]] in 2010; his wife Umm Hudaifa told the BBC that the family moved to the Idlib countryside in Syria in January 2012, at which point it became clear to her that he was the emir, and that he then began wearing Afghani dress, grew a beard and carried a pistol.[2] A photograph of him speaking at a mosque in 2014, before the beard and change of wardrobe, is offered as a likeness comparison.[2] In 2004, a year after the U.S.-led invasion of Iraq, American forces detained him for roughly a year at Camp Bucca alongside many men who would become senior ISIS and jihadist figures.[2]

In April 2013 al-Baghdadi announced that the [[al-nusra-front|Al-Nusra Front]] was an extension of Al-Qaeda in Iraq inside Syria, and that his group had supplied al-Nusra with strategy and monthly funding.[2] Reuters reported that in May 2013 he travelled from Iraq to Syria's Aleppo Governorate to take over al-Nusra's leadership — placing him in Syria in the same month as McCain's visit.[2] The author infers that McCain arrived with a large sum of money, reading Q's line "What was delivered? Smiles" accordingly.[2]

## Death in the 2019 raid

The article closes its ISIS thread with the October 2019 raid in which U.S. forces killed al-Baghdadi.[2] Quoting the Washington Post, it describes [[donald-trump|Trump]] announcing from the Diplomatic Room that the leader of the Islamic State had died in an Idlib tunnel, detonating a suicide vest as American forces and dogs closed in, having taken three small children into the dead-end passage with him.[2] Trump called his followers "losers" and "frightened puppies" and said al-Baghdadi "died like a dog... whimpering, screaming and crying."[2] The author notes that the military also recovered a large volume of documents and information from the site, and predicts that this evidence will implicate further traitors.[2]
