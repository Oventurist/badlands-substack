# Institute for Statecraft

The Institute for Statecraft is a British organization described in Badlands Media coverage as "spook infested" and central to Anglo-American information operations directed against Russia.[1]

## Relationship with the Global Engagement Center

According to Badlands reporting, the Institute for Statecraft made use of the U.S. [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] to pump out anti-Russian, pro-war propaganda.[1] The GEC itself has been summarized by critical outlets as a "de facto U.S. government propaganda operation," and it eventually shifted its mission statement from countering "terrorist and extremist groups" to countering "foreign state and non-state propaganda and disinformation efforts" — a change the article frames as part of an effort to control the narrative and bolster calls for a "Cold War 2.0," including censorship of material contradicting the "unprovoked Russian invasion" framing.[1]

## The Integrity Initiative

The Institute for Statecraft ran the now-disgraced Integrity Initiative, a project exposed through leaked documents and described in investigative reporting as a covert British military intelligence smear machine meddling in American politics.[1] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] noted that he had briefly addressed the Institute and the Integrity Initiative in an earlier piece, "Rattling a Broken Sabre."[1]

## Founding, funding and exposure

The Institute was formed in 2006 and received UK charitable status in 2009, after which it took in millions of pounds from the [[foreign-commonwealth-and-development-office|Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office]] despite assurances that it was an independent organization.[2] It sired the [[integrity-initiative|Integrity Initiative]], the project that drew the most attention and controversy.[2]

A November 2018 leak exposed the Institute as what DeLarme called a UK deep faction obsessed with a permanent-war mentality and promoting a new, profitable cold war against Russia, staffed largely — perhaps exclusively — by intelligence and deep-state operatives.[2] The documents described shaping media narratives, briefing politicians, and targeting domestic figures, most notably Labour leader [[jeremy-corbyn|Jeremy Corbyn]], whom the network allegedly sought to discredit as a security risk over his perceived softness on Russia.[2] The leaks also revealed an annual Foreign Office budget of more than £2 million and lists of individuals in its clusters.[2]

## Successors

DeLarme argued that the [[european-council-on-foreign-relations|European Council on Foreign Relations]], of which EU foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] is a member, overlaps considerably with the Institute for Statecraft and can be described as its spiritual successor.[2] In his framing of the Ukraine–Russia proxy war, when one such deep-state cut-out falls, several new ones immediately spring up: "the organ might fail, but the mission carries on unimpeded."[2]
