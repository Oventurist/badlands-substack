# Miles Klee

Miles Klee is an American journalist and culture writer for [[rolling-stone|Rolling Stone]], best known within the Truth Community for authoring the magazine's July 2023 review of the anti-child-trafficking film [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]].[1] Prior to Rolling Stone he wrote for the digital outlet Mic.[1]

## Sound of Freedom review

Klee's review described [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]] as a "QAnon-tinged thriller about child-trafficking" that was "designed to appeal to the conscience of a conspiracy-addled boomer."[1] It attacked the film's lead, [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]], as "a prominent figure on the conspiracist right," and dismissed the actor's past claims about elite paedophile rings that kidnap, rape and murder children in order to harvest [[adrenochrome|adrenochrome]] — a compound Klee's critics noted was popularised in the film Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas.[1] Klee wrote that the picture was "a stomach-turning experience, fetishizing the torture of its child victims and lingering over lush preludes to their sexual abuse," adding: "At times I had the uncomfortable sense that I might be arrested myself just for sitting through it."[1]

## Reaction

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] aggregated the review under the headline "'Why Do You Support Child Trafficking?' Rolling Stone Slammed Over Negative 'Sound Of Freedom' Review," describing Klee as a "pothead journalist," pointedly asking why he might feel at risk of arrest for watching the film, and noting that the movie held a 99 percent user rating on [[rotten-tomatoes|Rotten Tomatoes]] against 75 percent from professional reviewers.[1] Commentators observed that the film was inspired by the real work of [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]] and [[operation-underground-railroad|Operation Underground Railroad]], and that Caviezel's personal beliefs were being used to invalidate the film's premise.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] grouped Klee's review with what she termed the "mockingbirds' movie critics," writing that critics were uniformly labelling the film QAnon material and instructing Americans not to see it, and that this said far more about the journalists and their publications than about the film.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the review as part of a wider [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] effort to frame a film about real-life child trafficking busts as Alt Right and QAnon-adjacent.[1]
