# PepsiCo

PepsiCo is one of the handful of parent corporations that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] identifies as monopolizing the packaged food industry, and one of his primary worked examples of concentrated institutional ownership.[1]

## Brands and market position

Tropicana orange juice is largely owned by PepsiCo, and the company owns a long list of food, beverage and snack brands.[1] PepsiCo is a member of the S&P 500 index, placing it among the 500 biggest companies in the world.[1] DeLarme groups it with [[coca-cola|The Coca-Cola Company]], Nestlé, General Mills, Kellogg's, Unilever, Mars, Kraft Heinz, Mondelez, Danone and Associated British Foods as the dozen or so parent companies that together own virtually every food brand found in a typical supermarket.[1]

## Institutional ownership

A review of PepsiCo's top institutional investors places [[vanguard-group|the Vanguard Group]] and [[blackrock|BlackRock, Inc.]] at the top of the list, the same pattern DeLarme finds at Coca-Cola, where the two firms appear just below Bill Gates and Warren Buffett's [[berkshire-hathaway|Berkshire Hathaway]].[1] On this basis he argues that the megacorporations behind familiar brands can themselves be understood as subsidiaries of their largest investors, and that the Coke-versus-Pepsi rivalry is a facade because the same shareholders profit whichever brand the consumer picks — an instance of [[horizontal-shareholding|horizontal shareholding]].[1]

## Health critique

DeLarme argues that the packaged foods produced by companies such as PepsiCo are among the leading causes of obesity, heart disease and other ailments, and asks why asset managers willing to pressure firms into climate commitments do not use the same leverage to force healthier products — particularly when the same managers also hold the pharmaceutical companies that treat the resulting illnesses.[1]
