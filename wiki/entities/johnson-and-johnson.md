# Johnson & Johnson

Johnson & Johnson is a major American pharmaceutical and consumer-health company that produced one of the COVID-19 vaccines authorized in the United States, and one of three manufacturers named in a May 2023 investigation announced by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]].[1]

## Texas investigation

Paxton announced that his office would investigate Johnson & Johnson alongside [[pfizer|Pfizer]] and [[moderna|Moderna]] for deceptive trade practices, alleging that the companies had misrepresented the safety and efficacy of their vaccines.[1] The attorney general said the inquiry would examine whether "profit motive or political pressure" played a role in "pandemic interventions forced on the public."[1]

In a press statement, Paxton said that "the development of the Covid-19 vaccine, and the representations made by and knowledge of Pfizer, Moderna, and Johnson & Johnson, are of profound interest to the public's health and welfare," adding that the investigation aimed "to discover the truth."[1] He further stated that any company that illegally took advantage of consumers or compromised people's safety to increase profits would be held responsible, and that if public health policy had been developed on the basis of flawed or misleading research, the public must know.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] welcomed the investigation as a possible start of a trend, arguing that lawmakers and elected officials had repeatedly failed to hold corporate powers in the pharmaceutical sector to account for manipulating studies, hiding dangers from public inspection, and profiteering at the expense of consumers.[1]

## Talc powder and asbestos

Badlands Media cites the company's baby talc powder, which contains asbestos, as one of two recent examples of everyday consumer products finally admitted to be carcinogenic — the other being [[monsanto|Monsanto]]'s pesticide Roundup.[2] The pairing appears in an argument that harmful chemicals introduced over the last 120 years into food, household products, and cosmetics have been linked to nearly every modern chronic affliction, particularly cancers and neurodegenerative disorders.[2]

## Federal vaccine contracts and the Badlands "supervax" thesis

[[woody-johnson|Woody Johnson]], a minority owner from the founding family and a longtime friend of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], served as U.S. Ambassador to the United Kingdom from 2017 until 2021.[3] During his tenure the Trump administration awarded contracts worth $905,645,089 to the company's [[janssen-pharmaceuticals|Janssen Pharmaceuticals]] unit — $689,525,867 on December 19, 2019 from the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response, ostensibly for influenza vaccine work, and a further $216,169,222 in February 2020.[3] Badlands contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] argues the December 2019 award, predating widespread awareness of the coronavirus, shows Trump anticipating a pandemic and pairing the federal government with an American manufacturer owned by his friend's family, later folding the work into [[operation-warp-speed|Operation Warp Speed]].[3]

The company's single-dose COVID-19 vaccine was approved on February 28, 2021 and was paused by the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] and [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] on April 13, 2021 over rare blood-clot reports before the pause was lifted on April 23 — the [[2021-johnson-and-johnson-vaccine-pause|2021 J&J vaccine pause]].[3] Gillespie speculates that the pause was an attempt to discredit a product created outside the cabal's control, and that J&J vials may have contained saline or the Army's [[spike-ferritin-nanoparticle-vaccine|SpFN "supervax"]]; she also records Trump introducing Woody and Suzanne Johnson at the Greensburg, Pennsylvania Save America rally on May 6, 2022, where the crowd booed.[3] Using Our World in Data figures she counted 66,885,169 J&J doses administered across 44 countries and the EU, led by the United States (18,950,000), the European Union (18,680,000) and South Africa (9,370,000).[3] These readings are the author's speculation.[3]
