# National Law Enforcement Telecommunications System

The National Law Enforcement Telecommunications System, known as Nlets, is an obscure nationwide computer network that typically allows law enforcement agencies to search driver's license records across state lines, letting police officers easily look up the driving records of out-of-state motorists.[1]

## Access dispute with Homeland Security (2025)

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] said as recently as October 2025 that federal officials wanted to obtain state driver's license records through the network as part of its hunt for alleged noncitizen voters.[1] Four Republican states — Florida, Indiana, Iowa and Ohio — committed to helping the administration gain that access under a settlement agreement filed on Friday, November 28, 2025, in a federal lawsuit the states had originally brought the previous year alleging that the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was not doing enough to help states verify voter eligibility.[1]

Under the settlement with the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], the federal department is required to continue developing the citizenship verification program known as [[save-program|SAVE]], which federal officials had earlier in 2025 repurposed into a system capable of scanning millions of state voter records for instances of noncitizen registered voters.[1] In return, the four states agreed to support the department's efforts to access Nlets.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the outcome as a setback for [[david-becker|David Becker]] and renewed her call to prosecute ERIC states and their accomplices.[1]
