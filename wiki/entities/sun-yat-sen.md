# Sun Yat-sen

Sun Yat-sen was a Chinese revolutionary leader and former president of China, cited in Badlands Media commentary as an early exponent of the moral framework underlying open-system political economy.[1]

## Three Principles of the People

In his Three Principles of the People, Sun articulated what [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies as the principle of "Right makes Might" — an early formulation of [[win-win-cooperation|win-win cooperation]].[1] Writing in 1924, Sun is said to have understood, as Presidents [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] are argued to understand today, that a nation adhering to right-makes-might thinking will never lose the Mandate of Heaven, or Tianxia.[1]

## Legacy in the multipolar framework

Ehret places Sun's principle alongside the [[peace-of-westphalia|Principle of Westphalia]] and the UN Charter as parallel Eastern and Western expressions of the same moral concept, contrasted with the closed-system, "might-makes-right" outlook he associates with [[thomas-malthus|Thomas Malthus]], [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] and [[al-gore|Al Gore]].[1] The lineage is invoked to argue that China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] rests on a philosophical foundation predating the modern People's Republic.[1]
