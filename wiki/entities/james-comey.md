# James Comey

James Comey is a former Director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]]. In coverage from January 2026 he appears in connection with the collapse of federal indictments brought during the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1]

## Invalidated indictment

The court-ordered dismissal of US Attorney [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]] in Virginia led to the invalidation of indictments against both Comey and New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[1] Attempts to re-indict James in Virginia subsequently failed.[1] The episode formed part of a wider pattern of judicial pushback against US attorneys installed without Senate confirmation.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recounted that Comey was indicted on federal charges of lying to [[congress|Congress]] in 2020, but that the judge found Halligan's appointment unlawful under the interim-US attorney statute, meaning all actions flowing from that defective appointment — including the Comey indictment — had to be set aside and the case dismissed.[2] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] appealed the ruling.[2]

## March 2026 conspiracy subpoena

In March 2026 Comey was subpoenaed in a federal investigation examining alleged conspiracy-related matters tied to prior [[donald-trump|Trump]]-era probes.[2] The subpoena is part of a broader Justice Department inquiry reviewing actions taken during earlier investigations involving Trump, with investigators seeking documents and testimony related to decision-making, coordination, and potential misconduct in those cases.[2] The probe was described as ongoing with no charges announced at that stage, and marked a significant escalation in efforts to scrutinize the origins and conduct of past federal investigations involving Trump.[2]

## Classified leaks case (2026)

In 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was reported to be moving forward with a potential new round of charges against Comey alleging that he leaked classified information, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Badlands.[3] Federal prosecutors in the Eastern District of Virginia revived a separate but connected investigation focused on Comey's alleged transmission of documents to Columbia Law professor [[daniel-richman|Daniel Richman]].[3] Sources described ongoing meetings between a small group of EDVA prosecutors and officials in the deputy attorney general's office since [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] was elevated to acting attorney general; the probe had stalled after an initial review the prior year but regained traction under Blanche.[3] No decision had been made on venue — options included Eastern Virginia, where Comey resides, or New York, where Richman is based — and the timing remained unclear.[3]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recounted discussing the earlier "8647" indictment with [[canncon|CannCon]] on Badlands Daily, where both judged it "weak sauce" and likely protected by [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] speech rights, drawing criticism from parts of the audience.[3] She argued that a classified-leaks case was a far worthier use of public resources, noting that depending on the information, the recipient, and the motive, an FBI director's leaking could arguably amount to treason — while insisting prosecutors "do it by the book" and leave the Bill of Rights alone.[3]

## Amicus brief ban (June 2026)

In June 2026 the federal judge overseeing the criminal case against Comey barred the filing of amicus ("friend of the court") briefs, limiting outside participation in the proceedings as the case moved forward.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the development as one of several concurrent [[weaponization-of-government|weaponized government]] storylines, remarking that Comey claims the government is being weaponized against him precisely because the government is investigating his own weaponization of government against President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his supporters.[4]

## DOJ denial of presidential direction

In April 2026, acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] stated that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had no role in directing or initiating any prosecution involving Comey, and that any legal action tied to him was being handled independently through the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[5] The statement followed reporting that Comey had been indicted, which prompted scrutiny over whether the prosecution was connected to Trump's longstanding public criticism of the former FBI director.[5] Blanche said prosecutorial decisions rest on evidence and standard DOJ procedures rather than presidential direction.[5]

## Second indictment: "86 47" threat case (April 2026)

On Tuesday, April 28, 2026, Comey was indicted for a second time — in the Eastern District of North Carolina — on charges of making a threat against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and transmitting a threat in interstate commerce.[6] The charges center on a May Instagram post showing seashells on a beach arranged to spell "86 47," which some read as an implied code to "get rid of" the 47th president, and which Comey deleted the same day.[6] He denied any violent intent, saying he opposes violence and did not initially recognize the post's coded meaning.[6]

The case followed a prior September 2025 indictment accusing him of lying to [[congress|Congress]] about leaks to the press, which was later dismissed.[6] The prosecution was brought under the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] leadership of Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and was announced the same day as the indictment of [[david-morens|David Morens]], reflecting what Badlands described as intensified efforts to pursue accountability tied to the COVID-19 origins investigation and alleged threats against the president.[6] Comey, who became a prominent Trump critic after his 2017 firing amid the Russia investigation, thereby faced federal prosecution in a second venue.[6]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] said he felt good about both DOJ indictments while cautioning that neither guarantees accountability, adding that this was "not his first indictment, nor do I believe it will be his last" and describing the sequence as the early innings of a "reverse humiliation ritual."[6]

## May 2026: scope of the 8647 case and a reported second indictment

Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] said on NBC's Meet the Press in early May 2026 that the [[comey-8647-indictment|"8647" indictment]] involves more than the Instagram post that triggered it, stating that intent would be proven with witnesses, documents and materials, and that a public trial would reveal the government's evidence.[7] Bloomberg Law separately reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that the Justice Department was pursuing a distinct indictment of Comey over leaks of classified documents, referencing material he passed to Daniel Richman that surfaced in a 2017 New York Times article.[7] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed Comey's 2017 conduct as the cover-up of [[barack-obama|Obama]]'s 2016 actions and asked whether the second matter would be a separate trial or additional overt acts in a conspiracy spanning nearly a decade.[7]

## October 2025 arraignment in Virginia

Comey was scheduled to be arraigned on the morning of Wednesday, October 8, 2025, in a federal court in Alexandria, Virginia, weeks after a federal grand jury indicted him on an obstruction of justice charge and for allegedly making a false statement.[9] He was expected to plead not guilty, having maintained his innocence in a video message released shortly after the indictment came down the previous month.[9]

The case centered on allegations that Comey misled the [[congress|Senate]] during testimony in late September 2020, when he reiterated his May 2017 denial that he had authorized an [[fbi|FBI]] leak of information to the media about the Trump–Russia investigation or Clinton-related investigations; the indictment further alleged that he obstructed Congress by lying to the Senate.[9]

Ahead of the hearing, top [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] officials denied reports that law enforcement would stage a theatrical arrest and escort of Comey to the Virginia courthouse.[9] Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] told Fox News that "Mr. Comey has been directed to appear, and I expect that he will," dismissing "the noise from MSNBC and from retired agents or unnamed anonymous sources about perp walks" as "just noise."[9]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] predicted the proceeding would be anticlimactic — Comey turning himself in with no perp walk, and arraignments being quick affairs — while expecting a media circus regardless, and questioned why professional journalists would react emotionally to the prosecution of a former FBI director for alleged criminal conduct, suggesting the reaction resembled "the expected conduct of co-conspirators realizing the jig is up."[9]

## Withheld referral memorandum (Durham testimony, 2023)

At the [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|House Judiciary Committee hearing]] of June 21, 2023, Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]], under questioning from Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], said that Comey as [[fbi|FBI]] Director had withheld a referral memorandum from the agents working the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] case.[10] The memo contained intelligence from a high-level briefing with [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] regarding [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s attempts to tie [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Russia.[10] Jordan characterized the omission as investigators being denied "key intelligence."[10]


## September 2025 indictment

On Thursday, September 25, 2025, Comey was indicted by a federal grand jury in Virginia on charges that he made false statements to [[congress|Congress]] when he denied leaking to the media, placing him in legal jeopardy after he had doubled down during Senate testimony in 2020 on his 2017 denials.[11] The Justice Department indictment charged him with lying to Congress and obstruction of a congressional proceeding, and stemmed from allegations that he misled the Senate in late September 2020 when he reaffirmed his May 2017 denial that he had ever authorized a leak of information to the media about the Trump–Russia investigation or the Clinton-related investigations.[11]

Comey's alleged leaking had already been part of the FBI's earlier "Tropic Vortex" classified-leaks inquiry and was reviewed by then-U.S. Attorney [[john-durham|John Durham]], but he never faced criminal charges over the alleged leaks until the grand jury acted.[11] Comey, fired as FBI director in 2017 by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], had overseen the investigation into [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s use of a private email server for classified information as well as the Trump–Russia collusion inquiry.[11] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pamela Bondi]] said minutes after the indictment that "no one is above the law."[11]

Badlands contributor Jon Herold connected the charge to newly declassified documents reported in August 2025 showing that Comey had ordered the [[fbi|FBI]] to "assist the New York Times" in writing one of its articles, with FBI officials [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] and Lisa Page interviewed by the paper as part of that assistance.[11] Herold called the indictment "one down," suggested former director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] could be pursued next for lying to Congress, and argued the FBI should be dismantled.[11]

## Declassified memos on authorized 2016 leaks

Federal prosecutors gathered evidence from Comey's top lieutenants that he authorized the leak of classified information to reporters just before the 2016 election, but declined to bring criminal charges, according to recently declassified memos reported by Just the News in August 2025 — documents that call into question the former [[fbi|FBI]] director's testimony to [[congress|Congress]].[12] The revelations involved former FBI general counsel [[james-baker|James Baker]] and former Comey chief of staff James Rybicki, and were memorialized in documents that FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] discovered earlier that year; the relevant passages had originally been redacted by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] in versions sent to Congress.[12] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] intervened to eliminate the redactions and dispatched new versions of the memos to the House and Senate Judiciary committees.[12] The memos detail evidence and interviews gathered by U.S. Postal Inspection Service agents concerning classified information leaked to *The New York Times* in October 2016, ahead of the election in which [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defeated Hillary Clinton.[12]

## Family connections: the Combs prosecution

Comey's daughter, [[maureen-comey|Maureen Comey]], was one of the federal prosecutors in the case against music mogul [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]], which ended on July 2, 2025 in acquittals on racketeering and sex-trafficking counts and convictions on two lesser prostitution-transportation charges.[13] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], noting that Maureen Comey is "the daughter of the infamous former FBI Director," reported speculation that she had been positioned to "land the plane" for Combs and his network, and argued that the outcome served that end rather than representing a loss.[13]

## Subversion and racketeering exposure (August 2025)

In August 2025, former [[fbi|FBI]] agent and Navy SEAL Jonathan Gilliam said on the "Just the News, No Noise" TV show that prosecutors may be able to build a criminal subversion or racketeering case against the government officials who carried out the [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] scandal.[14] "I think also what we have to start looking at is things like subversion," Gilliam said, adding that subversion, treason and insurrection "are all criminal actions that there's law for."[14] [[just-the-news|Just the News]] had reported that newly declassified memos showed federal prosecutors gathered evidence from Comey's top lieutenants — ex-general counsel [[james-baker|James Baker]] and ex-chief of staff James Rybicki — that Comey authorized the leak of classified information to reporters just before the 2016 election, but declined to bring criminal charges.[14] Those passages had been redacted by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] in versions sent to [[congress|Congress]] until Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] eliminated the redactions and sent new copies to the House and Senate Judiciary committees; the underlying documents had been discovered by FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]].[14] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] noted that [[john-solomon|John Solomon]]'s reporting placed [[john-durham|John Durham]] in charge of the March 2017 leak inquiry, which found Baker leaked with Comey's instruction and authorization, yet Durham recommended no prosecution of Baker or anyone else.[14]

## Prosecutorial upheaval in the Eastern District of Virginia (October 2025)

In early October 2025, two senior federal prosecutors departed the [[eastern-district-of-virginia-us-attorneys-office|U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Virginia]], the office that had recently brought criminal charges against Comey.[15] [[michael-benary|Michael Ben'Ary]], who led the office's national security section, was reportedly fired hours after conservative journalist [[julie-kelly|Julie Kelly]] publicly flagged his prior role as senior counsel to former Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]].[15] Kelly suggested Ben'Ary may have been part of an "internal resistance" to the Comey prosecution, though a person familiar with the matter told the *Associated Press* he had no involvement in the case.[15]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright treated the prosecution as an opening move, saying, "It's clear, I think that Comey is the beginning of something," and asking how the story of a RICO investigation could be told "in a way that's going to be digestible."[15] He described the period as "pure narrative warfare" and framed the convergence of "the Actual and Narrative" — "[f]rom Comey to Soros, J6 to the Government itself" — as the mechanism of awakening.[15]

## "Victim card" boomerang (November 2025)

In November 2025, Comey's strategy of fighting his indictment on charges of misleading [[congress|Congress]] by casting himself as a victim of political persecution was reported to have boomeranged, as prosecutors and his former agency released an avalanche of new evidence.[16] The material was said to show that the ex-FBI director hoped to please [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], cheered on media leaks he claimed he had not sanctioned, and wrote emails and notes that directly conflict with his past congressional testimony.[16]

Prosecutors revealed on Monday, November 3, 2025 that part of that unflattering portrait came from long-hidden files that FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] found in burn bags and secret storage rooms at the bureau's headquarters.[16] The evidence included proof that Comey used a private email account to conduct FBI business — including coordinating media strategy with a top lieutenant — at the very time his agency was investigating Clinton for improperly using a private email account for government business.[16] In one such message to FBI special government employee [[daniel-richman|Daniel Richman]], with whom he discussed influencing a *New York Times* reporter's coverage of the Clinton email scandal, Comey wrote, "Perhaps you can make him smarter."[16] Prosecutors indicated the evidence would be used at trial to show that Comey misled Congress when he denied authorizing staff to leak anonymously or speak to reporters.[16]

Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] said Comey had been advancing "a false narrative that he did nothing wrong" while the facts told a different story, and asked what the revelations meant for those in the media who had defended and perpetuated his claims.[16] Host Chris Paul offered a dissenting read, arguing that "there is no such thing as nine year-old BREAKING NEWS" and characterizing the disclosures as a limited hangout operation in which the public is shown information the government concealed for nearly a decade.[16]

## Firing of Maurene Comey (July 2025)

In July 2025 Comey's daughter, federal prosecutor [[maurene-comey|Maurene Comey]], was fired by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]; she had worked on the criminal cases of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] and prosecuted the [[sean-combs|Sean Combs]] trial.[17] The termination notice gave no reason, citing the president's broad constitutional powers, and was signed by [[francey-hakes|Francey Hakes]] of the Executive Office of U.S. Attorneys.[17] Reporting noted it was not known whether the firing was connected to the Epstein case or to her relation to the former FBI director, who was himself fired during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term.[17] Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] highlighted the claim that Maurene Comey had reviewed the footage of Epstein's death and was dismissed the same week Trump alleged the [[epstein-files|Epstein files]] had been manipulated.[17]

That same month, Fox News reporting on a criminal investigation into Comey and former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] used the word "conspiracy," and commentary framed the [[grand-conspiracy-probe|grand conspiracy probe]] as reaching far beyond the two men — invoking [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]]'s text to [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] during the 2016 campaign, "POTUS wants to know everything we are doing," a reference to then-President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[17] Page, deputy to Comey's own deputy [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]], served as the FBI's White House liaison during the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and Trump investigations.[17]

## October 2025: "starter charges" and the Siebert resignation

An October 2025 essay in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] argued that the [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] indictment should not be grouped with the prosecutions of Comey and New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]], describing the latter two cases as so weak that a U.S. attorney — [[erik-siebert|Erik Siebert]] — resigned rather than present them to a grand jury, and that career prosecutors told his replacement the government would probably lose at trial.[18]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] inverted the framing, asking "what if the others are just like the Bolton case?" and contending that the counts against Comey and James are merely starter charges given the criminal conduct the administration has already revealed.[18] She predicted superseding indictments that could add both new charges and new defendants as evidence developed, argued the administration should take note of any government employee arguing to abandon public abuses rather than prosecute them, and asked pointedly who the U.S. attorney was that resigned rather than prosecute Comey.[18]

## Invoked in later commentary

Writing in November 2024, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recalled Comey's declaration as FBI director that no reasonable prosecutor would bring a case over [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s handling of her communications, presenting it as evidence that the [[state-department|State Department]] had "for generations, operated outside the law."[19] Comey was also referenced in reporting on the succession at the bureau, since [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] had been nominated in 2017 to a ten-year term after Comey's ouster.[19]

## Crossfire Hurricane declassification letter (2023)

In June 2023, a letter signed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on January 19, 2021 was published by [[dc-draino|DC Draino]] and reported as declassifying all [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] documents — material described by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] as proving that Comey, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], [[james-clapper|James Clapper]], the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[state-department|State Department]] had spied on Trump while knowing the Trump–Russia collusion allegations were a hoax.[20]

## Possible re-indictment (December 2025)

In early December 2025, Politico reported that the Justice Department was quietly weighing whether to seek new indictments against Comey and New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] after a federal judge threw out both cases a week earlier.[21] Despite White House and Justice Department vows to appeal quickly, prosecutors had taken no public steps to revive the cases, and people familiar with the deliberations said the department was strongly considering forgoing an appeal in favor of asking one or more grand juries to re-indict.[21] The approach was described as politically fraught, since a new grand jury could decline to charge, risking Trump's ire and a further embarrassment for the department.[21]

## Dismissal and appeal (November 2025)

The federal case against Comey — in which he was initially charged with making a false statement and obstruction of a congressional proceeding relating to his 2020 Senate testimony, where he denied authorizing FBI officials to leak information to the press — was thrown out in November 2025 after U.S. District Judge [[cameron-mcgowan-currie|Cameron McGowan Currie]], a Clinton appointee, ruled that prosecutor [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]] had been illegally appointed.[22] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said Comey and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] "got out on a technicality," adding that "if you look at the actual charges, I think anybody that looks at it very fairly would say, boy, are they guilty."[22] Trump emphasized that Currie's orders did not address the substance of the allegations and that prosecutors retained the option to re-bring or appeal the case; the administration announced on November 25 that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] would appeal.[22] Badlands writer Ashe in America wrote that the pair were "not off the hook."[22]

## Cited in the March 2024 Wray hearing

At the House Intelligence Committee's Annual Threat Assessment hearing on March 12, 2024, Representative Elise Stefanik invoked Comey's record while pressing FBI Director Christopher Wray, emphasizing that Comey had "testified that he did not follow the proper protocol regarding the notification of Congress of the opening of the Crossfire Hurricane" — the counterintelligence investigation into Donald Trump in 2016.[23] She used the precedent to argue that the Intelligence Authorization Act obliges the bureau to inform congressional leadership of counterintelligence investigations into federal candidates for office.[23]

## Ratcliffe perjury allegation (July 2025)

In a July 28, 2025 Fox News interview, [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] claimed that Comey, former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] had lied under oath about their involvement in the Russian election-interference narrative, and indicated that he had made criminal referrals accordingly.[24] Host Maria Bartiromo pressed Ratcliffe on whether indictments and prosecutions remained possible for the three; he replied, "Well, that's why I've made the referrals that I have."[24]


## May 2025 book tour and Secret Service inquiry

On Monday, May 19, 2025, Comey appeared at a Barnes & Noble in New York City, where he sat on a three-legged stool and promoted his new crime novel to a few dozen attendees at a book signing.[25] Days earlier he had provoked a firestorm on the right — and a [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] investigation — by posting an Instagram photograph that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and senior administration officials insisted was a call for the assassination of the president.[25] Talkative and confident, Comey brushed aside the possibility of criminal charges, calling the Secret Service agents who interviewed him "total pros" and saying of the controversy, "maybe it'll go away."[25]

NBC News paired the scene with the announcement by [[alina-habba|Alina Habba]], acting U.S. attorney in New Jersey, of criminal charges against Representative [[lamonica-mciver|LaMonica McIver]].[25] In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of May 21, 2025, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] asked what would have happened had [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] or [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] posted a death threat against the president during the Biden administration while they were media personalities and private citizens — noting that Comey now occupied exactly that role, on a book tour, while Patel and Bongino were running the [[fbi|FBI]].[25] She closed with the outlet's recurring refrain: "No one is above the law."[25]
