# Christopher Wray

**Christopher Wray** served as Director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] during the period in which the bureau conducted the investigation code-named [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]].[1]

## Arctic Frost scrutiny

Newly declassified revelations concerning Arctic Frost, published in October 2025, chronicled what Republicans characterized as a 2022 lawfare assault against then-former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the wider MAGA movement, with criminal inquiries accelerating as Trump moved toward another presidential run.[1] The revelation that the FBI had obtained the phone records of Republican members of Congress during its January 6 investigation brought greater scrutiny to Wray, during whose tenure the bureau effort occurred, and to then-Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], who led the Biden Justice Department's investigation into Trump.[1]

The same declassifications placed a spotlight on former FBI official [[timothy-thibault|Timothy Thibault]], whom Republicans accuse of extreme anti-Trump bias.[1]

## Withheld documents (March 2025)

In March 2025 the [[fbi|FBI]] produced hundreds of pages of subpoenaed material to the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] that Republicans said Wray had withheld during his tenure as director under the Biden administration.[2] The records concerned FBI inquiries into threats to school administrators, the pipe bombs discovered near the Capitol on January 6, 2021, alleged extremist ties to a faction of Catholicism, and the bureau's engagement with social media companies over foreign interference in the 2020 election.[2] See [[fbi-subpoenaed-document-production-to-the-house-judiciary-committee|FBI subpoenaed document production to the House Judiciary Committee]].[2]

## Terror threat warning after October 7 (2023)

On Tuesday, October 31, 2023, Wray warned the [[senate-homeland-security-committee|Senate Homeland Security Committee]] that the threat of a terror attack against Americans had been raised to a "whole other level" because of the ongoing conflict in the Middle East.[3] "The reality is that the terrorism threat has been elevated throughout 2023, but the ongoing war in the Middle East has raised the threat of an attack against Americans in the United States to a whole other level," he told lawmakers.[3] The warning followed the deadly [[hamas|Hamas]] attack on [[israel|Israel]] of October 7 and the ensuing Israeli military response, which renewed U.S. concern about copycat attacks by foreign, domestic, and homegrown terrorists.[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Wray as an interesting and enigmatic "[[deep-state|Deep State]]" figure whose factional loyalties he preferred to leave to others — such as Badlands analyst [[just-human|Just Human]] and his [[kayfabe|Kayfabe]]/false-feud theories — in favor of assessing the net effects of his narrative deployments.[3] By that reading, Wray's comments both ratcheted tension within the U.S. portion of the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]] and, to many in the Anon, Truth, and Conspiracy community as well as a growing plurality of moderates, carried with FBI backing "the shadow of a threat" amid rising bipartisan distrust of U.S. intelligence agencies and federal law enforcement over their perceived weaponization against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[3]

## Davos 2023 cybersecurity panel

On Tuesday, January 17, 2023, Wray spoke at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s annual meeting in Davos, appearing on a panel on national security and cybersecurity and separately holding a private meeting with global banking leaders to brief them on cyber threats.[4] The panel used the war in Ukraine as a lens through which to discuss partnerships between technology companies and government, artificial intelligence, and autonomous vehicles.[4] "The sophistication of the private sector is improving, and particularly important, the level of collaboration between the private sector and the government. Especially the FBI has I think made significant strides. We are focused on looking at cyber attacks," Wray said.[4] Reporting noted that his framing aligned with WEF founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]]'s call for leaders to "master the future" through public-private partnership.[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded that espousing collaboration between the federal government and big tech was unlikely to earn public trust, and left open whether Wray was playing a part or had cast his lot with the international ruling elite.[4]

## Preparations to resign (December 2024)

In December 2024, sources inside the bureau familiar with the director's thinking told the Washington Times that Wray did not want to be fired and would resign before President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was sworn into office.[5] Trump, who originally hired Wray but grew dissatisfied with him after years of what critics described as weaponization of the bureau - including the raid on Mar-a-Lago - had recently nominated [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] as a replacement.[5] Wray was also among President Joe Biden's allies whom Democrat and media supporters believed should receive a preemptive pardon; White House lawyers were reported to be studying such pardons, which commentators said would appear to project an admission of guilt.[5]

Reporting indicated that following Wray's departure Deputy Director [[paul-abbate|Paul Abbate]] would become acting director and appoint an acting deputy director, Abbate having originally planned to stay until May or June to help with the transition to a new FBI director.[5] Patel, Trump's pick to lead the bureau and a former chief of staff to the defense secretary and senior adviser to the National Security Council, was meeting Republican senators on Capitol Hill that week, having been tapped to shake up FBI management.[5] One source told the Times: "He's going to be gone at the inauguration. On or before the inauguration."[5]

Badlands commentary welcomed the departure, comparing theories that Wray is a secret white hat to similar claims about Mike Pence and observing that if he were playing a role, the goal of making people hate him was being achieved.[5] It argued Wray had to go so that Patel could take his seat, execute extreme declassification, and punish those who weaponized institutions against Americans.[5] The commentary disputed the [[fbi|FBI]]'s claim that the bureau had not weaponized justice, asserting that the bureau persecuted the Biden administration's political opponents and previously subverted the Trump administration before Trump took his oath, citing the case of General Michael Flynn.[5] Because Wray was leaving voluntarily, no fight was expected over the remainder of his term.[5]

## Expected removal after the 2024 election

By November 2024 Trump had signalled that he would fire Wray, whom he nominated in 2017 to a ten-year term after ousting [[james-comey|James Comey]], with reporting indicating that [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] was the likely successor at either the [[fbi|FBI]] or the Justice Department.[6]

## October 2023 warning on terror threats

Speaking at the International Association of Chiefs of Police annual conference in San Diego on Saturday, 14 October 2023, Wray, then 56, warned that the number of terror threats against the United States was rising and urged law enforcement to be extra cautious about ripple effects from the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.[7] "History has been witness to antisemitic and other forms of violent extremism for far too long," he said according to an [[fbi|FBI]] transcript, adding, "In this heightened environment, there's no question we're seeing an increase in reported threats, and we've got to be on the lookout, especially for lone actors who may take inspiration from recent events to commit violence of their own."[7] His central concern was described as the possibility of lone wolves carrying out [[hamas|Hamas]] copy-cat attacks on US soil, and the warning came a day after former Hamas chief [[khaled-meshaal|Khaled Meshaal]] designated a "Day of Jihad" on October 13.[7]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the statement as a rare "Narrative Deployment" legible to both mainstream and dissident audiences — read by the former as a genuine spillover risk and by the latter as a veiled threat, predictive programming, or a Hegelian manoeuvre by the domestic arm of the Deep State — while noting it underscored the sociopolitical tension of the [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] era relative to nostalgia for the [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] years.[7]

## January 5 pipe bomb inquiry

Wray was the recipient of sustained congressional pressure over the [[january-5-2021-pipe-bombs|pipe bombs placed at the DNC and RNC headquarters]] on January 5, 2021.[8] In early 2023 he told Fox News anchor Bret Baier that the bureau was working very hard on the case, while refusing to discuss informants or agents who might have been involved on January 6.[8] In May 2023 [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], Representative [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] and Representative [[bill-posey|Bill Posey]] wrote to Wray documenting briefing requests dating to September 2, 2021 that the FBI had left unanswered for more than a year and a half, and citing a whistleblower disclosure raising questions about the "unusual" nature of the investigation.[8]
