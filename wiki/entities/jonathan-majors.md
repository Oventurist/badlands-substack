# Jonathan Majors

Jonathan Majors is an American actor who, by early 2023, had become one of Hollywood's most rapidly ascending stars, appearing in *Creed III* and *Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania* and taking on the role of the central antagonist Kang in [[marvel-studios|Marvel Studios]]' slate of interconnected films.[1]

## March 2023 arrest

On the morning of Saturday, March 25, 2023, Majors, then 33, was arrested in New York City in connection with an alleged domestic dispute, according to the [[nypd|New York Police Department]].[1] He was taken into custody after a 911 call was placed from an apartment in the Chelsea neighborhood of Manhattan.[1] In a statement, police said the victim — a 30-year-old woman — informed officers that she had been assaulted and had sustained minor injuries to her head and neck.[1] Majors faced charges of strangulation, assault and harassment.[1]

An attorney for Majors, Priya Chaudhry, said the actor was "completely innocent" and was "provably the victim of an altercation with a woman he knows."[1] Chaudhry added that his team was "quickly gathering and presenting evidence to the District Attorney with the expectation that all charges will be dropped imminently."[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands Media commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the Majors arrest within a broader pattern of what he described as an escalation striking "some of the biggest woke entertainment brands in the world," listing it alongside heavy financial losses on recent Marvel films, mounting net losses at Disney+, and the abrupt firing of Marvel executive [[victoria-alonso|Victoria Alonso]].[1] Because Majors had been positioned as Marvel's next tentpole villain, his arrest was read as a structural blow to the studio's multi-film plans, summarized in the line "Pride ... meet Fall."[1]

## December 2023 conviction and Disney firing

On December 18, 2023, Majors was found guilty of assault and harassment stemming from a March incident involving his ex-girlfriend, while being acquitted of other charges including assault with intent to cause injury.[2] Within an hour of the verdict, [[walt-disney-company|Disney]] cut ties with him — a move the industry broadly regarded as a no-brainer — after he had already been dropped by his management and PR teams and after Disney's specialty arm Searchlight Pictures pulled his Sundance-acquired star vehicle "Magazine Dreams" from its release schedule.[2] One producer told Business Insider anonymously, "I wouldn't hire him. He was on his way to being big, but I can't have him front a movie now."[2] Because Majors had been cast as Kang the Conqueror — the cornerstone villain of the next phase of the [[marvel-cinematic-universe|Marvel Cinematic Universe]], slated to return in 2026's "Avengers: The Kang Dynasty" after "Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania" and season two of "Loki" — the verdict forced [[marvel-studios|Marvel]] to reset a swath of interconnected billion-dollar story arcs.[2] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the episode to what he called Disney's broader "Crisis Cascade" of lawsuits, box-office failures and a board proxy fight, and to the resurfacing Epstein thread.[2]
