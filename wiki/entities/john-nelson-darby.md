# John Nelson Darby

John Nelson Darby was the godson of Admiral Horatio Nelson, founder of the "Exclusive Brethren" branch of the [[plymouth-brethren]], and, in the article's phrase, the "father of modern rapture theology."[1]

## Doctrine and American tours

Darby, who considered himself a prophet, conducted six tours of the United States, seeding his doctrine into dozens of gnostic cults, each of which taught followers to interpret Bible prophecy the same way.[1] That interpretation, the article says, required sending all Jews to Palestine, after which a "secret rapture" for believers would unfold, followed by a hellscape of pain for heathens left behind under global war and the anti-Christ.[1] His scheme of [[dispensationalism]] divided history into seven phases, ending with a "Thousand Year Rule" preceded by a Rapture and followed by a seven-year period of absolute dominance by the Anti-Christ.[1]

In 1856 Darby taught that Russia — then Britain's dominant nemesis after the United States — was the anti-Christ and that the American Civil War was a sign of the End Times, encouraging his American followers not to fight to save the Union since that would go against God's will.[1]

## Influence

Movements influenced or created by Darby and the Plymouth Brethren include the work of Cyrus Scofield, whose 1909 reference Bible became the most popular in the US during the 20th century and drew heavily on Darby; Charles Fox Parham, founder of Pentecostalism; [[george-hawkins-pember]], originator of the "fallen Nephilim" interpretation of demonology now advanced by the alien disclosure movement; Dwight Lyman Moody, founder of Moody Bible College; and James Hall Brookes, founding president of the Niagara Bible Conference, which spread Dispensationalism across America.[1] The article argues that the entire [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionist]] movement, "from John Hagee to Benny Hinn and Pat Robertson," sits on foundations created by Darby's sect rather than the Bible.[1]

Darby is also said to have infused his Bible translations with language and terms used only by the Theosophists, and the self-professed satanist [[aleister-crowley]] emerged out of Darby's Plymouth Brethren milieu.[1] William Eugene Blackstone, the American who persuaded [[theodor-herzl]] that Jerusalem was the divinely ordained site, was a Darby devotee.[1]
