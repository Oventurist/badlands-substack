# UNRWA

The United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) is the [[united-nations|United Nations]] body responsible for services to Palestinian refugees and, as of April 2024, was described as the most important aid agency operating in [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## 2024 funding suspension

The United States suspended its funding to UNRWA in late January 2024 in response to Israeli allegations that a significant number of the agency's employees were members of terrorist organizations.[1] An independent report released on April 22, 2024 found that Israel had not provided any evidence to support that claim.[1]

Despite the report, the Israel aid legislation passed by the Republican-controlled House of Representatives over the weekend of April 20–21, 2024 would prohibit funding for the organization.[1] Senator [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] announced an amendment that would restore U.S. funding to UNRWA, alongside a separate amendment to "protect essential humanitarian operations" in the Gaza Strip, where at least 28 children under the age of 12 were reported to have starved to death in the preceding weeks amid what critics described as an illegal Israeli blockade.[1]

## Origins and legal basis

UNRWA was established in 1949 to deal with Palestinian refugees after the creation of the state of Israel led to the Arab–Israeli war.[2] In 1967, following the Six-Day War, Israel requested that UNRWA continue its operations in Gaza and the West Bank, and the agency agreed to facilitate that arrangement.[2]

## 2024 Israeli termination of the 1967 agreement

In early November 2024 Israel formally notified the [[united-nations|United Nations]] that it was terminating the 1967 agreement, having accused the organization of ties to [[hamas|Hamas]] and other Palestinian militant groups.[2] The Israeli parliament had passed legislation the previous week banning the agency's work in the country.[2] Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] said that "UNRWA – the organization whose employees participated in the October 7 massacre and many of whose employees are Hamas operatives – is part of the problem in the Gaza Strip and not part of the solution," adding that the UN had been presented with "endless evidence about Hamas operatives working at UNRWA and about the use of UNRWA facilities for the terror purposes and nothing was done about it."[2]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] welcomed the move, arguing that by branding UNRWA a terrorist organization [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had effectively accused the United Nations itself of sponsoring terrorism — and that whether the allegations were true was beside the point, since America's "greatest ally" had now made the charge on the eve of the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 general election]].[2]

## Israeli ban (October 2024)

On Monday, October 28, 2024, Israel's parliament, the [[knesset|Knesset]], voted to ban the operations of UNRWA, the main humanitarian aid agency operating in [[gaza|Gaza]].[3] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] reiterated accusations that UNRWA employees were involved in terrorist activities in the region in a statement accompanying the legislation, and his office said that UNRWA personnel "must be held accountable."[3]

An investigation by the U.N. Office of Internal Oversight Services closed in August 2024, debunking some of the allegations and noting that others lacked sufficient evidence; the agency nevertheless said nine employees may have been involved in the October 7 attack if the evidence were "authenticated and corroborated," and Commissioner-General [[philippe-lazzarini|Philippe Lazzarini]] said their contracts would be terminated.[3] Netanyahu's office stated that in the ninety days before the legislation took effect — and afterward — Israel stood ready to work with international partners to ensure it continued to facilitate humanitarian assistance to civilians in Gaza in a manner not threatening Israeli security.[3]

The ban was reported alongside a bipartisan letter from more than one hundred members of [[congress|Congress]] to Secretary-General [[antonio-guterres|António Guterres]] warning that any downgrade of Israel's standing at the [[united-nations|UN]] would bring a corresponding downgrade in U.S. support for the organization.[3]
