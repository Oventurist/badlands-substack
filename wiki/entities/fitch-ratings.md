# Fitch Ratings

Fitch is one of the major credit ratings agencies whose sovereign assessments are used by investors to gauge the risk profile of companies and governments raising financing in the debt capital markets.[1]

## 2023 downgrade of the United States

On Tuesday, August 1, 2023, Fitch downgraded the United States government's top credit rating from AAA to AA+, citing an expected fiscal deterioration over the next three years as well as a high and growing general government debt burden.[1] The dollar ticked lower following the announcement.[1] In its statement the agency said that "there has been a steady deterioration in standards of governance over the last 20 years, including on fiscal and debt matters, notwithstanding the June bipartisan agreement to suspend the debt limit until January 2025."[1]

The downgrade came two months after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and the Republican-controlled House reached a debt ceiling agreement following months of political brinkmanship, a deal that lifted the government's $31.4 trillion debt ceiling.[1] Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] said she disagreed with the downgrade, calling it "arbitrary and based on outdated data."[1]

Fitch's move placed it alongside [[standard-and-poors|Standard & Poor's]], which had cut the United States to AA+ in 2011 and never restored the top rating.[1]

## Warning on China's A+ rating (2023)

Two weeks after downgrading the United States, Fitch signalled that China's A+ sovereign credit rating could also come under review if the country's non-government debt liabilities ballooned amid a faltering economy.[2] [[james-mccormack|James McCormack]], the agency's global head of sovereigns, told Bloomberg TV on August 16, 2023 that if Beijing expanded its balance sheet to support the economy, "we might think again, because the debt-to-GDP ratio is a little bit on the high side for a single 'A' credit."[2]

McCormack said he did not expect an imminent downgrade and that recent evidence did not indicate such an expansion was coming, but warned that rising corporate and banking sector debt could become "real liabilities for the government."[2] The warning followed the [[peoples-bank-of-china|People's Bank of China]] injecting its largest short-term cash volume since February, one day after cutting interest rates.[2]
