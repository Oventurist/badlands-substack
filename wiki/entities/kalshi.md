# Kalshi

Kalshi is a regulated prediction market platform that allows users to trade contracts on politics, elections, sports, and current events.[1] In April 2026 it became the first such platform to issue insider trading bans against political candidates who wagered on their own races.[1]

## First insider trading bans (2026)

Kalshi suspended three political candidates and issued its first-ever insider trading bans for betting on their own 2026 primary races.[1] The candidates were [[mark-moran|Mark Moran]], an Independent running in the Virginia U.S. Senate Democratic primary; [[matt-klein|Matt Klein]], a Democrat in a Minnesota primary race; and [[zeke-enriquez|Zeke Enriquez]], a Republican in a Texas primary race.[1] All three had wagered on their own election outcomes on the platform.[1]

The candidates were flagged under newly implemented safeguards designed to block political candidates from trading on their own races.[1] Kalshi's head of enforcement said that any trade by a candidate capable of influencing a market outcome violates the rules regardless of size, described the cases as political insider trading, and confirmed that the matters were not referred to the [[commodity-futures-trading-commission|Commodity Futures Trading Commission]] or the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[1]

## Scrutiny of prediction markets

The enforcement actions came amid growing scrutiny of prediction markets such as Kalshi and Polymarket, with lawmakers warning that large stakes on elections could incentivize interference or otherwise undermine the electoral process.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that even with insider trading occurring inside predictive gambling markets, the results may be more reliable and legitimate — and are certainly more auditable — than the reported outcomes of U.S. elections.[1] She noted that weighting the predictions has been a problem for Kalshi from the start, since political markets can swing thirty percentage points in a day as political action committees flood the site, making prices an indicator only of where money is rather than of underlying sentiment.[1] Because reported outcomes on the site can nonetheless sway voters, she framed the manipulation of predictive markets as a potential catalyst for public anger about election integrity.[1]

## September 2024 court victory on election contracts

On Friday, September 6, 2024, a federal judge cleared the way for Americans to place bets on the outcome of congressional elections through Kalshi, a ruling described as potentially opening the door to legalized wagering on U.S. elections generally.[2] In a one-page order, U.S. District Judge [[jia-cobb|Jia Cobb]] of the District of Columbia sided with the startup and threw out a 2023 decision by the [[commodity-futures-trading-commission|Commodity Futures Trading Commission]] that had blocked Kalshi from listing its congressional-control contracts.[2]

Kalshi co-founder Luana Lopes Lara said the company would make its congressional-control contracts — including markets on whether Democrats would retake the U.S. House of Representatives in November — available to users the following week, and would move quickly to list other kinds of political-event contracts.[2] It remained uncertain over the weekend whether the markets would go live, as regulators filed an emergency motion seeking a two-week delay while they considered an appeal.[2] The item was carried among the bonus stories in the September 10, 2024 Badlands News Brief.[2]
