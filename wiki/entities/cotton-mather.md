# Cotton Mather

Cotton Mather was a Boston Puritan divine presented in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin" as the mentor and secret patron of the young [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] and as the leader of a colonial republican resistance to occult networks operating out of the British Empire.[1]

## The witch trials

Cotton Mather and his father [[increase-mather|Increase Mather]] are said to have played a dual role during the New England witch panics: exposing the actual satanic covens on the one hand, while simultaneously resisting the more fanatical zealots among superstitious Puritans who threatened to transform a serious fight against evil into a paranoia-led terror.[1] The essay situates this within the wider frenzy of witch covens — often overseen by Druid priests typically left untouched by the witch trials of Europe — that spread deeply into the colonies, including the Connecticut-centred trials of 1648–1663 with 80 accused and 15 executions, and the [[salem-witch-trials|Salem witch trials]] of 1692–1693 with 19 executions.[1]

## Republican organising and Franklin

In 1710 Mather launched a broadened republican organising effort with his *Essays to Do Good*.[1] Franklin later described the book as "an influence on my conduct through life; for I have always set a greater value on the character of a doer of good, than on any other kind of reputation; and if I have been, as you seem to think, a useful citizen, the public owes the advantage of it to that book."[1]

With the death of Queen Anne and the ascension of George I of Hanover to the English throne in 1714, the essay says the Hell-Fire Club high priests became the dominant force of British imperial strategy, and an aging Cotton Mather recruited the young Franklin to combat them.[1] Quoting [[graham-lowry|Graham Lowry]], the article states that in 1721, at the age of fifteen, Franklin joined the battle against these forces in the middle of one of the most intensive campaigns to destroy the Mathers that their opponents had ever mounted — a campaign in which Franklin's own half-brother [[james-franklin|James Franklin]] was on the enemy side, allied with the Anglican propagandist [[john-checkley|John Checkley]] and the radical Whigs of Elisha Cooke, Jr.'s political machine.[1] After his return from England, Franklin became a leading republican figure within the networks Mather had established.[1]
