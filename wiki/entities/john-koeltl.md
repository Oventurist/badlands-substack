# John Koeltl

John Koeltl is a United States District Court judge based in Manhattan, appointed by President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]], who presided over a lawsuit brought against the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]] by journalists and allies of [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] founder [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]].[1]

## Assange visitors lawsuit

In December 2023, Koeltl declined the spy agency's bid to dismiss the case, allowing four Americans who had visited Assange during his stay at the Ecuadorian Embassy in London to proceed with claims that a security contractor gave the CIA data copied from their phones during those visits.[1] In a 27-page decision, Koeltl rejected portions of the suit, including allegations that the CIA violated the plaintiffs' rights by eavesdropping on embassy conversations and by obtaining copies of their passports.[1]

Koeltl nevertheless held that accessing the contents of the visitors' phones, if it occurred, invaded their privacy rights under the Constitution, writing that "the misconduct alleged is a violation of the plaintiffs' reasonable expectation of privacy in the contents of their electronic devices under the Fourth Amendment."[1] He threw out the portion of the lawsuit seeking money damages from former CIA Director [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]], but ruled that the plaintiffs could continue to seek an order requiring the agency to destroy any records gleaned from their phones.[1] Spokespeople for the CIA and for the U.S. Attorney's Office in Manhattan, representing the federal government, declined to comment.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[just-human|Just Human]] argued that Assange was "no ally or hero" and that the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration was right to indict him, while contending that claims Pompeo plotted to assassinate Assange were an inversion designed to blame the Trump administration for what the wider intelligence apparatus itself desired.[1]
