# Muslim Brotherhood

The Muslim Brotherhood — specifically the Syrian Muslim Brotherhood — is named in the article as an intermediary in the [[cia|CIA]]-directed smuggling of weapons into Syria.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Citing the World Socialist Web Site's summary of a June 2012 New York Times report, the article states that CIA operatives deployed to [[turkey|Turkey]] directed a massive smuggling operation in which automatic rifles, rocket-propelled grenades, ammunition and some antitank weapons were funneled mostly across the Turkish border by way of "a shadowy network of intermediaries, including the Syrian Muslim Brotherhood," paid for by [[qatar|Qatar]], Saudi Arabia and Turkey.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article closes by highlighting this detail — that the CIA's partner in Turkey for arming [[isis|ISIS]] was the Muslim Brotherhood — and links it to its recurring question about why [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] staffed his administration with figures the author describes as hostile to America, promising to address it in a subsequent piece.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## The release of Abdelhakim Belhaj

"A Conflict of Interest" places the Brotherhood at the centre of the 2009 release of [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] from Libyan custody: with the help of the Muslim Brotherhood preacher Ali Sallabi, Belhaj negotiated his liberation with [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]]'s son Saïf al-Islam.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article treats Sallabi's involvement as an important data point, arguing that the newly inaugurated Obama administration had asked Gaddafi to release Belhaj and other members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], and that Gaddafi complied to preserve goodwill with the incoming president.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It asserts that Obama has strong ties to the Muslim Brotherhood and promises to present that evidence in later articles.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Infiltration through Huma Abedin

Part 2 of the America for Sale series treats the Brotherhood as the organisation that "infiltrated" the U.S. government through [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], quoting Q drop 71 of November 4, 2017, which asked who connects HRC and the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] to Saudi Arabia, who the Muslim Brotherhood is, and who has ties to it.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Abedin is described as deeply connected to the Brotherhood and as having remained assistant editor of the Brotherhood journal her parents helped found in Jeddah throughout her service at the [[state-department|State Department]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Through her, the article argues, the Brotherhood was connected to everything she touched — the Clinton Foundation, [[teneo-holdings|Teneo]] and Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s office.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The author repeats the formulation that "it was an infiltration not an invasion," attributing the Brotherhood's access to the approval of [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]] after he installed [[barack-obama|Obama]] as president.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Jordanian ban (2025)

On Wednesday, April 23, 2025, [[jordan|Jordan]]'s Interior Ministry announced a sweeping set of measures against the Muslim Brotherhood, formally declaring the group dissolved and illegal.[4] Interior Minister [[mazen-faraya|Mazen Faraya]] said membership in or affiliation with the group was prohibited by law, that all of its offices across the Kingdom had been permanently closed under a judicial order, and that security forces had been deployed to enforce the decision and confiscate the organization's assets.[4] He described the group's continued activities as a threat to citizens, a barrier to national development, and a destabilizing force, alleging that members had planned to target sensitive sites, stored weapons and explosives in residential neighborhoods, and operated covertly to undermine public security.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the ban came the same day [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]] met Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and framed the Brotherhood as an instrument of Western intelligence — quipping that Jordan had banned "MI6 — er, I mean the Muslim Brotherhood" — and casting the move as part of the [[cia|CIA]] and [[mi6|MI6]] being pushed out of the Middle East and Africa.[4]

## Qatari hosting in the Badlands framework

Badlands Media identifies the Muslim Brotherhood as one of several "Deep State proxy groups" whose leadership is hosted in [[qatar|Qatar]], attributing the organization's sponsorship to [[mi6|MI6]].[6] In the same account, [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]] is described as purportedly a Muslim Brotherhood outlet — which the writer suggests "probably means it's connected to London, too" — and as the main cheerleader for the color revolutions of the [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]].[6]
