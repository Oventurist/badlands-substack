# Muslim Brotherhood

The Muslim Brotherhood is a transnational Sunni Islamist organization founded in Egypt in 1928. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," the Muslim Brotherhood is portrayed as a key ideological and organizational force behind the Obama administration's Middle East policy, and as a network that facilitated the release of terrorists and the overthrow of secular Arab governments.

According to the article, Muslim Brotherhood preacher Ali Sallabi negotiated the release of [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] from a Libyan prison in 2009, acting on behalf of the Obama administration, which wanted to maintain good relations with the new president. The source claims that "Obama has strong ties to the Muslim Brotherhood," and that this relationship influenced U.S. policy in Libya and Syria.

The article asserts that the Muslim Brotherhood's strategy was to encourage Islamists to become political forces in Arab countries, using the same playbook that the CIA allegedly used in Lebanon to empower Hezbollah. It claims that this approach allowed terrorist networks to gain political legitimacy while continuing to fund and carry out violent operations. The source frames the Muslim Brotherhood as part of a broader cabal that uses both political and violent means to expand its control over Middle Eastern countries and divert Western taxpayer money to its causes.

According to the article, the Muslim Brotherhood's influence extended to the Obama administration's decisions to support rebel groups in Libya and Syria, and to pressure [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]] to release Belhaj and other LIFG members. The source claims that these decisions were not driven by democratic ideals but by a desire to place terrorist-friendly governments in power that would continue the flow of money and weapons to U.S. enemies.

## Stealth jihad and the Abedin network (America for Sale - Part 1)

The December 2024 essay [[america-for-sale-part-1|America for Sale - Part 1]] presents the Brotherhood's strategy as explicitly twofold. Publicly the organization denounced violence — in the author's reading, to avoid designation as a terrorist organization in the United States — while continuing to fund groups such as Hamas, Hezbollah, and [[al-qaeda|al-Qaeda]] through global charities. Its second track was to "infiltrate the governments of non-Muslim countries by recruiting from within," a program the article labels [[civilization-stealth-jihad|"civilization/stealth jihad"]] and summarizes as "Infiltration, not invasion."

Universities are identified as the mechanism. The article describes the *[[journal-of-muslim-minority-affairs|Journal of Muslim Minority Affairs]]*, published by the [[institute-of-muslim-minority-affairs|Institute of Muslim Minority Affairs]] and founded in Jeddah in the late 1970s with the help of [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]]'s father, as "an international propaganda journal for the Muslim Brotherhood." Its patron [[abdullah-omar-naseef|Abdullah Omar Naseef]], secretary-general of the [[muslim-world-league|Muslim World League]] from 1983 to 1993, is said to have underwritten efforts to indoctrinate young Muslims at educational institutions. The campus arm is the [[muslim-students-association|Muslim Students Association]], with hundreds of chapters in North America, whose alumni include [[anwar-al-awlaki|Anwar al-Awlaki]] and [[wael-jalaidan|Wael Jalaidan]]. Quoting PJMedia, the article states the goal is "incrementally to infiltrate [[sharia-law|sharia]] principles in our law, our institutions, and our public policy," and argues the campaign "culminated with the installment of the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration."
