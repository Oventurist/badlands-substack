# Keir Starmer

Keir Starmer is the British prime minister, cited by Badlands Media as one of the Western leaders who echoed the standard triumphal script after the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in December 2024.[1]

The article notes that Starmer delivered his remarks while he was, "coincidentally," abroad in Dubai meeting with the autocrats of the United Arab Emirates — a juxtaposition used to underline the article's argument that Western objections to dictatorship are selective.[1] It states sardonically that Starmer is "very pleased" that al-Qaeda is running the show in Syria, a reference to the al-Qaeda lineage of [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]].[1]

## Mandelson scandal and record-low approval (2026)

By early 2026 Starmer faced resignation calls over a scandal involving [[peter-mandelson|Peter Mandelson]], a former ambassador linked to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], and held record-low approval ratings.[2] He was grouped with French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] and German Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]] among European incumbents whose unpopularity has driven far-right parties to lead or near-lead polls in Germany, the U.K., France, and Italy amid inflation, economic stagnation, and immigration backlash.[2]

## Comment on the Kanye West festival booking (2026)

In April 2026, Starmer said the decision to book [[kanye-west|Kanye West]] as headliner for all three nights of London's [[wireless-festival|Wireless Festival]] in July was "deeply concerning," amid criticism over West's past antisemitic statements.[3] Pepsi and Diageo withdrew sponsorship from the festival following the backlash.[3]

## Epstein-related staff departures (February 2026)

In February 2026 Starmer lost senior aides — including chief of staff Morgan McSweeney and communications director Tim Allan — amid scrutiny tied to [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]]-related associations.[4] Media reports, including in The Economist, suggested Starmer was under pressure over questions of judgment, vetting and institutional accountability; no charges have been reported for Starmer or his staff.[4] The UK departures ran in parallel with a stalled US congressional track, where [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] invoked the Fifth Amendment rather than testify.[4]

## Response to the Unite the Kingdom rally (2026)

Starmer condemned the May 2026 "[[unite-the-kingdom-rally|Unite the Kingdom]]" rally in London, organized by activist [[tommy-robinson|Tommy Robinson]], as promoting "hatred and division"; police reported dozens of arrests, including several tied to alleged hate-crime offenses.[5] His condemnation stood in direct contrast to US Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], who told the rally's supporters it was "OK to defend your culture" and urged them to "keep on going."[5]

## Resignation and Pride remarks (July 2026)

In July 2026, as outgoing prime minister, Starmer marked Pride events at Downing Street by highlighting his government's record on LGBTQ+ issues, saying he was "really proud" that the United Kingdom has "the gayest parliament… anywhere in the world."[6] He also pledged that his government would continue to defend the rights of lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender people, and pointed to a proposed ban on conversion practices as a key policy initiative.[6] The remarks came as Starmer prepared to leave office following his resignation announcement and the start of the governing [[labour-party|Labour Party]]'s leadership transition.[6]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the episode as a study in [[memetics|memetics]], noting that nobody would refute the claims themselves but that their virality was the point, and describing Starmer as the seventh prime minister in ten years whose legacy was thereby secured.[6] The commentary placed the remarks against what it called an intensifying [[humiliation-ritual|humiliation ritual]] directed at the British people amid the ongoing revelation of [[grooming-gangs|grooming gangs]].[6]

## Response to the 2026 U.S. troop withdrawal

Following President Trump's decision to withdraw thousands of American troops from Germany, Starmer highlighted the need to address tensions within alliances, joining other European leaders responding to the drawdown.[7]


## Expected resignation (June 2026)

The Observer reported in June 2026 that Starmer was expected to announce a timetable for his departure from Downing Street as early as the following Monday, having concluded after discussions with cabinet ministers, advisers, trade union leaders and party donors that his position was no longer tenable.[8] The report said [[andy-burnham|Andy Burnham]], fresh from victory in the Makerfield by-election, was due to be sworn in as a member of Parliament that Monday and was expected to meet Starmer early in the week, with Burnham's supporters claiming the backing of more than 201 [[labour-party|Labour]] MPs for a leadership challenge if Starmer did not step down voluntarily.[8]

Some Labour figures expected an orderly transition potentially culminating in a leadership change later in the year, though a Downing Street source dismissed reports of an imminent resignation as "just speculation," and Starmer had said on the preceding Friday that he would stand in any Labour leadership contest and that he had "more to do" as prime minister.[8] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] mocked the timing, noting that England was simultaneously dominating the [[2026-fifa-world-cup|World Cup]] as a top-three side while its prime minister prepared to resign.[8]

## Ukraine long-range weapons discussions (September 2024)

In September 2024, as British Prime Minister, Starmer was due to meet U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in the United States amid signs that both Washington and London were growing more receptive to allowing Ukraine to use Western-supplied arms against targets farther inside Russia than previously permitted.[9] His Foreign Secretary, [[david-lammy|David Lammy]], had travelled to Kyiv with U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and pledged to bring Ukrainian requests to their leaders.[9] Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] responded that such approval would place [[nato|NATO]] countries "at war" with Russia.[9]

## September 13, 2024 Washington meeting

Starmer flew from London to Washington on Friday, September 13, 2024, reportedly to deliver a target list for long-range missile strikes inside Russia.[10] According to Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]], Starmer was told to keep the target list in his bag because the missiles were not being deployed, per the [[pentagon]] — a reversal coming roughly 24 hours after [[state-department|State Department]] leaders had openly said the deal was done.[10] [[scott-ritter|Scott Ritter]] said [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] had overruled the military's initial denial because the matter had become political, but that "phone calls were made" and Pentagon officials intervened at the meeting to tell Biden and Starmer the deal was off.[10] Badlands commentary described the episode as two of the world's most powerful leaders being put "into time out" by US military officials.[10]


## Eastern Sentry and Polish air defense (September 2025)

After Russian drones breached Polish airspace in September 2025, Starmer backed the deployment of [[royal-air-force|RAF]] Typhoons to conduct air defense missions over [[poland|Poland]] as part of the [[nato|NATO]] operation [[eastern-sentry|Eastern Sentry]].[11] He emphasised that "Russia's reckless behaviour is a direct threat to European security."[11] His Defence Secretary [[john-healey|John Healey]] said NATO had responded with unity to the challenge posed by Moscow.[11]

## Grooming gangs inquiry dispute (January 2025)

In January 2025 Starmer criticised [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and others for jumping on the "bandwagon of the far-right" by raising criticisms over the [[grooming-gangs|grooming gang]] child rape scandal and demanding fresh national investigations.[12] In his first major comments after international scrutiny turned to the failures to protect thousands of mostly young white girls from predators, mostly of Muslim extraction and from Pakistan, he again rejected the idea that a national public inquiry was necessary and complained about "misinformation" on X.[12]

Starmer denied seeking to obscure his own history, noting that "there is nothing secret about being director of public prosecutions" and that his record "is open," and said that those "spreading lies and misinformation as far and as wide as possible" were "not interested in victims, they are interested in themselves."[12] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] included "the UK PM's role in UK grooming gangs" among the power-dynamic stories converging on the "city states" of Washington, D.C., the City of London and Vatican City two weeks before Inauguration Day, while [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] predicted Starmer would be ushered offstage as [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] had been.[12]

## Franco-British peacekeeping proposal (March 2025)

In March 2025 the United Kingdom under Starmer joined France in leading efforts to form a "coalition of the willing" to deploy peacekeeping troops to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] once a truce was reached, a plan under which French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] suggested "a few thousand troops per country" would be positioned at key locations.[13] Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] rejected the proposal outright before the Italian Senate, calling it "a very complex, risky and ineffective option."[13]

Badlands commentary characterized the initiative as "the beginning of the end of NATO," on the reasoning that Starmer and Macron were effectively proposing an offensive campaign on behalf of a defensive alliance in a conflict to which neither Ukraine nor [[nato|NATO]] is formally a party, and that member states' appetite for such a war was plainly not unanimous.[13]


## Rome meeting with Giorgia Meloni on migration (September 2024)

On September 16, 2024, Starmer met Italian Premier [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] in Rome and praised her nationalist conservative government's "remarkable progress" in reducing the number of migrants reaching [[italy|Italy]]'s shores by boat.[14] The meeting followed the deaths of at least eight seaborne migrants off the French coast over the preceding weekend while attempting to cross the English Channel.[14] Though the Labour prime minister was not a natural ally of Meloni, who leads the far-right Brothers of Italy party, migration had risen up the U.K. political agenda and Starmer sought to apply Italy's tough approach to stopping crossings in flimsy, overcrowded boats.[14] More than 22,000 migrants had crossed from [[france|France]] to the U.K. that year, slightly more than the same period in 2023, with several dozen deaths including the eight killed when a boat carrying about 60 people ran aground on rocks.[14]

Talks were held at the Villa Doria Pamphilj, a 17th-century mansion in a large park near the Vatican, with migration forming the core of the agenda; Italian Interior Ministry figures showed boat arrivals down 60% in the first half of 2024 versus 2023.[14] At a joint news conference Starmer credited Italy with cracking down on smuggling gangs and "working with countries along migration routes as equals."[14] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the meeting as evidence that European leaders across the political spectrum were converging on migration restriction, arguing the lesson for Americans was that reversal is possible and simply requires will.[14]

## Opposition to a fresh grooming gangs inquiry

In the first parliamentary exchange on child sex gangs after the issue regained public prominence, on January 8, 2025, Starmer maintained that Britain faced a binary choice between acting on recommendations already made by past, less comprehensive reports and launching a fresh years-long investigation — arguing that doing both was somehow impossible.[15] Conservative leader [[kemi-badenoch|Kemi Badenoch]] responded that the full extent of the [[grooming-gangs|grooming gangs]] scandal remained unknown because the necessary research had never been done, telling the House that "there is still work to be done."[15] The clash extended the bitter argument between Labour and the Conservatives over responsibility for decades of industrial-scale child rape by migrant-heritage gangs.[15]

## Emergence as a transatlantic broker on Ukraine (March 2025)

By early March 2025, Politico described Starmer — "hardly the most obvious wartime envoy" — as having emerged as a leading player in the international response since [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] first shocked allies with his stance on Ukraine.[16] His first seven months in Downing Street were characterized as unsteady: he struggled to set a clear domestic agenda, took a hammering in the polls, fumbled the handling of a scandal over freebies, lost his chief of staff, and introduced an unpopular hit on pensioners' benefits.[16] His bureaucratic, plodding style left even his own supporters exasperated at times, and his quest for economic growth proved fruitless.[16] Yet after Trump threatened to turn his back on Ukraine and opened talks with Russia while sidelining Kyiv, Starmer played an increasingly visible and assured role in transatlantic diplomacy.[16]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright recounted that Trump had asked Starmer the previous week whether the UK would take on Russia by itself and received only a strained smile in response, arguing that both men know the EU bureaucracy is powerless without US military backing and that such backing is now at an end.[16]

## Scotland meeting with Trump on Gaza (July 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] made his remarks that there was "real starvation" in [[gaza|Gaza]] while speaking during a meeting with Starmer in Scotland in late July 2025.[17] Trump used the occasion to distance himself from Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s denial of hunger in the territory, saying "nobody's done anything great over there" and that he had told [[israel|Israel]] "maybe they have to do it a different way."[17]

## White House visit and Ukraine disagreements (February 2025)

Starmer met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the White House in late February 2025, in a visit the BBC described as one where personal rapport mattered as much as substance.[18] An invitation from King Charles for an unprecedented second state visit to the United Kingdom set a flattering tone; Trump called it "a great honour, because it's never happened before," adding that the honour was "not for me, it's for our country."[18] Despite Starmer's diplomatic language — including praise for Trump for creating conditions in which a ceasefire in Ukraine could be discussed — significant disagreements remained over the shape of any peace deal, with Trump saying he believed he could trust [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to abide by a deal and Starmer stressing that any truce must endure and must not favour the aggressor.[18]

Starmer said the UK was "ready to put boots on the ground and planes in the air to support a deal, working together with our allies, because that is the only way that peace will last."[18] Trump declined to guarantee U.S. backup for British forces deployed to Ukraine, saying British troops "can take care of themselves" and "don't need much help," and sidestepping a question about support in the event of clashes with Russian forces.[18] Badlands contributors mocked Starmer's performance, with one writing that Trump had "spanked" him and expressing a preference for [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]], and another concluding that the episode marked the end of "the illusion of Europe."[18]

## Discussions on long-range missiles for Ukraine (September 2024)

On Friday, September 13, 2024, Starmer and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] discussed allowing Ukraine to use long-range European-made cruise missiles to strike targets deep inside Russia, according to US and Western officials.[19] Western backers had until then balked at permitting Kyiv to use long-range weapons such as the British-French Storm Shadow inside Russian territory for fear of escalating the conflict, with particular concern that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] could retaliate by arming the Yemen-based [[houthis|Houthi]] rebels attacking shipping in the Red Sea.[19] Putin warned the United States and its allies that permitting such strikes would mean [[nato|NATO]] countries were "at war" with Russia; asked about that warning shortly before his White House meeting with Starmer, Biden said, "I don't think much about Vladimir Putin."[19]

## Congratulating Trump on the 2024 election

Starmer, the Labour prime minister of the United Kingdom, congratulated [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on a "historic election victory" in November 2024, posting on X: "As the closest of allies, we stand shoulder to shoulder in defense of our shared values of freedom, democracy and enterprise. From growth and security to innovation and tech, I know that the UK-US special relationship will continue to prosper on both sides of the Atlantic for years to come."[20] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] grouped Starmer with Emmanuel Macron and the Brussels institutions among the world leaders lining up to pay respects to the president-elect.[20]

## Recognition of Palestinian statehood (September 2025)

On a Sunday in late September 2025 Starmer announced that the United Kingdom, alongside Canada and Australia, recognized the state of Palestine, saying the move was intended "to revive the hope of peace for the Palestinians and Israelis."[21] In a video message posted on X he said, "We recognized the state of Israel more than 75 years ago as a homeland for the Jewish people. Today we join over 150 countries who recognize a Palestinian state also. A pledge to the Palestinian and Israeli people that there can be a better future."[21] He insisted the recognition was not a reward for [[hamas|Hamas]], which carried out the 7 October 2023 attack on Israel that killed some 1,200 people, mostly civilians, and led to 251 abductions.[21] Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] rejected the move, vowing that "a Palestinian state will not be established west of the Jordan River."[21]


## Response to the August 2024 riots

On Monday, August 5, 2024, Starmer said that a "standing army" of specialist police would be established to deal with rioting and that the justice system would be ramped up to handle hundreds of arrests, after violent disorder rocked cities across Britain over the preceding week.[22] He convened an urgent meeting over lawlessness he attributed to "far-right thuggery," driven in part by social-media misinformation that inflamed anger over a stabbing rampage at a dance class which killed three girls and wounded ten people; false rumours that the suspect was a Muslim asylum-seeker preceded attacks on immigrants and mosques.[22] "Whatever the apparent motivation, this is not protest. It is pure violence and we will not tolerate attacks on mosques or our Muslim communities," Starmer said, adding that "the full force of the law will be visited on all those who are identified as having taken part in these activities."[22] The statement followed a Sunday in which mobs attacked two hotels housing asylum-seekers and dozens of police officers were hospitalised over six days.[22] See [[southport-riots|the Southport riots]].[22]
