# Félix Tshisekedi

Félix Tshisekedi is the President of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]].[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] has argued that Tshisekedi's government has faced repeated regime-change attempts, both "hard" and "soft" coup efforts, the most recent involving the [[m23-rebels|M23]] group and its River Alliance Coalition.[1] In early January 2026, M23 forces moved south from Bukavu, took the city of [[uvira|Uvira]], and were reported to be expanding their territory and attacking Congolese military bases in the North Kivu region.[1]

In this analysis, the stakes in the Congo — described as "Conflict B: The War for Resources" — include an estimated $24 trillion in rare earth minerals used across high-technology manufacturing, much of the world's supply of which is concentrated in the country.[1]

## Minerals diplomacy with the West (February 2025)

In his first interview after a Rwanda-backed armed group seized swaths of Congolese territory in early 2025, Tshisekedi offered the United States and Europe a stake in his country's vast mineral wealth, a sector then dominated by China.[2] Congo is considered among the world's wealthiest countries in natural resources, supplying much of the world's coltan — used in smartphones and computers — and holding more than half of global cobalt reserves, used in electric vehicles.[2]

Tshisekedi likened the conflict in his country to Russia's invasion of Ukraine and pinned his hopes on Western pressure against [[rwanda|Rwanda]], calculating that major investments in Congo, including a possible minerals deal, would bring greater security and stability.[2] He characterised Rwandan behaviour as a "mania to be the apex predator."[2] At the time, China enjoyed far greater access to Congolese minerals than the United States, while the European Union had negotiated with Rwanda, agreeing to provide about $935 million in return for access to minerals including tin, tungsten and gold.[2]

Badlands commentary noted that Tshisekedi, who in 2018 replaced [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]] — the man who granted [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] his mining monopoly in the first place — had turned to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for help, offering the same mineral rights to America in exchange for assistance against Rwandan mercenaries.[2] The analysis held that Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] was acting on behalf of Tel Aviv, making the Rwandan force effectively Israeli-directed.[2] Separately, the law firm [[amsterdam-and-partners|Amsterdam & Partners]], acting for the DRC government, filed suit against [[apple|Apple]] over alleged laundering of smuggled Congolese minerals.[2]

## Minerals agreement talks with Washington (2025)

In April 2025 Tshisekedi's government was reported to be nearing a [[us-dr-congo-critical-minerals-deal|strategic critical minerals agreement]] with the United States that would open the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DR Congo]]'s lithium, copper and cobalt reserves to greater American private-sector investment in exchange for increased U.S. backing for his embattled government.[3] [[massad-boulos|Massad Boulos]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Africa adviser, confirmed that a "path forward" had been agreed with Tshisekedi during recent talks, in a video statement released by the Congolese government.[3]

## Commutation and transfer of the American coup convicts (2025)

In early April 2025 Tshisekedi commuted to life imprisonment the death sentences of three Americans — Marcel Malanga Malu, Tylor Thomson and Zalman-Polun Benjamin — convicted over the [[2024-dr-congo-coup-attempt|failed May 2024 coup attempt]] against his government.[4] The [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DR Congo]] presidency announced days later that the three had boarded a flight to the United States to serve the remainder of their sentences, describing the transfer as "part of a dynamic effort to strengthen judicial diplomacy and international cooperation in matters of justice and human rights between the two countries."[4] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the arrangement followed Tshisekedi's request for U.S. assistance with the crisis involving [[rwanda|Rwanda]], with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] trading help for custody of the convicted men.[4]

## Refusal to "auction" Congolese mineral wealth (2025)

In September 2025, while addressing reporters in New York during the [[united-nations-general-assembly|UN General Assembly]], Tshisekedi said his government would not "auction" the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]]'s mineral wealth to the United States as part of Washington's efforts to end the protracted violence in the country's eastern provinces.[5] His office said he also met with UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres to discuss the DRC peace process.[5]

Clashes between [[m23|M23]] fighters and government forces had escalated in the mineral-rich east since January 2025, with the rebels seizing key mining hubs including Goma, the capital of North Kivu, and Bukavu, the capital of South Kivu, reportedly leaving thousands dead.[5] In June, the DRC signed an agreement with [[rwanda|Rwanda]] — which Kinshasa accuses of arming the rebels, a charge Kigali denies — mediated by the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]; [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the deal, which calls for a joint security mechanism, also granted Washington rights to local mineral wealth, alongside a 90-day Rwandan troop withdrawal and disarmament and reintegration steps.[5] Tshisekedi said the agreement had failed to stop the clashes, though he thanked Trump for his efforts.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the key detail was the DRC's claim that Rwanda had increased rather than withdrawn M23 troops, and framed Rwanda as representing what he called the unipolar regime's interest in an estimated $24 trillion in mineral wealth — assets he said were taken from the Israeli Diamond Cartel via Trump's Executive Order 13818 in December 2017, frozen and later stripped by the Biden Justice Department in a British court.[5] He linked the M23 deployment from Rwanda in December to the failed May 2024 coup attempt by Congolese-American Christian Malanga and his son Marcel, describing the militia's seizure of the two major mining hubs as a move to control where raw ore is refined into a saleable commodity, smuggled into Rwanda and laundered with false certifications to conceal child-slave mining.[5]

## December 2025 White House summit

Tshisekedi joined Rwandan President Paul Kagame at the White House on December 4, 2025, where President Donald Trump brought the two leaders together to sign new deals aimed at stabilizing the war-scarred eastern Congo region and attracting Western mining investment.[6] Analysts said US diplomacy had paused escalation of the fighting but failed to resolve core issues, and days earlier the DRC military and M23 rebels had accused one another of violating the ceasefire.[6]

## Survival against successive destabilization attempts

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the June 2025 [[rwanda-drc-peace-agreement-2025|Rwanda–DR Congo peace agreement]] as the possible bookend of a long arc that began with Trump's [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] sanctioning [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] and continued through Tshisekedi's displacement of Gertler's business partner [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]].[7] In this account, Tshisekedi's government survived successive overthrow attempts — first by Gertler and former Mossad director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]], then by operative [[christian-malanga|Christian Malanga]], then by Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] through the [[m23|M23]] militia — all motivated by roughly $24 trillion in rare earth minerals.[7]

## Cohen visits and the May 2024 coup attempt

Haaretz reported that Tshisekedi was aware of then-[[mossad]] director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]]'s surprise 2019 visits to the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DRC]], none of which were coordinated with his government, and that Cohen had been meeting Tshisekedi's predecessor [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]]; some of Tshisekedi's aides feared Cohen was helping Kabila acquire arms for a coup attempt.[8] On Cohen's second visit, dated October 10, 2019, Tshisekedi flew from Goma to Kinshasa in his presidential plane, and another aircraft in his entourage took off shortly afterward and crashed with no survivors.[8] When Cohen returned for a third secret trip, DRC authorities met his private plane on the tarmac and brought him in to meet Tshisekedi.[8] In May 2024 Congolese forces thwarted an attempted coup against Tshisekedi's government led by [[christian-malanga|Christian Malanga]], which Badlands commentary linked back to this history.[8]
