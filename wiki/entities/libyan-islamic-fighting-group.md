# Libyan Islamic Fighting Group (LIFG)

The Libyan Islamic Fighting Group was the main insurgent outfit that fought the government of [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] and, in 2011, formed the core of the Benghazi-based rebellion that toppled him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Its founder and emir was [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Formation and record

LIFG announced its formation in 1995 among Libyans who had fought Soviet forces in Afghanistan and the Gaddafi regime at home.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It is believed to have participated in planning the May 2003 Casablanca suicide bombings and has been linked to the 2004 Madrid attacks.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) From the late 1990s many LIFG members fled Libya for other countries, particularly the United Kingdom — which the article attributes not to Gaddafi sponsoring terrorism but to Gaddafi waging war on and arresting these terrorists inside his own country, contrary to his media portrayal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Terrorist designations

LIFG was named a Specially Designated Global Terrorist in the annex to Executive Order 13224 on 23 September 2001, and added to the UN 1267 Committee Consolidated List of individuals and entities associated with Al-Qaeda or the Taliban on 6 October 2001.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) In early November 2007 Ayman al-Zawahiri announced that LIFG had become an official affiliate of [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) On 30 October 2008 the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]] designated three LIFG members under E.O. 13224, with OFAC director Adam Szubin stating that "LIFG, along with other al Qaida affiliates, seeks to exploit our globalized world, raising funds in Europe for transfer to terror cells operating in North Africa."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The [[state-department|State Department]] likewise designated LIFG as Al-Qaeda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The group maintained two training camps in Afghanistan at the time of the 9/11 attack.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Libya as a source of fighters

Research by the Combating Terrorism Center at West Point cited in the article found the LIFG had officially joined Al-Qaeda in 2007 and that Libya supplied the most foreign fighters in Iraq relative to population size, with LIFG training fighters in Libya to fight American soldiers in Iraq.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Libya thereby became, in the article's framing, the new "safe haven" training ground for the enemy after Afghanistan.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## 2011 and after

Despite these designations, [[john-mccain|Senator John McCain]] travelled to [[benghazi|Benghazi]] in April 2011 to meet LIFG leaders, described them as "brave fighters" who "are not Al-Qaeda," and pressed the U.S. government to arm, train and fund them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article characterises the goal of that visit as making "kosher this band of hardline Islamists" in the eyes of the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration as it weighed intervention.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)
