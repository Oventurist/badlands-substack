# Libyan Islamic Fighting Group

The Libyan Islamic Fighting Group (LIFG) was the main insurgent outfit battling the government of [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] during the 2011 Libyan uprising, and the group whose leaders [[john-mccain|John McCain]] traveled to [[benghazi|Benghazi]] to meet.[1] Its emir and founder was [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]].[1]

## Formation and designations

According to a [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]] press release, the LIFG officially announced its formation in 1995 among Libyans who had fought Soviet forces in Afghanistan and the Gaddafi regime in Libya.[1] It is believed to have participated in planning the May 2003 Casablanca suicide bombings and has been linked to the 2004 Madrid attacks.[1] The LIFG was named a Specially Designated Global Terrorist in the annex to Executive Order 13224 on 23 September 2001 and added to the UN 1267 Committee Consolidated List of individuals and entities associated with al-Qaida or the Taliban on 6 October 2001.[1] In early November 2007 Ayman al-Zawahiri announced that the LIFG had become an official affiliate of [[al-qaeda|al-Qaida]].[1] On 30 October 2008 Treasury designated three LIFG members under E.O. 13224, with OFAC director Adam Szubin stating that "LIFG, along with other al Qaida affiliates, seeks to exploit our globalized world, raising funds in Europe for transfer to terror cells operating in North Africa."[1] The [[us-state-department|State Department]] likewise designated the group as al-Qaeda.[1]

## Military assessment

The Combating Terrorism Center at West Point, studying which groups and countries were supplying fighters in Iraq, found that the LIFG had officially joined al-Qaeda in 2007 and was sending large numbers of fighters to kill American soldiers in Iraq — with Libya supplying the most foreign terrorists in Iraq relative to its population.[1] The article argues Libya had become the new "safe haven" training ground for the enemy after the loss of Afghan camps, and that the LIFG maintained two training camps in Afghanistan at the time of 9/11.[1]

## Flight from Libya

Treasury noted that since the late 1990s many LIFG members had fled Libya for various countries, particularly the United Kingdom.[1] The article reads this as evidence against the media portrayal of Gaddafi as a sponsor of terrorism: members were fleeing because Gaddafi was waging war on and arresting them inside his own country.[1]
