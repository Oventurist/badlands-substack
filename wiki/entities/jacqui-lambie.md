# Jacqui Lambie

Jacqui Lambie is an independent Australian Senator representing Tasmania who drew international attention in April 2024 for calling for [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to be imprisoned over his refusal to comply with Australian government demands to remove video of a violent attack on a Christian bishop in Sydney.[1]

## The Musk censorship dispute

In comments to Sky News, Lambie charged that Musk was "creating hatred" by ignoring official requests to remove what the Australian government classified as harmful content.[1] She described the X owner in derisive personal terms and said that "someone like that should be in jail and the key be thrown away," arguing that he should not be permitted to operate what she characterized as his own ideology platform and expose such material to children.[1]

Lambie further announced that she would personally boycott X, saying she would deactivate her use of the platform that same day, and urged the other members of the Australian Parliament — she cited a body of 227 members — to do the same in order to show Musk that they meant business.[1]

## Commentary and significance

Badlands commentary framed the episode as having little to do with X, Musk personally, hate speech, or disinformation, and everything to do with control over the governing narrative.[1] The argument advanced was that the Senator's call for speech bans followed from a collapse in public belief in her government's central narrative, and that trust in government was moving toward becoming effectively mandatory rather than earned — in Australia and elsewhere.[1] Commentators noted the boldness of openly calling to criminalize speech critical of government, and questioned whether populations had become sufficiently domesticated that leaders could propose removing rights without political consequence.[1]

The dispute placed Lambie within a broader pattern of Western governments pressing global social media platforms for content removal, a pattern Badlands writers treat as a central front of the information war.[1]
