# Kim Jong Il

Kim Jong Il was the leader of [[north-korea|North Korea]] from 1994 until his death in 2011. He appears in the Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 2 as the host and, according to the article, the instigator of [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]]'s secret 2009 mission to Pyongyang.

The article states that Clinton traveled to North Korea at Kim Jong Il's request to negotiate the release of two American journalists sentenced to twelve years of hard labor for spying, and asks why the North Korean leader specifically requested Clinton and why [[doug-band|Doug Band]] accompanied him. In the photograph the author identifies as Q drop 227 (November 24, 2017), Kim Jong Il is seated with Clinton, with Band standing behind the North Korean leader and [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] standing behind Clinton.

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the meeting as more than a humanitarian negotiation, arguing that hostage releases have functioned historically as cover for other transactions and promising to explain the significance in a subsequent article.

## Relationship with Bill Clinton

America for Sale — Part 3 argues that Kim Jong Il specifically requested [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] as the negotiator for the 2009 release of two detained American journalists because the two already had a working relationship dating to the 1994 [[agreed-framework|Agreed Framework]]. A memo attached to a hacked email published by [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] and reported by [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed News]] records that Kim "asked that President Clinton return often, officially and unofficially," suggesting a future visit "to tour someday when there were no problems in bilateral relations," and offered to show Clinton "a much more beautiful place" than the seaside scene in the Paekhwanwon Guest House painting. The article treats the tone of the exchange — old friends rather than adversaries — as proof that the hostage negotiation was a cover story.
