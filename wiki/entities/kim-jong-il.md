# Kim Jong Il

Kim Jong Il was the leader of [[north-korea|North Korea]] at the time of the 2009 secret mission by former president [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] to secure the release of two detained American journalists.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## The 2009 meeting

The article states that Bill Clinton undertook the mission at Kim Jong Il's specific request, and asks why the North Korean leader would want Clinton in particular to negotiate the hostage release, and why [[doug-band|Doug Band]] accompanied him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) A photograph from the meeting — posted by [[qanon|Q]] on November 24, 2017 as a drop containing no text at all — shows Clinton with Kim Jong Il, [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] standing behind Clinton and Band standing behind the North Korean leader.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

The author treats the wordless drop as an instruction to examine the episode, arguing that hostage negotiations have historically served as cover for other transactions and hinting that the North Korea trip connects to leadership changes in several countries after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Friendly relations revealed by the Podesta memo

Part 3 of the series draws on a Buzzfeed report describing a memo attached to a hacked email sent to John Podesta, who accompanied Clinton on the mission and whose account was later published by [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) According to the memo, the 2009 visit went so well that Kim Jong Il "asked that President Clinton return often, officially and unofficially," suggesting he come back "to tour someday when there were no problems in bilateral relations."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Clinton responded that he would like to visit the seaside location depicted in a painting in the entry hall of the Paekhwanwon Guest House, to which Kim replied that he would show him a much more beautiful place and that Clinton should return to the DPRK on holiday.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The article argues that this exchange, never meant to be public, shows the two men behaving as old friends rather than adversaries in tense hostage talks, and that the hostages were merely a cover story.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## The 1994 working relationship

The author answers his own question about why Kim wanted Clinton specifically by pointing to their earlier dealings: the 1994 [[agreed-framework|Agreed Framework]], announced by Clinton on October 18, 1994, under which North Korea was to freeze its nuclear program in exchange for American oil and two "light fuel" plants.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) North Korea broke the agreement almost immediately.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) In the article's reading, the oil supplied under the deal freed North Korean cash for weapons work, and the arrangement was always about oil and money rather than nonproliferation — with [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]], who had traded with Kim's father Kim Il Sung, positioned as a likely broker of Iranian oil bound for Pyongyang.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
