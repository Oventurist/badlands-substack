# Rodney King

Rodney King was the man beaten by [[lapd|LAPD]] officers in 1991 in an incident captured on camcorder, an event Erik Carlson uses as the opening case study in his essay on [[partial-truths|partial truths]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## The 1991 beating

Carlson recounts his own initial reaction as appallment at the violence, cruelty and apparent racism of officers who had sworn an oath to protect and serve and who appeared to be trying to kill a man. He allowed that the officers may have been driven by job pressures and that King was intoxicated, speeding and resisting arrest, but concluded on the basis of their conduct that they should no longer be police officers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Revised reading

On later reflection, Carlson says he never asked who was recording the beating or why that person happened to have a camcorder ready — a question sharpened by the fact that the incident predates cell-phone cameras. He finds it odd in hindsight that the famous footage was captured so perfectly from an apartment balcony at night, calling it not impossible but unlikely that the situation occurred as organically as portrayed, and ultimately describing the recording as staged.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Aftermath

A year later, four officers tried for the beating were acquitted by a jury, sparking the [[la-riots-1992|1992 Los Angeles riots]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) Carlson places the King beating within an alleged roughly thirty-year cycle of [[manufactured-race-riots|manufactured race riots]] running from Harlem in 1935 through Watts in 1965 to the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] incident in 2020.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)
