# Rodney King

Rodney King was the Black motorist beaten by [[lapd|Los Angeles Police Department]] officers in 1991 in an incident captured on camcorder, which became one of the defining American racial flashpoints of the decade.[1]

## The 1991 beating

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] recalls being appalled by the footage at the time, questioning how officers sworn to protect and serve could appear to be trying to kill a man.[1] He acknowledged mitigating context — that King was intoxicated, speeding, and resisting arrest, and that officers face job pressures — while concluding that the officers involved should not have remained police.[1]

## Questions raised about the footage

Carlson notes that he never asked at the time who was recording the beating or why that person had a camcorder ready, emphasizing that the incident predated cell-phone cameras.[1] He argues in hindsight that it is odd that the famous footage was captured so perfectly at night from an apartment balcony, calling it not impossible but unlikely that the situation occurred as organically as portrayed, and elsewhere in the essay refers to it as what he now believes to be a staged recording.[1]

## Aftermath

A year later, four officers tried for the beating were acquitted by a jury, sparking the [[la-riots-1992|1992 Los Angeles riots]].[1] Carlson places the King episode within an alleged roughly thirty-year cycle of engineered racial unrest running from the [[harlem-riots-1935|Harlem riots of 1935]] through the [[watts-riots-1965|Watts riots]] to the 2020 [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] incident.[1]
