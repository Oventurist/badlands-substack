# United States Space Force

The US Space Force is the branch of the American armed forces responsible for military space operations, including satellite communications architecture and space acquisitions.[1]

## Space Data Network Backbone award

In May 2026 the Space Force awarded [[spacex|SpaceX]] a $2.29 billion contract to rapidly build a new satellite communications network intended to securely move military data around the world at high speeds, creating a critical "backbone" for future US military operations.[1] According to the service, the program — the [[space-data-network-backbone|Space Data Network Backbone]] — will use a constellation of low-Earth orbit satellites linked by optical, laser-based communications, allowing US forces to rapidly and securely share targeting information, sensor data, battlefield communications, and other intelligence globally, even in contested environments.[1]

The award requires delivery of a fully operational prototype by the end of 2027 and is to be coordinated with the [[space-development-agency|Space Development Agency]]'s Transport Layer program, with both networks expected to operate together within a larger War Department communications architecture.[1] Space Force officials described the project as part of a broader effort to accelerate military space acquisition through rapid prototyping and commercial partnerships, while expanding the industrial base supporting US national security space programs.[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the award as further evidence of the contract-level alliance between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], with military space infrastructure serving as load-bearing structure for a claimed transition toward distributed capability and abundance.[1]

## Burt Pride remarks controversy (June 2023)

In June 2023 Space Force Chief Operating Officer Lt. Gen. [[deanna-burt|DeAnna Burt]] spoke at a Pride event at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], where she criticized more than 400 state regulations she characterized as "anti-LGBTQ+" and argued they threatened US military readiness and the wellbeing of individual soldiers.[2] Burt suggested such laws would distract service members from their duties by preventing them from being their "true selves," and indicated the threat extended to soldiers' transgender children who might be denied gender-affirming healthcare.[2] The remarks were widely criticized in alternative media; Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the episode to the promotion of [[rachel-levine|Rachel Levine]] and argued that the armed forces were catering to a fringe while mentally sound fighting-age men and women chose other paths.[2]


## Muon Space weather satellite contract (October 2025)

In October 2025 the Space Force announced a contract with commercial weather satellite operator [[muon-space|Muon Space]] for three prototype spacecraft to provide data such as cloud forecasting for terrestrial and maritime operations.[4] The $44.6 million Phase III Small Business Innovation Research award followed a December 2024 contract under which Muon further developed an electro-optical/infrared sensor to "provide comprehensive cloud characterization and theater weather imagery."[4] Space Systems Command said the contract "leverages the company's existing commercial mission and builds upon the successful outcomes of its previous FireSat prototype launch," referring to the company's March 2025 launch of the first prototype of a planned 50-satellite wildfire-monitoring constellation developed with the Earth Fire Alliance.[4]

## MILNET partnership with SpaceX (2025)
In June 2025 the Space Force was reported to be partnering with [[spacex|SpaceX]] to develop [[milnet|MILNET]], a government-owned, contractor-operated satellite communications constellation in low Earth orbit, according to Delta 8 Commander Col. Jeff Weisler.[3] MILNET, transitioning into Space Force operations via Space Systems Command and Delta 8, will consist of over 480 SpaceX-operated satellites using [[starshield|Starshield]] terminals with enhanced encryption and interoperability with the commercial Starlink network, and will support the military's emerging "hybrid mesh network" integrating commercial and Defense Department satellites.[3] A Delta 8 mission director is to oversee operations to align with wartime tempo; Delta 8, based at Schriever Space Force Base in Colorado, manages satellite communications for senior U.S. leadership and global military forces.[3]
