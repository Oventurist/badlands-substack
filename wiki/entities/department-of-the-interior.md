# Department of the Interior

The United States Department of the Interior is the federal executive department responsible for the management of federal lands and natural resources, and the parent agency of the [[national-park-service|National Park Service]].[1]

## 2026 Washington restoration work

In May 2026 the Interior Department publicized the results of the Park Service's fountain repair program in Washington, D.C. The station WJLA reported on May 18 that the department had released video showing water running at the Cascading Fountain in [[meridian-hill-park|Meridian Hill Park]], also known as Malcolm X Park, describing the landmark as a 13-basin fountain and one of the longest in North America.[1] The fountain and its reflecting pool had reopened to visitors on May 14, 2026 after years of work at the lower plaza.[1]

The department's promotion of the reopening formed part of a broader effort by the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to restore the appearance of the national capital, alongside [[executive-order-14344|Executive Order 14344]], "Making Federal Architecture Beautiful Again."[1]

## ANWR lease cancellation (2023)

In September 2023 the Interior Department, under Secretary [[deb-haaland|Deb Haaland]], confirmed the cancellation of seven oil and gas leases in the [[arctic-national-wildlife-refuge|Arctic National Wildlife Refuge]] that had been awarded from a 2021 sale.[2] Haaland told reporters that President [[joe-biden|Biden]] had directed the department on day one of the administration to review leases sold in the refuge by his predecessor, and that its analysis found the sale itself "seriously flawed and based on a number of fundamental legal deficiencies."[2] The department simultaneously proposed a rule protecting 13 million acres in a different part of Alaska.[2] Critics characterized the action as illegal and reckless, framing it as NIMBYism — or something more nefarious — colliding with national security.[2]
