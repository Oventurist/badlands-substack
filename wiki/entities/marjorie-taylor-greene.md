# Marjorie Taylor Greene

**Marjorie Taylor Greene** is a Republican member of the United States [[congress|Congress]] from Georgia, associated with the [[america-first-movement|America First]] wing of the party.[1]

## Opposition to war with Iran

The March 10, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] recorded that Greene publicly opposed war with [[iran|Iran]] and was attacked for that position by Congressman [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]], who claimed Iran was planning to detonate a nuclear weapon in the United States and accused Greene of "wanting to see America get nuked" because of her anti-war stance.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the exchange as an example of what he described as the derangement of pro-war legislators once "the mask drops," arguing that the escalatory rhetoric aimed at Greene reflected the accusers' own dispositions rather than any assessment of the threat.[1] In this framing Greene's dissent placed her with the MAGA base's core demand for restraint in the Middle East, against the Christian Dispensationalist and neoconservative bloc pushing for strikes.[1]

## Retirement announcement (November 2025)

In November 2025 Greene surprised Washington by announcing that she would retire from [[congress|Congress]] within 42 days, claiming that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and House Republicans had abandoned America First priorities.[2] ZeroHedge, citing Punchbowl News, reported that other GOP members were considering following her out the door, with one disaffected Republican saying "this entire White House team has treated ALL members like garbage" and that Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] "has let it happen because he wanted it to happen."[2] The same member predicted more explosive early resignations, described morale as never lower — "It's a tinder box" — and forecast that Johnson would be stripped of his gavel and the party would lose its majority before the term was out.[2]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded that too many self-described truthers assume Trump's promise to return power to "you, THE PEOPLE" meant routing it through unelected bureaucrats, urging readers to take him at his word and asking whether the threatened resignations were "threats ... or promises?"[2]

In a stunning announcement posted to Twitter/X on a Friday night in November 2025, Greene said she would resign her House seat on January 5, 2026.[18] The announcement came after she publicly skirmished with Trump over the release of the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] files; Trump responded by calling her a "traitor," a "lunatic," and "wacky," saying he would refuse to take her phone calls and would consider supporting a primary challenger against her.[18] In her announcement Greene decried those attacks, writing that "Loyalty should be a two-way street," and said she was being punished by Trump and the larger Republican Party for demanding a full release of the Epstein files: "Standing up for American women who were raped at 14, trafficked and used by rich powerful men, should not result in me being called a traitor and threatened by the President of the United States, whom I fought for."[18] She added that she refused to be "a 'battered wife' hoping it all goes away and gets better."[18] The resignation was reported by [[mother-jones|Mother Jones]].[18]

Badlands host [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded that asking "wait a minute, is Trump actually doing the thing that we sent him there to do?" is "absolutely the right mentality to always have no matter who it is you voted for," while affirming trust in Trump and acknowledging that he plays rhetorical games.[18] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Trump's 2017 inauguration speech — with its promise of transferring power from Washington, D.C. back to the American people — was the strategy paper for saving the country, and that of all the legislators under observation, Greene was modeling that strategy the best.[18] Talking to activists on the ground in Georgia, she wrote, Greene will be missed, and it remained unclear what her constituents would gain from "this latest episode of 'the plan.'"[18] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] observed that the news cycle was simultaneously telling audiences that Trump had a better relationship with a communist — incoming New York City Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] — than with Greene, and asked what she had actually done to deserve the "traitor" label beyond Trump applying it.[18]

## Motion to vacate against Mike Johnson (2024)

Greene's threatened motion to vacate the chair against Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-La.) struggled from the outset for want of Republican support, with only two right-wing House Republicans signing on — enough to remove Johnson only if virtually every Democrat voted with them.[3] On April 30, 2024, House Democratic leadership confirmed it would move to table the motion, which Axios described as potentially the nail in the coffin for the effort.[3] Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] and his deputies stated flatly, "If she invokes the motion, it will not succeed."[3]

## Motion to vacate against Speaker Johnson (May 2024)

On 8 May 2024 Greene forced a vote on a [[may-2024-motion-to-vacate-against-speaker-mike-johnson|motion to vacate]] seeking to strip [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] of the Speakership, having met with him twice that week to air her grievances and demands.[4] House Majority Whip [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] moved to table the resolution, and Democrats joined Republicans to shelve it 359 to 43, with only 11 Republicans voting to advance the ouster.[4] Speaking on the House floor during what was intended to be the final vote of the week, Greene delivered a litany of complaints against Johnson and drew loud booing from members present when she brought the resolution forward.[4] She was accompanied by [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]], one of just two Republicans who had openly backed the effort, while Johnson had denounced the attempt as a "dangerous gambit."[4]

## Call for a Speaker Trump

In October 2023, following the [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy|removal of Speaker Kevin McCarthy]], Greene was among several conservatives who publicly called on former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to serve as Speaker of the House, presenting the office as one way of helping him become president again.[5] Trump responded from a New York courthouse on October 4 that he had been approached about the role and would be willing to take it on, saying he would do "whatever's best for the country and for the Republican Party."[5]

## Call to prosecute Jamaal Bowman (2023)

After Representative [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] (D-NY) was recorded pulling a fire alarm in the Cannon House Office Building on September 30, 2023, during Republican efforts to pass a stopgap spending bill, Greene publicly demanded that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] prosecute him under the same obstruction-of-an-official-proceeding statute applied to January 6 defendants.[6] Writing on X, she said Bowman "interrupted the official proceedings of the House as Republicans worked to keep the government open" and that she was "calling on the DOJ to prosecute him using the same law they used to prosecute J6 defendants."[6] She also charged that "the Democrats literally will do anything to shut OUR government down because billions aren't going to their favorite country: Ukraine."[6]

## Hunter Biden contempt hearing (January 2024)

During the January 10, 2024 [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] hearing that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] attended by surprise, he fled the room as Greene began to speak, an exit ZeroHedge described as hilarious.[8] The hearing followed his refusal to sit for a closed-door deposition tied to the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and also featured Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] excoriating him as "the epitome of white privilege."[8]

## Account of the Hunter Biden deposition (February 2024)

Following [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s deposition on Wednesday, February 28, 2024, Greene told Breitbart News exclusively that Hunter Biden did not deny that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] is the "big guy."[9] The Bidens had previously neither confirmed nor denied that Joe Biden was the "big guy" designated to receive ten percent of a deal with a Chinese Communist Party entity.[9]

"At one point, we asked Hunter about the ten percent for the 'big guy'," Greene said. "We showed him the email that that it laid it all out."[9] According to her account of the deposition, Hunter replied, "Oh, that was after my father left office," and then sought to excuse the ten percent stake for his father by asking, "What's wrong with having a pie-in-the-sky idea? When he [Joe Biden] left office in 2017, it thought he was done. I had no idea was gonna run for president. What's wrong with just some pie?" — thinking that Joe Biden could be in the business.[9] Greene explained that while Hunter did not deny that Joe Biden was the "big guy," he did deny that the business plan called for a ten percent cut.[9]

## Reaction to the Jack Smith target letter

Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene called the anticipated January 6 charges against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "ridiculous" and accused Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] of "weaponizing" the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[10] She said that if this was the direction America was heading, "we are worse than Russia, we are worse than China. We are worse than some of the most corrupt third world countries, and this needs to end," adding that the case was "an absolute lie."[10] She also referred to Smith on Twitter as a "weak little bitch."[10]

## Hunter Biden laptop display in committee (2023)

[[james-comer|James Comer]] credited Greene with presenting evidence during a House committee session that he said demonstrated a violation of the [[mann-act|Mann Act]] by [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], including a sexually explicit image printed on a poster from the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]], plane tickets and payment records allegedly showing that a prostitute had been paid with company funds.[11] Comer said the display rebutted Democratic claims that no evidence existed.[11]

## Criticism of Speaker Johnson (December 2025)

In an interview with The New York Times published on December 29, 2025, Greene argued that Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] was "100 percent" following orders from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and thereby relinquishing the power of [[congress|Congress]].[12] "I want you to know that Johnson is not our Speaker. He is not our leader," she said, adding that "in the legislative branch — a totally separate body of government — he is literally 100 percent under direct orders from the [[white-house|White House]]. And many, many Republicans are so furious about that, but they're cowards."[12] The remarks came as she prepared to resign from Congress effective January 5, 2026, and amid broader complaints from House members that the chamber was ceding too much sway to the executive branch.[12]

## Georgia 2020 election investigation request (2025)

In September 2025, Greene sent an official request to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] seeking an investigation into the 2020 presidential election in Georgia.[13] The formal request was addressed to Marshall Yates, the FBI's Assistant Director in the Office of Congressional Affairs, and Dario Camacho, the Supervisory Official of the Office of Legislative Affairs, and called for an investigation specifically into the Georgia Secretary of State's Office and [[fulton-county|Fulton County]].[13]

The request cited [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]] complaint 2023-025, which the board had in July 2025 made the subject of a resolution asking for an investigation into "outstanding allegations."[13] That complaint was the product of extensive research and investigation conducted by [[kevin-moncla|Kevin Moncla]] of the Election Oversight Group and [[joe-rossi|Joseph Rossi]].[13] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[canncon|CannCon]], who had covered the underlying work extensively, described the referral as enormously important, writing that "it's yuge."[13]

## ChatGPT refusal episode, February 2023

Greene featured in a February 2023 controversy over political bias in [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]], after tech writer Andy Meek found that the model declined to write a poem about her, replying that she was "a controversial figure" and that it was not appropriate for an AI language model "to take a partisan stance or express opinions."[14] Summit News noted that the same model readily produced an effusive poem praising [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], whom it characterized as no less controversial.[14]

## Chair of the DOGE subcommittee (November 2024)

In November 2024, sources told Fox News Digital that Greene would chair the Delivering on Government Efficiency Subcommittee, a new [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] panel created by Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] to work with the newly formed Department of Government Efficiency ([[doge|DOGE]]) led by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]].[15] The subcommittee was expected to focus on rooting out waste, fraud and abuse in the federal government, investigating wasteful spending, examining ways to reorganize federal agencies to improve efficiency, and identifying solutions to eliminate bureaucratic red tape.[15] Comer met with Ramaswamy and his incoming DOGE team ahead of the announcement, and Ramaswamy confirmed the meeting with Comer and Greene.[15]

## IRS whistleblower hearing (July 2023)

During the July 19, 2023 [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] hearing with IRS whistleblowers [[gary-shapley|Gary Shapley]] and [[joseph-ziegler|Joseph Ziegler]], Greene produced one of the proceeding's most dramatic moments by walking through salacious allegations concerning [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s personal conduct.[16] Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] grouped her with other America First Republicans whose public statements, he argued, would shape voter perceptions heading into the 2024 general election.[16]

## Atlanta rally on Ukraine aid (October 2024)

Speaking at a campaign rally in Atlanta on Wednesday, October 16, 2024, Greene said Americans were furious with the administration for sending billions of dollars to Ukraine while treating its own citizens "worse than dirt."[17] She contrasted the response of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] to domestic disasters with their eagerness to fund Kiev, naming Lahaina, East Palestine, and western North Carolina — the sites of catastrophic wildfires, a toxic train derailment, and flooding caused by Hurricane Helene.[17]

"There is anger in this country that is unspeakable, over how the Biden-Harris administration has thrown away these Americans as if their lives do not matter. As if their tax dollars never mattered. And treated them so that they're only worth 750 measly bucks," she said, adding, "Oh, but Congress will write that check for $60 billion over and over again for Ukraine. Won't they?"[17] U.S. lawmakers had approved almost $180 billion in Ukraine aid since 2022, and Greene has repeatedly voted against those spending packages.[17]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded that only a handful of members of [[congress|Congress]] — naming [[eli-crane|Eli Crane]] among them — were worth anything, and that even figures like [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] underestimated the depth of public contempt for Washington's permanent class.[17] He described that contempt as a "gravity well" of unseen but felt centrifugal force capable of annihilating the capital's cherished institutions, and argued the accumulated disasters of the Biden years had only deepened it.[17]

## Trump endorsement withdrawal and America First feud (November 2025)

In mid-November 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] abruptly withdrew his endorsement of Greene, calling the Georgia Republican "wacky" and a "ranting lunatic" in the post distancing himself from her.[19] The feud escalated publicly, with Trump doubling down by calling her Marjorie "Traitor" Greene, while Greene suggested the president was fostering an environment in which she might face death threats.[19] The longtime allies had diverged over U.S. support for [[israel|Israel]] during the war in [[gaza|Gaza]] — Greene called for resources to be focused on improving conditions at home — and over release of the [[epstein-files|Epstein files]], which she strongly favored while Trump called the matter a "Democrat hoax" meant to distract from his administration's achievements.[19] Throughout her criticisms of the administration, Greene maintained that she is "America First, America Only," and coverage tied the showdown to a broader "Move Over MAGA" hat trend and a push around [[nick-fuentes|Nick Fuentes]].[19]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded that "MAGA is America first" and that the psyop is trying to convince the public otherwise.[19] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the "MAGA is dead" propaganda was designed to encourage "unity" between [[maga-coalition|MAGA]] and the social identity groups that had attached themselves to it — warmongers, Zionists, a controlled-opposition "Nazi" operation, tech figures, and [[make-america-healthy-again|MAHA]] — none of whom MAGA needs while all of them need MAGA.[19] In his reading the episode is not MAGA dying but MAGA shedding dead weight, with most adherents remaining once the manufactured identity groups are sifted out; the impostors, he wrote, are easy to identify because "they're always desperate for allies and demanding unity."[19]

## Blackmail claim against Speaker Johnson (April 2024)

In April 2024 Greene introduced speculation that Speaker of the House [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] was being "blackmailed" because, she said, his views had changed so much since taking the gavel.[20] Speaking to [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] on his show Tucker Carlson Uncensored, the Georgia Republican — by then a vocal Johnson critic — said the Louisiana Republican "has completely changed his character in a matter of about five months after he has become speaker of the House."[20] Newsweek noted that no evidence had been produced to substantiate the claim and that it had contacted Johnson's office for comment.[20]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] observed that mileage varies within the [[truth-community]] over how much stock to place in the bombast of self-described America First representatives such as Greene and [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]], and questioned why patriots would want to be "in control" of a runaway train during the worst stagflationary spiral in half a century.[20]


## House Oversight Committee appointment (2023)

In January 2023 the [[white-house|White House]] denounced the appointment of Greene and Rep. [[paul-gosar|Paul Gosar]] to the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]], with spokesperson Ian Sams suggesting the assignments showed House Republicans were setting the stage for "divorced-from-reality political stunts" rather than bipartisan work, and demanding that GOP leaders reveal what secret deals were made with "extreme MAGA members" to elect a Speaker.[21] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] countered that the committee was stacked with America First firebrands, some previously stripped of assignments by the prior Congress, and would investigate legitimate concerns including fentanyl crossing the border, the Afghanistan withdrawal, COVID-19 origins and Biden family influence peddling.[21]

## Clash at the Garland contempt hearing, May 2024

During the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]]'s May 16, 2024 contempt hearing for Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]], Greene derailed proceedings by attacking Rep. [[jasmine-crockett|Jasmine Crockett]] of Texas, telling her "I think your fake eyelashes are messing up what you're reading" after Crockett asked, "Do you know what we're here for?"[22] The exchange escalated into a fiery back-and-forth with Rep. [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], who asked Greene whether her feelings were hurt and referred to her as "baby girl."[22]

## Rejection of the MAGA label (December 2025)

In a "60 Minutes" interview aired on a Sunday in December 2025, Greene — once a staunch supporter of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s MAGA agenda — suggested she no longer associated herself with that label.[23] Asked by anchor [[lesley-stahl|Lesley Stahl]], "Are you MAGA?", Greene replied, "I'm America first."[23] Pressed on whether that was different from MAGA, she answered that "MAGA is President Trump's phrase. That's his, his political policies," referring to the "Make America Great Again" slogan that propelled Trump's 2016 campaign to victory, adding, "I call myself America first."[23] When Stahl said, "But you're not saying you're MAGA. That's over," Greene shook her head and repeated, "I'm America first. Yep."[23]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode as part of "nearly a year of engineered intra-MAGA turmoil," arguing that the establishment was "spinning out in a void of their own making" because it was beginning to realize the movement Trump began was not ending but "being reborn," and that "the sifting is the signal."[23]
