# Robert Livingston

Robert Livingston (1742–1813) was an American statesman, the first Secretary of Foreign Affairs of the United States from 1781 to 1783, and US ambassador to France during [[thomas-jefferson|Thomas Jefferson]]'s first term.[1] In Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] he is described as the dominant force behind the Federalist Party once [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] was removed from the picture in 1804, and as a principal sponsor of [[john-trumbull|John Trumbull]]'s [[american-academy-of-fine-arts|American Academy of Fine Arts]].[1]

## Alleged subversion

Although celebrated as a patriot, Livingston is alleged by Ehret to have taken part in at least three attempts to destroy the young republic, in 1800, 1804 and 1807, led by the former vice-president [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]], who in 1799 had simultaneously established a financial control grid within the United States — dubbed "Wall Street" — described as a branch of the City of London inside America.[1] Ehret situates this pro-British, Anglophile movement of aristocrats as the basis of America's deep state, typically operating in opposition to the slaveholding southern party then called the Democratic Party led by Jefferson, though he argues in *The Clash of the Two Americas* that both parties functioned as a controlled gang-counter-gang operation serving British intelligence.[1] A further clue offered is that Livingston had been one of the leading assistants to General Benedict Arnold, who betrayed the revolution and defected to the British Empire in 1778.[1]

## Patronage of the arts

Livingston took a keen interest in the arts, and the article treats his sponsorship of Trumbull's academy as the explanation for why no revolutionary school of aesthetics developed in the young republic.[1] With Trumbull he presided over an institution that the article says used art for speculation and money laundering rather than creative development; after [[samuel-morse|Samuel F. B. Morse]] defeated Trumbull in public debate, the Trumbull–Livingston academy disintegrated.[1]
