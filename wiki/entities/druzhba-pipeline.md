# Druzhba Pipeline

The Druzhba pipeline is a major oil transport system carrying Russian and Central Asian crude westward into Central Europe. It is divided into a southern branch, which supplies [[hungary|Hungary]] and Slovakia, and a northern branch, which serves [[germany|Germany]] and Poland. The April 24, 2026 Badlands Brief treats the pipeline as the central node of what its authors call a European pincer.

## Southern branch repairs and the Hungarian election

According to the source, [[ukraine|Ukraine]] announced that it had completed repairs to the southern branch, damaged by a Russian strike in January, and was ready to resume pumping Russian oil to Hungary and Slovakia. The announcement came just days after [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]]'s landslide election victory over [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]. President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] confirmed the pipeline could resume operation, and the timing was expected to unlock a long-delayed €90 billion [[european-union|European Union]] aid package to Ukraine that Orbán had vetoed. Magyar stated that his government would not block the loan but would refuse to contribute to it, would not support Ukraine's EU membership, would reject the EU Migration Pact, and would strengthen Hungary's borders.

## Northern branch suspension

Separately, Russia announced that it would halt Kazakh crude oil shipments to Germany through the northern branch starting May 1, citing technical issues. The move affects the PCK Schwedt refinery, which supplies most of the fuel, jet fuel, and heating oil consumed in Berlin, where Kazakh oil had covered roughly 17% of needs, with volumes reaching nearly 60,000 barrels per day in the first quarter of 2026. Germany's Economy Ministry stated that the suspension does not jeopardize overall petroleum product supply security, since northern pipeline sections and increased deliveries via Poland's Gdansk port can cover shortfalls even if Schwedt operates at reduced capacity.

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

[[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reads the sequence as a tug-of-war between competing power centers: Ukraine's sudden completion of repairs immediately after Magyar's victory, followed by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s announcement of technical difficulties on the Russian side, which effectively denies energy access to Germany and much of the EU. He raises two competing readings — that Putin is targeting Magyar as a Deep State replacement for Orbán, or that Magyar represents an earnest continuation of the Orbán arc and the Russian delay is a ploy aimed at higher powers in the EU — and concludes that Europe's energy crisis is likely to worsen either way.
