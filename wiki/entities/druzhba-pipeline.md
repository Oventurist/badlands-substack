# Druzhba Pipeline

The Druzhba pipeline is a major oil transmission system carrying Russian and Kazakh crude westward into Central Europe, split into a southern branch serving [[hungary|Hungary]] and Slovakia and a northern branch serving [[germany|Germany]].[1] In April 2026 it became a focal point of what Badlands commentary described as a European "pincer" in which energy flows were used as political leverage.[1]

## Southern branch repairs and the Hungarian election

[[ukraine|Ukraine]] announced that it had completed repairs to the southern branch, which had been damaged by a Russian strike in January, and declared itself ready to resume pumping Russian oil to Hungary and Slovakia.[1] The announcement came just days after [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]]'s landslide election victory over [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]].[1] President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] confirmed that the pipeline could resume operation, and the timing was expected to unlock a long-delayed €90 billion [[european-union|EU]] aid package to Ukraine that Orbán had vetoed.[1]

Magyar stated that his government would not block the loan but would refuse to contribute to it, would not support Ukraine's EU membership, would reject the EU Migration Pact, and would strengthen Hungary's borders.[1]

## Northern branch suspension

Separately, [[russia|Russia]] announced that it would halt Kazakh crude oil shipments to Germany through the northern branch of the Druzhba pipeline starting 1 May, citing technical issues.[1] The move affected the PCK Schwedt refinery, which supplies most of the fuel, jet fuel, and heating oil consumed in Berlin, and where Kazakh oil had previously covered roughly 17% of needs, with volumes reaching nearly 60,000 barrels per day in the first quarter of 2026.[1] Germany's Economy Ministry stated that the suspension did not jeopardize overall petroleum product supply security, since northern pipeline sections and increased deliveries via Poland's Gdansk port could cover shortfalls even if Schwedt operated at reduced capacity.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the sequence as a tug-of-war beginning with the defeat of Orbán, whose support for [[israel|Israel]] had begun to conflict with his ostensibly pro-Russia position as tensions between Israel and Russia escalated amid the Iran war.[1] He observed that Magyar largely mirrors Orbán's platform apart from his strong support for Hungarian EU membership, where Orbán had been prepared to leave the bloc.[1] He questioned whether [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s announcement of technical difficulties immediately after Zelensky's repair announcement was aimed at Magyar as a suspected deep-state replacement for Orbán, or was instead a ploy against higher powers in the EU — comparing Magyar's possible function to that of [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Jolani]] in [[syria|Syria]], a figure enabling narrative resolution and sanctions relief.[1] Either way, he concluded, Europe's energy crisis appeared set to worsen as the continent was forced to re-evaluate its political priorities.[1]

## Leaked Ukrainian sabotage plans (May 2023)

In May 2023, [[hungary|Hungary]] reacted with outrage to leaked Ukrainian plans to blow up a vital oil pipeline serving the country, a story reported alongside claims of intercepted communications describing the scheme.[2] The item appeared in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] within a cluster of same-day developments that [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read as evidence of escalating pressure in the [[russia-ukraine-war|Ukraine–Russia theater]], including [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s accession to the [[nato|NATO]] cyber defence centre and warnings that Western aid funding would soon run out.[2]
