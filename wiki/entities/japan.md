# Japan

Japan is an East Asian island nation and a principal treaty ally of the United States. In March 2026 it featured prominently in Badlands coverage of a White House meeting between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Japanese Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]], at which large energy and nuclear investment commitments were announced alongside a controversy over Trump's invocation of the 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor.[1]

## Constitutional constraints on military participation

Japan's ability to participate in military operations remains constrained by its pacifist constitution, a limitation Takaichi reiterated during the meeting even as she affirmed continued economic and strategic cooperation with the United States.[1] The constraint was salient because the United States was pressing allies, including Japan, to take a more active role in securing key shipping routes in the Middle East amid escalating tensions with [[iran|Iran]] and the closure crisis at the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

## Energy and investment commitments

The two countries advanced a major nuclear energy initiative including plans for small modular reactor development in the United States, with project estimates around $40 billion.[1] Japan pledged tens of billions of dollars in U.S. energy sector investment as part of a broader economic agreement covering nuclear power, natural gas and infrastructure projects, along with cooperation on critical minerals and rare earth supply chains aimed at reducing reliance on [[china|China]] and stabilizing inputs for energy and technology production.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] detailed the specifics: at the March meeting Trump announced that Japan would invest $40 billion in small modular nuclear reactor plants in Tennessee and Alabama, as well as $33 billion for natural gas generation facilities in Pennsylvania and Texas, with additional discussion of joint ventures in space exploration, technology development, supply-chain strengthening and cultivation of deep-sea rare earth minerals.[1] The month before, as part of the 2025 U.S.-Japan Strategic Trade and Investment Agreement, Japan had announced a $36 billion program to build in Ohio what was described as the largest natural gas facility in history, a deepwater crude oil export facility in the Gulf of America, and facilities allowing the United States to onshore 100% production of synthetic diamond grit, a product necessary for industrial and mining operations.[1]

## Diplomatic symbolism

Japan gifted 250 cherry trees in honor of the 250th anniversary of America's founding, a gesture tied to the widely celebrated Washington, D.C. cherry blossom season.[1] Badlands commentary framed Japan as a continuing great partner for America as the country prepares to enter what its writers call the Golden Age, invoking the memory of former Prime Minister Shinzo Abe.[1]

## Relations with Russia and the 2023 Patriot missile dispute

Japan and [[russia|Russia]] were historically enemies but in recent decades moved to being "frenemies," with improved relations offset by the simmering standoff over ownership of the Kuril island chain just north of Japan.[2] Relations grew tense again during Russia's operations in Ukraine and as Tokyo deepened defense cooperation with the United States, a shift that also drew Beijing's ire.[2]

In December 2023 the Japanese government announced it was preparing to ship Patriot anti-air missile defense systems to assist Ukraine, a plan the Biden administration had sought and regarded as a major diplomatic victory.[2] Japan announced a significant change to its arms export rules to make the transfer possible, a move the White House welcomed at a moment when Kyiv was running low on ammunition and advanced arms, and one widely read as further evidence that Japan was abandoning its post-WWII neutrality and pacifism.[2] Moscow blasted the decision, with Kremlin spokesperson [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] warning Tokyo of "grave consequences" and saying the Japanese side had lost control over weapons "with which Washington can now do whatever it wants."[2]

## July 2025 US tariffs

On Monday, July 7, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] set a 25 percent tax on goods imported from Japan, alongside [[south-korea|South Korea]] and new tariff rates on a dozen other nations, to take effect on August 1.[3] Trump gave notice by posting letters on Truth Social addressed to the leaders of the various countries, including Japanese Prime Minister [[shigeru-ishiba|Shigeru Ishiba]], warning them not to retaliate with their own import taxes: "If for any reason you decide to raise your Tariffs, then, whatever the number you choose to raise them by, will be added onto the 25 percent that we charge."[3] Reporting framed the letters as another episode in a global economic drama that had raised fears growth would slow to a trickle or leave the US and other nations vulnerable to recession.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the Japan tariff as the culmination of a 40-year odyssey, noting that Trump appeared on multiple television programs in the 1980s — most notably Oprah Winfrey — warning of the massive trade imbalance with Japan, explaining that most electronics, particularly VCRs, were by 1987 made in Japan and that it was nearly impossible to bring a US-manufactured good such as a car into the Japanese marketplace.[3]
