# US Virgin Islands

The US Virgin Islands is a United States territory in the Caribbean that became the lead government plaintiff in civil litigation arising from the sex-trafficking operation of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], who maintained private islands within the territory.[1]

## Litigation against JPMorgan Chase

The territory's attorney general brought a case alleging that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] facilitated Epstein's sex-trafficking ring by handling his payments to recruiters and victims.[1] As part of discovery in that case, prosecutors sought testimony from prominent figures with potential ties to Epstein, including bank executives and technology-industry principals.[1]

## Efforts to subpoena Larry Page

In May 2023, federal court filings revealed that the US Virgin Islands was unable to personally serve a subpoena on Google co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]] because investigators could not physically locate him.[1] A motion filed by the territory's attorney general stated that investigators had identified four possible addresses for Page, then 50 years old, none of which proved valid.[1] Prosecutors therefore asked the federal court to permit service by "alternative" means — through [[alphabet|Alphabet]], [[google|Google]]'s parent company, on whose board Page still sat.[1] The motion was filed in early May 2023.[1]

Reporting noted that Page's whereabouts were largely unknown and that he had reportedly spent the bulk of the COVID-19 pandemic on one of a growing number of private islands he owned, some of them located within the US Virgin Islands itself.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued that Page had "conveniently" gone missing precisely as the territory sought to subpoena him over "enabling a sex trafficking ring," suggesting he had retreated abroad because "the walls are closing in," and adding that the Epstein matter "is not going away" but "only getting bigger."[1]

## Subpoena of Les Wexner (2023)

In February 2023 the US Virgin Islands was granted permission by a New York judge to mail a subpoena to former Victoria's Secret boss [[les-wexner|Les Wexner]] over his ties to Epstein, after a security guard allegedly prevented the billionaire from being served at his home.[2] The order was issued on Tuesday, February 21, 2023, after the territory said it had been frustrated in seeking documents from Wexner as part of its civil suit against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]].[2] The territory stated it had attempted personal service at Wexner's Ohio home on February 8 and February 11, and also at his charitable foundation — of which Epstein was once a trustee — but was blocked on every occasion.[2] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] cited the subpoena, alongside document unsealings, emails, suits against JPMorgan Chase and [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]] and an appeal by [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]], as evidence that the Epstein matter had regained momentum.[2]

## August 2023 filing against JPMorgan

On August 15, 2023, the U.S. Virgin Islands filed court papers in Manhattan federal court to bolster its allegation that [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] played a key role as an investment "advisor" to [[google|Google]] and [[sergey-brin|Sergey Brin]], and that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] bankers — including [[mary-erdoes|Mary Erdoes]] — organized client coverage around him.[3]

## $190 million damages demand against JPMorgan (2023)

In July 2023 the territory stated in a Manhattan federal court filing that it was seeking at least $190 million from [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase & Co.]] in its suit accusing the bank of enabling [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s sex trafficking, along with a commitment to anti-trafficking reforms.[4] Attorney General [[ariel-smith|Ariel Smith]] said more needed to be done "to ensure this does not happen to another generation of women and girls."[4] The suit was one of two brought against JPMorgan in late 2022; the bank settled the companion class action by Epstein victims for $290 million without admitting liability.[4] Former territorial First Lady [[cecile-de-jongh|Cecile de Jongh]] was accused of covering for Epstein in a quid pro quo scheme.[4]

## 2024 Republican caucuses

The territory held Republican presidential caucuses on February 8, 2024, going third in the nation and using ranked-choice voting, a contest won overwhelmingly by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5] Territorial GOP Chairman Gordon Ackley promoted the format as "an unrigged caucus" giving every candidate "a fair and equal playing field" and predicted that "the Virgin Islands will decide the trajectory of the Republican nomination," a claim Badlands writers rejected while criticizing the broader push for [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked-choice voting]].[5] Turnout was minimal, with 182 votes cast for Trump in Delegate [[stacey-plaskett|Stacey Plaskett]]'s district; details appear at [[2024-us-virgin-islands-republican-caucuses|2024 US Virgin Islands Republican caucuses]].[5]

## Litigation against JPMorgan (September 2023)

In its lawsuit against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]], the U.S. Virgin Islands claimed in September 2023 that the bank had flagged over $1 billion in suspicious transactions tied to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and reported them to the federal government in a 2019 Treasury filing.[6] Territory attorney Mimi Liu told a federal court in Manhattan that "JPMorgan was a full-service bank for Jeffrey Epstein's sex trafficking," arguing that the sum bolstered the territory's allegation that the bank knowingly benefited from Epstein's wrongdoing.[6] Epstein had trafficked some of his victims to a private island in the USVI, and the territory had previously told the judge the bank facilitated over $1.1 million in payments from him to "girls or women," many with Eastern European surnames.[6]
