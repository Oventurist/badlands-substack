# NATO

The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) is the Western military alliance whose eastward expansion Badlands Media identifies as the central, suppressed cause of the war in Ukraine.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Expansion eastward

NATO first began its expansion during the Clinton administration, creeping steadily eastward toward Russia in exactly the way that [[mikhail-gorbachev|Mikhail Gorbachev]] had been promised would never happen after the fall of the Soviet Union.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) During negotiations over the reunification of Germany, it was agreed that NATO would not expand "one inch" eastward; the Russians, distrustful of nations that had been their declared enemies for decades, treated that assurance as foundational.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) A reunified Germany was itself a difficult concession for Moscow, which had lost tens of millions of citizens to Germany across the two world wars.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The United States honored the agreement for a time, but according to the article it never intended to honor it indefinitely: plans moved forward under [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]], continued through the Bush administration, and reached a fever pitch during the Obama era.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Ukraine membership

For years, concerns that Ukraine would join NATO were dismissed as "a figment of the paranoid Russian imagination" and as a pretext invented to justify invasion.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) That framing collapsed when U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], at a summit of NATO foreign ministers, said unequivocally that "Ukraine WILL become a member of NATO."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article notes the deliberate shift in language: officials no longer speak of discussions or of negotiating a possible Membership Action Plan (MAP), but treat Ukrainian membership as decided and inevitable.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Membership is attractive to Kyiv because it would oblige every member state, including the United States, to treat an attack on Ukraine as an attack on itself under [[article-5|Article 5]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) It is also, the article argues, the single step that would take negotiations with Russia completely off the table — a fact the neoconservatives in the [[us-security-state|Security State]], in [[us-congress|Congress]], and at the [[state-department|State Department]] not only understand but desire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Article 5 precedent

After 9/11, Article 5 was invoked, obliging member nations to follow the United States into Afghanistan and to offer assistance for Iraq.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) By that precedent, Ukrainian accession while the war continues would likely mean a hot war with Russia in Eastern Europe.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## First-strike doctrine

Badlands Media commentary on the 2025 [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|SCO]] summit argues that the next-generation hypersonic missile technologies unveiled by both Russia and China — including underwater drones — have demonstrated that a first-strike monopoly by NATO is a pipe dream.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) No attack on Russia or China, the author writes, could be carried out without a vicious retaliation that would take out every major city across the [[rules-based-international-order|Rules Based International]] free world.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) Rand Corporation analysts and representatives of the military industrial complex who treat a new world war as an exciting and winnable prospect on the basis of computer-modelled scenarios are said to be detached from objective reality.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## NATO in the post-Soviet cage

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the unipolar order established after the collapse of the Soviet system converted the trans-Atlantic into a cage of "post-sovereign nations," and that under NATO even national militaries were subject to technocratic control.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) The [[blair-doctrine|Blair doctrine]] of the late 1990s, which called for a "post-Westphalia" world order, is said to have unleashed regime change wars in the Arab world beginning with 9/11 while expanding NATO's hegemony against the potential re-emergence of Russia and China.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Ehret further observes that the NATO proxy war against Russia has only strengthened the Russian economy, counting this among the failures that mark the end of the unipolar script.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## The March 2026 Hormuz ultimatum

In a Financial Times interview during the war with [[iran|Iran]], [[donald-trump|President Trump]] warned that NATO faced a "very bad" future if allies did not assist the United States in reopening the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[8] He called on countries heavily dependent on Gulf oil — including China, the United Kingdom, France, Japan and South Korea — to contribute military assets such as minesweepers and air defenses to secure the waterway.[8]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the demand as a narrative pincer against NATO.[8] With the Iran war threatening maritime LNG shipments and Vladimir Putin suggesting Russia could halt pipeline gas to European markets, the United States would become Europe's sole LNG supplier at severe consumer cost.[8] NATO's European members were therefore forced to choose between deploying ships to Hormuz — risking entanglement with Iran and the loss of Persian Gulf LNG — or refusing Trump, their only reliable remaining supplier, leaving negotiation as the sole viable option.[8] The analysis is developed further in the [[global-energy-war|global energy war]] framing.[8]

## Transatlantic rift under Trump (2026)

By February 2026, European officials were reporting a shift toward viewing the United States as a rival rather than an ally, citing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s dismissal of NATO allies' sacrifices in Afghanistan among the precipitating incidents.[9] Others included the U.S. embassy in Denmark removing flags honoring fallen Danish soldiers, which sparked protests; U.S. agents providing Olympics security in Italy, where Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] was booed; and the U.S. ambassador to Poland criticizing a lawmaker over Trump's Nobel nomination, prompting a rebuke from Prime Minister [[donald-tusk|Donald Tusk]].[9] The aftermath of Trump's retracted threats to acquire [[greenland|Greenland]] deepened the rift further, fueling anti-American sentiment that exceeded 60% unfavorable views in several nations and peaked at 84% in Denmark, and prompting Europe to pursue "de-risking" through diversified ties and a rethinking of defense.[9]

## Film industry outreach and 2026 troop drawdown dispute

In May 2026, NATO officials held discussions with film and television industry representatives as part of broader efforts to shape public understanding of the alliance's mission.[10] NATO sources described the engagement with media creators as strategic communications and public outreach, a practice common among governments and defense alliances seeking to explain policies and maintain public support, while critics characterized it as an attempt to influence narratives through entertainment media.[10]

The outreach coincided with growing tension over potential US troop reductions in Europe. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signaled he was considering reducing or repositioning US forces stationed in parts of Europe, including Spain and Italy, amid disputes over defense spending and alliance burden-sharing.[10] Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] said she would oppose any unilateral US troop withdrawal from Italy, and other European officials warned a drawdown could weaken deterrence at a time of heightened geopolitical tension.[10] Reports also indicated internal disagreements within NATO over strategy and posture, with some members urging stronger collective defense commitments while others navigated political and economic constraints at home.[10]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicted films glorifying NATO and vilifying [[russia|Russia]], and noted the timing of Trump's posturing as Europe faced a jet fuel shortage and broader [[european-fuel-supply-crisis-2026|energy crisis]].[10] He argued Trump was simultaneously motivating European countries to redevelop their oil sectors and to establish their own national defenses as the US scales back NATO funding and manpower, contending that Europeans could buy Russian oil and gas but that NATO complicates it because the organization exists to combat Russia.[10]


## 2026 Russian accusations
Russian officials accused NATO in June 2026 of supporting Ukrainian strikes on Russian airfields, as Moscow claimed to be expanding operations in western [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[11] The accusation coincided with [[germany|Germany]]'s preparation of conscription legislation and [[poland|Poland]]'s reinforcement of its border with Germany, developments framed as part of a wider European defense adjustment.[11]

## Alliance crisis over the Iran war (April 2026)

Tensions within NATO intensified following disagreements over the U.S.-Israeli war against [[iran|Iran]], with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] criticizing European allies for refusing to support the military campaign.[12] The conflict began on February 28, 2026, when U.S. and [[israel|Israeli]] forces launched strikes against Iran, an action that strained relations across the transatlantic alliance and raised concerns among European governments about the legality of the war.[12] Trump expressed frustration with member states' reluctance to contribute, including their refusal to deploy naval forces to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], and questioned whether the United States should remain committed to the alliance.[12]

Officials and analysts described the dispute as a significant crisis for NATO, with divisions among member states raising concerns about cohesion and the alliance's future role.[12] Secretary-General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] was expected to meet Trump in Washington in an effort to ease tensions, as European leaders weighed the implications of continued U.S. pressure.[12] Although Trump raised the possibility of withdrawing from NATO, U.S. law requires congressional approval for such a move, limiting his ability to act unilaterally.[12]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that EU and NATO leaders, scrambling to react to and circumvent Trump's "Discombobulator," were forced into primetime addresses urging fuel conservation and warning of harder months ahead while refusing the commitments Trump described as their baseline obligations to the alliance.[12] He argued NATO's cohesion — "once the iron spine of the postwar order" — now strains visibly as the United States signals it will retake Hormuz on its own timeline, leaving allies to secure their own lifelines or pivot to American energy exports at premium rates.[12] In his framing, Russia and China operate from strategic depth and industrial leverage while the United States retains reserves, production capacity and a renewed mandate for domestic drilling, nuclear revival and fusion acceleration — an inversion he called the [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] template written large across the battlespace.[12]

## April 2026: Trump signals possible US withdrawal

In remarks reported from an April 1, 2026 interview with [[the-telegraph|The Telegraph]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was "strongly considering" pulling the United States out of NATO, a claim whose substance was independently matched by multiple major outlets.[13] Trump tied the threat to NATO allies' refusal to back US military action and maritime operations connected to the [[iran-war|Iran war]], including pressure around the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[13]

The remarks formed part of a broader escalation: on March 27 Trump said the United States does not "have to be there for NATO," and on March 31 Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] declined to reaffirm the US commitment to the alliance's collective-defense clause, saying that decision was up to Trump.[13] Trump has described NATO as a "paper tiger."[13]

In parallel, the administration promoted a "sovereign nations" framework in place of older alliance language: the White House summary of Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s February Munich speech said Trump's foreign policy "brings sovereign nations together in common purpose" and rejects "outdated globalist structures."[13] The same framing appeared in the January 7 White House memorandum announcing withdrawals from international organizations deemed contrary to US interests and sovereignty.[13] Finland's president said after speaking with Trump on April 1 that a "more European NATO" is taking shape, underscoring that European leaders were already discussing a security architecture less reliant on Washington.[13]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded to the "paper tiger" line by saying, "I always knew they were a paper tiger, and Putin knows that too, by the way," reading the episode as confirmation of his [[sovereign-pincer|Sovereign Pincer]] thesis.[13]

## Ankara summit and burden-sharing (July 2026)

At the alliance summit in Ankara, Secretary-General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] urged members to acknowledge that U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been correct in demanding higher defense spending and greater burden-sharing from European allies, crediting him with advancing a long-standing American goal of equalizing contributions and calling the period transformational.[14] Rutte said the 31 non-U.S. members had committed $250 billion in new defense investments and programs over the preceding two years, with further pledges expected, and pointed to European basing, airfields, and power projection support for the recent U.S.-led campaign against [[iran|Iran]].[14] He argued the alliance must build sustainable capabilities so that Europeans and Canadians carry a larger share of the load, framing the shift as a response to threats from Russia, China, Iran, and North Korea.[14]

Trump arrived at the summit still angry that allies had declined to open air bases for strikes on Iran or contribute forces to help reopen the Strait of Hormuz, mocking Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni and U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer, brushing off Rutte's praise, and declaring, "I just want loyalty."[14] He refused to rule out further reductions in the U.S. military footprint in Europe; the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had already scaled back some forces and begun a six-month posture review, with Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] calling the refusal of base access "shameful."[14] Trump said he attended only because the summit was hosted by Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]].[14]

Badlands commentary described NATO as a "defunct and inept" alliance and read Rutte's posture as evidence of a transition from unipolar hegemony to a multipolar world order in which the United States no longer serves as Europe's protector.[14]

## Article Five and the US southern border (January 2026)

In January 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] suggested that NATO members could demonstrate the alliance's value by sending troops to help secure the United States' southern border, musing on social media that invoking the alliance's mutual defense obligations under Article Five — a provision traditionally applied to military attacks on member states — might compel allied forces to assist with border security.[15] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the remarks as Trump trolling the alliance in response to congressional Republicans' backing of Venezuelan opposition figure [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], describing the threat to invoke Article Five as "a brilliant moment of TrumpaMania."[15]

Separately, the alliance featured in the resolution of the January 2026 [[greenland|Greenland]] tariff standoff: Trump announced a "framework of a future deal" with Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] that temporarily paused tariff actions rattling global markets, even as NATO allies warned that his rhetoric about Greenland's legal status undermined long-standing security arrangements and trust.[15]

## April 2026 Germany troop dispute

In April 2026 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] indicated the United States could reconsider its military presence in [[germany|Germany]] after German Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]] criticized US policy in the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]].[16] Trump suggested continued US troop deployments depend on allied support and burden-sharing, reiterating long-standing concerns about the value of US commitments within NATO.[16] Germany hosts tens of thousands of US troops, a key component of NATO's European defense posture and logistics network, and any reduction would represent a significant shift in US force positioning on the continent.[16]

## Alliance fractures during the 2026 Iran conflict

The US-led [[iran-war|conflict with Iran]] exposed and intensified fractures between the United States and its allies, particularly within NATO, where multiple member countries declined to support military operations or provide logistical access.[18] The Pentagon declined to reaffirm the alliance's collective-defense commitment, saying the matter was up to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[18] Trump's criticism of allies and his statement that he was “absolutely considering” withdrawing the United States from NATO further fueled uncertainty about American commitments and prompted European leaders to consider more independent defense strategies.[18]

## 2026 US force-pool reduction

In May 2026 the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration was reported to be preparing to reduce the pool of US military forces committed to NATO's crisis-response framework, the [[nato-force-model|NATO Force Model]], under which member states designate troops and capabilities available during major conflicts or emergencies.[19] Reuters reported that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] planned to scale back the American contribution significantly, amid mounting friction between Washington and European capitals over defense spending, Ukraine, Iran and broader strategic priorities.[19] Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] said any US reductions would be "gradual and structured," while European allies pressed for clearer communication after reports of delayed deployments to Poland and reductions in Germany.[19]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the drawdown as rendering NATO obsolete, arguing the alliance was never a defensive pact among equals but a mechanism of European dependency on the American military backbone, and that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] had placed both the NATO bloc and the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] in its crosshairs as emblems of globalism.[19]

## 2026 U.S. drawdown in Germany

European leaders reacted with surprise to President Donald Trump's decision to withdraw thousands of American troops from Germany, reading it as a signal that Europe must take greater responsibility for its own security.[20] Kaja Kallas called for a stronger European pillar within the alliance, Keir Starmer highlighted the need to address tensions in alliances, and Jonas Gahr Støre downplayed the numbers while urging that changes be handled harmoniously within the NATO framework.[20] Secretary General Mark Rutte pointed to European steps including implementing basing agreements and pre-positioning assets, while some officials affirmed the long-term resilience of the transatlantic relationship.[20] See [[us-troop-withdrawal-from-germany-2026|the 2026 U.S. troop withdrawal from Germany]].[20]


## Greenland deployment and European weakness (January 2026)

In January 2026, [[denmark|Denmark]] and other European NATO allies began sending military resources to [[greenland|Greenland]] amid escalating pressure from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for US control of the territory.[21] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Trump was leveraging the threat of Russia and China to induce NATO to divert resources from Eastern Europe and Ukraine toward Greenland, where he judged they would be strategically useless, and noted that the alliance was "running on fumes" as the [[german-industrial-crisis-2026|German industrial crisis]] deepened.[21] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the resulting force — thirteen Germans for reconnaissance, two Norwegians, one Briton, some Swedes, and French units under [[operation-arctic-endurance|Operation Arctic Endurance]] — as evidence of the weak power projection of an America-less NATO, with Finland, Canada and the Netherlands still hesitating.[21]

Separately, commentary on Gaza noted that [[turkey|Turkey]]'s NATO membership raises the possibility of an [[article-5|Article 5]] invocation should Israeli forces clash with Turkish personnel during the Gaza stabilization mission.[21]

## The Greenland stress test (January 2026)

In January 2026 the alliance faced an internal crisis over American ambitions in [[greenland|Greenland]].[22] Denmark's prime minister publicly stated that a US military attack on Greenland would undermine NATO, while European officials discussed increasing military presence on the island and officials in the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]], [[germany|Germany]], and [[france|France]] worked on a coordinated approach to reduce tensions and reaffirm existing Arctic security arrangements.[22]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the European response — a scramble to assemble a multinational NATO Arctic force as a counter-proposal — as appeasement of "the American sovereign," arguing that "even the institutions built to constrain American power now react with the instinctive deference of entities that remember, deep in their foundational coding, who ultimately underwrote their existence."[22]

## Expanded Ukraine funding pipeline (2026)

In May 2026, NATO officials were reported to be discussing plans to significantly increase long-term financial and military support commitments to [[ukraine|Ukraine]], seeking to sustain Kyiv's war effort against Russia and stabilize the Ukrainian defense sector.[23] The proposal advanced despite years of public warnings from watchdogs, Western officials, and anti-corruption groups about entrenched corruption inside Ukraine's political, military, and procurement systems.[23]

The renewed funding push reflected concern within the alliance that a prolonged conflict could strain Ukraine's economy, manpower, and infrastructure without expanded outside assistance. Critics across Europe and the United States continued to question oversight mechanisms, accountability standards, and the long-term sustainability of channeling ever-larger sums into a wartime government still contending with corruption scandals and internal political tensions.[23]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the funding story broke as former Zelensky chief of staff [[andriy-yermak|Andrey Yermak]] stood trial for massive corruption and as [[timur-mindich|Timur Mindich]] was accused of embezzling hundreds of millions of dollars before fleeing to Israel.[23] He questioned whether Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] was simply inept or was deliberately maximizing the humiliation of "the crooks in and around NATO," observing that Rutte's repeated deference to [[donald-trump|Trump]] suggested to him that Rutte might be an asset.[23]

## Steadfast Defender and the 2023 posture shift

In September 2023 NATO announced preparations for [[steadfast-defender-exercise|Steadfast Defender]], its biggest live joint command exercise since the Cold War, assembling more than 40,000 troops to practise repelling Russian aggression against a member state.[24] The exercise was described as part of the alliance's rapid push to transform from a crisis-response body into a war-fighting alliance, prompted by the invasion of Ukraine, and as part of a new training strategy under which NATO would run two large exercises per year instead of one.[24] Sweden, whose accession bid was still awaiting ratification by [[turkey|Turkey]] and [[hungary|Hungary]], was to be included, bringing participation to 32 nations.[24]

Also in September 2023, Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] told the European Parliament's foreign affairs committee that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had in autumn 2021 sent a draft treaty demanding a promise of no further NATO enlargement and the removal of NATO military infrastructure from allies that joined after 1997, as a precondition for not invading Ukraine, and that the alliance rejected both demands.[24] Stoltenberg concluded that Putin "went to war to prevent NATO, more NATO, close to his borders," remarks amplified by [[caitlin-johnstone|Caitlin Johnstone]] as an admission at odds with prevailing Western framing.[24]

In September 2024 Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said that Western approval for Kyiv to use longer-range weapons against Russian targets would mean NATO was "at war" with Russia.[25] "It would mean that NATO countries, the US, European countries, are at war with Russia," he said, adding that clearing Kyiv to strike deep into Russia "is a decision on whether NATO countries are directly involved in the military conflict or not."[25] The warning came as U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and British Foreign Secretary [[david-lammy|David Lammy]] discussed easing restrictions on Western weapons and Polish Foreign Minister [[radoslaw-sikorski|Radoslaw Sikorski]] argued that Ukraine had a right to use them to prevent war crimes.[25]

Badlands commentary was scathing about alliance performance in the field, arguing that the 31 Abrams tanks sent to Ukraine failed because crews used them as stationary pillboxes rather than mastering maneuver-warfare doctrine against a Russian military it described as the world's best at that style of combat.[25] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] separately framed the surrounding World War III doom-mongering as a "Bicameral Narrative Deployment" in what he calls the War of Stories.[25]

## Distrust of Hungary and Turkey (March 2023)

In March 2023 the German newspaper Die Welt published an opinion piece by Clemens Wergin arguing that NATO should not trust [[turkey|Turkey]] and [[hungary|Hungary]] within the alliance, citing Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]]'s continued blocking of Sweden's NATO accession and Hungary's demand for EU money in exchange for approving the membership of both nations.[26] Wergin claimed both nations had developed "unseemly" ties to [[russia|Russia]] and asked whether the alliance should continue sharing sensitive data with them, writing that in "their turn toward authoritarianism, Ankara and Budapest have also distanced themselves significantly from the community of values for which NATO stands."[26]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the piece as evidence that establishment, NATO-aligned and globalist outlets were "piling on" nations sitting on the sidelines of the emerging [[multipolar-world|multipolar world order]] advanced by Presidents [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi]], arguing that such bullying and threats only accelerated the shift.[26]

## Rutte's warning on Ukraine (December 2024)

Secretary-General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] used his first Financial Times interview as alliance chief to warn [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that a "bad" Ukraine peace deal favorable to Moscow would leave the United States and Europe facing a "dire threat" from Iran, China and North Korea.[27] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] read the intervention as Brussels talking tough ahead of an administration that had previously criticized NATO members for failing to pay their share of defense spending while relying on Washington to carry the burden.[27] All the states Rutte named had deepened relations with Russia during the nearly three-year war; Russia and North Korea signed a defense pact in mid-2024 that resulted in roughly 10,000 North Korean soldiers deploying in support of Russian forces, and all were coordinating on evading U.S.-led sanctions.[27]

## Turkey and the alliance

The Brief reiterated that Turkey has been a key NATO member since 1952 because of its geography relative to Russia and the Black Sea, and asserted, citing Colonel Towner-Watkins, that the country served as a major hub for [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[27] It argued that NATO, the [[cia|CIA]] and British intelligence remain deeply entrenched in Turkey and may retain coercive influence over President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]].[27]

Separately, retired admiral [[james-stavridis|James Stavridis]], a former NATO supreme allied commander, said the Israel–[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] ceasefire was a "direct result" of the incoming Trump administration, while cautioning that unpredictability deployed against allies "can have consequences that are less good."[27]


## European reaction to Trump's 2024 election win

Although it was not on the agenda, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election victory dominated the three-day IISS Prague Defence Summit held in the Czech capital from November 8, 2024, with NATO members rapidly reassessing their plans for supporting [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s defense and their spending commitments to the alliance.[28] Experts said that although Trump was perhaps unlikely to withdraw the United States from NATO, the coming years would be challenging for the alliance because many countries had failed to prepare for his return.[28]

"It's a bad surprise for most governments in Europe," former NATO official Edward Hunter Christie told Newsweek. "European policymakers feared—and they never concealed it—they feared the prospect of a second Trump presidency."[28] In February 2024 Trump had said he would "encourage" [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s [[russia|Russia]] to attack any NATO countries he judged to be falling short of their financial commitments; then-Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] — succeeded in October 2024 by former Dutch leader [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] — responded that any suggestion "allies will not defend each other undermines all of our security."[28]

Czech President [[petr-pavel|Petr Pavel]], a retired general and former chairman of NATO's military committee, doubted Trump's pledge to end the Ukraine war "within 24 hours" but expected him to seek a deal with Putin that would "most probably not be in our interest and in the interest of Ukraine."[28]

In November 2024 Trump named [[matthew-whitaker|Matt Whitaker]], a former acting Attorney General, as U.S. ambassador to NATO — an unusual choice given his law-enforcement rather than foreign-policy background, and a sensitive post given Trump's complaints that numerous members were not meeting the 2%-of-GDP defense spending commitment.[28]

## 2025 Ukraine arms-transfer scheme

On July 14, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] unveiled a scheme at the [[white-house|White House]], alongside NATO Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]], under which the alliance would purchase American weapons — including Patriot anti-missile batteries — and distribute them to [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[29] The aid was to be bankrolled by European NATO members and coordinated by Washington's envoy to NATO, [[matthew-whitaker|Matthew Whitaker]].[29] "We've made a deal today where we are going to be sending them weapons, and they're going to be paying for them," Trump said, describing billions of dollars of equipment purchased from the United States and routed through NATO to the battlefield.[29] The announcement accompanied Trump's ultimatum giving Russia 50 days to resolve the war or face roughly 100% secondary tariffs targeting Moscow's remaining trade partners.[29]

Retired French Brigadier General [[francois-chauvancy|Francois Chauvancy]] questioned the plan's feasibility, telling *Le Figaro*: "I don't see how NATO can purchase weapons for a third country. It doesn't have the financial means and can't empty its stockpiles in the areas it controls, as they are few in number. Finally, it can't deliver weapons overnight without approval from NATO structures."[29] He suggested Trump was "primarily aiming for publicity" and that "as always, domestic politics govern his foreign policy."[29] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] took the general's admission as confirmation that Trump is deliberately bankrupting the alliance by granting Europe the war it demanded while removing the American taxpayer subsidy.[29]

## June 2024 land-corridor planning

In early June 2024 it was reported that NATO was drawing up plans to send American troops to the frontlines of Europe in the event of an all-out conflict with [[russia|Russia]].[30] New "land corridors" were said to be under construction to funnel soldiers rapidly through central Europe without local bureaucratic impediments, allowing NATO forces to move at short notice should the war in Ukraine spread westward.[30] Officials told The Telegraph that the plans included contingencies for Russian bombardment, permitting troops to sweep into the Balkans via corridors in Italy, Greece and Turkey, or toward Russia's northern border via Scandinavia.[30]

The reporting came as [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] openly acknowledged the "possibility" of "a full-scale Third World War" and threatened "fatal consequences" for Western allies permitting Ukraine to use their weapons on Russian soil, while European leaders hardened their stance on Moscow, some threatening to send troops east and making record defence investments.[30]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the story through Sun Tzu's maxim "pretend to be strong when you are weak," calling it "the primal scream of a dying regime."[30] He restated the argument, long made by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], that alliance members do not contribute their fair share and that the entire structure is financed by the American taxpayer.[30] He noted that Turkish Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] had said days earlier that "we do not want NATO to take part in this war," and had subsequently signalled in Beijing that Turkey wished to join [[brics|BRICS]] — an overture the Kremlin promptly welcomed.[30] He further argued that the [[north-south-transport-corridor|International North-South Transport Corridor]], organized through the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]], would divert freight and oil away from the Suez Canal through Iran's port at Bandar Abbas, constituting "the death nail for NATO" and Putin's checkmate against the empire.[30]


## Eastern Sentry (September 2025)

In September 2025 NATO launched Operation [[eastern-sentry|Eastern Sentry]] to reinforce its eastern flank after Russian drones breached Polish airspace.[31] Under the initiative British [[royal-air-force|RAF]] Typhoons based in Lincolnshire flew air defense missions over [[poland|Poland]] alongside forces from Denmark, France and Germany, supported by a Voyager refuelling aircraft, with the aim of deterring potential aggression from [[russia|Russia]].[31] UK Defence Secretary [[john-healey|John Healey]] said NATO had responded with unity to the challenge posed by Moscow, and Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Sir Keir Starmer]] called Russia's behaviour a direct threat to European security.[31]

Separately, the United States pressed NATO and [[g7|G7]] countries in the same period to impose tariffs on [[china|China]] over its purchases of Russian oil, a demand Beijing denounced as unilateralism and economic coercion.[31]

## 2024 flashpoints: Turkey and the African proxy war

In late July 2024 Israeli Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] called on NATO to expel Turkey from the alliance after President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] threatened to invade Israel — a threat Badlands commentary read as posturing given continued Turkish fuel and resource supplies to Israel and Erdoğan's simultaneous courtship by BRICS and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization.[32]

Badlands commentary also framed the July 2024 ambush of [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] contractors and Malian troops in [[mali|Mali]], for which Ukraine's [[main-directorate-of-intelligence|GUR]] claimed planning credit, as an episode in an ongoing proxy war between Russia and NATO waged through groups including Wagner and ISIS, and funded in part by US taxpayers.[32]

## WW3 casualty planning (September 2024)

In September 2024 Lieutenant-General [[alexander-sollfrank|Alexander Sollfrank]], head of NATO's logistics command, confirmed the alliance was developing mass evacuation and rescue plans for a possible future war with Russia, aiming to build the operational capability to extract large numbers of wounded troops from the front lines.[33] Sollfrank warned that, unlike allied experience in Afghanistan and Iraq, an all-out war with Russia would produce heavy losses across a huge battlefield, and that Russian air power and missile stockpiles would make aerial medical evacuation too risky — potentially forcing NATO to operate "hospital trains."[33] The warning coincided with German military assessments that Russia could be capable of attacking a NATO country as soon as 2029.[33]

Separately, Admiral [[rob-bauer|Rob Bauer]], chair of NATO's Military Committee, said Ukraine had the legal and military right to strike deep inside Russia, while acknowledging that contributing nations retain sovereign discretion over restrictions on the weapons they supply.[33] Czech General Staff chief Karel Řehka, standing beside him, said his country imposes no such limits.[33] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded by calling for NATO to be dismantled in short order and for all of its leaders to be investigated for crimes against humanity and prosecuted to the fullest extent possible, and read the headlines as a public-relations campaign to build support for long-range strikes that the alliance had not been authorized to conduct.[33]


## Estonian treason convictions and credibility crisis (December 2025)
In December 2025 an Estonian court handed lengthy prison sentences to the leaders of the anti-NATO Koos party, convicted of working on behalf of Russia to undermine national security: co-founder Aivo Peterson received 14 years for treason, with associates Dmitri Rootsi and Andrei Andronov given 11 years and 11 years and six months.[34] Prosecutors alleged the defendants spread "narratives supporting Russia's foreign and security policy" intended to undermine public trust in NATO and Estonia's military aid to Ukraine.[34] Koos, founded in 2022, calls for Estonia to leave NATO, become a neutral state and remove foreign troops from its territory.[34]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that "NATO's public credibility continues imploding, and its obsolescence feels inevitable," describing the prosecutions as a regime targeting its opposition and predicting further cycles of populist nationalism and regime reaction.[34]

## Italian refusal of the "coalition of the willing" (March 2025)

In March 2025 Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] rejected proposals by France and the United Kingdom to deploy peacekeeping troops to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] to secure an eventual truce, telling the Italian Senate that sending Italian troops "has never been on the agenda" and calling the plan "a very complex, risky and ineffective option."[35] French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] had suggested that "a few thousand troops per country" would be deployed at key locations in Ukraine as part of a "coalition of the willing."[35]

Badlands commentary treated the refusal as "the beginning of the end of NATO," arguing that [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] and Macron were, perhaps unwittingly, establishing a new mandate for the alliance by proposing what amounted to an offensive campaign, given that Ukraine is not a NATO member and NATO is not formally a party to the conflict.[35] Such a mandate would require all members to contribute soldiers, yet the appetite for war was not unanimous across the continent.[35] The commentary posed the question of how NATO and EU leadership would respond to defiance in the ranks, suggesting that if Meloni held her ground and demonstrated that NATO had no power to enforce a call for troops, no member nation would sacrifice its soldiers for a lost cause in a non-member state.[35]

## July 2024 Washington summit and escalation

At the alliance's [[2024-nato-washington-summit|2024 Washington summit]], officials announced a series of moves that observers characterized as a significant collective escalation with [[russia|Russia]]: dozens of additional US anti-air defense systems for Ukraine, and a joint statement from Washington, The Hague and Copenhagen confirming that an initial batch of US-made F-16 fighter jets was en route to Kyiv despite Moscow's vow to target them.[36] Poland's army chief of staff General [[wieslaw-kukula|Wiesław Kukuła]] called on [[poland|Poland]] to prepare for full-scale rather than asymmetric conflict.[36]

The Washington Summit Declaration pledged continued support for Ukraine "on its irreversible path to full Euro-Atlantic integration, including NATO membership," prompting Russian Security Council Deputy Chairman [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]] to say Moscow must ensure that path ends in "the demise of either Ukraine or NATO. Or — even better — both."[36]

Coverage of the 32-member summit dwelt on the frailty of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] at a moment when the alliance was described as never stronger, with European officials alarmed by his decline and resigned to a November victory by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that they feared could reverse the alliance's momentum.[36] Latvian foreign minister [[baiba-braze|Baiba Braže]] insisted "the problem is not Ukraine. The problem is Russia," a framing Badlands commentary inverted with the assertion that "the problem is NATO."[36]

## Trump's 5% spending demand and the Spain dispute (2025)

After returning to office in January 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] intensified longstanding demands that the alliance's European members shoulder more of the military spending burden, pushing for a target of 5% of GDP.[37] On October 14, 2025 he singled out [[spain|Spain]] as the only member that had not increased its spending to that level, threatened punitive tariffs against Madrid, and said he was "very unhappy" with a country he described as "unbelievably disrespectful" of the bloc.[37] Earlier that month he had floated expelling Spain from NATO entirely.[37] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as another step in what he framed as a deliberate campaign to dissolve NATO as an "obsolete and useless entity."[37]

## Vilnius summit and Ukraine's membership path (2023)

Ahead of the July 2023 Vilnius summit, AFP reported that NATO was set to drop its long-standing [[membership-action-plan|Membership Action Plan]] requirement in Ukraine's case — a major hurdle that every member admitted between 1999 and 2020 had been required to clear.[38] Secretary-General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] promised to push for a fast-tracked process at whatever future point the alliance was prepared to seriously consider Ukraine's application, and heads of state in Vilnius crafted a statement on Ukraine's "path to membership."[38]

President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] nonetheless attacked the alliance's "soft language" for lacking a clear timeline.[38] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] characterized the pattern as one in which NATO pledges, arms transfers, F-16 commitments, pilot training programs, and eastern-flank build-ups were persistently treated by Kyiv as "never enough."[38]

## Land-for-peace controversy (August 2023)

In August 2023 [[stian-jenssen|Stian Jenssen]], chief of staff to Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]], angered Kyiv by appearing to suggest that Ukraine might cede territory to Russia in exchange for peace and NATO membership, telling Norwegian journalists that the issue had already been discussed within the alliance and that "I'm not saying it has to be that way, but it could be a possible solution."[39] The remark broke with Stoltenberg's consistent public position that the decision on whether to negotiate with Russia belongs to Ukraine alone and not to third countries.[39]

Jenssen retracted the comment the following afternoon, calling it "a mistake" and explaining that it had formed part of a larger discussion of possible future scenarios, while adding that if Ukraine reached a position to negotiate, the military posture on the battlefield would decisively shape the terms of any peace.[39] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the sequence through his "Overton's Goalpost" frame — deny, diminish, qualify, distract, admit — asking which stage the alliance had reached with respect to the war.[39]

## Debate over a more aggressive posture (November 2025)

In an interview with the *Financial Times* published on 30 November 2025, Admiral [[giuseppe-cavo-dragone|Giuseppe Cavo Dragone]], chair of the NATO Military Committee, said member states had been weighing options to respond to what he described as Russia's "hybrid war," and that "being more aggressive or being proactive instead of reactive is something that we are thinking about."[40] He suggested a "pre-emptive strike" could be considered a "defensive action," while acknowledging that this would sit far from the alliance's normal way of thinking.[40] Eastern European diplomats were reported to be the most vocal advocates of tougher measures, including retaliatory cyberattacks, though Dragone noted that legal, ethical and jurisdictional constraints limited NATO's decision-making.[40]

Badlands commentary ridiculed the framing, arguing that an aggressive posture toward [[russia|Russia]] had always been NATO's raison d'être and that the timing — with the Ukrainian military position deteriorating, Ukrainian officials resigning over aid embezzlement, and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] reportedly days from announcing a peace deal — indicated an attempt to escalate precisely as a settlement neared.[40]

## Badlands revisionist genealogy (July 2024)

A July 2024 Badlands brief responded to POLITICO reporting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would probably not quit NATO in a second term but would instead make it unrecognizable.[41] That reporting held that Trump, despite repeated threats to leave the 75-year-old alliance, would be unlikely to exit outright according to former Trump national security officials and defense experts likely to serve again; in exchange for continued U.S. participation he would demand drastically higher European spending and what defense expert [[dan-caldwell|Dan Caldwell]] called a "radical reorientation" of the alliance.[41] Caldwell told POLITICO Magazine, "We don't really have a choice anymore," citing rising U.S. debt, flagging military recruiting, and a defense industrial base unable to keep pace with the challenge from both [[russia|Russia]] and China.[41]

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry used the item to advance a revisionist account of NATO's origins, tracing a sequence of steps he argued were "pursued for purportedly noble purposes — to halt authoritarianism — yet the exact opposite is the truth."[41] The chain began with the 1900 Anglo-French Alliance between the historic rivals France and Britain, a relationship in which the French Empire had become Britain's junior partner after Napoleon's failed Russian campaign and the 1815 Congress of Vienna restored Europe's monarchies.[41] Next came the Western Union of 1948, adding the Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg, and in 1949 NATO was formally created as part of the [[truman-doctrine|Truman Doctrine]].[41]

The account tied NATO's founding to the postwar standoff over the Turkish Straits, in which the Soviets sought Mediterranean access from the Black Sea; the Allies pressured Turkey to refuse, and when Britain ended naval assistance in 1947 President Truman dispatched the Franklin D. Roosevelt carrier battle group along with $100 million in "foreign aid." Turkey joined NATO in 1952.[41]

The commentary further alleged that in staffing NATO's military leadership the Allies turned to former Waffen SS intelligence officers as the professionals who knew the communists best.[41] It cited Alexander Dolezalek — a Race and Settlement Main Office officer later assigned to lead the roughly 60-man Britisches Freikorps propaganda unit — who changed his surname to Bomhoff after the war and was hired by NATO to run its "All-European educational project."[41] It also cited [[adolf-heusinger|Adolf Heusinger]], the former Operations Chief of the German Army High Command, who headed the West German military from 1957 to 1961 and chaired NATO's military body from 1961 to 1964, during John F. Kennedy's presidency.[41] The piece closed by claiming that the founding documents of the [[european-union|European Union]], created in 1993, were "basically photocopies" of Third Reich plans for postwar Europe, concluding that "NATO is not what we have been told it is, nor is the European Union."[41]

## Trump's February 2024 "do whatever the hell they want" remark

At a campaign rally on Saturday, February 10, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would be willing to let Russia "do whatever the hell they want" against NATO allies that do not contribute sufficiently to collective defense.[42] The New York Times reported that long before the remark, European leaders had been quietly discussing how to prepare for a world in which America removed itself as the centerpiece of the 75-year-old alliance, and that Trump's statement threatened to force that debate into a far more public phase.[42] European media discussion had focused on whether Trump, if returned to office, would pull the United States out of NATO, but the larger implication of his statement was read as an invitation to Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to pick off a NATO nation as a warning and a lesson to the 30 or so others about heeding Trump's demands.[42]

The statement stunned many in Europe, particularly after three years in which President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had attempted to restore confidence in the alliance lost during Trump's four years in office and had repeatedly pledged that the United States would "defend every inch of NATO territory."[42] White House spokesman [[andrew-bates|Andrew Bates]] denounced the comments as "unhinged," but by Sunday morning they had already resonated with those who argue Europe cannot depend on the United States to deter Russia.[42]

## Badlands reading of the 2024 Kursk incursion

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] attributed the August 2024 [[kursk-incursion|Ukrainian incursion into Kursk]] to what he called the "NATO Nazi high command," ascribing to it a three-fold strategic intent: drawing Russian forces away from the east so Donetsk could be retaken, terrorising Russian civilians so that support for [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s war would wane, and attacking the [[kursk-nuclear-power-plant|Kurskaya nuclear power plant]] deep inside Russian territory.[43] He argued the plan mirrored the German operation at Kursk in 1943 — comparing the 1943 and 2024 battle maps — and that the alliance had blundered by attacking a sparsely populated region of pasture and forest, a point he credited to Colonel Lawrence Wilkerson in an interview with Judge Napolitano.[43]

On this reading NATO failed to foment unrest against Putin while killing enough Russians to secure his wrath, and the forces committed to the incursion left Ukraine's eastern front exposed to the Russian drive on [[pokrovsk|Pokrovsk]].[43] He further alleged that NATO had previously launched drones at the Russian-held Zaporizhia plant in May and was threatening the families of Ukrainian workers there to extract sensitive information, characterising the alliance as the progeny of the Third Reich and Eastern European Zionists and calling for NATO to be dismantled and for war crimes tribunals to prosecute officials at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and [[state-department|State Department]] responsible for the war.[43]

## Negotiated-settlement signalling on Ukraine (November 2023)

In November 2023 a post by the US Mission to NATO appeared to endorse an eventual negotiated peace in Ukraine, stating that Ukraine had "taken back more than half of its territory seized by Russia's forces since February 2022" and that "we will continue to support them to be in the strongest possible position at the negotiating table when the time comes," while pledging to stand "#UnitedWithUkraine."[44] An official denial followed hours later, prompting analysts to ask what the alliance's actual position was.[44]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] read the episode as evidence that the Ukraine proxy war against the Russian Federation was near "the end stage of Overton's Goalpost," with attention displaced by the Gaza crisis and a former "win at all costs" posture giving way to the language of negotiation — an outcome he predicted [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would win, observing that "a negotiating table is not the place you want to go after 3 failed counter-offensives."[44]

## Finland's border confrontation with Russia (November 2023)

Border tensions rose between Russia and [[finland|Finland]], the alliance's newest member, which had joined in April 2023 and drew a Russian vow of "countermeasures."[44] Finland accused Moscow of weaponizing migrants to create a crisis at its crossings along the roughly 830-mile frontier, which is also an external border of the European Union, and its military moved to bolster the border wall.[44] European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] and Finnish Prime Minister [[petteri-orpo|Petteri Orpo]] jointly condemned Russia's "instrumentalization of migrants," which von der Leyen called "shameful."[44]

## Hegseth's Brussels message (February 2025)

On Wednesday, February 12, 2025, US Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] went to Brussels and told European allies to "take ownership of conventional security on the continent."[45] Although Hegseth recommitted to NATO, CNN judged that something fundamental had changed, pairing the speech with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s call to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] as two "geopolitical thunderclaps" that would transform transatlantic relations and end "Europe's American century."[45] Hegseth also made clear that Ukraine's hopes of regaining all its lost land were unrealistic.[45]

In response, Germany, France, Poland, Italy, Spain, the United Kingdom, the European External Action Service, and the [[european-commission|European Commission]] issued the [[weimar-plus-statement|Weimar+ Statement]], insisting that Ukraine and Europe must be part of any negotiations and that a just and lasting peace in Ukraine was "a necessary condition for a strong transatlantic security."[45]

## Transformation into "Global NATO"

In "The End of History Delusion," Matthew Ehret argues that the promise made by then-Secretary of State [[james-baker|James Baker]] not to move NATO "one inch beyond its 1992 configuration" was increasingly abandoned after the Cold War, as the alliance transformed from a Cold War defensive body into an aspiring global offensive structure that absorbed as many former Soviet nations as it could acquire.[76] [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]], serving as US Ambassador to NATO after a stint as Dick Cheney's assistant, oversaw the bloc's expansion from 16 to 24 nations by 2008, and under her lead Georgia's and Ukraine's aspirations to join were officially welcomed.[76]
