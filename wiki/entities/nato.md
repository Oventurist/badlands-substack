# NATO

The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) is the Western military alliance whose eastward expansion, and specifically its bid to admit Ukraine, forms the central subject of the Badlands Media article "A Bright Red Line."[1]

## Article 5 and collective defence

NATO's binding commitment is [[article-5|Article 5]] of the North Atlantic Treaty, which provides that an armed attack against one member in Europe or North America "shall be considered an attack against them all," obliging each member to take such action as it deems necessary, including the use of armed force, to restore and maintain the security of the North Atlantic area, with all measures reported to the United Nations Security Council.[1] The article notes that membership therefore ensures every existing member, including the United States, has an obligation to respond to any attack on a new member's territory as if it were an attack on its own.[1]

Article 5 was invoked in the wake of the September 11 attacks, obliging member nations to follow the United States into Afghanistan and to offer assistance for Iraq.[1] By that precedent, the article argues, Ukrainian accession would likely mean a hot war with Russia in Eastern Europe.[1]

## Eastward expansion

According to the article, NATO first began its expansion during the Clinton administration, creeping steadily eastward toward Russia in precisely the manner that [[mikhail-gorbachev|Mikhail Gorbachev]] had been promised would never happen after the fall of the Soviet Union.[1] During negotiations over German reunification it was agreed that NATO would not expand "one inch" eastward; the United States honored the agreement for a time but, in the article's telling, never intended to honor it indefinitely.[1] Planning moved forward under Clinton, continued into the Bush administration, and reached "a fever pitch" during the Obama era.[1] See [[nato-expansion]].

## The Ukraine membership question

The article observes a shift in official language: where officials once spoke of "discussions" or of negotiating a possible Membership Action Plan (MAP) for Ukraine, U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] told a summit of NATO foreign ministers unequivocally that "Ukraine WILL become a member of NATO."[1] The article contends this single step removes any possibility of negotiations with Russia, and that neoconservatives in the U.S. security state, in [[congress|Congress]] and at the [[state-department|State Department]] are not merely aware of this but hungry for it.[1]

The 2008 diplomatic cable by [[william-burns|William Burns]], published by [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]], described Ukrainian entry into NATO as "the brightest of all redlines" for the Russian elite, not merely for [[vladimir-putin|Putin]].[1]
