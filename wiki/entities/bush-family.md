# Bush Family

The **Bush family** is an American political dynasty whose members held the offices of Director of Central Intelligence, Vice President, President, and state governor across four decades. In Badlands Media commentary by Joe Lange, the family is treated as the central node of an alleged network linking [[central-intelligence-agency|the CIA]], Middle Eastern royal families, rogue banking and war profiteering.[1]

## Alleged role as generational intermediaries

The article asserts that the Bush family have been "puppets for the cabal for generations," and raises the question of whether by 2017 they were serving [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1] Q drop #119 asked what family history "goes back pre_WW1/2," why the Bush family had broken silence to attack the sitting president, and whether the timing was a coincidence relative to the Saudi arrests.[1]

The stated motive for the family's hostility to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is that a sitting president holds the constitutional power to declassify evidence held in presidential libraries under classification markings.[1]

## Middle Eastern entanglement

Quoting a 2004 [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] opinion piece, the article states that four generations of the family embroiled the United States in the Middle East through "CIA connections, arms shipments, rogue banks, inherited war policies and personal financial links."[1] As early as 1964, [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]], running for the U.S. Senate from Texas, was labeled by incumbent Democrat Ralph Yarborough as a hireling of the sheik of Kuwait, for whom Bush's company drilled offshore oil wells.[1] The Bushes are described as the first U.S. political clan to thoroughly entangle themselves with Middle Eastern royal families and oil money.[1]

All four sons — [[george-w-bush|George W.]], [[jeb-bush|Jeb]], [[neil-bush|Neil]] and [[marvin-bush|Marvin]] — are said to have lined up business deals with Saudi, Kuwaiti and Bahraini financiers and to have "cozied up" to [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]], with the region functioning as a "family money spigot."[1]

## BCCI and war as distraction

The article argues that the BCCI scandal was more dangerous to George H.W. Bush than either the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra affair]] or Iraqgate, because it was a family affair.[1] It contends that Bush deliberately initiated the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]] as a distraction from congressional and criminal investigations into BCCI, and that the destruction of [[kuwait|Kuwait]] served to destroy evidence of the family's dealings there.[1] Reconstruction contracts are said to have flowed to firms including [[halliburton]] and the [[carlyle-group|Carlyle Group]].[1]

The article further alleges that the family helped promote and fund Alwaleed's proxy groups such as [[hezbollah]], and encouraged a global drug trafficking network uncovered in an undercover investigation in Latin America.[1]

## Institutional vehicles

The [[george-w-bush-institute|George W. Bush Institute]] is described as one of a class of institutes and foundations — alongside John McCain's institute and the Clinton foundation — presented in the article as vehicles set up for receiving bribes and payoffs from external patrons.[1]

## Assessment

These characterizations are Q-adjacent interpretation built atop selectively quoted journalism, not established findings. Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors.[1]


## Chapter 18 on the Rothschild connection

Chapter 18 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series reads Q drop #119's question about which family's history dates back to before the First and Second World Wars as pointing to the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]], and argues that the Bush family publicly attacked [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] because of its own long and rich history with the Rothschilds and with other figures and organisations the author regards as malign.[2] The chapter maintains that the Bush family sat near the very top of the pyramid for decades and that Trump helped bring that rule to an end, which it offers as the motive for the family's hostility.[2] It also points to the funeral of George H. W. Bush — an event the author says Q predicted — where something communicated through letters distributed to attendees visibly unsettled the assembled establishment figures.[2] Elsewhere the chapter argues that whoever authorised the departure of Saudi nationals and members of the [[bin-laden-family|Bin Laden family]] from the United States after the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]] must be located within the [[george-w-bush|Bush]] administration, while declining to name the president himself.[2]
