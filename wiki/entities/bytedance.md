# ByteDance

ByteDance is a Chinese technology company best known internationally as the parent of TikTok. In the Badlands corpus it appears principally in connection with its generative video model Seedance 2.0 and the intellectual-property confrontation that model triggered with major American film studios.

## Seedance 2.0 and the studio dispute

According to the February 17, 2026 Badlands Brief, ByteDance released Seedance 2.0 in China in February 2026. The tool generates short cinematic videos from text, image, audio, and video inputs. Within days of its release, clips generated with the model — featuring recognizably realistic portrayals of Hollywood actors and characters, including scenes interpreted as resembling big-budget productions — circulated widely on social media.

Major studios, notably the [[walt-disney-company|Walt Disney Company]] and [[paramount-pictures|Paramount]], responded by sending cease-and-desist letters alleging that Seedance 2.0 uses copyrighted material and characters without authorization. The letter was not publicly distributed but was first reviewed and reported by [[axios|Axios]]. Disney's attorneys accused ByteDance of "hijacking Disney's characters by reproducing, distributing and creating derivative works featuring those characters" and described the conduct as a "virtual smash-and-grab of Disney's IP" that was "willful, pervasive and totally unacceptable." The [[human-artistry-campaign|Human Artistry Campaign]] also condemned the launch. ByteDance responded by promising improved safeguards against intellectual-property theft.

The Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as a test of the enforcement capabilities of US and global intellectual-property frameworks, comparing it to the Napster era and arguing that the technological transformation would proceed regardless of litigation outcomes.
