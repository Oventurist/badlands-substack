# King George III

George III was King of Great Britain during the American Revolution and the principal royal patron of [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents him as a far more contradictory figure than the "mad king" of popular culture.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Patron of West

Born in 1737, the same year as West, the king extended his patronage to the painter shortly after West's arrival in England, and in 1769 purchased *The Departure of Regulus for Carthage* for a thousand pounds.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) When the artistic establishment objected that West's *The Death of General Wolfe* used contemporary rather than Greco-Roman costume, George III was asked to arbitrate and was so impressed by West's reasoning and talent that he bought the painting, settling the debate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

From 1769 the king spent evenings in philosophical dialogue with West at Buckingham Palace, holding him for hours discussing the nature of government and of mankind, during which West made his pro-republican views well known.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The article argues that the king admired human genius and virtues antagonistic to the oligarchical organising principles of the British Empire into which he had been born, and that this nuance is lost on modern historians.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) West's 1779 portrait shows the king having just received word of the French–Spanish Armada threatening England with land invasion at the height of the American Revolution.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Insanity

George III is popularly remembered as the king who went insane and was removed from power in 1810, but the article notes he had several bouts of insanity beginning in the early 1780s with signs appearing earlier still.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Ehret suggests the cause may lie in two contradictory impulses within him, comparable to those he attributes to the Marquis de Lafayette.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) West's paintings of *Saul and the Witch of Endor* and of *King Lear* are read as messages directed at a monarch struggling with insanity in an unjust world.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) By 1816 the king had gone completely insane and been taken out of power, leaving West — still president of the Royal Academy — without royal sponsorship and largely isolated.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The 1776 self-portrait in which West embedded his coded anomaly was composed several weeks after George III received the Declaration of Independence.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## The opium mandate

In the account given in [[dope-inc-book|*Dope, Inc.*]], George III mandated the [[british-east-india-company|East India Company]] to begin shipping large amounts of opium from British-controlled Bengal, India, to China during the eighteenth century, as English domestic manufacturing fell behind and a trade deficit with China drained British silver reserves.[2] The policy was intended both to reverse the deficit and to create a country of drug addicts who would be more easily controlled, setting in motion the traffic that culminated in the [[opium-wars|Opium Wars]].[2]
