# King George III

George III was King of Great Britain during the American Revolution and the principal patron of the painter [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]]. The Badlands Media essay [[a-new-american-renaissance|A New American Renaissance]] by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues against the "simplistic dopy evil image of the king that has been passed down to us through pop culture," presenting him instead as a divided figure.

## Relationship with West

Born in 1737, the same year as West, the king purchased *The Departure of Regulus for Carthage* in 1769 for a thousand pounds. When the art establishment attacked West for painting *The Death of General Wolfe* in contemporary rather than Greco-Roman costume, George III was so impressed by West's reasoning that he bought the painting, settling the controversy. Beginning in 1769 the king kept West at Buckingham Palace for long evenings of philosophical dialogue on government and human nature, during which the painter made his republican views explicit. West painted the king in 1779 receiving word of the French-Spanish Armada that threatened England with land invasion at the height of the Revolution.

## The question of his madness

The source acknowledges that George III went insane and was removed from power in 1810, but stresses that his affliction did not begin then: he suffered several bouts starting in the early 1780s, with signs earlier still. The article proposes that, like the Marquis de Lafayette, the king contained two contradictory impulses — genuine admiration for human genius and the virtues antagonistic to oligarchy, set against the oligarchical organizing principles of the British Empire into which he was born. West's paintings of *Saul and the Witch of Endor* (1777), *King Lear* (1791) and *Pharaoh and Host Lost at Sea* (1792) are read in the essay as coded messages to a monarch struggling with insanity, superstition and the consequences of misrule. By 1816, with the king removed from power, West retained the presidency of the Royal Academy but lost royal patronage and became isolated.
