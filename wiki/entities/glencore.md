# Glencore

Glencore is a multinational commodity trading and mining group with major copper and cobalt interests in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]].[1]

## Orion CMC memorandum of understanding (2026)

In early February 2026 Glencore announced that it had entered a non-binding Memorandum of Understanding with the [[orion-critical-mineral-consortium|Orion Critical Mineral Consortium]] regarding a potential acquisition by Orion CMC of a 40% stake in Glencore's interests in its DRC assets, [[mutanda-mining|Mutanda Mining]] (Mumi) and [[kamoto-copper-company|Kamoto Copper Company]] (KCC).[1] The transaction was expected to imply a combined enterprise value for Mumi and KCC of around $9 billion.[1]

Under the proposed terms, Orion CMC would have the right to appoint non-executive directors for the assets and to direct the sale of its relevant share of production to nominated buyers, in accordance with the U.S.–DRC Strategic Partnership Agreement, thereby securing critical minerals for the United States and its partners; Mumi and KCC would continue to be managed as part of the Glencore Group.[1]

## Gertler connection

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Glencore, Mutanda and Kamoto are the three companies most commonly named in court cases and reporting surrounding Israeli businessman [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]].[1] During the 2007–2008 financial crisis, facing insolvency, Gertler accepted a $45 million investment from Glencore in exchange for an ownership stake in his companies, and later brokered deals that brought both Mutanda and Kamoto under Glencore's control while retaining some ownership himself.[1]

## 2024 UK bribery charges

In August 2024 the UK [[serious-fraud-office|Serious Fraud Office]] charged Glencore's billionaire former head of oil, [[alex-beard|Alex Beard]], and four other former executives — Andrew Gibson, Paul Hopkirk, Ramon Labiaga and Martin Wakefield — with conspiring to make corrupt payments, after a long-running investigation into alleged bribery by the UK-listed commodity trader in Africa.[2] The charges related to oil contracts awarded in Glencore's interests, and the defendants were ordered to appear before Westminster Magistrates' Court on September 10, 2024.[2] The SFO had opened the investigation in 2019 over allegations connected to Glencore's London-based West Africa desk, which sourced and traded crude oil across the continent, and delayed announcing charges in 2023 after receiving further evidence.[2]

## Venezuela and the Maduro lawsuit

Beyond mining rights in Africa, Glencore — described as the largest commodity trading company in the world — also targeted oil rights in Venezuela.[2] In March 2018, Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] filed suit in U.S. court against Glencore, Trafigura and Vitol, the three biggest commodity trading houses with combined annual revenue of a trillion dollars, in what Badlands commentary called one of the largest civil actions in U.S. history.[2] The suit named roughly 40 executives, involved over 70 attorneys, and was handled by [[david-boies|David Boies]].[2] The alleged bribery scheme served to secure state-awarded oil contracts, avoid government audits, and mitigate legal liabilities with government officials.[2] Badlands commentary linked the case to the sanctioning of [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] in December 2017 and to a broader campaign against a globalist resource-extraction network.[2]

## Alleged link to the cartel campaign (2025)

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote in August 2025 that his own research had led him to conclude that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] were both targeting the same corrupt corporate executives at the world's most powerful commodity trading firms, naming Glencore as an example.[3] He contrasted the prosecution of former Glencore CEO Alex Beard with the far larger undertaking of dismantling all of the Central American cartels at once, which reporting two weeks earlier suggested Trump was preparing to pursue with the U.S. military.[3]

## Katanga, corruption judgements, and forced divestitures

Badlands commentary in November 2024 traced the origin of the legal campaign against Glencore to Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], who in March 2017 was given a "laptop from hell" by the scorned ex-wife of one of his oil officials; the laptop revealed that his officials were engaged in corruption with Glencore executives among others.[4] Lawsuits filed by Maduro were ultimately dismissed but appeared to yield materials that led to the prosecution of Glencore officials, including former CEO [[alex-beard|Alex Beard]], who faces corruption charges alongside five former colleagues.[4]

After [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]]'s assets were frozen in 2017, Glencore was forced to buy out his shares of [[katanga-mining|Katanga Mining]] and became its sole owner.[4] The company subsequently faced a series of judgements from what Badlands writers term lawfare cases, including a high-profile 2022 case resulting in a multi-billion dollar judgement payable to five African countries, including the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DRC]].[4] Earlier in 2024 it reached a $50 million settlement with Nigeria, and while still operating and reporting record profits, it continued to be sued by a growing list of nations and to see a revolving door of executives targeted in corruption probes.[4] As part of the 2022 settlement deal, the United Arab Emirates acquired [[mopani-mining|Mopani Mining]] from Glencore in a forced sale, an operation in the Zambia region directly adjacent to Katanga.[4]


## Cited in Venezuela commentary (September 2025)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Glencore in September 2025 as an example of the powerful commodity trading companies whose corrupt executives had been targeted by an international prosecution effort, arguing that Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] had contributed significantly to that effort.[5] In this reading, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been working with Maduro to bring the transnational corporate cartel to heel through deception and misdirection before pivoting to the drug cartels.[5]

## Swiss settlement (August 2024)

In August 2024 Glencore agreed to pay $152 million after Swiss authorities concluded an investigation into the commodities trader and miner, an outcome that marked the end of more than six years of legal probes across Europe and the Americas.[6] Swiss prosecutors found that Glencore failed to take reasonable measures to prevent the bribery of a Congolese public official by a business partner in 2011, according to a statement from the office of the attorney general.[6] Glencore said it did not admit the findings but agreed not to appeal the summary penalty order in the interests of resolving the matter, and a parallel investigation by the Dutch Prosecution Service was concluded and dismissed.[6] The company noted that the OAG's order did not identify any Glencore employees with knowledge of the business partner's bribery, nor any financial benefit to Glencore from that conduct.[6]

With those penalties, Glencore had paid at least $1.7 billion to resolve bribery and corruption investigations worldwide; in 2022 it pleaded guilty to corruption and market manipulation cases in the United States and United Kingdom, admitting it had paid bribes to win business in eight countries from Brazil to South Sudan.[6]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the settlement as the end of the particular case concerning mining rights in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with Israeli billionaire [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] at the center of the plot.[6] He connected it to criminal charges against [[alex-beard|Alex Beard]], a Glencore oil executive whose 2019 departure appeared linked to the exits of executives Telis Mistakidis, who retired in December 2018, and Ivan Glasenberg, who left in July 2021.[6] The charges against Beard, in this account, traced to a civil action brought by Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in March 2018 against 40 executives from Glencore, [[trafigura|Trafigura]], and [[vitol|Vitol]] — the three largest commodity trading companies in the world — a year after Maduro received a "laptop from hell" from the scorned ex-wife of one of his oil officials.[6] That investigation was said to have provided materials used by the Trump [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] to pursue oligarchs under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]].[6]

Citing a 2018 Bloomberg feature, the commentary held that Gertler, Mistakidis, and Glasenberg worked closely together to exploit the Congolese people in their mining operations, with Mistakidis serving as a board member for [[katanga-mining|Katanga Mining]] — Gertler's company — as well as for [[mopani-mining|Mopani Copper Mines Limited]], lawfully acquired by International Resources Holding in March 2024.[6] IRH is a UAE company owned by [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohammed bin Zayed]], a close ally of Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.[6] Noting that although Glasenberg is a South African citizen his father was a Jew born in Lithuania, the writer argued from his study of the Israeli Mafia and the international cartel that the link to Israeli oligarchs such as Gertler was not coincidental, and concluded that Africa was being liberated from globalist oligarchs.[6]

## Cited in 2025 Venezuela commentary

Badlands commentary on the August 2025 U.S. reward offer for Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] pointed to former Glencore oil chief [[alex-beard|Alex Beard]], arrested in Britain in 2024 on corruption charges tied to Venezuelan oil schemes run against Maduro's government, as an example of the commodity-trading interests aligned against Caracas.[7]
