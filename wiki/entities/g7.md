# G7

The G7 is the grouping of large industrialized Western democracies whose leaders meet annually to coordinate positions on trade, security, energy, and technology. In June 2026 the group convened in Évian-les-Bains, France, with an agenda expected to cover Iran, Ukraine, trade disputes, energy security, artificial intelligence, and China's economic influence.[1]

## The 2026 summit and the question of relevance

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arrived at the summit shortly after announcing a framework agreement aimed at ending the recent conflict with [[iran|Iran]], giving him a significant foreign-policy accomplishment heading into meetings with allied leaders.[1] The summit came amid broader questions about the future role of the G7 itself: while leaders were expected to discuss coordinated approaches to trade, critical minerals, artificial intelligence, and the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, reporting noted that [[china|China]]'s economic power and the state of US–China relations continued to shape most of the issues under discussion.[1]

European officials and analysts expressed growing concern that the world's most consequential decisions are being shaped less by traditional multilateral institutions such as the G7 and more by the bilateral relationship between Washington and Beijing — the arrangement described as a [[g2|"G2"]].[1] Trump was also expected to press for progress on [[ukraine|Ukraine]] following his Iran effort, while European leaders sought continued US engagement on security and economic matters.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the G7 as a "Global Collectorate" whose rigged architecture the American and Chinese economies possess sufficient gravitational mass to circumvent — rerouting trade flows, resetting financial rails, and redirecting energy corridors on a scale the older order cannot absorb.[1] In that reading, Trump's earlier framing of a G2 with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] was not merely rhetorical but a public acknowledgment of a pincer closing on the post-war order, with the true target of Trump's campaign being globalism itself rather than any individual proxy state.[1]

## Core 5 proposal and Putin's dismissal (December 2025)

In December 2025 the G7's standing came under pressure from two directions. Media reports described an unpublished, longer draft of the Trump administration's National Security Strategy proposing a five-nation bloc — the [[core-5|"Core 5"]] — comprising the United States, Russia, China, India and Japan, to serve as a forum for great-power dialogue outside the G7 framework, with regular thematic summits beginning with Middle East security.[2] The [[white-house|White House]] denied that any version beyond the official 33-page document exists.[2]

Separately, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said Russia has no plans to rejoin the group, observing that many G7 economies now lag emerging economies in purchasing-power-parity terms and that the bloc's share of the global economy has been steadily shrinking, while conceding it remains "an important platform."[2] The G7 comprises the US, the UK, France, Germany, Italy, Canada and Japan.[2] Badlands commentary recalled that the group had been the G8 until Russia's expulsion under [[barack-obama|Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], and argued Putin's refusal to return reflected rejection of the bloc's cultural agenda.[2]


## Secondary tariffs over Russian oil (September 2025)

The Financial Times reported in September 2025 that Washington had asked G7 finance ministers to consider secondary tariffs of 50–100% on Chinese and Indian imports tied to purchases of Russian oil, part of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign to pressure [[russia|Russia]]'s trading partners and, he argued, help end the Ukraine conflict.[3] [[china|China]] condemned the effort to pressure G7 and [[nato|NATO]] countries into tariffs and warned it would retaliate if its interests were harmed, with Foreign Ministry spokesperson [[lin-jian|Lin Jian]] describing the request as "a typical move of unilateralism, bullying, and economic coercion."[3]

## Origins and the 2024 Italy summit

Badlands commentary traces the G7's origins to the 1973 oil crisis, provoked by Israel's Yom Kippur War, which prompted the creation of a forum in which the most powerful industrialized nations could gather and "solve" the world's problems.[4] Although membership is limited to seven nations plus the [[european-union|EU]], other relevant states are routinely invited to attend.[4]

At the [[2024-g7-summit-in-italy|2024 summit in Italy]], leaders agreed on June 13 to send [[ukraine|Ukraine]] $50 billion in aid financed from profits on frozen Russian sovereign assets, while very few invited non-member states accepted their invitations — the UAE and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] declined, Turkey's [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] skipped the opening day, and China and Brazil refused to attend Ukraine diplomacy that excluded Russia.[4] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the poor showing a humiliation and, noting that the New York Times itself described the assembled leaders as weakened, argued it was poetic that the Israel–Hamas conflict served as the backdrop for what he termed the death knell of the G7.[4]

## Cited by Trudeau in June 2024

Pressed in June 2024 on whether he was the main reason his party was struggling, Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] pointed to conversations "with other leaders at the G7 and in Switzerland at the peace conference for Ukraine," saying that people everywhere were struggling with inflation, cost of living, interest rates, housing and childcare.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the answer as laughable clout-hyping of the G7, arguing that those very conditions were the fault of the G7, Trudeau and their global handlers.[5]
