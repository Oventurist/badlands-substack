# Samsung Electronics

Samsung Electronics Co. is the South Korean electronics and semiconductor conglomerate whose foundry business is one of the two leading contract chipmakers in the world, alongside Taiwan's [[tsmc|Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.]][1]

## Taylor, Texas fab

Samsung announced in 2021 a $17 billion chip fabrication plant in Taylor, Texas, saying at the time that production would begin in the second half of 2024.[1] In December 2023 the Seoul Economic Daily reported, citing a speech by Choi Siyoung, president of Samsung's foundry business, at an industry event in San Francisco, that mass production at the new fab had been pushed back to 2025.[1] A company spokesperson said Samsung could not confirm the mass production schedule.[1]

The delay followed an earlier decision by TSMC to postpone production at its new Arizona fab from 2024 to 2025, a slip attributed to shortages of experienced construction workers and machine-installation technicians.[1] Any delay at the US sites of the world's two largest contract chipmakers was described as a setback to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s plan to expand domestic chip production and insulate the United States from supply disruptions of the kind that struck in 2021 and cost companies hundreds of billions in revenue.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the delay through the lens of the [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS Act]], noting that the legislation's original structure was assembled under the Trump administration and only signed into law by Biden.[1] Under his "Switch" thesis, the beneficial long-term economic effects of the chips program — tens of thousands of high-paying jobs and the erosion of the China–Taiwan near-monopoly on semiconductors — would only be realized on a delay, after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] returned to office.[1]
