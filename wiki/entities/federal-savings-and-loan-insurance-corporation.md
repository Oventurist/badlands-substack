# Federal Savings and Loan Insurance Corporation

The Federal Savings and Loan Insurance Corporation (FSLIC) was the federal insurer and receiver for thrift institutions during the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]].[1]

Its regulators took over the $1.5 billion [[sunrise-savings-and-loan|Sunrise Savings]] operation of Boynton Beach, Florida in July 1985 after learning that the thrift's losses for June alone totalled $22 million.[1] A federal grand jury then examined Sunrise's lending practices, including the possibility that borrowers paid kickbacks to obtain loans, while shareholders who had watched the stock fall from $25 to 25 cents filed at least four class-action suits.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] frames the agency's belated interventions within a broader indictment of the regulators, asking whose side they were on and noting that federal [[deposit-insurance|deposit insurance]] ensured taxpayers would absorb the losses of every failed institution.[1]
