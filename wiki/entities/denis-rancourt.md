# Denis Rancourt

Denis Rancourt is a researcher whose analyses of all-cause mortality during the COVID-19 period are described as making a persuasive case that COVID-19 was at worst a novel pathogen but not a cause of mass death.[1]

His argument, as summarized by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], holds that the 2018 seasonal influenza season produced higher all-cause mortality than the 2020 declared pandemic.[1] On this reading, the observed spikes in deaths were caused not by the virus but by lockdowns, the isolation of fragile elderly people, and unnatural medical interventions such as unnecessary intubations mandated by the [[world-health-organization|WHO]] onto western nations.[1] Rancourt's work is further cited for the claim that all-cause mortality rates only began rising dramatically beyond the norm after the rollout of the experimental vaccines.[1]

This mortality analysis functions in Ehret's essay as a closing consideration weakening the premise on which the [[lab-leak-theory|Wuhan lab leak hypothesis]] rests — namely that an unusually lethal engineered pathogen was released at all.[1]
