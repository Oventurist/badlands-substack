# Special Air Service

The **Special Air Service (SAS)** is the principal special forces regiment of the British Army, with 22 SAS as its regular unit, organized into sabre squadrons designated A, B, D, and G. It operates under the Directorate of United Kingdom Special Forces and has been deployed extensively in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]], Syria, Iraq, and Northern Ireland.

## 2026 mass resignations

In April 2026, dozens of 22 SAS troops filed for premature voluntary release amid a human rights investigation into alleged war crimes committed during operations in Afghanistan between 2010 and 2013, as well as in Syria and Northern Ireland. D and G Squadrons were reported as the most heavily affected. The departures were driven by fear of prosecution over allegations of extrajudicial killings of unarmed civilians and detainees during night raids, the planting of weapons on bodies, and systematic cover-ups by senior officers. A memo circulated to SAS associations disclosed that lawyers were pursuing 242 special forces personnel, including 120 still serving, with potential legal sanctions for non-compliance. Insiders described "considerable disquiet," collapsing morale, and a perceived "witch hunt."

## Underlying allegations

A 2022 [[bbc|BBC]] *Panorama* investigation identified 54 suspicious deaths caused by a single SAS unit during one six-month tour in Helmand province, alleging that unarmed Afghan men were routinely shot in cold blood and that evidence was falsified afterward. A subsequent public inquiry heard testimony that the director of UK Special Forces had made a "conscious decision" to conceal potential wrongdoing, and that [[operation-northmoor|Operation Northmoor]], the main military investigation running from 2014 to 2019, was closed "inappropriately and prematurely" despite serious unresolved concerns.

## Badlands treatment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he would once have defended special forces reflexively but now distinguishes collateral damage in combat from targeted, politically motivated violence, while acknowledging genuine grey areas where civilians take up arms and where malice cannot be established beyond reasonable doubt. He cautioned that elite training is "not a cure for sociopathy," and argued that accountability should not stop at the soldiers on the ground: intelligence services including the [[cia|CIA]] have used serving and retired special forces for illegal operations against foreign governments, and, in his view, possibly against the U.S. government under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].
