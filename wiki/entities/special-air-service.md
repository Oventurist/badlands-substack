# Special Air Service

The Special Air Service (SAS) is the United Kingdom's elite special forces regiment. In April 2026 it became the subject of Badlands coverage after dozens of troops from 22 SAS began resigning en masse amid a human rights investigation into alleged war crimes.[1]

## The 2026 resignations

The investigation covers alleged war crimes during operations in Afghanistan between 2010 and 2013, as well as in Syria and Northern Ireland.[1] At least two squadrons — D and G — were heavily affected, with soldiers filing for premature voluntary release over fears of prosecution.[1] The allegations include extrajudicial killings of unarmed civilians and detainees during night raids, the planting of weapons on bodies, and systematic cover-ups by senior officers.[1]

A memo shared with SAS associations revealed that lawyers were pursuing 242 special forces personnel, including 120 still serving, with potential legal sanctions for non-compliance.[1]

## Origins of the inquiry

The resignations followed a 2022 BBC Panorama investigation that identified 54 suspicious deaths caused by a single SAS unit during one six-month tour in Helmand province, alleging that unarmed Afghan men were routinely shot in cold blood and evidence falsified.[1] A later public inquiry heard testimony that the director of UK Special Forces made a "conscious decision" to conceal potential wrongdoing, while the main military probe, [[operation-northmoor|Operation Northmoor]] (2014–2019), was closed "inappropriately and prematurely" despite serious concerns.[1]

Insiders described "considerable disquiet," plummeting morale, and a perceived "witch hunt," with one source telling media that "morale is s**t at the moment."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued for distinguishing collateral damage in combat from politically motivated targeted violence, acknowledging grey areas where civilians take up arms, and cautioning that elite training is not a cure for sociopathy.[1] He contended that accountability should extend beyond the soldiers on the ground, noting that the CIA and other groups within government have used serving and retired special forces to conduct illegal operations against foreign governments and possibly against the U.S. government under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]
