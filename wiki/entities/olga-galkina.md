# Olga Galkina

Olga Galkina is a contact of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] identified during the FBI's examination of the sourcing of the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]], and a focus of internal FBI investigative work described at Danchenko's 2022 trial.[1]

## FBI scrutiny

FBI intelligence analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]], assigned to the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel investigation]] in 2017, compiled a case file on Galkina using information drawn largely from FBI databases and internal holdings.[1] The material she had developed on [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles "Chuck" Dolan Jr.]] was included in the same file, and Hertzog filed — "serialized" — the electronic communication in three separate places (the Washington Field Office, FBI Headquarters, and the Office of the Inspector General) in the hope that someone would take notice and open proper investigative steps on both Galkina and Dolan.[1] This occurred even after the Special Counsel's Office had instructed the team not to take further action on the Dolan–Danchenko relationship.[1]

Supervisory Special Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] likewise pursued Galkina and Dolan; her testimony describing what Galkina had told her about Dolan was struck as hearsay after a defense objection and sidebar.[1] At trial, phone records analysis by Special Agent Ryan James distinguished calls to Danchenko from known contacts such as Galkina, Dolan, and [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] from telemarketing and "unavailable" numbers.[1] The Badlands author suggested that Anderson and Hertzog had each identified a significant portion of the alleged criminal conspiracy early on but were met with unresponsive superiors.[1]

## Background and role as "Source 3"

Galkina had known Danchenko since the two attended school together in Perm.[2] In 2016 she was working in Cyprus for [[xbt-holdings|XBT Holdings SA]], the web-services company that operated Webzilla internet hosting and that is owned by Russian entrepreneur [[aleksej-gubarev|Aleksej Gubarev]], who is described in the dossier as a "hacking expert."[2] Galkina was the source for the dossier claim that Webzilla played a key role in the early-2016 hacking of the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]], and for the erroneous claim that [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] met Russian intelligence officials secretly in Prague in the summer of 2016 to arrange payment of the hackers and cover up the operation.[2] Everyone implicated denied the allegations and the Mueller Special Counsel found no evidence for them.[2] Within the dossier's sourcing chain Galkina became known as "Source 3," feeding her claims to Danchenko, who passed them to [[christopher-steele|Steele]].[2]

[[john-durham|Durham]] began introducing Galkina to the jury at the end of his direct examination of [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] on the second day of trial, but could not develop the subject far: although Auten had travelled to Cyprus to interview Galkina, he was not present when questions about her were put to Danchenko, those having been posed later by Danchenko's handling agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]].[2]

## Day three testimony

Galkina featured prominently in the third day of the Danchenko trial. Exhibits established that she met with Danchenko and [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] in Washington, D.C. in March 2016, and that she viewed [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] very favourably.[3] In the pseudonymous scheme used on pages 11 and 12 of the Danchenko indictment she is "Russian Sub-Source-1," alongside "Business-1" (XBT Holdings/Service.com), "Country-1" (Russia), "PR Firm-1" ([[kglobal|kglobal]]) and "PR Executive-1" (Dolan).[3]

Dolan testified that he remained in contact with Galkina through 2016, planning a trip to Cyprus to meet her and to make a presentation to her boss, and that kglobal and service.com eventually signed a contract to work together.[3] Screenshots of conversations about Dolan's business pitch to Galkina were moved into evidence during the testimony of [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]], as were Facebook messages between Galkina and Danchenko that Helson had never seen before trial preparation — indicating he had never obtained Danchenko's Facebook records.[3] Asked by [[john-durham|Durham]] about Galkina, Helson largely responded that he did not recall or that he would have liked to have known or understood the material.[3]
