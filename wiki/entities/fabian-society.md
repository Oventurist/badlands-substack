# Fabian Society

The Fabian Society is a British organization which, according to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], has worked alongside the [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholarship]] program for over a century in advancing a re-organized British Empire.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Canadian branch

A self-professed "Fabian Society of Canada" was set up at the height of the Depression by five Rhodes Scholars, with the stated aim of creating a Canadian fascist regime in 1932.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) This organization, known as the [[league-for-social-reconstruction|League of Social Reconstruction]], founded a political party called the Cooperative Commonwealth Federation (CCF), which changed its name to the New Democratic Party (NDP) in 1961.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Ehret notes that while good people have found themselves members of the NDP and the Liberals over the years, the rotten core of this lineage, tied to the highest echelons of the British oligarchy, is real.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## Claimed continuity with the Illuminati

In "Legacy of the Bavarian Illuminati – Part 2," the Masonic researcher [[william-stuart|William Stuart]] is cited as claiming that after the suppression of the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]] in 1787 there have been multiple incarnations of the same agenda, with the Fabian Society and the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg Group]] named among the more recent examples.[2]

## Doctrine as described in the Gladio primer

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] dates the formalization of a common elite way of thinking to the creation of the Fabian Society in the late 1800s.[3] In her account the Society held that the majority of the world's population were incapable of managing themselves and that a body of informed leaders — mostly Oxford graduates — was more worthy of the task, and that the world should be governed by a single unified structure under their direction.[3]

She describes their envisioned map as a grouping of territories: pan-America led by the United States, pan-Europe led by Germany, pan-Britain led by the UK, and pan-Asia led by Japan.[3] Russia was absent from that list because the plan divided it at the Ural Mountains, assigning the western portion to pan-Europe and the eastern portion to pan-Asia.[3] The Middle East and Africa were likewise excluded from apportionment because many original members regarded their populations as incapable of self-government and favoured their remaining perpetual sources of slave labour for the enrichment of other communities.[3]

Watkins argues that "racist" is too tame a description of this belief system, since the Society favoured [[eugenics|eugenics]] — the selective killing or euthanasia of unproductive people — and produced figures such as [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]] of Planned Parenthood, whose work she casts as culling those the Society deemed inferior so that only strong members would reproduce and investments would be maximized.[3]

## Three world wars and the Strategy of Tension

According to the primer, the Society's dilemma was how to reorder the world unnoticed, and its answer was to envision three world wars that would redistrict populations into controllable groupings without the public grasping how or why.[3] Watkins says they test-drove favoured models of government by broadcasting them through figures such as [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]], [[karl-marx|Karl Marx]], [[benito-mussolini|Mussolini]] and Franco.[3] Citing [[antony-sutton|Antony Sutton]], she states the same group funded the Bolshevik revolution as well as Hitler and [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]], and notes that FDR's National Recovery Act — which she says would have brought socialism to the United States — was repealed by the Supreme Court, leaving him with the New Deal as "socialism lite."[3]

The Fabian concept of manufacturing chaos to impose control is, in Watkins's framing, the [[strategy-of-tension|Strategy of Tension]] at the core of [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]], and many Society members were involved in colonizing Africa for its gold, diamonds and other resources.[3]
