# Fleury Mesplet

Fleury Mesplet was a French republican printer recruited by [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] and identified in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin" as the founder of Montreal's *Gazette*.[1]

Franklin recruited him, the essay says, in order to help counteract the destructive effects the French feudal system had exerted on the cognitive powers of the Quebec colonists, whose rampant illiteracy dovetailed with their non-existent appetite for representative government or freedom.[1] In that feudal culture, blind obedience to authority — whether political or religious — was seen as preferable to thinking for oneself.[1]

Mesplet's paper formed part of Franklin's wider effort in [[canada|Canada]], which also included the creation of the first postal service there in 1753.[1] Despite these cultural milestones and Franklin's active diplomacy to persuade the Quebecois to become a fourteenth member of the united colonies, the mission failed amid bribes, acts of treason and short-sighted thinking, and the Quebecois chose submission to the Crown.[1]
