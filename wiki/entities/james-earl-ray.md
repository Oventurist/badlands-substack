# James Earl Ray

James Earl Ray was the man convicted in the official account of the 1968 assassination of Dr. [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] In the Badlands Media essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that Ray was not the shooter but a designated fall guy, and cites a civil jury verdict to that effect.[1]

## The 1999 Memphis civil verdict

After four weeks of testimony and more than seventy witnesses in a civil trial in [[memphis|Memphis]], Tennessee, twelve jurors reached a unanimous verdict on 8 December 1999 that King was assassinated as the result of a conspiracy.[1] The defendant, Lloyd Jowers, named Memphis police officer Lt. Earl Clark as King's assassin.[1] Jowers testified that a Memphis produce dealer who was involved with the Mafia gave him $100,000 to hire an assassin, and assured him that police would not be at the scene of the shooting.[1]

Following the verdict, King's widow stated that the jury "was clearly convinced by the extensive evidence that was presented during the trial that, in addition to Mr. Jowers, the conspiracy of the Mafia, local, state and federal government agencies, were deeply involved in the assassination of my husband," and that the jury "also affirmed overwhelming evidence that identified someone else, not James Earl Ray, as the shooter, and that Mr. Ray was set up to take the blame."[1]

## Alleged intelligence connections

Carlson accepts the verdict's conclusion that Ray was a patsy and then asks how Ray was connected to the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]].[1] The article states that Ray, a military policeman, and CIA agents had connections to another racial shooting in Germany, and that Ray accepted a sum of money from mobsters and CIA agents in Illinois and Canada to carry out other assignments.[1] It further alleges that since 1968 the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] has buried all connections between Ray and the CIA.[1]

Ray's case is used in the article to extend its central pattern beyond the Kennedy killings: the accused party has a prior operational history with the agencies, and that history is subsequently suppressed rather than examined.[1]
