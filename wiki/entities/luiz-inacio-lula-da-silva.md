# Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva

Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva is the President of [[brazil|Brazil]] and a leading figure in the [[brics|BRICS]] bloc. In January 2025 he placed a phone call to Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] to discuss strengthening bilateral ties between Brazil and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[1]

## January 2025 call with Mohammed bin Salman

According to the Saudi Press Agency, the two leaders reviewed the strong relations between their countries and emphasized a commitment to further developing cooperation across various fields.[1] The discussion also covered a range of topics of mutual interest, with both leaders reaffirming a shared vision for addressing global and regional challenges and a dedication to collaboration that benefits their nations and contributes to global progress.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied the call to Saudi Arabia's successful pilot program to extract lithium from oil-field brine, funded by [[saudi-aramco|Aramco]] and the national mining company [[maaden|Ma'aden]], noting that Brazil was the ninth largest oil supplier in the world in 2022.[1] He observed that Saudi Arabia, invited to join BRICS, had elected to delay entry for unspecified reasons, speculating this was a favor to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] intended to keep the US dollar propped up.[1] If the Saudis share their lithium extraction technology with countries such as Brazil, he argued, many new suppliers could enter the lithium market, preventing another energy oligarchy on the model of OPEC from dominating the world economy — a leveling of the playing field few thought possible.[1]

## Response to US visa sanctions (July 2025)

In a statement issued on Saturday, July 19, 2025, Lula condemned the U.S. decision to impose visa bans on officials involved in the trial of former President [[jair-bolsonaro|Jair Bolsonaro]] as "arbitrary" and "baseless," arguing that the action violated fundamental principles of respect and sovereignty between nations and that foreign interference in the judiciary was "unacceptable."[2] The sanctions, announced the previous day, targeted Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]], his family, and other unnamed court officials, and came amid President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threat of 50 percent tariffs on Brazilian goods from August 1.[2] "I am certain that no form of intimidation or threat, from anyone, will compromise the most important mission of Brazil's powers and institutions, which is to permanently defend and uphold the democratic rule of law," Lula said.[2]

## Call with Trump on sanctions and trade (December 2025)

On Tuesday, December 2, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters at the White House that he had spoken by telephone with Lula about the sanctions the United States had imposed on Brazilian officials, a signal that relations between the two countries were continuing to improve.[3] "We had a great talk. We talked about trade. We talked about sanctions, because, as you know, I sanctioned them having to do with certain things that took place. But we had a very good talk. I like him," Trump said.[3] Earlier in 2025 Trump had sanctioned Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]], his wife and an institute affiliated with him, in retaliation for the prosecution of former President [[jair-bolsonaro|Jair Bolsonaro]] over his role in a failed coup attempt.[3] Lula had gradually won Trump over after ties reached a low point during the Bolsonaro probe.[3]

In a statement posted afterward, Trump said the two had discussed trade, cooperation against organized crime, the sanctions imposed on Brazilian dignitaries and tariffs, adding that the relationship established at a meeting at the United Nations "set the stage for very good dialogue and agreement long into the future" and that "much good will come out of this newly formed partnership."[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] quipped that "Brazilian Joe Biden sounds like Our Boy."[3]

## Call for a BRICS trade currency (April 2023)

On April 13, 2023, Lula called on [[brics|BRICS]] nations to devise an alternative to the dollar in foreign trade, backing China's push against U.S. global economic dominance just as he prepared to meet Chinese president [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] in Beijing.[4] He made the remarks during a visit to the Shanghai-based [[new-development-bank|New Development Bank]], the BRICS-created institution then newly headed by former Brazilian president [[dilma-rousseff|Dilma Rousseff]].[4]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the episode as part of a slow-then-sudden paradigm shift in which the BRICS economies were displacing the [[g7|G7]], describing the transition as a planned demolition of the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] and the globalist West.[4]


## Refusal of tariff talks with Trump (August 2025)

As US tariffs on Brazilian goods jumped to 50% on Wednesday, August 6, 2025, Lula told Reuters he saw no room for direct talks with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which he said would likely amount to a "humiliation."[5] He stated that Brazil would not announce reciprocal tariffs and would not abandon cabinet-level negotiations, but that he personally was in no rush to call the White House.[5] "The day my intuition says Trump is ready to talk, I won't hesitate to call him," he said from his presidential residence in Brasilia. "But today my intuition says he doesn't want to talk. And I won't humiliate myself."[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the posture was itself humiliating, criticizing Lula for accepting a 50% tariff without negotiating and for moving to prosecute Bolsonaro over his own outreach to Trump.[5] He framed the episode as a demonstration that the United States does not need any particular trading partner, summarizing the MAGA message to the world as "We don't need you. You need us."[5]
