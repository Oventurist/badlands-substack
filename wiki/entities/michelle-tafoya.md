# Michelle Tafoya

**Michelle Tafoya** is a former ESPN correspondent and NBC sportscaster who has publicly criticized [[cancel-culture|cancel culture]] and urged Americans to speak out against pressures to remain silent.[1]

## Commentary on speech and courage

In an interview cited by Badlands Media, Tafoya said: "My message is to people: Please be courageous. Speak out. I believe that the majority of the country believes in the goodness of us, in the goodness of Americans—the American people. But they've been told to shut up."[1]

Her remarks were invoked in support of the argument that athletes and public figures who break with prevailing orthodoxy — such as [[ivan-provorov|Ivan Provorov]], [[aaron-rodgers|Aaron Rodgers]], [[kyrie-irving|Kyrie Irving]] and [[novak-djokovic|Novak Djokovic]] — need visible public backing so that others will follow, in an atmosphere where many are "manipulated into toeing a corporate line for fear of losing their careers and/or reputations."[1]

## Claim of hidden allies in sport

The Badlands essay "Sports in Society" cites Tafoya's remarks on a recent podcast for the proposition that "we have far more on our side in the sports world than we can imagine."[2] Her comments are used to support the author's contention that athletes themselves are tired of the saturation of cheating, doping, officiating and gambling scandals and of politicized messaging in sport.[2]
