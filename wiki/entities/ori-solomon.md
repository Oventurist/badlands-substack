# Ori Solomon

Ori Solomon is an Israeli citizen residing in the United States on a non-immigrant visa who served as the property manager for the east [[las-vegas-biolab|Las Vegas rental home]] where an illegal biological laboratory was discovered in late January and early February 2026.[1]

## Arrest and charges

Solomon was arrested in February 2026 after the [[fbi|FBI]] and local police searched the property and found lab equipment, test kits, vaccines, influenza samples and other biological materials stored in a locked garage.[1] Because he held a nonimmigrant visa, his possession of six firearms at the time of arrest was unlawful, and federal prosecutors charged him with illegal firearm possession.[1]

Solomon had hired the house cleaner and permitted others to live at the property; multiple people with access to the home became "deathly ill" after exposure to the specimens, and the cleaner described the house as reeking of "foul stagnant hospital air."[1] He is reported to have managed 37 properties in the Las Vegas area.[1]

## Release and dismissal

In February 2026 a federal magistrate judge, [[elayna-youchah|Elayna J. Youchah]], released Solomon pending trial, citing no concern that he would fail to return.[1] In May 2026 federal prosecutors under First Assistant U.S. Attorney [[sigal-chattah|Sigal Chattah]] dismissed the firearms complaint without prejudice, citing the interests of justice after reviewing additional information.[1]

Solomon still faces a pending Clark County state charge for improper disposal of hazardous waste, with a status hearing set for June 4.[1]

## Commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] emphasized that both the releasing judge and the dismissing prosecutor are Jewish and that Chattah was born in [[israel|Israel]], making her a dual citizen, and argued that the outcome — given Solomon's Israeli citizenship and Israel's reputation for blocking U.S. extradition requests — looked like "the control of the US justice system for the benefit of a foreign country."[1]

The property was owned through an LLC by Chinese national [[jia-bei-zhu|Jia Bei Zhu]], who hired Solomon to manage it and who was separately convicted over the [[universal-meditech|Universal Meditech]] operation in California.[1]

## Additional reporting (February 2026)

Reporting by *The Washington Times*, citing court records, described Solomon as the manager of a Las Vegas short-term rental property where officials discovered what they characterized as a possible unlicensed biological laboratory.[2] He faces Nevada state felony charges related to hazardous waste disposal and a federal firearms charge alleging unlawful possession as a non-immigrant visa holder, but he has not been charged with operating a biolab or with handling biological agents.[2] Investigators seized laboratory equipment and more than 1,000 samples of unknown liquids, and Solomon was released pending trial.[2]

The investigation is examining links to Chinese nationals, including the property owner, who has been connected in reporting to a prior illegal biolab case in Reedley, California.[2] Solomon holds Israeli and French passports and was in the United States on an E-2 visa, a non-immigrant visa for foreign investors and key employees from countries with a qualifying treaty with the United States.[2]
