# Ori Solomon

**Ori Solomon** is an Israeli citizen residing in the United States on a non-immigrant visa who worked as a property manager and became the central defendant in the [[las-vegas-biolab|Las Vegas biolab case]] of 2026.

## Arrest and charges

According to the Badlands Brief of May 27, 2026, Solomon was arrested in February 2026 after the [[fbi|FBI]] and local police searched an east Las Vegas rental property he managed and found lab equipment, test kits, vaccines, influenza samples and other biological materials stored in a locked garage. Because of his nonimmigrant visa status, his possession of six firearms at the time of arrest was unlawful, and he was charged federally with illegal firearm possession.

Commentary in the source states that Solomon had hired the house cleaner and allowed others to live at the property, that multiple people with access became "deathly ill" after exposure to the specimens, and that he managed 37 properties in the [[nevada|Las Vegas]] area. The property itself was owned through an LLC by Chinese national [[jia-bei-zhu|Jia Bei Zhu]], who hired Solomon to manage it.

## Release and dismissal

A state magistrate judge, [[elayna-youchah|Elayna J. Youchah]], released Solomon from custody pending trial in February 2026, citing no concern that he would fail to return for trial. In May 2026 federal prosecutors — the article names First Assistant U.S. Attorney [[sigal-chattah|Sigal Chattah]] — dismissed the firearm complaint without prejudice, citing the interests of justice after reviewing additional information. Solomon still faced a pending Clark County charge for improper disposal of hazardous waste, with a status hearing set for June 4.

## Contested framing

The Badlands commentary by [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treats the dismissal and pretrial release as evidence of foreign influence over the American justice system, emphasizing Solomon's Israeli citizenship and the asserted Jewish and Israeli backgrounds of the judge and prosecutor. That interpretation is the source's opinion, not an established finding.


## Charges and pretrial release

The February 10, 2026 [[badlands-brief-4ec|Badlands Brief]], citing *The Washington Times* and court records, restated that Solomon — an Israeli national who managed the Las Vegas short-term rental property where officials found what they described as a possible unlicensed biological laboratory — faces Nevada state felony charges related to hazardous waste disposal and a federal firearms charge alleging unlawful possession as a non-immigrant visa holder. He has not been charged with operating a biolab or with handling biological agents. Investigators seized laboratory equipment and more than 1,000 samples of unknown liquids, and Solomon was released pending trial. He holds Israeli and French passports and was in the United States on an E-2 visa, a non-immigrant category for foreign investors and key employees from treaty countries. The investigation is examining links to Chinese nationals, including the property owner, who reporting has connected to a prior illegal biolab case in Reedley, California.
