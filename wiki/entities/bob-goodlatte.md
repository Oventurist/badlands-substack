# Bob Goodlatte

Bob Goodlatte is a former Republican congressman from Virginia who chaired the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]]. The Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 2 presents him, alongside [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]], as one of the principal congressional investigators of alleged [[barack-obama|Obama]]-era corruption and as a key figure in what the author calls the coming prosecution phase.

## The DOJ settlement "slush fund"

*Forbes* reported in October 2017 that internal [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] documents confirmed the existence of a departmental "slush fund" under the Obama administration and that DOJ officials "went out of their way" to exclude conservative groups — a claim Goodlatte made to fellow lawmakers ahead of a House vote on legislation prohibiting settlement agreements that direct payments to entities other than the United States. Goodlatte said the legislation was needed because an extended Judiciary Committee investigation had found the DOJ systematically subverting Congress's budget authority by using settlements with financial institutions to funnel money to "left-wing activist groups."

Hot Air quoted Goodlatte on the House floor: "It is not every day in congressional investigations that we find a smoking gun." The emails he released, in that account, showed that only approved left-wing groups were eligible to receive payouts from bank settlements. His committee found that over two years the Obama DOJ directed approximately $1 billion to outside organizations, and in January had requested the DOJ preserve all related documents. See [[doj-slush-fund|DOJ settlement slush fund]].

## Classified briefings and resignation

Citing Q drop 2462 — "Who briefed GOODLATTE & Gowdy on classified intel re: DOJ & FBI?" — the article identifies [[ezra-cohen-watnick|Ezra Cohen Watnick]] as the source of the classified intelligence provided to Goodlatte and Gowdy, and claims [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] installed Watnick at the DOJ for that purpose. It notes that Goodlatte, Gowdy and Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] all resigned around the same time, argues this was coordinated rather than coincidental, and asserts that Goodlatte remains active in background investigations.

The essay further states that Sessions launched the [[john-huber|Huber]] investigation in response to a request from Goodlatte, Gowdy and Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]], and that after concluding their House inquiries Goodlatte and Gowdy handed their evidence to Grassley in the Senate. These claims about ongoing secret investigations and sealed indictments are contested.
