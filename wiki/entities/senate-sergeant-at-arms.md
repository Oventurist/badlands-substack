# Senate Sergeant at Arms

The Senate Sergeant at Arms is the chief law-enforcement and protocol officer of the [[us-senate|United States Senate]], responsible among other duties for the physical security of senators and for continuity of Senate operations.[1] The office took on heightened prominence after the assault on the U.S. Capitol on January 6, 2021, following which new leadership assumed the post.[1]

## 2023 satellite phone program

In May 2023, amid growing concern over security risks to members of [[congress|Congress]], the office was reported to have issued satellite phones for emergency communication to more than 50 senators as part of a series of new security measures offered to members.[1] The technology was offered to all 100 senators, with at least 50 accepting; Senate administrative staff recommended that recipients keep the devices in close proximity while traveling.[1] Federal funding was designated to pay for the satellite airtime required to use the devices.[1]

Sergeant at Arms [[karen-gibson|Karen Gibson]] told the [[senate-appropriations-committee|Senate Appropriations Committee]] in April 2023 that satellite communication was being deployed "to ensure a redundant and secure means of communication during a disruptive event," and described the phones as a backstop for an emergency that "takes out communications" in part of America.[1]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] treated the program as a signal worth interrogating rather than a routine upgrade, asking whether nothing had been in place before, what triggered the ramp-up in redundancy, whether the change carried significance for [[devolution|devolution]] or an ongoing global sting operation, who accepted the phones, and what inferences could be drawn from that list.[1] He noted that [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] had predicted a cyber attack capable of taking down the power grid — an event that would help usher in the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] — and asked whether the politicians who accepted the phones knew something.[1]
