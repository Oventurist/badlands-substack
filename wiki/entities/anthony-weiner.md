# Anthony Weiner

Anthony Weiner is a former Democratic congressman from New York whose name became attached to the controversy over [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s handling of classified material through his connection to Clinton aide Huma Abedin and the discovery of Clinton-related emails on his laptop.[1]

## Reference in the Trump Bedminster recording

Weiner was invoked in the July 2021 Bedminster, New Jersey audio recording of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] discussing classified documents, which [[cnn|CNN]] obtained and aired in June 2023.[1] After Trump described a document as "secret information," a staffer joked that "Hillary would print that out all the time, you know. Her private emails," to which Trump responded, "No, she'd send it to Anthony Weiner," prompting laughter in the room.[1]

The exchange formed part of a two-minute recording that prosecutors treated as evidence in special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s indictment of Trump over the handling of classified information.[1]

## Invoked in 2024 SDNY commentary

After the September 2024 indictment of [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]] in the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]], Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the same district had convicted and sentenced Weiner, whose laptop — the subject of the "Weiner Laptop" controversy — was alleged to have contained an "insurance folder" featuring prominent Deep State figures, a claim he said both General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] and [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]] had corroborated.[2]

## The laptop and Hillary Clinton's emails (Badlands account)

The August 2023 Badlands article "Clowns in America" treats the evidence found on Weiner's laptop as the mechanism by which the Clinton email matter could be reopened in the future, noting that [[qanon|Q]] drop 1981 placed [Weiner][Huma][HRC] in targeted brackets and arguing that the material exposes not only [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] but everyone around her.[3] Writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] recounted the common explanation that Weiner had kept the material as an insurance policy out of fear of Clinton, or as a bargaining chip against prosecution.[3]

Citing the Daily Wire, Badlands noted that within hours of the September 26, 2016 search warrant FBI technicians found 340,000 emails on the laptop between Clinton and her top aide — Weiner's wife [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] — many from domains such as hillaryclinton.com and state.gov; one participant at an FBI briefing that week said the revelation was like "dropping a bomb in the middle of the meeting," though the discovery fell through the cracks because senior officials were overwhelmed by the Russia probe.[3] The find forced FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] to reopen the Clinton investigation weeks before the 2016 election, torpedoing her campaign.[3]

Lange speculated that Weiner had been leveraged by military intelligence long before the laptop became public, given knowledge of his crimes involving minors, and that he downloaded the emails in order to cut a deal.[3] He pointed to a 2015 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] tweet as prescient and to Trump's August 2016 statement on the Abedin separation — "I know Anthony Weiner well," and Clinton was "careless and negligent in allowing Weiner to have such close proximity to highly classified information. Who knows what he learned and who he told?" — as deliberate trolling of the enemy.[3] Because the physical evidence survived on the laptop, Lange argued, Clinton's deletion of emails and destruction of her BlackBerry phones did not matter.[3]

## The unreviewed email trove

In "Disinformation is Necessary," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] cites Real Clear Investigations reporting that FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], in closing the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] email investigation for a second time days before the 2016 election, certified to Congress that his agency had "reviewed all of the communications" discovered on the personal laptop used by Weiner and his wife [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] — an account the outlet said was virtually untrue.[4] A career FBI special agent involved in the case complained to New York colleagues that Washington officials tried to "bury" the trove, which he believed contained the full archive of Clinton's emails including missing messages from her first months at the State Department.[4]

Lange argues that because that period covered the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] deal, the laptop held material exposing [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]'s role in the cover-up, giving Comey reason to protect his longtime friend as well as Clinton.[4] The Weiner/Abedin laptop was among the investigations overseen by the [[southern-district-of-new-york|SDNY]], where [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]]'s law-enforcement relationships are said to have given him access to far more than was disclosed publicly.[4] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
