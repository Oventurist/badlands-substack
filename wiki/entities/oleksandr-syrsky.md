# Oleksandr Syrsky

Oleksandr Syrsky is the commander-in-chief of Ukraine's armed forces.[1]

## Kursk counteroffensive claims

On December 17, 2024, Syrsky said Russian forces had spent three consecutive days conducting intense offensive operations in the Kursk region, "actively using North Korean army units," as Moscow sought to push Ukrainian troops back across the border.[1] Ukrainian forces had launched an incursion into the border region in early August, seizing large swaths of territory.[1] Syrsky claimed that "North Korean mercenaries have already suffered heavy losses" while Ukrainian defenders held the line, though he provided no further details.[1]

Ukrainian military intelligence, the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and South Korean authorities have all stated that Pyongyang deployed roughly 10,000 troops to Russia, with Western officials believing the deployment was exchanged for shipments of air defense systems to North Korea.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the North Korean troop narrative as a likely psyop given the volume and inconsistency of Western coverage, while noting reports from the Wall Street Journal that a North Korean general had been killed.[1] The commentary framed the deployment as the payoff of the earlier [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]–[[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] rapprochement.[1]

## Withdrawal from Avdiivka

Shortly after midnight on Saturday, February 17, 2024, Syrsky — newly installed as Ukraine's top commander after [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] fired his predecessor — announced that he had ordered troops to evacuate the eastern city of [[avdiivka|Avdiivka]].[2] Ukrainian soldiers there were surrounded on three sides by Russian forces in what was described as a rapidly tightening noose.[2]

"Based on the operational situation around Avdiivka, in order to avoid encirclement and preserve the lives and health of servicemen, I decided to withdraw our units from the city and move to defense on more favorable lines," Syrsky said in a Facebook post.[2] The loss of the city represented Russia's most significant battlefield gain since the capture of Bakhmut in May 2023.[2]
