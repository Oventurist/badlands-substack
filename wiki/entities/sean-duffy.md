# Sean Duffy

Sean Duffy is the United States Secretary of Transportation in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, with cabinet-level responsibility for the [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]].[1]

## El Paso drone incursion announcement (February 2026)

Duffy served as the administration's public voice on the February 2026 cartel drone incursion near El Paso, Texas.[1] After the FAA abruptly closed airspace above the city up to 18,000 feet, Duffy posted on X: "The FAA and DOW acted swiftly to address a cartel drone incursion. The threat has been neutralized, and there is no danger to commercial travel in the region. The restrictions have been lifted and normal flights are resuming."[1]

The statement confirmed a joint FAA and [[department-of-war|Department of War]] operation against drones linked to Mexican cartels, though neither Duffy nor the department specified the counter-drone technology employed.[1]

## Commercial driver's license crackdown

As Transportation Secretary, Duffy led a federal crackdown on [[commercial-drivers-licenses-for-immigrants|commercial driver's licenses issued to immigrants]] that by December 2025 had found problems in eight states following several deadly crashes.[2] He publicly threatened to withhold millions in federal money from California, Pennsylvania, Minnesota and New York after investigations found licenses that remained valid long after an immigrant's legal status had expired, and his department quietly sent letters detailing similar concerns to Texas, South Dakota, Colorado and Washington during the government shutdown.[2] Duffy proposed new restrictions in September 2025 that would severely limit which noncitizens could obtain a license to drive a semi or a bus, but a court placed the rules on hold.[2]

## Comments on Key Bridge rebuild costs (2025)

As Transportation Secretary, Duffy responded to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s suggestion that federal funding for the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Francis Scott Key Bridge]] rebuild might be reconsidered by saying he was concerned about the federal government covering all of the bridge's rebuilding costs.[3] He explained the concern with an analogy: "if you go to dinner and someone else is buying, you might order the finest, most expensive steak on the menu."[3] The remarks came as estimates for the Baltimore rebuild rose above $5 billion.[3]
