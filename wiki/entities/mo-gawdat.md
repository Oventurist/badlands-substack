# Mo Gawdat

Mo Gawdat is an Egyptian-born technologist and author, formerly Chief Business Officer of [[google-x|Google X]], the clandestine research-and-development arm of Google. He is the most extensively quoted individual in the July 2023 Badlands Media article "[[artificial-intelligence|Artificial Intelligence: Hysteria vs. Reason]]," where he serves as the article's principal exponent of the view that the AI [[technological-singularity|singularity]] has already arrived.

## The 2018 interview

In a 2018 interview with Michael Sugarman and Thierry Daher, Gawdat argued that attempts to control artificial intelligence through programming or regulation would ultimately fail:

> "Some of us believe that we can control what those artificially intelligent machines are going to be doing by programming them differently or by regulating them in a highly regulated government environment or whatever that is. Yeah, a bit of that is needed and of course there are efforts to do all of that, but that's not going to deliver the promise because there will be a point in singularity where those machines will decide for their own benefit what it is that they need to learn next. When we reach that moment, it will be too late. We will have come to the point where those machines are already smarter than we are."

In the same interview he described the upside in expansive terms: "AI is the most amazing thing we've ever created. 30 years from now, we will have intelligence that's a billion times superior to this, that has access to all of the knowledge we'll ever develop, that can connect across the world. Imagine what you can do with this … Truly, you can understand quantum physics at a much deeper level. You can understand how you can sustain the environment in so many unprecedented, unexpected ways. It's just incredible."

## "The genie is out of the bottle"

Five years later, the article reports, Gawdat believes the singularity point has been passed. "The truth is, the genie is out of the bottle," he says in a later interview. His prescription is societal restructuring rather than containment: "Within the next year to 10 years, those who have a symbiotic relationship with AI will actually become more successful than those who don't. We will need to reset our social norms, our economic norms, and our jobs. If we don't do that, the current systems will not work."

On the question of control, Gawdat offers a parenting analogy:

> "How do we contain them? We don't contain them at all. You never contain your children. If you lock your children in a room, that's not the best way to educate them at all. The best way to raise wonderful children is to be a wonderful parent. It is to live the value system that you want your children to become. You can tell them not to lie but if you lie, they will lie. It's as simple as that. You can tell them to love everything around you but if you hate, they will hate."

The article also attributes to Gawdat a set of "three inevitables": that AI is happening, that AI is becoming far smarter than humans, and that really bad things will happen.

## Critical treatment

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarizes Gawdat's position as holding that "the only way AI doesn't end badly is for us to quickly solve the darkness of human nature," after which AI delivers a utopia — a formulation she calls "the starting premise of every communist dictator in history," where citizens are told to surrender liberty and abandon individualism in exchange for utopia, with the genocide left unmentioned. She credits Gawdat, however, for acknowledging a turbulent "teenage years" phase for artificial intelligence that, in her reading, "sounds a lot like revolution."

The article compares his rhetoric of resetting norms to Michelle Obama's 2008 campaign statement that "Barack knows that we are going to have to make sacrifices; we are going to have to change our conversation; we're going to have to change our traditions, our history; we're going to have to move into a different place as a nation." It also notes, as an aside on [[reality-collapse|reality collapse]], that Gawdat physically resembles the *Westworld* engineer Arnold Weber, played by Jeffrey Wright, and is "effectively asking humanity the same question now": "Have you ever questioned the nature of your reality?"
