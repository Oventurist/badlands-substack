# Mo Gawdat

Mo Gawdat is a technologist and author who served as Chief Business Officer for Google X, described in the Badlands corpus as Google's clandestine research-and-development arm.[1] He is the most extensively quoted figure in Badlands Media's essay on [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]], serving as the representative voice of the view that the [[technological-singularity|singularity]] is inevitable and containment impossible.[1]

## On the singularity

In a 2018 interview with Michael Sugarman and Thierry Daher, Gawdat argued that attempts to control intelligent machines through different programming or a highly regulated government environment would not deliver on their promise, because "there will be a point in singularity where those machines will decide for their own benefit what it is that they need to learn next. When we reach that moment, it will be too late. We will have come to the point where those machines are already smarter than we are."[1] Five years later Gawdat believed that point had already been passed, stating in a subsequent interview that "the genie is out of the bottle."[1]

## On social reset

Gawdat's prescribed response is a restructuring of society: "Within the next year to 10 years, those who have a symbiotic relationship with AI will actually become more successful than those who don't... We will need to reset our social norms, our economic norms, and our jobs. If we don't do that, the current systems will not work."[1] Badlands compares this rhetoric to Michelle Obama's 2008 campaign line about changing traditions and history and moving to a different place as a nation, arguing that such rhetoric preceded what it calls the greatest expansion of communism in American history.[1]

On containment, Gawdat used a parenting analogy: "We don't contain them at all. You never contain your children... The best way to raise wonderful children is to be a wonderful parent. It is to live the value system that you want your children to become."[1] The Badlands reading summarizes this as requiring humanity to quickly solve the darkness of human nature in exchange for utopia — the starting premise, it argues, of every communist dictator in history — while crediting Gawdat for candidly describing a tumultuous "teenage years" period with AI that sounds a lot like revolution.[1]

## Three inevitables and optimism

Gawdat's "three inevitables" are cited in the debate over the [[ai-pause-open-letter|proposed training pause]]: AI is happening; AI is becoming far smarter than humans; really bad things will happen.[1] He is nonetheless quoted optimistically from the 2018 interview: "AI is the most amazing thing we've ever created. 30 years from now, we will have intelligence that's a billion times superior to this, that has access to all of the knowledge we'll ever develop, that can connect across the world," enabling deeper understanding of quantum physics and unprecedented environmental sustainability.[1]

The article notes that Gawdat physically resembles *Westworld*'s chief engineer Arnold Weber, played by Jeffrey Wright, and is effectively asking humanity the same question about the nature of its reality — a nod to [[reality-collapse|reality collapse]].[1]
