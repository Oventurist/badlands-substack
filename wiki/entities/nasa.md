# NASA

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) is the U.S. civil space agency. In April 2026 it released the first images of Earth captured during the [[artemis-ii|Artemis II]] mission, showing the planet from lunar distance as part of the agency's return to crewed deep-space exploration.[1]

Artemis II is the first crewed mission to travel around the Moon since the Apollo program and is designed as a flyby rather than a landing, carrying four astronauts who test spacecraft systems during the journey.[1] NASA and its partners made mission tracking tools available to the public, allowing users to follow the spacecraft's trajectory and receive near real-time updates.[1] Officials described the mission as a critical step toward future lunar landing missions under the Artemis program.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the mission's redesigned space toilet had drawn more coverage than the flyby itself, questioning why a "historic lunar flyby" was being upstaged by hardware failures and why the mission does not include a landing and moonwalk.[1] The commentary treated the framing as evidence of a weak narrative around crewed lunar spaceflight, joking about a "wellness check" on the cameraman supposedly left behind in 1969.[1]

## Dependence on Starship for lunar landings

As of October 2025 NASA could not land astronauts on the Moon by the end of the decade without [[spacex|SpaceX]]'s 403-foot (123-meter) Starship, the reusable vehicle intended to carry crews from lunar orbit down to the surface and back up.[2] The agency's need was described as more immediate than that of SpaceX founder [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], whose stated goal for the vehicle is sending people to Mars; the program's eleventh full-scale test flight, [[starship-flight-11|Starship Flight 11]], launched from Texas on October 13, 2025.[2]

## Return of the extended ISS crew (March 2025)

Two NASA astronauts whose planned eight-day stopover on the International Space Station turned into a nine-and-a-half-month mission returned to Earth on Tuesday, March 18, 2025.[3] [[sunita-williams|Sunita Williams]] and [[butch-wilmore|Butch Wilmore]] splashed down at 5:57 pm EDT off the coast of Tallahassee, Florida, aboard the [[spacex|SpaceX]] Dragon capsule *Freedom*, together with NASA astronaut Nick Hague and Roscosmos cosmonaut Aleksandr Gorbunov.[3] The crew returned in 78 degree Fahrenheit weather, with dolphins circling the capsule before recovery teams brought it aboard ship and opened it.[3]

Badlands commentary used the intense public interest in the "Spaceman Splashdown" as a contrast with public indifference to election integrity questions, while Stormy Patriot Joe noted that the digits of the 5:57 pm splashdown time sum to 17.[3]

## 2024 YR4 lunar impact study (2025)

Several NASA scientists were among more than a dozen researchers who authored a 2025 paper examining what humanity should do if an asteroid were on a collision course with the Moon — nudge it aside or destroy it with a nuclear explosion.[4] The paper, uploaded to the preprint site arXiv on September 15, 2025 and not yet peer-reviewed, focused on [[2024-yr4|2024 YR4]], estimated to have a 4% chance of hitting the Moon in 2032.[4] The researchers warned such a collision could produce debris "up to 1,000 times above background levels over just a few days, possibly threatening astronauts and spacecraft" in low-Earth orbit, and discussed nuking the asteroid — a "robust disruption" — to avoid that debris field.[4]

## SpaceX Crew-11 launch coverage (July 2025)

NASA announced in July 2025 that it would provide coverage of prelaunch and launch activities for the agency's [[spacex|SpaceX]] Crew-11 mission to the International Space Station.[5] Liftoff was targeted for 12:09 p.m. EDT on Thursday, July 31, 2025, from Launch Complex 39A at NASA's Kennedy Space Center in Florida, with docking targeted for approximately 3 a.m. on Saturday, August 2.[5] The agency said launch coverage would be carried on NASA+, Netflix, Amazon Prime and other outlets.[5]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] offered a wry response to the announcement's accompanying imagery, joking that given the size of NASA's budget one would expect the agency to be able to design a spacesuit that did not make astronauts look like the Olympic fencing team.[5]
