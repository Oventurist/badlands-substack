# Neil Bush

Neil Bush is a son of [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and, in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], the family member who extracted the single most expensive sweetheart deal of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]] — one the Orlando Sentinel estimated would cost the public $1 billion.[1]

## Denver, JNB and Silverado

Bush set up his base of operations in Denver, Colorado, where two close friends who were real estate developers became his partners.[1] In 1983 he formed an energy company, JNB, with just $100 in cash; Denver real-estate developer [[william-walters|William Walters]] invested what Bush described as "a small amount" — $150,000, a sum equal to Bush's salary from the company for the next two years.[1] In 1985 Bush was made a director of the [[silverado-savings-and-loan-association|Silverado Banking, Savings and Loan Association]] of Denver.[1]

He then voted to approve $96 million in loans to Walters without telling the bank that Walters was his benefactor and that he owed Walters money — a conflict of interest Lange characterizes as a small gift to Neil Bush buying a $96 million loan.[1] Separately, in 1984 another Denver businessman, [[kenneth-good|Kenneth Good]], gave Bush $100,000 to invest as a loan; the investment failed and the loan was then "forgiven."[1] In 1986 Bush asked Silverado, of which he was a director, to approve a $900,000 line of credit for Good, and because he considered Good a business partner he abstained on votes approving $34 million in loans to him.[1] Between them the two partners received some $130 million in loans plus the line of credit.[1]

## Collapse and protection

Walters and Good defaulted on the Silverado loans for a total of $106 million.[1] In 1987 federal regulators in Topeka, Kansas accused Silverado of "self-dealing" and making preferential loans to favored customers including its own officers; the bank collapsed and the bailout of its federally insured deposits was expected to cost $1 billion.[1] Neil Bush insisted he had done nothing wrong, as did his father the president and [[marlin-fitzwater|Marlin Fitzwater]], the [[white-house|White House]] press spokesman.[1]

Lange notes that regulators purposely waited until after George H.W. Bush was elected president in 1988 to announce they were taking over the bankrupt thrift, and that the White House then gave clear marching orders to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Department of the Treasury]] and banking investigators everywhere: lay off Neil Bush.[1] Federal banking regulators who might have looked askance at Silverado saw the vice president's son, and knew the vice president chaired the [[task-force-on-regulatory-relief|task force]] [[deregulation|deregulating]] the banking industry.[1] As a director Bush could and did approve million-dollar salaries for the bank's officers, who could and did then recommend multimillion-dollar loans to Bush's friends, which Bush could and did vote to approve.[1]

Lange presents Neil alongside his brother [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] as evidence that both sons profited from a corrupt system their father built.[1]


## Kuwait and Jamal Daniel

"Profiting From War" quotes the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] noting that Neil Bush, best known for the scandal at Colorado's [[silverado-savings-and-loan-association|Silverado Savings & Loan]] where he served as a director during the 1980s, also "picked plums from Persian Gulf orchards."[2] In 1993, after his father left the White House, he travelled to [[kuwait|Kuwait]] with his parents, his brother [[marvin-bush|Marvin]] and former Secretary of State [[james-baker|James A. Baker III]]; when the rest of the party departed, Neil stayed on to lobby for business contracts.[2] After returning home he developed a set of lucrative relationships with the Syrian-American businessman [[jamal-daniel|Jamal Daniel]], who hired him and partnered with him in several ventures.[2] Daniel, whose Houston mansion sits in the home city of the [[bush-family|Bush]] dynasty, founded the news site [[al-monitor|Al-Monitor]] and bought a large stake in the Beirut daily [[as-safir|As-Safir]], outlets the article says gave pro-[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] journalists mainstream credibility.[2]

## Hinckley dinner and the 1981 assassination attempt

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" reports that the brother of would-be assassin [[john-hinckley-jr|John W. Hinckley]] had scheduled dinner with Neil Bush the very night [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] was shot in 1981 — a shooting that, but for a quarter of an inch, would have elevated his father, Trilateralist [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]], to the presidency seven years early.[3]
