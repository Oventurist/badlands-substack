# William Seward

William Seward was United States Secretary of State under [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and his successor, and the American principal in the 1867 [[alaska-purchase|purchase of Alaska]] from Russia.[1]

On 26 December 1864, as the Union's victory in the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] was becoming apparent, Seward encouraged [[grand-duke-constantine|Grand Duke Constantine]] to come to America, writing that a visit "would be beneficial to us, and by no means unprofitable to Russia," and pointedly declining to specify his reasons.[1]

On the evening of 29 March 1867, Russian Ambassador [[eduard-de-stoeckl|Eduard de Stoeckl]] brought Seward the news that the Czar had approved the sale of Alaska at his private residence in Washington, D.C.[1] Asked whether he wished to convene a meeting the following day, Seward replied, "why wait until tomorrow what could be done that very evening?"[1] At midnight the State Department offices were opened with a small group including Senator [[charles-sumner|Charles Sumner]], Seward, and trusted members of the Russian consulate alongside Stoeckl.[1] By sunrise on 30 March the treaty had been drafted, finalizing the sale for $7.2 million, and it was presented to a shocked Congress, which passed it within weeks.[1]

Seward, along with Sumner, later advocated that Britain surrender its Canadian possessions as payment in the [[alabama-claims|Alabama Claims]] arbitration; weaker figures in America instead accepted a $15 million settlement in 1872.[1]
