# Paul Kagame

Paul Kagame is the president of [[rwanda|Rwanda]].[1] Badlands commentary characterizes him as an ardent Zionist and Israel ally, and identifies Rwanda as the operating base for the [[m23|M23]] rebel movement fighting in the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]].[1]

In that account, M23's campaign around Goma and Bukavu in the North and South Kivu provinces was likely dispatched by the Israeli diamond cartel to reclaim mines lost after [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] was sanctioned under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]].[1] The fighting coincides with a starvation crisis affecting 25 million Congolese, concentrated in the same M23-controlled territory.[1]

## Role in the December 2025 Uvira offensive

The Congolese government attributed the December 2025 "generalized offensive" on the Kamanyola–[[uvira|Uvira]] axis jointly to Rwanda's Defense Forces and the [[m23|M23]] rebels, reporting more than 100 killed and over 200,000 newly displaced.[2] Badlands analysis held that the capture of Uvira would leave the entire North and South Kivu provinces under effective Rwandan control, making the traceability of rare earth minerals from the region impossible.[2] Under this account, ore mined with child slave labour in the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DRC]] is smuggled into Rwanda after refining and issued forged certificates attesting to clean sourcing before being sold to Fortune 500 companies including [[apple|Apple]].[2] Rwanda under President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] was characterized as a globalist proxy and ally of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], with [[burundi|Burundi]] and the DRC forming the regional opposition to it.[2]

## March 2025 collapse of the Luanda talks

In March 2025 Kagame's government was accused by Badlands commentary of using [[european-union|EU]] sanctions as a pretext to abandon peace negotiations over eastern Congo.[3] The [[alliance-fleuve-congo|Alliance Fleuve Congo]] rebel coalition, which includes [[m23|M23]], withdrew from talks scheduled in Angola after Brussels sanctioned five senior rebel leaders on the eve of the discussions.[3] Rwanda simultaneously cut diplomatic ties with its former colonial ruler Belgium, with Kigali accusing Brussels of attempting to "sustain its neo-colonial delusions."[3]

The Badlands analysis held that it is [[rwanda|Rwanda]] that funds the M23 forces seizing towns along the border, towns that host the processing plants for rare earth minerals mined in the region before refinement and sale to major corporations such as Apple, Sony and Lockheed Martin.[3] Control of those towns was described as a foothold on the minerals themselves, with unmined eastern DRC deposits estimated at over $24 trillion.[3] The commentary attributed the violence to Kagame's backing of M23 after the globalist "regime" lost its monopoly over the minerals when Trump froze the assets of Israeli oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] under Executive Order 13818 in December 2017.[3]

## Kabarebe sanctions and the February 2025 escalation

In February 2025 the United States sanctioned Gen. [[james-kabarebe|James Kabarebe]], a Rwandan deputy minister of foreign affairs, over violence in eastern Congo, where Rwanda-backed rebels had captured two major cities in a conflict that threatened the Congolese government itself.[4] Kagame's government asserted solidarity with Kabarebe, calling the sanctions "unjustified and unfounded" and arguing they made "no contribution toward long-term security, peace and stability for all the countries of the Great Lakes region."[4] Kigali maintained that securing the border with Congo was "a matter of national security, and that is our only driver."[4]

Badlands commentary described Kagame as "an ardent Zionist and loyal henchman to the Israeli oligarchy," speculating that he was working on behalf of Netanyahu and [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] when mercenaries descended on the eastern Congo from Rwanda.[4] The same analysis noted that Congolese President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]] had turned to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for assistance against the Rwandan mercenaries, offering American access to mineral rights in return.[4] The European Union, meanwhile, had agreed to pay Rwanda about $935 million for access to minerals including tin, tungsten and gold.[4]
