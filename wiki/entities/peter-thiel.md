# Peter Thiel

Peter Thiel is an American technology investor whose alleged influence over US government personnel is a recurring subject of debate within the Badlands audience.[1]

## The "Thiel Doomer" debate

Badlands contributor Ashe in America described a faction she calls "Thiel Doomers," who argue that Thiel is "shadow running our government through his vast network of insider placements."[1] Writing after the March 2026 [[presidents-council-of-advisors-on-science-and-technology|PCAST]] appointments, she said the list would give that faction a field day, classifying roughly half the appointees — [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]], Fred Ehrsam, [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], David Friedberg, and Jensen Huang — as Thiel-aligned and the remainder as not.[1]

She positioned herself as sympathetic but skeptical, noting that having read *1984*, *Brave New World*, and other tech-enabled dystopian works, she recognizes "all the classic earmarks of doomer's delight," while also conceding that the apparent celebration of a technocratic board of governors "is definitely part of someone's plan. Trump's? Thiel's? Truth's?"[1]

## Erebor bank venture (2025)

In July 2025 the Financial Times reported that Thiel's venture capital fund, [[founders-fund|Founders Fund]], would be among the investors in [[erebor|Erebor]], a new United States bank being organized by [[palmer-luckey|Palmer Luckey]] to serve start-ups and cryptocurrency businesses in the gap left by [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]].[2] Also backing the venture was [[joe-lonsdale|Joe Lonsdale]], founder of [[8vc|8VC]] and a co-founder of Thiel's defence group [[palantir|Palantir]].[2] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] framed the launch as a major development, describing Erebor as a digital bank built for crypto and tech start-ups.[2]

## Badlands critique of Thiel's worldview

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] published an extended critique arguing that Thiel "seems obsessed with the worldly" and frames every case in worldly terms, treating slowed technological advancement — what Thiel calls stagnation — as the world's great evil.[2] In Paul's reading this makes Thiel progressive rather than conservative, citing his favorable arguments about Franklin D. Roosevelt and the New Deal on the grounds that turning the United States into a socialist nation was good for Science, even while professing opposition to centralization, and describing him as a libertarian whose definition of freedom is the ability to do whatever one wants.[2]

Paul counters that true freedom is the ability to make moral choices unobstructed, that freedom is meant to be constrained by morality, and that where it is not it must be constrained by the State — which cares nothing for morality.[2] He further argues that Thiel advocates free speech and free thought while building tools that can deter or limit speech and thought, from a worldview itself formed under censorship and propaganda, and that Thiel's longing for a new world in which one can transform oneself into anything or nothing amounts to a worldly usurpation of God's order.[2] Paul dismisses Thiel's historical examples of scientific advancement as either fictions or so entangled with layered psychological operations that they cannot serve as models, each representing a different aspect of what he calls the Scientific Materialist inversion of God's order.[2]

## Withdrawals from Silicon Valley Bank

According to a Bloomberg report cited by Badlands Media, Thiel's [[founders-fund|Founders Fund]] had no money with [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] as of Thursday morning, March 9, 2023, as the bank descended into chaos, having withdrawn millions from the institution.[3] Founders Fund joined other venture funds that took dramatic steps to limit exposure to the failing bank, and advised its portfolio companies that there was no downside to moving their money away from SVB even if the risk was low.[3] On the same Thursday the firm initiated a capital call — a routine venture-capital activity in which limited partners are asked to transfer money for investment in startups — initially directing those backers to send funds to its SVB accounts as it had done for years.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that while the collapse could not be attributed to one man or institution alone, Thiel was "certainly one of the major inside money market movers," and that his withdrawal and advice to portfolio companies created "an inside money stampede" of blue-chip investors performing a digital run on the bank.[3] Burning Bright noted that Thiel is one of the most outspoken allies of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Trump-endorsed candidates in the financial world, a heterodox thinker opposed to the agenda he described as funded and largely supported by banks like SVB, and concluded that rather than a planned demolition of the banking system the episode indicated "quite the opposite."[3]
