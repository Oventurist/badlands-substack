# International Monetary Fund

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) appears in the Badlands Media account as the instrument by which an indebted Venezuela was, in the author's framing, sold out to "the cabal."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

In February 1989, at the beginning of his second term as president, [[carlos-andres-perez|Carlos Andrés Pérez]] accepted an IMF proposal known as the Washington consensus; in return the Fund offered Venezuela a loan of US $4.5 billion. The cooperation came weeks after his victory in the 1988 presidential election.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article's reading is that Pérez had blown or stolen the oil profits of the boom years, and that the resulting debt burden delivered the country to the IMF.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Origins in the Bretton Woods system

A later Badlands Media essay places the IMF among the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods]] institutions created after the Second World War, alongside the World Bank.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) In that account the institutions were founded on noble premises — fixed exchange rates to block unbounded speculation, and the emission of long-term credit for large-scale development projects intended to internationalise the New Deal — but were subsequently hijacked, together with the United States government itself, by deep-state operatives representing what the author calls the worst oligarchical inclinations of humanity in the post-war years.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) The essay argues that the noble origins of these bodies, which [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and to a lesser extent Donald Trump attempted to revive, should not be ignored despite that capture.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## 2024 Russia Growth Forecast

In April 2024 the IMF projected that Russia's economy would grow 3.2% in 2024, outpacing the United States and all other advanced economies despite the war and sanctions, with high investment and robust private consumption cited as drivers.[3] The Fund expected that momentum to fade the following year, forecasting Russian growth of 1.8% in 2025.[3]

## Gold sales and the Trilateral Commission

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" cites the IMF's disposal of part of its gold reserves as an example of Trilateralist policy recommendations becoming reality.[4] [[richard-n-cooper|Richard Cooper]], the Yale economist who headed the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]]'s task force on monetary policy, recommended selling off official gold reserves to private markets, then became undersecretary of state for economic affairs and "just happened to be presiding" as the IMF sold off a portion of its gold.[4]
