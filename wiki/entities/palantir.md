# Palantir

**Palantir** is referenced in Badlands Media commentary as a defence-technology firm whose chief executive publicly urged Americans to get ready for a three-front war with Russia, China and Iran.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Role in the article's argument

Contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] cites the Palantir statement while describing a class of actors — including [[rand-corporation|RAND Corporation]] analysts and representatives of the [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]] — who in his view regard a new world war as an exciting prospect and, on the basis of computer-modelled scenarios, a winnable one.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) Ehret rejects that assessment, arguing that hypersonic missile systems and underwater drones fielded by Russia and China have ended any prospect of a [[nato|NATO]] first-strike monopoly, so that no attack on either power could occur without retaliation destroying every major Western city.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Context

The reference forms part of the essay's case that an emergency peace summit modelled on the constructive precedents of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods]] and [[united-nations|UN]] settlements must be convened before nuclear war, rather than after it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Speculation about DOGE tooling

Discussing the [[war-on-fraud|War on Fraud]] led by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in February 2026, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that [[doge|DOGE]] had functioned primarily as a technology transformation program installing monitoring tooling across federal agencies, and asked readers: "Given the choice of fraud czar, do you guys think the DOGE tooling came from Palantir?"[2] The question was posed as open speculation rather than a documented finding.[2]

## Associated ventures

[[joe-lonsdale|Joe Lonsdale]], a co-founder of Palantir and founder of the venture capital firm [[8vc|8VC]], was reported in July 2025 to be among the backers of [[erebor|Erebor]], a proposed United States bank for start-ups and cryptocurrency businesses led by [[palmer-luckey|Palmer Luckey]] of [[anduril|Anduril]].[3] The Financial Times described Palantir as [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]]'s defence group, and Thiel's [[founders-fund|Founders Fund]] was also reported to be an investor in the bank.[3]

## National citizen database controversy

In June 2025, Newsweek reported that the [[white-house|White House]] had contracted Palantir — described as a Colorado-based analytics company co-founded by [[donald-trump|Trump]] supporter [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] — to assist in compiling a database of personal information on American citizens, according to unnamed government officials and Palantir employees who spoke with The New York Times.[4] The purported deal followed project talks Palantir had held with the [[social-security-administration|Social Security Administration]] and the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]].[4]

Supporters of Trump expressed anger and disbelief online, with reactions such as "Trump flipped on us" reflecting growing unease within conservative circles and indicating a rare rupture between the president and key segments of his constituency.[4] Coverage framed the controversy as underscoring nationwide anxieties around privacy, civil liberties, and the growing influence of technology firms over personal information management.[4] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] pointed readers to his discussion of Palantir with Jon on episode 359 of the Devolution Power Hour.[4]

## Stock performance and surveillance-state backlash (2025)

Palantir Technologies Inc. (NASDAQ: PLTR) finished about 3% lower at the market close on June 6, 2025, a pullback at an all-time high that came after the stock had risen over 67% in 2025 and over 400% in the preceding 12 months.[5] The company faced headwinds over concerns that its software would be used to build a surveillance state, stemming from the federal government's announcement that it intended to use Palantir technology to consolidate federal data.[5] Market Beat framed the situation as a clash between an irresistible force — the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] and its [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] — and an immovable object, namely concerns over civil rights and due process, leaving investors to weigh whether Palantir is "a hero, a villain, or merely a company with superior technology that's caught in the middle."[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted a discussion of Palantir between [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] on a Saturday Devolution Power Hour, quoting the framing that "maybe the world would be better if these tools didn't exist. But they do exist. We have to figure out how to have a good world with these tools in it," while asking what such a world would actually look like.[5]

## Michael Burry's put position (November 2025)

In November 2025, investor [[michael-burry|Michael Burry]]'s [[scion-asset-management|Scion Asset Management]] disclosed in a 13F filing that roughly 80% of its put positions were concentrated in Palantir and [[nvidia|Nvidia]], marking the company as a central target of the most prominent bearish bet against the artificial-intelligence trade.[6] Burry later clarified on X that he had purchased 50,000 Palantir put contracts at $1.84 each — 100 shares per contract, about $9.2 million in total premium rather than the $912 million figure circulated online — giving him the right to sell Palantir shares at $50 in 2027.[6] Days afterward Scion terminated its SEC registration, and commentary in the [[badlands-media|Badlands]] brief treated the wager as a bet against "the very hype that's inflated" the era's bubble.[6]

## Mentioned in X shareholder coverage (2024)

Reporting on the August 2024 court-ordered unsealing of [[x-corp|X]]'s shareholder list identified Palantir as the intelligence contractor and data analysis platform co-founded by [[joe-lonsdale|Joe Lonsdale]], whose venture firm [[8vc|8VC]] held a stake in X Holdings Corp.[7]

## Monitoring of Tech Settlement Disclosures (2025 claim)

An August 2025 Badlands Media essay on Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]]'s litigation campaign claimed that the compliance reports required under the state's privacy settlements with [[meta-platforms|Meta]] and [[google|Google]] are not simply filed and shelved but analyzed continuously using "Palantir-level analytics" and other advanced AI systems.[11] According to the author, this monitoring tracks every transaction and shift in data flow so that any attempt to bend the numbers or bury a trail would surface on a dashboard "before the ink on their report is dry."[11]

## In "Soul Governance"

The Badlands Media essay [[soul-governance|Soul Governance]] lists Palantir among the "new altars" of a digital religion, writing that "Palantir knew your fear before you did."[12] The company appears alongside search engines said to have replaced prayer, artificial intelligence said to have replaced intuition, behavior scores said to have replaced conscience, and [[darpa|DARPA]] said to train machines to think as the user rather than for the user.[12] The essay characterizes these systems as entrapment marketed as convenience and programming marketed as inclusion.[12]

## Army/Navy game advertising (December 2024)

Chapter 22 of the "Q for Dummies" series read Palantir's messaging around the December 2024 [[2024-army-navy-game|Army–Navy game]] as deliberate signalling, describing a company post with notably pointed wording and an advertisement run during the broadcast.[10] The author's first association with the ad was the Q phrase "Watch the water," and he noted that drones appear at the seventeen-second mark while ships are shown earlier being halted by a geofence — evidence, in his argument, that the government could geofence areas against drones during the ongoing sightings but is not doing so.[10] The same commentary argued that [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]]'s long-standing support for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] makes it plausible that Thiel is not the figure he has been portrayed as, and may have served as a "white hat" counterweight inside the technology sector.[10]
