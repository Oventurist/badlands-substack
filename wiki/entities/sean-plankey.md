# Sean Plankey

Sean Plankey is the nominee put forward to lead the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]].[1] As of March 2026 his nomination remained stalled, leaving the agency under acting leadership.[1]

The vacancy contributed to a period of instability at CISA, during which [[nick-andersen|Nick Andersen]] was named acting director in place of [[madhu-gottumukkala|Madhu Gottumukkala]], chief information officer [[robert-costello|Robert Costello]] announced his departure, and acting chief human resources officer [[kevin-diana|Kevin Diana]] received transfer orders.[1]

## Nomination (March 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] named Plankey to lead CISA in March 2025, at a moment when the agency was reviewing its election-security work.[2] CISA operates under the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], whose Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] had promised to "rein" in the agency after alleging during her January confirmation hearing that CISA had drifted "far off mission."[2]

Plankey retired from the [[us-coast-guard|United States Coast Guard]] in 2023 and joined the Trump administration for a second time with the nomination.[2] During Trump's first term he served as director for cyber policy at the [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] and subsequently as principal deputy assistant energy secretary for cybersecurity, energy security, and emergency response.[2] U.S. Telecom President and CEO Jonathan Spalter praised his return, saying that "as our nation navigates an increasingly complex cyber threat landscape from sophisticated adversaries, effective public-private sector collaboration is more essential than ever to ensure our security," and congratulating Plankey on the nomination.[2]

The nomination followed Trump's creation of the agency in 2018 and his firing of its first director, [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]], after Krebs assured voters that the 2020 election was "the most secure in American history"; Brian Ware, then CISA's assistant director for cybersecurity, also resigned at the time.[2]

Badlands commentator [[canncon|CannCon]] welcomed the appointment, contrasting Plankey — a Coast Guard, University of Pennsylvania and Cyber Command-trained technologist with experience running cyber operations in Afghanistan alongside Special Operations — with the "shill environmental lawyer" who had preceded him, and expressing hope that his Department of Energy and NSC background meant he understood that electronic voting machines cannot be secured, particularly under the current VVSG standards.[2] The only worrisome element, he noted, was that the environmental lawyer in question had endorsed Plankey.[2]
