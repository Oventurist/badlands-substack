# Ireland

Ireland drew international attention in April 2023 when its lawmakers advanced hate speech legislation criminalizing the mere possession of "hateful" material on personal devices.[1]

## The 2023 hate speech legislation

Irish lawmakers passed legislation making it a crime to possess "hateful" content on phones and computers, an offense punishable by imprisonment.[1] Critics noted that the bill did not define "hateful speech" and gave legal force to contemporary definitions of gender, such that fluctuations in those definitions could expose individuals to prison time.[1] The measure attracted commentary from [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and became a reference point in wider Western debates over [[hate-speech|hate speech]] law.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that Western countries were "getting more intolerable by the day" and that globalist actors were tightening their leash on the Irish people.[1] He observed that the legislation followed shortly after [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s visit to Ireland and, as an Irishman himself, said he would be ashamed if the Irish public did not resist it.[1] DeLarme argued that although America is thought of as a beacon of free speech, the proposed [[restrict-act|Restrict Act]] would permit comparable outcomes in the United States, because its vague wording would let the government define the act's terms at its own discretion — a totalitarian pattern he traced to the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]], which he noted is ironically used to pursue patriots.[1]


## Push for EU sanctions on Israel (September 2025)

In September 2025 Ireland took a leading role in efforts to sanction [[israel|Israel]] over the Gaza conflict.[2] Deputy Prime Minister and top diplomat [[simon-harris|Simon Harris]] reached out to [[european-union|EU]] foreign ministers, asking counterparts already weighing unilateral trade measures to co-sign a letter to bloc foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] urging a swift decision on sanctions, according to Irish public broadcaster RTÉ.[2]

Ireland was among the member states — alongside Spain, Sweden, Denmark and the Netherlands — calling for the EU–Israel free trade agreement to be suspended, a step also proposed in part by European Commission president [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]]; Germany, Hungary and the Czech Republic opposed sanctioning Israel.[2]
