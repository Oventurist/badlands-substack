# Pete Simi

Pete Simi is an American sociologist specializing in far-right extremism who served as the first expert witness called by the petitioners in the Colorado trial seeking to disqualify former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the state's 2024 presidential ballot under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].[1]

## Testimony in the Colorado disqualification trial

On the second day of the trial, October 31, 2023, Simi testified that Trump's January 6, 2021 speech was unmistakably interpreted by his most militant supporters as "a call to violence," a claim central to the challengers' argument that Trump "engaged" in insurrection and is therefore ineligible for office.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], covering the proceedings live, summarized his testimony in six points and treated it as self-refuting.[1] According to her account, Simi defined far-right extremists as those who believe corruption is a significant problem and that special interests run the world — a definition she noted would, per an October 2022 New York Times/Siena poll, encompass 68 percent of the country.[1] He identified [[alex-jones|Alex Jones]], [[enrique-tarrio|Enrique Tarrio]], [[stewart-rhodes|Stewart Rhodes]] and [[nick-fuentes|Nick Fuentes]] as far-right extremists with whom Trump communicates through coded messages and dog whistles in order to preserve plausible deniability.[1] He offered the [[unite-the-right-rally|"Unite the Right" rally]] in Charlottesville as a predecessor to January 6 and as evidence that Trump endorses his followers' behavior; the "very fine people" clip was played by petitioners without Trump's accompanying condemnation, while Trump's attorney played the full clip.[1]

Simi further testified to a theory of "doublespeak" under which Trump's statements mean their opposite — so that "peacefully and patriotically" is heard by extremists as incitement — and asserted that typing in all capital letters and invoking 1776 are markers of escalating extremist behavior.[1] Ashe characterized his final position as the claim that only trained social scientists who have profiled Trump can decode his true meaning.[1]

## Cross-examination

Ashe in America wrote that Simi was "rightly destroyed on cross examination," hesitated verbally so often that her viewing audience abandoned a drinking game keyed to it, and conceded that he had no direct evidence of Trump inciting anything.[1] The defense also played a montage of Democratic elected officials inciting violence during 2020, which in her assessment exposed the witness as "an absolute fraud."[1]
