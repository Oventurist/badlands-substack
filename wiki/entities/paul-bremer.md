# Paul Bremer

Paul Bremer served as the American proconsul in Iraq following the 2003 invasion, heading the occupation authority that administered the country in the immediate aftermath of the fall of Saddam Hussein's government.[1] In the Badlands Media article "Bad Actors," he appears as the official who personally authorised the physical airlift of billions of dollars in cash into Baghdad.[1]

## The cash airlift

Reporting summarised by ShadowProof and quoted in the article states that in the first days after the fall of Baghdad and continuing for over a year, Bremer, "on his own," somehow ordered $12–14 billion to be sent to Iraq by air, with an additional $5 billion transferred electronically; some sources put the total as high as $20 billion.[1] The article draws attention to the two-billion-dollar margin of uncertainty in the figure itself as evidence of the absence of accounting.[1]

The head of the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] team working on Iraq's financial reconstruction after the invasion said flatly: "We did not know that Bremer was flying in all that cash... I can't see a reason for it."[1] Joe Lange treats this as an important point — that Treasury was not informed and could identify no legitimate rationale — and argues that no reason existed unless the intention was to make theft easier, with the [[cia|CIA]] and shadow government controlling the cash flow into and out of Iraq.[1]

Portions of the money were later traced by [[stuart-w-bowen-jr|Stuart W. Bowen Jr.]] to a bunker in rural Lebanon, an investigation that was obstructed before it could be completed.[1]
