# Subrahmanyam Jaishankar

Subrahmanyam Jaishankar is India's Minister of External Affairs. In mid-July 2025 he visited China for the first time in nearly six years, an event described as underlining a tentative but important reset in relations between Asia's two major powers.[1]

## Beijing visit

Jaishankar met with Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], Foreign Minister Wang Yi, and Vice-President Han Zheng in Beijing. The visit did not produce a radical breakthrough, but it marked a significant step toward restoring top-level engagement and inching toward normalization.[1] The trip coincided with the 75th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations between the two nations and reflected what analysts described as a broader duality of gradual rapprochement alongside enduring strategic competition.[1]

Since 2019 Jaishankar has represented the moderate and pragmatic wing within India's foreign policy establishment, advocating cooperation with China and broader engagement in multilateral institutions such as the Shanghai Cooperation Organization and [[brics|BRICS]]. His presence in Beijing was emblematic of India's effort to rebalance its approach to its northern neighbor.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry contrasted the reset with the 2022 Indo-Chinese border skirmishes and contemporaneous speculation that India and China would fight on opposite sides in a third world war. The commentary identified October 2024 — when [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] hosted Xi and Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] at the BRICS summit in Kazan — as the specific turning point in the relationship, adding that Putin sits "literally in the middle of this outbreak of peace."[1]
