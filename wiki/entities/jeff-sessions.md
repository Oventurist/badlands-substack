# Jeff Sessions

Jeff Sessions is a former United States Senator from Alabama who served as Attorney General during the first term of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. Widely criticized by Trump supporters at the time for recusing himself from the Russia investigation, Sessions is reinterpreted in the Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 2 as the architect of a covert prosecutorial strategy.

## Ending the DOJ "slush fund"

The article cites Q drop 36 (November 2, 2017), which asked: "What SLUSH FUND did AG Sessions (through DOJ) put an end to? How does Soros, Obama, Clinton, Holder, Lynch, etc all net many millions of dollars (normally within a single tax year)." [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] answers that Sessions ended the Obama-era practice of directing settlement money from financial institutions to favored outside organizations. See [[doj-slush-fund|DOJ settlement slush fund]].

## The Huber appointment

In November 2017, according to the article, Sessions secretly appointed U.S. Attorney [[john-huber|John Huber]] of Utah, whose first investigation focused on the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]]. Breitbart is quoted citing Professor Jonathan Turley, who called the decision "brilliant" for combining the powers of the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s inspector general with a prosecutor able to bring charges, seek indictments and get results far more quickly than a second special counsel.

The essay explains the design as a deliberate bypass of a corrupted [[fbi|FBI]] and DOJ: Sessions assigned Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]] to investigate instead of the FBI, and Huber to prosecute instead of Main Justice. It quotes Q drop 1517 — "IG started long before Huber setting stage… Think: IG = FBI, Huber = DOJ (no DC)" — in support. Sessions' March 29 letter to Congress, as reported by Breitbart, noted that Horowitz commanded 470 investigators, that inspector general jurisdiction extends to "actions taken by former employees after they have left government service," and that the IG's remit covers violations of established departmental practice as well as of law.

The article states that Sessions launched this effort in response to a request from [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]], [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] and Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]], and that Sessions, Goodlatte and Gowdy all resigned around the same time as part of a coordinated plan — a claim the author describes as "military planning at its finest" and which remains contested.
