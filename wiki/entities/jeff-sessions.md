# Jeff Sessions

Jeff Sessions served as Attorney General of the United States during the first term of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. In Badlands Media's account he is not a passive or obstructive figure but the architect of a covert investigative strategy that routed around a compromised [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and [[fbi|FBI]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## The Huber appointment

Sessions "secretly" appointed U.S. Attorney [[john-huber|John Huber]] in November 2017, days after the arrest of [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]] in Saudi Arabia on November 5, 2017 — a sequence the author believes was not coincidental.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Huber's first investigation focused on the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Legal analyst Jonathan Turley called the move "brilliant," describing it as combining the powers of the DOJ's inspector general with a prosecutor able to bring charges and seek indictments faster than a second special counsel could.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

Sessions launched the effort in response to a joint request from [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]], [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] and Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]], telling Congress that all matters they recommended fell "fully within the scope of [Huber's] existing mandate."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) He also noted that [[michael-horowitz|Inspector General Michael Horowitz]], working with Huber, commanded a staff of 470 investigators — investigative firepower far exceeding any special counsel — and that the IG's jurisdiction covers actions taken by former employees after leaving government service as well as violations of established practice, not only statutes.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Slush fund

A [[qanon|Q]] drop from November 2, 2017 asked what "SLUSH FUND" Sessions, through the DOJ, had put an end to, and how Soros, Obama, Clinton, Holder and Lynch all netted many millions of dollars, normally within a single tax year.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The article concludes that Sessions ended the Obama-era [[doj-slush-fund|DOJ settlement slush fund]] that was enriching them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Resignation

Sessions resigned around the same time as Goodlatte and Gowdy, which the author regards as part of a coordinated plan rather than coincidence, arguing that all three continued to play roles in the background because much of the evidence is classified.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Resignation and the handoff to the Senate

Badlands Media's article "Do You Believe in Coincidences?" treats Sessions's departure as a scripted move rather than a firing in anger.[2] He resigned in November 2018, immediately after the midterm elections in which Republicans lost control of the House of Representatives, writing to the president: "Dear Mr President, at your request I am submitting my resignation."[2] The article stresses that Trump initiated the removal by requesting it, and that Sessions was replaced by his own chief of staff [[matthew-whitaker|Matthew Whitaker]] as Acting Attorney General.[2]

By that point, according to the piece, Sessions had completed his mission: launching large RICO investigations in the background and leak investigations in public, and appointing [[john-huber|John Huber]] as a special prosecutor in November 2017 without public announcement, tasking Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]] to work alongside him on the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA]] matter, Uranium One, the Clinton Foundation and Hillary Clinton's private server.[2] Huber reported directly to Sessions, deliberately bypassing Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]].[2]

Q material quoted in the article groups Sessions with House investigators [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]] and [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]], all of whom resigned; Badlands reads the trio as "expert witnesses" transferring the [[spygate|Spygate]] and [[obamagate|Obamagate]] investigations to the [[united-states-senate|Senate]], whose Judiciary Committee holds direct oversight of the DOJ and FBI.[2] Sessions's long Senate service is cited as what made him valuable in that handoff.[2]

## Classified letter to the Supreme Court

The article highlights a Q drop dated September 17, 2018 asserting that Sessions sent a classified letter to the two most senior justices of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] concerning recusal, "unrecusal" and conflicts, receiving "highest court authority approval."[2] On the same day Trump publicly called for declassification of documents related to the Russian collusion investigation.[2] The author concludes that Sessions ceased to be recused from those investigations from that date — a development never disclosed publicly — and that at least two justices were therefore knowing participants in the wider plan.[2]

Q also defended Sessions against public criticism, quoting Peter Schweizer's 2011 book "Throw Them All Out" to the effect that he was the one member of Congress against whom no corruption allegation could be sustained, and adding that "sometimes you need to take one for the team publicly before you are vindicated as a hero."[2] Trump himself, posting as "Q+," thanked Sessions for his service less than an hour after a near-identical board post — a coincidence the article uses as its titular example.[2]

## Work under the cover of recusal

In "Disinformation is Necessary," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that Sessions's recusal from the Russia investigation never neutered him but served as cover for extensive background work.[3] Q drop 1318 (May 8, 2018) asked why the DOJ and [[fbi|FBI]] "must be CLEANED FIRST," "When does SESSIONS step back in? Already is?" and "Why did SESSIONS secretly engage HUBER? Why did SESSIONS reveal HUBER?"[3] Lange writes that throughout the recusal period Sessions was cleaning out the corrupt FBI and DOJ and had launched criminal investigations into leaks, giving him broad authority over matters involving the same officials who had launched the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] conspiracy.[3]

Sessions had secretly engaged [[john-huber|John Huber]] to work with Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]], an inquiry described in the Q drops as the biggest criminal investigation in history and encompassing corruption at the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]].[3] Huber had already been working for months before Sessions revealed the assignment publicly.[3] Lange contends Huber's work was subsequently handed off — part to [[john-durham|John Durham]], appointed by [[william-barr|William Barr]], and part to a second, still-unnamed prosecutor outside Washington operating with the same mandate and authority.[3]

Sessions also placed [[geoffrey-berman|Geoffrey Berman]] into the temporary role of United States Attorney for the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]] after [[donald-trump|Trump]] fired Preet Bharara, an appointment Lange treats as essential to the New York investigations that followed.[3] Q drop 1370 asked "WHO HAS THE ULTIMATE AUTHORITY? DOES IT FALL UNDER THE RUSSIA RECUSAL?" — a question Lange answers with Sessions's name.[3] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
