# Bank of America

Bank of America is a major American commercial and investment bank.[1] Together with [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] and [[citigroup|Citigroup]], it was to participate in a planned $20 billion bank-led debt facility for [[argentina|Argentina]] under the [[argentina-bailout-package-2025|2025 Argentina bailout package]].[1]

The facility was shelved in November 2025 as bankers pivoted to a smaller, short-term loan package for the financially distressed government; the private-sector loan never got off the ground while banks awaited guidance from the U.S. Treasury Department under Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]].[1]

## Debanking allegations (January 2025)

In his January 23, 2025 video address to the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called out Bank of America and [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] of JPMorgan Chase for "de-banking" conservatives over their politics, telling the audience "I hope you and Jamie open things up because what you're doing is wrong."[2] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that Bank of America issued a post on X denying the allegation and was subsequently slapped with a Community Note stating that the bank was lying.[2]

## Retreat from coal financing pledge (2023–2024)

Although Bank of America pledged in 2021 to stop financing any new coal mines and plants or Arctic oil drilling, its December 2023 "Environmental and Social Risk Policy Framework" replaced those commitments with a promise that such projects would face "enhanced due diligence."[3] The December 2021 framework had stated that the company "will not directly finance new thermal coal mines or the expansion of existing mines," including "petroleum exploration or production activities in the Arctic," and that it would not "directly finance the construction or expansion of new coal-fired power plants," with certain caveats; those statements were removed from the 2023 version.[3] The bank told FOX Business: "We have a risk-based process for client transactions. Certain client relationships or transactions that carry heightened risks will continue to go through an enhanced due diligence process involving senior level risk review."[3] The change came as states including New Hampshire, Texas and West Virginia passed laws to prevent banks from refusing to finance coal projects and moved to penalize [[esg|environmental, social and governance]] principles within companies.[3]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] paired the reversal with Reuters data showing US thermal coal exports topping $5 billion in 2023, arguing that the bank backing off its former ESG commitments alongside rising domestic fossil-fuel production amounted to a directional proof of [[devolution|Devolution]] — "Winning is everywhere."[3]

## Anti-money-laundering enforcement warning (October 2024)

In a filing made on Tuesday, October 29, 2024, Bank of America revealed that it was in talks with several federal regulators "in relation to certain aspects of the Corporation's Bank Secrecy Act/anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance programs (Programs), including transaction monitoring, training, governance, and customer due diligence."[4] The bank added that it had been implementing enhancements to those programs in cooperation with regulators and that "resolution of these discussions may include one or more public orders by the regulators."[4] The filing did not specify the underlying anti-money-laundering issues.[4]

The disclosure came a little more than two weeks after [[toronto-dominion-bank|Toronto-Dominion Bank]] pleaded guilty to multiple criminal charges and paid $3 billion in fines and penalties to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and financial regulators for failing to monitor money laundering by fentanyl traffickers, suggesting that the investigation had expanded to major US institutions.[4] Journalist [[sam-cooper|Sam Cooper]] and former [[state-department|State Department]] investigator [[david-asher|David Asher]] had anticipated the shift in September 2024, with Asher saying, "We'll see which one of the big four US banks gets named next."[4]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] situated the warning within a timeline that included [[warren-buffett|Warren Buffett]]'s September 2024 sell-down of Bank of America shares and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s proposal to seize cartel assets, closing with the questions "Who controls the cartels? Who controls the banks? What is the Deep State?"[4]

## January 6 bank records disclosed to the FBI (2023 whistleblower testimony)

Whistleblowers told Republicans on the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] in May 2023 that Bank of America had given the [[fbi|FBI]] access to bank records connected to January 6, 2021 without customers' knowledge or consent.[5] According to their testimony, the bank transferred information to the FBI on transactions that took place in Washington, D.C. on that date, and customers were not informed of the data transfer.[5] The same whistleblowers claimed the FBI had become "enveloped in politicization" and alleged "serious abuses" over an extended period.[5]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] compared the episode to the origins of the credit-rating industry in the mid-20th century, which he described as private companies that, like intelligence services, gathered any information they could about a person — travel, expenditures, sexual encounters, drug and alcohol use, marital fidelity and other seemingly unrelated data points — amounting to "a fiscal version of a spy agency" whose data was tightly held by large financial institutions and governments.[5] He argued that the same bankers who helped make credit scores publicly acceptable, and thereby paved the way for social credit scoring, were now sharing private information with the FBI for use in generating predicates to investigate law-abiding Americans.[5]

Deschamps contended that the harm is widely under-appreciated: in the modern technocratic age the mere existence of an investigation can act against a citizen's interests, effectively reversing the presumption of innocence, since agents questioning friends, family and business contacts create the impression that "if there's smoke, there's fire."[5] Until the public recognizes these "subtle subversions" of freedom, he wrote, "we will continue to suffer under the yoke of tyranny."[5]
