# Yevgeny Primakov

Yevgeny Primakov was a Russian statesman and grand strategist credited with authoring the doctrine of multipolarity that underpins the modern Eurasian security architecture.[1] He served as Director of Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service from 1991 to 1996, as Foreign Minister from 1996 to 1998, and as Prime Minister from 1998 to 1999.[1]

## Architect of the SCO

The actual birth of the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]] is attributed to Primakov's insight in bringing Russia into alliance with the Chinese-led security compact established in 1996 known as the [[shanghai-five|Shanghai Five]], a grouping created to secure China's borders amid an anticipated era of radical Islamist warfare across Eurasia.[1] Primakov read that coming turbulence as the product of a deliberate balkanization and asymmetric-warfare strategy of the kind described in the writings of [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]].[1]

Primakov spearheaded a new Eurasian security and cooperation doctrine centered on multipolarity and built around a combined Russian–Chinese–Iranian core alliance.[1] This doctrine was formalized in the 1997 Russian-Chinese Joint Declaration on a Multipolar World and the Establishment of a New International Order, adopted in Moscow on 23 April 1997 and transmitted to the United Nations the following month.[1]

## Sponsorship of Putin

Primakov is also credited with sponsoring a then-little-known official named [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], who replaced Boris Yeltsin as Russia's leader on Christmas Day 1999.[1]

## Legacy

In the Badlands account, Primakov's Russia–China–Iran triangle was dismissed at the time by Western figures such as then-Senator [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], who in 1997 expressed incredulity at Russian warnings that continued [[nato|NATO]] expansion would drive Moscow toward Beijing.[1] Two and a half decades later, that alignment is described as having "come back to bite" the architects of NATO enlargement.[1]
