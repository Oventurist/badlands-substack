# Horace Cooper

**Horace Cooper** is a senior fellow with the [[national-center-for-public-policy-research|National Center for Public Policy Research]] and a former constitutional law instructor at George Mason University.[1]

## Prediction on the Colorado ballot case

In December 2023, Cooper predicted that the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] would strike down the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] ruling that removed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the state's 2024 primary ballot, but would do so narrowly — most likely by holding that the disqualification clause of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] does not apply to the presidency — thereby avoiding any ruling on whether Trump's conduct amounted to insurrection.[1] He suggested the justices would seek to avoid influencing the election, citing the reputational damage the Court absorbed in 2000 for weighing in on Bush v. Gore.[1] Cooper also argued that the Colorado court should have been more prudent in addressing the insurrection question.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] agreed that Cooper's predicted outcome was the likely one but called his analysis "disturbing," arguing that leaving an improper criminal-style finding of insurrection intact in a civil case would unfairly penalize Colorado voters, could bar military-veteran January 6 defendants from running for office, and would green-light further "lawfare-as-election-interference."[1]
