# Jerry Marzinsky

**Dr. Jerry Marzinsky** is a therapist who worked with schizophrenic patients in maximum security hospitals and who, in the account given by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] in the [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series, "demonstrated beyond any reasonable shadow of a doubt" that the voices heard by schizophrenic patients are not hallucinations but evil spirits with an independent existence of their own.[1] He was first introduced to readers of the series in its third installment.[1]

## Engineering Sanity

Marzinsky maintains a YouTube channel titled Engineering Sanity, on which he has repeatedly recounted the story of a colleague who worked with the voices therapeutically rather than attempting to suppress them, and who successfully helped one of them "go back into the light."[1] The colleague is not named publicly, according to the account, because discussing such work can bring serious professional trouble.[1] Marzinsky is also the author of a book on the psychotic mind, cited in the article alongside his channel.[1]

## The hierarchy of voices

A central observation attributed to Marzinsky is that the voices are organised into a hierarchy, in which some spirits act as "handlers" for others in order to maintain their programming — a structure American Hypnotist compares directly to the way the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] is said to operate on the material side of the veil.[1] Within that hierarchy, enforcer spirits use threats and deception to keep lower spirits in line, summarised in the article as the message that God hates them and that disobedience will be punished with consignment to a deeper rung of Hell.[1]

## The rescued voice

In the story as retold, Marzinsky's colleague gained the trust of one such voice and asked whether it had any light in it; the voice replied that it had been told there was none.[1] Asked whether it had ever looked, the voice protested that it would be thrown into the pit if it stopped tormenting the patient, but on looking inward reported "a dim orange color."[1] Confronted with the discovery that its own hierarchy had lied to it, the voice initially resisted out of fear that the light would burn it, then entered the light — after which the patient reportedly never heard voices again.[1] American Hypnotist draws from this the conclusion that the primary enforcement mechanisms of hell's hierarchy are deception and fear, and that even a spirit lost between realms can be helped.[1]

## Cautions

The article that recounts these stories warns readers against making spirit rescue their personal mission unless their material needs are met and they are in peak health in every domain, noting that seeking contact with spirits can precipitate schizophrenia, and arguing that dealing with the [[deep-state|cabal]] should take priority.[1]
