# Yahya Sinwar

Yahya Sinwar was the leader of [[hamas|Hamas]] in the Gaza Strip during the war that followed the group's October 2023 attack on Israel.[1]

## Encirclement in Gaza City

On Tuesday, November 7, 2023, Israeli Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] issued a battlefield update stating that Israeli troops were for the first time "in the heart" of Gaza City, and that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had Sinwar surrounded in a bunker.[1] Gallant described Gaza as "the largest terrorist base ever built" and declared, "We are going to destroy Hamas," while noting that fighting was taking place in residential areas.[1]

Gallant also rejected U.S. pressure for a humanitarian "pause," emphasizing that "there will be no humanitarian truce without the return of the hostages."[1] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], speaking from the Kirya military headquarters in Tel Aviv, affirmed the same position, saying the IDF had reached deeper into Gaza than Hamas ever imagined and that "Gaza City is surrounded."[1] He warned Lebanon's [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] that opening a new full-on war front would be "the greatest mistake of its life," and said there would be no entry of fuel into Gaza and no ceasefire without the return of the kidnapped people.[1]

## Named in the 2024 ICC warrant applications

Sinwar was among the [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders named in [[international-criminal-court|ICC]] prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]]'s May 2024 arrest warrant applications, alongside Muhammad Deif and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[2] Britain objected to the equivalence the applications drew between these figures and Israeli leaders [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]].[2]

## Death in Gaza (October 2024)

Israeli forces killed Sinwar in Gaza on Wednesday, October 16, 2024, with the military announcing the death the following day.[3] Troops appeared to have encountered him unknowingly during a battle in southern Gaza, only discovering afterward that a body in the rubble was Israel's most-wanted man.[3] Israeli leaders celebrated the killing as a settling of scores just over a year after Hamas-led militants killed some 1,200 people in Israel and kidnapped 250 others, and presented it as a turning point in the campaign to destroy Hamas, urging the group to surrender and release roughly 100 remaining hostages.[3] "Hamas will no longer rule Gaza. This is the start of the day after Hamas," Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said.[3]

U.S. officials expressed hope that Sinwar's removal would open a path to a ceasefire, though observers cautioned that eliminating him might not end a war in which Israel had destroyed much of the Gaza Strip and killed more than 42,000 Palestinians according to the Gaza Health Ministry, which does not distinguish combatants from civilians but says more than half of the dead were women and children.[3] Sinwar's death was described as a crippling blow to [[hamas|Hamas]], though the Iran-backed group had proven resilient to past losses of leaders, and there was no immediate confirmation from Hamas itself.[3]

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] called Netanyahu from Air Force One en route to Germany, congratulated him on the operation, and said afterward that it was time to "move on" and end the war.[3] Biden had been pushing personally for months for a hostage and ceasefire deal and hoped for a breakthrough in stalled negotiations over the 101 hostages still held before leaving office on January 20.[3]

### Commentary and the drone footage

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu had turned his enemy into a martyr, immortalizing Sinwar's death in a way that elevated him to legendary status and galvanized his supporters.[3] He noted that the IDF filmed the death by drone and published the footage: the drone descends on a bombed-out building and pans onto a masked, seated figure whose right hand had been severed and who was bleeding out, and who — on noticing the drone — used the last of his strength to hurl a stick or piece of rebar at it in a futile act of defiance before an IDF sniper shot him in the head.[3] The net effect, the writer contended, was that the internet began glorifying Sinwar as a hero with an iconic death, while Netanyahu's online critics circulated side-by-side videos of the scene and of Netanyahu hurrying to his bunker during a purported missile strike.[3]

The same commentary advanced the view that figures like Sinwar are cultivated by intelligence services such as the [[cia|CIA]] and [[mi6|MI6]] through lifelong indoctrination, lived trauma, and other means of radicalization, so as to supply the villains needed to justify wars of conquest — with funding routed through royal princes, oil barons, or cartel figures to maximize degrees of separation.[3] It also read Biden's call for an end to the war, 18 days before the U.S. election, as partly posturing and partly stage-setting for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to deliver the same message, noting that with both [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] and Sinwar dead the moment seemed apt to halt the killing.[3]

## Named in the ICC warrant applications (May 2024)

On May 20, 2024, ICC chief prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] announced he was seeking an arrest warrant for Hamas leader Yehiya Sinwar on charges of war crimes and crimes against humanity, alongside applications targeting [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], [[mohammed-deif|Mohammed Deif]] and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[4]
