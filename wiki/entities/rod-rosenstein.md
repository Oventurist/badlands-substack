# Rod Rosenstein

Rod Rosenstein is an American lawyer who served as United States Deputy Attorney General from April 2017 until May 2019, spanning the period of the Russiagate investigations.[1]

## Representation of NSO Group

Rosenstein later appeared as one of the lawyers defending the Israeli cybersecurity firm [[nso-group|NSO Group]] in the long-running U.S. lawsuit brought against it by [[whatsapp|WhatsApp]] over the [[pegasus|Pegasus]] spyware.[1] According to leaked files reported by The Guardian, Rosenstein at one stage appears to have asked one of Israel's U.S. lawyers whether the Israeli government would "come to the rescue" in the legal battle with WhatsApp.[1] The Israeli government subsequently seized documents about Pegasus from NSO Group in order to prevent the company from complying with disclosure demands in the U.S. court, a manoeuvre officials believed necessary to avoid "serious diplomatic and security damage."[1]

Badlands commentary treated Rosenstein's involvement as the most striking element of an under-covered story, arguing that the episode highlights the tight and "precarious" relationship between the Israeli state and its corporate partners, and speculating about what interest [[israel|Israel]] had in shielding NSO's internal documents.[1]

## Role in the Russiagate narrative

Badlands Media's account of the Russian collusion affair casts Rosenstein as "the enemy's pawn" whom President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] deliberately left in place at the [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]] until he could be leveraged and flipped.[2] As Deputy Attorney General he appointed [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] as Special Counsel and retained oversight of the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller investigation]], while being kept entirely outside the parallel inquiry run by [[john-huber|John Huber]], who reported directly to Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]].[2]

The article tallies three "strikes" against him. The first was his signature on page 391 of one of the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA warrants]], alongside [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]], [[james-comey|James Comey]], [[sally-yates|Sally Yates]] and [[dana-boente|Dana Boente]].[2] The second was the claim, drawn from Q material, that he already knew of [[bruce-ohr|Bruce Ohr]] and [[nellie-ohr|Nellie Ohr]]'s role in producing and circulating the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] before he signed — despite testifying to Congress that he had not fully read the application.[2] The third was the September 2018 New York Times report, relayed by The Hill, that he had proposed secretly recording conversations with Trump in the Oval Office and discussed invoking the 25th Amendment to remove the president after Comey's firing.[2]

## The Air Force One flight and the alleged flip

Badlands reports that Rosenstein twice requested meetings with Trump in September 2018 and was refused both times, and that he telephoned former Attorney General [[loretta-lynch|Loretta Lynch]] four times in eleven days as pressure mounted.[2] On October 8, 2018 he flew with the president aboard [[air-force-one|Air Force One]] to a police chiefs' conference in Florida; Trump told reporters beforehand that he had no plans to fire him.[2] The author contends that this thirty-minute flight was where Rosenstein was confronted with accumulated surveillance evidence and accepted a deal, ending any real threat the Mueller investigation posed to Trump while the enemy still believed Rosenstein controlled it.[2]

Under Acting Attorney General [[matthew-whitaker|Matthew Whitaker]], the article says, Rosenstein's expanded "scope document" articles were stripped from the Mueller mandate, putting him on a leash.[2] He was retained to the end so that he would sign off on [[william-barr|William Barr]]'s summary of the Mueller report and concur that no obstruction of justice had occurred.[2] A March 2019 Q drop declared his debrief complete.[2] Rosenstein retired weeks later with a full Justice Department celebration, and his resignation letter praised Trump's inaugural goals and stressed the department's duty to the Constitution and the rule of law — a posture Badlands presents as proof that he had switched sides.[2]

## Uranium One and the "common denominator"

In "Disinformation is Necessary," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents Rosenstein and [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] as bound together by their participation in the cover-up of corruption benefiting [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] — a "protection racket."[3] Q drop 3 (October 29, 2017) stated that Rosenstein and Mueller "were key players in the Uranium scandal," and Q drop 1704 (July 25, 2018) reduced the relationship to a formula: if Rosenstein is dirty, Mueller must also be dirty, and vice versa, a "common denominator" Lange identifies as the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One]] cover-up.[3]

Lange argues that [[donald-trump|Trump]] never called for either man's recusal because allowing the enemy to place its pawns in full control of the special counsel created the opportunity to flip them.[3] Q drop 1287 noted that Rosenstein had signed off on the FBI raid of [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]]'s office, which Lange frames as the mechanism for legally introducing evidence into criminal investigations.[3] By April 27, 2018, per Q drop 1286, Rosenstein had told Trump he was not being investigated.[3] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
