# David Boies

David Boies is a prominent American trial lawyer who appears in Badlands Brief commentary as the attorney speculated to be the likely choice — personally or through an associate at his firm — to defend [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in his US narcoterrorism case, once sanctions relief allowed the Venezuelan government to fund that defense.[1]

The commentary by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] notes that Maduro first hired Boies in 2018 to sue 42 of the world's most powerful oil executives, and characterizes that lawsuit as the catalyst that "kicked off this whole geopolitical op against the banking and energy sectors."[1]

## The PDVSA litigation

Boies served as a lawyer in the matter arising from a laptop given to Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in March 2017 by the scorned wife of one of his corrupt officials.[2] Assessing the material, Boies said: "In terms of the quality of the evidence, the extent of the misconduct, the amount of money, this was right at the top of anything I've seen in my career. It really demonstrated a long-running, massive and very sophisticated conspiracy."[2]

One year later he filed civil lawsuits in the District of Southern Florida against 42 oil-industry executives, and filed parallel actions in Geneva, Switzerland, where the companies involved all had offices.[2] Swiss authorities promptly raided the [[helsinge|Helsinge]] office in Geneva, seizing servers that launched further investigations and arrests across subsequent years.[2] The Florida suits were later dismissed after defense attorneys argued that discovery would violate national security under [[executive-order-13850|Executive Order 13850]].[2]

## Client list and the Venezuela oil litigation

Badlands commentary profiled Boies — billed as "the most famous lawyer in the world" — through his client list, which includes Harvey Weinstein (in connection with which Boies was accused of hiring the Tel Aviv-based private intelligence firm Black Cube to run smear campaigns against Weinstein's accusers), Jeff Bezos, the NFL against the Players' Union, Theranos and Elizabeth Holmes, and Al Gore in Bush v. Gore.[3] He was also responsible for the 2009 lawsuit against California's Proposition 8, whose overturning the commentary credits with launching a broader lawfare campaign.[3] On the other side of the ledger, Boies represented the Department of Justice in the 2001 landmark antitrust action against Microsoft over restrictions on PC manufacturers' ability to uninstall Internet Explorer and use competitors such as Netscape or Java, prompting Bill Gates to accuse him publicly of trying to destroy the company.[3] Boies also represented Virginia Giuffre pro bono against Prince Andrew — settling before Andrew was deposed — along with other Epstein victims.[3] In late July 2024, Delta Air Lines retained Boies to pursue CrowdStrike and Microsoft over the massive IT outage.[3]

Most consequentially for the Badlands narrative, Boies represented Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in his March 2018 U.S. lawsuit against [[glencore|Glencore]], Trafigura and Vitol, targeting foreign executives accused of striking predatory deals to exploit Venezuelan resources by bribing corrupt officials.[3] The case named roughly 40 executives and involved more than 70 attorneys.[3]

## Google Incognito settlement (2024)

Boies represented the consumer plaintiffs in the class action alleging that [[google|Google]] tracked users of Chrome's private "Incognito" mode without their knowledge, settled in a filing on April 1, 2024 in San Francisco federal court.[4] He said the settlement requires Google to delete and remediate "in unprecedented scope and scale" the data it improperly collected, adding: "This settlement is an historic step in requiring honesty and accountability from dominant technology companies."[4] See [[google-incognito-privacy-settlement]].[4]
