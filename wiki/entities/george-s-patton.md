# George S. Patton

**George S. Patton** was the American general famous for his role in winning the Battle of the Bulge during the Second World War.[1]

## Role in the Lockwood parallels

Patton appears in [[the-white-pill-emporium|The White Pill Emporium]] because the Trump family has a dog named Patton.[1] In the 1890s books of [[ingersoll-lockwood|Ingersoll Lockwood]], the character Baron Trump keeps a pet dog named Bulger — a name that proponents of the parallel connect to the Battle of the Bulge and thence to Patton, and so to the present-day Trump family dog.[1] The correspondence is cited by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] as one of several "uncanny" resemblances between Lockwood's fiction and the Trump story.[1]

## Anti-communism and death in Heidelberg

A separate Badlands Media essay treats Patton as one of a group of American anti-communists who died in suspicious circumstances, noting that he died at an Army hospital in Heidelberg, Germany, on 21 December 1945.[2] The article calls him arguably the greatest military leader the United States has ever known, and at minimum the greatest of the Second World War: a no-nonsense, "anti-woke" commander, brash and indifferent to feelings.[2] It states that Patton's efforts were often sabotaged by other US military leaders and by figures in the federal government, but that he overcame this through superior strategy and the confidence and admiration of his soldiers — a pattern the article explicitly likens to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s situation.[2]

Patton is described as having believed that the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]] posed a greater long-term threat to the United States and to democracy worldwide than the Nazis did, and as having argued that once the Nazis were defeated the allies should march into Russia and defeat it as well, while it was weakened, so as to end [[communism|communism]] for good.[2] The article contends that this view antagonised those in the US military and government who had been secretly commissioned to spread communism — the [[deep-state|Deep State]] Cabal's puppets within America — and that because Patton sought communism's destruction and had the American people behind him, these players had him assassinated in a manner designed to look like an accident.[2]

The essay groups Patton with [[james-forrestal|James Forrestal]], [[joseph-mccarthy|Joseph McCarthy]] and [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] as men who fought communism and were attacked by the press while alive before dying mysteriously, and concludes that Trump is "a modern-day Joseph McCarthy, James Forrestal, General Patton and JFK all rolled into one."[2]

## Cited alongside Forrestal

Badlands writer [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] invoked Patton's death as a parallel to that of [[james-forrestal|James Forrestal]], who was forcibly admitted to Bethesda Naval Hospital for psychiatric treatment in 1949 and reported to have jumped from a sixteenth-floor window: "As with General Patton's death, this could have been an assassination by his own government."[3]
