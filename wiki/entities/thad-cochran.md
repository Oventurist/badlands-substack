# Thad Cochran

Thad Cochran was a Republican United States Senator from Mississippi who was among the members of [[us-congress|Congress]] examining the transfer of American space satellite technology to China during the [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] administration.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

## Role in the satellite technology inquiry

Several congressional committees investigated whether the administration's policy of exporting space satellite technology to China had helped China and other countries develop and use nuclear missiles.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) The inquiries followed reporting in the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] disclosing a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] finding that the reliability of China's nuclear missiles was significantly advanced in 1996 after scientists working for American space satellite companies provided Beijing with expertise on guidance systems — technology functionally similar to that which guides a long-range nuclear missile to its target.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

Cochran framed the problem as one of American rather than Chinese conduct, stating: "I'm not blaming China. We're talking about our own Government policy-makers responsible for the transfer of this technology, and this case is a glaring example."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) The Badlands account uses his remark to underline its thesis that the security breach originated in decisions made in Washington, including waivers signed by President Clinton, rather than in Chinese espionage.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)
