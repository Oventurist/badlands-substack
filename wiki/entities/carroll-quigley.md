# Carroll Quigley

**Carroll Quigley** was an American historian best known for his study of transatlantic elite networks, cited by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as the author who named the phenomenon at the heart of U.S. policy toward [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[1]

Quigley referred to the sprawling marriage of government and corporations as the "Anglo-American Establishment."[1] DeLarme invokes the term to describe how that establishment — operating through the [[imf|International Monetary Fund]], the [[world-bank|World Bank]], and middleman consulting firms — has extended itself into foreign countries, a mechanism paralleling the "economic hit man" model documented by [[john-perkins|John Perkins]].[1]

## Cited in "Wilted Ivy"

Badlands Media's May 2025 essay "Wilted Ivy" describes Quigley as "a historian with privileged access" and quotes [[tragedy-and-hope|Tragedy and Hope]]'s statement that "the powers of financial capitalism had another far-reaching aim... nothing less than to create a world system of financial control in private hands."[2] The article uses the passage as its foundation for arguing that [[harvard-university|Harvard]] and the wider [[ivy-league|Ivy League]] functioned as a cultivated instrument of that system rather than as neutral educational institutions.[2]
