# Hezb-e Islami

Hezb-e Islami was the [[mujahideen|mujahideen]] organization led by [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]] during and after the [[soviet-afghan-war|Soviet–Afghan War]].[1]

## CIA and ISI funding

When the Soviet–Afghan War began in 1979, the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] began funding Hekmatyar's rapidly growing Hezb-e Islami organization through the Pakistani [[inter-services-intelligence|ISI]]. Hekmatyar received more CIA funding than any other mujahideen leader during the war, and his prominence in Afghanistan's underworld allowed him to leverage more money and guns from the Americans and the Saudis than any of the fifteen jihadist groups fighting the Soviets.[1]

## Drug trafficking

In the late 1980s, Hekmatyar and his organization used the funds and weapons provided by the CIA to begin trafficking opium and later moved into manufacturing heroin, establishing themselves among the leading heroin suppliers in the Middle East. Given the CIA connection, this became a subject of diplomatic embarrassment for the U.S. foreign service.[1]

Badlands Media argues that Hezb-e Islami functioned in effect as a private army created by the CIA and the [[shadow-government|shadow government]] to protect their heroin operation in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]], while being sold to the American people as "freedom fighters."[1]

## Post-Soviet period

During the post-Soviet anarchy and the rush for the spoils of war, Hekmatyar spent most of 1992–96 raining rockets and artillery shells on the people of Kabul, leaving the city, in the words of one account, a smoking tomb.[1]
