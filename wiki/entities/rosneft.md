# Rosneft

Rosneft is the Russian energy company that figures in Badlands commentary both as the alleged financing link behind an Italian political scandal and as an infrastructure arm of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[1]

Italian and European authorities placed [[matteo-salvini|Matteo Salvini]] and his party [[lega-nord|Lega]] under investigation, accusing the group of allegiance to the Kremlin after financing for the party was allegedly connected to Rosneft.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the same company is now building nuclear power plants around the world for the Sovereign Alliance, treating the earlier accusations as an attempt to stall a populist ascent that culminated anyway when Lega aligned with Brothers of Italy in 2022 to elect [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] as Prime Minister.[1]

## The "Rosneft transaction"

Rosneft features in analysis of the Biden family's 2017 dealings with [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC China Energy]].[2] According to [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], two days after a 13 September 2017 off-site meeting in Ireland attended by [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and [[jim-biden|Jim Biden]], a follow-up email from Sam Morison of the associated "Trade Group" laid out two options for Jim Biden, one of which was described as "the Rosneft transaction."[2]

## Scale and Sovereign Alliance framing

In August 2025 Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Rosneft as the second-largest Russian company and the 24th largest oil company worldwide, and listed it alongside [[intesa-sanpaolo|Banca Intesa]] among the entities he had recorded as belonging to the Sovereign Alliance during on-stream conversations with Alex Krainer of Zero Hedge.[3] Both names appeared in the Wikipedia account of the founding of [[matteo-salvini|Matteo Salvini]]'s 2018 campaign party, which the commentator read as evidence that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was working with Banca Intesa during President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term to covertly fund Salvini's populist party in Italy.[3]

## Absorption of Yukos assets

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in October 2025 that [[yukos|Yukos Oil]], once [[mikhail-khodorkovsky|Mikhail Khodorkovsky]]'s empire, was dismantled through tax evasion charges and its assets absorbed by state-controlled Rosneft as part of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s campaign to subordinate the 1990s oligarchy to sovereign will.[4] He presented this de-privatization — alongside [[gazprom|Gazprom]]'s reclamation of majority control over natural gas assets and outright state takeovers in aerospace and nuclear energy — not as a return of communism but as the reclamation of Russia's destiny from Western-backed predators.[4]


## October 2025 U.S. Sanctions

In late October 2025 the Trump administration sanctioned Rosneft, described as one of Russia's largest oil companies, alongside [[lukoil|Lukoil]], while urging Moscow to agree to an immediate ceasefire in Ukraine.[5] President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] dismissed the measures, saying they would have little effect on the Russian economy and that "no self-respecting country ever does anything under pressure."[5] Two days after the sanctions were announced, Kremlin envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]] arrived in the United States for multi-day talks with [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and other administration officials, prompting Badlands commentary to characterize the sanctions as "pincer moves" within a broader negotiated arc rather than straightforward punishment.[5]

## Losses in the April 2024 Ukrainian drone campaign

In late April 2024, days after the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration signed a new multibillion-dollar military aid package for Ukraine, Kyiv launched a series of suicide drone attacks on Russian oil refineries and fuel infrastructure.[6] A source in the Ukrainian defence sector told AFP that drones operated by the [[security-service-of-ukraine|Security Service of Ukraine]] had carried out the attacks and claimed two oil depots were destroyed in the Smolensk region: "Rosneft lost two storage and pumping bases for fuels and lubricants in the towns of Yartsevo and Rozdorovo," the source said of the state-controlled energy giant.[6] Smolensk Governor Vasily Anokhin wrote on Telegram that the region was again under attack by Ukrainian UAVs, while a separate strike hit the Lipetsk region, where Governor Igor Artamonov said "the Kyiv criminal regime tried to hit infrastructure in Lipetsk industrial zone."[6] The strikes came despite pleas from senior Biden officials that Kyiv stop attacking Russian energy infrastructure, out of concern that turmoil in crude markets would raise US pump prices ahead of the November presidential election.[6]
