# Group of Seven

The Group of Seven (G7) is an informal forum of advanced industrial democracies — Canada, France, [[germany|Germany]], [[italy|Italy]], Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States — whose leaders meet annually to coordinate economic and foreign policy positions.

## The 2018 Canada summit

The Badlands Brief of May 5, 2026 recalls the G7 summit held in Canada on June 9, 2018, at which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] warned German Chancellor [[angela-merkel|Angela Merkel]] and other European leaders about their dependence on [[russia|Russian]] energy. Contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked the summit — and the widely circulated photograph from it — as the moment Trump identified the contradiction later borne out by the [[european-energy-crisis|European energy crisis]]: that United States taxpayers were being asked to fund the defense of a continent reliant for energy on the adversary it was being defended against.

## The 2026 Évian-les-Bains summit and the G2 question

The Badlands Brief of June 16, 2026, titled "The G7 Laments the G2," reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arrived Monday in Évian-les-Bains, France, for the G7 summit after announcing a framework agreement aimed at ending the recent conflict with [[iran|Iran]] — giving him a major foreign-policy accomplishment heading into the meetings with allied leaders. The published agenda covered Iran, [[ukraine|Ukraine]], trade disputes, energy security, artificial intelligence, and [[china|China]]'s economic influence. Trump was expected to press for progress on Ukraine on the strength of the Iran effort, while European leaders sought continued American engagement on security and economic matters.

The Brief's central theme is a crisis of relevance for the forum itself. Citing Politico, it reports that European officials and analysts are increasingly concerned that the world's most consequential decisions are being shaped less by traditional multilateral institutions such as the G7 and more by the relationship between Washington and Beijing — the [[g2|"G2"]]. Several reports noted that China's economic power and the state of US–China relations continue to shape most of the issues on the G7's own agenda, even as leaders discussed coordinated approaches to trade, critical minerals, AI, and the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East.

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] takes the harsher view, describing the G7 as "the Global Collectorate that refers to itself as the 'G7'" and arguing that the American and Chinese economies, when they circumvent that architecture rather than compete inside it, "possess the gravitational mass to reroute trade flows, reset financial rails and redirect energy corridors on a scale the old order cannot absorb." In his reading Trump's earlier G2 framing with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] was "a public acknowledgment of the pincer already closing on the post-war order." This is interpretive commentary rather than reporting. The same edition records that in a meeting with [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] at the summit Trump said he and Iran "get along very well" and that he wants to talk to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] to resolve the conflict in Lebanon.
