# George Moose

**George Moose** is an American diplomat who served as acting president and chief executive officer of the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] (USIP) until his dismissal by the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration in 2025.[1]

## Removal from USIP

Following the issuance of [[executive-order-14217|Executive Order 14217]] on February 19, 2025, which designated USIP among the entities to be reduced to their "statutory minimums," the administration fired 11 of the institute's 15 board members — apparently without providing legal or factual justification, in violation of the for-cause removal provisions of the USIP Act — and dismissed Moose from the acting presidency.[1] His position was initially filled by a staffer from the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] before [[darren-beattie|Darren Beattie]], founder of Revolver News and a former Trump and Matt Gaetz speechwriter, ultimately assumed the role.[1]

## Litigation and reinstatement

Moose and the dismissed board members were parties to the ensuing litigation, in which USIP argued that the administration's actions were unlawful because the institute is an independent nonprofit not subject to executive-branch authority.[1] In May 2025, U.S. District Judge [[beryl-howell|Beryl Howell]] ruled that the board firings, leadership replacements, staff terminations and asset transfers were illegal and "null and void," reinstating both the fired board members and Moose and holding that the president lacked the authority to dismantle the institute.[1] That ruling was stayed in late June 2025 by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit, which reasoned that USIP exercises "substantial executive power" and that the president likely enjoys at-will removal authority over its board — allowing the administration to regain control of the institute's headquarters pending further appeal.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] framed the fight over Moose's removal and the wider USIP takeover as one of the most consequential flashpoints in what he characterized as the war between populism and globalism.[1]
