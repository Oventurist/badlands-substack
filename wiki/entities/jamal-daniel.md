# Jamal Daniel

**Jamal Daniel** is a Syrian-American businessman and investor who developed what the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] described as a set of lucrative relationships with [[neil-bush|Neil Bush]] after the latter returned from lobbying for business contracts in [[kuwait|Kuwait]] in 1993.[1] He hired Neil Bush and partnered with him in several business ventures.[1] Badlands Media commentary notes that Daniel's large mansion is located in Houston, Texas — the base of the [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] political dynasty — and treats the geography as significant rather than coincidental.[1]

## Media holdings

Daniel is the founder and owner of [[al-monitor|Al-Monitor]], an online news site that conveys and translates news from the Middle East.[1] Citing TheTower.org, the article reports that Al-Monitor faced questions over perceptions that it conveyed the line of Bashar al-Assad's Syrian government, with heavy coverage of Assad government arguments in its "Lebanon Pulse" section and an emphasis on translated material from pro-[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], pro-Assad outlets.[1]

In August 2011 Daniel bought a large share of [[as-safir|As-Safir]], a Beirut daily that the New York Times has variously described as a "pro-Assad Lebanese newspaper" and "a left-leaning publication that often supports the pro-Assad Lebanese group Hezbollah."[1] The article argues that by publishing pro-Hezbollah journalists alongside mainstream policy analysis, Daniel legitimized writers who had previously been confined to resistance media outlets, thereby lending Hezbollah credibility.[1]

## Alleged significance

The article uses Daniel as a connective link between the [[bush-family|Bush family]] and [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]], noting that Alwaleed's mother was Lebanese, that he spent part of his childhood in Lebanon, and that his grandfather was the first Prime Minister of Lebanon.[1]
