# Colorado County Clerks Association

The Colorado County Clerks Association (CCCA) is the professional association representing county clerks and recorders in Colorado. In March 2026 it became the subject of public controversy when [[steve-schleiker|Steve Schleiker]], Clerk and Recorder of El Paso County — the state's largest — resigned as the association's Vice President and withdrew his county from active membership.[1]

## The Schleiker resignation

Schleiker cited concerns over transparency and internal governance, including being barred from key executive meetings with the Colorado Secretary of State's office on what he described as the false premise of a "lack of trust."[1] He criticized the CCCA's push for a uniform "Gold Standard" narrative about Colorado's elections and questioned whether the association consistently operates in a non-partisan manner focused on professionalism and the rule of law.[1] He further pointed to "the role of external influence within what is intended to be an independent association."[1]

## Broader critique of clerks' associations

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] situated the CCCA alongside the [[arizona-association-of-county-recorders|Arizona Association of County Recorders]] as examples of professional associations that act as subject-matter experts, escalation points, and public-relations wings for election officials.[1] She argued that such entities exist nationwide without meaningful oversight or lawful authority and effectively function as "handlers" of election officials, making their exposure a necessary step toward election reform.[1]
