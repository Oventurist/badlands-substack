# Johnny Depp

Johnny Depp is an American actor whose 2022 defamation trial against his former wife Amber Heard is used by Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] as an analogy for the legal battles facing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Depp v. Heard

Depp sued Heard for defamation, alleging that she had accused him of being psychologically and physically abusive while in fact she had been the abusive party.[1] The trial was held in Virginia and was televised, and it drew intense public attention in April 2022.[1] Carlson writes that he avoided the trial coverage, viewing it as a distraction from information emerging about the Ukraine conflict, and only later grasped the scale of American obsession with the case after watching a Netflix documentary series about it.[1]

Most of the public came to view Depp as the victim of both lies and physical abuse, while Heard was widely seen as a liar and possibly unstable; some commentators argued she had damaged the "Me Too" movement by making future abuse victims less likely to be believed.[1] Carlson is careful to say he is not suggesting Depp is a good or even decent person, but that going to court allowed the public to learn what had and had not actually happened.[1]

A pivotal moment came when Heard referred to Depp pushing a former longtime girlfriend down the stairs — a rumour later refuted by that girlfriend, Kate Moss.[1] Depp's attorneys had originally sought to call Moss as a character witness, but the judge deemed it irrelevant; Heard's reference opened the door to her testimony, providing evidence that Depp had no history of prior abuse and that Heard's claim was false.[1]

## Analogy to the Trump trials

Carlson draws a direct parallel: as with Depp before his trial, many believe Trump is guilty simply because of the volume of indictments against him, citing an Emerson poll showing 64% of District of Columbia residents would find Trump guilty of 2020 election tampering before trial.[1] He argues that Trump's televised Georgia case will function as Depp's did — exposing accusers as liars and turning public opinion once evidence is presented in court.[1]
