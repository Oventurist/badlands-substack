# Jesus

**Jesus** appears in the Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers" as the subject of its first historical case study, in which His parables are analyzed as cryptographic puzzles.[1]

## Parables as cryptographic puzzles

In the Bible, Jesus uses parables as relatable metaphors to communicate heavenly things to earthly beings.[1] Asked about their use, He delineates two groups: those around Him and His disciples, who could see His message within the parables, and those who are outside, who could not.[1] The article reads this as an indication that Jesus intentionally used parables knowing the former possessed the key to unlock His message — the key being a relationship with Him.[1] Other cryptographic keys in the Bible are suggested as well, including faith, prayer, and worship.[1]

## Information warfare reading

Being the Word (the Logos) of God and the Truth, everything about Jesus is characterized as [[information-warfare|information warfare]].[1] Had those outside understood His message at the time, the article argues, they would have moved more quickly to persecute and kill Him in an attempt to stop Him; the parables therefore functioned as a form of memetic warfare that simultaneously grew His network, propagated His message, and protected the network from attackers.[1] The author compares them to Biblical [[qanon|Q]] drops.[1]

## Jesus as a decentralizing force

In the October 2024 essay "Revolution Founded at the Crossroads," the [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] author argued that the reason Jesus was hated so intensely was that "He was one of those decentralizing forces in society, in human nature, in human evolution."[2] His teaching that "you don't have to come to a central body to find God" made access universal — "decentralized, and open" — and thereby threatened every intermediating power structure.[2] The author described the resulting persecution as "a nothing can stop what is coming type of event": local religious factions crucified Him, Saul was sent after the early church, and most of the apostles were hanged or crucified.[2]

The essay notes that even the apostles initially misunderstood the mission, expecting a political leader who would free the nation from worldly oppression rather than God freeing all people from spiritual oppression.[2] When James and John asked to sit beside Jesus in His glory, they were seeking high positions in what they imagined would be an earthly political system, and Jesus replied, "you do not know what you ask"; He later told Pontius Pilate, "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36).[2] A paradigm shift was required before they could appreciate what was actually happening.[2]

Jesus is described as knowing that fulfilling the law, defeating the wages of sin, decentralizing access to God through the torn veil, and giving individuals peaceful, non-kinetic "spiritual power projection" via the Holy Spirit was revolutionary, and that centralized power hierarchies would oppose it until Revelation.[2] The author characterizes salvation through Christ as itself a revolution — "a sudden, radical or complete change" — that is universally accessible and entirely voluntary.[2] His life, crucifixion, and resurrection are cast in Bitcoin metaphor as the [[proof-of-work|Proof of Work]] required to mint humanity's next block, the New Covenant, paying the debt in full on the ledger of the Old Covenant law, with His birth described as a "Genesis" that redefined the modern calendar.[2]

The essay emphasizes a combative portrait: Jesus travelled "dropping non-stop Truth bombs" on those He called hypocrites, serpents, and a wicked generation until the god-kings killed Him, and flipped the tables of the money changers set up in the Court of the Gentiles at the Temple in Jerusalem, quoting Jeremiah 7:11 to indict them for turning God's house into a den of robbers.[2] The author's summary: "He was meek, not weak."[2] The claim is extended to American founding: "America wouldn't be America without Jesus," since God grants the unalienable rights the founders wrote about, and the American revolution was inspired by His Truth, His Word, and His Revolution.[2]
