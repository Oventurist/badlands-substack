# Antonio Pomerleau

Antonio "Tony" Pomerleau (died 2018) was a wealthy Vermont real estate developer who became the central foil of [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]]' first successful campaign for public office.[1]

## Target of the 1980 Sanders campaign

When Sanders entered the crowded 1980 race for mayor of [[burlington-vermont|Burlington]] as a socialist, he leveled a series of attacks at Pomerleau, and that critique of a wealthy land developer — paired with socialist rhetoric — launched the Bernie brand.[1] Pomerleau's plan at the time was to build a condominium and shopping center on Burlington's lakefront, a roughly $30 million project that Sanders denounced as an "enclave for the rich."[1] Sanders campaigned on the slogan "Burlington is not for sale!", aimed specifically at Pomerleau.[1] Sanders won the election by ten votes out of 8,650 cast.[1]

## Post-election alliance with Sanders

Once Sanders was safely past Election Day, he struck an alliance with the wealthy Pomerleau family — a relationship that lasted until Pomerleau's death in 2018.[1] Sanders eased back on his rhetoric and began collaborating with Burlington's upper class on a range of projects.[1] He did end the Pomerleau waterfront plan as promised, but replaced it with a substantially larger development called "Aldean" that would "dwarf the $30 million Pomerleau project" and relied on outside investors rather than local ones.[1] A 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited the Pomerleau episode as early evidence that Sanders' anti-establishment posture was rhetorical rather than substantive.[1]
