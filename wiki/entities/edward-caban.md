# Edward Caban

Edward Caban was the Commissioner of the New York City Police Department who announced his resignation on Thursday, September 12, 2024, roughly a week after federal agents raided his home as part of a broader federal investigation touching multiple senior officials in the administration of Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]].[1]

## Resignation

In a letter to the department, Caban wrote that the news surrounding the raids had "created a distraction," and made his resignation effective Friday, September 13, 2024.[1] He stated that he held "immense respect and gratitude for the brave officers who serve this department," and that the [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]] "deserves someone who can solely focus on protecting and serving New York City, which is why -- for the good of this city and this department -- I have made the difficult decision to resign as Police Commissioner."[1]

Mayor Adams said he respected Caban's decision to step down and touted departmental achievements under his leadership, including the removal of more than 18,000 illegal guns from the streets and major reductions in shootings and robberies.[1] Adams appointed [[tom-donilon|Tom Donilon]] to replace Caban.[1]

## Family and the federal probe

Badlands commentary emphasized that Caban's departure was part of a pattern involving family relationships among raided officials. Caban's brother, James Caban, was reported to be under scrutiny by federal authorities for allegedly working as a fixer for Manhattan restaurants and nightclubs that were having trouble with the NYPD.[1] A second pair of brothers was also implicated: [[philip-banks-iii|Philip Banks III]], appointed deputy mayor for public safety in January 2022, and [[david-banks|David Banks]], the Commissioner of Schools, whose fiancée [[sheena-wright|Sheena Wright]] served as a deputy mayor.[1] A third Banks brother, [[terrence-banks|Terrence Banks]], operates a firm representing companies holding city contracts.[1]

Edward Caban, James Caban, and Philip Banks were all reported to have been NYPD officers, with the remaining figures serving as government officials, lobbyists, or NGO-linked actors.[1] Badlands framed the affair as an illustration of corruption inside public-private partnership arrangements in New York City government, noting that federal authorities remained tight-lipped and had not indicated that Adams' inner circle itself was under raid.[1]

## Aftermath: raid on his successor

About ten days after Caban's resignation, federal authorities raided the home of his interim successor at the NYPD, [[thomas-donlon|Thomas Donlon]], on Friday, September 20, 2024.[2] Donlon said the search recovered "materials that came into my possession 20 years ago" that were unrelated to his work with the department.[2] Badlands commentary noted that Caban had resigned after being raided over what were ostensibly public corruption charges involving his brother and others in Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]]' office, and read the back-to-back raids as the opening of a public disclosure phase in a broader [[southern-district-of-new-york|SDNY]] investigation.[2]
