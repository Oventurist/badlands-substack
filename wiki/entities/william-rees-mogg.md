# William Rees-Mogg

Lord William Rees-Mogg was a British journalist and public figure credited by Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] with manufacturing much of the persona of [[george-soros|George Soros]] as an investment genius.[1] (The article renders his name "Rees-Mog.")

## Career and influence

Rees-Mogg was the editor of the London Times for 14 years and became vice chairman of the [[bbc|BBC]].[1] Carlson asks whether the name sounds "British enough," presenting him as an establishment media figure who continually showered praise on Soros's investment prowess.[1]

## Manufacturing the Soros legend

Under Rees-Mogg's promotion, Carlson argues, Soros became something like what Warren Buffett has become more recently: when he invested in something, large numbers of people jumped in immediately afterwards, raising the value of the investment and making the original investor look like a genius.[1] Carlson likens the mechanism to [[jim-cramer|Jim Cramer]] telling CNBC audiences to "buy, buy, buy" or "sell, sell, sell" some twenty years ago, with retail investors following his lead — a scam he says Cramer's controllers appear to have lost, given the recent correlation between Cramer's buy recommendations and immediate share price declines.[1]

## Connections

Rees-Mogg was a close personal friend of the British Royal Family as well as of [[jacob-rothschild|Jacob Rothschild]].[1] Carlson uses these ties to place him inside the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]] network that he says created and controlled Soros.[1]
