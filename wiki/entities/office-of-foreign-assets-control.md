# Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)

The Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) is the sanctions-enforcement arm of the U.S. [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Department of the Treasury]].[1]

OFAC is the body conducting the probe into American participants in the March 2026 [[nuestra-america-convoy|Nuestra America Convoy]] to [[cuba|Cuba]], with more than 40 Americans reportedly under scrutiny for potential violations of U.S. sanctions law.[1] Subpoenas served on streamer [[hasan-piker|Hasan Piker]] and activist [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]] sought financial, logistical and communications records related to the trip.[1]

The enforcement action coincides with a tightened Cuba blockade, Cuba's designation as a state sponsor of terrorism, and a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] indictment of [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]] under the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Iran General License X (2026)

In June 2026, OFAC issued [[iran-general-license-x|"Iran General License X"]], a temporary 60-day authorization for transactions involving Iranian crude oil, petrochemical products, and petroleum products through August 21, 2026.[2] The license followed a framework agreement with [[iran|Iran]] announced by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] after talks in Switzerland, which included commitments on free transit through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspections.[2] It authorized production, transport, shipping services, and related maritime operations, permitted certain payments in US dollars, and included limited authorizations for imports into the United States tied to permitted transactions.[2]

## Venezuela oil-belt crackdown

In an action reported January 2, 2026, OFAC sanctioned four companies — Corniola Limited, Krape Myrtle Co LTD, Winky International Limited, and Aries Global Investment LTD — for operating in [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s oil sector and helping Venezuela evade sanctions, and designated four oil tankers as blocked property in connection with the designations.[3] The companies were designated under [[executive-order-13850|Executive Order 13850]], "Blocking Property of Additional Persons Contributing to the Situation in Venezuela," issued November 2, 2018.[3] Transactions by U.S. persons involving these blocked or designated persons are generally prohibited, and violations could lead to civil or criminal penalties.[3]

One day earlier, on December 30, 2025, OFAC updated its Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) List to add ten individuals and entities based in Venezuela and [[iran|Iran]] over their involvement in the Iran–Venezuela weapons trade and related military-industrial activity; the same update removed six persons — four women and two men from Russia, Switzerland, Cyprus, and Israel — from the list.[3]
