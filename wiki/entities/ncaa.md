# National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA)

The National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) is the governing body for intercollegiate athletics in the United States, setting eligibility and participation rules for member institutions and administering national championships.[1]

## February 2025 transgender participation policy change

On Thursday, February 6, 2025, the NCAA updated its transgender athlete policy to limit competition in women's sports to student-athletes assigned female at birth.[1] The change followed an executive order issued by the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] intended to bar transgender athletes from competing in girls' and women's sports.[1]

The revision was adopted by a vote of the NCAA Board of Governors, which amended the Association's participation policy for transgender student-athletes.[1] Under the new rules, student-athletes assigned male at birth may still practice with women's teams and receive associated benefits such as medical care while practicing, but may not compete.[1]

The policy also addressed athletes assigned female at birth who have begun hormone therapy, such as testosterone: such athletes may not compete on a women's team, and if they do, the team becomes subject to NCAA mixed-team legislation and loses eligibility for NCAA women's championships.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the policy reversal as "a huge – and immediate – win for women and girls," and argued that public support for what she characterized as gender-based discrimination had collapsed once the "dark money" funding it was exposed.[1] She singled out the Yahoo! News headline framing — "Women's Sports Now Only For Student-Athletes Assigned Female At Birth" — as evidence that legacy outlets were "still clinging to madness" while the wider culture returned to sanity.[1]
