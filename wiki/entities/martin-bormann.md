# Martin Bormann

**Martin Bormann** was [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]]'s personal lieutenant and secretary within the Nazi leadership.[1]

In 1941 Bormann stated that "National Socialism and Christianity are irreconcilable," a remark cited by Matthew Ehret as evidence of the regime's settled hostility to Christianity in the same year Hitler privately described Christianity as "a rebellion against natural law" and equated it with the pox.[1]

The American historian William Laurence Shirer wrote that "under the leadership of Rosenberg, Bormann and [[heinrich-himmler|Himmler]]—backed by Hitler—the Nazi regime intended to destroy Christianity in Germany, if it could, and substitute the old paganism of the early tribal Germanic gods and the new paganism of the Nazi extremists," a judgment quoted from *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (1960).[1] Bormann's position therefore places him alongside the [[reichs-bible|Reichs Bible]] project and the [[german-christians|German Christians]] front as part of the wider de-Christianization program.[1]
