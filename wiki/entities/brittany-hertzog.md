# Brittany Hertzog

Brittany Hertzog is a former FBI intelligence analyst who testified for the prosecution on the fourth and final day of the trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] in October 2022.[1] At the time of her testimony she led a global ethics team at [[nasdaq|NASDAQ]].[1]

## FBI career

Hertzog worked at the [[fbi|FBI]] as an intelligence analyst assigned to the Directorate of Intelligence out of headquarters from 2008 to 2019, with a primary focus on Russian counterintelligence.[1] In the summer of 2017 she was assigned to the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel investigation]], brought in by her supervisor, Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]].[1] After joining the Special Counsel's Office she was brought up to speed and given the task of "looking into" the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[1]

## Investigation of Galkina and Dolan

Hertzog conducted substantive work on the sourcing of the dossier, drawing heavily on FBI databases and internal "holdings."[1] She assembled a case file on [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]], a Danchenko sub-source, and included in that file the information she had developed on [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles "Chuck" Dolan Jr.]].[1] Concerned that the material would be buried, she "serialized" — filed — the electronic communication in three separate places: the FBI's Washington Field Office, FBI Headquarters, and the Office of the Inspector General.[1] The Badlands account characterized the filing with the Inspector General as a particularly clever act of institutional self-protection, undertaken even after the Special Counsel's Office had instructed the team "not to take further action on the matter involving Mr. Dolan and Mr. Danchenko's relationship."[1] The FBI's electronic filing system is known as Sentinel, and such filings are referred to as electronic communications, or ECs.[1]

## Testimony

Assistant Special Counsel [[michael-keilty|Michael Keilty]] conducted the direct examination, which was short but, in the author's assessment, significant.[1] Because Hertzog's account was damaging to the FBI rather than to the defendant, she was treated as a friendly witness by the prosecution.[1] Her testimony added little to the 18 U.S.C. § 1001 false-statement charges against Danchenko but contributed to a broader case against agents within [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]], the Mueller Special Counsel's Office, the Washington Field Office, and FBI Headquarters, and to what [[john-durham|John Durham]] has described as a "joint venture conspiracy."[1] Defense counsel Danny Onorato conducted a very short cross examination, followed by a brief redirect from Keilty.[1] The Badlands writer concluded that the work of Hertzog and Special Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] "was not in vain," since Dolan was eventually investigated and became a subject of the [[durham-special-counsel-investigation|Durham Special Counsel investigation]].[1]

## Mentions on the opening day of the trial

Hertzog's work featured in the prosecution's opening statement on day one, where Assistant Special Counsel [[michael-keilty|Michael Keilty]] told the jury that the [[fbi|FBI]] intelligence analyst had prepared a report on [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] and worked out his connections both to the Russian government and to [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]].[2] Later that day [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] testified that Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] joined the team with the transition into the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel]] to perform what was called "dossier validation," and that Anderson was assisted in that work by Hertzog.[2]
