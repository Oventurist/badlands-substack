# Capitol Police Board

The Capitol Police Board is a three-member body that makes security decisions for the United States Capitol complex, working with the [[united-states-capitol-police|United States Capitol Police]].[1]

## February 2023 fencing vote

Ahead of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s [[state-of-the-union|State of the Union]] address, the board split 2–1 in favor of erecting a temporary security fence around the Capitol. The lone vote against was [[william-mcfarland|William McFarland]], the House's newly appointed sergeant at arms, who had been installed under Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]].[1]

The fencing was installed regardless, but the divided vote was described as a notable shift; Capitol Police and House Sergeant at Arms officials declined to comment. A person familiar with the vote said McFarland's rationale was that there was "no actionable intelligence to necessitate putting up a fence," and noted that State of the Union addresses before the January 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol had not required one, though Capitol Police had reinstalled fencing for the 2022 speech.[1]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that the fencing had itself become a news story, argued about in both directions by politicians and outlets, and singled out as notable the fact that McCarthy's own sergeant at arms voted against it.[1]
