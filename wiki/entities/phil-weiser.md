# Phil Weiser

Phil Weiser is the Attorney General of Colorado and one of the officials named by [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]' legal team as a political adversary opposing her clemency.[1]

## Opposition to Peters clemency

When Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] indicated in March 2026 that he was weighing commutation of Peters' nine-year sentence, Weiser joined Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] and election official Matt Crane in issuing statements demanding that no mercy be shown.[1] Peters' attorney [[john-case|John Case]] described the trio as "Tina's political enemies."[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the swift and absolute position taken by Weiser and other Colorado incumbents ahead of the primary confirmed their resolve but also demonstrated how important the Peters case is to their standing.[1] She questioned whether these attorneys could persuasively maintain that their treatment of Peters constituted justice, noting they had struggled to convince the appellate court and had not convinced Polis, and expressed hope that they would eventually have to argue the point before what she called a "Grande RICO Jury."[1]

## 2026 gubernatorial nomination

In the 2026 Colorado Democratic primary Weiser secured the party's nomination for governor, while Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] secured the nomination for attorney general.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the outcome as voters in both parties choosing change down ballot while keeping existing party leadership in play, and closed with the sardonic aside that when "Phil becomes Emperor and Jena becomes 'The Law'," she hoped someone would break her out of the camps.[2]

## Litigation against federal policy (2026)

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Weiser's more than 50 lawsuits "against the Republic and her voters' agenda" as strengthening the case that Colorado officials — alongside Denver Mayor [[mike-johnston|Mike Johnston]]'s [[denver-executive-order-152|Executive Order 152]] restricting federal immigration enforcement — were engaged in what the Union would have called an insurrection.[3]

## Gubernatorial Campaign Criticism (June 2026)

In June 2026 Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] predicted that Weiser, then Colorado attorney general and a candidate for governor, would be among the first to sue over the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] directive instructing [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] to deport noncitizens who vote illegally, writing that he was "probably looking for plaintiffs now."[4] She also objected that Weiser was citing his official acts as attorney general as campaign accomplishments, argued that this should trigger a corruption investigation, and noted that in practice no one investigates a sitting attorney general.[4]

## Response to the Tina Peters pardon (December 2025)

Following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 11 December 2025 announcement of a full pardon for former Mesa County clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], Colorado's Democratic leadership — Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]], Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]], Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] and the state Democratic Party — issued variations on a common statement rejecting the pardon's effect.[5] The statement held that Peters "was convicted by a jury of her peers, prosecuted by a Republican District Attorney, and found guilty of violating Colorado state laws, including criminal impersonation," and that "no President has jurisdiction over state law nor the power to pardon a person for state convictions," adding that the matter was for the courts and that the state would abide by court orders.[5]

## Role in the Peters prosecution as described in March 2025

In a March 2025 summary posted on X after the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] announced a review of the case, Article III Project founder [[mike-davis|Mike Davis]] named Weiser, as Colorado Attorney General, among the officials who brought what he characterized as trumped-up felony charges against Mesa County clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], alongside Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] and local District Attorney Dan Rubenstein.[6]
