# Big Pharma

"Big Pharma" is the collective term [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] uses for the pharmaceutical industry in *A Virus, or a Metaphor?*, where it is cast as an interested party in prolonging the pandemic rather than ending it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## Role in the vaccine argument

Carlson believes [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] forced Big Pharma to speed up its process of developing a vaccine for Covid, on the reasoning that an accelerated vaccine was the only thing that would allow the [[covid-19-lockdowns|lockdowns]] to end before permanent damage was done to society.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) Left to itself, he argues, Big Pharma would have spent years developing vaccines precisely in order to keep everything shut down longer.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

The industry is thus positioned in the article as aligned with the [[deep-state-cabal|Cabal]]'s interest in an extended shutdown, with Trump's pressure presented as the countermeasure — and the resulting [[covid-19-vaccines|vaccines]] as a compromise whose harms Carlson judges smaller than those of continued lockdown.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## Broadcast advertising and media capture

Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote in April 2025 that 70–75% of advertising revenue on broadcast television comes from pharmaceutical companies, and asked why the industry advertises to viewers at all when its real customers are the physicians who decide who receives which drugs.[2] His answer was that the ad spend buys control of the media's voice: no outlet can speak against the industry while risking three-quarters of its revenue.[2]

On this reading, the [[make-america-healthy-again|MAHA]] campaign against pharmaceutical broadcast advertising accomplishes two things at once — it frees honest journalists to criticize the industry and the wider medical system, and it defunds what Carlson called fake-news propagandists — mirroring the way [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] began by eliminating [[usaid|USAID]].[2] Carlson argued the next step is removing the legal and narrative protections shielding manufacturers from vaccine-injury liability, after which demand for vaccines would collapse without any ban.[2] He also relayed the claim that the [[city-of-london|City of London]] runs the American pharmaceutical industry.[2]
