# Microsoft

Microsoft is an American software and cloud computing corporation co-founded by [[bill-gates]]. In the Badlands corpus it appears as a fixture of the top five American companies across a quarter century and as one of the firms whose origins Erik Carlson questions.

## Market position

America Has a CIA Run Economy lists Microsoft among the five largest American companies by market capitalization in 2010, 2020, and 2025 — the only firm besides [[apple-inc|Apple]] to appear on all three of the modern lists in the essay's table. In the 2025 ranking it is placed third, behind Apple and [[nvidia]].

## Origins and the Gates rumor

The essay repeats a rumor that Gates's mother "hired engineers from IBM to come up with the early Microsoft software," offered as one instance of the argument that big-tech founder narratives are constructed rather than accurate. This sits within the article's broader claim that technology developed at [[darpa]] with public money was rebranded under private founders and used by the [[cia]] for influence and monitoring.

## Trump-era realignment

Microsoft is named among the companies that donated one million dollars to [[donald-trump]]'s inaugural fund, a group Carlson describes as uniformly massive, big-tech, and heavily invested in AI. The essay also notes that Gates is planning to fund nuclear power plants to supply electricity for Microsoft's AI operations, listing this among recent developments the author reads as constructive.


## Cybersecurity enforcement referral (May 2026)

The May 22, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the [[american-accountability-foundation|American Accountability Foundation]] referred former Biden Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]] to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], accusing her of shielding Microsoft from cybersecurity enforcement actions before joining the company in 2025 as President of Global Affairs. The referral alleges the Biden DOJ failed to pursue major action against Microsoft despite multiple cyber intrusions between 2019 and 2023 tied to Russian and Chinese hackers that reportedly compromised sensitive US government systems. The foundation noted that the DOJ's [[civil-cyber-fraud-initiative|Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative]], which Monaco oversaw, targeted other contractors for less serious alleged misconduct while no comparable case was brought against Microsoft. It also cited former Biden administration cybersecurity officials who later took jobs with Microsoft, including [[john-p-carlin|John P. Carlin]] and [[bryan-vorndran|Bryan A. Vorndran]], and asked whether Microsoft could face liability under the [[false-claims-act|False Claims Act]] for representations made to federal agencies about the security of its systems.
