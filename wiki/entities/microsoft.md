# Microsoft

**Microsoft** is an American technology company co-founded by [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] and one of the five largest U.S. companies by market capitalization in 2025.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Market position

Microsoft appeared in the top five American companies by market cap in 2000-era rankings' successors — specifically 2010, 2020 and 2025 — as documented in *America Has a CIA-Run Economy*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) It is grouped among the AI-focused big tech firms that dominate the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500 Index]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Allegations

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] includes Microsoft among the mega-caps he argues serve the [[cia|CIA]] as instruments of influence and propaganda, and repeats the rumor that Gates's mother hired IBM engineers to produce the early Microsoft software rather than Gates writing it himself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Trump-era realignment

Microsoft was among the companies donating a million dollars to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inaugural fund.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Gates is described as planning to fund nuclear power plants to supply electricity for Microsoft's artificial intelligence operations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## 2026 Justice Department referral

In May 2026 the [[american-accountability-foundation|American Accountability Foundation]] filed a referral with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] accusing former Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]] of shielding Microsoft from cybersecurity enforcement actions before joining the company in 2025 as President of Global Affairs.[2] The referral alleges the Biden DOJ failed to pursue major action against Microsoft despite multiple cyber intrusions between 2019 and 2023 tied to Russian and Chinese hackers that reportedly compromised sensitive US government systems.[2]

The complaint contrasts that inaction with the DOJ's [[civil-cyber-fraud-initiative|Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative]], which targeted other contractors for allegedly less serious misconduct, and names former officials [[john-p-carlin|John P. Carlin]] and [[bryan-vorndran|Bryan A. Vorndran]] among Biden-era cybersecurity personnel who later took jobs with Microsoft or connected firms.[2] Inspectors general were asked to consider whether Microsoft could face liability under the [[false-claims-act|False Claims Act]] for representations made to federal agencies about the security of its systems.[2]


## AI capital expenditure, 2026

Microsoft was identified as one of four U.S. hyperscalers — with [[amazon|Amazon]], [[alphabet|Google]] and Meta — guiding toward $600–700 billion in combined 2026 capital expenditure, largely for data-center and AI infrastructure, against evident power constraints.[3] The figures were cited as the American answer to China's roughly $295 billion [[china-ai-data-center-buildout|AI data center buildout]].[3]

## Three Mile Island power purchase

In September 2024, the owner of the shuttered [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island nuclear power plant]] announced plans to restart the reactor so that Microsoft could buy the output to supply its data centers.[4] The arrangement preceded October 2024 announcements by [[google|Google]] and [[amazon|Amazon]] of investments in [[small-modular-reactor|small modular reactors]], part of a broader technology-sector search for carbon-free electricity to meet demand from data centers and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[4]

## Precedent for the 2023 Google case

Microsoft's late-1990s antitrust battle over its dominance of personal computer software served as the reference point for the U.S. government's case against [[google|Google]] that went to trial in September 2023, described as the biggest American antitrust trial in roughly a quarter century.[5] Reporting noted that the Google case mirrored the Microsoft action in many ways, including the existential threat it posed to a renowned technology giant whose products billions of people rely on.[5] See [[united-states-v-google-antitrust-trial]].[5]


## Three Mile Island power purchase (2024)

On September 20, 2024, Microsoft agreed to buy power from the shuttered [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island]] nuclear plant, intending to begin drawing that electricity by 2028 to run data centers; the reactor's output is enough to supply roughly 700,000 homes.[6] Because slow grid interconnection could delay the restart by years, Pennsylvania Governor [[josh-shapiro|Josh Shapiro]] wrote to [[pjm-interconnection|PJM Interconnection]] urging that the reactor be permitted to skip the queue rather than be treated as an entirely new development, and PJM said it was developing a "fast track" process.[6] Reporting framed each month of delay as costly at a time when technology giants were competing for supremacy in artificial intelligence.[6]

## 2023 Azure government cloud exposure

In February 2023 an exposed server hosted on Microsoft's Azure government cloud for [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] customers was found to have been spilling internal U.S. military emails to the open internet for about two weeks.[7] The Azure government environment uses servers physically separated from other commercial customers so that sensitive but unclassified government data can be shared on it, but a misconfiguration left this particular server without a password, exposing roughly three terabytes of mailbox data to anyone who knew its IP address.[7] See [[2023-defense-department-email-server-exposure|2023 Defense Department email server exposure]].[7]

## 2024 Russian domain seizures

On October 3, 2024, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] disclosed that it had seized 41 internet domains used by Russian intelligence agents and their proxies to steal information from U.S. government computers and email accounts, coordinating the takedowns with Microsoft, which seized an additional 66 unique domains operated by the same group.[8] The unsealed warrant accused the [[callisto-group|Callisto Group]], a unit under Russia's [[fsb|FSB]], of an "ongoing and sophisticated spear phishing campaign" targeting former U.S. intelligence employees, current and former Defense Department personnel, [[state-department|State Department]] and [[department-of-energy|Energy Department]] staff, military contractors and U.S.-based companies.[8]

## Red Sea cable cuts and Azure latency (2025)

On September 6, 2025, Microsoft said that clients of its Azure cloud platform might experience increased latency after multiple international cables in the [[red-sea-cable-cuts|Red Sea were cut]].[9] The company said in an online status update that traffic traversing the Middle East originating or terminating in Asia or Europe was affected and that its engineering teams were working to mitigate the situation; the notice did not say how the cables had been severed.[9] "Undersea fiber cuts can take time to repair, as such we will continuously monitor, rebalance, and optimize routing to reduce customer impact in the meantime," Microsoft said.[9] Repairs were expected to be complicated by continued [[houthis|Houthi]] attacks on vessels in the area.[9]

## Elimination of the DEI team (July 2024)

In July 2024, Business Insider revealed that Microsoft had laid off an internal team focused on [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|diversity, equity, and inclusion]].[10] In an email viewed by the outlet, a Microsoft "team leader" told employees that "true systems-change work associated with DEI programs everywhere are no longer business critical or smart as they were in 2020."[10] The email stated that the team had been eliminated due to "changing business needs" as of July 1, and the number of affected employees remained unclear.[10]

Commentary framed the cut as part of a broader unraveling of DEI and ESG initiatives across corporate America, noting data from AlphaSense showing that first-quarter mentions of "DEI" had collapsed from their 2021 peak.[10]

## Digital Defense Report (October 2024)

In a report published on Tuesday, October 15, 2024, Microsoft said that Russia, China and Iran were increasingly collaborating with cybercrime networks to launch attacks against their adversaries, including the United States.[11] The attacks ranged from ransomware to phishing and were carried out for "espionage, destruction or influence," with cybercrime gangs working alongside these nations to share hacking tools and tactics; the company's Digital Defense Report examined cyber threats from July 2023 through June 2024.[11] In one influence operation, an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps group used cyber personas beginning in 2023 to sell stolen Israeli dating website data.[11] In another case, Russian threat actors used new malware and appeared to outsource some cyberespionage operations to criminal groups, with one June 2023 operation compromising at least 50 Ukrainian military devices in an apparent attempt to access information for the Russian government.[11] Microsoft further said the attacks included attempts to influence the US election ahead of November, with Russia continuing operations intended to undermine trust in democratic institutions and Iran and China escalating their influence campaigns over the past year.[11]

## New York Times copyright lawsuit (December 2023)

On December 27, 2023, [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] named Microsoft alongside [[openai|OpenAI]] in a copyright infringement lawsuit over the training and output of AI systems including [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]], seeking billions of dollars in statutory and actual damages.[12] See [[new-york-times-v-microsoft-and-openai|New York Times v. Microsoft and OpenAI]].[12]

## Volt Typhoon disclosure

Microsoft helped surface the threat posed by the China-backed [[volt-typhoon|Volt Typhoon]] hacking group in May 2023, publishing research warning that the actors had been active since 2021 and were targeting United States cyber infrastructure using living-off-the-land techniques.[13] The company urged affected customers to change their security details.[13] The group was later described by [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] as having been shut down by the Bureau in January 2024.[13]

In commentary on the episode, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that cloud providers including Google, [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]], and Microsoft had marketed centralization to businesses on the premise that they could afford the best security talent money can buy, and argued that a decade later cybercrime was up 600 percent in part because shared security responsibilities between provider and customer are widely misunderstood.[13]
