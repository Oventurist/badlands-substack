# Hester Peirce

Hester Peirce is a Commissioner of the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] who is cited in Badlands Media's May 2025 essay "Wilted Ivy" as an example of dissent against ESG enforcement emerging from inside the regulatory apparatus itself.[1]

## Criticism of ESG enforcement

According to the article, Peirce "began openly criticizing ESG enforcement," describing it as "a form of backdoor regulation inconsistent with democratic accountability."[1] The essay presents this remark as significant because it came not from an outside political critic but from a sitting commissioner of the agency that would ordinarily be the vehicle for such mandates, signaling that the regulatory consensus behind [[esg|ESG]] was fracturing.[1]

## Significance in the Badlands narrative

"Wilted Ivy" situates Peirce's criticism alongside [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]]'s retreat from ESG advocacy and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]]'s campaign against ESG as a "cancer on capitalism," treating the three together as visible cracks in what the author calls a system of "capital-driven ideological conformity" that had begun to cannibalize itself.[1] In this reading, the withdrawal of regulatory legitimacy from ESG contributed to the weakening of the financial pipelines sustaining [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[vanguard|Vanguard]] and [[state-street|State Street]], and by extension the endowments of [[harvard-university|Harvard]] and its peer institutions.[1]
