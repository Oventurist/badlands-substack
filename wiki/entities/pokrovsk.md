# Pokrovsk

**Pokrovsk** is a city in the Donetsk region of eastern Ukraine that served as a key Ukrainian logistical hub during the war with Russia.[1]

By early October 2024, Russian forces had advanced to within a few kilometers of Pokrovsk as part of a wide-reaching offensive in eastern Ukraine that continued making steady gains while a looming major war between Israel and Iran took over the news cycle.[1] Analysts cited by Badlands assessed that the collapse of Pokrovsk would likely portend a Russian takeover of the whole of Donetsk, given the city's role in sustaining Ukrainian supply lines across the region.[1] The advance came in the same period as the Ukrainian withdrawal from [[vuhledar|Vuhledar]], reinforcing the view that Russian forces would continue to plow through Ukrainian defenses.[1]

## September 2024 Russian advance

By early September 2024, Russian forces were closing in on Pokrovsk, described as a key rail junction town in the Donetsk region, after taking vast swaths of territory over the preceding month along the war's roughly 600-mile eastern front.[2] Badlands commentary argued that the Ukrainian troops committed to the [[kursk-incursion|Kursk incursion]] had left the eastern front vulnerable, allowing the Russians to march "full tilt" on Pokrovsk while Kyiv panicked and evacuated civilians.[2]

## Russian summer 2024 offensive

Russian forces mounted an arc of attack in Ukraine's eastern Donbas region through the intense summer heat of 2024 in a bid to capture Pokrovsk, described as a key transit junction, and to extend Moscow's steady territorial gains.[3] The offensive proceeded as Ukraine suffered a shortage of soldiers and as election turmoil in the United States fuelled speculation that Kyiv might soon be forced to negotiate a surrender of lands.[3] After an influx of American weapons and money helped Ukraine blunt the renewed invasion of the Kharkiv region in May — preventing a breakthrough and dashing Moscow's hopes of surrounding Ukraine's second-largest city — Russian commanders refocused on the Donetsk region, described as perhaps [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s top territorial goal.[3] A battalion commander of the 47th brigade fighting near Ocheretyne, identified only as Oleksandr, said Ukrainian forces were struggling and that Russia was "almost at the point of capturing the Donetsk region," describing the Kharkiv reinvasion as a clever diversion designed to concentrate and then distract Ukrainian strength.[3]
