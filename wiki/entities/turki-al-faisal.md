# Turki Al Faisal

Prince Turki Al Faisal is a senior Saudi royal and, in Badlands Media's framing, one of the top advisors to Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1] He is the son of the late King Faisal.[1]

## Role as Saudi spokesman

Badlands notes that Turki is "no random figure": it was Turki whom MBS selected to deliver the first public statement on behalf of the Saudi government in the days following the October 7 attacks.[1]

## Criticism of Netanyahu

In December 2024, Prince Turki called the government of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] "genocidal," expressing hope that Netanyahu would be brought before a court to answer for purported war crimes, and calling on incoming President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to bring peace to the Middle East.[1]

## Lineage

Turki's father, King Faisal, met secretly with President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] in 1961 while still Crown Prince; JFK persuaded Faisal to view the Saudi people as the nation's most precious commodity and to invest the royal family's wealth accordingly.[1] After becoming King the year following Kennedy's assassination, Faisal abolished slavery in Saudi Arabia and imposed a ten-point plan including free health care.[1] In 1975 — two years after Secretary of State [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] pressed Faisal into the agreement that created the petrodollar, following Faisal's oil embargo against the West over its support for Israel in the Yom Kippur War — Faisal was assassinated by a young nephew said to have just returned from an extended stay in the United States and believed to be mentally ill.[1] Badlands presents MBS as the spiritual successor to his uncle Faisal, in the way Trump is presented as the spiritual successor to JFK.[1]

## Asia Society and the Harvard control grid

"The Harvard Control Grid" notes that Prince Turki al-Faisal, described as the longtime head of Saudi Arabia's General Intelligence Directorate, sits on the board of the [[asia-society|Asia Society]] alongside [[michael-chae|Michael Chae]], CFO of [[blackstone|Blackstone]] and a member of the [[harvard-management-company|Harvard Management Company]] board.[2] The article treats the shared board seat as evidence of "intelligence convergence," arguing that Harvard's capital handlers thereby exchange ideas with foreign intelligence veterans, corporate strategists and "post-national development technocrats."[2]

## Funding the Afghan mujahideen through BCCI

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" identifies Prince Turki al-Faisal, alongside [[kamal-adham|Kamal Adham]], as one of the Saudi intelligence chiefs who met repeatedly with [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] Director [[william-casey|William Casey]] and General [[muhammad-zia-ul-haq|Zia]] in [[pakistan|Pakistan]], in meetings arranged by [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] head [[agha-hasan-abedi|Agha Hasan Abedi]]; both Saudi officials were BCCI shareholders.[3]

As a result of those conclaves, Prince Turki distributed more than $1 billion in cash to Afghan guerrillas, an amount matched by another billion from the CIA.[3] Because the Saudis provided funding, the administration was able to bypass Congress.[3] The money reached the field through Pakistani channels: bags of dollar bills flown into Pakistan and banked by [[inter-services-intelligence|ISI]] director [[akhtar-abdur-rahman|Akhtar Abdur Rahman]] in accounts at the National Bank of Pakistan, BCCI and the Bank of Oman.[3]
