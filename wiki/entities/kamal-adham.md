# Kamal Adham

**Kamal Adham** was the head of Saudi Arabian intelligence, a brother-in-law of King Faisal, and an early insider of the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]] (BCCI).[1]

According to the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] material quoted in Badlands Media commentary, [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] worked closely with Adham after taking the helm of [[central-intelligence-agency|the CIA]] in January 1976, cementing strong relations with the intelligence services of both Saudi Arabia and the shah of Iran.[1] The article notes that it has previously addressed Bush's close connection to Adham, treating the relationship as one of the load-bearing links between American intelligence and the BCCI network.[1]

## Assessment

The biographical and relational details come from quoted journalism; the wider allegations of a coordinated corrupt network are the author's interpretation. Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors.[1]

## Career and CIA liaison role

Adham joined the Saudi government as chief of the external intelligence agency in 1964, and King Faisal appointed him head of Al Mukhabarat Al A'amah — later renamed the General Intelligence Presidency (GIP) — in 1965, making him the agency's first president.[2] His tenure lasted fourteen years until 19 January 1979, when he was dismissed and replaced by Turki bin Faisal.[2] He was additionally the primary liaison of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] for the Middle East as a whole from the mid-1960s until 1979, fostering ties with several Arab intelligence services as they built independent institutions, and he closely worked with [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]], who was appointed CIA director in 1976.[2]

## The BCCI plea bargain

In July 1992, New York City prosecutors told Adham's lawyers that their client was running out of time and was about to be indicted in the multibillion-dollar fraud and conspiracy case surrounding the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]].[2] He could ignore the indictment and remain in his mansion in [[jeddah|Jidda]], avoiding countries with easy extradition arrangements with the United States; he could come to New York and risk imprisonment; or he could cut a deal.[2] Adham chose the deal, which after two weeks of brutal negotiations cost him more than $100 million in fines and restitution but all but guaranteed he would stay out of American prison, while also requiring him to provide information.[2]

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the settlement as a de facto admission of guilt, and notes that beyond money laundering Adham served as one of the wealthy Arab front men BCCI used to purchase [[first-american-bankshares|First American Bank]], the largest bank in Washington, D.C.[2] He also emphasizes Adham's residence in Jidda — also rendered Jeddah — as a significant data point connected to many other threads.[2] Because Adham was the CIA's Middle East liaison and a Bush associate, Lange concludes that "BCCI was a George H.W. Bush operation" in which all the major players had ties to Bush and the agency.[2]

## BCCI shareholder and Casey's Saudi counterpart

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" names Kamal Adham, alongside [[turki-al-faisal|Prince Turki al-Faisal]], as one of the Saudi intelligence chiefs who met with [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] Director [[william-casey|William Casey]] and General [[muhammad-zia-ul-haq|Zia]] in [[pakistan|Pakistan]], in meetings arranged by [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] head [[agha-hasan-abedi|Agha Hasan Abedi]].[3] Both Adham and Turki were BCCI shareholders.[3]

The article identifies three intelligence services as jointly running the BCCI operation: the CIA and the services of Saudi Arabia and Pakistan.[3]
