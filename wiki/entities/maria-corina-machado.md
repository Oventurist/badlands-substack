# María Corina Machado

María Corina Machado is the Venezuelan opposition leader who, after the removal of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in January 2026, was passed over by Washington in favor of acting president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]].[1]

An unverified political commentary claimed that Machado brought into the [[white-house|White House]] an advisor who had previously publicly called [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "an idiot" for accusing former President Barack Obama of spying on him.[1] Asked why he supported Rodríguez rather than Machado, Trump cited Iraq, where the wholesale firing of police and generals produced ISIS — an answer Badlands commentary read as asserting that installing Machado would yield the same result.[1]

## Congressional backing (January 2026)

Later in January 2026, a group of U.S. lawmakers reportedly expressed support for Machado as a potential future leader of Venezuela, with members of the [[house-foreign-affairs-committee|House Foreign Affairs Committee]] said to have unanimously endorsed her after a closed-door meeting on Capitol Hill.[2] The lawmakers praised her long opposition to Maduro's government and called for free and fair elections.[2] The endorsement came despite Trump having previously dismissed her prospects as Venezuela's leader, describing her as unpopular and lacking the respect needed to lead the country.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the GOP's continued championing of Machado — whom he nicknamed "Maria Hakuna Matata" — as a further demonstration of the Republican Party acting subversively against Trump's Venezuela policy.[2]

## Absence from the transition (February 2026)

As [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s transitional government advanced a limited [[venezuela-amnesty-law|amnesty law]] and reshuffled its prosecutorial and human rights offices in February 2026, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] closed her commentary by asking simply, "Where's Machado?" — pointing to the opposition leader's conspicuous absence from a settlement in which the [[delcy-rodriguez|Rodríguez]] siblings retained power.[3]

## Blocked return to Venezuela (July 2026)

In July 2026 Deputy Secretary of State [[chris-landau|Chris Landau]] was accused by senior Trump administration officials of twice miscommunicating US policy regarding Machado's efforts to return to Venezuela following the June 24 earthquakes.[4] Landau reportedly appeared to signal support for her travel in conversations with Dutch and Panamanian officials, despite the administration's stated position that it would not facilitate her return.[4] One of Machado's travel plans was reversed after Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] personally reiterated US policy to her.[4]

Badlands commentary framed the episode as a narrative shift in which Machado — for more than twenty years a favorite of the GOP and the "Free Venezuela" movement, and nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize by Rubio and five other Republican senators in August 2024 — is recast as an intelligence-linked operative determined to seize power, while the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]]/[[delcy-rodriguez|Rodríguez]] government appears cooperative with Washington.[4] Her stated reason for returning is to challenge the Maduro government by running in a next election that interim President Delcy Rodríguez has not yet scheduled; both Rodríguez and President [[donald-trump|Trump]] have said elections would be inappropriate in the near term given the country's instability.[4]

## January 2026 White House meeting and Nobel handover

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said on Truth Social in January 2026 that he had met with Machado at the [[white-house|White House]], writing: "It was my Great Honor to meet María Corina Machado, of Venezuela, today. She is a wonderful woman who has been through so much. María presented me with her Nobel Peace Prize for the work I have done. Such a wonderful gesture of mutual respect."[5] Trump had previously declined to endorse Machado to lead [[venezuela|Venezuela]], and Israeli media citing unnamed sources had reported that his dismissal of her stemmed from her acceptance of a Nobel Peace Prize nomination he had sought for himself — claims for which no verification was provided.[5] No official US government statement detailed outcomes or next steps from the meeting, including on sanctions, recognition policy, or diplomatic status toward Venezuela.[5]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the encounter as a deliberate snub, noting that during an interview with [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] Trump had appeared unenthused at the prospect of the meeting, remarking only, "Yeah, I heard she wants to say 'hello.' I look forward to doing that."[5] He wrote that Machado was made to enter the White House grounds through the guard gate like an ordinary tourist, that nobody came outside to receive her, and that nobody escorted her out on departure — treatment he contrasted with the reception given [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[5] He argued the mainstream press had intended to frame the meeting as a major geopolitical event and that Trump had deflated it.[5]

The same commentary criticized Machado for remarks on Donald Trump Jr.'s podcast in which she disparaged Saudi Arabia, telling Americans to "forget about Saudi Arabia" because Venezuela would supply all the oil the United States wanted — a position Ghost dismissed by pointing to Saudi investment in the American economy and the standing of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[5] He also noted that Trump used the occasion to speak favorably to the press about [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], and that [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] recycled its earlier claim that two White House aides attributed Trump's souring on Machado to the Nobel Prize.[5] The overall message, in his reading, was that "groveling will not be rewarded nor tolerated."[5]

## "Grotesque political opportunism" (July 2026)

Following the [[2026-venezuela-earthquakes|deadly Venezuela earthquakes]], the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration criticised Machado's efforts to return to the country, with a senior [[white-house|White House]] official describing the move as "grotesque political opportunism."[6] Some reports indicated that US officials support her eventual return but believe doing so during the humanitarian crisis could undermine relief operations and destabilise the country, with the administration saying its immediate priority was earthquake response, including search-and-rescue operations and humanitarian assistance.[6]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] greeted the statement with "Finally!", arguing that when the administration "breaks character and starts speaking honestly" it provides social permission for others to follow, shifting the [[overton-window|Overton Window]].[6] He wrote that Machado "was always a bad guy," describing her original story as that of "literally a creation of [[usaid|USAID]]," dispatched by the Bush administration to topple [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]], and called it sad that so many promoted her simply because [[con-inc|Con Inc]] did so.[6]

## Vow to return (January 2026)

In interviews published in the week of January 7, 2026, Machado vowed to return to Venezuela and to reject the authority of the country's interim leadership following the US-led [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]], in which [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] was captured and removed from power.[7] She said she planned to return despite security risks and ongoing government crackdowns, asserting that Venezuelans deserved a democratic transition and free elections, and rejected the legitimacy of interim leader [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], whom she accused of corruption and repression.[7]

Machado said US officials had told Venezuela's new leadership they expected steps toward a political transition, although Washington had not formally endorsed her as a transitional leader.[7] She said she had not spoken directly with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in recent months but publicly welcomed US pressure aimed at forcing political change.[7] Trump said that while he considered Machado "a nice woman," he did not believe she had enough political support in Venezuela and indicated a preference for dealing with Rodríguez.[7] The Venezuelan government tightened internal security, moved against opposition figures and accused Machado and her allies of seeking to provoke unrest — claims the opposition denied.[7]

## Badlands commentary (January 2026)

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called Machado a "globalist stooge" openly campaigning for a second [[color-revolution|regime change operation]] in Venezuela, and argued that Trump had executed a "fake" regime-change operation in order to intercept the genuine one that globalist actors had spent a decade preparing.[7] In that reading, extracting Maduro while leaving his administration in control under Trump's authority siphoned the energy from the enemy's color revolution and "rug-pulled its final stage."[7] Burning Bright wrote that this outcome would not satisfy the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] or "the ghost of [[usaid|USAID]]," both of which he said had donated millions to Machado's campaigns en route to a Nobel Peace Prize denied to Trump — which he described as one of the cleanest reverse indicators available in the info war.[7]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] concurred that Trump may have launched the Maduro operation to derail an attempt to install Machado, adding that Trump had "chummed the waters" and that neoconservatives such as Senator [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]] had taken the bait and revealed themselves.[7]

## Trump's public rejection (January 2026)

In the immediate aftermath of [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] dismissed the notion that Machado — described in Badlands coverage as the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s preferred replacement — would become the leader of [[venezuela|Venezuela]], saying it would "be very tough" for her because she lacks support or respect within the country.[8] In the same remarks he said the United States was "going to take back the oil that, frankly, we should have taken back a long time ago," and instead honored [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]'s designation of [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] as acting president.[8] Badlands summarized the rebuff in a single word: "Brutal."[8]

## Sidelined after the transition (January 2026)

When 283 Venezuelan lawmakers elected the previous May were sworn in alongside interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] in January 2026, Reuters reported that few could be categorized as opposition because the faction of candidates directed by Machado had boycotted the contest.[9] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] circulated commentary mocking her as having "decided to show up and kiss Trump's ass after he dismissed her," with reports that she planned to return to Venezuela.[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that globalist anger over [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]] stemmed not from [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]'s ouster — his administration remaining largely intact — but from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s refusal to endorse and his active condemnation of Machado, whom he characterized as the NGO-funded, Nobel Prize-"winning" globalist opposition leader.[9]

## 2024 CIA payroll allegations

During the dispute over the [[2024-venezuelan-presidential-election|2024 Venezuelan presidential election]], commentator [[niko-house|Niko House]] alleged that Machado "has been operating as a foreign agent on behalf of the United States" and that "the [[cia|CIA]] literally writes her a paycheck."[10] The accusation came as Venezuela's Supreme Court of Justice reviewed the election results and the opposition declined to participate in the proceedings or submit requested evidence, drawing criticism from Venezuelan constitutional expert Dr. Olga Alvarez.[10] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the claim was not definitive proof of CIA backing but described House's supporting logic — centered on Venezuela's oil reserves and the [[glencore]] litigation pursued by [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] — as compelling.[10]

## Exit from Venezuela and appearance in Oslo (December 2025)

As the principal opposition figurehead and rival to Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolas Maduro]], Machado had reportedly been in hiding for many months, having last appeared in public nearly a year earlier and having been briefly detained in Caracas on 9 January.[11] Fearing an arrest that could imprison her indefinitely, she avoided public political and protest events even as her international profile rose with the award of the Nobel Peace Prize.[11]

In December 2025 reports indicated she had been safely whisked out of the country while Caracas authorities were distracted by the US seizure of a Venezuelan oil tanker.[11] She then surfaced in Oslo, Norway, announcing from a hotel balcony that many people had "risked their lives" to get her there: "I am very grateful to them, and this is a measure of what this recognition means to the Venezuelan people."[11] Accounts described her wearing a wig and disguise as she left a Caracas-suburb hideout on the Monday of that week.[11]

Badlands commentators treated the escape narrative with open scepticism. [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] questioned why her X account had geolocated her in the United States only weeks earlier, and [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] itself remarked that the purported details "sound straight out of a Hollywood movie."[11] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the globalist media was parading a "'populist' Nobel-winning puppet" whose backstory was invented and whose campaign was NGO-funded, while cautioning that Maduro might likewise not be what audiences assumed.[11] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the Oslo sequence as a "bizarre and contrived psyop" running in parallel with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s alleged seizure of Maduro's oil tankers, likening the whole affair to professional wrestling.[11]


## Escape from Venezuela and Nobel ceremony (December 2025)
Machado spent more than a year in hiding after her opposition movement defeated President Nicolás Maduro in the 2024 election by a wide margin according to voting records validated by international observers; Maduro refused to leave office and ordered a massive crackdown on the opposition.[12]

Getting Machado out of Venezuela and safely to Oslo for the Nobel Peace Prize ceremony required an operation planned in a single week by U.S. Special Forces veteran Bryan Stern and his Grey Bull Rescue Foundation team, codenamed Operation Golden Dynamite in a nod to Alfred Nobel, the prize's founder and the inventor of dynamite.[12] Stern, whose team has carried out hundreds of extractions worldwide, called the mission unusually difficult because of her profile, describing her as "the second most popular person in the Western Hemisphere after Maduro."[12]

In her first U.S. interview after winning the prize, Machado told CBS News that she "absolutely support[s] President Trump's strategy" in Venezuela as his administration escalated its pressure campaign to remove Maduro.[12] Maduro, for his part, claimed days earlier that Machado was part of a human trafficking ring and said he had given evidence to the previous U.S. administration.[12]

## Support for US military escalation against Venezuela (November 2025)

In November 2025, Machado — that year's Nobel Peace Prize laureate — said that the US military buildup off [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s coast could help bring about regime change, and signaled that she would welcome US strikes on her own country if they helped remove President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[13] Asked on Bloomberg's *The Mishal Husain Show* whether she backed US military action, she replied, "I believe the escalation that's taking place is the only way to force Maduro to understand that it's time to go."[13]

The remarks came as Washington accused Maduro of ties to drug cartels and labeled him a "narcoterrorist," after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] deployed a naval armada to the western Caribbean and US forces began striking alleged drug-smuggling vessels off the Venezuelan coast in September.[13] Media reports indicated Washington was expanding its naval presence, with analysts suggesting the mission could extend beyond counter-narcotics; Trump denied planning direct strikes inside Venezuela but reportedly reviewed a list of potential targets.[13]

Badlands host GhostofBasedPatrickHenry called the resulting headline — a Nobel Peace Prize winner calling for a military attack on her own country — his favorite of 2025, and criticized Machado for claiming that Hamas was operating inside Venezuela as a justification for bombing.[13] He disputed her credentials as a prospective leader, noted her promises to reverse Maduro-era prohibitions on matters such as abortion and transgender education, and suggested her elevation reflected a regime-change operation associated with the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]].[13] He also addressed a weekend post by [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]] disavowing his commentary, saying he held no hard feelings but that Byrne had never answered his question: if Maduro is a dictator, he is "the weakest dictator of all time" for allowing a public figure like Machado to shoot marketing campaigns in Venezuela over 25 years.[13]

## Exclusion from the 2024 ballot

Machado, then 56, won the Venezuelan opposition primary in 2023 and was favored in polls to defeat [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in the July 2024 elections, potentially ending the authoritarian rule of Hugo Chávez's successor.[14] The Maduro government barred her from the ballot and then blocked the Democratic Unitary Platform (PUD) opposition coalition from registering a candidate before a midnight deadline in the early hours of March 26, 2024; her chosen successor, Dr. [[corina-yoris|Corina Yoris]], was also blocked.[14] The [[white-house|White House]] criticized the exclusion through Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]], who said Washington was deeply concerned by the National Electoral Council's decision and suggested Venezuela could lose sanctions relief absent free and fair elections.[14]

## Reaction to the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize award

Machado's receipt of the [[2025-nobel-peace-prize|2025 Nobel Peace Prize]] for her "tireless work promoting democratic rights for the people of Venezuela" drew an accusation from the [[white-house|White House]] that the Nobel Committee had placed "politics over peace" by passing over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; Trump nonetheless called her to congratulate her and said she deserved the award.[15] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized her as "another one of these NGO clones," spawned during the Bush administration, whose organization Súmate was funded by the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], and who has spent 22 years trying to overthrow the Venezuelan government — first under Hugo Chávez and then under [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[15]

He further reported that on December 4, 2018, Machado sent a letter to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] through the Argentinian embassy in Venezuela asking for his assistance in overthrowing Maduro, and that she later appeared on Israeli television promising to move the Venezuelan embassy in Israel to Jerusalem if installed in office.[15] Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] posted about the 2018 letter on X, asserting that it disqualified her from the prize and accusing her of attempting to bring "the Zionist genocide" to South America.[15] The commentary also noted that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had nominated Machado for the prize in August 2024, and that her win drew attention to a shadow group within the US government working to overthrow the Venezuelan government for more than two decades.[15]

## Assange criminal complaint

In December 2025, WikiLeaks co-founder [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] filed a criminal complaint with Sweden's Economic Crime Authority and War Crimes Unit against the [[nobel-foundation|Nobel Foundation]], arguing that awarding Nobel Peace Prize funds to Machado misused those funds because Alfred Nobel's will bars their use to advance war or foreign military intervention.[16] The complaint cites a series of public statements in which Machado endorsed potential US military intervention in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and defended Washington's use of force amid its military buildup in the Caribbean, including justifying US strikes on boats off Venezuela's coast that had killed 95 people to date.[16]

Assange warned that the prize could be turned into an "instrument of war," and Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the filing accuses Machado of serious crimes including "facilitation of war crimes and crimes against humanity," alongside all 30 members of the Nobel Board of Directors.[16]

## Named in Maduro's Musk accusation (October 2024)

In October 2024, Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] accused [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] of spending at least $1 billion to incite violence in Venezuela after the July 28 presidential election, and named Machado — whom he described as his political opponent and a businesswoman — as the distributor of the alleged funding to "fascist" groups.[17] Maduro claimed that the US government was ultimately behind the attempts to oust him from power.[17] Maduro had been declared the winner of the July vote by national election authorities, though the United States maintained that victory had been stolen from opposition candidate Edmundo Gonzalez.[17]

## July 2024 election aftermath

Speaking to The Guardian after Venezuela's disputed July 2024 election, Machado urged [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] to accept that his exit from power was inevitable and to come to terms with the end of his 11-year rule, during which Venezuela fell into a devastating economic and humanitarian crisis that forced millions to flee abroad.[18] She and candidate [[edmundo-gonzalez-urrutia|Edmundo González Urrutia]] said they could prove their victory, having obtained 73.2% of the polling-station voting tallies.[18] Their statements came as thousands of protesters took to the streets to repudiate Maduro's disputed claim to a third term.[18]

## The 2025 Nobel Peace Prize and the "Venezuelan squeeze"

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], writing in October 2025, placed Machado at the center of the endgame in [[venezuela|Venezuela]], describing her as another engineer-turned-dissident whose 2025 Nobel Peace Prize cast her not merely as a national icon but as the West's anointed spearhead in the final push against [[chavismo|Chavismo]].[19] Critics lambasted the award as the Nobel Committee's latest endorsement of regime change, exposing the prize itself as a hollow farce.[19]

Detractors argue Machado is no unifier, contending that her free-market zeal and U.S. ties — decades of [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]] funding and whispers of [[cia|CIA]] briefings — alienate the Chavista base, the slum-dwellers [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] once lifted with petrodollar largesse.[19] [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]]'s camp erupted, with state media branding her "La Sayona," a vengeful specter from Venezuelan folklore, while loyalist rallies torched her effigies.[19]

DeLarme writes that the Nobel gave Machado leverage: she used her acceptance speech, delivered by video from hiding, to thank the Venezuelan people and to call on the United States and its allies for support.[19] The second Trump administration responded with tighter sanctions on Russian and Chinese firms operating in Venezuela and increased pressure on Maduro's inner circle.[19] DeLarme characterizes Machado as "clearly the spiritual successor" to [[juan-guaido|Juan Guaidó]], and notes the [[white-house|White House]] justification for renewed pressure — alleged prisoner releases into the United States and drug smuggling — is widely regarded as a pretense for foreign policy objectives decades in the making.[19] Machado refuses dialogue with Maduro unless he agrees to a verified transition.[19]
