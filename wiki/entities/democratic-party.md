# Democratic Party

The Democratic Party is one of the two major United States political parties. In March 2026 it was reported to be undergoing an internal identity crisis as it looked past the presidency of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] toward the 2028 election cycle.[1]

## Post-Trump identity debate

According to [[axios|Axios]] reporting, Democrats were engaged in internal debates over the party's identity, policy priorities, and electoral strategy, with some strategists and party figures saying the party lacks a clear unifying message heading into 2028.[1] Several Democrats argued the party has been defined primarily by opposition to Trump and must now articulate a broader agenda beyond that focus.[1]

[[jim-messina|Jim Messina]], who managed [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2012 campaign, told Axios that "You can't win a presidential election on opposition alone," estimating that the midterms would be 85–90% driven by voter opposition to Trump and only 10–15% by what Democrats stand for — a calculation he said could not be relied upon in 2028.[1]

The internal split ran along policy lines as well: some Democrats believe the party should concentrate on working-class economic issues, arguing that recent elections exposed weaknesses with certain voter groups, while others say Democrats should continue emphasizing social and cultural policy priorities alongside economics.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the debate as Democratic anxiety about winning elections *after* the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterms]], when opposing Trump becomes an irrelevant platform assuming the President is not running again in 2028.[1]
