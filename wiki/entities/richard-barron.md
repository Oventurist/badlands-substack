# Richard Barron

Richard Barron is the elections director of [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia, whose December 2020 testimony became central to allegations of vote-count manipulation in the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]] as presented before the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]] in January 2026.

## Testimony during certification week

According to investigator [[joe-rossi|Joe Rossi]]'s presentation to the State Election Board on January 21, 2026, Barron testified during the certification week of December 2020 that the county's count was short of expectations. Rossi quoted Barron directly: "He said, 'We knew we were short.' He said, 'We called the state.' I'm assuming that's the Secretary of State's office."

Rossi's account placed Barron's testimony in a timeline in which Fulton County's counts did not match as of November 19, 2020, and a report was run at 12:06 p.m. and emailed at 12:13 p.m. on Thursday, December 3, 2020 to consultant [[ryan-macias|Ryan Macias]] — at the time affiliated with [[the-elections-group|The Elections Group]]. The January 23, 2026 Badlands Brief summarizes the implication: "They knew on November 19, 2020 that the counts didn't match. So on December 3, 2020, it appears that, they made them match."

The Brief presents Barron's testimony as evidence that Fulton County officials knew their vote totals were below what they thought they needed, and links it to the January 2, 2021 phone call in which Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] was alleged to have misled President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] about the state of the count. Badlands commentators read the episode as corroboration of Trump's claim that the vote was "under what they thought they needed."
