# Non-Aligned Movement

The **Non-Aligned Movement** is described in Badlands Media commentary as the political current whose tone was set by the [[bandung-conference|Bandung Conference]] of 1955.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Origins

Contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] traces the movement to the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence that emerged from Bandung, presenting those principles as a restatement of the same universal freedoms found in [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]]'s [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]] of 1941, the [[atlantic-charter|Atlantic Charter]], the [[united-nations|UN Charter]], the original designs of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]], and the [[universal-declaration-of-human-rights|UN Declaration of Human Rights]] of 1947.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Continuity with the multipolar alliance

Ehret's essay positions this tradition as the ancestor of the contemporary multipolar bloc gathered at the 2025 [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organisation]] summit in Tianjin, whose [[tianjin-declaration|Tianjin Declaration]] rests on sovereign equality and the rejection of unilateral coercive measures.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) In both cases, he argues, the common theme is the common good, common security and coordination of action to improve the conditions of life of peoples and sovereign nations, as outlined by the UN Charter rather than by the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)
