# Defense Intelligence Agency

The Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) is the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s military intelligence arm. In June 2026 it became the center of a major counterintelligence story when NBC News and *The New York Times* reported that the agency had elevated its assessment of the threat posed by Israeli espionage to a "critical" level.[1]

## The "critical" Israeli espionage assessment

The reported assessment consisted of a seven-page document and an accompanying chart evaluating Israeli intelligence-gathering capabilities.[1] It allegedly identified concerns about Israeli efforts to collect information on U.S. administration deliberations concerning [[iran|Iran]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[1] Citing unnamed sources, the reports named [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], [[elbridge-colby|Elbridge Colby]] and [[michael-dimino|Michael DiMino]] among officials allegedly targeted by collection efforts.[1]

The Pentagon declined to comment.[1] A [[white-house|White House]] official told NBC News that "the entire story is false," and a spokesperson for the Israeli Embassy in Washington dismissed the allegations as politically motivated, saying Israeli intelligence activities are directed at adversaries rather than allies.[1] The assessment surfaced amid broader tensions between the Trump administration and the Israeli government over Iran and developments in Lebanon.[1]

## Badlands reading

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the report "an interesting narrative deployment," noting that its origin in NBC News and [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] made it possible — if not likely — to be fake news, while conceding the story was "more than plausible."[1] He argued the inclusion of DiMino made the report compelling, since DiMino's stated view that [[israel|Israel]] is a security liability for the United States gives [[mossad|Mossad]] an obvious motive to surveil him.[1] Whether or not the reporting is true, he wrote, was secondary to its entry into the public zeitgeist at a moment when public sentiment was turning against Israel and Trump was ostensibly turning against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] — a convergence he read as "Game Theory in motion" rather than coincidence.[1]

## Firing of Lt. Gen. Jeffrey Kruse (August 2025)

In August 2025, Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] fired Lt. Gen. [[jeffrey-kruse|Jeffrey Kruse]], the head of the DIA, after the agency's initial intelligence assessment of damage to Iranian nuclear sites from U.S. strikes angered President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], according to two people familiar with the decision and a White House official.[2] The preliminary assessment, details of which had leaked to the media a few months earlier, found that [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program had been set back only a few months by the strikes, contradicting assertions by Trump and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[2] The dismissal was described as the latest upheaval in U.S. military and intelligence leadership.[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] joked that the headline should have read "Trump fires general," since Kruse had "denied the success of Top Gun: Maverick 2 - IRL."[2]

## February 2024 leadership change

Defense Intelligence underwent a command change on February 2, 2024, presided over by Deputy Secretary of Defense [[kathleen-hicks|Kathleen Hicks]] together with a change at [[us-cyber-command|Cyber Command]] the same day and at [[united-states-northern-command|Northern Command]] on February 5.[3] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the agency's mission statement — that it "provides intelligence on foreign militaries and their operating environments so the United States and its allies can prevent and decisively win wars" — and treated the clustering of these command changes just before Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]]'s hospitalization as significant.[3]

## June 2025 leaked Iran strike assessment

Numerous outlets reported on June 25, 2025 on an early Defense Intelligence Agency assessment suggesting that U.S. strikes had set Iran's nuclear program back only a few months.[4] The assessment was characterized as low-confidence and preliminary.[4] The White House strongly disputed its conclusions and criticized leakers within the intelligence apparatus, with press secretary Karoline Leavitt calling the leak "a clear attempt to demean President Trump, and discredit the brave fighter pilots who conducted a perfectly executed mission to obliterate Iran's nuclear program."[4] Secretary of State Marco Rubio argued at the NATO summit that anti-Trump elements in the intelligence community had manipulated media outlets into unfavorable coverage of the operation.[4] The Iranian foreign ministry itself subsequently admitted that its nuclear facilities had been "badly damaged" by the strikes, undercutting the leaked assessment.[4]

## Flynn's tenure and subsequent litigation (2023)

Coverage of [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]]'s March 2023 malicious prosecution lawsuit identified him as the former head of the Agency under the Obama administration before he became national security adviser to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5] The suit, filed against the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the [[fbi|FBI]], alleges that the counterintelligence investigation opened against him in August 2016 over supposed ties to Russia was baseless and politically motivated.[5]
