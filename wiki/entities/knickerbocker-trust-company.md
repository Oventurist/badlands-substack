# Knickerbocker Trust Company

The Knickerbocker Trust Company was a bank based in New York City that became a central player in the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]].[1] It is cited in a July 11, 2024 Badlands Media essay on the exploitation of trust as a closing example of how deception can be used to exploit the trust that depositors and beneficiaries place in institutions bearing the name.[1]

## Founding and business

The bank was chartered in 1884 by Frederick G. Eldridge, a friend and classmate of the financier [[j-p-morgan|J.P. Morgan]].[1] As a trust company, its main business was serving as trustee for individuals, corporations and estates — the classic fiduciary role in which property is held by a third party who owes a defined duty to act for the good of a beneficiary.[1] That structure is precisely the sense of "trust" that the essay identifies as personal, private property valuable enough to safeguard, and therefore as an asset attractive to dishonest actors.[1]

## Collapse

In 1907 Knickerbocker's funds were being used in a plan to drive up the cost of copper by cornering the market.[1] The gamble came undone due to the dumping of millions of dollars in copper.[1] When the National Bank of Commerce announced it would no longer clear Knickerbocker's checks, the announcement triggered a run of depositors demanding their funds, forcing Knickerbocker to suspend operations.[1]

## Consequences

The failure exacerbated an ongoing decline in the stock market that saw the Dow Jones Industrial Average lose 48 percent of its value from January 1906 to November 1907.[1] The resulting banking crisis is also seen as the final event that led Congress to form the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]] in 1913.[1] In the essay's framing, an institution founded on fiduciary trust was used to speculate against the interests of those who had extended that trust, and the crisis it produced became the justification for centralising monetary authority still further — a pattern the author places alongside [[operation-trust|Operation Trust]] and the broader argument of [[trust-the-plan|"TRUST the plan"]].[1]
