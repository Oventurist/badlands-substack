# Arthur Andersen

Arthur Andersen LLP was a major American accounting firm destroyed by its criminal prosecution during the Enron scandal, a case led by [[andrew-weissmann|Andrew Weissmann]] as deputy and later director of the Enron Task Force.[1]

## Prosecution and reversal

The conviction obtained by Weissmann's team wiped out the venerable firm along with roughly 85,000 jobs worldwide.[1] In 2005 the United States Supreme Court **unanimously** overturned the conviction, a result the Badlands Media feature "Enjoy the Show" cites as evidence of how badly the prosecution had trampled the defendants' rights — the article argues it takes extraordinary misconduct to draw a unanimous reversal.[1]

Commentary in *The Hill*, quoted in the article, notes that courts repeatedly reversed Weissmann's most touted victories over his tactics, including the related prosecution of four Merrill Lynch executives over a business transaction with Enron; those executives served up to a year in prison without bail pending appeal before the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed the bulk of the case.[1] Weissmann quietly resigned from the Enron Task Force as the judge in the Enron Broadband prosecution began excoriating his team.[1]

## Significance in the article

Badlands author [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents the Andersen case as the defining exhibit in Weissmann's record of "scorched earth" and unethical prosecution — behavior for which, in Lange's view, he should have been disbarred and charged, but which instead made him an "untouchable" architect of the [[two-tier-justice-system|two-tier justice system]].[1] The article also asks whether that same history is why [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] tapped Weissmann to target Paul Manafort.[1]
