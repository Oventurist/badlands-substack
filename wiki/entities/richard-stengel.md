# Richard Stengel

Richard Stengel is a former Time magazine editor and a former senior official in the Obama administration's [[state-department|State Department]], where he served as Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs.[1] Badlands Media's "The Information Imperium" highlights Stengel's presence on the advisory board of [[newsguard|NewsGuard]] and his self-description as having been the U.S. government's "chief propagandist."[1]

## Affiliations

The article lists Stengel's institutional memberships — the Council on Foreign Relations, the World Economic Forum and the Atlantic Council — and notes that the same cluster of affiliations recurs among other NewsGuard advisers, including former NSA and CIA director [[michael-hayden|Michael Hayden]].[1] For the article's authors this overlap is the point: a company marketing itself as a neutral referee of news credibility is advised by figures whose careers were built inside the national-security and transatlantic policy establishment.[1]

## Significance in the article

Stengel functions in "The Information Imperium" as the clearest illustration of its thesis that state propaganda capability has migrated into nominally private institutions.[1] Where the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] once planted stories directly, and the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] later provided overt funding for what had been covert work, NewsGuard's board — with an admitted former chief propagandist among its advisers — represents the commercial layer of the same apparatus, shaping which outlets advertisers and institutions treat as legitimate.[1]
