# Delcy Rodríguez

Delcy Rodríguez is the Venezuelan politician who became acting president of Venezuela following the January 3, 2026 U.S. military operation that captured [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1]

## U.S. engagement

[[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] traveled to Caracas to meet Rodríguez in what was described as the highest-ranking U.S. official visit since Maduro's ouster; the two-hour meeting focused on strengthening bilateral cooperation, including security and efforts to curb Venezuela's role as a haven for criminal networks.[1] The visit followed Trump's [[safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue-executive-order|executive order safeguarding Venezuelan oil revenue]] of January 9, 2026.[1]

Rodríguez had earlier been the object of American backchannel diplomacy: Blackwater founder [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]] secretly traveled to Caracas in November 2019 to meet her, which mainstream commentary at the time speculated was a Trump attempt to establish a backchannel with the Maduro regime even as the administration publicly backed opposition leader Juan Guaidó.[1] Her brother [[jorge-rodriguez|Jorge Rodríguez]] met U.S. envoy [[richard-grenell|Ric Grenell]] in Mexico City in late 2020 and again in Caracas in January 2025.[1]

## Trump's preference over the opposition

Asked why he was supporting Rodríguez over opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] invoked Iraq: "If you ever remember a place called Iraq where every single person was fired—the police, the generals, everybody was fired and they ended up being ISIS… I remember that."[1] Erik Prince, appearing on Steve Bannon's show, likewise argued that the United States should not repeat its Iraq mistakes and should allow Rodríguez to contract directly with any private military contractor needed to shore up security.[1] Badlands commentary reads her government as operating under a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocol involving figures from [[devolution|Devolution]] theory.[1]

## Hydrocarbons law overhaul (January 2026)

On January 29, 2026, as Acting President of [[venezuela|Venezuela]], Rodríguez signed an overhaul of the nation's hydrocarbons law opening the oil industry to foreign and private investment, after the measure passed the National Assembly.[2] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] told the Senate Foreign Relations Committee that the law "basically eradicates many of the Chavez era restrictions on private investment in the oil industry," calling it a big step even if insufficient to attract full investment.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that while Rodríguez publicly feigns contempt for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and GOP neoconservatives, she is simultaneously accelerating capital investment in Venezuela, and that Trump's "arrest" of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] allowed her to place the government into a state of continuity that may have fortified it against subsequent overthrow attempts.[2]

## ICC withdrawal (July 2026)

In the weekend before July 27, 2026, a group of Zionist congressmen traveled to Caracas to meet with Rodríguez, described in Badlands coverage as Venezuela's interim president.[3] Rodríguez then announced [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s decision to withdraw from the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]], prompting allegations that she was acting on behalf of [[israel|Israel]] and was a "Zionist puppet."[3] Badlands offered an alternative reading: the ICC had been investigating the Venezuelan government since [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] took office in 2014 and had refused all requests to close its inquiries, apparently intending to indict both Maduro and Rodríguez, giving her an independent motive to exit the court.[3] The [[state-department|State Department]] then welcomed the withdrawal and used it to urge other countries to follow.[3]

## Prosecutorial stand-down (May 2026)

In late May 2026 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] directed federal prosecutors in Miami to stand down on criminal investigations into Rodríguez, then Venezuela's acting president.[4] The move was intended to support efforts to stabilize [[venezuela|Venezuela]] following [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]'s ouster and to facilitate U.S. investment in the country's oil sector, with prosecutors told to avoid actions that could upset ongoing diplomatic and economic initiatives with Rodríguez's government.[4]

A longtime target of the [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] with prior U.S. sanctions for alleged involvement in drug trafficking, gold smuggling, and money laundering, Rodríguez has never been criminally charged in the United States, and sanctions against her were lifted after she was recognized as Venezuela's sole head of state.[4] She has hosted U.S. delegations and participated in ceremonies with American oil executives as oil flows have increased.[4] The directive was described as aligning with a broader pattern of pausing high-profile probes into certain foreign leaders during the current administration.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], calling her "Our Girl, Delcy 'Kokomo' Rodriguez," noted the irony that it was the first Trump administration that sanctioned her and opened the investigation, and asked whether she had "never been the target" — whether the entire sequence was misdirection or a ruse to mislead certain parties.[4] He attributed the original sanctions to [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] (in office April 2018 – September 2019) and pointed to [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]]'s secret November 2019 trip to Caracas to meet Rodríguez on Trump's behalf as the pivot point, situating it in a chain that included the January 3, 2020 strike on [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]], friction with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over West Bank settlements and the Abraham Accords, and the 2020 election.[4] "The timeline is the story, and the story makes sense," he wrote, suggesting a similar dynamic might be in play with Maduro.[4]

## The "15 minutes to comply" recording

In late January 2026 a recording of a meeting held after the U.S. operation that captured [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] was leaked to a local journalism collective called La Hora de Venezuela, in which Rodríguez said U.S. forces had given members of Maduro's cabinet "15 minutes to comply or they would kill us."[5] She also claimed the remaining leadership was initially told that Maduro and his wife Cilia Flores had been killed rather than captured.[5]

Badlands commentator [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who nicknamed her "Kokomo," read the episode as theater: he noted that Rodríguez had already affirmed Maduro signed a Continuity of Government order in December in anticipation of his departure, and that her compliance with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s vision for Venezuela suggested the account was an embellishment if not a fabrication.[5] In his reading, the narrative gives Rodríguez credibility as an opponent of unipolar hegemony while advertising to the world that the U.S. military is fielding new weapons technologies such as the [[trumps-discombobulator|Discombobulator]] — a coordinated deployment that reasserts American military dominance while sparing her the label of puppet leader.[5]

## Oil sector reopening

As acting president Rodríguez championed opening [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s oil industry to increased foreign investment following Maduro's ouster, backing legislative measures granting private and foreign companies greater operational autonomy, lower royalty and tax requirements, and international arbitration provisions.[5] She and Trump worked together to bring the Venezuelan oil industry back online, with Trump calling on the commodity trading firms [[vitol|Vitol]] and [[trafigura|Trafigura]] to broker deals with potential buyers.[5] See [[venezuelan-oil-sector-liberalization-2026|Venezuelan oil sector liberalization]].

## US relations under Trump (February 2026)

In February 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described relations between [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and the United States as "extraordinary" and said Washington was "dealing very well with President Delcy Rodriguez, and her Representatives."[6] He reported that oil was starting to flow and that "large amounts of money, unseen for many years, will soon be greatly helping the people of Venezuela," crediting Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and other US officials.[6] Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] toured Venezuelan oil fields the same week.[6] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the abrupt shift in tone, observing that critics who had pushed for war with Venezuela were "silent on the administration's rhetoric" — offering "no shout outs for Machado. No shade on Rodriguez."[6]

## CIA engagement (January 2026)

CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] became the first senior Trump administration official to visit Caracas, meeting Rodríguez and Venezuelan military leaders to warn that the country could no longer serve as a safe haven for US adversaries such as [[china|China]], Russia and [[iran|Iran]].[7] Reporting indicated that Washington had backed Rodríguez over opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] on the basis of classified CIA assessments.[7] The agency prioritized establishing an intelligence annex for informal liaison channels with Venezuelan officials, factions and opposition figures, while the [[state-department|State Department]] handled long-term diplomacy and assessed reopening the embassy shuttered since 2019.[7] See [[cia-re-entry-into-venezuela-2026|CIA re-entry into Venezuela (2026)]].[7]

## Amnesty and institutional reshuffle (February 2026)

During his February 2026 [[state-of-the-union|State of the Union]] address, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] discussed his strong partnership with Rodríguez.[8] The following day it emerged that her brother [[jorge-rodriguez|Jorge Rodríguez]] was pushing a limited [[venezuela-amnesty-law|amnesty bill]] through the [[national-assembly-of-venezuela|National Assembly]] to release hundreds of political protestors, a measure reportedly omitting homicide and other violent crime.[8] In the same period Attorney General [[tarek-william-saab|Tarek William Saab]] resigned and was made acting Ombudsman, with [[larry-devoe|Larry Devoe]] named acting Attorney General.[8]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted the oddity that Delcy and Jorge Rodríguez, presented in [[ralph-pezzullo|Ralph Pezzullo]]'s *Stolen Elections* narrative as regime insiders, were now releasing political prisoners their own government had jailed, asking whether an overthrown regime's succession plan is normally followed and its next-in-line members employed to run the new government.[8]

## Removal of Alex Saab from government posts

After Rodríguez took power on January 3, businessman [[alex-saab|Alex Saab]] was removed from key government positions, including his Cabinet role as Minister of Industry — a post [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] had given him in October 2024 — and his position overseeing foreign investment deals tied to Venezuela.[9] Rodríguez announced the departure in a Telegram post, thanking Saab "for his service to the Homeland" and saying "he will be taking on new responsibilities."[9] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the phrase "new responsibilities" as foreshadowing public testimony in a major U.S. criminal case, and argued that Rodríguez, Maduro and the Venezuelan government were coordinating with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against a cabal controlling both countries' energy sectors.[9] Saab was deported to the United States in May 2026.[9]


## Acting presidency (January 2026)

After the U.S. operation that detained President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], Venezuela's Supreme Court ordered Rodríguez to assume the role of acting president "in the absence" of Maduro, stating that she would ensure continuity of government.[10] She was formally sworn in on January 5, 2026 at the National Assembly while Maduro appeared in a New York court on federal drug charges.[10] [[nicolas-maduro-guerra|Nicolás Maduro Guerra]] held the Bible at the ceremony, and Rodríguez immediately clarified that Nicolás Maduro remained the one true president of Venezuela.[10]

Under reported Qatari-brokered transition proposals described as "Madurismo without Maduro," Rodríguez would remain a continuity figure while retired Gen. [[miguel-rodriguez-torres|Miguel Rodríguez Torres]] led a transitional government, in exchange for U.S. corporate access to Venezuelan oil and mining industries.[10]

## January 2026 defiance of Washington and prisoner releases

In her first public address since [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] was removed from power, Rodríguez declared that she had had "enough orders from Washington" and called for Venezuela's internal conflicts to be resolved by Venezuelan politics without foreign interference.[11] "Enough orders from Washington on politicians in Venezuela. Let Venezuelan politics resolve our differences and internal conflicts. Enough of foreign powers," she said, according to AFP.[11]

Since January 8, 2026, Venezuela has released political prisoners under the interim government, and Rodríguez announced that 626 individuals had been released as of January 23, 2026, though human rights NGOs disputed the figures amid family protests over remaining detainees.[11] President [[donald-trump|Trump]] praised the releases on Monday, January 26, calling them a "powerful humanitarian gesture" and thanking the leadership of Venezuela, while his public praise of the interim government drew criticism regarding Rodríguez's leadership and her reported accomplishments.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the exchange as "political theater from Kokomo," arguing that despite the theatrics the evidence suggests Rodríguez and Maduro are working with Trump against the [[deep-state|Deep State]]/[[cia|CIA]], [[mi6|MI6]], and Israel/[[mossad|Mossad]].[11]

## Contested legitimacy (January 2026)

Opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] rejected Rodríguez's legitimacy as interim leader, accusing her and other officials of corruption and repression, and vowed to return to Venezuela to press for a democratic transition and free elections.[12] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] indicated a preference for dealing with Rodríguez rather than backing Machado as a transitional leader, saying Machado lacked sufficient political support inside the country.[12] Machado said US officials had told Venezuela's new leadership they expected steps toward a political transition, though Washington had issued no formal endorsement of a transitional figure.[12]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he had settled on "Kokomo" as Rodríguez's "Trump-a-Mania stage name," a nickname that recurred across later Badlands coverage of her interim government.[12] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that by swapping [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] out for his second-in-command while leaving the administration in place, Trump was baiting globalist actors into attempting a second [[color-revolution|color revolution]] within weeks of the first.[12]

## Appointment and Trump's ultimatum (January 2026)

Immediately after [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]], Venezuela's Supreme Tribunal of Justice named Rodríguez acting president, though her legitimacy was disputed internationally.[13] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] clarified that [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] had himself designated her as acting president and said he would honor the appointment, while dismissing the prospect of opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] taking power on the grounds that Venezuelans neither respected nor supported her.[13] Trump paired that recognition with an explicit threat: if Rodríguez did not grant the United States "total access" to rebuild Venezuela's infrastructure and oil operations — work he said the oil companies would pay for — "she will pay a very big price — probably even bigger than Maduro."[13]

Rodríguez's initial public statement on Maduro's arrest blamed "the Zionists" for attempting to enslave Venezuela, and she insisted that there was only one president of Venezuela and that it remained Nicolás Maduro.[13] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the entire sequence as a negotiated surrender rather than a decapitation, noting the incongruity of honoring the succession designated by a man being prosecuted as a narco-terrorist dictator.[13]

## Swearing-in and Trump's conditions (January 2026)

After the US raid that captured [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], Rodríguez was sworn in as interim president, condemning the raid but calling for "peace and dialogue" with Washington and proposing a "balanced and respectful" cooperative agenda while insisting Venezuela would "never return to being the colony of another empire."[14] She chose to take the oath atop the sarcophagus of [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]] at his monument in Caracas, then delivered a speech honoring Chávez and echoing Maduro's rhetoric that there is only one true president of Venezuela — Maduro.[14]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly backed Rodríguez on the grounds that she was "picked by Maduro," having dismissed the idea of installing [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]].[14] He placed her on what he called a "short leash," demanding she crack down on drug trafficking, expel Iranian and Cuban operatives, halt oil sales to US adversaries, release detained Americans and eventually hold free elections before stepping aside, warning she could pay a "bigger price" than Maduro if she resisted while asserting the US would temporarily "run" Venezuela to exploit its oil reserves.[14] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the near-identical rhetoric of Trump and Rodríguez and revisited [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]]'s meeting with her in Venezuela six years earlier, which many analysts wrongly treat as the moment she betrayed Maduro.[14]

## Formal swearing-in as interim president (January 2026)

Following the removal of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], Rodríguez — Venezuela's Vice President and Oil Minister — was formally sworn in as interim president in early January 2026, alongside 283 lawmakers elected the previous May.[15] Reuters reported that few of the incoming legislators could be categorized as opposition, since candidates directed by Nobel laureate [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] had boycotted the election.[15] The only lawmaker absent was first lady [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]], who was in US custody.[15]

Gunfire was reported at [[miraflores-palace|Miraflores Palace]] following the swearing-in; according to some accounts, security forces at the palace were firing at a drone.[15]
