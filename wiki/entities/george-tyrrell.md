# George Tyrrell

George Tyrrell was a leading modernist theologian whose influence fell upon the young [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] while the latter completed his studies in England after the Jesuit order was barred from France in 1901.[1]

According to Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the modernists around Tyrrell were obsessed with reconciling Christianity with the new ethics and science emerging in the modern age.[1] The most difficult challenge confronted by Jesuit modernists of this period was the attempted reconciliation of two seemingly irreconcilable systems: Christianity, which saw mankind as sacred, and Darwinism, whose mechanistic universe denied the existence of the divine in humanity or in the broader cosmos.[1] Harmonizing these two worlds became Chardin's mission in life, making Tyrrell's modernist milieu the formative intellectual environment for what Ehret calls the Darwinian perversion of Christianity.[1]
