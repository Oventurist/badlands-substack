# J. P. Morgan

J. P. Morgan was the banker and financial house at the center of the article's account of American banking power in the early twentieth century.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Two of the seven men present at the secret 1910 [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]] meeting were tied to the firm: Henry P. Davison, Senior Partner of the J. P. Morgan Company, and [[benjamin-strong|Benjamin Strong]], who attended representing J. P. Morgan.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Charles D. Norton, also present, was President of the Morgan-dominated First National Bank of New York.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The [[pujo-committee|Pujo Committee]] was formed specifically to investigate the monopoly of J. P. Morgan and New York's other powerful bankers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Its report found that no fewer than eighteen major financial corporations were under the control of a cartel led by J. P. Morgan, George F. Baker, and James Stillman, and identified over $22 billion in resources and capitalization controlled through 341 directorships held in 112 corporations by members of the empire headed by Morgan.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Representation at Jekyll Island

Badlands Media commentary states that the six men who met secretly at the [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]] in November 1910 — including [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson Aldrich]] and [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]] — represented wealthy bankers such as J.P. Morgan, the Rockefellers and the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]], and that the plan they wrote laid the foundation for the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]].[3]

## Role in the Creation of the Federal Reserve

Badlands Media describes J.P. Morgan as an alleged [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]] agent and the founder of J.P. Morgan and Co., a predecessor of three of the largest banking institutions in the world — Chase Manhattan, Morgan Stanley and Deutsche Bank.[4]

It is widely believed, the article states, that in 1907 Morgan and his cohorts — colloquially known as the [[money-trust|money trust]] — secretly crashed the stock market, knowing that thousands of small banks were vastly overextended, some with reserves of only 1 percent under the [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve]] principle.[4] In the conventional telling, copper magnate F. Augustus Heinze's attempt to corner the copper market caused runs on major banks, and Morgan arrived at the height of the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]] to save the banking community in an act of heroic altruism, afterward being touted as a beacon of financial wisdom and the savior of the nation.[4]

Morgan absorbed the Mercantile Trust and six more trust companies and banks, including The Trust Company of America and Lincoln Trust, with his Guarantee Trust and Manhattan Trust companies taking control and the Rothschilds' US emissary [[august-belmont|August Belmont]] heading the merger.[4] He promised Charles Barney he would help save the Knickerbocker Trust, a competitor of his Bankers' Trust Co., but pulled the plug at the last minute in what the article calls an act of calculated cruelty; Barney committed suicide soon afterward.[4]

## Aftermath and Investigation

In 1908 Morgan was hailed as a hero by Princeton University president [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]], who said that "all this trouble could be averted if we appointed a committee of six or seven public spirited men like J.P. Morgan, to handle the affairs of our country."[4] Critics regard Morgan instead as a monster who fed off economic destruction; either way his actions consolidated banking power into the hands of a few large banks and catalyzed one of the largest corporate consolidations in American history.[4]

The [[pujo-committee|Pujo Committee]] was later created to investigate Morgan and other Wall Street bankers, finding that he and others including Rockefeller, [[paul-warburg|Warburg]] and [[jacob-schiff|Schiff]] had gained control of major manufacturing, transportation, mining, telecommunications and financial markets throughout the United States.[4] After the [[aldrich-plan|Aldrich bill]] failed, Morgan, Warburg, [[bernard-baruch|Bernard Baruch]] and others advanced the Glass-Owen Bill that established the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]].[4]

## The 2012 London Whale loss

JP Morgan sustained roughly $6 billion in losses in the 2012 [[london-whale|"London Whale"]] episode.[2] According to Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], [[boaz-weinstein|Boaz Weinstein]] of [[saba-capital|Saba Capital]] learned that the bank was shorting a particular investment class, bought into that class himself, and then — speaking at an investment conference hosted by JP Morgan — urged the attending investors to buy the same instruments, producing a short squeeze that cost the bank billions while Weinstein made a fortune.[2] Carlson cited the episode as proof that [[deep-state|Deep State]] institutions readily plunder one another.[2]
