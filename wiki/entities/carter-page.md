# Carter Page

Carter Page is a former [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign adviser who became the subject of FISA surveillance during the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] investigation and subsequently sued the [[fbi|FBI]] over that surveillance.[1]

## Supreme Court denial, June 2026

In June 2026 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|US Supreme Court]] declined to revive Page's lawsuit against the FBI.[1] Page had sought damages stemming from the surveillance conducted during Crossfire Hurricane, arguing that the government relied on flawed information in obtaining its FISA warrants.[1] The Court's refusal left the lower-court rulings against Page in place.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] grouped the denial with contemporaneous [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] developments as further evidence of [[weaponization-of-government|weaponized government]], noting that Page was denied a chance to argue again that the government had been turned against him during [[james-comey|James Comey]]'s tenure.[1] The commentary observed that the ruling brought FISA abuse back into public discussion at a moment when [[congress|Congress]] was struggling to pass a FISA extension.[1]

## Target of the FISA surveillance

In "Do You Believe in Coincidences?", Page is described as the nominal target of the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|four FISA warrant applications]] used to surveil the 2016 [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign, applications signed by [[james-comey|James Comey]], [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]], [[sally-yates|Sally Yates]], [[dana-boente|Dana Boente]] and [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]] and repeatedly declaring him a Russian agent.[2] Despite that framing, the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller investigation]] never indicted him.[2] Badlands attributes this to Page having been a CIA asset rather than a Russian one — a fact the [[fbi|FBI]] allegedly knew and concealed from the FISA court, with attorney [[kevin-clinesmith|Kevin Clinesmith]] admitting he altered an email by adding "and not a source."[2] The article says the deception was exposed by the [[john-durham|Durham]] investigation and entered into court records for future prosecutions.[2]

## The FISA warrant and its renewals

Evidence surfaced by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] at the [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] trial established that the [[fbi|FBI]] verified not one of the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]] allegations before lifting them into the surveillance warrant it obtained on Page.[2] When the Bureau could not corroborate the material, it offered [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] up to $1 million in October 2016 to do so, without success, and at the time the warrant issued Steele had not even identified his primary sub-source.[2] After learning that the sub-source was Danchenko, a man its Baltimore field office had investigated for suspected espionage, the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team and, in the final instance, the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel's Office]] renewed the warrant three times.[2] Badlands contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] argued there was no excuse for the three renewals, since by the third nobody involved could have retained illusions about the dossier's veracity.[2]

## The FISA warrant as cover (Badlands account)

"[[the-biggest-boomerang-in-history|The Biggest Boomerang In History]]" states that [[james-comey|Comey]]'s [[fbi|FBI]] altered documents and hid exonerating evidence from the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|FISA]] court judges in order to obtain a warrant on Page, which then allowed the bureau to spy on the Trump campaign and frame [[donald-trump|Trump]].[3]

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s central point is that this warrant was not the beginning of the surveillance but its cover: the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration was already spying on the campaign and on [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] through [[five-eyes|FVEY]] partners well before any FISA application, making [[fisagate|FISAGATE]] a cover-up for [[spygate|SPYGATE]].[3] The article adds that White House visitor logs existed for the FISA court judge who approved the Page warrants on what Lange calls phony evidence and doctored emails, and that Obama classified this material and moved it to his presidential library.[3] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
