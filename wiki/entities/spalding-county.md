# Spalding County, Georgia

**Spalding County** is a county in the U.S. state of Georgia that became a focal point of election-integrity scrutiny after the November 7, 2023 municipal elections. Spalding is one of several Georgia counties whose local rules require a post-election hand count of ballots, a requirement its election board adopted amid what the [[atlanta-journal-constitution|Atlanta Journal-Constitution]] described as "deep distrust of Georgia's voting machines."[1]

## 2023 hand count

During the required hand count of the November 7 results, observers reported that in every race across every precinct the hand-count totals did not match the machine counts.[1] Anecdotal reports from observers during the advance-vote portion of the count were described as explosive, with estimates of hundreds or more ballot inaccuracies.[1] Notably, the hand counts of Election Day and absentee ballots did not produce the same variances; the discrepancies were confined to advance (early) voting — the same pattern later observed in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]].[1]

The certified figures were far smaller than the initial anecdotal reports suggested: a total variance of nine votes, netting to a five-vote impact on outcomes. In most races other than the ballot measure, the machine count added votes relative to the hand tally.[1]

## Certification

Despite a push to delay certification until the cause of the mismatches could be established, the county election board voted 2–1 on the Tuesday following the count to certify the results. The lone holdout was Board of Elections Secretary [[roy-mcclain|Roy McClain]], who could not be reached for comment.[1] Critics argued that although the variances were immaterial to outcomes, certifying without explanation failed to address the machine-manipulation concerns that had prompted the hand count in the first place.[1]

The voting devices used in Spalding County are the same statewide systems at issue in [[curling-v-raffensperger|*Curling et al v. Raffensperger et al*]].[1] The episode is treated in detail at [[2023-georgia-municipal-election-discrepancies]].[1]
