# Hunter Biden

Hunter Biden is the son of President Joe Biden and the central figure in House Republican investigations into the Biden family's foreign business dealings.[1]

## Contempt of Congress resolutions

On Monday, January 8, 2024, House Republicans introduced resolutions to hold Hunter Biden in [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] for failing to comply with subpoenas, which GOP congressional leaders said violated federal law.[1] Both the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight and Accountability Committee]] and the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] released resolutions and identical supporting reports.[1] The reports accompanying the resolutions stated that by failing to follow the subpoena, "Biden has violated federal law, and must be held in contempt of Congress."[1]

The dispute arose from Hunter Biden's decision the previous month to appear at the Capitol on the day he was scheduled to be deposed, where he said he would testify publicly but would not sit for a closed-door deposition with members of [[congress|Congress]] about his family's business dealings.[1] House Republicans announced on Friday, January 5, 2024 that they would move to hold him in contempt after he refused to comply with the subpoena.[1]

## Collapse of the Delaware tax case (2023)

In August 2023, Delaware U.S. District Judge [[maryellen-noreika|Maryellen Noreika]] granted a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] request to dismiss without prejudice the two tax misdemeanors that had been filed against Biden, preserving the option of charging him in Washington, D.C. or the Central District of California.[2] The dismissal followed the earlier collapse of his plea agreement, which had generated national coverage.[2]

The day after Noreika's ruling, three [[latham-and-watkins|Latham & Watkins LLP]] attorneys — Brian C. McManus, Timothy H. McCarten and Matthew S. Salerno — moved to withdraw as his counsel, bringing the total number of departing defense lawyers to five.[2] Their motion said Biden consented to the withdrawal, that it caused him no prejudice, and that he remained "completely satisfied" with the continued representation of [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] and co-counsel Jones.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that the departures made sense given the client's toxicity and the prospect of far graver exposure, and argued that the plea deal's collapse had punctured the narrative bubble and enabled what [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] calls "re-runs" of the Biden family's documented conduct — including the 459 crimes catalogued by [[marco-polo|Marco Polo]] in its October 2022 [[hunter-biden-laptop|laptop report]] and the disclosure of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s email aliases.[2]

## Guilty plea to federal tax charges (September 2024)

On Thursday, September 5, 2024, Hunter Biden entered a guilty plea to all nine federal tax charges brought against him by special counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]], his attorney said in court, a development that shocked federal prosecutors.[3] Defense attorney [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] said earlier that day in federal court that Biden intended to switch his plea, having initially pleaded not guilty.[3] Federal prosecutor Leo Wise responded that "this is the first we are hearing about this," and a source familiar with the special counsel's team told Fox News Digital that the matter was not a done deal but rather an offer the defense had put on the table.[3]

The judge had Biden agree under oath that he would no longer remain silent and advised him that a guilty plea would surrender all of his trial rights.[3] Before a 30-minute recess, Judge Mark Scarsi observed that "the court doesn't need the government's agreement to accept an Alford plea."[3] The president's son remained free on bond pending sentencing scheduled for December 16, 2024.[3] The reversal came after his lawyers had prepared to argue that he was too intoxicated to pay his taxes.[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] characterized the plea as "SIGNAL AF," endorsing [[just-human|Just Human]]'s reading that the Biden family has been more overtly controlled by patriots than commonly assumed, and arguing that a shock plea appearing to surprise Biden's own attorneys on the eve of trial was evidence that "the machine did not expect" the move.[3]

## Shokin, Burisma and the European Commission report (2023 revisit)

In September 2023 the New York Post reported that the [[european-commission|European Commission]] had praised Ukrainian Prosecutor-General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]]'s anti-corruption work in a December 18, 2015 progress report published nine days after then-Vice President Joe Biden demanded Shokin's ouster, undercutting the claim that the European Union had joined that demand.[5] Badlands commentary treated the document as further evidence that aid was withheld from Ukraine until the prosecutor investigating Hunter Biden's business associates was fired, recalling [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]]'s testimony that [[burisma]] pressured the Bidens to remove Shokin and that the gas company paid Hunter roughly $83,000 per month for "the brand" — in that reading, payment for access to his father's influence over U.S. foreign policy.[5]

## CIA blocking of the Kevin Morris interview

A whistleblower told House impeachment leaders that the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] blocked federal investigators from interviewing [[kevin-morris|Kevin Morris]] — a Hollywood lawyer and patron of the first son, described in press coverage as his "sugar brother" — during a five-year probe into Hunter Biden's alleged tax crimes.[6] According to the account given to [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight]] chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] and Judiciary chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], the agency stopped [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] and [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] investigators from interviewing Morris in August 2021, summoning two DOJ officials to Langley to be told he "could not be a witness."[6] The claim was set out in a letter to CIA Director [[william-burns|William Burns]].[6]

## 2023 lawsuit against the IRS

On September 18, 2023, Hunter Biden sued the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] in federal court in Washington, D.C., alleging that its agents illegally released his tax return information and that the agency failed to establish safeguards protecting the confidentiality of his records.[7] He sought all documents involving the disclosure, $1,000 for each unauthorized disclosure, and attorneys' fees.[7] The suit did not name IRS whistleblowers [[gary-shapley|Gary Shapley]] and [[joseph-ziegler|Joseph Ziegler]] as defendants but centered on disclosures they and their lawyers made in public statements, congressional testimony, and interviews; Judge [[timothy-kelly|Timothy Kelly]], a Trump appointee, was assigned the case.[7] It was filed amid other legal exposure, including a three-count felony gun indictment brought the prior week by special counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] and potential additional tax charges.[7] The case is covered at [[hunter-biden-v-internal-revenue-service|Hunter Biden v. Internal Revenue Service]].[7]

## The 2015 loan-guarantee memos

In August 2023, [[just-the-news|Just the News]] reported on U.S. government memos indicating that an interagency task force had recommended a third $1 billion loan guarantee for Ukraine in October 2015, weeks before then–Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] threatened to withhold it unless Prosecutor General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] was fired.[8] Shokin's office was at the time investigating [[burisma|Burisma Holdings]], the energy firm that employed Hunter Biden and paid him millions while his father was vice president, and which the [[state-department|State Department]] deemed to have engaged in bribery.[8] Badlands commentary concluded that Burisma was using Hunter to pressure his father into securing Shokin's removal, and that U.S. officials in the Kyiv embassy told Ambassador [[marie-yovanovitch|Marie Yovanovitch]] at the end of the Obama administration that Hunter Biden had in fact impacted the U.S. anti-corruption agenda in Ukraine.[8]

## January 2024 associate testimony

In the week beginning January 22, 2024, two of Biden's business associates were scheduled to appear for closed-door transcribed interviews before the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight]] and [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary]] committees as part of the House impeachment inquiry into his father, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[9] [[mervyn-yan|Mervyn Yan]], who worked with Biden through the Chinese energy company [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC]] and Hudson West III and had been subpoenaed for financial records the previous year, was set to testify on Thursday.[9] [[rob-walker|Rob Walker]], also subpoenaed in 2023, was expected before both committees on Friday.[9]

Financial records subpoenaed by the Oversight Committee showed that members of the Biden family, including Hallie Biden and Sara Biden, received more than $1 million in payments from accounts related to Walker and his Chinese business ventures with Hunter Biden.[9]

## Threatened defamation suit against Fox News (April 2024)

In April 2024, Hunter Biden threatened to sue [[fox-news|Fox News]] for defamation, exploitation of his image, and publication of hacked photographs, according to a letter obtained by [[axios|Axios]] that had been sent to the network the previous week.[10] Axios framed the move as the latest salvo in the president's son's more aggressive legal and press strategy amid criminal charges and tabloid coverage, and his first major action after navigating the impeachment inquiry into his father.[10] His legal team wrote that it anticipated "imminent" litigation and asked that prominent Fox hosts — including [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]], [[jesse-watters|Jesse Watters]] and Maria Bartiromo — preserve documentation.[10]

The lawyers also demanded that Fox News take down "The Trial of Hunter Biden," a six-part mock trial focused on his foreign business dealings.[10] Tina Glandian, a partner at Geragos & Geragos, wrote that "while routinely defaming and disparaging Mr. Biden, FOX has simultaneously sought to profit by the unlawful exploitation of Mr. Biden's image, name, and likeness for commercial purposes and reprehensible dissemination of salacious photographs depicting Mr. Biden."[10] Glandian further demanded that Fox hosts update viewers about the February 2024 indictment of Alexander Smirnov, a former informant charged with lying to the FBI about Hunter Biden's business dealings.[10]

In February 2023, Summit News reported that [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] refused to write a poem about Republican Congresswoman [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] on grounds of her being controversial, yet produced an effusive poem praising Hunter Biden when asked — including lines urging readers to judge him "not by his surname, / Or by the actions that bring him shame."[11] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cited the contrast as proof of political bias baked into big tech's AI systems.[11]

## Plea deal collapse, August 2023

On the night of Sunday, August 13, 2023, Hunter Biden told a federal judge that the Justice Department was attempting to renege on a major part of his agreement with the government — his enrollment in a diversion program for gun offenders, which he said he had signed and which granted him broad immunity from future federal prosecution.[13] The claim came in a court filing by his lawyer, Christopher Clark, and formed the latest exchange in the back-and-forth between Biden and [[david-weiss|David C. Weiss]], the Trump-appointed prosecutor leading the long-running investigation into the president's son.[13]

Shortly after Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] elevated Weiss to special counsel, government lawyers stated in court papers on the preceding Friday that they and Biden were at an impasse over plea negotiations and that no agreement had been reached.[13] Under the proposed deal, Biden would have pleaded guilty to two tax misdemeanors and entered the diversion program, allowing him to avert prosecution on a gun charge; in his Sunday filing he rebutted the prosecutors' account, saying he had signed the agreement in court the previous month and intended to abide by it.[13] The investigation had appeared to be concluding in June 2023 when both sides announced a deal, but at a Delaware hearing the following month the two sides expressed drastically different views of the immunity provision and the judge placed the matter on hold.[13]

## December 2023 standoff over public testimony

In a December 2023 letter to Rep. [[james-comer|James Comer]], chairman of the [[house-oversight-committee|House Committee on Oversight and Accountability]], Hunter Biden's attorney [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] reiterated that his client would testify before Congress only in public.[14] Lowell wrote that Biden had offered to appear on December 13, 2023 — the date Comer had reserved — or another date that month, to answer any question pertinent to the subject matter of Comer's November 8 letter, and framed the choice as a response to Comer's own repeated public remarks.[14] He argued the committee "uses closed-door sessions to manipulate, even distort, the facts and misinform the American public," while a public hearing "would ensure transparency and truth."[14] Comer had rejected the November 28 offer, insisting testimony occur behind closed doors.[14]

The standoff came as House Republicans neared a vote to formally authorize the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], following subpoenas Comer issued to Hunter Biden, the president's brother [[james-biden|James Biden]] and other family members and business associates; Hunter was directed to appear for a deposition on December 13 and James on December 6.[14] Badlands writer [[just-human|Just Human]] summarized the week as James Biden failing to appear, Hunter attempting to duck the closed-door deposition, the [[house-ways-and-means-committee|House Ways and Means Committee]] again interviewing IRS whistleblowers, and special counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] arguing that Hunter should not be permitted to subpoena documents from Trump, Barr, Rosen and Donoghue.[14]

## Refusal to attend the March 2024 public hearing

In March 2024 Hunter Biden refused to attend a public House Oversight Committee hearing scheduled for March 20 alongside several of his former business associates who had been critical of him.[15] His attorney Abbe Lowell told Oversight Chairman James Comer in a letter that neither Biden nor his counsel could attend because of a court date in California the following day, but added that "the scheduling conflict is the least of the issues."[15] Lowell wrote that "if you are serious about pursuing this oversight purpose in a legitimate and bipartisan fashion, you would hold a hearing with relatives of former President Trump about whom you indicated you 'would ask'," in which case "Mr. Biden would consider an invitation for that event."[15]

The exchange followed Hunter Biden's closed-door deposition, during which he had raised the Trump family's financial dealings and Comer said he would ask the Trump family about them.[15] Biden had said in November that he would testify before the Oversight Committee only if the hearing were public, while Comer proposed a closed-door session first followed by a public one; after House Republicans moved toward holding him in contempt of Congress for defying a congressional subpoena, he agreed to the closed-door deposition, whose transcript was released the previous month.[15]

## Suspension of D.C. law license

On June 25, 2024, the [[dc-court-of-appeals|D.C. Court of Appeals]] suspended Hunter Biden's law license after his conviction earlier that month on three federal gun charges, ordering him "suspended immediately from the practice of law" in the District after considering the indictment and jury verdict.[16] The court directed D.C.'s Board on Professional Responsibility to institute a formal proceeding to determine the nature of the offense and whether it involved moral turpitude, following an earlier finding by the D.C. Office of Disciplinary Counsel that his offenses amounted to a "serious crime" triggering automatic suspension.[16] Reporting noted that he faced up to 25 years in prison, though as a first-time offender the maximum was considered unlikely.[16]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the framing that the trial had aired the Biden family's darkest moments, calling the case a diversion that neutralized the sex crimes, business crimes and influence peddling associated with Hunter Biden and his uncle Jim, and arguing he would face no material consequences because his father controlled the Justice Department.[16] She noted that sentencing had not been scheduled, that Biden had moved for a new trial on the argument that the federal court in Delaware lacked jurisdiction — a motion she believed should have preceded trial — and that he was due back in court in California in September in the tax case brought by Special Counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]].[16]

## Overlap between family business and presidential aides (June 2024 reporting)

Politico reporting in June 2024 found that for years [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] shared a bookkeeper with Hunter, and that when [[jim-biden|Jim Biden]] wanted to learn more about one of Hunter's associates he hired the former head of Joe Biden's Secret Service detail to investigate.[17] House impeachment inquiry interviews, public records and emails reviewed by the outlet showed that many of the president's closest staffers and advisers doubled as his relatives' business associates, both during and after their government service, complicating efforts to distance the president from the family's ventures.[17] The report noted that several of Jim and Hunter Biden's business partners had been convicted of federal fraud and corruption crimes in recent years.[17]
