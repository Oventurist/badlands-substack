# CodePink

CodePink Women For Peace is a US antiwar activist organization founded by [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]].[1]

## Funding allegations

In the 2026 federal grand jury investigation of [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]]'s funding web, CodePink was identified as one of six key nonprofits receiving money routed from Shanghai, with $1,330,000 attributed to the group.[1] Reporting described Benjamin as Singham's wife and ally, situating CodePink within a network that also included [[peoples-forum|People's Forum Inc.]], [[tricontinental|Tricontinental Ltd.]], and Breakthrough BT Media Inc.[1]

## Nuestra America Convoy (2026)

CodePink helped organize the March 2026 [[nuestra-america-convoy|Nuestra America Convoy]], which delivered roughly 20 tons of goods to Havana, [[cuba|Cuba]].[2] Federal investigators subsequently subpoenaed Benjamin and streamer [[hasan-piker|Hasan Piker]] as part of a [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] probe into the trip, with more than 40 Americans reportedly under scrutiny by the [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] for potential sanctions violations.[2] Piker was photographed with CodePink figures in Havana.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described CodePink as an NGO that "has long operated in the same ecosystem of activist NGOs that parallel the [[southern-poverty-law-center|SPLC]] model — leveraging humanitarian framing and narrative positioning to advance anti-American rhetoric while maintaining operational ties that are now facing scrutiny."[2]

## State Department report (2026)

A [[state-department|State Department]] report previewed in July 2026 is expected to examine Code Pink as part of an allegation that [[cuba|Cuba]]'s communist government has for decades supported and cultivated radical left-wing movements in the United States and the Western Hemisphere.[3] The report discusses financial backing from businessman [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]] for Code Pink and the [[peoples-forum|People's Forum]], alleging that these organizations promoted positions aligned with the Cuban government and other authoritarian regimes opposed to US interests.[3]
