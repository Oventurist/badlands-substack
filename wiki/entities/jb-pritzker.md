# JB Pritzker

JB Pritzker is the Democratic Governor of Illinois and one of the most prominent state-level critics of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's immigration enforcement and domestic military deployment policies.[1]

## Racial profiling allegations against ICE

Pritzker accused [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] of targeting individuals on the basis of their "brown or black" skin color during the administration's immigration crackdown.[1] The allegation was put to [[white-house|White House]] deputy chief of staff for policy [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] by [[cnn|CNN]] anchor [[boris-sanchez|Boris Sanchez]] on October 6, 2025; Miller dismissed the framing as "a dumb question" and rejected the accusation outright, arguing that illegal immigration harmed black, white, and Latino workers alike.[1]

## National Guard litigation

Illinois and the city of Chicago filed suit on October 6, 2025 to block Trump's move to federalize and deploy the [[national-guard|National Guard]], with lawyers for the state and city arguing that the foundational principle separating the military from domestic affairs was "in peril."[1] The filing asked a federal judge to block the "relocation, federalization, or deployment" of any state's National Guard to Illinois, asserting that the state was being singled out because of its political leadership, that federal law permits federalization only in cases of rebellion or invasion, and that the move violated the [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus Act]].[1]

## Response to the Supreme Court National Guard ruling (December 2025)

After the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] turned away the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s emergency request to deploy [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops in Illinois on December 23, 2025, Pritzker said in a statement: "This is an important step in curbing the Trump Administration's consistent abuse of power and slowing Trump's march toward authoritarianism."[2] The administration had argued the troops were needed to protect federal agents conducting immigration enforcement in the Chicago area, over the strenuous objections of local officials.[2]
