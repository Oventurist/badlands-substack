# People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals (PETA)

People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals, commonly known as PETA, is an animal-rights organization that has long opposed federally funded animal experimentation. In Badlands Media coverage of May 2025 it appears as one of two groups — with the [[white-coat-waste-project|White Coat Waste Project]] — whose campaigns against "cruel" research projects were advanced by the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's cancellation of research grants.[1]

## The 2025 Harvard grant cancellations

Reporting relayed by Badlands from the *Washington Examiner* described how Trump's targeting of [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] research funding ended grants supporting experiments on baby monkeys removed from their mothers, including tests that examined the harm to developing brains when the eyes of young monkeys were sewn shut.[1]

PETA senior vice president Kathy Guillermo responded: "PETA applauds the Trump administration's decision to strip funding from one of the cruelest experiments ever devised and perpetrated on infant monkeys."[1] The episode was framed within a broader "Making America Greater for Animals" movement that portrayed Trump, in continuation of his first term, as the "best friend of animals."[1]

## Significance

The alignment of PETA — an organization normally associated with progressive activism — with a Trump administration policy action is treated in the Badlands framing as an illustration of how the second-term deregulation and spending-cut agenda produced unexpected coalitions.[1] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] commented chiefly on the incongruity of the resulting headline.[1]

## Praise for the FDA and EPA animal-testing phase-out (April 2025)

An earlier instance of the same alignment came in April 2025, when the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] drew an outpouring of support from animal advocacy groups and lawmakers for announcements ending animal testing within programs at the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] and the [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]].[2] PETA senior vice president Kathy Guillermo said the group "applauds the FDA's decision to stop harming animals and adopt human-relevant testing strategies for evaluating antibody therapies," calling it "a significant step towards meeting the agency's commitment to replace the use of animals — which PETA has worked hard to promote."[2] She added that "all animal use, including failed vaccine and other testing on monkeys at the federally-funded primate centers, must end," and urged the FDA to "further embrace 21st-century science."[2]

The statement followed an FDA announcement that it would phase out an animal testing requirement for antibody therapies and other drugs in favour of testing on materials that mimic human organs.[2] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that PETA was simply acknowledging good policy, that MAGA and PETA should be able to find common ground in the MAHA era where all parties claim to advocate healthier and more ethical science, and that she expected leftists might rebuke PETA as they had Tesla — while noting the pull of politics makes the durability of such an alliance uncertain.[2]
