# Abu Mahdi al-Muhandis

Abu Mahdi al-Muhandis was an Iraqi militia commander who led the Kataib Hezbollah militia and served as a senior figure in Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces, the coalition of predominantly Shiite militias formed in 2014 to fight ISIS.[1] He is described in Badlands Media's account of the Trump administration's Middle East campaign as the operational link between Iranian direction and armed Shiite factions inside Iraq.[1]

## Role in the Baghdad embassy attack

Al-Muhandis was among three leaders of powerful militia groups seen at the December 31, 2019 protest in which demonstrators attacked the United States embassy in Baghdad, scaling the walls and forcing the gates of the compound.[1] The demonstrators were drawn largely from the Popular Mobilization Units, a force that a 2016 Iraqi law recognized as an independent military body answering directly to the prime minister, though most observers regarded groups such as [[kataib-hizballah|Kataib Hezbollah]] as maintaining strong links to [[iran|Iran]] and its security forces, from which they received funding, support and direction.[1] The protest followed five United States airstrikes in Iraq and Syria on facilities controlled by Kataib Hezbollah, which killed at least 25 people and wounded 51 in the first significant American military response to weeks of rocket attacks on US-Iraqi targets.[1]

## Relationship with Soleimani

According to reporting cited in the article, [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]] secretly met with Shiite militia leaders in mid-October 2019 to coordinate attacks on American forces, in the hope of triggering a US reprisal that would shift Iraqi public anger from Iran toward the United States.[1] Soleimani delegated that campaign to al-Muhandis in his capacity as a head of the Popular Mobilization Forces and commander of Kataib Hezbollah.[1]

## Death

Al-Muhandis was killed on 3 January 2020 in the same American drone strike near Baghdad International Airport that killed Soleimani and nine others.[1] Badlands Media characterises the strike as removing Soleimani, al-Muhandis and other militant leaders in a single operation, a decapitation of the command structure the author argues had sustained decades of conflict in the region.[1]
