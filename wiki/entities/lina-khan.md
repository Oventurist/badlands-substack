# Lina Khan

Lina Khan served as Chair of the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]] during the Biden administration and was the public face of the agency's April 2024 rule banning nearly all noncompete agreements in American employment contracts.[1]

## The noncompete ban

On Tuesday, April 23, 2024, the Federal Trade Commission voted narrowly to prohibit nearly all noncompetes — employment agreements that typically prevent workers from joining competing businesses or launching their own.[1] The agency received more than 26,000 public comments in the months preceding the vote.[1]

Khan publicly referenced worker testimony gathered during that comment period, saying the Commission had heard from employees who were stuck in abusive workplaces because of noncompetes.[1] She cited one account in which an employer merged with an organization whose religious principles conflicted with a worker's own, and the noncompete locked that worker in place, unable to freely switch to a job that did not conflict with their religious practice.[1] These accounts, Khan said, pointed to the basic reality that robbing people of their economic liberty also robs them of all sorts of other freedoms.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary treated the ruling as, on paper, largely good — a genuine push-back against industries that had gone too far in stripping employees of leverage — and conceded broad agreement with the FTC's position that noncompetes should be unenforceable.[1] The critique offered was structural rather than substantive: that the announcement, paired the same week with the Department of Transportation's airline refund rule, carried a Trojan Horse in the form of a tacit endorsement of the administrative authority of federal agencies over private enterprise and the free market.[1] In a truly free market divorced from administrative oversight, the argument went, such abuses would correct themselves.[1]
