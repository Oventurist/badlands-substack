# Minnesota

Minnesota is a US state that in late 2025 and early 2026 became the focal point of the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s interior immigration enforcement effort.[1]

## Operation Metro Surge

Beginning in December 2025, [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] deployed federal immigration agents across the state and produced more than 4,000 arrests in the Minneapolis–St. Paul metropolitan area.[1] The crackdown drew violent protests, and federal authorities announced its conclusion in February 2026.[1]

Border Czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], reporting from the ground in Minnesota, said the operation had reduced [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]]'s target list, secured what he called unprecedented cooperation from Minnesota counties on jail notifications and custody transfers, and coincided with a decrease in what he described as unlawful agitator activity as state and local law enforcement stepped up.[1] Homan concluded that "Minnesota communities are safer as a result," while agents deployed there were to be redirected to enforcement elsewhere in the country.[1]

## Medicaid funding suspension (February 2026)

In February 2026 the Trump administration suspended [[medicaid|Medicaid]] payments to Minnesota, citing concerns about fraud in the state's program, as the opening move of the federal [[war-on-fraud|War on Fraud]] led by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]].[2] Federal officials said approximately $259 million in Medicaid funding was being withheld while the review proceeded, and Minnesota officials were given 60 days to address federal concerns and implement corrective measures in order to potentially restore the funds.[2] Vance announced the action alongside [[centers-for-medicare-and-medicaid-services|Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services]] Administrator [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]].[2] Minnesota Democratic leaders criticized the suspension as politically motivated and warned it could impact beneficiaries.[2]

## Federal surge and fraud probes (January 2026)

In early January 2026 the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] launched a large-scale deployment of roughly 2,000 federal agents drawn from [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]], [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] and [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] to Minneapolis and St. Paul under [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]].[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] subsequently ordered an additional 1,000 CBP agents to the Twin Cities, potentially raising the total federal presence to about 3,000 personnel.[3] Trump posted on Truth Social: "FEAR NOT, GREAT PEOPLE OF MINNESOTA, THE DAY OF RECKONING & RETRIBUTION IS COMING!"[3]

White House deputy chief of staff for policy [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] told Fox News that federal officers possess immunity in the conduct of their duties and that any city or state official, illegal alien or agitator who obstructed them would be committing a felony and could face charges of criminal conspiracy against the United States.[3]

Minnesota also became the seedbed of the administration's expanding fraud investigations: Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] pledged a nationwide expansion of the Minnesota-originated probes, and Trump publicly denounced Minneapolis food-stamp businesses as fronts for fraud, calling for those involved to be deported.[3]

## Federal sanctuary lawsuit, September 2025

The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] sued Minnesota, [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] and [[saint-paul|St. Paul]] on 29 September 2025 over their [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary city]] policies, with Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] accusing state officials of jeopardizing the safety of their own citizens by allowing illegal aliens to circumvent the legal process.[4] The [[doj-lawsuit-against-minnesota-sanctuary-policies|34-page complaint]] sought invalidation of sections of the state constitution, Twin Cities municipal codes, and Hennepin County administrative orders that federal prosecutors said unconstitutionally prevented local cooperation with Immigration and Customs Enforcement.[4]

## HHS child care funding freeze (December 2025)

On December 30, 2025, the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] froze hundreds of millions of dollars in child care funding for Minnesota, the latest in a series of federal moves against Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]]'s administration as it sought to manage fraud allegations.[5] The freeze followed probes by the [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] into allegations that the state's Democratic leaders were complicit in exploiting federal nutrition and child care funds.[5] Federal prosecutors had by then charged more than 90 people with defrauding the federal government in an investigation dating back to 2021, and two leaders of the Minnesota nonprofit [[feeding-our-future|Feeding Our Future]] were convicted of stealing federal nutrition funds in May.[5] A state audit triggered by the allegations concluded that the Walz administration "did not effectively exercise its authority" to prevent the fraud.[5] HHS Deputy Secretary [[jim-oneill|Jim O'Neill]] announced the freeze, citing a decade of taxpayer money allegedly funneled to fraudulent daycares.[5]
