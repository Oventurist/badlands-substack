# Rafah Border Crossing

The Rafah border crossing is the land crossing between the southern Gaza Strip and Egypt, and the only Gaza crossing that does not border [[israel|Israel]] directly. In May 2024 it became the subject of an unusual arrangement between Washington, Israel and Cairo after Israeli forces seized it.[1]

## The May 2024 seizure

An [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] tank unit took control of the crossing in early May 2024. An Israeli press report indicated that the operation was undertaken with some level of cooperation and coordination with Washington, reportedly to ensure that no weapons destined for [[hamas|Hamas]] could pass into the Strip through the large crossing.[1] Reporting also indicated that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had effectively greenlighted the takeover of the crossing during a call with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], and that a senior Israeli official said Biden "didn't pull the hand break" on the capture during that conversation.[1] By that Wednesday the IDF announced it had reopened the crossing while retaining full control of it.[1]

With the crossing in Israeli hands, Israel controlled all of Gaza's border crossings for the first time in nearly twenty years — a development Badlands commentary characterised as the re-establishment of a military occupation of [[gaza|Gaza]] that had been given up in 2005–2006, following an Israeli military presence dating to 1967.[1]

## Private American security firm

The United States, Israel and Egypt agreed that a private American security firm — staffed in part by US special operations forces veterans — would take control of the Rafah crossing and oversee its operation.[1] Badlands commentary framed this as a further parallel between the Gaza and [[ukraine|Ukraine]] conflicts, arguing that the appeal of mercenaries to war planners lies in the absence of legal oversight, the rarity of accountability for misconduct, the lack of formal public reporting requirements, and the absence of the political cost associated with state funerals for fallen soldiers.[1] The commentary warned that inserting American contractors would likely antagonise Hamas, which had explicitly stated that any foreign personnel brought in to fight alongside the IDF would be targeted.[1]

## Context

The crossing's status was inseparable from the broader dispute over the [[rafah|Rafah]] operation, in which Biden threatened to withhold offensive weapons if Israel launched a full-scale invasion of the city while the administration simultaneously withheld ammunition and bomb shipments as a signal.[1]
