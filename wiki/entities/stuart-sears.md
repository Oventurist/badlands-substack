# Stuart A. Sears

Stuart A. Sears is a defence attorney with [[schertler-onorato-mead-and-sears|Schertler, Onorato, Mead & Sears]] who, with partner [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]], represented [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] at his 2022 trial in the Eastern District of Virginia.[1] The two were assisted by three paralegals, Charlie Tent, Grace McMahon and Matt Milzman; the same firm had represented Hillary for America in the [[michael-sussmann|Michael Sussmann]] case.[1]

## Arguments on the opening day

Sears raised the emails between [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] and Danchenko, telling [[anthony-trenga|Judge Trenga]] that through the defence's own research — and, he expected, Dolan's own account — the information Dolan supplied had been public when he provided it, and that a series of articles published before the 20 August 2016 email covered every detail contained in it.[1] The defence therefore sought to admit those articles to establish that the material was publicly available.[1]

Sears also told the court that two, and possibly three, exhibits the defence intended to rely on had disappeared from the government's exhibit list: Amtrak records placing Danchenko in New York from about 26 to 28 July 2016, when he believed he would meet the anonymous caller, and a 28 July 2016 Facebook message Danchenko sent his then-wife from New York with a photograph of giraffes at the Bronx Zoo and the words "Another meeting tonight" — the night, in the defence's view, that he was to meet [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] or the person he believed to be Millian.[1] [[john-durham|Durham]] said he was confident the parties could resolve the matter and could offer the same exhibits.[1]

On the earlier counterespionage investigation of Danchenko, Sears equivocated before ultimately asking that it not be mentioned at all.[1] The defence's central theory, previewed in argument and in its motion to dismiss, was that the interviewing agents had not been clear enough and that Danchenko had given truthful answers to ambiguous questions, particularly as to whether all of [[dossier-report-2016-95|report 2016/95]] was attributed to "Source E."[1]
