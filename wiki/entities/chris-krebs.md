# Chris Krebs

Chris Krebs was the director of the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] (CISA) during the 2020 U.S. presidential election. He is best known in the Badlands corpus for the public declaration that the November 2020 election was "the most secure in U.S. history."[1]

That statement is treated as directly contradicted by the declassified January 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo, which had warned before the election that foreign adversaries could compromise voter registration databases, pollbooks, and election websites.[1] Post-election evidence indicated that China obtained access to voter registration data in multiple states and that [[iran|Iran]] compromised election-related systems, breaches reportedly suppressed for years.[1] The Intelligence Community's inspector general opened a full investigation into the alleged suppression after Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] declassified the memo.[1]

## April 2025 presidential memorandum

On April 9, 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed a memorandum titled [[addressing-risks-from-chris-krebs-and-government-censorship|"Addressing Risks from Chris Krebs and Government Censorship"]], which named Krebs "a significant bad-faith actor who weaponized and abused his Government authority."[2] The memorandum charged that Krebs, through [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|CISA]], promoted the censorship of election information — including known risks associated with certain voting practices — and "falsely and baselessly denied that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen," dismissing widespread election malfeasance and vulnerabilities in voting machines.[2] Trump directed agency heads to revoke any active security clearance held by Krebs, to suspend clearances held by individuals at entities associated with him including [[sentinelone|SentinelOne]], to review his activities as a government employee and his leadership of CISA, and to recommend remedial or preventative actions.[2]

Badlands contributors treated the order as a landmark: [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] called it "the most secure memorandum in American history," while [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Krebs as "patient zero," the 2020 narrative peddler and "unqualified lawyer with his undergrad in environmental science" who was "inexplicably the nation's top 'cyber expert' in 2020."[2] Trump publicly called Krebs a fraud and a "bad guy" around the signing.[2]

## Replacement at CISA

Krebs was replaced after the 2020 election by [[jen-easterly|Jen Easterly]], a career intelligence official who had served as special assistant to Barack Obama from October 2013 to February 2016 and as the National Security Council's senior director for counterterrorism.[3] In October 2024 [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] revisited Krebs' record for the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], describing him as "a lawyer with his undergrad in environmental science" and "the guy that gave us the 'safest and most secure election in history'" — a claim she said was exposed again as a bold lie by that week's election hearing in Georgia.[3]

## Executive order directing a DOJ investigation (April 2025)

In April 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed executive orders directing the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to investigate Krebs, the former federal cybersecurity chief, for his actions during the 2020 election.[4] The orders were signed a day before Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] told a Cabinet meeting that electronic voting systems had long been vulnerable to hacking and exploitation, and called for a nationwide switch to paper ballots.[4]

## Resignation from SentinelOne (April 2025)

A week after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order targeting Krebs and calling on the government to suspend the security clearances of any entities with whom he was associated — an order that specifically named [[sentinelone|SentinelOne]] — Krebs announced his resignation from the company on Wednesday, April 16, 2025.[5] SentinelOne, a cybersecurity firm with a $5.6 billion market capitalization, had employed Krebs as chief intelligence and public policy officer since late 2023, when it acquired his consulting firm.[5]

Krebs said the choice to leave was his alone, writing in an email to staffers posted on the company's website that "for those who know me, you know I don't shy away from tough fights," but that this was one he needed to take on fully outside of SentinelOne, describing it as "a fight for democracy, for freedom of speech, and for the rule of law."[5] CNBC characterized the swift departure as an example of Trump's effect on the private sector when pressuring people and institutions he personally dislikes.[5]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America described Krebs as "patient zero in the 2020 coup," noting that a lawyer with an undergraduate degree in environmental science had become the nation's top "cyber expert" and had assured the public that 2020 was the safest and most secure election in history.[5] She added that while Krebs was out at SentinelOne because the firm did not want the heat, it remained to be seen whether the company was off the hook, given that the presidential memorandum directed the suspension of "any active security clearances held by individuals at entities associated with Krebs, including SentinelOne."[5]

## Cited in election-security criticism

Writing in early 2024, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] singled out Krebs — whom she described as a lawyer with an undergraduate degree in environmental science — as the source of the official assurance that American elections are safe from cyberattack.[6] She juxtaposed that assurance against [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]]'s testimony about the [[volt-typhoon|Volt Typhoon]] intrusion into critical infrastructure, arguing that elections run on the same technology stack and are therefore no more secure than the sectors the government admits are under attack.[6]

## "Safest and most secure election" claim contested

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], describing Krebs as an "environmental scientist-turned-lawyer," argued in January 2024 that the report [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] released on January 2 documenting alleged 2020 fraud in Michigan, Wisconsin, Arizona, Pennsylvania and Georgia undercut Krebs' well-known claim that 2020 was the safest and most secure election in American history.[7]
