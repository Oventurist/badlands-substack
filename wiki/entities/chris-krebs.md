# Chris Krebs

**Christopher Krebs** was the first director of the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] (CISA), the Department of Homeland Security component created in 2018 with responsibility for critical infrastructure protection, including election systems designated as critical infrastructure.

Krebs is best known for the joint statement issued after the November 2020 vote declaring the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]] "the most secure in American history." He was dismissed by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] shortly afterward.

## Relevance to the declassified NIC memo

The 2026 declassification of a January 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo authored by [[christopher-porter|Christopher Porter]] placed that public assurance under renewed scrutiny. The memo had warned before the election that voter registration databases, pollbooks, and election websites were vulnerable to compromise by [[russia|Russia]], [[china|China]], [[iran|Iran]], North Korea, and non-state actors, and post-election evidence indicated that Chinese access to voter registration data in multiple states and Iranian intrusions into election-related systems had in fact occurred. In the Badlands Brief account, Krebs and other officials made their "most secure" declaration despite the pre-election warnings, and the Intelligence Community inspector general has opened an investigation into the alleged suppression of the breach findings.
