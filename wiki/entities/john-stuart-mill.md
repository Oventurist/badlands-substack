# John Stuart Mill

John Stuart Mill is identified in Badlands Media reporting as one of the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]] economists whose "dismal science" was refuted by [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s economic advisor [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]].[1] Together with [[david-ricardo|David Ricardo]], Mill advanced the pseudo-scientific [[law-of-diminishing-returns|law of diminishing returns]], which presumed a deterministic devaluation of land over time as rents increased under a "law of exploitation" of the unfit by the "more fit".[1]

Carey, writing in *Unity of Law* (1872), observed that Mill had told his readers that the law of the constantly decreasing productiveness of agricultural labor was "the most important proposition in political economy", and that "were the law different, nearly all the phenomena of the production and distribution of wealth would be different."[1] Carey argued that this assumption established a tendency toward the subjection of the masses — in other words toward slavery — as a supposedly necessary result of divine institutions.[1]

The closed-system theories associated with Mill are presented as ancestors both of Marxist class struggle theory and of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s 1968 neo-Malthusian revival, which fed into the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and modern [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] ideology.[1]

## Rejected by Sun Yat-sen

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] lists Mill among the liberal theorists — with [[adam-smith|Adam Smith]], Malthus and Ricardo — whose doctrines [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]] refused to adopt.[2] Ehret argues that Sun's programme of applying advanced technology and machine power to replace human labor was intended to overcome the "law of diminishing returns" advanced by Mill, by leaping from lower to ever higher powers of productivity and thereby offsetting the tensions [[karl-marx|Marx]] attributed to class struggle.[2]
