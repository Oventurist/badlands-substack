# Zoe Saldaña

Zoe Saldaña is an American actress best known for starring as Neytiri in the Avatar franchise and for her role in Guardians of the Galaxy.[1]

## Oscars comments

In December 2024 Saldaña said the Academy Awards' failure to recognize Avatar was "quite deflating," adding that it makes her feel "overlooked and then minimized and completely disregarded."[1] Asked whether awards shows struggle to look past cobalt-blue skin or digitally engineered doe eyes when choosing nominations, she answered simply, "They do."[1] Speaking to the Independent, she said: "Old habits die hard, and when you have old establishments, it's really hard to bring forward change. And I understand that, so I'm not bitter about it, but it is quite deflating when you give 120 percent of yourself into something."[1] She added that "not winning is okay" and "not being nominated is okay," but objected to being "overlooked and then minimized and completely disregarded."[1]

Responding to those who argued her Avatar performance was the achievement of the films' technicians alone, Saldaña said, "I know the difference between that and what we did," while insisting she carried no chip on her shoulder: "At some point you have to ask yourself: why is it that I do what I do? Is it so others can give me approval? Or is it because I don't want to do anything else?"[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the episode to earlier coverage of the Hollywood writers' strike and the reshaping of entertainment production by automation and artificial intelligence.[1] Director James Cameron told Variety that the Oscars were overdue to recognize Saldaña's work as Neytiri, complaining that "because in my film she's playing a CG character, it kind of doesn't count in some way, which makes no sense to me whatsoever."[1] Ashe asked how long it would be until the Academy itself is a machine and machines give awards to other machines.[1] Commentator Paul Fleuret offered a more hopeful reading, arguing that being ignored was "probably a good sign for where she stands within Hollywood" — "if she were one of [them], she'd be nominated with no questions" — and noting that Avatar 2 was largely about building and protecting the nuclear family.[1]
