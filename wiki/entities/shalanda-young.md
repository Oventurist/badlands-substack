# Shalanda Young

Shalanda Young served as Director of the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] in the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

## 2023 national security supplemental request

In October 2023 Young transmitted the administration's request for more than $105 billion in national security funding to Speaker Pro Tempore [[patrick-mchenry|Patrick McHenry]], the House's caretaker presiding officer during the speakership vacancy.[1] The package included $61.4 billion in aid for Ukraine, $14.3 billion in aid for Israel, $9.15 billion in humanitarian assistance, $7.4 billion for Taiwan and the Indo-Pacific region, and $13.6 billion for security at the US–Mexico border.[1]

The administration framed the request as addressing "the global humanitarian impacts of Russia's brutal invasion of Ukraine and of [[hamas|Hamas]]' horrific attacks on Israel, including by extending humanitarian assistance to civilians in [[gaza|Gaza]]."[1] In her letter Young wrote, "The world is watching and the American people rightly expect their leaders to come together and deliver on these priorities," urging [[congress|Congress]] to act on a comprehensive, bipartisan agreement.[1]

## Warning on exhaustion of Ukraine funding (December 2023)

On Monday, December 4, 2023, Young sent a letter to congressional leaders of both parties warning that the United States would run out of money to aid [[ukraine|Ukraine]] by the end of the year and that a failure by [[congress|Congress]] to approve new support would "kneecap" Kyiv.[2] "Without congressional action, by the end of the year we will run out of resources to procure more weapons and equipment for Ukraine and to provide equipment from US military stocks," she wrote, adding: "There is no magical pot of funding available to meet this moment. We are out of money — and nearly out of time."[2] The letter represented the most specific assessment to that point of Washington's waning support, coming as President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s request for $106 billion in emergency funding for Ukraine, [[israel|Israel]] and the Indo-Pacific remained stalled on Capitol Hill amid mounting Republican opposition.[2]
