# Martin Luther King Jr.

Martin Luther King Jr. was the American civil rights leader assassinated on April 4, 1968. In the Badlands Media corpus he appears principally as a forensic and political parallel to the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]], in the April 6, 2026 Badlands Brief, "Jesus Walks", published shortly after the anniversary of his death.

## The autopsy and ballistics record

The Brief notes that the official autopsy determined King died from a single gunshot wound, and quotes an extended analysis supplied by a commenter. According to that account, a single bullet entered the right side of King's face roughly an inch to the right of and half an inch below his mouth, fractured his jaw, exited the lower part of the face and reentered the body through the neck. It severed multiple vital arteries, fractured the spine in several places, and came to rest beneath the skin on the left side of the back, travelling in a downward and rearward direction.

The recovered projectile was a deformed .30-06 caliber rifle slug, designated exhibit Q64 in subsequent investigations, found lodged just beneath the skin near the left shoulder blade in a trajectory spanning several inches through the body. A Remington 760 Gamemaster rifle, designated Q2, was recovered near the crime scene and identified as the suspected murder weapon. When the [[house-select-committee-on-assassinations|House Select Committee on Assassinations]] later convened a firearms panel, the experts found that the bullet's class characteristics matched the rifle — both showing six grooves and six lands with a right twist — but that individual identifying characteristics did not permit a conclusion. The panel found itself "unable positively to identify or eliminate the Q64 bullet as having been fired from the Q2 rifle."

## Use as a parallel in the corpus

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], preparing an America 250 segment on the anniversary of King's death, was struck that the bullet that killed King was allegedly a .30-06, the same class of round at issue in the Kirk case. A commenter identified as Chris Paul asked whether Charlie Kirk is "the 'conservative' Martin Luther King Jr in 30 years? Seems like a reboot." Ashe draws out the shared features: an inconclusive bullet-to-rifle match in the prosecution of [[tyler-robinson|Tyler Robinson]] echoing the King panel's finding, both concerning "prominent liberty activists at transformational moments of populism in America," and both official inquiries concluding that no U.S. government agency participated in the assassination — a conclusion she answers with a single word: "Sure."
