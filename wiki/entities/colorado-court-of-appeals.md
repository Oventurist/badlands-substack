# Colorado Court of Appeals

The Colorado Court of Appeals is the intermediate appellate court of the State of [[colorado|Colorado]], sitting in divisions of three judges to review decisions of the state's district courts. In January 2026 its Division Five heard the appeal of former [[mesa-county|Mesa County]] Clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] from her August 2024 criminal conviction.[1]

## Division Five and the Peters oral argument

Oral arguments were held on a Wednesday in January 2026 in Denver before a three-judge panel led by Craig R. Welling and flanked by Ted C. Tow III and Lino S. Lipinsky de Orlov.[1] According to [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who has covered the case since Peters' office was raided in August 2021, the hearing went unexpectedly well for Peters, to the surprise of both parties and observers, and the panel "diffused the political storytelling" surrounding the case in dramatic fashion.[1]

Three features of the argument were highlighted. First, the question of jurisdiction dominated much of the discussion despite Peters' own counsel choosing not to argue it. Second, the judges appeared hostile to both parties and arrived with their own concerns about the trial court's handling of the case. Third, the State's commitment to its narrative about Peters was described as extending "to a fault," a posture read as implying that Peters would receive some form of relief.[1] The panel was described as seeming outraged at times by the conduct of the state.[1]

## Anticipated outcomes

Speculating as a non-attorney, Ashe in America predicted that the conspiracy felony would be thrown out, the official misconduct misdemeanor thrown out, and the remaining counts resentenced before a different judicial officer — potentially resulting in release on time served. She characterized this as the minimum needed to remedy what she described as overt injustice in Mesa County in August and October of 2024, while arguing that the State's refusal to acknowledge its own errors supported the allegation, and a then-current [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] investigation, that Colorado had weaponized government against Peters.[1] Under the maximum outcome she envisioned, the conviction would be overturned in its entirety, Peters immediately released, and the State and County tried for prosecutorial misconduct.[1]
