# American Philosophical Society

The American Philosophical Society was proposed by [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] in his 1743 *Proposal for Promotion of Useful Knowledge*, in which he wrote that "One society be formed of virtuosi or ingenious men, residing in the several colonies, to be called the American Philosophical Society, who are to remain in constant correspondence."[1]

The Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin" lists the proposal among the keystones of Franklin's life, placing it within his broader effort to coordinate an international network of collaborators among the enlightened intelligentsias of Russia, France, Germany, Prussia, Spain, Italy, and even India and Morocco.[1] In this the essay says the scientist, poet and statesman walked in the footsteps of Gottfried Leibniz, who had attempted a similar grand design when Franklin was still a boy.[1]
