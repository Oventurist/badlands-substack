# Central Commission for Discipline Inspection

The Central Commission for Discipline Inspection — referred to in the Badlands Media feature "Empire's Enigma" simply as the Discipline Commission — is the internal disciplinary organ of the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] and the principal instrument of [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s anti-corruption campaign.[1]

## Expansion under Xi

The article reports that Xi spent the years 2013 to 2017 waging a highly public war against party corruption, officially punishing 1.4 million party members including more than 100 generals and admirals, with the stated goal of restoring "public respect for the Party" in what he framed as a matter of "survival or extinction."[1] He subsequently expanded the Discipline Commission's remit to seek out corruption and levy punishment against local and government-adjacent officials, including doctors, professors and executives.[1]

The piece links this expansion to a wider reallocation of authority: whereas local councils had historically appointed judges, Xi transferred that responsibility to internal affairs, meaning that he and his loyalists have installed all Chinese judges since 2013.[1] Read together with the removals of [[zhou-yongkang|Zhou Yongkang]], [[bo-xilai|Bo Xilai]], [[ling-jihua|Ling Jihua]] and the generals [[xu-caihou|Xu Caihou]], [[guo-boxiong|Guo Boxiong]] and [[sun-zhengcai|Sun Zhengcai]], the author presents the Commission as the vehicle for dismantling the patronage networks that flourished under [[hu-jintao|Hu Jintao]].[1]
