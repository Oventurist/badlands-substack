# Mark Zuckerberg

**Mark Zuckerberg** is the founder and chief executive of [[meta-platforms|Meta]], formerly Facebook.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Badlands framing

In *America Has a CIA-Run Economy*, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treats Zuckerberg as one of the installed public faces of a company he argues was seeded by [[cia|CIA]] shell-company funding and grew out of the cancelled [[darpa|DARPA]] program [[lifelog|LifeLog]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Trump-era conduct

Zuckerberg has agreed to open his platforms to more free speech and has terminated DEI programs.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Carlson counts him among the big tech CEOs who have endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] or at least stopped working against him, and among those apologizing for past behavior described as corrupt and arguably treasonous.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Carlson does not believe Zuckerberg and [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]] will escape their alleged crimes entirely unscathed, but accepts seeing them seated with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] at the inauguration.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## PCAST appointment (March 2026)

Zuckerberg was among the appointees named to the [[presidents-council-of-advisors-on-science-and-technology|President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology]] in the [[white-house|White House]] announcement of March 2026.[2] Badlands contributor Ashe in America counted him among the roughly half of appointees she described as "Thiel bros," and singled out his selection as the hardest to explain, asking whether the unresolved controversy over the Center for Tech and Civic Life was simply being forgotten and insisting that "public trust is conditioned on accountability."[2]

## Metaverse retreat and reversal (2026)

In March 2026 [[meta-platforms|Meta]] reversed its plan to end [[horizon-worlds|Horizon Worlds]] in VR, pivoting its metaverse experiences to mobile and mixed reality while continuing to fund [[reality-labs|Reality Labs]] despite tens of billions of dollars in accumulated losses.[3] Badlands contributor [[matthew-trump|Matthew Trump]] wrote that Zuckerberg's attempt to stake his company's future on a virtual reality avatar world "that almost no one wanted to explore" was the most mock-worthy episode of the social media era, and suspected the reprieve was issued partly out of spite toward critics who celebrated the shutdown.[3] He noted that few Trump supporters have affection for Zuckerberg, whom he accused of helping finance the theft of the 2020 election in swing states before bending the knee to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after the last election.[3] He predicted the concept will return in a less repulsive form and be remembered as an experiment ahead of its time, and warned that Zuckerberg's frantic effort to catch rivals in the AI space should not be dismissed given the "Himalaya Range of personal data at his disposal."[3]

## 2026 AI-driven workforce purge

In May 2026 Zuckerberg began cutting roughly 8,000 jobs at [[meta-platforms|Meta]] — about 10% of the workforce — as he accelerated the company's shift toward artificial intelligence, alongside the reassignment of about 7,000 employees into AI-focused roles and the closure of thousands of open positions.[4] Reuters reported that layoffs and reassignments together affected around 20% of Meta's workforce.[4] Zuckerberg told staff he did not expect more company-wide layoffs that year, though the move signaled that Big Tech's AI spending boom was increasingly being funded through white-collar workforce reductions.[4]

## Cage match with Elon Musk (2023)

In June 2023, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] challenged Zuckerberg to a "cage match" while replying to a user discussing [[meta-platforms|Meta]]'s plans to build a [[twitter|Twitter]] rival, and Zuckerberg answered with "Send me location" in an Instagram story.[5] Musk proposed the "Vegas Octagon," apparently a reference to the UFC Apex in Las Vegas, and said he would fight "if this is for real"; a Meta spokesperson told CBS News that "the story speaks for itself."[5]

Commenting in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used the episode to disparage Zuckerberg, repeating claims circulating in alternative media that he did not truly develop Facebook and is a mediocre programmer, and describing his body language and his appearance on Joe Rogan's program as those of a trust-fund heir insulated from ordinary life.[5] Deschamps accused Zuckerberg of using his platform to push what he called wokeism, transgenderism and globalism while suppressing honest speech, and said he would prefer Musk to win the fight.[5]


## Joe Rogan Interview (January 2025)

In a January 2025 appearance on Joe Rogan's podcast, Mark Zuckerberg voiced optimism about President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s support for the American technology industry.[6] "I think it's a strategic advantage for the United States that we have a lot of the strongest companies in the world, and I think it should be part of the U.S. strategy going forward to defend that," Zuckerberg said, adding, "And it's one of the things that I'm optimistic about with President Trump is, I think he just wants America to win."[6] He told Rogan that shifts at his company reflected the nation's "cultural pulse" as expressed in the presidential election results.[6] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded skeptically, arguing that the Truth Community was being asked to treat the Build Back Better agenda as friendly merely because "rich, powerful strangers said nice things about Donald Trump."[6]

## Metaverse hardware ambitions and the 2023 silicon layoffs

Zuckerberg's project to build augmented- and virtual-reality products giving access to the immersive worlds he calls the metaverse depends heavily on the hardware developed inside [[reality-labs|Reality Labs]], and he has predicted that AR glasses "will redefine our relationship with technology."[7] In October 2023 [[meta-platforms|Meta]] moved to lay off staff in the [[facebook-agile-silicon-team|Facebook Agile Silicon Team]], the roughly 600-person custom-chip unit within that division, after the company failed to produce silicon competitive with external suppliers and turned to [[qualcomm|Qualcomm]] for the chips in its shipping devices.[7] Reuters suggested that deep cuts could hamper Zuckerberg's hardware program, while [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] countered that shedding the unit's fully loaded costs would free capital for reinvestment in exactly that vision.[7]

## December 2024 inaugural fund donation

In December 2024, [[meta-platforms|Meta]] donated $1 million to President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inaugural fund, a contribution confirmed by both Trump representatives and the company.[8] The donation came two weeks after Zuckerberg travelled to Mar-a-Lago to dine with Trump, with whom he had previously had a fractious relationship.[8] According to public records cited by the Wall Street Journal, neither Zuckerberg nor Meta had donated to Trump's inaugural fund in 2017 or to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s fund in 2021.[8]

The reversal was striking given the intervening history: Meta banned Trump from Facebook and Instagram after the January 6 Capitol riot, only removing the restrictions during the 2024 campaign in July, and Zuckerberg had criticized Trump's first-term executive orders on immigration.[8] Trump, for his part, wrote in a book published in August 2024 that Zuckerberg would "spend the rest of his life in prison" if he tried to influence the election outcome.[8] Zuckerberg said he had reached out to Trump following the attempted assassination over the summer and described Trump's response to the attack as "badass."[8] A Meta spokesperson confirmed the donation but declined further comment. Zuckerberg joined Amazon founder Jeff Bezos, Google CEO Sundar Pichai and other tech leaders in rushing to congratulate Trump after his election win.[8]

### Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the sequence under the heading "Mark Zuckerberg, Whistleblower?", noting his August 2024 letter to Congress claiming the Biden administration in 2021 "repeatedly pressured our teams for months to censor certain Covid content, including humor and satire."[8] She argued that by August it was clear to anyone with data access that Trump would win, drawing Zuckerberg's timing into question: in her reading, he took steps to officially document himself as a victim of the regime and then began "dropping millions and shilling Trump."[8] She characterized the $1 million as the billionaire equivalent of a thank-you note after a Mar-a-Lago lunch invitation, and concluded that Zuckerberg "isn't real" but rather "a wholly-owned creation of DARPA, an actor," now "under new management."[8]

## Hawaii bunker denial

In a December 2024 Bloomberg interview with Emily Chang at his Lake Tahoe estate, Zuckerberg denied that the roughly 4,500-square-foot underground structure on his 1,500-acre Kauai ranch was a [[zuckerberg-hawaii-bunker|doomsday bunker]], calling it a "little shelter ... basement" and saying the ranch "got blown out of proportion as if it was some kind of doomsday bunker, which is just not true."[9] Wired had reported in late 2023 that the structure included a blast-resistant door and enough food for Zuckerberg and companions to survive an apocalypse, prompting speculation about what he might anticipate regarding future world events.[9]

## Letter on Biden administration censorship pressure (August 2024)

In August 2024 Zuckerberg said he regretted bowing to what he called pressure from the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration to "censor" content on Facebook and Instagram during the coronavirus pandemic.[10] In a letter sent to a U.S. House committee chair, he said some material — including humour and satire — was taken down in 2021 under pressure from senior officials, while the White House defended its actions on the grounds that it had encouraged "responsible actions to protect public health and safety."[10]

Zuckerberg also said his firm briefly "demoted" content relating to [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] ahead of the 2020 election after the [[fbi|FBI]] warned of "a potential Russian disinformation" operation; it later became clear the content was not part of such an operation and should not have been temporarily taken down.[10] He did not give further detail about the pandemic-era actions he regretted, but maintained that the decisions were his business's own even though "government pressure was wrong," adding: "We made some choices that, with the benefit of hindsight and new information, we wouldn't make today."[10] He said he and [[meta-platforms|Meta]] would be ready to "push back" if something similar happened in the future.[10]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Zuckerberg as "the most notorious of the social media censor czars" disclosing what critics already knew — that he had acted as a key vector of the globalist regime's enforcement arm — and argued that in a war defined by the dissemination, obfuscation and cultivation of information, censorship is an act of war whose practitioners should be treated as enemy combatants.[10] He contrasted the admission with France's simultaneous imprisonment of [[telegram|Telegram]] founder [[pavel-durov|Pavel Durov]] on charges he said would not look out of place in an Orwell novel, reading the convergence as a sign of enemy panic and patriot narrative control rather than enemy dominance.[10]
