# Tim Ballard

Tim Ballard is an American anti-trafficking activist and the founder of [[operation-underground-railroad|Operation Underground Railroad]], an organisation dedicated to rescuing children from sex trafficking operations.[1] His life and work are the basis of the 2023 film [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]], in which he is portrayed by [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]].[1]

The film's commercial success — $100 million at the box office within weeks of release, on a reported $14 million budget, achieved despite hostile coverage from outlets including Rolling Stone and Bloomberg News — brought Ballard's work to a mass audience and made child trafficking a subject of unusually wide public discussion in 2023.[1]

## QAnon allegations in mainstream coverage (2023)

A July 2023 [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] opinion column tying [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]] to [[qanon|QAnon]] described Ballard as a former Homeland Security operative and located the film's controversy in the QAnon links attributed to both Ballard and the actor who portrays him, [[jim-caviezel|Jim Caviezel]].[2] The column stated that Ballard had promoted the claim that the furniture retailer Wayfair was involved in child trafficking, and that Caviezel had spoken at multiple QAnon events and repeated blood-harvesting claims in an interview with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]].[2]

At the time of that coverage the film had surpassed $50 million in United States box office receipts and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had announced plans to screen it at his New Jersey golf club.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the media campaign against the film and against child-trafficking exposure was backfiring, producing a convergence between the film's audience and the Q phenomenon.[2]

## The Atlantic profile and July 2023 commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] noted that Ballard had previously been attacked by [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] in a January 2022 piece entitled "The Great (Fake) Child-Sex-Trafficking Epidemic," which attempted to debunk child sex trafficking as a [[qanon|QAnon]] conspiracy theory and which Deschamps said failed to provide supporting facts or evidence.[3] Writing after the release of [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]], Deschamps described Ballard as humble, as serving a higher power, and as having sacrificed personal wealth to pursue justice for neglected children, crediting him with detailed knowledge of the trafficking network and documentation to support his accounts.[3] Deschamps also raised — without answering — the question of whether Ballard was knowingly participating in a wider white-hat information campaign timed to the film's release.[3]
