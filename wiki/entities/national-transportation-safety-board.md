# National Transportation Safety Board

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) is the independent US federal agency responsible for investigating civil transportation accidents and issuing safety recommendations.[1]

## East Palestine investigation

The NTSB, working alongside the [[federal-railroad-administration|Federal Railroad Administration]], investigated the February 3, 2023 [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine train derailment]] involving a [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern]] freight train in eastern Ohio.[1] The agencies determined that the derailment was caused by a failed and overheated wheel bearing on a railcar, a mechanical-failure finding announced in NTSB materials.[1] Twenty of the thirty-eight derailed cars carried hazardous materials, and the controlled venting and burning of vinyl chloride during the emergency response became the most contested element of the incident's public legacy.[1]

## 2025 Potomac midair collision investigation

The NTSB, together with the [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]], opened an investigation into the January 29, 2025 midair collision between an [[american-airlines|American Airlines]] regional jet operated by [[psa-airlines|PSA Airlines]] and a U.S. Army Sikorsky UH-60 helicopter near [[ronald-reagan-washington-national-airport|Reagan Washington National Airport]], according to a statement released by the FAA.[2] The event is described at [[2025-potomac-midair-collision]].[2]

## Baltimore bridge investigation

On 27 March 2024 the NTSB disclosed that the cargo ship [[dali|Dali]], which caused the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Baltimore bridge collapse]], had been carrying hazardous materials, with Chair [[jennifer-homendy|Jennifer Homendy]] reporting 56 containers holding corrosives, flammables and lithium ion batteries.[3] Homendy said some containers had been breached and a sheen identified in the water would be handled by authorities, and that the voyage data recorder had been recovered.[3] She said the investigation could take 12 to 24 months but that the board would not hesitate to issue urgent safety recommendations in the meantime, with a preliminary report expected in two to four weeks; she described the inquiry as "a massive undertaking" and the crash as "a very tragic event."[3]
