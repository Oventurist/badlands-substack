# Olivia Troye

Olivia Troye is a former Trump administration White House staffer who later became a public critic of Donald Trump. In Badlands Media commentary she is presented as one of the internal "moles" whose accounts of the first Trump term are treated as fabrications rather than testimony.[1]

## Portrayal in Badlands Media commentary

In the March 2024 article "The CIA is Being Thrown Under the Bus," Erik Carlson lists Troye alongside Alexander Vindman and John Kelly as examples of hostile insiders who, in his telling, sabotaged or misrepresented the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration from within. Carlson characterizes Troye as having lied about Trump treating women badly in the White House, and complains that such claims are still widely believed.[1]

The reference to Troye appears in a broader argument that a second Trump term would be staffed differently: with what Carlson calls the moles removed, the [[deep-state|Deep State]] would no longer be able to control America from inside its own government and agencies, and would instead be forced to fight Trump from outside through NATO, the United Nations and the European Union.[1]

Her inclusion in the article is illustrative rather than biographical — the piece offers no account of her career, only her role as an alleged unreliable narrator of the Trump White House.[1]

## Biography

Erik Carlson's December 27, 2023 article "The Sabotage of Trump's Administration" devotes its central section to Troye, reproducing her Wikipedia biography at length.[2] Originally from El Paso, Texas, she graduated from the University of Pennsylvania, the National Defense University College of International Affairs and the Naval Postgraduate School, worked for the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] after college, and entered national security work following the September 11 attacks.[2] She served in the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] as a George W. Bush administration appointee and was an intelligence official in the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] Office of Intelligence and Analysis as chief of strategy, policy and plans, before moving to the Office of the Vice President as homeland security and counterterrorism adviser to [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] and serving as an aide to the [[white-house-coronavirus-task-force|White House Coronavirus Task Force]].[2]

Troye says she resigned in August 2020; Pence's national security adviser [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]] says he fired her and "escorted her off the compound," and has more broadly accused her of lying about her time in the Trump–Pence administration, which she denies.[2] After leaving she became an outspoken critic of the administration's COVID-19 response, endorsed [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in a video released by Republican Voters Against Trump, and in 2021 drew attention to what she described as deliberate obstruction of Afghan visa processing during the Trump administration, especially by [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]].[2] In August 2020 she joined the [[national-insurance-crime-bureau|National Insurance Crime Bureau]] as vice president of strategy, policy and plans, and became an adviser to [[defending-democracy-together|Defending Democracy Together]], the group behind [[republicans-for-the-rule-of-law|Republicans for the Rule of Law]].[2] She was among more than 130 former Republican national security officials who signed a 2020 statement declaring Trump unfit to serve another term and pledging to vote for Biden, and she has described herself as a "[[john-mccain|John McCain]] Republican."[2]

## Carlson's reading of her career

Carlson argues Troye "checks off all the boxes" for a [[rino|RINO]]/[[neoconservatives|Neocon]] operative: an Ivy League degree, a Bush appointment, a path from Homeland Security to the Coronavirus Task Force, and an immediate post-firing career devoted to preventing Trump's reelection.[2] He treats the Kellogg account as decisive, concluding that she was caught sabotaging the administration and removed only months before the 2020 election, while leaving open whether she was fired the moment she was discovered or only when she ceased to be useful — and whether Pence himself was complicit.[2] For Carlson, her presence is evidence that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] infiltrated the Trump White House through Pence's staff.[2]

## The Bulwark article on coronavirus intelligence

In May 2021, nine months after leaving, Troye published "How the Trump Administration Twisted Coronavirus Intelligence" in [[the-bulwark|The Bulwark]], arguing that rather than using intelligence to inform the pandemic response the administration bent facts for political ends.[2] She recounted that a January 26, 2020 [[washington-times|Washington Times]] article linking the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute]] to Beijing's covert bio-weapons program caught attention in the White House, and that Pence and his chief of staff repeatedly pressed the intelligence community about the [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]] through February 2020 — behaviour she called normal but motives she called questionable.[2] She wrote that the administration appeared to attempt through the [[state-department|State Department]] what the intelligence community would not, that [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] publicly claimed "enormous evidence" for the lab leak without disclosing sources, and that Trump followed with a claim of "high degree of confidence" while refusing to identify his evidence.[2]

Carlson reads the same article against the grain: he takes it as confirmation that Trump, Pence and Pompeo were genuinely pursuing COVID's origin, that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] declined to do so, and that Troye was effectively protecting the agency's and China's reputations.[2] He treats her framing as an instance of the Deep State accusing Trump of what it is itself doing, comparing indifference to COVID's origin with declining to identify the source of food poisoning.[2] He also draws from her account the conclusion that Trump's inner circle no longer trusted the CIA in his final year and routed intelligence work through the State Department instead.[2]

## Media campaign against Trump

A September 2020 New Yorker interview headlined "It Was All About the Election" presented Troye as the first coronavirus task force staffer to go public, describing Trump as disruptive, unable to focus, consumed by his November prospects, and warning that a vaccine might be rushed to save the election.[2] She said civil servants like her were reviled as "deep staters" by political appointees, particularly after mid-level staffers including Pence's national security aide Jennifer Williams testified in the December 2019 impeachment, and she closed her Republican Voters Against Trump video by endorsing Biden.[2]

Carlson notes that Troye left the White House and immediately joined other Republican Trump critics rather than taking time off, because the election was three months away, and argues that criticism from apparent insiders is more potent with persuadable voters precisely because they are assumed to be Trump's own people.[2] He observes that by late 2023, with Trump leading the polls, Troye was again making media appearances — on The View pushing gun control, condemning his COVID handling, accusing him of behaving inappropriately toward women and of badmouthing his own MAGA supporters — and argues the accumulation of accusations becomes self-defeating.[2] He predicts more such figures will emerge before the 2024 election, and reads their number as a measure of Deep State desperation.[2]
