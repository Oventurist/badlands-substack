# People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan

The People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA) was the communist party that ruled [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] from 1978 until 1992. Badlands Media, citing The Diplomat, notes that the party remains largely forgotten in Afghanistan's long story of bloodshed.[1]

## Factions

The PDPA had two main factions: the Khalq ("masses") and the Parcham ("banner"). [[hafizullah-amin|Hafizullah Amin]] emerged as the leader of the radical Khalq faction, brutally eliminating his party rivals, forcibly disappearing thousands of Afghans, confounding the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]]'s leadership, and forcing a series of foreign interventions that drastically sealed Afghanistan's fate for the next forty years.[1]

## Coup within the coup

A communist government was already in place in Afghanistan before the Soviet invasion, a fact Badlands Media stresses in arguing that the standard narrative of Soviet territorial expansion does not hold. Amin, second in command under [[nur-mohammad-taraki|Nur Mohammad Taraki]] and in control of most of the military, outmaneuvered Taraki by packing the party's Politburo with loyalists and had him killed in October 1979.[1]

One Afghan witness quoted in the article argued that Amin's actions caused a split inside the PDPA and weakened the party, and that he acted arbitrarily and in a dictatorial manner as second-in-chief — behavior he attributed to Amin serving as an American chess piece on the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] payroll.[1]
