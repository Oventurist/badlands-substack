# Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency

The Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) is the United States Department of Defense component responsible for personnel vetting and the adjudication of security clearances across much of the federal government and the defense industrial base.[1]

## New York Times FOIA litigation

In 2024 [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] petitioned DCSA under the [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]] for records concerning the security clearances held by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1] The agency repeatedly rejected the request, arguing that releasing the information could constitute an unwarranted invasion of Musk's privacy.[1]

The newspaper sued, and on October 8, 2025 Judge [[denise-cote|Denise Cote]] ruled that the administration must reveal what kinds of clearances had been granted to Musk, finding that his public role as a "special government employee" leading [[doge|DOGE]] and his own public statements about holding a top secret clearance outweighed the privacy interest DCSA had asserted.[1]

## Significance

The case illustrates the tension between DCSA's default posture of treating individual clearance records as protected personnel information and the transparency claims that arise when a clearance holder occupies a highly visible, quasi-official government role.[1]
