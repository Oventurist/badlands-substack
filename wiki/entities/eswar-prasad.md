# Eswar Prasad

Eswar Prasad is a Cornell University professor and economist who has written and spoken extensively on the future of money and digital currency. He drew attention in the Badlands corpus for remarks on programmable [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currencies]] delivered at a [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] event in China in June 2023.[1]

## Remarks at Summer Davos 2023

Prasad spoke during a 30-minute panel discussion on June 28, 2023 at the WEF's 14th annual Meeting of the New Champions in Tianjin, China — the gathering known informally as "Summer Davos." He argued that the global economy is "at the cusp of physical currency essentially disappearing," and that programmable CBDCs and the underlying technology could take the international economic landscape either "toward a dark path or a better place."[1]

Among the "huge potential gains" Prasad identified in the digitization of money was the programmability of CBDC units, including the ability to attach expiry dates to them. He also noted that governments could use central bank money to socially engineer society, and discussed restrictions on "less desirable" purchases as a feature of such systems.[1]

## Reception

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded that "just like your milk, CBDCs could come with an expiration date," warning that such a system would force citizens to spend on the government's schedule and open the door to restrictions on what individuals could buy. DeLarme compared digital currency to nuclear energy — not inherently a problem, but dangerous depending on how it is used — and cited Canadian and Chinese interest in CBDC expiry dates as evidence the concept was not hypothetical.[1]
