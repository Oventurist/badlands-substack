# Pope Innocent III

Pope Innocent III was the pontiff whose eighteen-year reign vastly expanded the Crusades, leading, in Matthew Ehret's account, to the largest bloodbaths of Christians killing Muslims in Spain and the Holy Land.[1]

Innocent III ordered the construction in 1204 of the abbey at Trisulti to celebrate the [[fourth-crusade|Fourth Crusade]]; that monastery is today the headquarters of the [[dignitatis-humanae-institute|Dignitatis Humanae Institute]].[1] He also presided over the disastrous Children's Crusade of 1212, which saw thousands of European children sent to retake the Holy Land alongside seasoned Templar mercenaries; what became of them remains a mystery, though Ehret judges it likely they were killed or sold into slavery in what he calls the first recorded global child trafficking operation in world history.[1]

In 1209 Innocent III sanctioned a new order known as the Franciscans, led by "the ecologist's saint" Francis of Assisi, who professed he could talk to animals.[1] That order, the article states, later served as the basis for the creation of the [[jesuits|Jesuit order]] during the Spanish Habsburg takeover of Rome.[1]
