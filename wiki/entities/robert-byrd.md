# Robert Byrd

Robert Byrd was a long-serving United States Senator from West Virginia and a former high-ranking member of the [[ku-klux-klan|Ku Klux Klan]]. In Badlands Media's "Q for Dummies" series he appears as the answer to a question posed in Q drop #9, dated October 29, 2017, which asked readers to identify [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s mentor.[1]

## Role in the Q drop #9 decode

"Q for Dummies" Chapter 2 presents Byrd's identity as the drop's intended revelation, framing it as unsurprising only because Clinton's "less than savory connections" were already familiar to the audience.[1] The chapter accompanies the answer with photographs of Byrd in Klan regalia.[1]

The author places particular emphasis on Byrd's funeral, noting that Clinton, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], George W. Bush, Joe Biden "and many other deep state dipshits" delivered eulogies, holding Byrd up as "some kind of bastion of good faith and hope in America."[1] He treats this as an instance of projection: a political faction that accuses its opponents of racism while elevating a former Klansman inside its own government.[1]

## Context within the chapter's argument

Byrd is introduced immediately after the chapter's discussion of the 1924 Democratic National Convention, popularly nicknamed the "Klan Bake," which the author cites as evidence that the Klan was "a democrat-created institution."[1] The Byrd material serves the chapter's wider claim, developed from the same drop, that a political awakening among Black voters regarding the Democratic Party's history and conduct would be "a death knell to the establishment."[1]

## Chapter 20: mentorship of Hillary Clinton

Chapter 20 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series returns to Byrd in its reading of Q drop #122 of November 6, 2017, which asks "Who is HRC's mentor?"[2] The chapter answers that Byrd was [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s mentor, and presents accompanying graphics as making Q's point self-evident — namely that, in the author's phrasing, "[they] are the ones who are really racist."[2] The claim is placed alongside the assertions that the Democratic Party formed the [[ku-klux-klan|Ku Klux Klan]] and the Confederacy while Republicans freed the slaves, and beside a graphic pairing the [[1924-democratic-national-convention|1924 Democratic National Convention]] "Klan Bake" with [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] signing a law honouring the Confederacy.[2]
