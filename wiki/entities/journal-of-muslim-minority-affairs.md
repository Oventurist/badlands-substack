# Journal of Muslim Minority Affairs

The *Journal of Muslim Minority Affairs* (JMMA) is an international academic journal devoted to the Muslim diaspora in non-Muslim lands, founded in the late 1970s in [[jeddah|Jeddah]], Saudi Arabia.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) It was started with the help of Syed Abedin, the late father of [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], after Saudi financier [[abdullah-omar-naseef|Abdullah Omar Naseef]] recruited the Abedin family to run the [[institute-of-muslim-minority-affairs|Institute of Muslim Minority Affairs]], whose main purpose has been to publish the journal.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) When Syed Abedin died, Huma's mother Saleha took over operations at the journal.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

The Badlands account characterizes the JMMA as an international propaganda organ for the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) It was designed to create resources for universities around the world promoting the Brotherhood's perspective on all issues, especially relations between religions and governments, and its articles have been used in universities to teach the promotion of sharia law and the supremacy of the Muslim religion.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) This is presented as a major component of the Brotherhood's "civilization/stealth jihad" strategy of pushing acceptance of sharia in non-Muslim countries — infiltration, not invasion.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

Naseef remained closely involved with the journal for decades and sat on its advisory board, being removed from the masthead only in 2003, around the time he was named a defendant in the 9/11 victims' civil suit.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) Huma Abedin was assistant editor of the journal for a dozen years — from the time she began interning at the Clinton White House in 1996 until just before she joined the [[state-department|State Department]] in 2009 — overlapping with Naseef for seven of those years.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) The article treats her simultaneous service to [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and to the journal as a conflict of interest that should have barred her from a security clearance.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)
