# Justin Mohn

**Justin Mohn** is a Pennsylvania man who, at age 32, was accused of decapitating his father and posting a video online displaying the severed head alongside a politically charged rant.[1] He was arrested hours after the video appeared, found with a gun at a National Guard base, and was held without bond on charges of murder, abuse of a corpse, and other counts according to Pennsylvania court documents.[1]

## The video

The video circulated on [[youtube|YouTube]] for hours and drew more than 5,000 views before it was removed by the platform for graphic violence.[1] In the recording Mohn described his father as a federal worker, railed against the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration and the border crisis, and declared himself the new acting President of the United States under martial law.[1]

## Context and reaction

The case surfaced during what [[cnn|CNN]] characterized as a fraught national political environment, and on the same day that social media executives were being questioned by [[congress|Congress]] over their platforms' handling of graphic and violent content.[1] CNN law enforcement analyst and former [[fbi|FBI]] Deputy Director [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] said the "bigger picture is extremely concerning" heading into what he expected to be an incredibly heated political season.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the episode to defend the practice of criticizing doomers, black-pillers, and what he called glowy elements within the Truth Community.[1] He argued that the Establishment requires only a public mandate — or at minimum apathy — from what he termed the Normie Layers of the Collective Mind in order to wage an administrative war on peaceful, patriotic Americans by associating them with figures like Mohn.[1] In his framing the factual particulars of the case mattered less than demonstrating how quickly the narrative machine converges to push divisive, Hegelian rhetoric against people uninvolved in the crime, and he characterized what he called obvious false flags of this kind as signs of desperation.[1]
