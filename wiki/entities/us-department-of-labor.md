# U.S. Department of Labor

The U.S. Department of Labor is the federal agency responsible for labor standards, workforce programs, and the collection and publication of national employment statistics. Its data releases are the principal official measure of the health of the American job market.[1]

## Role in the 2026 AI employment debate

In March 2026, Labor Department figures became a reference point in the argument over artificial intelligence and jobs. The department reported that U.S. employers shed 92,000 jobs in February 2026, a sign of a slowing labor market that coincided with rising public concern about AI displacement.[1] A February 2026 YouGov/Economist survey found about 63 percent of Americans — including 62 percent of Republicans — believed the technology would result in fewer jobs.[1]

These figures accompanied research cited in the same coverage: a study referenced by [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] estimating that widespread AI adoption could replace up to 7 percent of U.S. jobs, and work led by [[erik-brynjolfsson|Erik Brynjolfsson]] at [[stanford-university|Stanford University]] finding a 16 percent relative decline in employment among workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations since AI tools emerged in late 2022.[1] Despite hearings and multiple bills, [[congress|Congress]] had enacted no legislation addressing AI's effect on workers.[1]
