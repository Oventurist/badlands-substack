# Baal Shem Tov

Israel ben Eliezer, known as the Baal Shem Tov ("good Master of the Name") and by the acronym "BeShT," is recognized as the father of Hasidic Judaism.[1]

The Badlands News Brief describes him as one of the Kabbalist mystics of 18th-century Eastern Europe who opposed the messianic claimant [[jacob-frank|Jacob Frank]], a follower of [[sabbatai-zevi|Sabbatai Zevi]].[1] He is characterized in that account as a "shaman" or mystic healer who claimed a "direct connection to the divine" and who could imbue decorative pieces of paper with incantations, called amulets, that were said to heal the sick when read over their bodies through divine magic.[1] Mystic leaders of this type distinguished themselves from traditional rabbis by taking the title "rebbe."[1]

Unlike his great-grandson [[nachman-of-breslov|Rebbe Nachman of Breslov]], the Baal Shem Tov never claimed to be the Messiah.[1] His movement forms the root of the [[breslov-hasidism|Breslov Hasidic]] lineage and of the broader Hasidic engagement with [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] that the Brief treats as a mystical departure from Torah-based Judaism.[1]
