# Aleutian Islands

The Aleutian Islands are the chain of islands extending westward from the Alaska Peninsula into the North Pacific, forming the outer maritime approach to the American homeland from Asia.[1] Their position makes the surrounding waters a recurring venue for foreign naval activity near United States territory.[1]

## 2023 joint Russian-Chinese patrol

In early August 2023, a combined Russian and Chinese naval patrol operated off the coast of Alaska, with Alaska Senators [[dan-sullivan|Dan Sullivan]] and [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] reporting that eleven Russian and Chinese vessels had been operating near the Aleutian Islands.[1] Four [[us-navy|US Navy]] destroyers were deployed in response, while [[united-states-northern-command|US Northern Command]] and [[norad|NORAD]] sent aircraft and ships to monitor the patrol, which remained in international waters and, according to a NORTHCOM spokesperson, did not pose a threat to the United States or Canada.[1]

Murkowski said she and Sullivan had received detailed classified briefings about the foreign vessels transiting US waters in the Aleutians.[1] Sullivan recalled that Chinese and Russian vessels had come similarly close to Alaska the previous summer, when they were met only by a [[us-coast-guard|US Coast Guard]] ship on routine patrol.[1]

Chinese Embassy spokesperson Liu Pengyu said the patrols were conducted under an annual cooperation plan between the Chinese and Russian militaries and were "not targeted at any third party."[1] Badlands commentary treated the episode as at least the third such patrol in roughly eighteen months and pointed to the absence of any [[white-house|White House]] statement as the notable feature of the American response.[1]
