# Thomas Noguchi

Thomas Noguchi was the Los Angeles County medical examiner and coroner who conducted the autopsy on Senator [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]] after the shooting at the Ambassador Hotel in June 1968.[1]

Noguchi's findings are central to the case against the official account of the killing. He determined that the bullet that struck Kennedy behind his right ear was fired from a distance of only one inch to one and a half inches, and that the killing shot entered at close range from the back of the neck rather than from the front.[1] Witnesses, by contrast, placed the convicted gunman [[sirhan-sirhan|Sirhan Sirhan]] no closer than eighteen inches from Kennedy and standing in front of him.[1]

Some observers proposed that Kennedy had turned his head at the critical moment, allowing Sirhan to hit him; others concluded from the same evidence that one of the security guards must have fired the fatal round.[1] Noguchi's measurements remain the foundation of the argument, later pressed by attorneys William Pepper and Laurie Dusek, that the neck bullet did not come from Sirhan's gun.[1]
