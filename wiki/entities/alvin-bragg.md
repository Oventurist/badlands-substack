# Alvin Bragg

Alvin Bragg is the District Attorney of Manhattan, New York County, whose prosecutorial decisions became a recurring point of conflict between New York City's small business community and the city's political leadership.[1]

## Criticism from New York small business owners

In April 2024 the Bodega and Small Business Group (BSBG), an organization representing thousands of New York City bodegas, issued a statement backing former President [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] that explicitly criticized Bragg.[1] The statement centered on the case of bodega worker [[jose-alba|Jose Alba]], who was arrested at the Blue Moon bodega in Manhattan after defending himself against a convicted felon inside his own store.[1] According to the BSBG, "The Manhattan DA, Alvin Bragg, only backed off after an angry public campaign from store owners and their supporters united behind Mr. Alba and his Constitutional rights."[1]

The episode was cited by store owners as emblematic of a broader pattern in which, in their telling, prosecutors and local politicians ignored widespread criminality in the city while pursuing charges against those who defended themselves and their property.[1]

## Context in the 2024 election cycle

Bragg's office was also the venue for the Manhattan criminal prosecution of Donald Trump, and commentary aggregated by [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] framed the actions against Trump as "unprecedented election interference" that inadvertently provided the public with "a master class in government corruption and how it can be prosecuted."[1] Trump's April 2024 visit to a Harlem bodega — where supporters chanted "four more years!" and "USA!" — was read in that commentary as evidence that the divide between the ruling class and ordinary New Yorkers had never been clearer.[1]

## The March 2023 hush-money case and the Cohen FEC letter

In March 2023 Bragg's office was reported to be preparing charges against Trump over the 2016 payment to adult film actress [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]], and a widely anticipated arrest expected on Tuesday, March 21 did not materialize.[2] Badlands commentary noted that the case was potentially undercut by a purported 2018 letter from a lawyer for [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] to the [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]] stating that Cohen had used his own personal funds to pay Daniels and that the Trump campaign was not a party to the transaction and did not reimburse him — material described as "totally exculpatory."[2]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized Bragg as a "blatantly corrupt Soros-backed DA" presiding over some of the most crime-ridden territory in the nation, and argued that his pursuit of a former president rested on allegations that had already failed in court, pointing to the imprisonment of Michael Avenatti and the roughly $300,000 in legal fees Daniels was ordered to pay.[2] In that reading the prosecutors knew there was no case and wanted "the show and the mug shot" — a [[nancy-pelosi|Pelosi]]-style "wrap-up smear" intended to entrap the more reactionary of Trump's supporters, with DeLarme urging massive peaceful but energized protest focused on [[george-soros|George Soros]].[2]

## Grand jury signal to Trump (March 2023)

On March 9, 2023, The New York Times reported that prosecutors in Bragg's office had signaled that criminal charges against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were likely, telling the former president that he could appear before a Manhattan grand jury the following week if he wished to testify — a strong indication that an indictment could soon follow.[3] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] dismissed the report as "another nothing-burger," arguing that the establishment had never been able to beat Trump directly or with the truth and therefore sought to delegitimize him with propaganda, and that any genuinely damaging material would already have leaked during earlier investigations.[3] See [[trump-hush-money-case|the resulting case]].[3]

## Exemption from the Merchan gag order

When Justice [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] issued a gag order against Trump on March 26, 2024, the order barred public comment on witnesses and counsel but expressly excepted Bragg himself, while extending protection to the staff of both the court and the district attorney's office as well as their family members.[4]

## Public opinion on the prosecution (June 2024)

An ABC News/Ipsos poll released in early June 2024 found that 47% of Americans believed the charges Bragg brought against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were politically motivated, while 38% said they were not.[5] At the same time 50% said Trump's guilty verdict in the Bragg case was correct, 27% said it was incorrect and 23% said they did not know.[5] Trump's favorability was essentially unchanged after the verdict at 31%, versus 29% in March 2024, as was [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s at 32% versus 33%.[5] Among the 55% of Americans who said they followed the case somewhat or very closely, 57% believed the verdict was correct and 35% did not.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the poll as a narrative deployment signaling panic, arguing that the United States is not a "50/50 country" and that [[abc-news|ABC]] and [[ipsos|Ipsos]] were manufacturing the appearance of a competitive race to make a Trump loss seem plausible.[5]

## O'Keefe insider claim (March 2023)

In late March 2023, as grand jury meetings in Bragg's case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were postponed, journalist [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]] suggested he had insiders within the district attorney's case, responding to a political operative's question with: "The real question is how many do we have on the inside? Stay tuned."[6] The operative had alleged that Bragg "likely hid exculpatory evidence from the Trump Grand Jury."[6] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] concluded that whether or not the insiders existed, "Bragg and company are sweating."[6]

## April 2023 arraignment and congressional backlash

Bragg's indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] produced the first arraignment of a former American president, with Trump traveling from his Palm Beach estate to New York on Monday, April 3, 2023, to surrender at the district attorney's office the following day and be arraigned in the Manhattan Criminal Courts Building.[7] Legal commentators across the political spectrum noted that Bragg had promised in advance to find a way to indict Trump, boasts compared by American Greatness to Soviet secret police chief Lavrentiy Beria's quip, "Show me the man and I'll show you the crime."[7]

The prosecution drew immediate congressional retaliation. House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] said Congress would take action to punish Bragg after he charged Trump following months of downgrading violent felonies against other offenders, and Representative [[austin-scott|Austin Scott]] floated stripping district attorneys of their exemption from civil liability.[7] Critics argued that a prosecutor of the same party as a sitting president seeking to destroy the viability of that president's likely opponent was a first in U.S. history and consistent with Third World norms.[7]

## America First Legal FEC complaint, June 2024

In June 2024, [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] filed a formal complaint with the [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]] against Bragg and Biden's principal campaign committee, Biden for President, alleging violations of the Federal Election Campaign Act.[8] The complaint centered on Bragg's December 2022 hiring of [[matthew-colangelo|Matthew B. Colangelo]], a former senior [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] official brought on to advance the office's investigation of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the strength of his prior work on Trump-related cases at the DOJ and the New York Attorney General's Office, and who had also donated to Bragg's campaign.[8] AFL argued that Bragg's prosecution of Trump was coordinated with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] with the aim of influencing the 2024 presidential election, noting that Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] had at a congressional hearing declined to deny the existence of communications between the Biden DOJ and Bragg's office while refusing to disclose them.[8]

## February 2024 removal calls

In February 2024, legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] cited mounting calls for Bragg's removal in New York, noting that he was accused of being lax on crime overall despite his determined effort to convict [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[9] The New York Post reported that Governor Kathy Hochul had been urged to remove Bragg after his office let a migrant mob walk free despite a caught-on-camera beating of police officers.[9] Turley grouped the episode with the collapse of the ballot disqualification effort and the Georgia prosecutorial scandal as evidence that Trump might prevail by attrition rather than vindication.[9]

## Grand jury delay (March 2023)

On 29 March 2023, after weeks of media anticipation of an imminent arrest of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Politico reported that the New York grand jury hearing evidence in Bragg's hush money probe was not scheduled to meet on the case until late April, pushing any indictment to that month at the earliest.[10] The outlet attributed the pause to a "previously scheduled hiatus," an explanation ZeroHedge questioned given that it had gone unmentioned while commentators predicted an immediate perp walk.[10]

The delay coincided with an Epoch Times report by Jack Phillips that an attorney in Trump's orbit who had testified before the Manhattan grand jury earlier that month believed there had been a shift in Bragg's case against the 45th president, saying "I think I got through to them."[10] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] mocked the hiatus as "the Trump indictment version of 'two weeks to flatten the curve.'"[10]

## Trump's response to the indictment (April 2023)

After a Manhattan grand jury indicted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the hush money payment, Trump issued a statement denouncing the case as "Political Persecution and Election Interference at the highest level in history," and describing Bragg as "hand-picked and funded by [[george-soros|George Soros]]" and "a disgrace."[11] Trump accused Bragg of doing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s "dirty work" and of ignoring "the murders and burglaries and assaults he should be focused on" amid what he called an unprecedented crime wave in New York City.[11] He predicted that his movement would "first defeat Alvin Bragg, and then we will defeat Joe Biden."[11]

The indictment drew supportive reactions from Republican figures, with [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] indicating Florida would not assist in extradition and [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] denouncing the prosecution as an injustice.[11] In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump himself was pushing the indictment narrative hardest, and that commentators within the [[media-industrial-complex|media industrial complex]] and the cultural left who called the move a miscalculation were effectively conceding it would raise Trump's political capital ahead of the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 campaign cycle]].[11]

## Daniel Penny verdict (December 2024)

After a Manhattan jury acquitted [[daniel-penny|Daniel Penny]] of criminally negligent homicide in the chokehold death of [[jordan-neely|Jordan Neely]] on December 9, 2024, Bragg said he respected the verdict.[12] "Their lengthy deliberation — and the totality of the facts and the evidence — underscored why this case was put in front of a jury of Mr. Penny's peers," he said in a statement, noting that the jury had deliberated about an hour on the criminally negligent homicide charge after roughly four days on the manslaughter count that the judge dismissed following a deadlock.[12]

## March 2023 Expected Indictment and Criticism

Ahead of the anticipated March 2023 indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], legal scholars suggested Bragg's case rested on questionable legal and ethical ground.[13] Bragg's office had not publicly confirmed reports that it might indict the former president over the allegedly misclassified $130,000 hush payment made to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] in 2016, and had announced no specific charges, though observers expected a charge of falsifying business records under New York Penal Law Section 175.[13]

House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] publicly attacked Bragg at a press conference during the House Republican retreat on March 19, 2023, saying "it should be equal justice in America" and telling prosecutors to "stop going after people because you have political differences."[13] McCarthy noted that Bragg "brags about lowering felonies to misdemeanors as a prosecutor" while crime in New York left people "afraid to walk the streets," argued that Republican victories in New York races were driven in part by the DA's failure to protect citizens, and asserted that the statute of limitations in the Trump matter had lapsed.[13] Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] separately described the Soros-backed DA's "made-for-TV" prosecution as "legally pathetic."[13]

## March 2023 Grand Jury Fumble and National Exposure

In late March 2023, as his Manhattan grand jury weighed charges against Donald Trump, Bragg drew withering criticism from the New York Post, which argued that he had "exposed his incompetence nationwide" by fumbling the case.[14] Bragg attempted to blame the circus atmosphere surrounding the probe on Trump himself, but critics noted that it was Bragg who took the case to a grand jury, and that the invitation for Trump to testify — traditionally a signal that grand jurors are ready to indict — was certain to leak, whether from the former president or from Bragg's own office.[14] Bragg also declined to cooperate with a House probe of his handling of the Trump case.[14]

Commentators tied the episode to Bragg's broader record, beginning with his Day One memo instructing prosecutors to stop seeking prison in a range of cases, a policy he had barely revised even as Manhattan crime — particularly shoplifting that was crushing retail establishments — continued to climb, with murders and shootings the exception thanks to targeted [[nypd|NYPD]] enforcement.[14] The Post concluded that the whole nation was learning what New Yorkers already knew: that Bragg simply was not very good at his job.[14]

Badlands Media's [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Bragg had "quickly shifted from hero of the Left to national laughing stock" — hated by the Right for partisan lawfare and by the Left for failing to consummate their anti-Trump fantasies — and that the public exposure of a corrupt and partisan establishment justice system was a significant net effect of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s narrative mastery.[14] Burning Bright further read Bragg's public meltdown, which specifically referenced Trump's own seeding of the indictment narrative, as evidence that the indictment story had been deployed deliberately to smoke out adversaries and pre-empt a media shock-and-awe campaign.[14]
