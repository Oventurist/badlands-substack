# Loicq de Lobel

**Loicq de Lobel** was a French engineer employed in 1902 by Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] to advance plans for a rail crossing of the Bering Strait.[1]

De Lobel is quoted as predicting that "we will be running through trains in five or six years," promising "no more seasickness, no more dangers of wrecked liners, a fast trip in palace cars with every convenience."[1] His work belonged to the same Russian imperial push that saw Prime Minister [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]] and Nicholas II sponsor teams of American engineers to survey the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] route, culminating in a March 23, 1906 New York Times headline reading "For Bering Strait Tunnel — Czar Approves Recommendation for All-Rail Route to America."[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that this trajectory was halted by what he characterizes as two early color revolutions and the murder of the czar, after which the project lay dormant until its revival by American Vice-President [[henry-wallace|Henry Wallace]] and his Russian counterparts in 1942.[1]
