# Poland

Poland is a Central European state and [[nato|NATO]] member whose border and defense posture featured in Badlands' June 2026 coverage of Eastern Europe.[1]

## Border measures

Poland has continued strengthening security measures along its border with [[germany|Germany]], even as Germany prepared legislation that could restore mandatory military service if voluntary recruitment falls short.[1] Both developments were presented as part of a broader adjustment of European defense and border policies amid the ongoing war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that headlines about Poland facing invasion, German conscription, and Ukrainian ties to cartels form "a constellation of insanity and satire that undermine the verisimilitude of the entire narrative," while concluding that Eastern Europe is nonetheless making itself into a powder keg because of Ukraine.[1] He noted the paradox that these states claim to be militarily aligned while behaving as though engaged in an arms race against one another.[1]

## Ammunition stockpiles and readiness (2025)

In March 2025 Dariusz Lukowski, head of Poland's National Security Bureau, told the broadcaster Polsat News that Poland had ammunition sufficient to hold the line for up to two weeks in the event of war, until allied forces arrived.[2] He said the country had been increasing arms production to build capacity to resist a potential Russian attack, noting that the [[nato|NATO]] member shares a 144-mile border with Russia's Kaliningrad exclave.[2]

Asked whether Poland's reserves might last only five days, Lukowski said that was "possible in many areas and types of ammunition," though not across all categories of munitions, and that "depending on how the fighting unfolds, we could maintain defensive operations for a week or two with the current level of stockpiles."[2] He identified ammunition for older weapons systems, for which production has ceased, as the most critical shortfall.[2] Moscow has consistently denied aggressive intentions toward NATO countries and maintains that the Ukraine conflict was triggered by the bloc's expansion toward Russia's borders.[2]

Badlands commentary reacted derisively to the two-week figure and to the reliance on allied reinforcement, describing European leaders as "kites dancing in a hurricane."[2]


## NATO air policing after Russian drone incursions (September 2025)

Following incursions of Russian drones into Polish airspace in September 2025, British fighter jets began conducting air defense missions over Poland under a [[nato|NATO]] initiative named [[eastern-sentry|Eastern Sentry]].[3] The [[royal-air-force|RAF]] Typhoons involved, based in Lincolnshire, operated alongside forces from Denmark, France and Germany to strengthen NATO's eastern defenses, supported by a Voyager refuelling aircraft.[3]

British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Sir Keir Starmer]] said "Russia's reckless behaviour is a direct threat to European security," while Defence Secretary [[john-healey|John Healey]] said NATO had responded with unity to the challenge posed by Moscow.[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] was sceptical, remarking that the allies were "not defending Poland as hard as they didn't defend 'Ukraine'."[3]

## Preparation for full-scale conflict (July 2024)

During the week of the [[2024-nato-washington-summit|NATO Washington Summit]], Poland's army chief of staff General [[wieslaw-kukula|Wiesław Kukuła]] called for the country to prepare its soldiers for all-out war.[4] "Today, we need to prepare our forces for full-scale conflict, not an asymmetric-type conflict," he told a press conference, adding that the shift "forces us to find a good balance between the border mission and maintaining the intensity of training in the army."[4]

The announcement was grouped by observers with other summit-week escalations — new US anti-air defense systems for Ukraine and the confirmed transfer of F-16 fighter jets — and with [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]]'s vow that Ukraine's path to [[nato|NATO]] must end in the demise of Ukraine, the alliance, or both.[4] Badlands commentary described Poland as preparing for conflict with "their historic nemesis" and read the sequence as evidence that the world had been "reforged into a powder keg."[4]

## Drone incursion dispute, September 2025

In early September 2025 Poland accused Russia of violating its airspace with a number of drones, with Foreign Minister [[radoslaw-sikorski|Radoslaw Sikorski]] warning that any intrusions would be met with force; Estonia levelled similar accusations, all of which Russia dismissed as unfounded.[5] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] argued at the 80th UN General Assembly session that the drones allegedly found on Polish territory had a flight range shorter than the distance from the Russian border to Poland and therefore could not have been launched from Russia.[5]

## Uranium discovery in the Świętokrzyskie region

In 2024 the Świętokrzyska Industrial Group Industria (ŚGP Industria) confirmed the presence of uranium in a deposit within the Świętokrzyskie region of southern Poland, a find made during exploratory activities whose exact location the company chose not to disclose.[6] The company announced plans for detailed work over the following months to establish the deposit's size, the content of its various elements, and potential methods of exploitation.[6]

The [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]] estimates that Poland holds approximately 100,000 tons of natural uranium, of which only about 7,000 tons had been confirmed at the time.[6] ŚGP Industria had received an exploratory concession from the Ministry of Climate and Environment covering a polymetallic deposit containing uranium in the region, and the discovery was framed as a potential domestic fuel source for a future Polish nuclear power plant.[6]

Poland also figured in the May 2024 *New York Times* report on Washington's mood ahead of a possible return of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to the presidency, in which a former investigator named Poland as the country he would relocate to in the event of such a result.[6]



## Belarus border reinforcement, August 2023

In August 2023 Poland announced it would send an additional 2,000 troops to reinforce its border with [[belarus|Belarus]] amid heightened tensions related to the war in Ukraine.[7] Deputy interior minister [[maciej-wasik|Maciej Wasik]] told the state news agency PAP that the deployment was double what the country's Border Guard had requested and that the reinforcements would arrive within two weeks.[7] The move followed mounting concern in Poland, a NATO member, over the presence of mercenaries from Russia's [[wagner-group|Wagner]] private military company in neighboring Belarus; Prime Minister [[mateusz-morawiecki|Mateusz Morawiecki]] had said there were at least 4,000 Wagner fighters there and warned against "provocations" and "sabotage actions," days after two Belarusian helicopters breached Polish airspace.[7]
