# Marsha Blackburn

Marsha Blackburn is a Republican United States Senator representing Tennessee (R-TN).[1]

## Alleged threat and arrest

In August 2025, a 22-year-old transgender person identified as [[penelope-convertino|Penelope Convertino]] was arrested after allegedly threatening to kill Blackburn.[1] Reporting by The Tennessee Star, based on yearbook photographs it obtained, indicated that Convertino had been a member of the Gay Straight Trans Alliance (GSTA) club at Riverdale High School in Murfreesboro, Tennessee, which the accused attended in 2019.[1] The 2019 yearbook reviewed by the outlet used the female first name Penelope, and listed participation in four school clubs, including the GSTA club.[1]

The case was carried in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief as a bonus item, part of a broader editorial theme of political violence and threats directed at sitting legislators.[1]

## June 2025 reaction to the China election intelligence disclosure

After [[kash-patel|FBI Director Kash Patel]] disclosed the recalled 2020 intelligence alleging a Chinese plot to manufacture fraudulent mail-in votes, Blackburn posted a public response that Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued should have drawn "unbridled rage" from MAGA rather than encouragement, contending that she was lying both about not having known and about the solution she proposed, and asking why anyone would settle for such a reply after five years of lies from the establishment GOP.[2] See [[fbi-recall-of-2020-china-election-intelligence|FBI recall of 2020 China election intelligence]].[2]

## December 2024 DOGE Acts

In early December 2024 Blackburn unveiled a package of bills she called the [[doge-acts|DOGE Acts]], aimed at cutting spending and freezing federal hiring and salaries.[3] Her spokesperson said the package was intended to hold the federal government "more accountable for managing taxpayer dollars" and that it coincided with the plan by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] to make the federal government more efficient.[3]

The legislation would cut discretionary spending, move federal agencies "out of the swamp," freeze federal hiring and salaries for one year, begin a merit-based compensation structure for federal employees, and require agencies to bring staff back into the office.[3] Individual bills would create a commission on relocating some federal agencies away from Washington, D.C., impose a yearlong pause on federal hiring and salaries, and establish a merit-based pay pilot program for civilian employees.[3] The package was framed as a legislative complement to the [[department-of-government-efficiency|Department of Government Efficiency]], announced by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] the previous month with Musk and Ramaswamy at its head.[3]
