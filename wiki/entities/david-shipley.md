# David Shipley

David Shipley is a newspaper editor who served as Opinion Section editor of [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] until his resignation in February 2025.[1]

## Resignation over Bezos opinion mandate

Shipley resigned after owner [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]] mandated that the Post's opinion section prioritize two topics — personal liberties and free markets — and decline to publish dissenting views in those areas.[1] In a note to staff on Wednesday, February 26, 2025, Bezos wrote, "This is a significant shift, it won't be easy, and it will require 100% commitment — I respect his decision," and said the paper would search for a new Opinion Editor "to own this new direction."[1]

The change followed Bezos's earlier decision to kill presidential endorsements, a move that cost the Post thousands of subscribers and prompted resignations from members of the Opinion Board.[1] Commentators noted the shift dramatically reshaped opinion coverage that had for decades spanned a broad array of politics and policy topics.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer Burning Bright argued that the episode's significance was that it forced "the media protectorate to admit they were playing" a game in the first place, contending that journalists were angry not at being given different rules but at the public learning what they are.[1]

## Role in the 2024 non-endorsement

As top opinion editor at [[washington-post|The Washington Post]], Shipley informed the paper's editorial board on Friday, October 25, 2024 that the paper would issue no presidential endorsement, a decision made by owner [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]].[2] Board members were reported to have been taken aback, having drafted an endorsement of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] earlier that month and forwarded it to Bezos; NPR reported that opinion staff learned the news at a tense meeting shortly before the publisher's announcement.[2] The episode preceded Shipley's own later departure and is treated by Badlands as a key moment in the [[2024-newspaper-non-endorsements|2024 newspaper non-endorsements]].[2]
