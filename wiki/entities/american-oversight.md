# American Oversight

American Oversight is a government watchdog organization that in March 2025 sued Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and other Trump administration officials over their use of the encrypted messaging application [[signal|Signal]] to discuss military plans to strike Houthi targets in Yemen.[1]

## The Signal lawsuit

The group alleged that the Signal chat — conducted on an unclassified commercial app and which mistakenly included The Atlantic's editor-in-chief [[jeffrey-goldberg|Jeffrey Goldberg]] — violated federal records laws.[1] Filed in a federal court in the District of Columbia, the suit named Hegseth alongside national intelligence director [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], CIA director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]], and [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] in his capacities as secretary of state and acting archivist.[1]

American Oversight said it was seeking to "recover unlawfully deleted messages and prevent further destruction."[1] The organization emphasized that the [[federal-records-act|Federal Records Act]] requires officials to preserve communications related to government business, and that agencies typically ensure retention of messages on encrypted apps by mandating that they be forwarded to official systems or otherwise archived.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary framed the timing of the suit as notable, coming as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was bringing the Crossfire Hurricane matter "back around."[1]
