# Financial Times

The Financial Times is a British business newspaper of record.[1]

## Epstein litigation coverage

In February 2023 the Financial Times reported that a New York judge had granted the [[us-virgin-islands|US Virgin Islands]] permission to mail a subpoena to former Victoria's Secret boss [[les-wexner|Les Wexner]] over his ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], after a security guard allegedly prevented the billionaire from being served at his home.[1] The report noted the territory's frustrated attempts to obtain documents from Wexner as part of its civil suit against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], and was carried in the Badlands News Brief of February 22, 2023.[1]

## Tariff coverage and Badlands criticism (2025)

In April 2025 the Financial Times reported that the latest round of tariffs unleashed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would hit almost every industry across the globe, presenting an unprecedented challenge for businesses already contending with weak demand and inflationary pressure.[2] The paper quoted industry executives warning that the biggest victims would be US consumers, who would pay more for goods ranging from Adidas trainers to Modelo, the country's top-selling beer, and noted that the baseline 10 percent levy plus additional duties of up to 50 percent on partners including the [[european-union|European Union]], Japan, Vietnam, and Cambodia would fall heavily on footwear and clothing brands sourcing from south-east Asia.[2]

Badlands commentators treated the paper's reaction as diagnostic rather than authoritative. Citing [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s characterization of the Financial Times of London as "the paper of record for the party of Davos" — that is, of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] — [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the paper's displeasure with the [[liberation-day-tariffs|Liberation Day tariffs]] itself revealed whose interests the tariffs threatened.[2] He also highlighted what he saw as a self-refuting juxtaposition on the paper's front page, where an op-ed titled "America's astonishing act of self-harm" ran beside a news article titled "Europe braces for a flood of Chinese goods after US tariffs," asking why redirecting cheap Chinese goods away from the American market and into the European one should count as self-harm, and suggesting Europe might instead tariff China as it tariffs the United States at some 40 percent.[2]
