# Ronald Reagan

Ronald Reagan was President of the United States during the 1980s and, in the argument of "A New Hollywood," the model for how a president can use the film industry as an instrument of national renewal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that Reagan took control of [[hollywood|Hollywood]] during his eight years in office, pushing for movies that promoted strong American characters like Rambo.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) The films made during his presidency made the American military seem powerful, which both inspired Americans and intimidated foreign adversaries.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Carlson notes that the original *Top Gun*, released in 1986 while Reagan was president, was the biggest military recruiting tool ever created.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

Carlson describes [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as "Reagan 2.0" — not a continuation of Reagan's efforts but a vastly improved version, one that does not include compromising with the enemy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Just as Reagan understood the power of Hollywood, he argues, Trump understands it too.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## The 1980 October surprise

The term [[october-surprise|"October surprise"]] entered the American political lexicon in 1980 out of fear within Reagan's presidential campaign that President [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] would win the release of the 52 hostages held in Iran for nearly a year, handing the incumbent a major diplomatic success.[2] Reagan campaign manager Bill Casey publicly warned that Carter might be planning such a surprise, and the campaign urged friends in the intelligence and military communities to flag any signs a release was in the works.[2] No release came before the vote; instead the anniversary of the captivity fell on election eve, and the hostages were freed minutes after Reagan's inauguration.[2]

A subsequent theory held that the real October surprise was an effort by the Reagan campaign to persuade Iran *not* to release the hostages until after the election.[2] A House investigation concluded in 1993 that there was "little or no credible evidence of communication between the 1980 Reagan campaign and the government of Iran," but biographer [[max-boot|Max Boot]], reviewing new documents on Casey's movements and later dealings between the Reagan administration and Iran, called the evidence "substantial and credible if still circumstantial."[2]

## Use of his tariff remarks in the 2025 Ontario ad

In October 2025, the government of [[ontario|Ontario]] aired an [[ontario-reagan-tariff-advertisement|advertisement]] in the United States featuring Reagan speaking negatively about tariffs, prompting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to terminate all trade negotiations with [[canada|Canada]] on October 23.[3] The Ronald Reagan Presidential Foundation and Institute said the ad misrepresented a presidential radio address Reagan delivered in April 1987 and that his remarks had been edited without permission, though it did not specify what was misleading.[3] Ontario Premier [[doug-ford|Doug Ford]] had said the province would spend $75 million on the ad campaign.[3]

## War on drugs and the assassination attempt

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] observes that beyond being former Republican US presidents from California, Reagan and [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]] shared two things: each declared a [[war-on-drugs|war on drugs]], and each faced an attempt to remove him from office.[4] Nixon was removed through what Carlson calls a hoax led by [[george-hw-bush|George Bush Sr.]] and the [[cia|CIA]], while Reagan was shot by an assassin with close ties to the Bush family.[4] Bush was Vice President at the time of the assassination attempt and a former Director of the CIA.[4] The [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra Affair]], funded in part by CIA cocaine trafficking into Los Angeles, was exposed during Reagan's presidency in 1986.[4]
