# Mike Simpson

Mike Simpson is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from Idaho who serves as chairman of the House Appropriations Subcommittee on Interior, Environment, and Related Agencies.[1]

## Fiscal Year 2026 Interior and Environment bill

On July 15, 2025, Simpson's subcommittee met to consider and approve its Fiscal Year 2026 appropriations bill.[1] In a statement accompanying the markup, Simpson said the measure "echoes this Administration's efforts to roll back costly regulatory overreach from the prior Administration and promote domestic energy production," specifically by halting Biden-era [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] regulations he described as "heavy-handed" and "job killing," limiting what he characterized as the prior administration's attempts to abuse the Endangered Species Act, ensuring continued access to public lands, and expanding access to hardrock and critical minerals.[1] He added that the bill "focuses spending where it is needed most" and "makes great strides to address critical needs across Indian Country," while helping to "unleash American energy and domestic mineral development."[1]

The markup drew criticism in Badlands commentary from [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]], who pointed to a provision he characterized as introducing "vaccine-like immunity for pesticide manufacturers" and called it "anti-MAHA" — a reference to the [[make-america-healthy-again]] movement.[1] The bill was advanced through the [[house-appropriations-committee|House Appropriations Committee]] process alongside subcommittee ranking and leadership participation by Tom Cole.[1]
