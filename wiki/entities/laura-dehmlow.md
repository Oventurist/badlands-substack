# Laura Dehmlow

Laura Dehmlow is a career official of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] who served as Section Chief of the Bureau's Foreign Influence Task Force, the unit responsible for liaising with social media platforms over suspected foreign information operations.[1] She became a figure of public controversy in July 2023 when her transcribed testimony to the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] and its [[house-weaponization-committee|Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]] was characterized by committee chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] as an admission that the FBI knew the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] was authentic even as Bureau personnel were priming social media companies to treat it as a possible Russian "hack and leak" operation.[1]

## Role in the laptop disclosure controversy

Dehmlow sat for the transcribed interview on Monday, July 17, 2023, and Jordan summarized her account in a letter to FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] issued that Thursday.[1] According to Jordan's letter, the same FBI personnel who warned platforms ahead of the 2020 election that the laptop material could be part of a Russian influence operation understood that the material was not disinformation.[1] Jordan wrote that after the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] published its initial laptop story in October 2020, the Bureau "made an institutional decision to refuse to answer direct questions from social media companies about the laptop's authenticity — despite months of constant information sharing up to that time."[1]

The practical effect, in Jordan's framing, was that the FBI first "conditioned" platforms to believe the laptop was the product of a hack-and-dump operation and then abruptly withdrew from the information-sharing relationship, "allowing social media companies to conclude that the New York Post story was Russian disinformation."[1] Dehmlow's position at the head of the Foreign Influence Task Force placed her at the institutional center of that sequence, and her testimony has been cited by critics of federal involvement in content moderation as direct evidence of a government role in suppressing accurate reporting.[1]
