# Commission on Presidential Debates

The Commission on Presidential Debates is the body traditionally responsible for setting the terms of United States presidential debates. In May 2024 it became a focus of Badlands criticism over the negotiation of the 2024 debate schedule between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Biden had demanded "very undemocratic debate conditions" — no audience, a mute button, and a hand-picked moderator team — and that the Commission's acquiescence to such terms "proves that we are living in a different America than we all believed possible."[1] The core objection was procedural: the commission, not the candidates, is supposed to set the terms, and the spectacle of one candidate setting all the terms was characterized as [[uniparty|uniparty]] manipulation "so transparent that only the intellectually dishonest can ignore it."[1]

The dispute arose after the Biden campaign rejected two further debate proposals plus a vice-presidential debate, having decreed strict conditions for two debates only — the June [[cnn|CNN]] and September [[abc-news|ABC]] events — while Trump accepted additional invitations from [[fox-news|Fox News]] and NBC/Telemundo.[1]

## Trump's March 2024 debate challenge

The commission featured in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s March 6, 2024 Truth Social post calling on [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to debate him "ANYTIME, ANYWHERE, ANYPLACE."[2] Trump wrote that "the Debates can be run by the Corrupt DNC, or their Subsidiary, the Commission on Presidential Debates (CPD)," a framing that treated the ostensibly nonpartisan body as an arm of the Democratic Party.[2] The challenge came days after [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] suspended her campaign following Super Tuesday losses, and after Trump had declined to participate in any Republican primary debate.[2] The Biden campaign dismissed the call as a bid for attention.[2]

## April 2024 Trump campaign demands

In April 2024 the commission's co-chairs, [[frank-fahrenkopf|Frank Fahrenkopf Jr.]] and [[antonia-hernandez|Antonia Hernandez]], received a letter from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] co-campaign managers [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] and [[chris-lacivita|Chris LaCivita]] demanding that the general-election debates with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] be moved "much earlier" and that additional debates be scheduled.[3] The commission had already announced three presidential debates and a vice-presidential debate for later in 2024; the Trump campaign wrote that it was "in agreement with the pending letter" from television networks advocating that the debates take place earlier in the year.[3]
