# France

France is a Western European nation-state, nuclear power and permanent member of the United Nations Security Council whose historical empire encompassed much of West and Central Africa. Its post-colonial relationships in the Sahara-Sahel region — sometimes described by critics as "Françafrique" — remain a central point of contention in contemporary multipolar politics.[1]

## Accusations of "revanchism" in Africa (2026)

In February 2026, Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] publicly accused France of pursuing "revanchist" policies in Africa.[1] In a speech to the Russian parliament, Lavrov said Paris was "attempting to overthrow undesirable nationalist governments in the Sahara-Sahel region and other parts of Africa," alleging that France was working through opposition figures, "outright terrorist groups and Ukrainian militants" to undermine states that had, in his words, "long since abandoned their ties to their former metropolis."[1]

The accusation arrived amid ongoing diplomatic tension between Russia and Western nations over influence on the African continent, where French garrisons and diplomatic missions have been expelled from several Sahelian states in recent years.[1] France has previously stated that it respects the sovereignty of African nations while simultaneously expressing concern about unconstitutional changes of government in the region — a formulation critics read as reserving the right to object to the military governments now in power.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands commentary places France's activity within a broader contest described as the War of Sovereignty, in which the targets are said to include the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] under Burkinabè President [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]].[1]

## Air strikes in Syria (December 2024)

France's defense ministry announced on Tuesday, December 31, 2024 that French warplanes had launched airstrikes against [[isis|Islamic State]] targets in [[syria]] over the preceding weekend — the first such French strikes since [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] took Damascus following the overthrow of President Bashar al-Assad on December 8.[2] Defense Minister Sébastien Lecornu said in a statement on X that "on Sunday, French air assets carried out targeted strikes against Daesh sites on Syrian soil," and published a video of the operation, adding that "our armies remain engaged in the fight against terrorism" in the region.[2]

The statement specified that French Rafale fighter jets and American Reaper drones "dropped a total of seven bombs on two military targets belonging to Daesh in central Syria."[2] The strikes came as HTS held Damascus and major cities across southern, central and coastal Syria, [[turkey|Turkey]] and its Syrian National Army proxies held parts of the north, the United States occupied the northeast through the [[syrian-democratic-forces|Syrian Democratic Forces]], and the Israeli military held an expanse out of the Golan Heights.[2]

## Evacuation from Niger, 2023

France began evacuating hundreds of French and European citizens from [[niger|Niger]] on Tuesday, August 1, 2023, following the [[2023-niger-coup|military coup]] that deposed President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]] and the warning by [[mali|Mali]] and [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] that outside intervention would be treated as a declaration of war.[3] As the former colonial power in the region, France was a focal point of the coup's politics: resistance figures labelled Bazoum "a French puppet."[3]

## Military presence in Africa (2024)

In September 2024, Badlands Media reported that the Prime Minister of [[chad|Chad]] had said US troops who withdrew in May 2024 would not return, and that Chad's grievance is with the French military rather than the American one — the Chadian public considering French forces a hostile adversary and demanding their expulsion.[4] If Chad expels those forces, it would mean that for the first time since 1659, when the French first built a port in what is now Senegal, France would have no military presence on the African continent, Chad being "the last bastion of French imperialism."[4] The item ran alongside criticism of President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s call for a European diplomatic force to "build bridges with Africa and Latin America."[4]
