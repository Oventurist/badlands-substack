# International Monetary Fund

The International Monetary Fund appears in "A Family Affair" as the instrument by which indebted states are captured — "the cabal's IMF" in the author's phrasing.[1]

In February 1989, at the beginning of his second term, Venezuelan president [[carlos-andres-perez|Carlos Andrés Pérez]] accepted an IMF proposal known as the Washington consensus, and in return the Fund offered Venezuela a loan of $4.5 billion. The arrangement came weeks after his victory in the 1988 presidential election, and followed years in which petrodollar revenues had been squandered, food imports had risen to 80 percent of consumption, agricultural production had stagnated and the national debt had skyrocketed.[1] The article reads the deal as Pérez selling out a country whose oil profits he had already blown or stolen.[1]
