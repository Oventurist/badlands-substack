# Turi Defense Group

Turi Defense Group was the Scottsdale, Arizona–based arms brokerage owned by [[marc-turi|Marc Turi]], and the corporate vehicle at the center of a federal arms-export prosecution arising from attempts to ship weapons into Libya during the 2011 civil war.[1]

## Business

By 2010 the company had "hit pay dirt," supplying $14 million in arms to Afghanistan's National Directorate of Security, described in reporting as the country's CIA-supported intelligence agency.[1] Turi described the firm to a staffer for [[john-mccain|Senator John McCain]] in March 2011 as "the single largest private supplier of weapons and ammunition into Afghanistan."[1] The company operated under a State Department license, one of several thousand issued to private U.S. arms contractors authorized to move weapons around the world.[1]

## The Libya and Qatar applications

With Arab Spring insurgencies opening new arms markets, Turi Defense Group applied to the [[state-department|State Department]] for a permit to ship "$195 million worth of Eastern European light and heavy armament and ammunition" to Libya — mortars, machine guns, anti-tank rockets, combat rifles and millions of rounds of ammunition.[1] The application was denied on March 22, 2011, the same period in which [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[christopher-stevens|Chris Stevens]] met with the leader of the [[national-transitional-council|National Transitional Council]] and [[barack-obama|Obama]] authorized air strikes on Gaddafi's forces.[1]

Days later the company submitted an identical weapons list naming [[qatar|Qatar]] as the recipient, and that application was approved, with the deal repriced at $276 million "because the U.S. Dept of State denied Libya."[1] The overall transaction was valued at $534 million.[1]

## Investigation

Federal agents raided Turi's Scottsdale residence on July 22, 2011 and seized his computer; the deal collapsed.[1] The *New York Times* reported in December 2012 that the company was under investigation although it had never actually sent weapons to Libya.[1] A four-count indictment followed in February 2014 on the prosecution theory that the Qatar transaction was designed to circumvent the Libyan embargo under the [[arms-export-control-act|Arms Export Control Act]].[1] The Justice Department abandoned the case in October 2016 rather than comply with a court order to produce documents.[1]
