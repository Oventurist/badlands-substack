# Simona Halperin

Simona Halperin is an Israeli diplomat who served as Israel's Ambassador to Russia. In July 2025 Russian media reported, citing embassy representatives, that she would leave her post early because of a new appointment, with her stay expected to end in the autumn.[1]

## Early departure from Moscow

The [[israeli-ministry-of-foreign-affairs|Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs]] announced on X that Halperin had been appointed deputy director-general for Europe. The ministry did not specify whether she would be recalled from Russia immediately, but Israeli embassy staff in Moscow told TASS she would remain until at least October.[1] An embassy representative said: "The ambassador will end her mission early, due to the fact that she received the position of head of the European Department at the Israeli Foreign Ministry… It is expected that Madam Ambassador will leave in October." No replacement had been named at the time of reporting.[1]

## Friction with the Kremlin

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that shortly after her Moscow posting began, Halperin was rebuked by the Kremlin for publicly accusing Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov of downplaying the Holocaust and of being too friendly with Hamas. Russian officials summoned her and called it "an extremely unsuccessful start" to her diplomatic posting.[1]

The commentary further alleged that while serving as an envoy in Ukraine, Halperin was directly asked by Ukraine for Israel to share intelligence it held on the drones Iran was supplying to Russia for use against Ukraine — an episode characterized as a duplicitous scheme against Moscow.[1] The assessment offered was that Halperin was not respected by the Russians and that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] therefore needed a replacement envoy, with the open question being whether he would dispatch "a war hawk" or "a soothsayer."[1]

## Strategic context

The same commentary framed the vacancy against Turkey's posturing in Syria, arguing that the Russia–Israel relationship should be the top priority for Netanyahu's foreign ministry, though "perhaps that ship has already sailed."[1] Netanyahu's unprovoked attack on [[iran|Iran]] was said to have carried predictable consequences for his relationship with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], with Russia's strategic military interests now threatened along Syria's coast.[1] It was suggested that Syria's new leader would sooner turn to [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] than to Putin, given that Putin is harboring his predecessor Bashar al-Assad, while Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] appears to be another handler and shares Putin's enthusiasm for developing the Global South.[1]

## Appeal to Russia over the April 2024 Iranian strike

In April 2024, following the [[april-2024-iranian-attack-on-israel|Iranian drone and missile barrage on Israel]], Halperin, as Israeli Ambassador to [[russia|Russia]], told RIA Novosti that her country expected "our Russian colleagues to condemn the unprecedented Iranian attack on Israeli territory."[2] Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] responded on Telegram that Russia did not feel obliged to condemn the strikes, since West Jerusalem had never denounced Ukraine's attacks on Russian civilians.[2]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the exchange as a telling contradiction in the information war, noting Zakharova's observation that the Israeli government regularly issues statements in support of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky.[2]
