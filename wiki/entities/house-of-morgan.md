# House of Morgan

The House of Morgan is the banking dynasty and network of firms centered on J.P. Morgan & Co., treated by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] not as a purely American institution but as the American arm of a British financial operation.[1]

## British origins

Quoting historian John Hoefle's 2009 study in *Executive Intelligence Review*, Ehret writes that "the House of Morgan was, in truth, a British operation from its inception. It began life as George Peabody & Co., a bank founded in London in 1851 by American George Peabody. A few years later, another American, Junius S. Morgan, joined the firm, and upon Peabody's death, the firm became J.S. Morgan & Co."[1] Junius Morgan brought in his son, J. Pierpont Morgan, to head the New York office of J.S. Morgan, and that office became J.P. Morgan & Co.[1]

From its original role in helping the British gain control of American railroads, the Morgan bank "became a leading force in the oligarchy's war against the American System, using the deep pockets of its imperial masters to become a powerhouse in not only finance but steel, automobiles, railroads, electricity generation, and other industries."[1]

## Role in the 1920s and the crash

"JP Morgan's President Coolidge" and Treasury Secretary [[andrew-mellon|Andrew Mellon]] are described as having deregulated the banks and created the broker-loan scheme that inflated the 1920s bubble.[1] When the loans were called in and markets collapsed, Morgan's "preferred clients" and other financial behemoths had sold before the crash and then bought up the physical assets of America for pennies on the dollar.[1]

By 1933 the House of Morgan had grown into "a multi-headed hydra, controlling utilities, holding companies, banks and countless other subsidiaries," a structure charted publicly by Senator [[george-norris|George Norris]].[1] [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] encouraged [[ferdinand-pecora|Ferdinand Pecora]] to take on [[jp-morgan-jr|John Pierpont Morgan Jr.]] before the [[senate-committee-on-banking-and-currency|Committee on Banking and Currency]].[1]
