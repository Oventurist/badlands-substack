# Ken Martin

Ken Martin is the chair of the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]], who took over the post in early 2025 and whose first months in the job were described by *The New York Times* as a period of chaos and acute financial strain for the party.[1]

## DNC finances under Martin

Reporting published on June 18, 2025 stated that the DNC's financial situation had deteriorated to the point that top officials had discussed whether the committee might need to borrow money during the year simply to keep paying its bills.[1] Fundraising from major donors slowed sharply, and some of those donors had reportedly still not been contacted by Martin personally.[1] At the same time, Martin expanded the party's financial commitments to every state party and to far-flung territories such as Guam, a commitment amounting to roughly $1 million in monthly spending, or about $12 million annually.[1]

The reported numbers were stark: the DNC's total cash reserves shrank by $4 million between January and April 2025, leaving the committee with $18 million on hand entering May against $67.4 million for the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]].[1] Federal Election Commission reports showed that only three people had given $100,000 or more to the DNC.[1] A New York fundraising event headlined by [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] raised roughly $300,000 — a fraction of the $1 million price attached to a single top-end ticket at a comparable Harris-headlined event the previous fall.[1] Amid this, the DNC increased Martin's own salary from $300,000 to $350,000.[1]

## Internal conflicts

Fellow Democrats complained that Martin had been badly distracted by internal battles and had failed to unite the party against Republicans, who controlled the federal government.[1] A protracted and public fight with [[david-hogg|David Hogg]], the 25-year-old activist turned DNC vice chair who blindsided officials with a plan to primary incumbent Democrats, worsened matters; the clash included the leak of embarrassing audio of Martin questioning his own role and ended with Hogg's departure in June 2025.[1]

Martin also declined to renew the assignments of labor leaders [[randi-weingarten|Randi Weingarten]] and [[lee-saunders|Lee Saunders]] on the powerful Rules and Bylaws Committee, which controls how the party nominates presidential candidates, while offering to keep them as DNC members.[1] Both leaders — representing a combined 3.2 million workers — subsequently left the committee, questioning the party's direction under Martin.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the DNC's cash crunch should be read through the lens of "follow the money," suggesting that shrinking donor flows exposed how much of the party's apparent popular support had been financed by taxpayer-funded NGO channels and campaign-finance laundering, citing the [[usaid|USAID]] scandals and the [[actblue|ActBlue]] allegations.[1] She noted that removing union leaders from the nominating machinery was arguably a good move, but one that explained the scapegoating Martin was enduring.[1] Martin publicly positioned himself as conducting a study and pledging transparency to restore trust with the base and donors.[1]
