# Taylor Lorenz

Taylor Lorenz is an American technology and internet-culture reporter who has written for The Atlantic, The New York Times and The Washington Post. In Badlands Media's coverage she is presented as the emblematic figure of a collapsing mainstream press — "the most lamentable tragedy of a journalist" — whose career and public persona illustrate the pathologies that the outlet argues have destroyed legacy journalism.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Career and beat

Lorenz began her career on the social media beat, a specialization that Badlands characterizes as "tattletale journalism" — reporting whose principal output is the exposure and social punishment of private internet users rather than the scrutiny of powerful institutions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) She subsequently moved through several of the outlets described in the article as historically friendly to the security state, including [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]], [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] and [[washington-post|The Washington Post]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Controversies

The article catalogues several episodes cited by her critics. Lorenz is said to have encouraged teenage TikTok influencers to attack their peers by publicly branding them racist or misogynist, and to have attempted to elevate Kellyanne Conway's underage daughter into a left-wing celebrity in a way that instigated a family feud.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) She gained wider notoriety on the American right after publishing identifying information about the popular account [[libs-of-tiktok|Libs of TikTok]], run by [[chaya-raichik|Chaya Raichik]], and then undertaking a media tour complaining about online harassment directed at herself in the aftermath.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

Badlands contrasts her record with that of reporters such as [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[gary-webb|Gary Webb]], arguing that Lorenz has never exposed consequential corruption within the [[cia|CIA]], the [[fbi|FBI]], Wall Street or the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]], and has instead pursued cancellation campaigns over language use.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## The "State of the Media" video

In February 2024 Lorenz posted a TikTok video functioning as a "State of the Media" address, which the article quotes at length. In it she states that "the entire journalism industry is basically in a freefall," citing the same-day layoff of 115 employees at the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]], the elimination of its Washington bureau in an election year, and the destruction of its sports, tech and business desks.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) She notes that tens of thousands of journalists had been laid off in the preceding year, that [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed News]] had shuttered, that [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]] had made deep cuts and that [[sports-illustrated|Sports Illustrated]] had effectively shut down.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) She adds that local news "has been obliterated," the newspaper industry is cratering, radio is essentially dead aside from a gutted [[npr|NPR]], hundreds of [[conde-nast|Condé Nast]] workers were on strike over impending layoffs, and that even billionaire-owned outlets such as The Washington Post and The Atlantic had cut staff.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Badlands' assessment

The author, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], concedes that Lorenz is "actually speaking the truth" in her description of the industry's hollowing out — an admission he treats as rare among corporate liberal journalists.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) He rejects her closing claim that the world would be worse without journalists, arguing that Lorenz and her peers do not perform the journalistic function at all: the purpose of a press protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] is to confront, undermine, subvert and check institutions of authority.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) DeLarme ultimately frames her as a symptom rather than the disease — the product of an industry that has become "anti-journalism," focused on propagandizing the public and policing thought.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## January 2024 remarks on the media collapse

In late January 2024, Lorenz vlogged about the wave of layoffs sweeping the industry, saying that "the entire journalism industry is basically in a free-fall" and that the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]]' troubles followed "months and months of layoffs in the media industry."[2] She added that "it's not just digital media sites," noting that local news had "been obliterated," the newspaper industry was cratering, radio was "essentially dead — aside from [[npr|NPR]] which has been gutted," and that hundreds of workers at [[conde-nast|Condé Nast]] — parent of GQ, Vogue, The New Yorker and Vanity Fair — were on strike.[2] Her comments came the same week the LA Times laid off 120 employees, around 20% of its newsroom.[2]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] responded that what was being lost was not journalism but narrative warfare, citing an LA Times headline about "how white and affluent drivers are polluting the air breathed by L.A.'s people of color" and the profusion of alternative explanations — car exhaust, climate change, black tea, loneliness, napping — that the press offered for post-vaccine blood clots and heart attacks.[2] Esler characterized Lorenz as "a member of the woke mob media, an expert herself in character assassination and double speak," but argued that the deeper truth was that the evil she represents is parasitic rather than personal: she is "the result of generations of social engineering," symptomatic rather than emblematic of a fading psychological operation called journalism.[2] Invoking the parable of red and black ants destroying each other in a shaken jar, he concluded that Lorenz is "in the jar like us" and, without realizing it, is showcasing the downfall of whoever is shaking it.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a shorter verdict: the old-guard millennial Twitterati had two options, "1) Become authentic (impossible.) 2) Learn to code."[2]

## Twitter Files disclosures (2023)

In May 2023 The DisInformation Chronicle published a tranche of Twitter Files material reporting that [[twitter|Twitter]] had provided privileged access to Lorenz, describing her as a "banning queen."[3] Shortly after [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] purchased Twitter in October 2022 and reinstated several banned accounts, Lorenz — then at [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] — published an article headlined "'Opening the gates of hell': Musk says he will revive banned accounts," warning that Musk planned to reinstate nearly all previously banned accounts "to the alarm of activists and online trust and safety experts."[3]

According to the newly disclosed files, Lorenz had herself successfully banned an account only a few weeks before publishing that essay, and other files showed her behind additional bans.[3] Twitter appeared to maintain a special relationship with her, sending out an internal alert after [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] aired a short segment on [[fox-news|Fox News]] ridiculing her reporting.[3]

The month before the "gates of hell" article, Lorenz successfully pushed to ban a small-following Twitter account called @fearthefloof, which had dug into her past and detailed her upbringing as, in the account's telling, a Manhattan rich girl who attended a Swiss boarding school and whose sister Brooke Lorenz is a publicist who has worked at [[cnn|CNN]], The Washington Post and [[cbs-news|CBS News]].[3] The now-deactivated account further claimed that Lorenz is able to scrub much of her past from web searches because her uncle owns the [[internet-archive|internet archive]] that stores old webpages.[3]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used the disclosure to argue that platform manipulation is "far more insidious and pervasive than we ever imagined," pointing to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s remarks at the CNN town hall about Twitter and the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] story.[3] He contended that Twitter and other large platforms deliberately use vague hate-speech rules so that no one — internally, among users, or among regulators — can apply objective, repeatable standards, producing a documented pattern of inconsistency that amounts to "no rule of law, no fairness—mainly tyranny and weaponization of positions of power and influence."[3] Comparing the process to abuser conditioning and grooming, he argued that gradual encroachment numbs the public to the abuse, and that "the more we accept and acquiesce to tyranny, the more bold and ambitious the tyrants become."[3]

## December 2024 posts on the UnitedHealthcare killing

In the hours after the fatal shooting of [[unitedhealthcare|UnitedHealthcare]] chief executive [[brian-thompson|Brian Thompson]] in Midtown Manhattan on December 4, 2024, Lorenz — described in the coverage as a former *Washington Post* reporter — wrote several social media posts appearing to celebrate the murder while suggesting other executives should also be targeted.[4] Referencing Thompson's death in a Bluesky post attached to a report that [[blue-cross-blue-shield|Blue Cross Blue Shield]] would no longer cover anesthesia for the full length of some surgeries, she wrote: "And people wonder why we want these executives dead."[4]

Lorenz also posted an image of Blue Cross Blue Shield chief executive [[kim-keck|Kim Keck]] alongside a similar article on both Bluesky and X, and reposted another user who asked whether it would be "an actionable threat to start emailing other insurance CEOs a simple 'you're next?'"[4] She later defended the posts, arguing that "people have very justified hatred toward insurance company CEOs because these executives are responsible for an unfathomable amount of death and suffering," and that as someone against death and suffering she thought it good to call out "this broken system and the ppl in power who enable it."[4]
