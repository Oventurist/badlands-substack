# Taylor Lorenz

Taylor Lorenz is an American journalist and technology correspondent known for covering internet culture and social media. In the Badlands Media article "A Glaring Double Standard," she is mentioned as the journalist who doxxed [[chaya-raichik|Chaya Raichik]], the operator of the Libs of TikTok account.

## Role in the article

The article recounts that Raichik "was doxxed by Taylor Lorenz and then went on a media tour warning of the dangers of doxxing, saying, 'Every time I have to give my name for something, my heart skips a beat. It's unnerving.'" The source uses this detail to highlight what it calls Raichik's hypocrisy — condemning doxxing after being doxxed herself while the [[stopantisemitism|StopAntisemitism]] group, which she amplified, engaged in doxxing of Israel critics.

## The 2024 "State of the Media" address

In A Swan Song for the MSM (February 2024), Ryan DeLarme makes Lorenz the central case study of what he calls the collapse of corporate journalism. He describes her as a former [[new-york-times|New York Times]] and [[washington-post|Washington Post]] writer who "began her career on the social media beat, engaging in a sort of 'tattletale journalism,'" and alleges that she encouraged teen TikTok influencers to brand their peers as racist or misogynist and attempted to turn [[kellyanne-conway|Kellyanne Conway]]'s underage daughter into a left-wing celebrity in order to instigate a family feud. The article also recounts her doxing of the [[libs-of-tiktok|Libs of TikTok]] account run by [[chaya-raichik|Chaya Raichik]], followed by a media tour complaining of online harassment.

The article quotes extensively from a TikTok video in which Lorenz delivered a "State of the Media" address. She stated that "the entire journalism industry is basically in a freefall," noting that the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] had laid off 115 employees and eliminated its entire Washington bureau in an election year, that "tens of thousands of journalists have been laid off in the past year," that [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed News]] had shuttered, that [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]] had cut staff and that [[sports-illustrated|Sports Illustrated]] "basically shut down last week." She concluded that "pretty much the entire digital media ecosystem that myself and a lot of other millennial journalists came up with has been completely hollowed out," adding that local news had been "obliterated," that radio was "essentially dead, aside from [[npr|NPR]], which has been gutted," that [[conde-nast|Conde Nast]] workers were on strike, and that even billionaire-owned outlets such as the Washington Post and [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] had conducted layoffs.

DeLarme concedes that Lorenz "is actually speaking the truth here," but rejects her closing claim about how bad the world would be without journalists, arguing that she and her peers "are not actually journalists" because they "do not perform the journalistic function." He contrasts her record with that of [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[gary-webb|Gary Webb]], neither of whose targets in the [[cia|CIA]], the [[fbi|FBI]], Wall Street or the [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]] she has ever confronted. He ends by softening the personal charge: "Taylor Lorenz herself isn't the problem, she's just a symptom of the problem. The problem is what the industry has devolved into. It is anti-journalism."
