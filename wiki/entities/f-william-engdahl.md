# F. William Engdahl

F. William Engdahl is a historian and author cited by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as an authority on the origins of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]].[1]

## Cited account of the Club of Rome

Engdahl is quoted at length on the Club's founding: "In 1968 [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]] founded a neo-Malthusian think tank, The Club of Rome, along with [[aurelio-peccei|Aurelio Peccei]] and [[alexander-king|Alexander King]]."[1] The quoted passage adds that Peccei was a senior manager of the Fiat car company owned by the Italian Agnelli family, that Fiat's Gianni Agnelli was an intimate friend of Rockefeller and a member of the International Advisory Committee of Chase Manhattan Bank, that Agnelli and Rockefeller had been close friends since 1957, that Agnelli became a founding member of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] in 1973, and that Alexander King, head of the [[oecd|OECD]] Science Program, was also a consultant to [[nato|NATO]].[1]

Engdahl's testimony functions in Ehret's argument as the documentary bridge between the environmental movement's founding institutions and Anglo-American banking and defence networks.[1]
