# Scottish Police Federation

The Scottish Police Federation is the staff association representing rank-and-file officers of [[police-scotland|Police Scotland]].[1] It became a central voice in the April 2024 controversy over Scotland's new hate-crime legislation.[1]

## Response to the hate-crime law

Federation chairman David Threadgold said the new legislation was being exploited to pursue personal and political vendettas.[1] Speaking to the BBC, he observed: "Police Scotland have gone public and said that on every occasion, reports of hate crime will be investigated. That creates a situation where we simply cannot cope at the moment."[1]

The complaint followed a deluge of vexatious and politically driven reports under the [[hate-crime-and-public-order-scotland-act-2021|Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021]], which exposed anyone deemed verbally "abusive" — including "insulting" — toward a transgender person, in person or online, to a prison sentence of up to seven years.[1] Reports came both from conservatives making a mockery of the system and from left-wing activists seeking to punish ideological adversaries.[1]

## Commentary

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that Threadgold's framing — objecting to people who "look to weaponize this legislation… for… political scoring" — revealed "the thin membrane of awareness" separating those who understand the nature of the conflict from those who see only ordinary politics.[1] In Esler's reading, the statute is not being weaponized; "the legislation is the weapon," and much of the resulting [[lawfare|lawfare]] now runs on autopilot through the momentum of ideological subversion.[1]
