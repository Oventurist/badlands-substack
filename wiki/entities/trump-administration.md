# Trump Administration

The **Trump Administration** is the executive branch of the United States government under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. In Badlands coverage from spring 2026 it is treated as a principal actor in the dispute with [[us-congress|Congress]] over the [[iran|Iran]] War.[1]

## Dispute with Congress over the Iran War

The fifteenth episode of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]], published 2 May 2026, opened with the then-ongoing debate between the Trump Administration and Congress regarding the Iran War.[1] Hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used that debate as a vehicle for attempting to define when a war is and is not happening at the same time — a question raised by the administration's position that hostilities did not constitute a war in the sense requiring congressional authorisation.[1]

## Role in the dissolution narrative

The episode situated the administration's Middle East posture within a wider account of the [[global-cartel|Global Cartel]] being dismantled in plain sight on kinetic, financial and energy grounds, with the Middle East and South America as the two principal theatres.[1] The hosts characterised these developments as the visible expression of a coherent plan that "seems to be coming together quite splendidly."[1]

## Iran policy in 2026 commentary

The nineteenth episode of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]], published 30 May 2026, described an "ongoing flirtation" between the Trump Administration and what its hosts called the "Real, but also Fake" Iranian regime, treating the administration's posture toward [[iran|Iran]] as the week's central geopolitical thread.[2] The hosts, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], connected that posture to the belated recognition of the underlying strategy by parts of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] and to the broader question of sustaining a stable [[multipolar-world|multipolar world]].[2]

## Rescission of the endangerment finding (2026)

The administration moved to rescind the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]]'s 2009 [[endangerment-finding|endangerment finding]], the determination that greenhouse gases endanger public health and welfare that had served as the legal foundation for regulating emissions under the [[clean-air-act|Clean Air Act]].[3] The final rule was to be issued following a White House ceremony with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and EPA Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]], after a proposed rescission announced the previous July and an Inauguration Day executive order directing a review of the finding's legality.[3] Officials described the move as the largest act of deregulation in U.S. history, projecting over $1 trillion in regulatory cost savings, chiefly through reduced vehicle compliance expenses averaging more than $2,400 per light-duty vehicle, and framed it as essential to energy affordability, fossil fuel production, economic growth, and national security amid broader efforts to support coal plants and re-industrialization.[3]

Environmental groups including [[earthjustice|Earthjustice]] and the [[environmental-defense-fund|Environmental Defense Fund]] condemned the action and pledged immediate court challenges, Democratic-led states signaled potential independent emissions standards, and legal experts anticipated prolonged litigation.[3]

## Election integrity and foreign policy posture

The administration's [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] filed court documents in February 2026 detailing an FBI investigation into [[fulton-county|Fulton County]]'s handling of 2020 ballots, an inquiry initiated by a referral from Trump-appointed election security director [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]].[3] Abroad, the administration opposed Israeli [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank annexation]] measures, pursued Oman-mediated nuclear talks with [[iran|Iran]] while announcing new sanctions, and strained relations with European allies over Greenland, tariffs, and the [[gordie-howe-international-bridge|Gordie Howe International Bridge]].[3]

## March 2026: war, deportations, and AI contracting

In early March 2026 the administration was simultaneously prosecuting the [[iran-war|U.S.–Israeli war on Iran]], defending its mass deportation program before Congress, and managing fallout from a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] artificial intelligence contract with [[openai|OpenAI]].[4] Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] publicly justified the Iran campaign as limited and preemptive, while officials insisted it would not become an endless war or pursue regime change.[4]

Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] absorbed bipartisan criticism at a [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing over [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] and over departmental contracting, while border enforcement under [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] drew comparative praise from the same senators.[4] On the economic front, [[donald-trump|Trump]] directed the [[development-finance-corporation|Development Finance Corporation]] to guarantee Persian Gulf shipping, easing oil markets.[4]

## Iraq-Syria pipeline support (July 2026)

US officials said in July 2026 that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] is supporting efforts to revive a long-idled crude oil pipeline connecting [[iraq|Iraq]] to [[syria|Syria]]'s Mediterranean coast, giving Iraq an alternative export route and reducing its dependence on the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Persian Gulf.[5] Officials described the proposal as part of a broader strategy to strengthen regional energy security and limit [[iran|Iran]]'s leverage over global oil shipments, though significant infrastructure repairs, security challenges, and political agreements between Iraq and Syria would still be required.[5] Iran-backed Iraqi militias opposed the plan, with one prominent group threatening to disrupt any future pipeline project.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the announcement came less than a week after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] revealed his intention to remove Syria from the state sponsors of terrorism list, opening the country to international investors, and predicted Saudi involvement.[5] See [[iraq-syria-crude-oil-pipeline|the pipeline]] for fuller coverage.[5]

## May 2026 pressures: elections, Europe, and Israeli nuclear disclosure

County officials characterized the April 2026 Fulton County grand jury subpoena as part of broader administration actions to obtain 2020 election records from swing states, including a January 2026 FBI seizure of ballots from a Fulton County warehouse.[6] The administration's decision to draw down U.S. forces in Germany caught European leaders off guard and accelerated debate over European defense burden-sharing.[6] Thirty House Democrats led by Rep. Joaquin Castro wrote to Secretary of State Marco Rubio demanding the administration disclose information about Israel's nuclear arsenal and related policies.[6]
