# Stephanie Lambert

**Stephanie Lambert** is an attorney and activist known for her work on election-integrity litigation.[1] She is described as a well-known election attorney who works with [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]].[1]

## Role in the Tina Peters clemency effort

In December 2025, attorney [[peter-ticktin|Peter Ticktin]] published a letter to President Donald Trump urging a pardon for former Mesa County clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1] Appearing on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s program to discuss the letter, Ticktin stated at the outset of the interview that the letter was the legal work of Lambert.[1]

The letter advanced a novel constitutional theory: that because the framers referred to the "united States" using plural pronouns — "they," "them," "their," as in Article III's treason clause — the presidential pardon power extends to all offenses, including state crimes, subject only to the impeachment exception.[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America summarized the argument as "America Uses 'They/Them' Pronouns So Trump Can Pardon Tina Peters," and criticized the framing as effectively giving Trump a deadline to act outside his authority, setting up a narrative in which any failure to free Peters by Christmas would be blamed on the President or his appointees.[1]

## The Dominion discovery leak and arrest (2024)

In March 2024, Lambert — also rendered in coverage as Stefanie Lambert — was at the center of a rupture in the defamation suit brought by [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] against former Overstock.com chief executive [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]].[2] According to Dominion, Byrne handed a trove of confidential discovery material to Lambert, who then leaked it to law enforcement and others; Dominion effectively confirmed the material's authenticity by notifying the presiding judge of the leak in a filing that Friday.[2] Lambert was arrested over the matter and filed a response defending her actions the following Monday.[2]

Lambert stated that Byrne "came into possession of email communications produced by Dominion during discovery (some written in Serbian and foreign languages) with and from top level Dominion employees directing and tasking foreign nationals to remotely access voting machines utilized in the United States during the November 3, 2020 election."[2] She added that the remote access occurred "while the states were still counting votes, determining a final tally, and prior to certification of the results," and that the emails further established that background checks of the Serbian Dominion employees did not take place, leaving the United States without knowledge or oversight of those individuals, including whether they had prior Serbian military experience.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who has hosted Lambert several times on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]], described her as a fearless election-integrity advocate who has helped many America First litigants, and noted that she had been arrested for giving evidence of a crime to law enforcement — a crime the public had been told was a defamatory conspiracy theory.[2] Ashe also emphasized that the episode unfolded in Michigan, where the state executive branch was reported to have covered up evidence of election fraud while continuing to prosecute a "fake electors" case.[2]
