# Jennifer Homendy

Jennifer Homendy is the chair of the [[national-transportation-safety-board|National Transportation Safety Board]] (NTSB), the U.S. agency responsible for investigating major transportation accidents.[1]

## Alaska Airlines 737 MAX 9 investigation

Homendy said on Sunday, January 7, 2024 that no data was available from the cockpit voice recorder of the [[alaska-airlines|Alaska Airlines]] [[boeing|Boeing]] 737 MAX 9 that lost a fuselage panel in flight on the preceding Friday, because the recorder had not been retrieved within the two-hour window after which recording restarts and erases the previous audio.[1] Her statement renewed long-running industry debate over recording duration: the United States requires cockpit voice recorders to log two hours of data, against 25 hours in Europe for aircraft manufactured after 2021.[1]

The industry has wrestled with the length of cockpit recordings since the 2014 disappearance of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH370, a Boeing 777 that has never been found and whose loss sharply increased efforts to monitor the longest possible modern flights and, where necessary, to recapture earlier legs.[1]

## Baltimore bridge collapse statements

As [[national-transportation-safety-board|NTSB]] chair, Homendy announced on 27 March 2024 that the container ship [[dali|Dali]], which caused the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse]], carried 56 containers of hazardous materials including corrosives, flammables and lithium ion batteries.[2] She confirmed that some containers had been breached, that a sheen was identified in the water for authorities to address, and that the voyage data recorder had been recovered.[2] Homendy estimated the investigation would take 12 to 24 months with a preliminary report in two to four weeks, said the board would not hesitate to issue urgent safety recommendations before then, and called the inquiry "a massive undertaking" arising from "a very tragic event."[2]
