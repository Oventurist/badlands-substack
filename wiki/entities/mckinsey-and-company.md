# McKinsey & Company

McKinsey & Company is the global management consulting firm cast in Badlands Media's labor coverage as the principal architect of the [[future-of-work|future of work]] — the engineered transition in which automation, robotics, and artificial intelligence displace human labor at scale.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Reach and influence

McKinsey, along with other large and medium professional services firms, delivers "solutions" to "problems" in every sector of every industry in every geography in the world: automotive and manufacturing, media and entertainment, healthcare, retail, forestry, aerospace, and agriculture.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Its client list includes the top players in every industry, who hire the firm to solve every conceivable problem.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The article further emphasizes that McKinsey is the strategic partner of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], describing it as essentially the world's strategy firm whose influence touches every aspect of human life.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## The future-of-work forecasts

A July 2020 McKinsey article, "The future of work: reskilling and remote working to recover in the next normal," is quoted: "Even before the current crisis, AI, automation, and other technologies were already creating major shifts in required workforce skills. In 2017, our scenarios suggested that by 2030, up to 375 million workers (14 percent of the global workforce) would have to switch occupational categories."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The piece notes that the firm published further future-of-work analysis in February 2021, eight months after that figure was given.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

The author's inference is that McKinsey was measuring these shifts in 2017 because it was studying them, and was studying them because it knew its own actions would bring about the consequences — the world producing these impacts on the global talent landscape being the world the firm is designing.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) On this reading, the correct "time to strike" would have been long before the infrastructure of that future was laid.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Generative AI research

In June 2023 McKinsey published research on generative [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] stating that "all of us are at the beginning of a journey to understand generative AI's power, reach, and capabilities," and that the technology is poised to transform roles and boost performance across functions such as sales and marketing, customer operations, and software development, potentially unlocking trillions of dollars in value across sectors from banking to life sciences.[2]

Badlands Media's July 2023 essay on AI cites this statement, together with those of the [[united-nations|United Nations]], the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], and [[pwc|PwC]], as evidence that institutional actors ignored the [[ai-pause-open-letter|call for a six-month pause]] and continued full steam ahead on AI development.[2]

## Transformation research and the DOGE framing

In April 2025 Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used McKinsey's own transformation research to argue that [[doge|DOGE]] was functioning as an eighteen-month transformational change program to optimize America and bring about the Golden Age.[3] Research cited in the piece holds that roughly 70 percent of business transformations fail to achieve their objectives, and McKinsey — described again as "the world's strategy firm" — attributes those failures to a small set of common-sense causes set out in its interview with Jon Garcia on common pitfalls in transformations.[3]

Four McKinsey findings were mapped onto the administration's conduct. The first, "a failure to set fact-based, high aspirations… Executives arrive at a number based on consensus rather than on data," was contrasted with Trump's aspirations being "so high they're on their way to Mars" and his indifference to consensus.[3] The second, that "successful at-scale transformations require that thousands—or tens of thousands—of employees choose to get on board with this new way of working," was answered with the claim that MAGA is itself a change network.[3] The third, that managers focus on activities rather than outcomes and "act too slowly when it comes to making difficult people decisions," was applied satirically to congressional distractions and side quests.[3] The fourth, that organizations "often fail to sustain the impact that they've achieved" because "incentives and budgets are not fully aligned with new objectives," was matched to the market repricing under the [[liberation-day-tariffs|Liberation Day tariffs]] as evidence that incentives and budgets were being realigned rapidly and despite resistance.[3]

The same brief treated McKinsey's published position papers, alongside the "thought leadership" of the World Economic Forum, as open broadcasts of what global corporations intend to do — material that only a handful of people notice, allowing consent to be presumed.[3]

## "A new operating model for a new world"

McKinsey's report "A new operating model for a new world" was the subject of an August 2025 discussion on the Badlands program [[culture-of-change|Culture of Change]] between [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[blue-eyes|Blue Eyes]], who examined the firm's success factors through the lens of the American people as shareholders and the Republic as the organization.[4] The commentary argued that the model McKinsey describes implies a future of work with far fewer jobs, since equal or greater output from fewer workers lowers the cost of doing business and raises profits — a trajectory the commentators contrasted with the [[2025-boeing-defense-workers-strike|Boeing machinists' strike]] over future contract terms.[4]

## Contract under Ursula von der Leyen's German defense ministry

Badlands contributor Ryan DeLarme reported that [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]], as German Minister of Defense from 2013 to 2019, awarded a large contract to McKinsey consultants during a tenure cited for "nepotism" and "incompetence."[5] He described the firm as a monolithic global management consultancy whose hands have been in many deep events, listing Enron, COVID, and the opioid epidemic.[5]
