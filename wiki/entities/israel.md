# Israel

**Israel** is a country in the Middle East that recurs throughout the Badlands Media corpus as both a geopolitical actor and a focal point of contested narratives. Among the articles processed so far, Israel appears in three main contexts: as the object of the [[israel-palestine-conflict]] and its media coverage; as a subject of campus speech controversies and censorship debates in the United States following the October 7, 2023 attack; and within conspiracy narratives that position Israel's leadership as a component of a larger [[deep-state]] structure. The corpus's treatment of Israel is uniformly opinionated, with each author framing the country through their own analytic lens.

## Israel in the Israel–Palestine Conflict

The [[israel-palestine-conflict]] is presented in the corpus as a defining test of media credibility and public polarization. In *A Hypnotist's Take on Israel, Palestine, and Q*, [[american-hypnotist]] argues that the conflict has become a "wrecking ball" for tribal loyalties on both the right and the left, destabilizing what he describes as the "mockingbird" media programming that has shaped public opinion. He frames the conflict as potentially serving as the "final straw" that breaks public trust in mainstream media, comparing the process to an avalanche that begins with small shifts before collapsing the entire structure. He connects the conflict to the corpus's broader information-war narrative, describing coverage of Israel's military actions as an exercise in "hypnotic propaganda" by what he terms the [[cabal]], and citing a [[clif-high]] observation that the public is being forced to "choose a side" and have its "emotions torqued" by media framing.

The author develops this argument through his "nominalization" framework: buzzwords without tangible definitions — of which he considers "antisemitism" a prime example — serve as hypnotic triggers that keep opposing groups fighting over words they cannot define. He applies this lens to the Israel-Palestine coverage, arguing that the conflict's quasi-religious nature, nonstop air time, and ancient history make it uniquely effective at activating mass emotion. He predicts the conflict will expose the machinery of media manipulation, prompting people to look "under the hood" at how wars are framed.

## Campus Speech Controversies and Censorship Debates

*A Glaring Double Standard* by [[ryan-delarme]] focuses on how the aftermath of the October 7 attack reshaped American campus politics and free-speech debates. The article argues that criticism of the government of Israel was reclassified as "antisemitism" under pressure from advocacy groups, leading to a wave of reported "antisemitic incidents" that the author contends were largely expressions of dissent. The [[anti-defamation-league]] reported roughly 1,200 such incidents on U.S. college campuses between October 7, 2023 and September 24, 2024, with the vast majority categorized as "harassment" and "hate speech." DeLarme asserts that these reports mostly captured "instances of a difference of opinion" — for example, expressions of sympathy for civilians in Gaza — rather than genuine threats.

The article documents several individuals who lost their jobs over statements about Israel's military actions in Gaza, including [[maura-finkelstein]], a tenured professor at Muhlenberg College; [[hesen-jabr]], a nurse at NYU Langone Health; and Nikki Noor Aytoglu, a dental hygienist. DeLarme attributes these terminations largely to pressure from [[stopantisemitism]], an advocacy group that pushed employers to fire the individuals and publicized them via its X account, amplified by [[chaya-raichik]].

DeLarme also describes political responses, including Florida governor [[ron-desantis]]'s directive to shut down the University of South Florida chapter of [[national-students-for-justice-in-palestine]], and later federal measures such as [[executive-order-14188]] and the [[antisemitism-awareness-act]], which he argues conflate legitimate criticism of Israel with antisemitism. He contends that prominent conservative figures including [[ben-shapiro]], [[bari-weiss]], and [[dave-rubin]] abandoned free-speech principles they had previously championed, instead supporting the silencing of Israel's critics. [[dave-portnoy]] and his company [[barstool-sports]] are likewise criticized for drawing a line at humor about Israel. DeLarme contrasts these figures' pre-October 7 defenses of controversial speakers — citing [[christopher-rufo]]'s defense of a fired teacher and [[gina-carano]]'s firing by Disney — with their silence over the firings of Israel critics, arguing their commitment to free speech was conditional.

## Israel in Conspiracy Narratives

*A Journey of Ever-changing Truths* by [[erik-carlson]] places Israel within a broader [[cabal]]-centric cosmology. Carlson describes initially accepting the idea that "Israel ran the world" to explain the country's perceived overrepresentation in conflicts and American foreign policy, but concludes that Israel and Jews serve as a scapegoat for a higher tier of control. He asserts that the [[city-of-london]] sits atop what he describes as the organized-crime structure of the world, and that it "controls the US, they control Israel, they control Iran and China as well." In this telling, Israel's leadership and intelligence agencies, including [[mossad]], are cast as "middle management" taking orders from the City of London, above which sits the [[deep-state]] and the [[cia]].

This narrative thread treats Israel's conflicts — including the war in Ukraine and the Israel–Palestine conflict — as distractions from Carlson's claimed real struggle between [[donald-trump]] and the City of London. He urges readers to regard Israel, Iran, and China as "the face you know" rather than "the one making the decisions," likening their leaders to a middle manager in the film *Office Space*.

## Israel in Multipolar Framings

A minor but distinct treatment appears in *A Gateway to a New World of Cooperation*, which lists "the just settlement of Palestinian issue" among the conditions for peace and stability in the Middle East within its broader argument for an alternative security architecture emerging among Russia, China, and the Global South. The reference is passing, but it reflects the corpus's tendency to fold the Israel-Palestine question into critiques of American-led foreign policy.

## As Portrayed by the Corpus

Across the ingested articles, Israel functions as a charged reference point through which the corpus explores media manipulation, free speech, and elite power structures. The treatments range from geopolitical commentary on the Israel–Palestine conflict to criticism of censorship surrounding Israel's military actions, to speculative narratives about Israel's place in a [[cabal]] hierarchy. The sources are opinion and analysis pieces from the Badlands Media network rather than neutral reporting, and the claims about Israel's role in global power structures are presented by the authors as theories rather than established fact.
