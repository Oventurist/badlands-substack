# Israel

Israel occupies a pivotal place in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s April 2025 essay as the case study for his argument that visible power is not real power.[1]

## The initial conclusion

Carlson recounts noticing the influence of the mafia he had been studying and observing that the heads of most movie studios are Jewish, that the CEOs of media companies and of large American corporations across sectors are overrepresented by Jews, and that Jews are overrepresented in Washington.[1] He asks how a group representing only 2% of the American population can be so overrepresented in positions of power and influence.[1] For a few months, he writes, he was content to settle on the idea that Jews and Israel ran the world, which also seemed to explain why Israel is in the middle of every conflict and why the United States sides with and protects it at all times.[1]

## The revised conclusion

Carlson then asks whether the Jews and Israel are merely a scapegoat, drawing the analogy to the drug dealers of South Central Los Angeles and to the [[italian-mafia|Italian Mafia]] propped up in front of the [[jewish-mafia|Jewish Mafia]].[1] He does not claim that none are willing participants, only that a group sits above them, and that global focus on Israel allows the people who actually control Israel to operate in the shadows.[1] In his final framework the [[city-of-london|City of London]] controls the United States, Israel, Iran, and China, with [[mossad|Mossad]] subordinate to [[mi6|MI6]].[1] He likens Israel, Iran, and China to the middle manager in Office Space — a recognizable face who is not making the decisions.[1]

## American public opinion (2026)

By July 2026 a growing number of major media outlets argued that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had inflicted lasting damage on Israel's standing with the American public, citing years of partisan alignment, the ongoing Gaza war, and repeated clashes with US administrations as having eroded once-broad bipartisan support.[2] [[pew-research-center|Pew Research Center]] found unfavorable views of Israel and declining confidence in Netanyahu had risen since 2022, with opinion worsening across demographic groups and among younger voters in both major parties.[2] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly criticized Israeli military operations during a meeting with Netanyahu while praising [[turkey|Turkey]] as a "tremendous ally," underscoring visible policy differences even as the US-Israel alliance remained intact.[2]

## Badlands framing

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized Israel as "America's greatest foreign adversary" and as an empire that in its own eyes "has only begun to tap into its true potential," arguing that when that potential is fully realized "Israel will rule the world."[2] He compared the Zionist State of Israel to the Sanhedrin of the ancient world, asserting that it "does not seek the glory of God, but only the glory for itself," and suggested that Trump's stated war against Israel "using Iran as his proxy" may mean reputational rather than violent conflict.[2]
