# Business Insider

Business Insider is an American financial and general-news outlet whose economic coverage has been excerpted repeatedly in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## August 2024 labor-market coverage

In August 2024 Business Insider published an analysis declaring that "America's hiring boom is officially over," arguing that public perception of a healthy labor market changed abruptly at 8:30 a.m. ET on the Friday the July jobs report was released.[1] The article reported that the US economy added 114,000 jobs in July against an expected 176,000, and that the unemployment rate rose to 4.3 percent from 4.1 percent the prior month, after having sat at 3.9 percent in April and below 4 percent for the longest stretch in decades.[1] It described the release as tipping "the worry scale" and sending markets into a selloff.[1]

The piece cautioned that a single data point should not overturn an entire narrative, but framed the report as a wake-up call that the cracks in the economic foundation were becoming impossible to ignore, noting that the labor market had been weakening for some time with no clear catalyst for reversal.[1] It observed that the report landed days after the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] held interest rates steady rather than cutting, fueling concern that the central bank was behind the curve and that recession might be approaching.[1] Guy Berger, director of economic research at the Burning Glass Institute, told the outlet that data previously "in the greenish area" was now "flashing yellow lights."[1] These claims are treated in more detail at [[july-2024-us-jobs-report|the July 2024 US jobs report]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the article's premise that observers had believed the economy was strong before the report, noting that the same piece recounted months of deteriorating metrics.[1] She argued that the United States had in fact been in recession since late 2021 and that official metrics had been manipulated and definitions changed, urging readers to treat resilience and family provision as individual responsibilities because "the economy is a house of cards, and the wind is blowing."[1]

## January 2024 layoffs

In late January 2024, Business Insider announced it was cutting 8% of its staff, according to reporting by Semafor.[2] The reduction came in the same week as 120 layoffs at the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]], 30 at [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]], and asset sales at [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed]] and [[vice-media|Vice Media]], a cluster of events that [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]] described as the entire journalism industry being "in a free-fall."[2]
