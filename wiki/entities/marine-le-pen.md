# Marine Le Pen

Marine Le Pen is the French politician associated with the National Rally (Rassemblement National, RN), who in February 2024 became the subject of the first opinion poll ever to show her winning the French presidency in a second round of voting.[1]

## The 2024 IFOP poll

In an exclusive poll conducted by IFOP, Le Pen was predicted to defeat Gabriel Attal in the second round of the 2027 presidential election by 51 percent to 49 percent — the first time any poll had shown her securing victory and becoming president.[1] The same poll found she would tie Édouard Philippe at 50 percent apiece.[1] Attal serves as prime minister in the [[emmanuel-macron|Macron]] government, and the poll drew front-page coverage in France.[1]

The result had been foreshadowed by political scientist Jérôme Fourquet, who told Le JDD in July 2022 that "we can't rule out a Marine Le Pen victory in 2027," citing figures published by his own institute, Ifop.[1]

## Conviction and electoral ban (2025)

In late March 2025 a Paris court found Le Pen — described as the leader of France's populist [[national-rally|National Rally]] party and the frontrunner for the 2027 presidential election — guilty of embezzling European Union funds.[2] The court sentenced her to four years in prison, with two years suspended and two under house arrest, along with a fine of €100,000, and barred her from participating in any elections for five years, rendering her unable to compete in the 2027 presidential race.[2]

Le Pen declared on April 2, 2025 that she would challenge the verdict.[2] While her appeal delays the imposition of the prison sentence and fine, her ineligibility to run for public office remains intact pending review.[2] The Paris Court of Appeal was set to evaluate her case with a decision anticipated by mid-2026, and French legal commentator Matthieu Almeras characterised the appeal as "a double-edged sword for Le Pen, who could receive a heavier or lighter sentence."[2] Should the initial judgment stand, further judicial avenues remain open, including the Court of Cassation — which reviews procedural adherence rather than the facts of a case — and potentially the European Court of Human Rights, although the exhaustive nature of those processes would push any resolution beyond the 2027 electoral timeline.[2] The National Pulse characterised the prosecution as a "heinous [[lawfare|lawfare]] case."[2]

## Fake jobs case and international reaction

The March 31, 2025 verdict came after Le Pen and 24 other officials of the [[national-rally|National Rally]] were accused in November of misusing European Parliament funds between 2004 and 2016, with prosecutors alleging that money intended to pay parliamentary aides was diverted to party expenses unrelated to European Union work in what was described as a "fake jobs" scam.[4] The EU estimated the allegedly embezzled sums at around €7 million; Le Pen and her co-defendants consistently denied wrongdoing across the nine-week trial, with Le Pen saying in November, "It's my political death they are after."[4] The ruling barred her from standing in French elections for five years even as she was gaining traction in polls ahead of the 2027 presidential election, and she was reported to be appealing.[4]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the prosecution reminded him of his own legal battles under the Biden administration, telling reporters in the Oval Office that the verdict was "a very big deal."[4] "I know all about it, and a lot of people thought she wasn't going to be convicted of anything," Trump said. "But she was banned [from] running for five years, and she's the leading candidate. That sounds very much like this country."[4] Trump has often described the court cases and investigations into his own activities as a politically motivated "witch hunt."[4]

Badlands commentary identified a disturbing European trend of either cancelling elections, as in Ukraine and Romania, or prosecuting populist candidates, as in France and Romania, arguing the same tactics had been deployed in America and that those trampling democracy were globalists acting in democracy's name.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the French and EU governments plainly sought Le Pen's "political death" because she had ignited the idea of France First, noting she had been under investigation since at least 2016.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that France and Romania had arrested their "opposition leaders," Poland was weighing arresting its own, and Germany was considering banning its opposition party — concluding that when they call you a threat to "their Democracy," they are finally telling the truth.[4] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the banning of Le Pen "another damning proof in the story of how Europe was lost."[4]

## Opposition to the EU Migration Pact

After the [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] approved the [[eu-asylum-and-migration-pact|EU Asylum and Migration Pact]] in April 2024, Le Pen of France's [[national-rally|National Rally]] wrote that "The Migration Pact organizes the tutelage and control of nations, the legal impunity of NGOs complicit with smugglers."[3] She vowed to "put an end to the accelerated pursuit of policies to encourage and organize mass immigration" at the June 9 European elections, in which her party was expected to win the most French seats.[3]
