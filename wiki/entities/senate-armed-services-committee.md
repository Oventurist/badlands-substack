# Senate Armed Services Committee

The Committee on Armed Services of the United States Senate is the standing committee with jurisdiction over the Department of Defense, the military services, defense policy and military nominations. Its senior members receive extensive classified briefings on U.S. military operations and on the identity and affiliations of armed groups the United States encounters abroad.

## Role in the Badlands corpus

In A Conflict of Interest, the committee is invoked chiefly to establish the knowledge attributable to [[john-mccain|John McCain]]. The article describes McCain as "the most senior Republican on the Senate armed services committee" at the time of his April 2011 visit to [[benghazi|Benghazi]], quoting The Guardian's report that he was the most senior U.S. official to visit the rebel-held eastern city.

The article turns this seniority into an argument: as the ranking member of the committee overseeing the armed forces, McCain would have had access to the U.S. military's own assessments — including the [[combating-terrorism-center|Combating Terrorism Center]] study on foreign fighters entering Iraq — showing that the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] had joined [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] in 2007 and was supplying fighters who killed American soldiers. The author asks how a senator in that position "could possibly not know" the affiliation of the men he publicly called "brave fighters," and concludes that McCain's statement that "they are not Al-Qaeda" was a knowing falsehood.

The committee thus functions in the article less as an actor than as a measure of culpability, establishing that the information contradicting McCain's public advocacy was institutionally available to him.
