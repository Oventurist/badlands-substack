# Clinton Foundation

The Clinton Foundation is the charitable vehicle of [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and his family that, in the article's account, served as the collection point for money generated by the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]] and by associates of [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Uranium-linked donations

The New York Times is quoted reporting that at the heart of the Uranium One story are several leaders of the Canadian mining industry who were major donors to the charitable endeavors of Bill Clinton and his family, and who built, financed and eventually sold the company to the Russians.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) As the Russians assumed control in three transactions between 2009 and 2013, Canadian records show a flow of cash to the foundation: Uranium One's chairman used his family foundation to make four donations totaling $2.35 million, none of which were publicly disclosed by the Clintons despite [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s agreement with the Obama White House to identify all donors.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Shortly after the Russians announced their intention to acquire a majority stake, Bill Clinton received $500,000 for a Moscow speech from a Kremlin-linked Russian investment bank promoting Uranium One stock.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

To avoid the perception of conflicts of interest, and beyond a ban on foreign government donations, the foundation was required to publicly disclose all contributors.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) By those disclosures the only [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] official to give was chairman [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]], at no more than $250,000 in 2007.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Canadian tax records for Telfer's Fernwood Foundation, however, show millions more — $1 million in 2009, $250,000 in 2010, $600,000 in 2011 and $500,000 in 2012 — given during and after the period when the foreign investment committee was reviewing his deal with the Russians.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) These came in addition to between $1.3 million and $5.6 million in reported contributions from people tied to Uranium One or [[urasia-energy|UrAsia]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article calls the concealment tax evasion, the same crime for which Rich was charged.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

[[doug-band|Doug Band]] was working for the foundation and the [[clinton-global-initiative|Clinton Global Initiative]] when he accompanied Clinton to [[north-korea|North Korea]] in 2009, which the article calls a key data point.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Its overall verdict is that money is at the heart of the story and is what the Clintons love most, because it gives them power.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Teneo, Huma Abedin and the State Department

Part 2 of the America for Sale series casts the foundation as the collection vessel of a [[pay-for-play|pay-for-play]] system running through the [[state-department|State Department]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] worked for the foundation while simultaneously serving as [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s closest aide at the department and drawing a $15,000-a-month consulting fee from [[teneo-holdings|Teneo]], her "[[special-government-employee|special government employee]]" status permitting the arrangement; at the foundation she assessed ongoing programs to prepare for Clinton's return after she left State in early 2013.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Breitbart is quoted reporting that a few months after Clinton took office in 2009, Abedin began communicating with foundation donors who were asking favours from the State Department.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The author asks how many Teneo clients donated to the foundation, how many people Clinton met as Secretary of State subsequently gave large sums, and how much Teneo was compensated for brokering those donations, concluding that "they were selling out America."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

[[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]] is described as one of the foundation's biggest donors while Clinton was Secretary of State, with Abedin acting as his liaison to the Clintons as part of his claimed sixteen-year plan to destroy America.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) [[declan-kelly|Declan Kelly]]'s unpaid post as Special Envoy to Ireland is likewise said to have produced lucrative deals with American corporations that funnelled money into the foundation.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## FIFA, Qatar and UBS donations

Citing Nonprofit Quarterly, the article reports that [[fifa|FIFA]] gave the foundation between $50,000 and $100,000 as a membership fee to participate in the [[clinton-global-initiative|Clinton Global Initiative]], plus a commitment to build 20 community centers in South Africa tied to the 2010 World Cup, while the Qatar 2022 Supreme Committee gave between $250,000 and $500,000 as a CGI sponsor in 2013 and the government of [[qatar|Qatar]] has given between $1 million and $5 million to the foundation.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Qatar won the World Cup bid, the foundation made a great deal of money, and FIFA's leadership was later arrested for corruption — with, the article says, no scrutiny of Clinton involvement.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

The Swiss bank [[ubs|UBS]], under IRS and Justice Department pressure over helping wealthy Americans evade taxes, expanded its foundation donations from less than $60,000 through 2008 to a cumulative $600,000 by the end of 2014, including a $350,000 donation in June 2011 and $100,000 for a charity golf tournament, and teamed with the foundation on the Clinton Economic Opportunity Initiative through a $32 million entrepreneur loan program.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) UBS also paid Bill Clinton more than $1.5 million in speaking fees between 2001 and 2014.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The article's summary is that the foundation sat at the centre of a "PAY-FOR-PLAY SPIDER WEB," and that the treason evidence gathered by [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]] and [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] and passed to [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] was connected to it.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) [[john-huber|John Huber]]'s first investigation after his secret appointment by [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] in November 2017 focused on the foundation.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## The largest contributor and the debt owed

"Bad Actors" poses the question of who was the biggest contributor to the Clinton Foundation, answering that among many donors over the years one was the largest and expected the most in return.[3] Joe Lange argues that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] owed a great deal of money to "bad actors" who had donated to the foundation on the assumption she would become the next president, and that she had made promises to those writing large cheques to what he calls her private piggy bank.[3] One donor in particular was not merely disappointed by her defeat but demanded his money back, on the principle that puppet masters do not lose their money because they control governments and treat them as personal piggy banks.[3]

That donor is identified as [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]], who is said to have expected a Clinton victory so that he could control the presidency "ONCE AGAIN," having controlled both Clinton and [[barack-obama|Obama]] as the architect of the [[sixteen-year-plan|sixteen year plan]].[3] Q drop 199 of November 22, 2017 is cited as connecting Obama's Harvard education with contributions to the foundation because both were obligations to the same person.[3] In Lange's account the debt was settled when Obama, in his final hours in office, directed $221 million in taxpayer funds to the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] through [[usaid|USAID]].[3]

## IRS criminal probe abandoned in 2019

Years after the [[fbi|FBI]] was forced to shut down multiple corruption probes of Bill and Hillary Clinton's charity, the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] opened a criminal tax investigation into the Clinton Foundation and its dealings with other players on the global charity stage, but abruptly stopped working with whistleblowers in the spring of 2019, according to IRS memos and internal emails reviewed by Just the News.[5] "Can't talk about the CF," one memo states, recounting how IRS agents suddenly cut off contact with two whistleblowers they had worked with for weeks; one of them was a decorated former federal money laundering analyst who had testified before [[congress|Congress]] on issues such as terrorism financing.[5] The documents, released under the Freedom of Information Act, added new evidence of federal concern about the former first family's charity and to what Just the News called a persistent pattern of federal agents being thwarted in investigations tied to major Democratic Party figures.[5]

On the Devolution Power Hour, [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] examined the newly revealed evidence of [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] corruption, arguing that investigations into the Clinton Foundation were deliberately shut down to protect political elites.[5]

## Durham Report disclosures (May 2023)

The [[durham-report|Durham Report]] released in May 2023 disclosed that the [[fbi|FBI]] had opened three concurrent investigations into the Clinton Foundation, all of which were shut down by senior bureau leadership during the 2016 election year.[6] Coverage of the disclosure recalled that the foundation had taken in millions of dollars in foreign donations while [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was widely expected to win the 2016 U.S. election, only for donations to plummet by roughly 90 percent after her defeat.[6] Attorney and commentator [[techno-fog|Techno Fog]] wrote that Durham's mandate encompassed FBI investigations "directed" at the Clinton campaign, apparently so that the favorable treatment Clinton received could be compared with the targeting of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[6] Reported particulars included a confidential human source's warning that a foreign government was sending a person to contribute to Clinton's anticipated campaign to gain influence with her, a [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|FISA]] request that headquarters ignored for four months because Clinton was "involved," and a $2,700 donation made by the source on behalf of a foreign insider in violation of the federal ban on foreign-national contributions.[6]
