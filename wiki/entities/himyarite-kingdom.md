# Himyarite Kingdom

The Himyarite Kingdom was a state occupying all of Yemen and major regions of Saudi Arabia which converted to Judaism in 380 CE and remained Jewish until roughly 525 AD.[1]

Matthew Ehret invokes the Himyarite conversion to argue that Byzantine persecution of Jews stemmed less from religious motives than from geopolitical ones: a Jewish kingdom astride a vital shipping corridor in today's Yemen disturbed Byzantine interests in controlling that route.[1] The article lists Emperors Heraclius (610–641), Justinian II (685–695), Leo III (717–741) and Romanos I (920–944) of the Byzantine Empire as major prosecutors of persecution against Jews in the period, in stark contrast to the tolerance Jews found under the [[tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] in China.[1] The Himyarite precedent also frames the later conversion of [[khazaria|Khazaria]] under [[king-bulan|King Bulan]] as less anomalous than modern readers assume.[1]
