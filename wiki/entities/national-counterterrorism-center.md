# National Counterterrorism Center

The National Counterterrorism Center (NCTC) is the U.S. intelligence organization responsible for integrating and analyzing terrorism intelligence, operating under the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.[1]

## 2026 leadership and the Kent resignation

In the second Trump administration the NCTC was directed by [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]], who simultaneously served as a top aide to Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]].[1] Kent's March 2026 resignation over the U.S. war in [[iran|Iran]] made the center's leadership the focal point of a public dispute inside the administration about whether U.S. Middle East policy was being driven by pressure from [[israel|Israel]] and its American lobby rather than by an "America First" assessment of threats.[1] Kent's letter stated that "Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation," a judgment notable coming from the official charged with the government's terrorism threat picture.[1]

The episode placed the NCTC, and the wider [[us-security-state|U.S. security state]], at the center of arguments over whether dissenting voices on Israel policy were being removed from office.[1]

## Kent's Syria disclosure (March 2026)

After leaving the center, Kent said publicly that the United States "worked directly with Al-Qaeda" and Islamic State militants during the Syrian conflict, describing support for opposition forces in [[syria|Syria]] linked to [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] and later [[islamic-state|ISIS]].[2] Reporting identified him as the former head of the NCTC who resigned over disagreements on U.S. policy toward [[iran|Iran]].[2] Badlands commentary treated the disclosure as unusually weighty precisely because it came from the government's former counterterrorism chief under the Director of National Intelligence.[2]

## Leadership vacancy and leak probe (March 2026)

The center's director, [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]], resigned on Tuesday, March 17, 2026, and his departure dominated the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]]'s annual hearing two days later.[3] The [[fbi|FBI]] had reportedly opened an investigation months earlier into allegations that Kent improperly disclosed classified information, according to sources cited by Semafor.[3] Kent gave his account of the departure in a [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] podcast interview that aired the day after his resignation.[3]
