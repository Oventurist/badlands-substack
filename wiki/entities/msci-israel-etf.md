# MSCI Israel ETF

The MSCI Israel Exchange Traded Fund is a passive investment vehicle that mirrors the overall performance of the Israeli stock market, giving investors — and short sellers — a single instrument through which to express a view on [[israel|Israel]]'s equities as a whole.[1]

## October 2, 2023 shorting spike

Short interest in the fund "suddenly, and significantly, spiked" on October 2, 2023, five days before the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 Hamas attack]], according to the study by [[robert-jackson-jr|Robert J. Jackson Jr.]] and [[joshua-mitts|Joshua Mitts]] citing [[financial-industry-regulatory-authority|FINRA]] data.[1] In the 3,570 trading days preceding the attack, that session recorded the 30th-highest daily shorting volume ever witnessed by the ETF — one of the largest bets placed since 2009 against the Israeli market.[1]

The volume significantly dwarfed the shorting of Israeli stocks at the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, a period that produced the largest intraday trading decline ever recorded when the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2,000 points in March 2020.[1] The authors concluded that "it is extremely unlikely that the volume of short selling on October 2nd occurred by random chance."[1]

## April 2023 parallel

The researchers identified similar patterns in the ETF's trading in April 2023, when Hamas was reportedly preparing an attack closely mirroring the later October operation, timed for the eve of Passover on April 5. Two days earlier, shorting of the fund peaked at levels very similar to those seen on October 2 and far higher — by an order of magnitude — than surrounding days.[1] The April operation was canceled after Israeli intelligence obtained advance notice and a national alert level was publicly issued, evidence the authors said strengthened the case that the trading was linked to the planned attacks rather than random noise.[1] See [[israel-october-7-foreknowledge-allegations|Israel October 7 foreknowledge allegations]] and [[short-selling|short selling]].[1]
