# Li Shangfu

Li Shangfu is a Chinese general who was named Minister of National Defense of the People's Republic of China in March 2023.[1] He had been under United States sanctions since 2018 over the purchase of combat aircraft and related equipment from Rosoboronexport, Russia's principal arms exporter.[1]

## Declined meeting with Lloyd Austin, 2023

In late May 2023, China declined a United States request for a meeting between Li and Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] at an annual security forum in Singapore, a refusal widely characterized in the press as a new sign of strain between the two powers.[1] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] confirmed the rebuff in a statement to the Wall Street Journal, saying that "overnight, the PRC informed the U.S. that they have declined our early May invitation for Secretary (Lloyd) Austin to meet with PRC Minister of National Defense Li Shangfu in Singapore."[1] The department reiterated its belief in open communication "to ensure that competition does not veer into conflict."[1]

The week before the refusal, [[white-house|White House]] spokesman [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] had said the Defense Department was working to arrange talks between Austin and his Chinese counterpart.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode was largely a manufactured controversy, noting that National Security Advisor [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] had met for more than eight hours on May 12 with [[wang-yi|Wang Yi]], director of the PRC's Office of the Foreign Affairs Commission, and that both sides agreed in the official readout to maintain that channel.[1] In this reading, Beijing had no diplomatic need to meet the Secretary of Defense — a member of the [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] whose agenda the National Security Advisor sets — when it already possessed an acknowledged direct line to the White House through Sullivan.[1] Declining the meeting instead propagated a "rising tensions" narrative said to benefit both the Biden administration and the PRC, and [[reuters|Reuters]] was faulted for omitting the Sullivan–Wang meeting from its coverage.[1]
