# Ryan DeLarme

Ryan DeLarme is the Badlands Media contributor credited with "A Bright Red Line," published 3 May 2024, which examines Blinken, Burns and NATO's bid for Ukraine.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Readers are directed to more of his work at the Post-Liberal, his own publication.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The piece carries Badlands Media's standard note that articles and features represent the opinions of contributing authors and do not necessarily represent the views of Badlands Media itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) DeLarme situates himself between the "black-pilled doomer" camp and the "patriots are in control" camp, arguing in either case that the Ukraine conflict is worth observing and dissecting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Later features

By late 2025 DeLarme was among the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributors whose feature articles were highlighted in the 7 November 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] retrospective.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) Two of his pieces were showcased: "The Venezuelan Squeeze" (30 October 2025) and "A More Perfect (Labor) Union" (23 October 2025).[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

In "The Venezuelan Squeeze" DeLarme stressed that his analysis was not necessarily an attack on the Trump administration specifically but on the broader Western foreign-policy establishment — politicians, corporations and tax-exempt organizations that profit from and perpetuate NATO-style globalism.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) "A More Perfect (Labor) Union" opened by challenging the "great American mythos" in which labor unions are depicted as bulwarks of the working class, gruff and principled warriors wrestling with corporate titans and shielding families from corporate greed.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

Earlier in 2025 DeLarme was likewise featured in the 15 August 2025 Badlands News Brief retrospective, which highlighted his 24 July 2025 article "And Iran, Iran So Far Away."[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features) That piece was presented as a direct continuation of his earlier work "The Deep Politics of the '12-Day War," extending his coverage of Middle East conflict and its underlying power interests.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features)

## "A More Perfect (Labor) Union"

Published 23 October 2025 and subtitled "The Role of Labor Unions in Covert Geopolitics," the piece argues that the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]] and its international arm, the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]], function as covert agents of the U.S. foreign policy establishment using taxpayer funds.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) DeLarme traces the relationship from Cold War covert operations to the modern "[[democracy-promotion|democracy promotion]]" agenda, applying the same "language of control" framing he has used in earlier posts.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

The article situates labor within his running series on the "deep government" and its "plausible deniability network," which he has previously traced through both political parties, the university system, the legal system, the international drug trade, information control, NGOs and activist networks.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) It closes by arguing that workers deserve unions that fight for their wages rather than Washington's quiet wars, and directs readers to more of his work at the Post-Liberal.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Media criticism

In February 2024 DeLarme published "A Swan Song for the MSM," an extended obituary for the mainstream press subtitled "The Citizen Journalist Will Write Its Epitaph."[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The essay argues that the death of the [[centralized-media|centralized media]] is no longer a question of if but when, catalogues the 2024 layoffs at the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]], [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]], [[sports-illustrated|Sports Illustrated]] and [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed]], and traces the entanglement of the press with the [[us-security-state|U.S. security state]].[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) It devotes a lengthy section to dissecting a TikTok "State of the Media" video by [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], whom DeLarme treats as a symptom rather than the cause of an industry that has become "anti-journalism."[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) He closes with the movement slogan "We are the news now," arguing that [[citizen-journalism|citizen journalism]] and the [[decentralized-media|decentralized media]] will replace the outlets now failing.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

DeLarme also writes for his own publication, the Post-Liberal, and readers of Badlands are directed there for more of his work.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## "And Iran, Iran So Far Away"

Published 24 July 2025 and subtitled "The True Implications of the Deep State's Global Power Play," this piece is a direct continuation of DeLarme's "The Deep Politics of the '12-Day War."[6] It argues that the motivation for U.S. and Israeli tensions with [[iran|Iran]] was not fear of "Schrödinger's nuke" but geopolitical favors and the question of which segment of the Western ruling elite would shape the future of the Middle East.[6] DeLarme frames the conflict as a struggle between an old guard — the interagency bureaucracy, the Obama-era inheritors of Kissinger and Brzezinski, giant investment firms and the think tanks behind the EU, UN and [[nato|NATO]] — and a still-emerging populist coalition around [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] backed by [[israel|Israel]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and rogue portions of the private sector.[6]

The article traces the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|JCPOA]] through the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]] and its energy-industry funders, the careers of [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], [[william-burns|William Burns]] and [[tom-donilon|Tom Donilon]], China's $400 billion agreement with Iran, and the removal of [[imran-khan|Imran Khan]] in [[pakistan|Pakistan]], concluding that the Iran question is "a geopolitical minefield" whose mishandling could fracture Trump's base and destabilise the region.[6] DeLarme closes by noting that while the "12-day war" is over, "the show has been renewed for another season."[6] The piece carries Badlands Media's standard disclaimer and directs readers to more of his work at the Post-Liberal.[6]


## "Assad Day in Damascus" (2024)

DeLarme wrote "Assad Day in Damascus," published December 12, 2024, analyzing the collapse of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government and the Western media's framing of it.[7] The piece argued that [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] was a rebranded [[al-qaeda|al-Qaeda]], traced U.S. regime-change ambitions in [[syria|Syria]] to the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]], and defended [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] against accusations of Russian sympathy.[7] The article closed by endorsing a noninterventionist U.S. posture and remarking that his money was not on the Syrian people benefiting most from the timing of events.[7] Readers were pointed to more of DeLarme's work at the Post-Liberal.[7]

## Badlands News Brief commentary (February 2023)

DeLarme contributed two "Our Take" segments to the February 13, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[10] On the House vote against the [[dc-noncitizen-voting-law|D.C. noncitizen voting law]], he argued through a Roman analogy that mass migration — as with the Huns' invasion of Europe in the late fourth century — contributed to imperial collapse, and predicted Democrats would direct migrant voters after providing benefits, enlarging sanctuary cities and their federal funding.[10] He listed economic troubles, military overspending, government corruption, political instability, loss of traditional values, weakening of the legions, and a rising eastern empire as Rome's other maladies, asking whether the United States mirrors them.[10]

On [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]]'s proposal to use AI against "digital misinformation," DeLarme asked who would program such a system, invoking [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]]'s alleged bias against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Green]], and warned it would "search for, find, and block competing ideas" on behalf of elites seeking narrative control.[10] He called Gates "the clown prince of disinformation."[10]

## Badlands News Brief commentary (May 2023)

DeLarme wrote the "Our Take" on Maricopa County supervisor [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]] in the May 9, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], calling him "this weasel back in the spotlight once again."[11] He tied Gates and county official Steven Ritcher to what he described as the blatantly rigged Arizona midterm gubernatorial election, and condemned Gates's claim of suffering PTSD comparable to World War II veterans as equating "being a Deep State patsy for personal gain to going overseas, fighting and dying for your country."[11]

DeLarme said he could not determine whether Gates was willingly corrupt, brainwashed, or had buckled under pressure from "powerful and deadly forces that had a vested interest in a particular outcome" in Arizona.[11] He argued the state was strategically pivotal — soon to be the nation's largest chip producer, and a gateway for illegal immigration and the flow of guns, drugs and human trafficking he suggested could be funding covert operations — and that establishment interests feared an America-first governor in [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]], making Arizona's midterm "probably the most critical for them to win."[11]

## Commentary on the Durham hearing (June 2023)

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] after the [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|June 2023 Durham hearing]], DeLarme said [[john-durham|John Durham]] "was fighting an uphill battle from the get go" and set out three competing readings: that Durham was "more rotten, spineless fruit from the [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]] tree"; that his team acted in good faith but found the system too corrupt; or that the whole sequence is kayfabe intended to push the public toward accepting military intervention.[12] He summarized the recurring pattern as Democrats fighting dirty, no consequences following, Republicans lacking spine, and [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] sitting down to pee, while adding that today's apparent nothingburger might prove instrumental tomorrow and that "Durham isn't the only game in town."[12]


## Commentary on Ireland's hate speech law (2023)

Responding to [[ireland|Ireland]]'s 2023 legislation criminalizing possession of "hateful" content, DeLarme wrote that Western countries were "getting more intolerable by the day" and that globalists were tightening their leash on the Irish people, remarking that their priorities seemed little changed since the Holy Roman Empire.[13] Noting that the measure followed [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s visit to Ireland, and writing as an Irishman himself, he said he would be ashamed if the Irish did not raise hell over it.[13]

He warned that the [[restrict-act|Restrict Act]] would enable the same outcome in the United States, because its vague wording lets the government define the act's terms at its discretion, and placed it in the totalitarian lineage of the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]], which he noted is ironically used to go after patriots.[13]

## Dimmitt dairy explosion commentary (2023)

In the April 2023 Badlands News Brief, DeLarme responded to the [[south-fork-dairy-explosion|South Fork Dairy explosion]] in Dimmitt, Texas, which killed roughly 18,000 cattle, by asking whether "machinery malfunction" had become "the new slang for sabotage," drawing a sarcastic parallel to equipment failures reported at Maricopa County election centers in November 2022.[14] He argued that no plausible factory system should be capable of producing a blast of that size and that the event more closely resembled a bomb detonation.[14]

DeLarme placed the incident within a pattern of roughly 300 comparable "accidents" affecting food infrastructure since [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] took office, and tied it to concerns about farmland ownership by [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] and Chinese interests, forecasting a diet of crickets, antibiotic-rich chicken, and produce treated with glyphosate and heavy metals.[14]

## Commentary on the Carlson footage release (2023)

In the March 9, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief]], DeLarme argued that the January 6 Capitol surveillance footage given to [[tucker-carlson]] should have gone to a figure such as [[elon-musk]] or, better, been released in full for internet researchers to examine, dismissing security objections on the grounds that the Capitol is a public building extensively photographed over the years.[15] He described [[rupert-murdoch]] in caustic terms, recalling that Murdoch had claimed [[donald-trump]] was losing his mind after the 2020 election and had chastised his own employees for reporting on election fraud, and concluded that media moguls treat party affiliation and political philosophy as markets to be cornered rather than causes to serve.[15]

## Commentary on the Manhattan Trump case (2023)

Writing in the March 23, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], DeLarme observed that the widely predicted Tuesday arrest of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had come and gone, remarking that "perpetually moving goal posts aren't exclusive to this community."[16] He asked how a "blatantly corrupt Soros-backed DA" — [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], who presides over some of the most crime-ridden areas in the nation — could pursue charges against a former president over allegations that had already failed in court, citing Michael Avenatti's imprisonment and the roughly $300,000 in legal fees [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] was ordered to pay.[16]

DeLarme concluded that prosecutors knew there was no case and wanted only "the show and the mug shot," describing the effort as "the Nancy Pelosi wrap-up-smear with added pizzazz" and an attempt to entrap the more reactionary of Trump's supporters.[16] He argued against refraining from protest, calling instead for massive, peaceful but highly energized demonstrations aimed specifically at drawing attention to [[george-soros|George Soros]].[16]

## Commentary in the March 2023 news brief

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] for March 14, 2023, DeLarme contributed two segments.[17] On the Republican primary, he argued that the widespread online expectation of a [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] victory was contradicted by every poll he had seen, and characterized the accounts pushing claims that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had "allowed many members of the Deep State into his WH" — citing Trump's support for [[ronna-mcdaniel|Ronna McDaniel]] and [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] — as anonymous spam.[17] He countered that draining the swamp immediately would have left vacancies with no prepared patriots to fill them, urged readers "Steady boys, steady," and said Trump's Monday night speech was infused with confidence even as the nation's infrastructure and economy crumbled.[17]

On the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]]'s agreement to hand over Biden family [[suspicious-activity-report|suspicious activity reports]], DeLarme said he would be "very, very suspicious" of what a Biden Treasury provided, warning documents could be missing, altered, or incomplete.[17] He contrasted the scrutiny applied to Trump by the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]], [[fbi|FBI]], and mainstream media with the treatment of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and predicted that the best case outcome would be a token indictment.[17]

DeLarme contributed three items to the February 3, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[18] On [[merck|Merck]]'s COVID-19 pill [[lagevrio|Lagevrio]] he wrote that the mutation findings should be "filed under 'surprising to no one,'" citing Merck's record with Vioxx and Gardasil, and asked whether the virus-mutating effect was "a happy accident, or by design."[18] On [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]]'s claim that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] made 12 or 13 trips to Ukraine in his last year as vice president, he speculated the travel facilitated family business and dismissed the national-security rationale for arming Ukraine.[18] On the [[chinese-spy-balloon-incident-2023|Montana balloon sighting]] he framed the standard analytic question as "ours or theirs" — US military, foreign state, or contractor such as Raytheon, Boeing or Lockheed Martin — concluding that [[china|China]] was interested in Montana's ICBM facilities rather than its farmland.[18]

## Commentary on James O'Keefe's Bragg claim (March 2023)

In the March 28, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], DeLarme responded to [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]]'s suggestion that he had insiders within Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]]'s case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], writing that it was good to see O'Keefe had "hasn't missed a beat since being railroaded out of his own company" and that being hated by many powerful people is what a good American journalist should look like.[20] He found it notable that O'Keefe appeared to be showing his hand, which he said is almost never seen, and asked since when O'Keefe tips people off beforehand and potentially puts his sources at risk.[20] He offered two possibilities: that there were no insiders and the statement was a ploy to sow mistrust inside Bragg's camp in hopes someone would slip up or flip, or — in his view more likely — that the operation was complete and [[project-veritas|Veritas]]-style preparation of the information had begun, concluding that "Bragg and company are sweating."[20]

## Commentary on the 2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive

In the June 8, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] brief, DeLarme described Ukraine as having become "both a figurative and literal charnel house" and argued that Western reporting on the war, including the hype around the spring offensive, had proved dramatically skewed.[21] He cited Ukrainian losses of 3,715 troops, 52 tanks, 207 armored vehicles, 143 motor vehicles, five aircraft, two helicopters and 48 pieces of field artillery, emphasizing the 3,715 families affected, and concluded that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was the only person speaking sensibly about the war — that it needed to end.[21] See [[2023-ukrainian-counteroffensive|2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive]].[21]

## Two-tiered justice commentary (June 2023)

In the June 15, 2023 brief, DeLarme argued that two stories were unfolding simultaneously — the [[joe-biden-bribery-allegation|Biden bribery scandal]] and the indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — and that the mainstream media had put in "zero effort" reporting the former while devoting enormous screen time to the latter.[22] He described the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]] as a century-old wartime statute historically used to squash dissent and revived under Obama against [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[chelsea-manning|Chelsea Manning]], now being turned on Trump.[22]

He contended that Trump is protected by the [[presidential-records-act|Presidential Records Act]], which in his reading supersedes the Espionage Act and makes the quantity of retained material irrelevant, while [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] stood accused of pay-to-play and bribery schemes and of using the [[fbi|FBI]] to cover them up.[22] He cited [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s admission that he gave half his salary to his father for three decades and the 459 alleged crimes documented on the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]], held by the FBI since December 2019, calling the media's focus on Trump "baffling."[22]

## Commentary on the New START suspension (February 2023)

In the February 23, 2023 Badlands News Brief, DeLarme responded to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s submission of a draft law suspending the [[new-start-treaty|New START]] treaty by mocking the claim that the "deep-state fixed the election and saved us all from Trump," recalling that the left had believed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would start a war while, in his view, [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had undone fifty years of diplomacy and his base now backed war with Russia.[23] He wrote that most of the Badlands team likely agreed there would be no actual nuclear attack and that the posturing was "more theater than anything else," while allowing for the possibility of what he called a mass near-death experience that might wake up "the stubbornly asleep."[23] He added that if he were wrong, [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] and "the depopulation crowd" would be thrilled, and speculated that a Trump-brokered deal averting nuclear annihilation would seal a 2024 win.[23]

## March 2023 commentary: J6 footage and gun statistics

In the March 8, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], DeLarme argued that it had been "fairly obvious that Democrats and RINOs were hiding the footage because it completely destroyed their J6 narrative," while cautioning that [[fox-news|Fox News]] was "a bunch of partisan hacks just as much as the other networks" and that [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] should have made everything he was given public immediately.[24] He called for the immediate release of January 6 defendants held in pretrial detention in Washington, and read the [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|newly aired footage]] as showing that participants were admitted and escorted by [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]], that they were peaceful, that police escorted [[jacob-chansley|Jacob Chansley]] around the Capitol for photo ops, that imposters were present, and that the protesters simply wanted [[congress|Congress]] to confront election fraud.[24]

In the same brief he attacked [[cnn|CNN]]'s use of [[gun-violence-archive|Gun Violence Archive]] data to claim more than 100 mass shootings in early 2023, writing that CNN and allied interests would rather Americans face "busloads of fentanyl" than keep a firearm at home, and listing depopulation, launderable federal funding for [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary cities]], an endless drug war and the prison industrial complex among the agendas served by an unabated flow of drugs and criminals across the border.[24] He added that anti-[[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] groups have always manipulated crime statistics, most cleverly by including suicides and accidents alongside homicides.[24]
