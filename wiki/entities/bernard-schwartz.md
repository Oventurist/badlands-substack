# Bernard Schwartz

Bernard Schwartz was the head of [[loral-corporation|Loral Corporation]], an American aerospace and satellite manufacturer, and a major donor to the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] during the [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] administration.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

## China trade mission

Schwartz accompanied Commerce Secretary [[ron-brown|Ron Brown]] on a 1994 [[department-of-commerce|Commerce Department]] trade mission to China. During that mission, Brown arranged a meeting between Schwartz and a Chinese government official.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) According to accounts compiled by researchers and [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]], that meeting led to a transfer of American missile technology to the Chinese that later became the subject of a congressional investigation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

The Badlands account presents Schwartz as the commercial node in a wider pattern in which access to foreign governments and to sensitive American technology was, in the author's framing, effectively for sale in exchange for campaign contributions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) Transfers of American technology approved and overseen by Brown's Commerce Department are described as having breached national security, with the [[communist-china|Chinese]] missile program advancing significantly as a result.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

## Political donations

Schwartz's status as a leading DNC donor is treated in the article as inseparable from the policy outcomes that followed: the same period saw waivers signed by President Clinton permitting satellite and guidance technology exports to China, and the sale of seats on Commerce Department trade missions to raise money for the DNC and the 1996 Clinton/Gore campaign.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)
