# Joseph N. McCormack

**Joseph N. McCormack** was secretary of the Kentucky State Board of Health and the organizer hired by [[george-h-simmons|George H. Simmons]] to build the political power of the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]].[1]

Simmons brought McCormack in to "rouse the profession to lobby" at a moment when state governments were reluctant to pass laws restricting competing healing arts such as chiropractic, homeopathy and herbalism. With McCormack leading the campaign, the AMA bolstered its ranks, promoted a professional ethics code that discouraged physicians from competing with one another or advertising their prices, and attacked rival practices as "quackery."[1]

The lobbying drive McCormack led supplied the organizational muscle that made the AMA's later ranking of medical schools — completed with Rockefeller and Carnegie money as the [[flexner-report|Flexner Report]] — enforceable across the profession.[1]
