# Office of Strategic Services

The Office of Strategic Services (OSS) was the wartime U.S. intelligence agency and the precursor to the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article dates the exploitation of American labor for political ends to the closing days of World War II, when the OSS began collaborating with American unions to counter emerging communist influence in Europe.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

That collaboration predated the 1955 creation of the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]] and ran in parallel with the AFL's own [[free-trade-union-committee|Free Trade Union Committee]], established in 1944.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) When the CIA was created and the Cold War came into focus, interventionists in government recognized the work the AFL had already been doing in Europe and chose to operate through union channels because foreign workers were more likely to trust fellow union members.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Transition into the CIA and the Gladio networks

The Badlands Media Gladio funding article describes the OSS as providing the wartime transition necessary to encapsulate the postwar realignment of organized crime into intelligence work.[2] Its chief, [[william-donovan]], directed Colonel [[paul-helliwell]] to covertly arm [[chiang-kai-shek]]'s [[kuomintang]] and created the [[world-commerce-corporation]] as a financial conduit, while [[allen-dulles]] served as European officer-in-charge and [[james-jesus-angleton]] helped design the covert network with [[mi6]]'s [[william-stephenson]].[2] [[e-howard-hunt]] and lawyer [[tommy-corcoran]] were also OSS men involved in the resulting operations.[2]

With the OSS in control of Italy's ports after [[operation-husky]], black-market goods enriched the postwar OSS/mafia network and Helliwell's design could be implemented.[2] The organisation subsequently became the Strategic Service Unit, then the National Intelligence Authority and Central Intelligence Group, and finally the [[cia]] in 1947 under the [[national-security-act-of-1947|National Security Act]].[2]

## Academic recruitment

The liaison between American intelligence and academia predates the CIA and begins with the OSS under Wild Bill Donovan.[4] The paths to power ran through the Ivy League universities and the OSS stationed recruiters along the way; [[yale-university|Yale]] and [[harvard-university|Harvard]] were used not only for recruitment but as middlemen in operations abroad.[4]

[[norman-holmes-pearson|Norman Holmes Pearson]], a Yale professor of English and American studies, was recruited into the OSS in London during World War II and by 1943 was working in the new X-2 counterintelligence branch, which linked the OSS to British Special Intelligence.[4] The historian [[robin-winks|Robin Winks]] judged that some of Pearson's best work for the OSS, done in its final months, consisted of analyses of the intelligence services of other nations.[4] The use of scholars as intelligence assets in the Second World War proved successful enough that the practice carried over into the Cold War and produced the present situation of intelligence assets embedded throughout Western academia.[4]

## Continuity with the CIA

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that although the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] was formally created in 1947 following World War II, it already existed in substance as the OSS, which performed covert operations during the war.[3] He used that continuity to date the agency's covert-action capability earlier than its statutory founding, noting that by 1953 it was overthrowing the Iranian government in the [[1953-iranian-coup|coup against Mohammad Mosaddegh]].[3]

## Alleged British creation and postwar continuation (Carlson, 2024)

Erik Carlson's "The CIA is Being Thrown Under the Bus" asserts that the OSS was created by [[mi6|MI6]] in 1942, shortly after the United States entered the Second World War, for the purpose of controlling the US during the conflict on behalf of the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]].[5] In 1947 the OSS is said to have morphed into the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], still fully controlled by the British elites and used thereafter to enforce the Cabal's agenda.[5]

Carlson treats the decision not to dissolve the OSS after the war as the pivotal counterfactual of the twentieth century: had it been disbanded and no CIA created, he argues, the United States might have drifted out of its British handlers' control, avoided Korea, Vietnam and the later Middle Eastern wars, and sustained the postwar economic boom without the roadblocks of the 1960s and 1970s.[5] Instead, he writes, the Cabal quickly created the CIA and changed directions, thereafter controlling the world through a Cold War in which it held both sides.[5]
