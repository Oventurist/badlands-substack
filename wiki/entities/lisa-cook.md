# Lisa Cook

Lisa Cook is a Governor of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] whose attempted removal by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] produced a case set for argument before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in January 2026.[1]

## The removal case

Cook urged the Supreme Court to prevent Trump from removing her, and the case has widely been framed as a test of the Federal Reserve's independence.[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued that this framing misses the point: the real question is in which constitutional box the disputed authorities reside, and whether [[congress|Congress]] may bypass the executive to insulate power from presidential control.[1]

## Constitutional argument

Ashe in America contended that the Constitution vests executive power in the president alone, that clear lines of accountability were intentional on the part of the Founders, and that removal rights are the ultimate test of who holds power and is therefore accountable.[1] On this view nothing in the Constitution creates or protects an "independent" central bank — the Federal Reserve exists entirely by statute, and while Congress may create offices and prescribe duties, it may not transfer executive authority away from the president by insulating officers from removal.[1] A "for cause" standard that effectively forecloses presidential judgment was said to undermine Article II, with loss of confidence itself constituting cause.[1]

Responding to the argument that presidential removal would threaten public trust in US monetary policy, Ashe in America replied that such trust does not exist and that the Constitution does not appoint a committee to override the people's representation.[1] The commentary concluded that a majority of the Court claiming originalism cannot hold that the Federal Reserve Act overrides the Constitution, and that a ruling for Trump would effectively neuter the "independence of the Federal Reserve" narrative.[1]


## June 2026 Supreme Court decision
In late June 2026 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] declined, by a 5-4 vote, to immediately back President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s effort to remove Cook from the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Board.[2] The justices sent the case back to a lower court, allowing Cook to remain in her position while the legal challenge continues.[2] The ruling does not bar Trump from ultimately removing her, but it rejected the administration's argument that courts have little authority to review such a decision, temporarily preserving the Federal Reserve's independence as litigation proceeds.[2] The outcome came the same day the Court ruled 6-3 that presidents hold broad removal authority over Senate-confirmed executive agency leaders generally.[2]

## Role in the 2025 pressure campaign on the Fed

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] identified the Cook controversy as a key fracture point in President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s escalating campaign against the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in August 2025, spotlighted as part of what he described as a controlled demolition of the Fed and the fiat apparatus.[3] In that account, the affair served to expose hypocrisy at the top of the institution and to cultivate a public mandate to question Fed independence, revealing how insiders had gamed the system for personal gain in a way that mirrored their broader manipulation of the economy.[3]
