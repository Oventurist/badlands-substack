# Reece Committee

The **Reece Committee** was a congressional committee that investigated [[tax-exempt-foundations|tax-exempt foundations]] from 1953 to 1955.[1] Its general counsel was [[rene-wormser|Rene A. Wormser]], whose book *Foundations: Their Power and Influence* is described by Badlands Media as a documented exposé of his experience with the committee.[1]

The committee examined the proposition that foundations, though created "to support existing institutions and to undertake certain 'operating' functions," had drifted into the funding of political movements — findings the article summarizes as pointing toward support for a socialistic, one-world government.[1] Wormser also warned that foundations combine in "cartel-like" fashion into what he called the "tax-exempt complex," a tendency he said threatened "the freedom of our intellectual and public life."[1]

Badlands Media places the Reece Committee alongside the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] as one of the two most important and revealing committees ever to emerge from the United States Congress.[1] Its work followed earlier scrutiny by the [[commission-on-industrial-relations|Commission on Industrial Relations]] and the 1952 inquiry in which [[norman-dodd|Norman Dodd]] served as director of research for the House Select Committee to Investigate Foundations and Comparable Organizations.[1]
