# Alex Jeffrey Pretti

Alex Jeffrey Pretti was a 37-year-old United States citizen who was fatally shot by federal immigration agents in Minneapolis, Minnesota, on January 24, 2026, during an enforcement operation.[1] His death became the flashpoint of a national controversy over the conduct of federal immigration enforcement in sanctuary jurisdictions and over federal–state relations more broadly.[1]

## The shooting

The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] stated that Pretti was armed with a handgun and resisted efforts by agents to disarm him before a [[united-states-border-patrol|Border Patrol]] agent opened fire, killing him at the scene.[1] Bystander videos and witness accounts circulated afterward offered a materially different account: they suggested that Pretti had initially been filming officers, that he was pepper-sprayed and pinned to the ground prior to the shots, and that some footage appeared to show an agent taking his weapon before other agents fired.[1]

## Aftermath

Border Patrol Chief [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]] publicly defended the agents involved, characterizing them as the victims of the encounter and asserting that Pretti had interfered with their operation.[1] The killing drew sharp rebukes from Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]], both of whom described the federal operation as excessive and called for the withdrawal of federal agents and for accountability.[1]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] used his Truth Social platform to back the federal agents, questioned why local police had not been involved, and accused Minnesota officials of "inciting insurrection."[1] In the same series of posts Trump tied the unrest to alleged "theft and fraud" involving billions of dollars connected to state officials, casting the episode in broader partisan terms.[1]

The incident intensified protests in Minnesota and elsewhere and became a reference point in the wider 2026 debate over immigration enforcement, sanctuary policy, and the limits of federal police power.[1]

## Background and body-camera account

Pretti was a former intensive care unit nurse at the Minneapolis VA Medical Center who had quit his job in mid-2025.[2] Body-worn camera footage released after the shooting showed him approaching agents while filming, resisting dispersal orders, and reaching toward his waist, where he lawfully carried a concealed firearm.[2] Colleagues described him as compassionate and previously apolitical.[2]

His father, Michael Pretti, told reporters that the family had noticed unusual behavior in the months before his death, including pulling away from routine activities and talking more about "standing up" against perceived federal overreach.[2] "He seemed to have joined some kind of group," the father said, adding that the family had warned him to avoid direct confrontation.[2] Extracted cellphone data reportedly revealed Pretti's involvement in coordinated anti-ICE protest networks, including encrypted Signal groups that shared agent locations and planned crowd mobilization to obstruct operations.[2]

## Narrative dispute

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] invoked a "72-hour rule" of withholding judgment until facts settle, and argued that the initial portrayal of Pretti as a VA nurse and first responder "brutally murdered" during enforcement had been substantially debunked.[2] Of the rumors circulating on January 27, 2026 — that he was known to ICE, had been fired or had left the VA months earlier, and had been radicalized online — she noted that only a broken rib sustained weeks earlier while interfering with ICE enforcement had been confirmed, and characterized him as an agitator whose armed and repeated interference made his death predictable.[2]

She further observed that Pretti's parents live in [[colorado|Colorado]], as did Renee Good, the other person killed in the Minneapolis confrontations, and that Colorado legislators had spent legislative time on January 26 mourning both and calling for federal resistance.[2] From this she inferred the earmarks of a deep state [[color-revolution|color revolution]] with Colorado as its domestic spearhead, and criticized prominent Republicans calling for temperance and middle ground as conceding selective application of the rule of law.[2]

The shooting also fed directly into two institutional confrontations: the contempt proceedings before Chief Judge [[patrick-schiltz|Patrick Schiltz]] over ICE's non-compliance with court orders, and the [[january-2026-government-shutdown-standoff|January 2026 government shutdown standoff]] over DHS funding.[2]

## Evidence-preservation order lifted (February 2026)

US District Judge [[eric-tostrud|Eric Tostrud]] in Minnesota lifted a prior court order that had required federal authorities to preserve evidence connected to the January 24, 2026 fatal shooting of Pretti, which occurred as he was interfering with federal agents in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]].[3] The ruling found no ongoing need for court supervision over evidence handling, while the shooting itself remained under federal investigation.[3]
