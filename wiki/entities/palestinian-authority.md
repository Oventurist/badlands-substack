# Palestinian Authority

The Palestinian Authority (PA) is the governing body established to administer Palestinian territories, and in the Badlands Media article "Bad Actors" it is the destination of a last-minute foreign aid transfer that Joe Lange interprets as a covert repayment to a foreign patron of the American political class.[1] Lange notes that the PA has receded from public attention since Hamas took control of Gaza, but argues that understanding what the PA is remains essential to understanding why [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] would direct so much taxpayer money to it in the final hours of his presidency.[1]

## The $221 million transfer

Citing The Hill, the article reports that the Obama administration sent $221 million to the Palestinian Authority on the Friday as Obama prepared to leave the White House for the last time, with the outgoing administration formally notifying [[congress|Congress]] of the decision just hours before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was inaugurated.[1] The Independent reported that Obama had overruled Congress to make the decision, that the money was nominally intended to support good governance and humanitarian aid, and that the Trump administration subsequently froze the package — PA Prime Minister Rami Hamdallah was told by US officials not to expect the $220 million payout.[1]

The mechanics ran through the [[state-department|State Department]]: acting spokesman Mark Toner said that "in a final act, then-Secretary of State John Kerry instructed USAID to release $220.3 million for Gaza recovery programs," with the House informed by Kerry hours before the inauguration.[1] Because Lange regards [[usaid|USAID]] as a [[cia|CIA]] front and taxpayer slush fund, he treats the routing itself as evidence that the money was not humanitarian in purpose.[1]

## The "pay to slay" program

Quoting Newsweek, the article describes the PA's "pay to slay" program, under which the Authority incentivizes violence and has enshrined the practice in its own laws.[1] In 2004 the PA passed a law guaranteeing salaries for convicted terrorists and their families, with legislation specifying that "prisoners and released prisoners are a fighting sector and integral part of the fabric of Arab Palestinian society."[1] In 2010 the PA passed resolutions stipulating that every Palestinian inmate in an Israeli prison convicted of a "terrorism-related offense" receive a monthly payment, and in 2013 Prime Minister Rami Hamdallah amended the law to add post-release benefits — any male ex-prisoner incarcerated ten or more years, and every female ex-prisoner who served five, becoming entitled to a position within the PA.[1]

Lange summarises the result as Obama giving large sums of American taxpayer money to a governing body operating a scheme to pay terrorists and their families for violence against Israelis, a program that had been law and public knowledge for many years.[1]

## Interpretation in the article

Lange frames the transfer as the repayment of a political debt rather than as aid policy. In his account, [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] owed money to donors to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] who had expected her to win the presidency, and one "bad actor" in particular — [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] — demanded repayment when she lost, with Obama arranging for his puppet master to be paid back with taxpayers' money.[1] The unplanned, last-minute character of the payout, made over Congress's objections in the final hours of the administration, is offered as evidence that it was a reaction to the unexpected 2016 result.[1] Trump's cancellation of the package is described as further "money flow disruption."[1]

## 2026 West Bank measures

The Israeli Security Cabinet's February 2026 [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank measures]] would allow Israeli citizens to purchase tracts of land in the West Bank and register those properties with the Israeli government rather than the Palestinian Authority, placing the land under Israeli jurisdiction and bypassing the Authority's registry role under the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]].[2] The package also shifts building authority in Hebron, repeals restrictions on non-Muslim real estate transactions, and expands Israeli enforcement into Palestinian-controlled areas.[2]

In Badlands commentary on the origins of October 7, the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank is described as having cut [[hamas|Hamas]] off financially, after which Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] appealed to [[qatar|Qatar]] for funds that were then walked into Gaza to families with known Hamas ties.[2]

## Israeli calls for dismantlement (2025)

After a shooting attack in Jerusalem on September 9, 2025, Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] publicly called for the Palestinian Authority to be dismantled, saying "the State of Israel cannot accept a Palestinian Authority that raises and educates its children to murder Jews" and that the PA "must disappear from the map."[3] His remarks followed by days his proposal that Israel annex 82 percent of the West Bank in response to Western plans to recognize a Palestinian state.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that an online psychological operation was simultaneously targeting PA President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], whom he described as having been the reasonable negotiating partner for some thirty-five years, in order to justify annexation.[3]

## Rejected as a Gaza governing alternative (2024)

In May 2024, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] ruled out any role for the Palestinian Authority in postwar Gaza, saying he was "not ready to replace Hamastan with Fatahstan" and that "as long as Hamas remains intact, no other party will step in to manage civilian affairs in Gaza, certainly not the Palestinian Authority."[4] He charged that the Authority "supports terror, educates for terror, funds terror."[4] The remarks answered Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]]'s demand that Israel declare it would establish neither civilian nor military governance in Gaza and raise a governing alternative to [[hamas|Hamas]] immediately.[4]

Badlands commentary recounted that the Palestinian Authority was established in 1993 with the signing of the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] between Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and PLO leader [[yasser-arafat|Yasser Arafat]], with peace talks first initiated by the then-new Russian Federation, and that it was a young [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] who signed on Palestine's behalf at the Clinton White House ceremony.[4] The gesture was intended to signal that the PLO would step aside for a peace-seeking Authority governing Palestine.[4] Since the 2006 Hamas election victory in Gaza and the expulsion of [[fatah|Fatah]] security forces from the Strip, the Authority's writ has been confined largely to the West Bank.[4]

## Ban on Al Jazeera (January 2025)

In the first days of January 2025 the Palestinian Authority temporarily banned [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]] from operating in the West Bank, saying the broadcaster had played a role in inciting unrest and "stirring strife."[5] The Palestinian news agency WAFA reported the decision, citing the PA's culture, interior and communications ministries, which said the Qatar-based network's coverage had "deceived" and "incited division" among its audience in reporting on recent clashes in the Jenin refugee camp.[5] In December 2024 Al Jazeera had covered a raid by PA forces against the Jenin Brigades splinter group; the camp, established in 1953, houses an estimated 11,000 to 22,000 people and is a longstanding stronghold of resistance to Israel.[5] The ban was announced as temporary with no stated end date.[5]

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry cautioned against reading the move simply as Islamic authoritarianism, noting that Al Jazeera had been banned on similar grounds by Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan and Egypt during the 2017 Qatar embargo and by Israel the previous year.[5] He argued that the PA governs a territory "fighting for its life" while inundated with psychological warfare operations designed to destabilize its society and push Palestinians toward escalation with Israel, and said he was grateful not to be the one making the decision.[5]

## Mourning after the al-Ahli hospital blast (October 2023)

Following the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|al-Ahli Baptist Hospital explosion]] on October 17, 2023, Palestinian Authority President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] announced days of mourning, a declaration the [[white-house|White House]] cited in postponing President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s planned summit with Abbas, [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]] and Egyptian President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah el-Sisi]] in [[jordan|Jordan]].[6]

## Role in the 2025 Gaza occupation debate

After the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] and Israeli settlers withdrew from Gaza in 2005, following 38 years of Israeli control beginning in 1967, the enclave was left to be governed by the Palestinian Authority, which also controlled parts of the occupied West Bank.[7] [[hamas|Hamas]] took control of [[gaza|Gaza]] after winning elections there in 2006, and no elections have been held since.[7] In 2025 commentary on [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s plan to fully occupy Gaza, the Authority under [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] was described as a third claimant to the territory alongside Israel and Hamas.[7]

## Saudi $90 million grant (December 2025)

On December 1, 2025, Saudi Arabia transferred a $90 million grant to the Palestinian Authority to allow the cash-strapped Ramallah government to continue operating.[8] Formal notice of the grant was presented to PA Finance Minister Estephan Salameh by Saudi Ambassador to the Palestinians Prince Mansour bin Khalid, who said the tranche demonstrated Riyadh's commitment to supporting the PA and helping it meet its financial obligations.[8] The transfer came amid continued Israeli withholding of tax revenues collected on the PA's behalf; Badlands commentary argued that analysts predicting Saudi betrayal of the Palestinians had badly misread the situation and that the real question was why Israel controls Palestinian tax collection and can withhold billions in revenue without recourse.[8]

## Abbas's 2024 United Nations appeal

In his September 2024 address to the United Nations, [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] argued that the Palestinian Authority should have control over all Palestinian territories and said it would hold elections once the war in Gaza ended.[9] He called for a comprehensive and permanent ceasefire, an end to attacks by Israeli settlers in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, delivery of humanitarian aid throughout Gaza, and a full withdrawal of the Israeli military from the enclave.[9]

## Conflict with Hamas

Badlands Media recounted the Authority's long antagonism with [[hamas|Hamas]]: its security forces were driven out of Gaza alongside the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] in 2007; Abbas subsequently refused dialogue with the group and declined to seat its officials in the Palestinian government unless it recognized Israel; and in 2018 Hamas attempted to assassinate the Authority's prime minister with a roadside bomb while he travelled to Gaza to negotiate a peaceful transfer of governance.[9] The outlet also stated that Abbas had criticized Hamas for launching the October 7 attack without informing the Authority and had blamed the group for the destruction that followed.[9]

## US proposals for post-war Gaza governance (December 2023)

In December 2023, U.S. National Security Adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] told Israel's Channel 12 that Washington's view was that governance of the West Bank and Gaza "needs to be connected under a revamped and revitalized Palestinian Authority."[10] He said that outcome would require reform, an updating of how the Authority approaches governance, and financial and other contributions from countries in the region, and that the aim was common leadership posing no terrorist threat to Israel.[10] The proposal set the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration at odds with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], who opposed any PA role in post-war Gaza.[10]
