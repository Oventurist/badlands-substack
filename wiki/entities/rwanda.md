# Rwanda

Rwanda is a central African state identified in Badlands coverage as the backer of militias that crossed into eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Congo]] and seized control of mining towns.[1] The [[m23-rebels|M23]] group and its political vehicle, the Alliance Fleuve Congo led by [[corneille-nangaa|Corneille Nangaa]], are described as Rwanda-linked.[1]

By late January 2026, Congress was reported to be discussing sanctions against Rwanda over its role in the conflict.[1] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized Rwanda as "long been an ally of Israel and a faithful purveyor of the Zionist lifestyle," and questioned when M23's leadership would be publicly linked to support for Israel.[1]

## Role in the December 2025 Uvira offensive

The Congolese government attributed the December 2025 "generalized offensive" on the Kamanyola–[[uvira|Uvira]] axis jointly to Rwanda's Defense Forces and the [[m23|M23]] rebels, reporting more than 100 killed and over 200,000 newly displaced.[2] Badlands analysis held that the capture of Uvira would leave the entire North and South Kivu provinces under effective Rwandan control, making the traceability of rare earth minerals from the region impossible.[2] Under this account, ore mined with child slave labour in the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DRC]] is smuggled into Rwanda after refining and issued forged certificates attesting to clean sourcing before being sold to Fortune 500 companies including [[apple|Apple]].[2] Rwanda under President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] was characterized as a globalist proxy and ally of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], with [[burundi|Burundi]] and the DRC forming the regional opposition to it.[2]

## March 2025 sanctions and rupture with Belgium

In March 2025 Rwanda cut diplomatic ties with its former colonial ruler Belgium, with Kigali accusing Brussels of attempting to "sustain its neo-colonial delusions," on the same day the [[european-union|European Union]] imposed sanctions on [[m23|M23]] and Rwandan officials.[3] The sanctions preceded the withdrawal of the [[alliance-fleuve-congo|Alliance Fleuve Congo]] coalition from peace talks scheduled in Angola.[3]

Badlands commentary held that Rwanda under President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] funds the M23 forces seizing towns along the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo, towns containing the processing plants where rare earth minerals mined in the region are refined before sale to corporations including Apple, Sony and Lockheed Martin.[3] Control of those towns was framed as a foothold on mineral deposits in eastern DRC estimated to be worth in excess of $24 trillion, and Kagame was described as using the EU sanctions as an excuse to abandon the negotiations.[3]

## Kabarebe sanctions and the mineral corridor (February 2025)

In February 2025 the United States sanctioned Gen. [[james-kabarebe|James Kabarebe]], a Rwandan deputy minister of foreign affairs, over violence in eastern Congo, where Rwanda-backed rebels had captured two major cities in a conflict that came to threaten the Congolese government.[4] Kigali described the sanctions as "unjustified and unfounded," asserted solidarity with Kabarebe, and argued that such action makes "no contribution toward long-term security, peace and stability for all the countries of the Great Lakes region," while insisting that securing the border with Congo is "a matter of national security, and that is our only driver."[4] Reporting noted that the designation was a blow to a state that had for years evaded international punishment over its aggression in neighbouring Congo.[4]

At the same time, the European Union had negotiated with Rwanda, agreeing to provide about $935 million in return for access to minerals such as tin, tungsten and gold, even as Congolese President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]] accused Rwanda of a "mania to be the apex predator" and offered the United States and Europe a stake in Congolese mineral wealth.[4] A report by the law firm [[amsterdam-and-partners|Amsterdam & Partners]] alleged that rare earth minerals were smuggled from the Congo into Rwanda so they could be laundered into the global supply chain as "clean" through certification bodies including the [[international-tin-supply-chain-initiative|ITSCI]] and the [[responsible-minerals-initiative|Responsible Minerals Initiative]].[4]
