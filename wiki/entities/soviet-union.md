# Soviet Union

The Soviet Union is treated in Badlands Media commentary not as an independent superpower but as an instrument of the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]], created and then used in sequence as ally, adversary, human shield and boogeyman.[1]

## Claimed British origin and instrumental use

In "The British Cabal" (August 7, 2023), [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] restated his position that England created [[communism|communism]] and that the British Cabal created the Soviet Union itself.[1] On his account the Cabal first used the Soviet Union as an ally and then converted it into an enemy, playing it off against the United States for decades, and in the early 1990s transitioned from controlling the world through Moscow to controlling it through the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]].[1] He compared the practice of switching enemies and alliances, and of "memory holing" inconvenient recent history, to the mechanics of Orwell's *1984*.[1]

Carlson argued that the United States was also fed into the same machine, though to a lesser extent, describing it as "thrown into the meat grinder to protect the British Cabal's interests."[1]

## Casualty argument

The article's principal evidentiary claim concerns Second World War military death tolls. Carlson gave approximately 417,000 American military deaths — despite the United States entering the war 27 months after Britain, France and the Soviet Union — against 383,000 British, 217,000 French, and 9 to 11 million Soviet.[1] He computed a ratio of 23 to 29 Soviet military deaths for every British military death among nominal allies.[1]

For comparison he cited Vietnam, where roughly 1,100,000 North Vietnamese soldiers died against 58,300 Americans — about 19 to one, even with what he described as vastly superior American firepower.[1] From this contrast he concluded that Soviet soldiers must have been "sent to the slaughterhouse," exemplifying the meaning of the word "expendable."[1]

## Aftermath

Carlson framed the fall of the Soviet Union and China's simultaneous emergence as an economic superpower in the 1990s as a single managed handover rather than a coincidence, noting that China's rise came through selling cheap goods below the cost at which American producers could compete — a pattern he compared directly to British dumping in the United States after the [[american-revolutionary-war|Revolutionary War]].[1] He also observed that the worldwide rise of communism followed immediately upon Britain's apparently voluntary surrender of an empire covering a quarter of the globe, which he declined to treat as coincidence.[1]
