# CNN

CNN is an American cable news network treated by Badlands Media as a core component of the [[centralized-media|centralized media]] and one of the outlets whose fortunes were temporarily revived, then abandoned, by the Trump era.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## The Trump ratings cycle

Badlands argues that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "temporarily saved the cable TV punditry single handedly": once-disinterested liberals became obsessed with politics in the age of "the great tyrant," and flocked back to staples like CNN and [[msnbc|MSNBC]], both of which had been hemorrhaging viewers before Trump.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The surge is described as "too little, too late" and, like all sugar highs, destined to wane.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Editorial limits

The article contends that "the corporate hacks at CNN" will not report on subjects covered by independent outlets such as [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]] or [[consortium-news|Consortium News]], just as Fox News avoids the difficult topics discussed on [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Although CNN and Fox appear to offer opposing perspectives, Badlands describes them as "two heads on the same wretched snake."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Reporting on Saudi nuclear cooperation (2026)

In July 2026 CNN reported, citing sources familiar with the matter and documents it reviewed, that the Trump administration had tentatively agreed to a draft [[us-saudi-civilian-nuclear-cooperation-agreement|civilian nuclear cooperation agreement]] permitting [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to enrich uranium, and that the deal remained unsigned and unsubmitted to [[congress|Congress]].[2] CNN also reported that the draft would not require Saudi adoption of the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] Additional Protocol.[2] Separately, CNN declined to air President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s primetime address on election security on its main television channel, one of the network decisions that prompted Trump to call for revoking broadcast licenses.[2]

## Immigration polling coverage (2026)

In early 2026 CNN senior data analyst [[harry-enten|Harry Enten]] discussed polling on air showing that public trust on immigration and border security had moved toward Republicans, who held a roughly five-point advantage — a reversal from 2018 — and said Democrats were in a weaker position on immigration than during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term.[3] Badlands headlined the segment as CNN admitting that Democrats were "tanking" on the immigration narrative.[3]

## Hiring of Rahm Emanuel (2025)

In February 2025 CNN hired [[rahm-emanuel|Rahm Emanuel]] — former U.S. ambassador to Japan, [[barack-obama|Obama]] chief of staff, Chicago mayor and House Democratic Caucus chairman — as a global affairs and political commentator, announcing that he "will bring his extensive knowledge of American politics and international affairs to CNN's on-air programming."[4] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the hire to argue that cable news casting runs through talent agencies, wondering whether Emanuel had become a client at WME, the agency headed by his brother [[ari-emanuel|Ari Emanuel]].[4]

CNN also produced key February 2025 reporting on [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s willingness to mediate between the Trump administration and [[iran|Iran]], and on U.S. concessions to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] ahead of Ukraine peace talks in Riyadh.[4]

## Coverage of the October 7 attacks (2023)

CNN carried two widely cited interviews in the immediate aftermath of the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 attacks]]. Former [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] international spokesperson [[jonathan-conricus|Jonathan Conricus]] told the network that "the entire system failed" and called the day Israel's "Pearl Harbor type of moment."[5] In an interview aired that Sunday, Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] said, "We have not yet seen evidence that Iran directed or was behind this particular attack, but there is certainly a long relationship."[5]

## Sudan evacuation reporting (2023)

In April 2023, CNN reported on the mounting anger of Americans stranded in [[sudan|Sudan]] who felt abandoned by their government as fighting between the [[sudanese-armed-forces|Sudanese Armed Forces]] and the [[rapid-support-forces|Rapid Support Forces]] escalated.[6] The network spoke with relatives of the "dozens" of Americans seeking to leave, who said the [[state-department|State Department]] had provided "barely any assistance," and quoted Muna Daoud describing herself as "incredibly shocked and disgusted by the American lackluster response."[6] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the story less as a factual dispute than as a narrative deployment notable for accusing the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration of a second failed evacuation.[6]

## John Griffin conviction

In June 2023, former CNN television producer [[john-griffin|John Griffin]] was sentenced to more than 19 years in prison, plus 15 years of supervised release, for luring a nine-year-old girl into illegal sexual acts at his Ludlow, Vermont ski house.[7] Badlands coverage treated the case as emblematic of what [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described as an engineered "epidemic of abnormal sexual habits" cultivated by elite networks rather than an isolated crime.[7]

## Biden Northern Ireland security breach (2023)

In April 2023 CNN reported that police had launched an investigation after a document outlining details of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s trip to Northern Ireland was found on the street by a member of the public.[8] The report cited the [[police-service-of-northern-ireland|Police Service of Northern Ireland]] as saying it was "aware of a security breach" and took the safety of visiting dignitaries, the public and its own staff "extremely seriously."[8] CNN noted the document appeared to outline police deployments for the visit, which began with events in Belfast marking a quarter-century of the [[good-friday-agreement|Good Friday Agreement]], and that the incident was first reported by BBC Radio Ulster.[8]

## 2023 Trump town hall

On May 10, 2023, CNN aired a 70-minute town hall with former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] before New Hampshire GOP primary voters at [[saint-anselm-college|Saint Anselm College]], moderated by "CNN This Morning" anchor [[kaitlan-collins|Kaitlan Collins]].[9] [[breitbart|Breitbart]] columnist [[john-nolte|John Nolte]] argued the broadcast "might've delivered the final killshot" to the network, which he said had already been taking a ten count in the ratings after averaging 587,000 primetime viewers in April 2023.[9] Nolte described the event as a catastrophe for what he called the "Hoax Network," citing the audible frustration of anchor [[jake-tapper|Jake Tapper]], who said afterward that Trump's "lies keep coming fast and furious."[9]

During the town hall Trump declined to say who should prevail in Russia's war against Ukraine, saying "I want everybody to stop dying" and pledging to have the conflict settled in 24 hours; pressed by Collins on whether he wanted Ukraine to deter Russia, he said he does not "think in terms of winning and losing" but "in terms of getting it settled so we stop killing all these people."[9] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the exposure of CNN and the media more broadly as escalatory and inflammatory warmongers would not be lost on a bipartisan viewing audience, calling it a win in the "Mind War."[9]

## Egg price coverage (May 2025)

Consumer price data released in May 2025 showed egg prices had dropped 12.7 percent the previous month, the biggest monthly decline since 1984, after the cost of eggs had risen in 17 of the previous 19 months.[10] CNN's David Goldman wrote that "for months, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has falsely claimed that egg prices are tumbling. It wasn't true then, but it's true now," noting Agriculture Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke Rollins]]' more conservative estimate that prices would normalize in the summer and disputing Trump's claim of a 93-94% decline since taking office.[10] Goldman conceded that consumer egg prices "were, indeed, falling sharply" when Trump made his remarks, since Consumer Price Index data was not yet available to confirm or deny them.[10] The Federalist argued that CNN nonetheless still claimed the "timing of his claim" was wrong a few sentences later, calling the network's handling of the data a struggle to process good news.[10]

## Reporting on CIA espionage threats (February 2025)

In February 2025 CNN, citing unnamed sources, reported that some senior [[cia|CIA]] officers were "quietly discussing" how [[doge|DOGE]]-driven dismissals "risk creating a group of disgruntled former employees who might be motivated to take what they know to a foreign intelligence service."[11] Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] responded by describing the network as the agency's "propaganda arm," saying the officers were "exposing themselves essentially by making this indirect threat using their propaganda arm through CNN that they've used over and over and over again."[11] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the report as an implicit threat that would amount to treason if acted upon.[11]

## Reporting on the Strzok and Page settlements (2024)

In July 2024 CNN obtained and reported on the settlement agreements under which the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] agreed to pay former [[fbi|FBI]] officials [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] and [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] a combined $2 million — $1.2 million and $800,000 respectively — over the department's 2018 release of their text messages criticizing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[12] The network reported the filings were finalized on Friday, July 26, 2024, that the plaintiffs had alleged a Privacy Act violation, and that the department declined to comment.[12]

CNN also conducted one of the three major post-withdrawal polls cited in late-July 2024 coverage of the presidential race, showing Trump three points ahead of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], down from a six-point lead over [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] after the debate.[12]

## 2023 Trump town hall

In early May 2023 CNN announced that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — who as president frequently derided the network as "fake news" and never granted an interview to a CNN journalist — had agreed to appear at a live town hall forum for CNN viewers in New Hampshire.[13] The [[2023-cnn-trump-new-hampshire-town-hall|event]] was scheduled for May 10 at St. Anselm's College in Goffstown, to be televised at 9 p.m. Eastern with [[kaitlan-collins|Kaitlan Collins]] moderating, with questions from Republicans and undeclared voters planning to vote in the 2024 GOP primary.[13] Coverage described the booking as a big deal for a slumping CNN as it worked to get beyond the era of former leader Jeff Zucker, and reported that network executives had made a compelling pitch to Trump.[13]

## Rehiring of Brian Stelter and 2024 Russian influence coverage

In September 2024 CNN announced the return of [[brian-stelter|Brian Stelter]] as "Chief Media Analyst," two years after he was let go during the network's reconstruction phase, with Stelter again helming the *Reliable Sources* newsletter.[14] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the rehiring called into question the strategic aims of a network that is consistently at the bottom of the prime-time cable news ratings, asking whether CNN had restructured only to double down on its prior, failed strategy.[14]

CNN also carried the Biden administration's September 4, 2024 announcement of sweeping actions against alleged Russian election-influence efforts, including criminal charges against two Russian nationals, sanctions on ten individuals and entities, and the seizure of 32 internet domains.[14] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that even amid what he called deafening fear-mongering about election interference, CNN was careful to state that "there is no evidence of successful efforts — foreign or domestic — to swing an American election by changing vote tallies," which he read as the reason officials consistently used the softer term "influence."[14]

## October 2025 Stephen Miller interview

On October 6, 2025, CNN anchor [[boris-sanchez|Boris Sanchez]] pressed White House deputy chief of staff for policy [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] on "CNN News Central" about whether the administration's immigration enforcement amounted to racial profiling, citing Illinois Governor [[jb-pritzker|JB Pritzker]]'s claims; Miller dismissed the query as "a dumb question."[15] [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] commentary argued that the [[white-house|White House]]'s continued willingness to supply guests to CNN and similar outlets legitimizes and financially sustains networks that would otherwise be failing.[15]

## Mass shooting statistics controversy (2023)

On March 6, 2023, CNN reported that "the U.S. has surpassed 100 mass shootings in 2023," using data from the [[gun-violence-archive|Gun Violence Archive]] and noting that America had "reached the grim number by the first week of March."[16] Critics argued that both CNN and the Archive had departed from the standard definition of a mass shooting, which the [[rand-corporation|Rand Corporation]] traces to the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 1980s definition of a "mass murderer" as someone who "kills four or more people in a single incident (not including himself)."[16] Under the counting method used, an incident with no fatalities still qualified, so drive-by shootings, gang hits, homicides and double homicides were relabeled and tallied as mass shootings.[16]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that CNN and allied institutions would rather Americans face fentanyl in their communities than keep a firearm at home, arguing that an unabated flow of drugs and criminals across the border served agendas including depopulation, launderable federal funding for [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary cities]], an endless drug war, and the prison industrial complex.[16] He added that groups opposed to the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] have long manipulated crime statistics, most cleverly by folding suicides and accidents in with homicides.[16]
