# Christopher Miller

Christopher Miller served as Acting Secretary of Defense in the closing weeks of the first administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], including on January 6, 2021.[1]

## January 6, 2021 deployment delay

In his suppressed January 28, 2022 transcribed interview with the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]], White House Deputy Chief of Staff [[anthony-ornato|Anthony Ornato]] described White House frustration with Miller's slow deployment of assistance on the afternoon of January 6, 2021.[1] That account cut against the committee's public narrative that the White House had made no effort to secure the Capitol, and formed part of the evidence the committee did not release.[1]

## Allegations of committee intimidation

In March 2024, Miller alleged that the now-defunct [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]] had threatened to "make his life hell" if he continued to claim publicly that Donald Trump had authorized [[national-guard|National Guard]] deployment on January 6, 2021.[2] Miller, a former Director of the [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]], said he was "fearful" of hostile measures used by the panel as it sought to stop him from describing events that did not align with its final report.[2] He said members used intimidating tactics, warning that they would bring him in for hours of testimony if he kept defending Trump's actions in television interviews, and that at the time he lacked the resources to keep fighting the committee or to endure further depositions for speaking to the media.[2]
