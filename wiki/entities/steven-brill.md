# Steven Brill

Steven Brill is an American journalist, media entrepreneur and co-founder and co-CEO of [[newsguard|NewsGuard]], the browser-extension venture that assigns "trust scores" to online news outlets.[1] Badlands Media's "The Information Imperium" presents Brill as an emblem of establishment media credentialing: a Yale Law graduate who, in the article's framing, "spent decades cozying up to the elite" before positioning himself as an arbiter of what counts as credible information for the general public.[1]

## Role at NewsGuard

Brill co-founded NewsGuard with [[l-gordon-crovitz|L. Gordon Crovitz]], the former publisher of the Wall Street Journal.[1] The article characterises both men as "pedigreed up to their eyeballs," with deep ties to corporate media, the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], and as general defenders of the incumbent power structure.[1] Under their direction NewsGuard rates websites against nine weighted criteria grouped under "credibility" and "transparency," issuing scores from 0 to 100 and displaying green check marks or red exclamation points to users of its extension.[1]

## The Hunter Biden laptop episode

Brill figures directly in the article's central example of NewsGuard's alleged narrative policing. When the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] published its October 2020 story on [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden's laptop]], Brill called the material a "hoax" in interviews, aligning with the establishment consensus that framed the story as Russian disinformation.[1] After outlets including the New York Times conceded the laptop's authenticity in 2022, NewsGuard revised some ratings — downgrading [[the-daily-beast|The Daily Beast]] from 87.5/100 to 57/100 — while Axios, BuzzFeed News, USA Today and the Washington Post retained perfect scores despite having voiced the same skepticism.[1] The article treats this asymmetry as evidence that Brill's ratings operation enforces credibility selectively rather than consistently.[1]

## Assessment in the article

"The Information Imperium" situates Brill within a broader "information imperium" — an apparatus rooted in the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and [[usaid|USAID]] — in which nominally private ventures perform narrative-management functions once handled covertly.[1] In that framing, Brill is not depicted as an intelligence operative but as a well-connected media insider whose product supplies the commercial and reputational enforcement mechanism for an official consensus.[1]
