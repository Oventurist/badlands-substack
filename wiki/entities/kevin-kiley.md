# Kevin Kiley

Kevin Kiley is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from California (R-CA) who took part in the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]]'s September 2023 oversight hearing with Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]].[1]

## Questioning of Merrick Garland

During the roughly five-hour hearing held on Wednesday, September 20, 2023, Kiley pressed Garland on the origins of the Justice Department's controversial memorandum directing federal law enforcement attention toward threats at school board meetings.[1] According to [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], Kiley "pinned Garland down on the inception of his infamous parent-targeting memo," with the Attorney General becoming flustered and citing mainstream media coverage as the basis for scrutiny of conservative parents at school board meetings.[1] Garland further confirmed under Kiley's questioning that he had not spoken with [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] before the memo was issued.[1]

Kiley's line of questioning was described as one of several viral moments generated by Republican members of the committee during the hearing, alongside exchanges led by [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] and [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]].[1]

## Medicaid eligibility bill (2025)

In January 2025, Kiley introduced a bill that would prevent states such as California from allowing illegal immigrants to enroll in [[medicaid|Medicaid]], estimating that the measure would save California $6.5 billion per year.[2] California had expanded Medicaid enrollment in January 2024 to include all illegal immigrants.[2]

"This common sense legislation ensures that taxpayer-funded healthcare benefits are preserved for those who lawfully qualify, prioritizing the needs of American citizens and legal residents while promoting fairness and fiscal responsibility," Kiley said in a statement on the bill.[2] He also noted that many Americans already enrolled in Medicaid — a taxpayer-funded public healthcare program for low-income individuals — were struggling to find doctors.[2]

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the legislation common sense given the insolvency of American entitlement programs, while arguing that it was shameful the policy objective required legislation at all and that accountability, not merely legal change, was owed by lawmakers who had saddled Americans with the cost of the world's healthcare.[2]
