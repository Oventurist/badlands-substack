# China-U.S. Exchange Foundation

The China-U.S. Exchange Foundation (CUSEF) is a Hong Kong–based organisation devoted to promoting relations between [[china]] and the United States. In the Badlands Media essay [[and-iran-iran-so-far-away|"And Iran, Iran So Far Away"]] it is presented not as a neutral cultural-exchange body but as a conduit for Chinese Communist Party influence inside American foreign policy institutions.

## Role in the corpus

The article raises CUSEF in the course of arguing that senior figures behind the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]] were financially entangled with Beijing. Citing a Daily Caller report, it states that [[william-burns]] and the [[carnegie-endowment-for-international-peace]] "made millions off of China." The quoted passage says that while serving as president of the Carnegie Endowment's board, Burns welcomed onto the board the Chinese businessman [[zhang-yichen]], described as having links to the Center for China and Globalization and the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference, and that Yichen donated between $750,000 and $1.5 million to Carnegie.

The same passage states that Carnegie "also received millions of dollars from another CCP-backed organization called China-U.S. Exchange Foundation (CUSEF)," which the source describes as "responsible for the United Front (a group that advances CCP interests) work in the United States." According to the article, the money was given to build the Carnegie–Tsinghua Center in Beijing.

For the author these transactions matter because Burns went on to become director of the [[cia]] under [[joe-biden]], the administration that within two months of taking office permitted China to sign a reported $400 billion oil, gas and infrastructure agreement with [[iran]] and to draw the country into the [[belt-and-road-initiative]]. CUSEF thus functions in the essay as one link in a chain connecting Chinese state-aligned money, the American think-tank world, and the policy reversals that followed [[donald-trump]]'s withdrawal from the Iran Deal.
