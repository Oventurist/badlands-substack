# BRICS

BRICS is the grouping of major non-Western economies that Badlands Media commentary presents as the principal challenger to dollar hegemony and the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Gold-backed currency initiative

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] notes that the fifty-year-old American statute barring payment of foreign entities in gold — enacted under [[richard-nixon|Nixon]] after France demanded gold repayment — was quietly repealed a couple of years ago, precisely when the BRICS nations began discussing the creation of their own gold-backed currency for international transactions in place of the US dollar.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He calls the timing ironic and invites readers to consider whether it is coincidental that these are the same countries whose leaders [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met with in 2017.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Rejection of the dollar system

Carlson argues it is unsurprising that BRICS states no longer wish to participate, describing the US government as "like a crack dealer who smokes half his supply and charges double for the crack he sells to make up the difference."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Under the petrodollar arrangement the whole world, not merely Americans, paid for money created by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] through [[inflation|inflation]] — and that money was often used to terrorize and control the same foreign nations funding it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Parallel financial system and digital currency

In "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", Joe Lange argues that the BRICS have already created a separate and parallel global financial system no longer dependent on the U.S. dollar or on the [[cabal|cabal]], striking bilateral trade deals denominated in their own currencies.[2] He predicts that as the dollar continues to devalue, more countries will follow.[2]

Citing The Cradle, the article reports that nineteen nations had expressed interest in joining the emerging-markets bloc of Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, according to Anil Sooklal, South Africa's ambassador to the group; among the formal applicants were Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Algeria, Egypt, Bahrain, and Iran.[2] Bloomberg analysis cited in the same report projected that by 2028 the G7 would account for just 27.8 percent of the global economy against 35 percent for BRICS, after both blocs contributed equally to global growth in 2020.[2]

Alexander Babakov, Deputy Chairman of Russia's State Duma, was reported to have revealed that BRICS was developing a "new currency" to be presented at the bloc's August summit in South Africa — a currency Lange says will be backed by gold along with other commodities outside the cabal's control.[2] He credits China with starting the movement through the [[shanghai-petroleum-and-natural-gas-exchange|Shanghai Petroleum and Natural Gas Exchange]], and Russia under [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] with leading the shift to gold-backed money and to a distributed-ledger cross-border payment system.[2] In this account BRICS is the force compelling Western central banks to develop [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDCs]], and the parallel system it has built is the destination the world will move into when the fiat order collapses.[2]


## Saudi engagement, June 2026

Saudi Arabia's turn as guest country at the [[2026-st-petersburg-international-economic-forum|2026 St. Petersburg International Economic Forum]] was described as reflecting shared Russian and Saudi priorities on sovereignty and a multipolar international order, alongside the kingdom's pragmatic engagement with BRICS structures while maintaining flexibility on regional diplomacy.[3]

## Expansion announcement, August 2023

Ahead of the bloc's August 22–24, 2023 summit in [[south-africa|South Africa]], Anil Sooklal, South Africa's ambassador to the five-nation group, said in a lecture at the University of KwaZulu-Natal that heads of state from [[brazil|Brazil]], [[russia|Russia]], [[india|India]], [[china|China]] and South Africa would make a pronouncement on enlargement, marking what officials described as a significant change in the global order.[4] Twenty-two nations had formally asked to become full-time members and more than twenty others had submitted informal requests.[4]

China favored a rapid expansion of the bloc, which would require consensus among members, but encountered opposition from India — which wanted strict rules on how and when other nations could move closer to the group without formal enlargement — and from Brazil, which was wary of alienating the United States and the European Union.[4]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the expansion as part of a "controlled implosion of the Cabal economy," in which parties unenthusiastic about the latest globalist banking system update pursue an alternative.[4] He described himself as optimistic but trepidatious, noting that operational security had left key details undisclosed and that historically better monetary systems — [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]], the Lincoln-Greenback, gold-backed currencies and colonial scrip among them — were repeatedly hijacked, ended, or manipulated into oblivion because the public did not understand money.[4]

## Johannesburg summit outcome, August 24, 2023

At the close of the Johannesburg summit, the Gulf oil powers [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] were formally invited to become members, marking the bloc's first expansion in more than a decade at a moment when China and Russia had envisioned BRICS as fundamentally an anti-Western bloc of developing nations.[9] South African President [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]] announced that "the membership will take effect from the first of January, 2024," and that Argentina, Egypt, Ethiopia and Iran would additionally be added to the fold the following year.[9]

Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] hailed the enlargement as "historic," saying it would "inject new impetus into the BRICS cooperation mechanism and further strengthen the power of world peace and development."[9] Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] congratulated the incoming members by video message — "I would like to congratulate the new members who will work in a full-scale format next year" — and pledged to continue "expanding the influence of BRICS in the world."[9] Indian Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] likewise welcomed the expansion as strengthening the bloc.[9]

Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Prince Faisal bin Farhan]] said the kingdom's "special, strategic relations with the BRICS nations promotes common principles, most importantly the firm belief in the principle of respect for sovereignty, independence and non-interference in internal affairs."[9] Speaking before the conference, he vowed that the kingdom would be a "secure and reliable energy provider," and noted that total bilateral trade between Riyadh and BRICS countries had exceeded $160 billion in 2022.[9]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that "BRICS has thrown down the gauntlet," arguing that while a gold-backed currency would likely still come later, the keystone to toppling Western hegemony had always rested on Saudi Arabia's decision.[9] With Riyadh's intent codified, he held, the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s military, financial and energy power projection had received "a stunning and irreversible blow."[9] He described the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] as existing to serve itself by subjugating nations that defy the [[petrodollar|US Petrodollar]], and the petrodollar itself as divorced from production and GDP, sustained by a forced market in which foreign nations must buy Saudi oil in dollars.[9] The most understated pillar, he argued, was energy projection — "real power" — with the enlarged BRICS set to control more than 60 percent of global energy markets even as the [[joe-biden|Biden administration]] drew down the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve and declined to restart domestic drilling permits.[9] He concluded: "The [[multipolar-world|Multi-Polar World]] isn't coming. It's here."[9]

## Cited as a Russia-China coordination channel (2023)

In his November 20, 2023 congratulatory letter to Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] named BRICS — together with the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organisation]] — as a multilateral mechanism through which Moscow and Beijing coordinate positions on major international issues and promote "a more just and democratic international order."[5] The letter came days after Xi's San Francisco meeting with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and framed the Russia-China partnership as being at the highest level in its history.[5]

## January 2024 expansion

On January 1, 2024, [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]], Egypt, Iran and Ethiopia formally joined BRICS, doubling the bloc's membership to ten alongside original members Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa.[6] Analysts said the addition of the Arab world's two largest economies was expected to open new investment opportunities for both states while boosting the group's global influence.[6] Ullas Rao, assistant professor of finance at Edinburgh Business School of Heriot-Watt University in Dubai, said the expansion "augurs extremely well amid ongoing geopolitical and economic challenges confronting the world economy," noting that Saudi Arabia and the UAE are among the richest countries on a per-capita basis and home to the biggest sovereign wealth funds, creating "enormous growth opportunities through investments, trade and commerce."[6]

## 2024 Kazan summit
The group's 2024 summit, chaired by Russia and held in Kazan in October, drew representatives from 36 countries — including China, India and Iran — over three days, and was described as the largest international gathering hosted by Vladimir Putin since the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine.[7] One of its main aims was to speed up ways to reduce the number of dollar transactions and thereby mitigate the United States' ability to use the threat of sanctions to impose its political will.[7]

UN Secretary-General António Guterres attended, as he had the previous year, a decision that infuriated many in the West given the ICC's outstanding arrest warrant for Putin.[7] Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman was absent; Badlands attributed this to the kingdom intentionally holding back from formally accepting its invitation to join BRICS, possibly awaiting the right moment to help end the fiat U.S. dollar.[7]

## South Africa's alignment in Badlands commentary (May 2025)

In commentary on the May 2025 Trump–Ramaphosa White House confrontation, Badlands noted that [[south-africa|South Africa]] — the nation accused in the exchange — is a BRICS member and therefore has a deepening alliance with Vladimir Putin and Russia, while also being the petitioner against Israel at the [[international-court-of-justice|ICJ]].[8]

## September 2025 virtual trade summit

China confirmed in September 2025 that President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] would take part in a virtual BRICS summit convened by Brazil to discuss [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s trade policy, with China's Foreign Ministry stating that Xi would attend the BRICS Leaders' online summit in Beijing on September 8 and "deliver an important speech."[10] Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] also took part, Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said, according to the Russian news agency Tass.[10] India's Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] skipped the meeting, sending a senior representative instead.[10]

Brazil's Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva called the gathering to discuss not only Trump's trade tariffs but also to rally fellow heads of major emerging-market nations behind multilateralism.[10] The bloc, which includes South Africa, had increasingly drawn Trump's ire as it expanded with new members from Asia and the Middle East; in July 2025 the U.S. president threatened additional tariffs on any country aligning itself with the group's "anti-American policies."[10]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump's apparent public contradictions regarding the growing confidence of the "New Axis" represented by Xi, Putin and others were not contradictions at all, asserting that "the Sovereign Alliance is real. And it's winning."[10]

## Extraordinary virtual meeting on Gaza (November 2023)

On November 21, 2023, BRICS convened an extraordinary virtual meeting devoted to the Gaza crisis.[11] Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] used the session to call for an immediate ceasefire and suspension of hostilities by all parties to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, for safe passage for humanitarian relief, for an end to the forced relocation of Gaza's civilian population, and for the restoration of water, energy and electricity supplies to the enclave.[11] He argued that only the [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]] and the establishment of a sovereign, independent State of Palestine could produce lasting peace and stability in West Asia.[11] Indian External Affairs Minister [[s-jaishankar|S. Jaishankar]], attending in place of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, condemned the killing of civilians but stopped short of echoing the Chinese demand for a ceasefire.[11]

## 2023 Johannesburg summit

At the [[2023-brics-johannesburg-summit|2023 summit in Johannesburg]], which opened on August 22, 2023, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] addressed the bloc remotely while Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] stood in for him in the family photo, because the International Criminal Court warrant and the Rome Statute would have obliged South Africa to arrest him.[12] Putin said [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] was "gaining momentum" and irreversible, claimed the five members had reached 26% of global GDP and 31% of the world economy by purchasing power parity against 30% for the [[g7|G7]], and cited a sixfold increase in mutual investment over the prior decade and 20% of global exports.[12] He denounced "illegitimate sanctions" and the "unlawful freezing of sovereign states' assets" while pledging increased fuel, food and fertilizer supplies to the Global South, as [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] urged the bloc to become a geopolitical rival to the G7.[12] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted rumors of a gold-backed BRICS currency and argued the summit represented an attack on the U.S. [[petrodollar|petrodollar]].[12]
