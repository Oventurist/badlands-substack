# Al Jazeera

Al Jazeera is the Qatar-based international news network. During the US-Israeli war on [[iran|Iran]], it served as a primary outlet for Iranian official statements and for running liveblog coverage of the conflict.[1]

## Coverage of the 2026 Iran war

In an interview with Al Jazeera, Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] confirmed that messages had been exchanged with the United States while insisting that Tehran was not engaged in negotiations with Washington and had "zero" trust in the US.[1]

The network's liveblog reported continued US-Israeli strikes on Iranian infrastructure, including civilian sites such as steel plants and ports, along with regional fallout that included a Kuwaiti oil tanker struck in Dubai port and the killing of three UN troops.[1] The same coverage relayed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s claim that the US campaign against Iran could end in "two to three weeks," and Araghchi's statement that Iran was prepared to defend itself and to ensure safe passage through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] for non-hostile vessels.[1]

## Palestinian Authority ban (January 2025)

In early January 2025 the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] temporarily banned Al Jazeera from operating in the West Bank, claiming the broadcaster had played a role in inciting unrest and "stirring strife" there.[2] The decision was reported by the Palestinian news agency WAFA, which cited the PA's culture, interior and communications ministries as saying the Qatar-based network's coverage had "deceived" and "incited division" among its audience in its reporting on recent clashes in the Jenin refugee camp.[2] In December 2024 Al Jazeera had reported on a raid by PA forces against a splinter group known as the Jenin Brigades; the camp, established in 1953 for Palestinians who fled or were expelled by the Israelis, has long been a stronghold of resistance to Israel and houses an estimated 11,000 to 22,000 people.[2] The ban was described as temporary, with no end date announced.[2]

## Origins and Badlands analysis

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued the ban required nuance rather than dismissal as simple authoritarianism, and traced the network's history: Al Jazeera launched on November 1, 1996 under the ruling family of Qatar, the House of Thani, immediately after the Saudi government moved to close the BBC Arabic station to thwart a documentary on Sharia law.[2] The new network hired the former BBC Arabic staff, began with six hours of daily programming and expanded to a 24-hour broadcast in 1999; after 9/11, demand for English-language coverage produced English shows, websites and offices in cities such as London, with the stated goal of connecting with Western audiences in close cooperation with the [[bbc|BBC]].[2]

He further argued that during the 2011 Arab Spring, Al Jazeera played a critical role in persuading the Arab public that a genuine populist revolution was underway when, in his account, it was an Operation Gladio-style [[color-revolution|color revolution]] led by the Muslim Brotherhood with British intelligence, NATO and likely [[mossad|Mossad]] involvement.[2] When Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman initiated the 2017 embargo of Qatar in coordination with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and other Arab partners, Al Jazeera was banned in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan and Egypt for reasons similar to those cited by the PA; Israel banned it the previous year on the same stated grounds of inciting violence with misinformation.[2] He concluded that while free speech and free press are cornerstones of American society, the actor here was the Palestinian Authority, governing a territory under psychological-warfare pressure designed to destabilize it and escalate conflict with Israel.[2]

## Badlands characterization

Badlands Media describes Al Jazeera as a [[qatar|Qatar]]-based outlet purportedly aligned with the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] — which, in the outlet's framing, "probably means it's connected to London, too."[3] It holds that the network became "the main cheerleader for the color revolutions" during the [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]] in 2011, when it became obvious that Qatar was connected to the orchestrated uprisings, generating lasting bad blood between Doha and the rest of the Middle East.[3] Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] justified the 2017 embargo of Qatar in part on the grounds that the country allowed the [[cia|CIA]] and ISIS to conduct psychological warfare operations through outlets such as Al Jazeera.[3] Qatari bankrolling of the network was also among the grievances Republican lawmakers raised against Doha ahead of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s September 2024 Mar-a-Lago meeting with Qatari leaders.[3]
