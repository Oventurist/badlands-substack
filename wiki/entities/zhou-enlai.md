# Zhou Enlai

Zhou Enlai was the Premier of the People's Republic of China who, in Matthew Ehret's Badlands account, originated the strategic program that allowed China to escape the "producer-consumer" role assigned to it by Western technocrats.[1] Ehret writes that China's leaders under Zhou's direction, and that of his disciple [[deng-xiaoping|Deng Xiaoping]], possessed a much longer-term strategic perspective than their Western partners imagined.[1]

## The Four Modernizations

Zhou first enunciated the [[four-modernizations|Four Modernizations]] mandate — industry, agriculture, national defense, and science and technology — as early as 1963, and restated the program in January 1976, weeks before his death.[1] The program was later institutionalized at the July 6, 1978 [[state-council-of-china|State Council]] Forum on the "Principles to Guide the Four Modernizations," which drew on the findings of exploratory missions led by economist [[gu-mu|Gu Mu]] to Japan, Hong Kong and Western Europe.[1]

Ehret contrasts Zhou's nationalist conception of the Four Modernizations — aimed at full-spectrum economic sovereignty and the cultivation of creative scientific capacities — with the information-society reinterpretation later advanced by [[zhao-ziyang|Zhao Ziyang]] under the influence of [[george-soros|George Soros]] and the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[1]
