# World Economic Forum

The **World Economic Forum (WEF)** appears in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as the institutional home of what contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] calls the "leading oligarchists" of the present era.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

In "On Ted Kaczynski and False Prophets" (25 October 2025), Ehret argues that [[ted-kaczynski|Theodore Kaczynski]] shared common axioms with the oligarchists running the World Economic Forum and the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] today, treating the Forum less as a business conference than as the coordinating body of an anti-industrial project.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) This framing links the WEF to the broader movements Ehret describes elsewhere as designed to induce humans to participate in the willful self-destruction of industrial civilization, including [[transhumanism|transhumanism]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

## Strategic partnership with McKinsey

Badlands Media's labor coverage identifies [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]] as the strategic partner of the World Economic Forum, describing the consultancy as essentially the world's strategy firm whose influence touches every aspect of human life.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Through that partnership the Forum is tied to the [[future-of-work|future of work]] agenda — the projected displacement of up to 375 million workers, 14 percent of the global workforce, by 2030 — and to the conditions the article says underlay the 2023 [[summer-of-strikes-2023|Summer of Strikes]], framed as groundwork for the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] and a New World Order.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Position on AI bias

In a June 2023 statement on artificial intelligence and robotics, the World Economic Forum wrote that [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] "can produce biased outcomes as its algorithms are based on design choices made by humans that are rarely value-neutral," but argued that recognizing AI's inclination to perpetuate inequities may give an advantage in the fight for fairness, and that analysing the common characteristics of inequitable outcomes and putting sensitive information back into datasets can help address AI bias.[4]

Badlands Media's July 2023 essay cites this alongside statements by the [[united-nations|United Nations]], [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]], and [[pwc|PwC]] as evidence that no institutional pause on AI development occurred, and argues that as long as AI is developed according to what it calls the communist principles of intersectionality and equity, the global governors are content to proceed at full speed.[4]

## Davos 2026 and the Board of Peace

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] scheduled a high-profile signing ceremony for his [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] during the Forum's annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, set for Thursday at 10:30 a.m. and intended to formally launch phase two of his Gaza peace efforts.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the move as Trump "launching a thousand ships" against the Forum, continuing a campaign of hyperbolic aggression that sowed narrative chaos and dread among the global elite.[5] He characterized the resulting confrontation as a professional-wrestling spectacle with Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] headlining against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the WEF.[5]

## Resignation of Børge Brende (2026)

On 26 February 2026, Forum president and chief executive [[borge-brende|Børge Brende]] stepped down after scrutiny of his past interactions with convicted sex offender [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[6] Documents and releases tied to the "Epstein files" showed that Brende had dined with Epstein multiple times and exchanged communications in 2018–2019, though he said he had been unaware of Epstein's criminal history.[6]

An internal review found no further concerns beyond what had already been disclosed, but Brende said he resigned so the Forum could continue its work "without distractions," and [[alois-zwinggi|Alois Zwinggi]] was appointed interim chief executive pending a permanent successor.[6] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry observed that the episode began three weeks earlier when Brende requested an internal investigation into himself following the release of the Epstein files, and placed it within a broader pattern of European elites quitting their jobs, being arrested, and attempting suicide.[6]

## 2026 annual meeting in Davos

The 2026 annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, became a stage for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s assertion of American leverage over Europe.[7] Addressing a packed audience on Wednesday, January 21, 2026, Trump boasted about his first-year accomplishments and American exceptionalism, renewed calls for the United States to acquire [[greenland|Greenland]], threatened tariffs on Europe, and then announced that he and [[nato|NATO]] Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] had agreed on a "framework of a future deal," leading him to drop the threatened tariffs.[7] In a widely circulated moment he said the Russia-Ukraine war "wouldn't have started if the 2020 US presidential election weren't rigged" and that "people will soon be prosecuted for what they did."[7]

The meeting's agenda featured extensive discussion of artificial intelligence's economic impact alongside infrastructure constraints such as electricity supply and grid capacity for large-scale compute.[7] [[blackrock|BlackRock]] CEO [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]] used his opening remarks to warn that rapid AI growth could harm working and professional classes and to frame AI as a major stress test for capitalism, while Trump said the United States was "going heavy into nuclear."[7] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the 2026 forum's shift of fear target from climate to AI as predictable, and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Fink's pivot as part of an engineered narrative sequence supporting a [[nuclear-renaissance|nuclear renaissance]].[7]

## Davos 2026: "A Spirit of Dialogue"

The 2026 annual meeting, convened under the theme "A Spirit of Dialogue," drew more than 60 heads of state and government, who addressed deepening geopolitical fragmentation, trade disputes, and the erosion of multilateralism, with the Trump administration's pursuit of [[greenland|Greenland]] emerging as the central flashpoint.[8] European leaders presented a united front against U.S. tariff threats imposed on eight nations that had backed [[denmark|Denmark]]'s sovereignty over the Arctic territory.[8] French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] warned of a "shift towards a world without rules" and urged the EU to consider its [[anti-coercion-instrument|anti-coercion instrument]] for the first time; European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] called the measures a "mistake between allies"; Belgian Prime Minister [[bart-de-wever|Bart De Wever]] said Europe could not be a "miserable slave" to U.S. demands; and Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] described a "rupture, not a transition" in the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]].[8] Danish Prime Minister [[mette-frederiksen|Mette Frederiksen]] warned that "the worst may still be ahead," while U.S. Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] sought to de-escalate and China's Vice Premier [[he-lifeng|He Lifeng]] observed that trade wars have "no winners" as markets tumbled.[8]

Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] opened for the U.S. delegation the day before Trump's address, telling the assembled elite plainly that globalism has failed.[8] [[blackrock|BlackRock]] chief [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]] argued that the current financial system was doomed and that tokenization was the future of banking and of all asset classes — a position echoed by the chief executive of [[ubs|UBS]] — and said the energy demands of AI and large datacenters required the Forum to reevaluate its position on solar and wind, which he called insufficiently reliable.[8]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] traced the shift in the Forum's mood to the 2024 annual meeting, where [[kevin-roberts|Kevin Roberts]] of the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] predicted a populist reckoning, followed by a 2025 meeting dominated by low energy, the resignation of [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], and the crisis of public trust in global institutions.[8] Having attended by video in 2025, [[donald-trump|Trump]] appeared in person in 2026, and Ashe argued that whereas Davos power had always been wielded by global central planners, this year the nations appeared to hold it, making the event a contest over who sets the rules and which institutions survive into the [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]].[8] The Forum was also the planned venue for the signing ceremony of Trump's [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]].[8]

## Davos 2026 Annual Meeting

The forum's [[davos-2026|2026 Annual Meeting]], scheduled for January 19–24, 2026, was expected to draw more than 3,000 leaders including nearly 60 heads of state under the theme "A spirit of dialogue."[9] Interim Co-Chair [[andre-hoffmann|Andre Hoffmann]] said key discussions would address growth, geopolitics, innovation and climate, stressing public-private cooperation and describing the forum's aim as pursuing pragmatic pathways to sustainable prosperity without harming the planet; artificial intelligence and its governance were also on the agenda.[9] The anticipated attendance of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was cast by the forum as consistent with its continued support for U.S. leadership on global issues, and the meeting was the first held without the leadership of founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]].[9] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the gathering within an ongoing war against the [[media-industrial-complex|media industrial complex]], the administrative state, the central banking cartel and the [[globalist-hegemon|globalist hegemon]].[9]

## Davos 2023 and Mexico

For its 2023 annual gathering in Davos, Switzerland, the WEF invited [[samuel-garcia-sepulveda|Samuel García Sepúlveda]], the 35-year-old Governor of the Mexican border state of [[nuevo-leon|Nuevo León]], who was the only Mexican politician in attendance even though other Mexican citizens represented banks and oil companies such as PEMEX.[10] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the Forum as one of several globalist front groups and wrote that founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] was "itching to penetrate another cabinet," while conceding that attendance alone does not make a politician a villain — citing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2018 and 2020 appearances, in which Trump warned against the "perennial prophets of doom" shortly before [[greta-thunberg|Greta Thunberg]] took the stage.[10]

## Davos 2025 and the Inauguration Conflict

The World Economic Forum's 2025 annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland opened on January 20, 2025 — the same day as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inauguration, which began at noon Eastern.[11] The overlap forced billionaires and chief executives invited to both to choose between face time with the incoming president and his inner circle and a day of networking with political leaders and business titans in Switzerland.[11] The first item on the Davos programme was itself titled "First Impressions: Inauguration Day."[11]

## Promotion of insect protein

The World Economic Forum has been a leading institutional promoter of edible insects, anticipating in a 2018 agenda piece the day when "we can all buy a bag of edible insects at our local grocery store."[12] In 2015 the Forum selected the Netherlands-based insect food manufacturer [[protix|Protix]] as one of its Technology Pioneers; Protix went on to produce roughly 14,000 metric tons of insect food per year under the stated rationale of fighting climate change, and in October 2023 entered a partnership with [[tyson-foods|Tyson Foods]] to build a large US insect-protein facility.[12]

Badlands coverage cited related regulatory and cultural developments — European Union approval of cricket powder in major processed foods, and cricket-based snacks introduced at 1,000 Australian schools — as evidence that insect consumption is being actively pushed by globalist governments and the Forum rather than emerging from consumer demand.[12]

## January 2023 annual meeting

The WEF's January 2023 annual meeting in Davos produced several statements that drew heavy criticism in alternative media.[13] FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] told a cybersecurity panel that the future of national security lay in partnership between tech companies and government, using the war in Ukraine as a framing device for discussion of AI and autonomous vehicles, and separately briefed global banking leaders in private.[13] European Commission Vice President for Values and Transparency [[vera-jourova|Věra Jourová]] predicted that the United States would soon criminalise hate speech as Europe had done.[13] Former U.S. Vice President [[al-gore|Al Gore]] delivered a widely mocked climate address in which he claimed emissions were trapping heat equivalent to "600,000 Hiroshima bombs daily."[13]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the forum had transformed from a secretive Club of Rome-style gathering of the self-described elite into a memetic public event at which the same figures alternately congratulate one another on draconian policies and demand further subjugation of ordinary people.[13] He observed that the January 2023 edition saw several familiar figures adopt markedly more aggressive tones amid rising public backlash against collectivist policies.[13]

## Davos 2025: Trump's video address

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appeared by video link on Thursday, January 23, 2025 — the fourth day of the annual Davos gathering in Switzerland — drawing pockets of laughter and a few moans with blunt comments to the international audience.[14] His address and answers to a handful of questions were the highlight of the day, and his return to the White House that week shaped other sessions, from a panel on tariffs to a fiery speech by Argentine President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]].[14] The executive orders Trump signed on taking office that Monday — withdrawing the United States from the Paris climate accords, ordering the Gulf of Mexico renamed the Gulf of America, and suspending refugee admissions — fed the chatter in the Davos Congress Center corridors all week.[14]

Trump promised global elites lower taxes if they relocated manufacturing to the United States and threatened tariffs if they did not, a signal that he would treat all business in the U.S., including foreign companies, as part of the "America First" landscape.[14] He stressed that his administration favored more U.S. drilling for oil and the use of what he called "good clean coal" than that of former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], saying: "The United States has the largest amount of oil and gas of any country on Earth, and we're going to use it."[14] He repeated his pledge to make the U.S. the "world capital" of both cryptocurrency and artificial intelligence, and called for denuclearization talks with Russia and China under the [[new-start-treaty|New START]] framework.[14]

## Schwab Resignation and Interim Chairmanship (2025)

On April 21, 2025 the Geneva-based WEF announced that founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], 87, had resigned as chair of its board of trustees with immediate effect, a step it had foreshadowed earlier that month without a timeframe.[15] The board accepted the resignation at an extraordinary meeting on April 20 and named Vice Chairman Peter Brabeck-Letmathe interim chairman.[15] Badlands commentary marked the end of Schwab's 50 years at the head of the organization and argued the forum should be dissolved instead of replacing him.[15]

## May 2024 governance transition

On 21 May 2024 the Forum confirmed that founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] was stepping back from day-to-day management after more than 50 years, moving to the role of chairman of the board of trustees.[20] In a statement to CNBC, the Forum said that 'since 2015, the World Economic Forum has been transforming from a convening platform to the leading global institution for public-private cooperation,' and that the organization had been undergoing 'a planned governance evolution from a founder-managed organization to one where a President and Managing Board assume full executive responsibility.'[20] [[borge-brende|Børge Brende]] was president at the time, and the trustee board was to be organized around four strategic committees; no successor as public face of the Davos meeting was announced.[20]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that through the WEF's change network the global governors drive their agenda through every institution, sector and geography, and recalled the preceding Davos meeting where the energy and rhetoric had shifted and [[kevin-roberts|Kevin Roberts]] of the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] told attendees, 'It's laughable that you or anyone would describe Davos as protecting liberal democracy.'[20] She concluded: 'The invisible hand is no longer invisible.'[20]

## "What If Ukraine Loses?" panel

At its annual Davos conference in January 2024 the WEF convened a discussion panel titled "What If Ukraine Loses?" — a step described as unthinkable a year earlier and read as an admission by the globalist class that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] could be defeated by Russia despite the money, armaments and intelligence assets supplied by Western governments.[21] Commentators noted that since 2022 the narrative had shifted from complete victory over Russia, including the retaking of the Donbas and Crimea, to merely holding the current front and sustaining supplies of ammunition and recruits.[21] The failure of the much-hyped counter-offensive, which gained no significant ground, was described as a considerable propaganda error that cast suspicion on Ukraine's other claims.[21]

The panel coincided with the Pentagon's announcement that it had run out of money for Ukraine.[21] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the convergence of these Narratives and Actuals as "a massive white pill," arguing that it indicated the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s actual source of power projection had been severely curtailed and that its operators were surprised by the development.[21]

## 2025 investigation into Klaus Schwab

In April 2025 the forum confirmed a Wall Street Journal report that its board of trustees had unanimously supported the Audit and Risk Committee's decision to launch an [[2025-wef-investigation-into-klaus-schwab|independent investigation]] after a whistleblower letter made allegations against former chairman [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]].[22] Schwab, then 87, denied the accusations, saying there was "not a shadow of proof," and told the Swiss newspaper Blick that he had begun defamation proceedings.[22] He had resigned abruptly the previous Monday "with immediate effect," and the board's statement at the time praised his "outstanding achievements" across 55 years leading the organization.[22]

## Brain monitoring showcase at Davos, January 2023

A presentation at the WEF's January 2023 Davos gathering displayed technology that would allow employers at major companies to monitor their workers' brain activity, with the power to fire employees and report them to government authorities for thinking the "wrong way."[23] The presentation further suggested that brain wave data could be used as evidence in a criminal proceeding, and the presenter characterized these applications as demonstrating the "positive use cases" of brain monitoring — a framing The Gateway Pundit called an Orwellian twist.[23] See [[workplace-brain-monitoring|workplace brain monitoring]] for fuller treatment.[23] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] asked what could go wrong when "a close-knit fraternity of sociopaths" gains the ability to observe and punish thought, and argued that a perceived threat plus a pervasive marketing campaign would be enough to secure public participation.[23]

## Urban Mobility Scorecard Tool and the 2050 private-car target

In May 2023 the WEF, under founder and executive chairman [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], published the Urban Mobility Scorecard Tool: Benchmarking the Transition to Sustainable Urban Mobility, produced in collaboration with Visa, as part of a broader vision for a "sustainable and low-carbon future."[24] Reporting by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] characterized the paper as setting an authoritarian goal of limiting 76% of private cars by 2050.[24]

The white paper projected that by 2050 people are likely to travel twice as much within cities as they do today, which absent behavioural change would mean 2.1 billion cars, buses and other vehicles on the road emitting 4.6 billion tons of carbon dioxide.[24] It argued that greater use of shared transport such as public buses and ride shares, combined with a shift to electric and self-driving vehicles, could reduce the vehicle count to roughly 500 million by 2050 and cut emissions by about 80%.[24]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded sarcastically that everyone must be eager to "own nothing" and eat bugs, and connected the mobility target to the push for [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]], in which all necessities are provided within a fifteen-minute distance of one's front door.[24] He argued that whether the vehicle for it is 15-minute cities or vaccine passports, the Davos globalists persistently devise ways to make the future look bleak, and noted that in his experience even committed leftists were not enthusiastic about that vision — something he expected a sufficiently good propaganda campaign to remedy.[24] He closed by saying he was more preoccupied with "eliminating the World Economic Forum by 100% by 2030."[24]

## Malthusian origins and the Great Narrative Project

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the Forum absorbed the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s 1968 neo-Malthusian computer models — used to justify supposedly fixed [[limits-to-growth|limits to humanity's growth]] — during its 1973 event, at which the [[davos-manifesto|Davos Manifesto]] outlining [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]]'s notions of [[stakeholder-capitalism|stakeholder capitalism]] was crafted.[28] In this account the Forum thereby institutionalized the closed-system economics of the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]] economists [[john-stuart-mill|J. S. Mill]] and [[david-ricardo|David Ricardo]], refuted a century earlier by [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]].[28]

Ehret also identifies [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] as "a leading figure of the WEF Great Narrative Project", describing the Forum's [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] gospel of new technology-based religions emerging from Silicon Valley and an emergent "high caste" of "golden collar Davos elites" presiding over a global [[useless-eaters|useless class]].[28] He contrasts this unipolar vision of stasis with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s call for sovereign states to set the rules of a [[multipolar-world-order|multipolar]] order.[28]

## Bilderberg, AI and biometric identity

The Badlands Media article "Dark Equation" describes a pipeline in which machinations born in the closed-door meetings of the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg Group]] are presented in a more public-facing way at the World Economic Forum in Davos the following year.[29] WEF President [[borge-brende|Børge Brende]] attended the [[2023-bilderberg-meeting|2023 Bilderberg meeting]] in Lisbon.[29]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the "globalist Davos crowd" has been repeatedly open about what it would like to impose on civilization: AI-powered biometric digital identities, digital currencies, and the transhuman integration of the Internet-of-Things into human bodies.[29] He identified [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]]'s [[worldcoin|Worldcoin]] as a continuation of a push by the United Nations, the World Economic Forum and a growing number of governments toward a global biometric identification system attached to a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]].[29]
