# Verkhovna Rada

The Verkhovna Rada is the unicameral parliament of [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1]

## May 2024 prisoner mobilisation bill

On 8 May 2024 the Rada passed a bill enabling certain prisoners to serve in the armed forces as the country faced a critical shortage of manpower on the front lines.[1] Kyiv had long opposed such a measure and had criticised Moscow's own mobilisation of prisoners, but reversed course amid fresh Russian advances on the battlefield.[1] Under the legislation, mobilisation would be voluntary and open only to certain categories of prisoners; the bill required the signature of the chairperson of the Rada and of President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] before entering into force.[1]

MP Olena Shuliak, head of Zelensky's party, announced on social media that "the parliament has voted 'yes'," adding that the draft law "opens the possibility for certain categories of prisoners who expressed a desire to defend their country to join the Defence Forces."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentary questioned the voluntariness of a choice framed as remaining in prison versus leaving it, and scrutinised the exclusion criteria — the ineligibility of former high-ranking officials and those guilty of serious corruption, and of prisoners who had killed two or more people.[1] The commentary tied its scepticism to Ukraine's record on corruption prosecutions, religious persecution allegations, and the cancellation of wartime elections.[1]

## 2024 ban on Russia-linked religious organizations

On Tuesday, August 20, 2024, the Rada approved, in its second and final reading, legislation banning religious organisations with ties to Russia, clearing the way for Kyiv to end the activities of the [[ukrainian-orthodox-church-of-the-moscow-patriarchate|Ukrainian Orthodox Church of the Moscow Patriarchate]] on Ukrainian soil.[2] The vote was 265 in favour and 29 against, according to MP Yaroslav Zheleznyak.[2] Ukrainian officials had long argued that the UOC-MP functioned as an arm of the Russian Orthodox Church and worked closely with the FSB.[2] The bill required President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]]'s signature to become law, and he had signalled days earlier that he would sign it, framing the measure as a duty "to guarantee Ukrainian spiritual independence."[2]

## October 2023 vote on Moscow-linked religious organizations

On Thursday, October 19, 2023, the Rada voted 267–15 to advance legislation seen as effectively banning the [[ukrainian-orthodox-church|Ukrainian Orthodox Church]] over its ties to Moscow, an earlier stage of the process that culminated in the 2024 final passage.[3] The measure required further voting before being finalized and reaching President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]]'s desk, and would prohibit religious organizations "affiliated with the centers of influence of a religious organization, the management center of which is located outside of Ukraine in a state that carries out armed aggression against Ukraine."[3] The church, whose leader [[metropolitan-onufry|Metropolitan Onufry]] had declared it the "sacred duty" of believers to defend Ukraine, insisted it had been fully independent of Moscow since May 2022, but many Ukrainians remained suspicious of its links to Patriarch [[patriarch-kirill|Kirill]].[3]
