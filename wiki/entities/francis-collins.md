# Francis Collins

Francis Collins is an American geneticist who served as Director of the National Institutes of Health through the initial phase of the COVID-19 pandemic. Alongside [[anthony-fauci]], he is one of the two federal officials most frequently named in congressional scrutiny of how early scientific opinion on the origins of SARS-CoV-2 was shaped.[1]

## The February 2020 conference call

Collins participated in the conference call that congressional investigators identified as the pivot point in the reversal of virologists [[kristian-andersen]] and [[robert-garry]], who had emailed Fauci on January 31, 2020 stating that the virus had "unusual features" that "(potentially) look engineered."[1] At the first hearing of the House select subcommittee on COVID-19 origins in March 2023, [[jim-jordan]] argued that a call with Fauci and Collins was the only intervening event between the scientists' private assessment and their public rejection of the [[lab-leak-theory|lab-leak theory]] three days later.[1]

Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] treated the episode as illustrative of what it termed the malfeasance of the biomedical industrial complex, in which senior officials controlling grant funding could effectively determine which scientific conclusions were publishable.[1]

## Congressional records request (February 2023)

As Director of the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]], Collins was named among the officials whose records the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] sought in February 2023, when chairman [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] requested phone records, calendars and dozens of sets of internal documents from top federal health officials.[2] The [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] subsequently released 900 pages of records to the panel, which was examining whether COVID-19 originated in a Wuhan laboratory and whether U.S. taxpayer funds had gone to that lab.[2]

## Refusal to comment on the 2023 Slack disclosures

When reporters from Racket and Public sought comment in 2023 on Slack messages relating to the drafting of the [[proximal-origin-paper|Proximal Origin]] paper, Collins declined, as did Jeremy Farrar and all of the paper's authors.[3]


## NIH royalty payments

Records published in August 2023 by the watchdog [[openthebooks|OpenTheBooks]], obtained after a mostly successful Freedom of Information Act battle with the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]], showed that Collins and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] — the longtime directors of NIH and its [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases]] respectively — personally received 58 royalty payments from companies licensing inventions developed with taxpayer money.[4] The unredacted disclosure ran to more than 1,500 pages and covered 56,000 transactions totaling more than $325 million across NIH scientists, although individual payment amounts were not itemized.[4]

## February 2020 origins call (March 2023 disclosures)

Congressional investigators on the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] reported in March 2023 that Collins, then Director of the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]], joined [[anthony-fauci|Dr. Anthony Fauci]] and at least eleven other scientists on a February 1, 2020 conference call during which several participants warned that COVID-19 may have leaked from a lab in Wuhan and may have been intentionally genetically manipulated.[5] Three days later, four of the call's participants drafted the [[proximal-origin-paper|"Proximal Origin of SARS-CoV-2"]] paper — which argued against a laboratory origin — and sent it to Fauci and Collins.[5]
