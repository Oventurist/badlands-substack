# Laxman Narasimhan

Laxman Narasimhan is a business executive who served as chief executive officer of [[starbucks|Starbucks]] during the company's sharp 2024 earnings downturn.[1]

## Second-quarter 2024 earnings miss

On the evening of April 30, 2024, Narasimhan opened Starbucks' earnings call with investors by stating his unhappiness with the quarter's results: "Let me be clear from the beginning. Our performance this quarter was disappointing and did not meet our expectations."[1] Starbucks had reported a 4 percent drop in same-store sales for the second quarter year over year, against analyst expectations of growth, with same-store sales in China plunging 11 percent.[1]

The following day, Starbucks shares fell roughly 16 percent in early trading, approaching the -16.2 percent level last seen during the Covid crash and putting the company on the brink of its worst single-day loss since the Dot Com crash of early 2000.[1] Analyst Adam Crisafulli of Vital Knowledge wrote that Starbucks had "reported what's perhaps the worst set of results of any large company so far" that quarter, and William Blair downgraded the coffee chain citing a "stunning across-the-board miss on all key metrics."[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the collapse as an early sign that large conglomerates operating on thin percentage margins would suffer more than small businesses in a high-borrowing-cost environment, presaging what he called a transfer of wealth away from the "UnFortunate 500" toward Main Street.[1]
