# PacWest Bancorp

PacWest Bancorp was a Beverly Hills, California-based bank holding company that became a focal point of the 2023 US regional banking crisis.[1] Its operations comprised a community lender known as Pacific Western Bank together with commercial and consumer lending businesses.[1]

## May 2023 collapse in share price

Shortly after the close of trading on May 3, 2023, Bloomberg reported that PacWest was weighing a range of strategic options, including an outright sale, a breakup, or a capital raise, and had been working with a financial adviser.[1] The company had not begun a formal auction process, and an outright sale was reported to be hindered because few potential buyers were interested in the entire bank; as with [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] and [[first-republic-bank|First Republic Bank]], any acquirer would potentially have to book a large loss marking down some of the bank's loans.[1] The stock cratered roughly 60 percent on the report.[1] The bank's financial condition appeared to be considerably worse than the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] had assumed when it hiked rates a further 25 basis points the same day.[1]

## Context and commentary

The news broke hours after Fed chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] told an FOMC press conference that the US banking system was "sound and resilient," a characterization [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] flatly called a lie given that more than half a trillion dollars in bank failures had occurred in preceding weeks — a scale exceeding the global financial crisis.[1] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], interpreted the sequence as "the controlled demolition of the economy in preparation for the 'rescue' of the financial system via [[central-bank-digital-currency|CBDCs]] at some point in the future."[1]

## May 2023 stock collapse

In the early hours of May 4, 2023, after its stock crashed as much as 60% and hit a record low in the afterhours session, PacWest confirmed that it was in talks with several potential investors.[2] The plunge followed a Bloomberg report that the California lender was seeking to sell itself or raise capital, which sparked concern that it was the next insolvent bank.[2] Attempting to calm markets, PacWest said core deposits had increased since March, stating that "the bank has not experienced out-of-the-ordinary deposit flows following the sale of First Republic Bank and other news" and that "our cash and available liquidity remains solid and exceeded our uninsured deposits."[2]

The episode was read as evidence that the regional bank crisis was spreading to larger institutions, with [[us-bancorp|U.S. Bancorp]] also tumbling amid widespread liquidations.[2] Two days earlier, after the [[first-republic-bank|First Republic]] take-under by [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] — praised by [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] as proof the "system works as it should" and echoed by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] chair — [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] had warned that the bailout would drain up to $75 billion in Fed reserves from the system, pushing small banks back to their reserve constraint and forcing another market rout or bailout.[2] [[western-alliance-bancorporation|Western Alliance]] came under similar pressure days later.[2]
