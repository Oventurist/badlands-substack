# Bessel van der Kolk

**Bessel van der Kolk**, described as one of the most influential trauma researchers alive, is quoted in "Hijacked Consciousness - Resonance Field," the fifth installment of the [[hijacked-consciousness-series|Hijacked Consciousness]] series published by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]] through [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on August 9, 2025.[1]

The article cites his line that "Trauma is not the story of something that happened back then. It's the current imprint of that pain, horror, and fear living inside people."[1] The author uses the quotation to argue that such an imprint cannot be argued away but can be re-patterned, and that the re-patterning happens through rhythm, presence, and a field safe enough that the body no longer needs to brace.[1] This supports the essay's wider claim that the nervous system begins to rest not because the world has become safe but because a person's frequency "no longer belongs to it," a mechanism the article develops under the heading of the [[resonance-field|resonance field]].[1]

## Cited in "The Bridge" (March 2025)

Van der Kolk is cited again, as the author of *The Body Keeps the Score*, in the March 1, 2025 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay "The Bridge: Awakening, Healing, and Ascension" by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]].[2] The essay summarises his position that trauma fundamentally reorganises how the mind and brain manage perceptions, and extends it to argue that prolonged uncertainty and fear reshape not only what people think about but their ability to think freely at all.[2] On this reading, when fear becomes constant the brain rewires itself to anticipate danger even in moments of safety, which the author offers as an explanation for why so many readers feel caught in cycles of hesitation, self-doubt and mistrust of their own instincts.[2]

## Cited in "The Remembering" (April 2025)

*The Body Keeps the Score* is quoted again in the April 19, 2025 Badlands Media feature "The Remembering: Fractured Selves and the Path Beyond Ego," also by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]].[3] The passage cited reads: "Trauma results in a fundamental reorganization of the way mind and body manage perceptions. It changes not only how we think and what we think about, but also our very capacity to think."[3] The author uses it to argue that the parts of a self formed under pressure speak through emotion, memory and behaviour, surfacing as resistance, irrational panic or disproportionate reaction, and that beneath each such moment "lies a messenger."[3] The quotation supports the essay's treatment of [[parts-work|parts work]] and [[psychological-integration|psychological integration]].[3]

## Cited in "Walking the Path" (March 2025)

Van der Kolk is quoted twice in the March 29, 2025 feature "Walking the Path: From Awakening to Integration," first as a trauma pioneer and author of *The Body Keeps the Score* for the line "Safety is not the absence of threat, but the presence of connection, to ourselves and others."[4] There the quotation answers the fear of losing identity during [[spiritual-awakening|awakening]], with the author concluding that peace lies in embracing the fluidity of self and trusting transformation to align a person with their essence.[4]

He is quoted a second time in the essay's section on healing — "The body is the bridge to healing, movement and breath bring us home" — supporting the argument that [[psychological-integration|integration]] regulates the swings between euphoria and despair by grounding them in the body rather than in belief.[4]
