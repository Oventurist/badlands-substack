# United Airlines

United Airlines is a major US carrier that in January 2025 announced an accelerated rollout of free in-flight WiFi powered by [[starlink|Starlink]], the satellite internet service operated by Elon Musk's SpaceX.[1]

## Starlink WiFi rollout

The company revealed on a Sunday that it would begin rolling out super-fast WiFi service powered by Starlink in the spring, a significant acceleration of the original timetable set out in the September announcement of the United–Starlink deal and an important development in competition among major carriers.[1]

United planned to introduce the service first on its Embraer E-175 jets, which typically feature two-by-two seating in the back and one-by-two up front.[1] Initial testing was to start in February, with the first Starlink-equipped flight expected to lift off in the spring, and the airline expected its entire two-cabin E-175 regional jet fleet to be equipped by the end of the year, along with the first flight on a larger aircraft by the same deadline.[1] Eventually every one of United's more than 1,000 aircraft is to have the service.[1]

## Flight 202 wheel well death

On Christmas Eve 2024, United Airlines confirmed that a body was found in the wheel well of one of the main landing gears of a Boeing 787-10 operating [[united-airlines-flight-202-wheel-well-death|Flight 202]], which had flown roughly 4,000 miles from Chicago O'Hare to Kahului airport in Maui.[2] The airline said the wheel well "was only accessible from outside of the aircraft" and that it was not clear how or when the person accessed it, adding that United was working with law enforcement on the investigation.[2] Maui Police described an active investigation into the circumstances, and the deceased was not named.[2]

## Starlink rollout approval (March 2025)

United received clearance from federal regulators to launch its first commercial flight with free [[starlink|Starlink]] Wi-Fi service in May 2025, after the Federal Aviation Administration approved use of the satellite internet service — a division of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s SpaceX — on a regional jet model.[3] The airline said it planned to install Starlink Wi-Fi on 40 planes per month and complete its fleet of more than 300 regional jets by the end of the year, running beta tests before the first customer flight.[3] United had signed a deal with Starlink the previous September to bring satellite internet to its more than 1,000 planes over several years, and said it would work with the FAA to obtain approval for every aircraft.[3] The service, which United says is 50 times faster than the internet then available on its regional planes, is free for customers enrolled in the airline's free MileagePlus frequent flyer program.[3]
