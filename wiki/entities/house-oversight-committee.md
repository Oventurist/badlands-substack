# House Oversight Committee

The House Committee on Oversight and Accountability is the principal investigative committee of the U.S. House of Representatives, and during the 118th Congress it served as the lead panel in Republican inquiries into the foreign business activities of the Biden family.[1]

## Bobulinski testimony

On February 13, 2024, the committee took closed-door testimony from [[tony-bobulinski|Tony Bobulinski]], a former business associate of the Bidens, jointly with the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]].[1] Bobulinski used his opening statement to allege that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had been aware of and had actively enabled an influence-peddling operation run by his son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and his brother [[james-biden|James Biden]] while Biden was vice president.[1] The session followed the committee's earlier July 2023 interview of [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]], whose account Bobulinski's statement echoed.[1]

## August 2023 Biden family bank records

In August 2023 Oversight Republicans released detailed bank records documenting approximately $20 million in payments to the Biden family and their partners from foreign actors in Russia, Ukraine and Kazakhstan during [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s vice presidency, while acknowledging that none of the newly released records directly linked payments to Joe Biden himself.[2] The committee's documents indicated that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and his former business partner [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]] had used shell companies to "hide" millions in payments from overseas companies and oligarchs, including $142,000 wired from a Kazakh oligarch that Hunter used to buy a Porsche before arranging a dinner with his father.[2] The records also detailed links to Russian billionaire [[elena-baturina|Yelena Baturina]] and $1 million in yearly payments to Hunter and Archer for their seats on the board of the Ukrainian oil company [[burisma|Burisma]], and were presented as corroborating Archer's earlier testimony to the committee.[2]

## July 2023 inquiries

Two matters covered in the July 12, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] involved the committee. Israeli whistleblower [[gal-luft|Gal Luft]] said he had been arrested in Cyprus specifically to stop him from testifying before the committee about alleged Biden family payments from individuals linked to Chinese military intelligence.[3] Separately, the committee requested a briefing from the [[us-secret-service|Secret Service]] on the [[white-house-cocaine-incident|White House cocaine investigation]] by Friday of that week.[3]

## Demand for Biden pseudonym records (August 2023)

In August 2023, House Republican investigators asked the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] to produce any unredacted records in which then-Vice President Joe Biden used a pseudonym, following disclosures from Hunter Biden's laptop that Biden had employed a "Robert.L.Peters@pci.gov" address in office.[4] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] wrote that "The Committee's need for these Vice-Presidential records is specific and well-documented," and that the Committee sought "to craft legislative solutions aimed at deficiencies it has identified in the current legal framework regarding ethics laws and disclosure of financial interests related to the immediate family members of Vice Presidents and Presidents — deficiencies that may place American national security and interests at risk."[4]

The underlying records showed that Biden aide John Flynn had copied Hunter on ten emails containing the Vice President's daily schedule between May 19 and June 15, 2016, including one detailing plans for a call with Ukraine's then-president [[petro-poroshenko|Petro Poroshenko]], sent to Hunter's [[rosemont-seneca-partners|Rosemont Seneca Partners]] address while he sat on the board of Burisma.[4] The episode is treated in detail at [[biden-pseudonym-emails|Biden pseudonym emails]].[4]

## Epstein–Summers document release (November 2025)

In November 2025, Republicans on the House Oversight Committee released a trove of documents revealing emails and text messages between [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and former Treasury Secretary [[larry-summers|Larry Summers]] that continued well after Epstein's 2008 guilty plea in Florida for sex crimes and up until his July 2019 arrest on federal child sex trafficking charges.[5] The disclosure prompted Summers to announce that he would step back from public commitments, saying he was "deeply ashamed" of his actions.[5]

## Biden family finances investigation (July 2023)

In July 2023 chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said the committee had obtained additional bank records relating to [[joe-biden|President Biden]]'s family finances and was preparing a third memorandum that he said would document transactions from Ukraine and Russia.[6] Comer also announced plans to file between six and ten criminal referrals against [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] once the investigation into the family's business dealings concluded, covering alleged money laundering, racketeering, wire fraud, [[foreign-agents-registration-act|FARA]] violations and a [[mann-act|Mann Act]] offence.[6]

## March 2024 influence-peddling hearing

On March 20, 2024, the committee held a nearly six-hour public hearing into the Biden family's alleged influence peddling, as congressional Republicans pushed forward with their impeachment inquiry against President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[7] Two former [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] business associates testified: [[tony-bobulinski|Tony Bobulinski]], a U.S. Navy veteran and former business partner, who appeared in person, and [[jason-galanis|Jason Galanis]], who testified virtually from a federal prison camp in Montgomery, Alabama, where he is serving a 14-year sentence.[7] Hunter Biden himself refused to attend.[7]

Bobulinski accused Hunter Biden of lying under oath about his father's involvement in his business dealings and said he had "begged" Rep. [[ro-khanna|Ro Khanna]] and his staff to review text-message evidence on his BlackBerry.[7] Democrats invited [[lev-parnas|Lev Parnas]] — sentenced to 20 months over campaign finance, wire fraud and false statements offences — to testify about his work with [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]] on Ukraine.[7] Rep. [[jared-moskowitz|Jared Moskowitz]] taunted chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] and Judiciary chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] to hold an immediate impeachment vote if they truly believed they had evidence of high crimes and misdemeanors.[7]

Badlands Media commentary characterized the hearing as one in which Republicans recapped evidence of alleged Biden family corruption while Democrats made the proceeding about former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], citing [[jamie-raskin|Jamie Raskin]]'s apparent suggestion that [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] was involved in [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]]'s murder, [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]]'s claim that RICO is not a crime, and [[dan-goldman|Dan Goldman]]'s imitation of [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]].[7] The commentary concluded that the forum had zero credibility with the public and that "uniparty" pressure on the Republican majority was keeping impeachment stuck in hearings.[7]

## Subpoenas over Biden's mental fitness (July 2024)

The GOP-led committee subpoenaed three senior [[white-house|White House]] aides in July 2024, demanding they sit for depositions concerning President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s mental state.[8] The subpoenas covered first lady Jill Biden's top aide [[anthony-bernal|Anthony Bernal]], deputy chief of staff [[annie-tomasini|Annie Tomasini]] and senior adviser [[ashley-williams|Ashley Williams]].[8] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said the White House had shielded the three from testifying about Biden's mishandling of classified documents and that reporting showed the same aides were "seeking to cover up President Biden's declining cognitive state inside the White House," adding that Biden was "clearly unfit for office."[8]

## ACE Act and election integrity (2023)

In June 2023, the Oversight Committee introduced the [[ace-act|ACE Act]], election-integrity legislation presented at a joint hearing with the House Administration Committee on June 7.[9] Testimony submitted to the hearing framed the bill as a response to a crisis of voter confidence, citing polling showing that 37 percent of Democratic-leaning voters and 71 percent of Republican-leaning voters had concerns about whether the election system accurately reflects the will of the voters.[9] The testimony rejected the characterization of such laws as "Jim Crow 2.0" and cited Georgia and Florida as states where comparable reforms had, in the sponsors' account, increased both participation and confidence.[9]

The bill would require voter identification, restrict ballot harvesting, guarantee public observation of the process, secure mail-in ballots, clean voter rolls, and bar non-citizens from voting, applying to the District of Columbia under [[congress|Congress]]'s oversight responsibilities for the federal district while respecting states' authority over their own elections.[9] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said the reforms would "hopefully create a system that other states will look to as a model for a secure election system."[9]

## January 2024 Hunter Biden associate interviews

In the week beginning January 22, 2024, the committee and the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] were scheduled to take closed-door transcribed interviews from two business associates of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] as part of the House impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[10] [[mervyn-yan|Mervyn Yan]], subpoenaed the previous year for financial records relating to his dealings with Hunter Biden through [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC]] and Hudson West III, was set to appear on Thursday, followed by [[rob-walker|Rob Walker]] on Friday.[10] Financial records subpoenaed by the committee showed that Biden family members, including Hallie Biden and Sara Biden, received more than $1 million from accounts related to Walker and his Chinese business ventures with Hunter Biden.[10]

## ActBlue subpoenas, June 2025

The GOP chairman of the House Oversight Committee joined the chairmen of the Judiciary and Administration committees in issuing subpoena letters on June 25, 2025 to ActBlue officials over potential "widespread" fraud on the Democratic fundraising platform.[11] The recipients were an unidentified current senior workflow specialist and former vice president of customer service Alyssa Twomey, both of whom had previously been prepared to sit for voluntary transcribed interviews.[11] Their counsel, Danny Onorato, asked the chairmen to withdraw the interview requests until the parallel Justice Department investigation authorized by President Donald Trump concludes or clarifies its position.[11]

## Ukraine spending inquiry (February 2023)

In February 2023, committee Republicans pressed the Biden administration to account for the billions of dollars [[congress|Congress]] had appropriated for [[ukraine|Ukraine]], as questions grew over whether the aid should continue at then-current levels.[12] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] and other panel members said in a February 22 letter that the committee was seeking a detailed record of where funding had gone so far and what safeguards the administration had put in place to prevent wasteful spending.[12]

The inquiry ran alongside oversight by inspectors general: the [[department-of-defense-office-of-inspector-general|DoD Office of Inspector General]] released an "Oversight Update: Ukraine Assistance" the same day noting five potential planned oversight projects.[12] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] assessed that the committees were delivering on their promises and that the [[ukraine-aid-funding-controversy|Ukraine spending fight]] coincided with a shift in Congress and among the public against further aid.[12]

## Hunter Biden testimony dispute (December 2023)

The committee, chaired by [[james-comer|James Comer]], was at the center of a December 2023 standoff over how [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] would testify: Comer rejected an offer of public testimony and demanded a closed-door transcribed deposition on December 13, while Hunter's counsel [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] argued that closed sessions were used "to manipulate, even distort, the facts and misinform the American public."[13] The panel's November subpoenas to Hunter Biden, [[james-biden|James Biden]] and other Biden family members and business associates were widely read as a sign that the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was entering a new phase.[13]

## December 2023 Hunter Biden deposition standoff

[[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] defied the committee's subpoena for a closed-door interview scheduled for December 13, 2023, appearing outside the U.S. Capitol to say he would testify only publicly.[14] On December 27, committee chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]], together with [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] of the [[house-judiciary-committee|Judiciary Committee]], requested White House records bearing on President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s alleged involvement in that decision, expanding the impeachment inquiry.[14]

## Clinton depositions in the Epstein-Maxwell investigation (2025)

In November 2025 the Committee ordered former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and former Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] to appear for closed-door depositions in December, rejecting an attempt by their attorney to block subpoenas tied to the panel's [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]]-[[ghislaine-maxwell|Maxwell]] investigation.[15] In a letter sent that Friday, Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] dismissed attorney [[david-kendall|David Kendall]]'s argument that the Committee should rely on written responses rather than question the Clintons directly, writing that the Committee "disagrees" that accepting limited written information would be "the most efficient and equitable way to proceed" and that Kendall's request relied on assertions already "disproved by public information."[15] Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] doubted the depositions would happen and objected to the closed-door format, writing that "there must be justice phase if there is to be a golden age."[15]

## Hunter Biden deposition (February 2024)

The Committee, alongside the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], took [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s closed-door deposition on February 28, 2024 as part of the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[16] Republicans had sought the testimony for months and moved in January 2024 toward holding him in contempt of Congress before he agreed to appear voluntarily, arguing his testimony was a "critical component" of an inquiry focused on allegations that the president profited from his family's foreign business dealings while vice president.[16] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said afterward that the inquiry would proceed to public hearings.[16]

## July 2023 bank-records disclosures

Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said on July 5, 2023 that he was "hopeful" more of the committee's "disturbing findings" in its investigation of the Biden family's alleged criminal scheme would be revealed the following week.[17] He reported that six of the twelve banks the committee had pursued had produced records that House Republicans believed showed [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and his family received millions of dollars from foreign agents, and reiterated his estimate that the family had taken in over $20 million from foreign sources in a scheme of "creating policy for money."[17]


## 2023 membership dispute

In January 2023 the [[white-house|White House]] publicly denounced the appointment of Republican Reps. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] of Georgia and [[paul-gosar|Paul Gosar]] of Arizona to the committee, with spokesperson Ian Sams saying House Republicans appeared to be setting the stage for "divorced-from-reality political stunts" and calling on GOP leaders to disclose what secret deals were struck with "extreme MAGA members" to elect a Speaker.[18] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] responded that the panel was a "dream team of America First firebrands," contrasted it with the January 6 committee, and listed its likely subjects as fentanyl trafficking across the border, the Afghanistan withdrawal, COVID-19 origins and Biden family influence peddling.[18]

## Garland contempt hearing, May 2024

The House Oversight Committee convened on Thursday, May 16, 2024 to hold Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] in contempt for refusing to comply with a subpoena for the audio recording of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interview with special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]].[20] The hearing quickly spiraled out of control, with lawmakers bickering among themselves less than an hour in.[20] [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] took shots at Rep. [[jasmine-crockett|Jasmine Crockett]] of Texas — telling her "I think your fake eyelashes are messing up what you're reading" after Crockett asked "Do you know what we're here for?" — and traded barbs with [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], who asked whether Greene's feelings were hurt.[20]

## Epstein files release (September 2025)
