# House Oversight Committee

The House Oversight Committee is the principal investigative committee of the United States House of Representatives, with broad jurisdiction to examine the conduct of federal agencies, programs and institutions. In the Badlands corpus it functions as one of the main vehicles through which [[congress|Congress]] applies pressure to entities the writers describe as part of an entrenched institutional order.

## Task Force on Defending Constitutional Rights and Exposing Institutional Abuse

The July 27, 2026 Badlands Brief identifies Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]], R-Texas, as chairman of the committee's Task Force on Defending Constitutional Rights and Exposing Institutional Abuse. In that capacity Gill said lawmakers are examining federal legislation that could strip the [[american-bar-association|American Bar Association]] of its authority to accredit U.S. law schools while leaving states free to decide whether to work with the organization. The committee's hearing on the subject featured testimony from ABA President [[michelle-behnke|Michelle Behnke]] defending the organization's diversity, equity and inclusion practices, and criticism from Gill alleging that the ABA's prior DEI requirements discriminated against certain students and that the organization has exerted outsized influence over law school admissions, curricula and continuing legal education.

## The Clinton contempt vote (January 2026)

The January 23, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the committee voted on Wednesday, January 21, 2026 to hold [[bill-clinton|Bill]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] in [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] after they did not appear for depositions related to the committee's [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] probe. The measure still requires approval by the full House before any referral to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].
