# Angola

Angola is a southern African nation that hosted President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in early December 2024, marking the first time he had set foot on African soil during his presidency.[1]

Speaking in Angola on Tuesday, December 3, Biden announced over $1 billion in new humanitarian support for Africans displaced from their homes by historic droughts and food insecurity, saying that "the United States continues to be the world's largest provider of humanitarian aid and development assistance" and that this would increase because it was "the right thing for the wealthiest nation in the world to do."[1] He added that the United States was expanding its relationship across Africa "from assistance to aid, investment to trade, moving from patrons to partners to help bridge the infrastructure gap."[1]

## Commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted the pledge with the continuing struggles of North Carolina residents rebuilding after Hurricane Helene, and noted that by various accounts two out of every five Angolans live on less than $1.90 a day, one in three lives in severe poverty, and there is a severe lack of clean water.[1] Against that backdrop he described Biden's self-deprecating joke — "We Bidens are like poor relatives. We show up when we're invited, we stay longer when we should, eat all your food, and don't know when to go home." — as unintentionally comic given the setting.[1] He expressed hope that the emerging Trump [[state-department|State Department]] would look to foster trade in Africa, while suspecting parts of the continent would be apprehensive about letting westerners back in, and offered good wishes to Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Traoré]] and [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]].[1]
