# Robert McBurney

Robert McBurney is a Georgia state judge who, in October 2024, blocked a rule requiring poll workers to hand-count ballots that had been adopted by the pro-Trump majority of the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]].[1]

## The hand-count ruling

McBurney ruled on Tuesday, October 15, 2024, that because early voting in Georgia had already begun, mandatory hand-counting would create chaos and delay the reporting of election results.[1] "The administrative chaos that will — not may — ensue is entirely inconsistent with the obligations of our boards of elections (and the State Election Board) to ensure that our elections are fair, legal, and orderly," he wrote.[1] He did not determine whether the rule was illegal, instead pausing it pending further review; an appeals court could potentially reverse the decision.[1]

The rule had been introduced by Sharlene Alexander, a Trump supporter appointed to the Fayette County board of elections in February 2024.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the ruling carried "foundational revelations" about the American election ecosystem.[1] In her reading, McBurney had effectively declared that certification is a meaningless rubber stamp and that state election boards — routinely presented to the public as the statutory authority and expert custodians of election integrity — are ceremonial bodies holding no actual authority.[1] She noted the apparent contradiction in a judge holding that a board may not "unilaterally" determine that an election requires additional security while the judiciary unilaterally places questioning of the election system off limits, even for election officials.[1] Her conclusion was that "a façade of security is no security at all," and that the case was, for those wanting the theater of U.S. elections exposed, a "massive win."[1]

## October 2024 certification ruling

On October 14, 2024, sitting as a Fulton County Superior Court judge, McBurney ruled that Georgia county election officials must certify election results by the statutory deadline regardless of irregularities or suspected fraud.[3] Georgia law requires county election superintendents to certify by 5 p.m. on the Monday following the election, or the Tuesday if that date falls on a federal holiday, as it did in 2024.[3] "No election superintendent (or member of a board of elections and registration) may refuse to certify or abstain from certifying election results under any circumstance," he wrote.[3] He added that where a superintendent determines that additional information is needed from the elections board or other officials, that information should be provided "promptly" where not protected by law, but that "any delay in receiving such information is not a basis for refusing to certify the election results or abstaining from doing so."[3] The suit had been brought by [[julie-adams|Julie Adams]], a Republican member of the Fulton County Board of Elections and Registrations, after the county's appointed election director allegedly denied her repeated requests for access to election results and processes.[3]

## December 2025 ruling on Fulton County 2020 ballots

As a Fulton County Superior Court judge, McBurney ruled in December 2025 that the Republican-led [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia Election Board]] would be granted access to [[fulton-county|Fulton County]]'s 2020 presidential ballots and documents, in connection with the board's reopened investigation into the county's handling of the 2020 election.[2] His ruling, issued on a Tuesday, specified that the board would need to cover the costs of producing the ballots and documents — an amount estimated at roughly $400,000.[2] County Chairman [[robb-pitts|Robb Pitts]] responded that the elections had been open and transparent and that Fulton County taxpayers would not bear the production cost.[2]
