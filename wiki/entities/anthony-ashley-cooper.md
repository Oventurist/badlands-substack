# Anthony Ashley Cooper, 7th Earl of Shaftesbury

Lord Anthony Ashley Cooper, 7th Earl of Shaftesbury, launched what the article calls a Zionist project in 1839 and is presented as one of the three leaders — with [[henry-drummond]] and [[john-nelson-darby]] — of the Anglican/Jesuit intelligence network behind the [[plymouth-brethren]].[1]

## Premillennial Dispensationalism

Cooper was a follower of Drummond and devoted himself to "Premillennial Dispensationalism" soon after the [[albury-conferences]] of 1826–1830.[1]

## The 1839 Quarterly Review article

In January 1839, Cooper wrote an article in the London *Quarterly Review* commonly referred to as the first public call for the restoration of the Jews in Palestine.[1] He argued that "the soil and climate of Palestine are singularly adapted to the growth of produce required for the exigencies of Great Britain," that the finest cotton could be obtained in almost unlimited abundance, and that with the presence of a British officer and the increased security of property this would confer, "the Jews, who will betake themselves to agriculture in no other land, having found, in the English consul, a mediator between their people and the Pasha, will probably return in yet greater numbers, and become once more the husbandmen of Judaea and Galilee."[1]

In 1840 Lord Palmerston — Cooper's cousin and British Foreign Secretary — echoed this proto-Zionist outlook in a letter to the British ambassador to Constantinople, instructing him "to hold out every just encouragement to the Jews of Europe to return to Palestine."[1] In 1853 Shaftesbury wrote to Prime Minister Aberdeen describing Syria as "a country without a nation, which should be matched to a nation without a country… The ancient and rightful lords of the soil, the Jews!"[1]

## The Palestine Exploration Fund

Shaftesbury recognized the need to map Palestine, including locating the site of Solomon's Temple, in preparation for the project, and worked with Palmerston and the Prince of Wales (later King Edward VII) to create the [[palestine-exploration-fund]] in 1865.[1] The article lists him alongside [[winston-churchill]] and [[arthur-balfour|Lord Balfour]] as figures who believed the British Empire had created political [[zionism]] as part of the Great Game.[1]
