# Mississippi

Mississippi is a US state whose absentee ballot statute was the subject of a June 2026 [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] decision.[1]

## Mail ballot law

Mississippi law allows election officials to count absentee ballots received up to five business days after Election Day, provided the ballots were postmarked on or before Election Day.[1] The [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] challenged the statute as preempted by the federal statutes establishing a uniform Election Day.[1]

The Court rejected that challenge 5-4, with Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]] writing that federal law sets Election Day but does not require every valid absentee ballot to be received by that date.[1] Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] joined the majority; Justice [[samuel-alito|Samuel Alito]] dissented.[1] The ruling left Mississippi's law intact and, in Badlands commentary, shifted the burden of reform onto [[congress|Congress]] and state legislatures rather than the judiciary.[1]
