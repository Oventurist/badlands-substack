# House Speakership Holdouts (America First Congressmen)

The "America First Congressmen" are the bloc of House Republicans who opposed [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s election as Speaker in January 2023 and extracted concessions in exchange for allowing his confirmation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## The standoff and its resolution

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that he "stood with the Congressmen who were opposing him as Speaker," and observed that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had endorsed both McCarthy and the members blocking him — a combination he read as evidence that Trump held control over McCarthy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) After McCarthy was confirmed, the opposing members "started getting important leadership roles on congressional committees," which Carlson took as confirmation of a prearranged outcome.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

He argues the optics were deliberate: it needed to appear that McCarthy had been forced to appoint these members in order to be confirmed, so that the [[neoconservatives|Neocons]] and Democrats would continue to believe he was working with them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## The impeachment mechanism

Carlson notes as established fact that the America First Congressmen "pushed for and got measures put in place that make it easier to impeach the Speaker of the House."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Following the [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|2023 debt ceiling agreement]], he treats that rules change as a live instrument — the means by which McCarthy could be quickly ousted, possibly as the intended purpose of Trump's endorsement all along.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)
