# Lord Lothian

Lord Lothian was a leading controller of the [[round-table-movement|Round Table Movement]] and later British Ambassador to the United States.[1] He is cited in Badlands Media's account of Anglo-American relations for his 1918 framing of what he called the "American problem."[1]

## The "American problem"

Writing to Lionel Curtis on October 15, 1918, Lothian complained that "there is a fundamentally different concept in regard to this question between Great Britain and the United States as to the necessity of civilized control over politically backward peoples."[1] He held that "the inhabitants of Africa and parts of Asia have proved unable to govern themselves," yet America "not only has no conception of this aspect of the problem but has been led to believe that the assumption of this kind of responsibility is iniquitous imperialism."[1] Americans, he wrote, took an attitude toward world government exactly analogous to the one they had earlier taken toward the world war, and if they were slow in learning, "we shall be condemned to a period of strained relations between the various parts of the English-speaking world."[1] His prescription was to "get into the heads of Canadians and Americans that a share in the burden of world government is just as great and glorious a responsibility as participation in the war."[1]

## Context

The letter is placed in the aftermath of the failed campaign, backed by [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]], to establish a [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]] as the answer to the "nationalist problem" blamed for the First World War.[1] Nationalist forces in America rejected the idea that the Constitution should be rendered obsolete and the plan for global governance failed — but, as Ehret notes, that did not stop the Round Table Movement from trying again, with Lothian among those pressing the case.[1]
