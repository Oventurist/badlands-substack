# Kim Jong Un

Kim Jong Un is the supreme leader of [[north-korea|North Korea]], and in the Badlands Brief's coverage he appears as the presiding figure at the opening of a museum in Pyongyang honoring North Korean soldiers who, according to the North Korean state account, helped "liberate" [[russia|Russia's]] Kursk region from Ukrainian forces.[1] Kim presided over memorial events attended by senior Russian officials, in what was described as one of the clearest public acknowledgments to date of Pyongyang's direct military role in the Ukraine war.[1]

## Kursk memorial and the museum

The museum ceremony was framed domestically as a legitimation of regime sacrifice and internationally as a signal of a hardened alliance structure opposing Western-backed [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the event as a "long-anticipated day" and as a major leap forward in what he called Kim's "character arc," noting that Kim was joined in Pyongyang by Russian leadership for the opening.[1]

According to that commentary, Kim placed on display a collection of Western-supplied armored vehicles captured from the Ukrainian military. The centerpiece is described as an M1A1 Abrams tank taken from Ukrainian forces — likely one of the 31 Abrams tanks sent to Kyiv by the Biden administration in 2023 — with a Bradley Fighting Vehicle also visible in photographs of the event, alongside Kim and Russian Defense Minister [[andrey-belousov|Andrey Belousov]].[1] A German-made Leopard 2 tank, captured by Russian forces and presented to Pyongyang as a gift from [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], is described as the other grand prize of the collection.[1]

## Badlands framing of Kim's arc

The Brief's commentary argues that the Kim family has long been a target of ridicule by Western media, and that the prior narrative of an imminent North Korean nuclear threat against the United States was defused when [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and Kim met in Singapore in 2018 and again at the DMZ a year later.[1] The commentary claims the [[cia|CIA]] had kept Kim ostracized from the world until Dennis Rodman's visits, and characterizes Kim today as surrounded by friends and "a genuinely happy guy," noting his Western suit and Western-styled office as symbolic details.[1]

The same commentary asserts that North Korean troops arrived at a critical juncture at Kursk, holding the northern front and preventing Ukrainian and NATO-backed forces from penetrating deeper into Russian territory, and that Putin has declared his intention to build monuments and statues honoring North Korean sacrifice.[1] It closes with a rhetorical demand that any peacekeeping force deployed to Israel be exclusively North Korean.[1]


## Speculation About Trump’s Second Inauguration

In January 2025 Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] named Kim Jong Un as one of two foreign leaders he would most like to see attend [[second-inauguration-of-donald-trump|Donald Trump's second inauguration]], while acknowledging the unlikelihood.[2] Another contributor compared the prospect of a Trump–[[vladimir-putin|Putin]] meeting to the moment Kim escorted Trump across the DMZ into North Korea during his first term.[2]

## Putin's June 2024 state visit to Pyongyang

In June 2024, North Korea's Korean Central News Agency and Russia simultaneously announced that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would make a two-day state visit to Pyongyang, the Russian leader's first trip to the country in 24 years, following months of speculation and amid international concern over military cooperation between the two states.[3] Kim had invited Putin to Pyongyang after travelling to a remote Siberian rocket launch facility to meet him in 2023.[3] Observers raised concerns about an arms arrangement in which Pyongyang would supply Moscow with badly needed munitions for the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] in exchange for economic assistance and technology transfers that would enhance the threat posed by Kim's nuclear weapons and missile programs.[3]

## Relationship with Putin and Trump

Badlands commentary traced the Kim–Putin relationship to the April 2019 Vladivostok summit, their first meeting, which followed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s June 2018 crossing of the DMZ escorted personally by Kim.[3] In February 2024, after learning that Kim admired Putin's personal Aurus limousine, the Russian president had one custom-made and shipped to Pyongyang for Kim's use, in violation of UN sanctions Putin had endorsed in 2017; Kim's sister Kim Yo Jong described the gift as a "clear demonstration of the special relationship" between the leaders.[3] Kim had also been shown the interior of the U.S. presidential limousine known as "The Beast" during his 2018 Singapore meeting with Trump.[3]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described an apparent personal rapport between Kim and Trump, citing a moment in which Trump joked about photographers making them look thin and the camera panned to Kim, and noting that Kim — educated at a Swiss boarding school and a longtime NBA fan — belonged to a generation for whom meeting Trump was an iconic milestone.[3] The same commentary noted reporting that Kim wished to sell North Korea's extensive munitions stockpile to Russia, aligning with claims by a former South Korean president that Kim was "desperate" to rid the country of its nuclear weapons.[3]

## Wonsan-Kalma resort

In July 2024, Kim was pictured grinning as he surveyed plans for a tourist resort under construction on North Korea's eastern coast, strolling along an immaculate white sand beach located next to a rocket-testing site.[4] The [[wonsan-kalma-tourist-zone|Wonsan-Kalma zone]], announced in 2015, had been furnished with luxury hotels and recreational facilities but suffered setbacks from construction-material shortages caused by toughened UN sanctions and COVID restrictions.[4] According to the Korean Central News Agency, Kim toured the site with top deputies and discussed preparations for an opening by May 2025, a date set by a ruling party meeting that January.[4]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] presented the resort as an instance of [[multipolar-devolution|Multipolar Devolution]] and as the fulfillment of promises exchanged with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2018, when Kim pledged a "Major Change" to his country after the DMZ meeting.[4] He again cited the memoirs of former South Korean president [[moon-jae-in|Moon Jae-in]], which quoted Kim as saying he had a daughter and did not want her generation to live with the burden of nuclear weapons.[4]

### Vladivostok meeting with Putin (September 2023)

Kim was expected to meet Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Vladivostok during the September 2023 Eastern Economic Forum, the same gathering at which Putin publicly mocked the United States over the prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5]

## Unconditional support for Russia (July 2025)

During Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]]'s three-day visit to Pyongyang in July 2025, Kim pledged "unconditional support" for Russia's war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and its preferred outcome, with both sides reaffirming plans to expand military ties under the defense pact he signed with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] the previous year.[6] According to the Korean Central News Agency, Kim said [[north-korea|North Korea]] "is ready to unconditionally support and encourage all the measures taken by the Russian leadership as regards the tackling of the root cause of the Ukrainian crisis."[6] Lavrov praised the growing military alliance and thanked Kim for deploying over 12,000 North Korean troops, who reportedly helped Moscow reclaim the Kursk region after a Ukrainian counteroffensive; South Korean intelligence estimated Pyongyang had also sent millions of artillery shells and was preparing to dispatch another 30,000 soldiers.[6]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read photographs of the Kim–Lavrov meeting as evidence of a shared joke between allies, arguing that Kim and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] are "homies," that Trump cut the strings of external control over Pyongyang and persuaded Kim to disarm and convert a missile testing site into a beach resort, and that Kim's transfer of his father's stockpiled munitions to Putin for the Ukraine war confirms the arrangement.[6] He cited Kim's statement to former South Korean President [[moon-jae-in|Moon Jae-in]] about dismantling the nuclear weapons program and pointed to Kim's western suit and tie in a recent North Korean government video — something he never wore before the 2018 Singapore Summit — as deliberate signal.[6]

## Kursk deployment order (April 2025)

In April 2025 North Korean state media reported that Kim had personally ordered his military to fight alongside Russian forces "to annihilate Ukrainian neo-Nazi occupiers and liberate the Kursk region."[7] Pyongyang officially confirmed the deployment on Monday, April 28, with the [[korean-central-news-agency|Korean Central News Agency]] citing a statement by the Central Military Commission that "the operations for liberating the Kursk area to repel the adventurous invasion of the Russian Federation by the Ukrainian authorities were victoriously concluded."[7] The troops served under the [[comprehensive-strategic-partnership-agreement|Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Agreement]] with Moscow.[7]

Russian Chief of the General Staff [[valery-gerasimov|Valery Gerasimov]] had praised the North Korean contribution in a report to President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] the previous week.[7] Russian accounts described the soldiers — nicknamed the "combat Buryats" — as training at Russian ranges, mastering drone operation, living in field conditions to avoid detection, and progressing from the third line of defense to frontline assaults, distinguishing themselves through coordination, discipline, endurance and "a fatalist disregard for death," with a strict rule never to be taken alive.[7] They were credited with contributing to the liberation of the Korenevsky District and fighting near Staraya and Novaya Sorochina before breaking through to Kurilovka.[7]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the confirmation as vindication for Kim and North Korea after years of Western vilification, noting these appeared to be the first combat reports on North Korean troops since the Korean War, and joking that the "NorKs" were "at [[trumpamania|Trump-a-Mania]], and running wild."[7]

## October 2024 Russia deployment imagery
Kim featured prominently in the imagery accompanying reports that North Korea had sent troops to Russia in October 2024.[8] A photograph circulated online by a Reddit moderator showed the purported "Special Buryat Brigade" — described by Politico as a 3,000-man battalion of North Koreans serving in the Ukraine theatre — posing before a Soviet monument in Russia in fatigues and carrying a flag bearing an image of Kim and Vladimir Putin shaking hands.[8] Pyongyang publicly dismissed the underlying deployment reports as groundless rumors at a United Nations General Assembly committee meeting.[8]

## Wonsan Kalma tourism project

In June 2025 state media reported that Kim was opening the [[wonsan-kalma|Wonsan Kalma]] beach resort on North Korea's east coast, a bet on tourism as a revenue source for the sanctioned state, with domestic visitors admitted from July 1, 2025 — six years after the resort's original completion date.[9] Kim grew up in luxury in Wonsan, where much of the country's elite keep private villas, and has sought to transform the town, which once hosted a missile testing site.[9] Badlands commentary used the opening to argue that mainstream media had misrepresented Kim.[9]


## North Korea as Russia's "most dangerous ally" (September 2024)

Speaking at an event in Kyiv in September 2024, [[kyrylo-budanov|Kyrylo Budanov]], head of Ukraine's military intelligence agency GUR, said that the Kremlin's most important wartime ally was not [[china|China]] but [[north-korea|North Korea]], an isolated state whose economy has been damaged by decades of sanctions.[10] "They supply huge amounts of artillery ammunition, which is critical for Russia," Budanov said, pointing to increased battlefield hostilities following such deliveries.[10] [[business-insider|Business Insider]] noted that the Ukraine war had allowed Kim to improve his position, including a valuable security pact formed with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Russia in June 2024.[10] Reporting also indicated Kim had been training with special forces.[10]

Badlands treated the framing with heavy irony, casting Kim as "The Rocket Man" entering [[trumpamania|Trumpamania]] 2024 in professional-wrestling terms and observing that he was sending older ammunition stocks to Russia while telling former South Korean President Moon that he sought disarmament and de-escalation with the West — a deal, in the Badlands reading, that would only be concluded with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] back in power.[10]

## Possible 2025 meeting with Trump

Speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One en route to Japan on Monday, October 27, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would extend his Asia trip that week if it meant meeting with Kim, saying he "got along great" with the North Korean leader and remained open to another encounter.[11] "I liked him. He liked me," Trump said. "If he wants to meet, I'll be in South Korea."[11]

The remarks followed comments Trump made the previous Friday welcoming another meeting with Kim, whom he first met in 2019, and noting that North Korea "has a lot of nuclear weapons but not a lot of telephone service."[11] "He probably knows I'm coming, right? Yeah, I'd be open to it, 100 percent," Trump added.[11]

Badlands commentary greeted the prospect enthusiastically, observing that Kim had appeared trim at the Beijing parade in early September and expressing hope that Trump would visit the [[wonsan-kalma|Wonsan Kalma]] Coastal Tourist Area — a beach that North Korea had traditionally used as a missile testing site and which Trump himself suggested converting into a resort during a 2018 exchange with Kim.[11]

## June 2024 Putin summit

In June 2024 Kim hosted Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Pyongyang for negotiations that produced an open Russian challenge to the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] sanctions regime against [[north-korea|North Korea]].[12] Speaking after the talks, Putin said the indefinite restrictive regime "inspired by the United States and its allies, must be revised," and pledged continued joint opposition to what he called sanctions strangulation.[12] At an accompanying press conference he compared the sanctions to the Nazi [[siege-of-leningrad|siege of Leningrad]].[12] Kim also presented Putin with a gift of dogs, an exchange widely circulated and read by some Badlands commentators as "dog comms," alongside a farewell scene on the tarmac.[12]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary treated the visit as historically significant rather than symbolic theater, describing it as a public relations campaign to reintroduce Kim to the Western public so that North Korea could eventually be recognized as part of an emerging free world.[12] Citing remarks by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] on the Devolution Power Hour, commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Kim as a young leader likely groomed to serve as a [[cia|CIA]] puppet whose strings had since been cut by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], leaving him in need of genuine mentorship from Putin as captain of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and neighboring head of state.[12] The episode is covered in detail at [[2024-putin-visit-to-north-korea-and-vietnam|the 2024 Putin visit to North Korea and Vietnam]].[12]

## The "Poop Balloon" counter-provocation (December 2024 assessment)

Reporting in December 2024 recast Kim's trash-filled balloon campaign against [[south-korea|South Korea]] as an act of counter-provocation with major geopolitical consequence rather than mere crudity.[13] According to allegations aired in the South Korean parliament, Seoul's defense minister [[kim-yong-hyun|Kim Yong-hyun]] and President [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]] had spent nearly a year attempting to provoke a North Korean first strike by sending military drones and balloons over Pyongyang to disperse propaganda flyers.[13] Kim's answer was to send balloons rather than munitions; when Yoon ordered strikes on their launch sites and further drone flights, Kim again responded only with more balloons.[13] South Korea's military leaders declined to treat the balloons as a grave threat and refused the strike order, leaving Yoon isolated after he declared martial law.[13]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the episode showed Kim "out-foxed the CIA and beat them at game theory," describing him as "a grand chess master of Game Theory" whose restraint both defused the crisis and destroyed the South Korean president attempting to escalate it.[13] He also noted with approval that North Korea's official statement on the crisis referred to Yoon as a "puppet" president.[13]

## Trash balloon campaign and missile tests (September 2024)

In September 2024 North Korea continued the campaign of trash-filled balloons that Kim devised in May of that year, a month after [[south-korea|South Korea]] sent 20 large balloons carrying more than 300,000 leaflets criticizing him — along with 2,000 USB sticks containing K-pop content — into [[north-korea|North Korea]].[14] A barrage launched between Sunday evening and early Monday morning led to flight suspensions at South Korea's main international airport, prompting Seoul's Joint Chiefs of Staff spokesman Col. Lee Sung-jun to warn of "stern military measures" if the North "crosses the line."[14]

Badlands Media treated the campaign as a comic tit-for-tat, likening it to a 1980s teen comedy in which the pranked misfits retaliate more humiliatingly than the original prank, and reserving its sharper criticism for [[reuters|Reuters]], which it said had meticulously tracked every wave of the balloon launches — counting more than 6,000 balloons, mapping flight patterns and termination points, commissioning animated graphics, and field-measuring the contents of the packages.[14] The outlet asked what would happen if comparable effort were applied to election integrity, government corruption, or Jeffrey Epstein.[14]

The brief also noted that corporate media had recently branded Kim as [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s "most dangerous ally," and that he had been testing new ballistic missiles with "super large" warheads — which Badlands framed jokingly as promotional material for the coming [[donald-trump|Trump]] election, then six weeks away.[14]

## Moon Jae-in memoir on denuclearization offer

In a memoir released in May 2024, titled "From the Periphery to the Centre," former South Korean president [[moon-jae-in|Moon Jae-in]] wrote that Kim had offered to give up his nuclear arsenal if America guaranteed his regime would survive.[15] Moon, who led South Korea for five years from 2017, was instrumental in brokering two high-profile summits between Kim and then-United States president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] aimed at securing Pyongyang's denuclearization in return for sanctions relief.[15] After the second summit collapsed in 2019, diplomatic outreach was abandoned, with inter-Korean relations falling to one of their worst points in years as Kim doubled down on weapons production and drew closer to Moscow.[15]

"Kim said he would forsake nuclear weapons if there was a guarantee of regime survival," Moon wrote, adding that he found the young North Korean leader "very honest."[15] Moon quoted Kim's reasoning: "I have a daughter and I do not wish her generation to live with nuclear weapons… Why would we continue to live in difficulty, under sanctions, with nuclear weapons if our security can be guaranteed?"[15]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that whoever had been controlling the Kim family lost that control during the Trump administration, and that Kim had since "rug-pulled" [[john-bolton|John Bolton]]'s hoped-for confrontation, offering rational common sense and an appeal as a father instead of the "madman" role assigned to him.[15] The commentary expressed hope that Kim would work with Trump to transform North Korea, citing Trump's remark that North Korean beaches had the potential to become world-class resorts.[15]

## April 2024 call to prepare for war

On Wednesday, April 10, 2024, Kim said that the unstable geopolitical situation surrounding North Korea meant it was time to be more prepared for war than ever, speaking during an inspection of the country's main military university.[16] He gave field guidance at Kim Jong Il University of Military and Politics — named for his father, who died in 2011 — which the KCNA news agency described as the "highest seat of military education" in the country.[16] Kim told staff and students that "if the enemy opts for military confrontation with the DPRK, the DPRK will deal a death-blow to the enemy without hesitation by mobilizing all means in its possession."[16] The remarks came as North Korea stepped up weapons development and forged closer military and political ties with Russia, allegedly aiding Moscow's war in Ukraine in exchange for help with strategic military projects.[16]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded to the item with sympathy rather than alarm, writing that Kim had appeared genuinely happy and had lost weight while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was in office and seemed to have relapsed into a role he did not want to play once the Biden phase began.[16] He placed Kim within the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and expressed hope that Kim and Trump would one day develop North Korea into a tourist destination and first-world nation.[16]

## Suicide drone inspection (August 2024)

In August 2024 state media reported that Kim had inspected small new "suicide drones" and urged the military to incorporate artificial intelligence in their development.[17] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the item lightly, saying he was covering the story mainly so he could post photographs of the [[north-korea|North Korean]] leader, and offered as "proof that Un is still our guy" the observation that Kim did not begin wearing Western suits until after meeting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the DMZ.[17]

## February 2025: Condemnation of the Gaza takeover proposal

On Wednesday, February 12, 2025, North Korean state media denounced U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s proposal to take over Gaza and relocate Palestinians as bluster, accusing Washington of robbery.[18] A commentary carried by the [[korean-central-news-agency|Korean Central News Agency]] said the slim hopes of Palestinians for safety and peace were being crushed by the proposal, and declared that "the world is now boiling like a porridge pot over the U.S.' bombshell declaration" — without naming Trump directly.[18] The commentary also criticized calls to take over the Panama Canal and Greenland and the decision to rename the "Gulf of Mexico" the "Gulf of America," urging that "the U.S. should awaken from its anachronistic daydream and stop at once the act of encroaching upon the dignity and sovereignty of other countries and nations," and calling the United States a "ferocious robber" and an "accomplice" to Israel's actions in Gaza.[18]

Badlands commentary read the omission of Trump's name as a deliberate signal that Kim was targeting neoconservatives in Trump's orbit rather than the president himself, likening the rhetoric to professional-wrestling heel work even as Trump spoke fondly of their friendship and of wanting to catch up by phone.[18]

## Severing of inter-Korean links (October 2024)

On October 15, 2024, [[north-korea|North Korea]] blew up the northern sections of the unused road and rail routes that once connected it to [[south-korea|South Korea]], days after Pyongyang claimed the South had flown drones over the capital.[19] The Associated Press described the choreographed demolition as underlining growing anger at South Korea's conservative government and as following Kim's vow to sever relations with the South and abandon the goal of peaceful Korean unification.[19] Observers considered a preemptive large-scale attack unlikely, since near-certain massive retaliation by superior US and South Korean forces would threaten Pyongyang's survival.[19] Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that Kim, alongside Vladimir Putin and other members of what he called the Sovereign Alliance, would help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] actualize the story of "Trump the Peacemaker."[19]

## Announcement of troops for Russia (June 2024)

In late June 2024, Kim announced that he would send North Korean troops to fight alongside Russian forces in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], a move circulated via BRICS-focused accounts on X.[20] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] had joked on the program Breaking History that the gesture must be symbolic, since North Korean troops could not be more effective than battle-hardened Russian troops, but retracted the quip after seeing footage circulating on X — offering an apology to "Mr. Supreme Leader."[20] He folded the deployment into a broader speculative scenario in which [[nato|NATO]] and the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] confront a coalition of Arabs, Russians and North Koreans in the Middle East.[20]

## Vladivostok summit toast (September 2023)

At the [[2023-kim-jong-un-putin-vladivostok-summit|September 13, 2023 Vladivostok meeting]] with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], Kim praised a "heroic" Russian army that he said Putin would lead to a "great victory" over its enemies.[21] Raising a toast at an official dinner, Kim said, "We are confident that the Russian army and people will win a great victory in the just fight to punish evil groups who pursue hegemony, expansion, and ambition," and added on Russian television, "I will always be standing with Russia."[21] Putin replied that "An old friend is better than two new ones" and hailed "the future strengthening of cooperation and friendship between our countries."[21]

Roughly two hours of one-on-one talks preceded the dinner, with heavy speculation that they concerned Moscow buying North Korean ammunition for use in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[21] Putin toured Russia's Far East spaceport, the Vostochny Cosmodrome, with Kim and observed that "the leader of the DPRK shows great interest in rocket technology, and they are trying to develop (their presence in) space."[21] As the two leaders — cast by the West as "pariahs" — prepared to meet, Pyongyang fired two ballistic missiles into the sea in a further warning to [[south-korea|South Korea]] and the United States.[21]

## Badlands assessment of the balloon campaign

Badlands commentary in August 2025 credited Kim Jong Un with a "PsyAct" that subverted escalation with South Korea by baiting President Yoon Suk Yeol into a martial law gambit that military leadership found too trivial to justify a kinetic response.[23] The same commentary noted his forces' role in the Russian region of Kursk and an offer to send 6,000 engineers and builders to assist with reconstruction there.[23]

## Alleged CIA hostage status (Badlands analysis)

Badlands contributor Joe Lange interprets [[qanon|Q]] drop 1245's instruction to "define hostage" as indicating that Kim Jong Un was himself a hostage of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] rather than an independent dictator, and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] set him free as part of making the world safer.[27] In this account the mechanism of control was technical: three CIA [[snow-white-supercomputers|supercomputers]] operating inside [[north-korea|North Korea]], installed with the help of [[eric-schmidt|Eric Schmidt]] and [[bill-richardson|Bill Richardson]], which [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] took offline.[27] Three North Korean generals were also freed from CIA control and closed the agency's "pathway" to those systems, which Lange offers as the reason [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] was unable to reach Kim Jong Un secretly after the puppet strings were cut.[27]


## 2019 DMZ meeting with Trump (Badlands account)

In June 2019 Kim met [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] at the Korean Demilitarized Zone, an encounter that made Trump the first sitting United States president to enter the DMZ.[30] Badlands Media described the [[2019-trump-kim-dmz-meeting|meeting]] as a demonstration of Trump's unconventional foreign policy that began a process toward peace with [[north-korea|North Korea]] and led Kim to back away from his nuclear rhetoric.[30]
