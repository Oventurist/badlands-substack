# Christopher Stevens

Christopher "Chris" Stevens was the United States Ambassador to Libya who was killed during the September 11, 2012 assault on American facilities in Benghazi.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Role in the Libya weapons network

According to the article, Stevens served as the **liaison** between weapons dealers and Al Qaeda elements in post-Gaddafi Libya, and helped coordinate the transfer of weapons from Libya to Syria to arm [[isis|ISIS]] fighters seeking to overthrow Bashar al-Assad.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) This is described as a secret operation run by the [[state-department|State Department]] under [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and the [[cia|CIA]], with the full approval of President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Stevens is said to have worked directly with the new Libyan government associated with [[abdelhakim-belhadj|Abdelhakim Belhadj]] and Libyan leader [[mustafa-abdul-jalil|Mustafa Abdul Jalil]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Benghazi and his death

Stevens was based at the embassy in Tripoli, which had greater security, yet was present at the lightly protected Benghazi compound on the night of the attack — a fact the article treats as central.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) His last known public meeting was with Turkish Consul General Ali Sait Akin, whom he escorted out the consulate front gate roughly an hour before the assault began at about 9:35 p.m. local time; the Libyan-flagged vessel Al Entisar, reportedly carrying weapons for Syrian rebels, had docked at the Turkish port of Iskenderun on September 6, five days earlier.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article argues Stevens was **sacrificed**: an insider who "thought he was untouchable" but became expendable once congressional and media scrutiny of the weapons pipeline intensified during the spring and summer of 2012.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Stevens died alongside information management officer Sean Smith and former Navy SEALs [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]] during an extended assault by more than 100 Islamist militants.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) It was the first time in 33 years that an American ambassador died in the line of duty.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Career and background

Stevens took up the ambassadorship only in May 2012, but he was not new to the region. An Arabic and French speaker, he had been a Peace Corps volunteer in Morocco and, after working in international trade law in Washington, served in Israel, Egypt and Saudi Arabia during 21 years with the [[state-department|State Department]]; he had also been the number two U.S. diplomat in Libya from 2007 to 2009.[2] His experience and credibility in Libya proved invaluable during the revolution, and his work "helped convince the Obama Administration to provide conclusive support to the besieged rebels."[2] The Badlands account stresses his trade-law specialty and his postings to three major purchasers of American weapons as evidence that his function was arms-related, given that the State Department authorizes sales of military hardware from private U.S. defense companies to foreign governments.[2]

## Liaison to the Libyan opposition

In March 2011 Stevens became the official U.S. liaison to the al-Qaeda-linked Libyan opposition, working directly with [[abdelhakim-belhadj|Abdelhakim Belhadj]] of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] — more than a year before he was named ambassador.[2] Belhadj also led the February 17th Martyrs Brigade, named for the day the anti-Gaddafi uprisings began; Stevens became its liaison the following month, and the same brigade was later tasked with helping secure the Benghazi compound where he was killed.[2] Some LIFG fighters reportedly participated in the attack that took his life.[2]

Stevens and his entourage arrived in Benghazi on April 5, 2011 aboard the Greek ship Aegean Pearl, with an assignment to "quietly develop a relationship with resistance forces."[2] One week before he was sworn in as ambassador, arms dealer [[marc-turi|Marc Turi]] emailed him seeking help with a State Department permit; Stevens replied that he would "keep it in mind and share it with my colleagues in Washington."[2] Contemporaneous reporting placed Stevens one step — Belhadj — from the Benghazi man who captained a Libyan ship carrying 400 tons of heavy weapons, including SA-7 surface-to-air missiles, to [[turkey|Turkey]] for onward delivery to Syrian rebels.[2]

The Badlands article rejects the media narrative that Stevens died trying to recover Gaddafi-era missiles, arguing instead that he was the middleman between weapons dealers and Al Qaeda, first in Libya and then in Syria, and that the light security at the Benghazi compound reflected [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s deliberate effort to keep the operation's footprint small and secret.[2]
