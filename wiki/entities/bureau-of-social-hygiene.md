# Bureau of Social Hygiene

The **Bureau of Social Hygiene** was the organization taken over by [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]] shortly after he created the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] in 1913, and given the task of conducting "research and education on birth control, maternal health, and sex education."[1]

In 1913 Cettie Rockefeller — John D.'s wife and John D. Jr.'s mother — gave $25,000 to the bureau to "promote instruction in social hygiene for female students around the country."[1]

The bureau in turn funded [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]]'s proposal for birth control clinical studies by the [[american-birth-control-league|American Birth Control League]], the organization that eventually became [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]]. Badlands Media notes that Sanger advocated limiting "dysgenic stocks" including Black, Hispanic, American Indian and Catholic populations, as well as "slum dwellers" such as Jewish immigrants.[1]
