# Soros Fund Management

Soros Fund Management is the investment firm founded by billionaire financier [[george-soros|George Soros]]. In February 2024 the firm drew attention in the Badlands News Brief coverage after Bloomberg reported that it was positioned to become the largest shareholder of the radio and podcast company [[audacy|Audacy Inc.]] once Audacy emerged from bankruptcy.[1]

## Audacy debt-to-equity position

According to bankruptcy court filings cited by Bloomberg, Soros Fund Management acquired more than $400 million of Audacy's highest-ranking debt, making it by far the largest member of a lender group planning to swap its loans for equity in the broadcaster.[1] Audacy publicly framed the conversion as "a significant vote of confidence in our company and the future of the radio and audio business," while Soros Fund Management and lawyers for the creditor group did not immediately respond to requests for comment.[1]

## Broader media portfolio

The Audacy position was described as the latest in a series of media investments by the fund. Soros Fund Management had previously participated in the lender group that bought [[vice-media|Vice Media]] out of bankruptcy in 2023, and had earlier taken a minority stake in the podcast company [[crooked-media|Crooked Media]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the transaction operated simultaneously on "Actual and Narrative grounds": acquiring the debt of one of the most leveraged and largest radio conglomerates in the United States and converting it to equity would give the fund direct influence over the network's business planning and, through that, over its "Narrative Deployments."[1] Burning Bright characterized the combination of radio, Vice Media and podcasting stakes as positioning Soros interests across information-distribution arms reaching all demographics ahead of the 2024 US election cycle, while suggesting the visibility of such headlines was itself training the wider public to recognize what concentrated information control looks like.[1]
