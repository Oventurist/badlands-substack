# Energoatom

Energoatom is Ukraine's state nuclear power operator, responsible for the country's nuclear generating fleet and associated procurement.[1] The company became the focus of corruption allegations in 2025 concerning procurement filed in the name of the [[zaporozhye-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant]] (ZNPP) after that facility had passed out of Ukrainian control.[1]

## Zaporozhye procurement allegations

Ukrainian lawmaker Vladimir Ariev claimed that officials in Kiev embezzled millions from contracts filed for the Russia-controlled ZNPP, Europe's largest nuclear power plant, which has been under Russian control since March 2022.[1] Kiev has rejected Russia's authority over the site as an "occupation," while Moscow has stated that the plant was incorporated into Russia's nuclear sector and that transferring control back to Ukraine is not possible.[1]

Ariev said more than 200 million hryvnia ($4.7 million) in goods and services were formally registered in the plant's name months after it came under Russian control, even though Kiev had no way to deliver anything to the site.[1] Speaking at a parliamentary commission on economic security, he claimed Energoatom continued filing procurement paperwork for the plant through most of 2022 despite having no physical authority over it: "From March 15 to August 25, 2022, Energoatom made purchases for the occupied Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant. Specifically, this included 210 million hryvnia for food and canteen supplies."[1]

## Links to the wider graft scandal

Badlands commentary placed the allegations alongside other reported corruption in the Ukrainian government, noting that Justice Minister [[german-galushchenko|German Galushchenko]] had been dismissed after being implicated in a major graft probe described as a "$100 million kickback scheme," and that Galushchenko had been Vice President of Energoatom before joining the government in 2021.[1] There were also calls for President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] to fire his chief of staff, [[andriy-yermak|Andrey Yermak]], who was alleged to be tied to the same scandal.[1] The commentary concluded that "the entire Ukrainian government is one giant organized crime syndicate," and questioned the judgment of the US [[congress|Congress]] — both Republican and Democrat — for having ardently supported Ukraine for more than three years.[1]
