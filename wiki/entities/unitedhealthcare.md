# UnitedHealthcare

UnitedHealthcare is one of the largest health insurance companies in the United States and a subsidiary of United Health Group.[1]

## Killing of its chief executive

On December 4, 2024, the company's chief executive, [[brian-thompson]], was shot dead outside the Hilton hotel on Avenue of the Americas in midtown Manhattan, where he had arrived early for UnitedHealthcare's annual investor conference.[1] Police characterized the shooting as a premeditated and targeted attack rather than a random act of violence, and opened a manhunt for the masked gunman.[1] The event placed the insurer, and by extension the American health insurance industry, at the center of national attention during the transition period between the [[joe-biden]] and [[donald-trump]] administrations.[1]

## Aftermath of the Brian Thompson killing

Following the fatal shooting of chief executive [[brian-thompson|Brian Thompson]] outside the New York Hilton Midtown on December 4, 2024, investigators reported that 9mm shell casings recovered at the scene had been engraved with the words "deny," "defend," and "depose" — an apparent reference to industry-critical shorthand for how insurers handle claims.[2] See [[deny-defend-depose|Deny, Defend, Depose]].[2] The [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]] released maskless photographs of the suspect on December 5 as the manhunt continued.[2]

The killing triggered a wave of hostile commentary directed at health insurers generally. Former *Washington Post* reporter [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]] wrote "And people wonder why we want these executives dead" alongside a report on [[blue-cross-blue-shield|Blue Cross Blue Shield]] limiting anesthesia coverage, and argued there is "very justified hatred" toward insurance company chief executives for what she described as an unfathomable amount of death and suffering caused by a broken system.[2]

## Aftermath of the Thompson killing (September 2025)

The prosecution over the killing of UnitedHealthcare chief executive [[brian-thompson|Brian Thompson]] advanced in September 2025 when Judge [[gregory-carro|Gregory Carro]] dismissed the terrorism charges against [[luigi-mangione|Luigi Mangione]] in New York State's case while retaining the second-degree murder charges.[3] Carro also rejected as premature the defense's argument that the state case and a parallel federal death penalty prosecution constituted double jeopardy.[3] Coverage noted that Mangione had attracted a following, largely on the political left, as a stand-in for public frustration with the health insurance industry — an indication of the reputational position the company occupied after the killing.[3]
