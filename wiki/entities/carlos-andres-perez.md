# Carlos Andrés Pérez

Carlos Andrés Pérez Rodríguez (27 October 1922 – 25 December 2010), known as CAP and often called El Gocho for his Andean origins, was a Venezuelan politician who served as president from 12 March 1974 to 12 March 1979 and again from 2 February 1989 to 21 May 1993. He was a founder of Acción Democrática, the dominant Venezuelan party of the later twentieth century, and his first presidency was dubbed "Saudi Venezuela" for the prosperity generated by petroleum exports.[1]

The Badlands article treats him as the political counterpart to [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]]'s banking work in Caracas, asking how much of Venezuela's "oil income" was in fact drug profits hidden in transactions with CIA shell companies.[1]

## Election

Nominated by AD in 1973, Pérez ran a vibrant and triumphalist campaign, among the first in Venezuelan history to use American advertising gurus and political consultants — which the author suggests points to Bush and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] installing another puppet government.[1] He was elected that December with 48.7 percent against his main rival's 36.7 percent, on a turnout of 97 percent of eligible voters, a level never since achieved and which the article calls suspicious.[1]

## Corruption and downfall

By the end of his first term his reputation was tarnished by accusations of excessive and disorderly government spending, and by allegations of corruption and influence trafficking involving his intimate circle — including his mistress Cecilia Matos and the campaign financiers known as the "Twelve Apostles."[1] By the 1978 elections many citizens felt the post-1973 petrodollar influx had been mismanaged: the country imported 80 percent of the foodstuffs it consumed, agricultural production was stagnant and the national debt had skyrocketed.[1]

In February 1989, at the start of his second term, he accepted an [[international-monetary-fund|International Monetary Fund]] proposal known as the Washington consensus in return for a $4.5 billion loan, weeks after his 1988 election victory — an act the article describes as selling the country out to the cabal's IMF after the oil profits had been blown or stolen.[1]

On 20 March 1993 Attorney General Ramón Escovar Salom brought action against Pérez for embezzling 250 million bolívars from a presidential discretionary fund, the *partida secreta*; his trial concluded in May 1996 with a 28-month sentence. In 1998 he was prosecuted again for embezzlement of public funds after secret joint bank accounts held with Cecilia Matos were discovered in New York — prompting the author to wonder whether he, like [[raul-salinas|Raúl Salinas]], had a private banker at Citibank.[1] On 20 December 2001, while he was in the Dominican Republic, a Caracas court ordered his detention on further embezzlement charges, and on 3 February 2002 his extradition was formally requested. He then self-exiled in Miami, Florida — the same city [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] made his base after leaving Venezuela — and became one of the most vehement opponents of President Hugo Chávez.[1]
