# Carlos Andrés Pérez

Carlos Andrés Pérez Rodríguez (27 October 1922 – 25 December 2010), known as CAP and often called El Gocho for his Andean origins, was a Venezuelan politician and president of Venezuela from 12 March 1974 to 12 March 1979 and again from 2 February 1989 to 21 May 1993. He was a founder of Acción Democrática, the dominant Venezuelan political party of the later twentieth century, and his first presidency was called the "Saudi Venezuela" for the prosperity brought by enormous petroleum export income.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

He was president while [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] ran [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]]'s Caracas branch, prompting the article to ask how much of that "oil income" was actually drug profits hidden in transactions with CIA shell companies.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Election

Nominated by AD in 1973, Pérez ran a vibrant and triumphalist campaign, among the first in the country's history to use American advertising gurus and political consultants — which the article suggests were supplied by [[george-hw-bush|Bush]] and the [[cia|CIA]] to install another puppet government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) He was elected that December with 48.7 percent against his main rival's 36.7 percent, on a turnout of 97 percent of eligible voters, a level never since achieved and which the article treats as suspicious.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Corruption and downfall

By the end of his first term his reputation was tarnished by accusations of excessive and disorderly government spending, with allegations of corruption and influence trafficking involving his intimate circle — his mistress Cecilia Matos and the campaign financiers known as the "Twelve Apostles."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) By the 1978 elections there was a widespread sense that the post-1973 petrodollar influx had been mismanaged: the country imported 80 percent of the foodstuffs it consumed, agricultural production was stagnant, and the national debt had skyrocketed.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

In February 1989, at the start of his second term, he accepted an [[imf|International Monetary Fund]] proposal known as the Washington consensus in return for a US $4.5 billion loan, weeks after his 1988 election victory — which the article frames as selling the country to the cabal's IMF after squandering the boom.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

On 20 March 1993 Attorney General Ramón Escovar Salom brought action against Pérez for embezzling 250 million bolívars from a presidential discretionary fund, the *partida secreta*; his trial concluded in May 1996 with a 28-month sentence.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) In 1998 he was prosecuted again for embezzlement of public funds after secret joint accounts held with Cecilia Matos were discovered in New York — prompting the article to wonder whether he, like [[raul-salinas|Raúl Salinas]], had a private banker at Citibank.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) On 20 December 2001, while in the Dominican Republic, a Caracas court ordered his detention on further embezzlement charges; extradition was formally requested on 3 February 2002, after which he self-exiled in Miami, Florida and became one of the most vehement opponents of Hugo Chávez.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

The article stresses the parallel with Mexico's Carlos Salinas — popular, then corrupt, then self-exiled — calls it a CIA template, and notes that Pérez chose as his refuge the same city where Jeb Bush had established his base after leaving Venezuela.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)
