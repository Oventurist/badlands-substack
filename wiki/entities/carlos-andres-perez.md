# Carlos Andrés Pérez

Carlos Andrés Pérez Rodríguez (1922–2010), known as CAP or "El Gocho," was a Venezuelan politician who served as president from 1974 to 1979 and again from 1989 to 1993. In the Badlands Media article [[a-family-affair]], he is portrayed as a corrupt leader installed or supported by CIA-linked networks, whose presidency facilitated oil-boom embezzlement and who later fled to miami, the same city where [[jeb-bush]] established his base after Venezuela.

## First presidency and oil boom

Pérez's first term (1974–1979) was known as "Saudi Venezuela" due to enormous petroleum export income. According to wikipedia, his administration was later tarnished by accusations of excessive government spending, disorderly spending, and corruption involving his mistress cecilia matos and financiers known as the "Twelve Apostles." By the 1978 elections, many citizens believed petrodollars had not been properly managed, with the country importing 80 percent of its food and national debt skyrocketing.

## Second presidency and IMF

In February 1989, at the start of his second term, Pérez accepted an International Monetary Fund proposal known as the washington consensus. In return, the IMF offered Venezuela a $4.5 billion loan. The article frames this as a betrayal of Venezuelan sovereignty, arguing that Pérez had "stolen" oil profits during the boom years and then "sold the country out to the cabal's IMF" due to massive debt.

## Embezzlement and exile

On March 20, 1993, Attorney General Ramón Escovar Salom introduced action against Pérez for the embezzlement of 250 million bolivars from a presidential discretionary fund, or partida secreta. Pérez's trial concluded in May 1996 with a sentence of 28 months in prison. In 1998, he was prosecuted again on charges of embezzlement after secret joint bank accounts held with Cecilia Matos were discovered in New York. In 2001, while in the Dominican Republic, a Caracas court ordered his detention; he self-exiled to Miami, where he became a vocal opponent of [[hugo-chavez]].

## Connection to Jeb Bush

The article notes that Jeb Bush was a leading banker in Caracas during Pérez's second presidency and that the two men's paths intersected through the Venezuelan oil industry and corrupt financial networks. The source draws a parallel between Pérez and [[carlos-salinas]], both described as corrupt leaders who fled to Miami after their regimes collapsed, and both connected to the Bush family network.
