# William Randolph Hearst

William Randolph Hearst, referred to in the article as Randolph Hearst, was an American newspaper mogul and the owner of Hearst Communications, which controlled thirty major newspapers in the largest cities in America.[1]

## Role in popularizing technocracy

By the early 1930s Hearst had become increasingly desperate to save his media empire in the wake of a deep economic depression and of reputational damage caused by his own papers' sensationalized stories, faked interviews and faked pictures. Badlands Media argues that this style of reporting distorted real events, leaving readers unable to recognize fake news — and largely indifferent to it — with the wider world detached from reality, a dynamic the author explicitly likens to the modern media environment.[1]

In the same period, [[nicholas-murray-butler|Nicholas Murray Butler]], president of [[columbia-university|Columbia University]], was endorsing a new economic system devised by scientists and engineers and promoted as a cure for America's economic troubles, replacing capitalism and free markets. That system was called [[technocracy|Technocracy]], and it piqued Hearst's interest.[1]

Seeing an opportunity to capitalize on growing public interest, Hearst decided to publish a series of articles exploring technocracy's potential. The articles generated widespread curiosity and sparked intense debate among intellectuals and the general public alike, helping establish technocracy as a mainstream topic of American discussion decades before figures such as [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] elaborated the idea into the "technetronic society."[1]
