# Temple Mount

The Temple Mount in Jerusalem is the elevated platform holding the [[dome-of-the-rock|Dome of the Rock]] and, in tradition, the site of [[solomons-temple|Solomon's Temple]]. Sir [[charles-warren|Charles Warren]] drew a layout of the Temple Mount and excavated the caverns beneath it — the former Templar headquarters in Jerusalem — on behalf of Edward VII.[1]

Warren's dig unearthed an array of caverns and tunnels he hypothesized to be conduits for mass human sacrifice overseen by an ancient priesthood, and in December 1867 he recorded that a shaft down to the water of the virgin's fount shed light on the purpose of the rock-cut canals around Jerusalem, which he took as proof that they had conducted away "the refuse and blood from the temple."[1] He believed the two small caverns under the sacrificial rock and the tunnels running from the lowest cavern were designed to infuse the soil of Jerusalem with the blood of her children in a pact with dark spirit forces.[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] connects these beliefs to the accusation that the Gnostic order of [[knights-templar|Templars]] was caught worshipping Baphomet beneath the Temple Mount in the early 14th century.[1]
