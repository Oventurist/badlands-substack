# John Riddle

John Riddle was a Texas attorney and thrift borrower identified by Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] as a link between the [[sunrise-savings-and-loan|Sunrise Savings]] failure and the mechanics of the wider [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan]] fraud.[1]

A major factor in Sunrise's collapse was $15 million in defaulted loans to Riddle, who was a friend of [[walter-mischer|Walter Mischer]], the Houston banker and longtime political fundraiser for [[george-hw-bush|George Bush]].[1] Riddle was also one of the five largest defaulters on thrift loans in the nation.[1]

In Dallas litigation a federal judge awarded a thrift more than $464 million in damages arising from civil fraud and racketeering lawsuits against borrowers from an associate institution, Western Savings Association.[1] The Sunbelt lawsuit accused Riddle, along with Richard Dover, of completing a series of real estate transactions known as [[land-flip|land flips]] that raised the value of property and created illusory profits for Western Savings.[1] Lange writes that Riddle's case reveals the whole scam: Bush's friend and many others across the country were buying real estate and flipping it among associates to manufacture collateral for loans they never intended to repay, laundering money in the process.[1]
