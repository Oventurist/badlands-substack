# Mara Gay

Mara Gay is a member of the editorial board of [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] and a frequent television commentator whose December 2023 remarks on [[msnbc|MSNBC]] about the disqualification of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the Colorado ballot drew sharp criticism from legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]].[1]

## Morning Joe remarks on Trump's disqualification

Appearing on MSNBC's *Morning Joe*, Gay was asked how she would respond to those who had questioned the constitutional basis for using the [[fourteenth-amendment|14th Amendment]] to bar Trump from state ballots.[1] She answered by asking, "Why are you standing with confederates who betrayed this country?" and characterized objections to the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s ruling as reflecting "the spirit of those confederates rather than the Americans who came together after a long and brutal Civil War that was fought to keep the Union together."[1] She argued that the post-war Americans "clearly saw a threat in ex-confederates running for office" and had amended the Constitution "to prevent those traitors from running for office."[1]

Turley wrote that the exchange "captured the unhinged coverage" of the Colorado disqualification, noting that the underlying 14th Amendment theory had already been rejected by jurists in various states, including many Democrats, and that Gay's framing recast anyone raising democratic or constitutional objections as "modern day confederates."[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated Gay's comments as an escalation of a previously "subtle theme" in coverage of the 14th Amendment challenges, in which [[jefferson-davis|Jefferson Davis]] was offered as a reasonable analogue for Trump.[1] Ashe argued that Gay had extended the comparison so that mere opposition to the Colorado ruling constituted modern confederate sympathy — implying, under "state propaganda," that January 6 had been a civil war — and predicted that such "dangerously hyperbolic and rapidly escalating rhetoric" would intensify into 2024 as the establishment failed in its effort to stop Trump.[1]
