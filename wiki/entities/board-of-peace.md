# Board of Peace

The Board of Peace is the body associated with the phased plan to assume administrative control of Gaza under the October 2025 U.S.-brokered [[gaza-ceasefire|ceasefire]] framework.[1]

## Phase Two dispute

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] intended to tell President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that the Board of Peace's Phase Two plan to take control of Gaza will not happen, and to press instead for immediate U.S. military action against [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Formal launch and charter

By January 2026 the Board of Peace had been formally launched as a new international body chaired by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], initially established to oversee postwar reconstruction and governance in Gaza under a fragile ceasefire following the Hamas-led October 7, 2023 attack and the war that followed.[2] It was backed by a November 2025 [[united-nations|United Nations]] Security Council resolution, with Russia and China abstaining, and its mandate was officially limited to Gaza until the end of 2027.[2] Under that mandate the board supervises a Palestinian technocratic administration and a separate 11-member Gaza Executive Committee that handles day-to-day operations.[2]

The board's draft charter, however, omits any specific mention of Gaza, criticizes past "failed" institutions — implicitly the United Nations — and positions the body as a "nimble and effective" alternative for global conflict resolution, with potential expansion beyond the Middle East.[2] Critics accordingly described the project as an attempt by Trump to establish his own version of the United Nations.[2]

## Davos signing ceremony

A high-profile signing ceremony was scheduled for Thursday at 10:30 a.m. during the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, intended to formally launch phase two of Trump's Gaza peace efforts.[2] Invitations were extended to leaders of roughly 60 countries, including European states (France, Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, Hungary, Poland, Canada), Middle Eastern states (Egypt, Jordan, [[turkey|Turkey]], [[qatar|Qatar]]), and others such as India, Pakistan, Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Australia, Argentina, Paraguay, Thailand, Vietnam, Morocco, Albania, Cyprus, Greece, Singapore and Uzbekistan.[2]

Confirmed acceptances included Hungary, whose Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]] announced unequivocal support, Kazakhstan under President [[kassym-jomart-tokayev|Kassym-Jomart Tokayev]], Belarus under President [[alexander-lukashenko|Alexander Lukashenko]], as well as Argentina, Vietnam and Morocco.[2] Italy's [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] and Canada's [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] expressed readiness to contribute in principle, while the United Kingdom under [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] signaled willingness regarding Gaza's second ceasefire phase.[2]

Participation nonetheless remained limited. France declined, citing conflicts with its UN commitments and the charter's broader scope; Poland raised concerns over the inclusion of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]; and many leaders avoided public commitments amid fears the board could undermine the United Nations' authority as the sole universal body for peace and security.[2] Putin received an invitation, and the Kremlin — through spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] — said it was seeking to "clarify all nuances" with Washington before responding, while Russia's special envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]] confirmed on X that Putin had accepted.[2] Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was invited and his office confirmed receipt, but he did not attend the Davos ceremony.[2]

## Israeli non-participation and the first DC meeting (February 2026)

In February 2026, Israeli cabinet minister [[ze-ev-elkin|Ze'ev Elkin]] of the Finance Ministry asserted that Israel would not financially contribute to the Board of Peace's Gaza reconstruction effort, which [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited as one in a series of Israeli slights against [[donald-trump|President Trump]] — following the formal rejection of the board the previous month by top officials in [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s government, including Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]].[3]

The Brief also noted that Trump had hosted the first Board of Peace meeting in Washington, DC the preceding week, at which he indirectly called out [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] and [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] for their attacks on Emir Tamim of [[qatar|Qatar]], a Board-aligned partner in the peace process.[3]

## Charter ratification at Davos (January 2026)

On Thursday, January 22, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held a signing ceremony at Davos to formally ratify the "Charter of the Board of Peace," which establishes the body as an "official international organization" with Trump serving as Chairman.[4] "The first steps toward a brighter day for the Middle East, and a much safer future for the world are unfolding right before your very eyes," Trump said.[4]

Trump stated that permanent members would pay $1 billion each and that, at that point, no permanent [[united-nations|UN]] Security Council member other than the United States had committed.[4] Named participants included [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], and [[tony-blair|Tony Blair]].[4] Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] accepted an invitation to join despite prior objections to the composition of the [[gaza-executive-board|Gaza Executive Board]], according to Israeli media citing unnamed Israeli officials.[4] Reuters reported that some major US allies balked at the proposal, though Badlands noted those reports appeared to link back to an October 2025 UN paper.[4]

Kushner presented a multi-phase reconstruction and economic plan for Gaza during the ceremony, aiming to raise Gaza's GDP and increase average annual household incomes over the following decade.[4] He closed by calling on several groups, including Israelis, to stop persecuting pro-Palestine activists online and to "give Trump 30 days" before another round of criticism.[4]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued Trump had strong-armed the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] into hosting the founding of an organization standing in direct opposition to the WEF's core mission.[4] Rubio used the occasion to explain how ineffective transnational organizations typically are, even between two earnest parties.[4] The appearance of Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] alongside Turkish Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] was read as a strategic symbol of Saudi-Turkish cooperation following their assistance in negotiating a Syria peace deal.[4] Observers also noted that the Board of Peace logo is essentially a rebranding of the United Nations emblem, which Trump had said the Board "may" end up replacing.[4]

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had not joined the board as of the ceremony, but met with Witkoff and Kushner; Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov described the talks as "substantive, constructive and very frank."[4] A first Russia-US-Ukraine working group meeting on security issues was scheduled for Abu Dhabi on January 23, 2026.[4] Separately, an Arab diplomat told Israeli press that Gulf appetite for deeper Gaza involvement had fallen sharply amid Israel's withholding of over $4 billion in tax revenue from the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], though Saudi Arabia and the UAE retained Board of Peace representatives.[4]

## First Washington meeting (February 19, 2026)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] convened the first meeting of the Board of Peace in Washington on February 19, 2026.[5] "What we're doing is very simple: peace. It's called the Board of Peace," Trump said, adding that "some of these leaders that are with us have helped me very much during just this first year."[5] The meeting was minimally reported by legacy media, with coverage focused on attendance: [[cbs|CBS]] noted that "few Western nations joined the board," that Britain and Germany sent their ambassadors to Washington as observers, and that Canada and France shunned the meeting entirely, while named attendees included [[israel|Israel]], [[egypt|Egypt]], Jordan and [[qatar|Qatar]].[5] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reported that representatives from over 40 countries participated.[5]

Trump pledged $10 billion from the United States, while [[qatar|Qatar]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] each promised at least $1 billion; in total $7 billion was secured from nine countries alongside the US pledge, for $17 billion.[5] "We will help Gaza," Trump said. "We will straighten it out. We'll make it successful… We will make it peaceful, and we will do things like that in other spots."[5] He framed the effort as achieving "the dream of bringing lasting harmony to a region tortured by centuries of war, suffering."[5]

On security, the board planned to deploy an [[international-stabilization-force|International Stabilization Force]] of 32,000 personnel — 12,000 police and 20,000 troops — drawn from countries including Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Kosovo and Albania, with training support from Egypt and Jordan.[5] [[turkey|Turkey]] volunteered soldiers and police, a contribution Trump has said he wants represented in the ISF but which [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and other Israeli officials have emphatically said will never be allowed.[5] Trump also announced plans to build a base for the ISF in southern Gaza housing 5,000 soldiers and other personnel.[5]

## Direct talks with Hamas, March 2026

In March 2026, representatives of [[hamas|Hamas]] met with members of the board — referred to in that reporting as the Gaza Peace Board — to discuss governance and security arrangements for [[gaza|Gaza]].[6] The discussions addressed potential security coordination and administrative control during a transition period and involved regional mediators and representatives connected to the peace board structure, though participants and outcomes were not fully disclosed.[6]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Hamas was reportedly represented by [[aryeh-lightstone|Aryeh Lightstone]], and that no mention was made of [[israel|Israel]] attending — which, he noted, would itself have been major news.[6] He argued that the significant development is structural: the meetings are no longer run by Israel but by a coalition of nations negotiating with Hamas, with Israel only one participant, raising the question of what happens if it attempts to bail or sabotage the process at the eleventh hour.[6] He proposed that the Board of Peace conduct a full investigation into anyone who contributed to the events of October 7th, including Israeli leadership, along with allegations of false flag attacks by Israel on Arab nations.[6]

## Eight additional countries join (January 2026)

The Foreign Ministers of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], [[turkey|Turkey]], [[egypt|Egypt]], Jordan, Indonesia, Pakistan, [[qatar|Qatar]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] announced their countries' decision to join the Board of Peace following an invitation from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[7] In a statement released on the Saudi Foreign Ministry's X account, the ministers reaffirmed support for Trump's peace efforts and pledged to help implement the Board's mission as a transitional administration under the [[comprehensive-plan-to-end-the-gaza-conflict|Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict]].[7] Endorsed by [[un-security-council-resolution-2803|United Nations Security Council Resolution 2803]], the plan seeks a permanent ceasefire, Gaza's reconstruction, and a lasting peace grounded in Palestinian self-determination and statehood while advancing regional security.[7]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Board was becoming a tangible qualifier for determining the roster of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]: European states including France, Norway, Sweden, [[denmark|Denmark]] and Slovenia had flatly rejected the invitation, while Italy, the United Kingdom and Germany remained noncommittal and were likely not to join.[7] By contrast, the familiar Arab states accepted quickly, as did Sovereign Alliance countries such as Hungary.[7] He cautioned that acceptance lists are not definitive membership tests — [[israel|Israel]] was invited for obvious reasons but he considered it one of the Alliance's greatest adversaries, noting that no Israeli political figure had been appointed to either Executive Board though a real estate developer had been included, and he likewise excluded [[canada|Canada]] given the open conflict between Trump and [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]].[7] He expected [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]]'s Italy to eventually come around.[7]

He further described the [[gaza-executive-board|Gaza Executive Board]] as a committee within the Board of Peace, with additional committees to be formed for each conflict Trump has tried to resolve, and predicted these committees would ultimately challenge the legitimacy of the [[united-nations|UN]] and the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]].[7] He noted that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was signaling willingness to join, stating Russia was prepared to pay the $1 billion price tag for a permanent seat — though from assets frozen by U.S. and European sanctions — and speculated that an Israeli-driven escalation might be the catalyst that finally brings Trump and Putin together.[7]

## Expansion beyond Gaza and the UN question (January 2026)

Ahead of the Davos ceremony, reporting described the Board of Peace — initially authorized by a U.N. Security Council resolution to support a stabilization force in Gaza following a ceasefire — as having expanded into a U.S.-led group chaired by [[donald-trump|Trump]] himself, tasked with strategic oversight, resource mobilization, and accountability for the territory's transition to peace and development.[8] More than 10 countries had signed on, with at least five publicly confirming participation: the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]], Belarus, Morocco, Hungary, and [[canada|Canada]].[8] Membership involves financial contributions — $1 billion for permanent status or smaller sums such as $20 million for limited terms — though Canada declined to pay, and the funds were designated for the board's Gaza mandate separately from broader rebuilding.[8] Invitations extended to nearly 60 nations, including [[russia|Russia]] despite its ongoing conflict in Ukraine.[8]

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] said the initiative reflected U.S. recognition of the need for cooperation among key countries and could prove constructive for Middle East stability if it implements existing U.N. decisions, particularly on creating an independent Palestinian state, while stressing that many questions remained and it was too early to confirm participation; the Kremlin echoed that caution.[8] Asked directly whether the board could replace the [[united-nations|United Nations]], Trump answered ambiguously with "Might," criticizing the U.N. for failing to live up to its potential while adding, "you got to let the U.N. continue because the potential is so great."[8] The expanding scope raised concerns among governments and experts that the board could rival or undermine the U.N., with some drawing comparisons to imperial structures, even as Washington faced allied pushback over tariff threats.[8]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] quoted a senior European official calling the concept "just very confused as an idea" and asking whether membership denotes an alliance or a body for mediation between adversaries.[8] He argued the board is the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] in tangible form, having moved from conceptual idea to formal organization created by executive order with a brick-and-mortar command center in southern Israel.[8] In his reading, Trump's response exposes the core defect of the U.N.: despite its power and influence it never earnestly attempts to mediate kinetic conflicts, whereas [[qatar|Qatar]] has demonstrated that mediation is possible when a host is willing to put warring parties in the same room.[8] He described the U.N. as illegitimate both for acting as a supranational regulatory authority in a long game toward one-world government and for failing its own stated mission as a neutral forum for averting war.[8]

## Security Council endorsement (November 2025)

The [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] resolution passed on November 17, 2025 endorsed the Board of Peace alongside the creation of the [[international-stabilization-force|international stabilization force]] for [[gaza|Gaza]].[9] The Board will oversee governance of Gaza and temporarily administer the enclave during the transition period, and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has said he will chair it.[9] The ISF is authorized to deploy under unified command acceptable to the Board of Peace.[9]
