# Amos Hochstein

Amos Hochstein is an American diplomat and energy envoy who served as the principal U.S. mediator in the negotiations that produced the November 2024 [[2024-lebanon-ceasefire|ceasefire in Lebanon]] between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1] Described in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as a "lifelong Democrat diplomat," Hochstein had long been the subject of speculation within the Badlands community that he was in fact operating on behalf of the so-called White Hats rather than the outgoing administration.[1]

## Reported direction by Donald Trump

According to the Brief, that speculation was resolved by reporting in Lebanese and Israeli media on 8 November 2024 — three days after the U.S. presidential election — indicating that Hochstein had been operating under direct orders from President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Israeli media quoted Trump as telling Hochstein, "Go and finish your work and make a deal with Lebanon."[1]

Newsweek subsequently reported comments by Lebanon's Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri, given to the Arabic-language Lebanese newspaper *Ad-Diyar*, attributing Lebanese optimism about a deal to "Amos Hochstein's confirmation during his last communication that he received the green light from U.S. President-elect Donald Trump to push the talks forward and reach a ceasefire."[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] concluded from this that the matter could no longer be described as speculation but as fact: "Amos Hochstein is working for President Trump."[1]

## Disputes over U.S. arms to Israel

Hochstein has previously stated publicly that claims the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration was withholding arms from Israel are untrue — a direct contradiction of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s assertions in the wake of the ceasefire vote that the administration had in fact been withholding weapons and that this would change once Trump took office.[1]

## 2024 emergence as Mideast diplomat

In July 2024 *The New York Times* profiled Hochstein as "a Biden confidant" who had emerged as a crucial Mideast diplomat, recounting that a few weeks before Hamas attacked Israel on October 7, 2023, he had toured the ancient ruins of Baalbek in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] — an area known as a [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] stronghold — in a sightseeing trip that doubled as a dramatic political statement.[2] Wearing white pants and a golf shirt and travelling with no security entourage, he photographed the onetime Roman city's crumbling walls and columns while presumed Hezbollah militiamen in black T-shirts watched from a distance; the visit caused a minor sensation in Lebanese media, which wondered how a top American official born in Israel could move so freely on Hezbollah turf.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], relaying a discussion from the [[devolution-power-hour|Devolution Power Hour]] featuring [[just-human|Just Human]], described Hochstein as an Israeli-born American citizen and longtime Democrat operative whose party history was concerning but not disqualifying, arguing that in the Information War the relevant test is whether an operator is trying to prevent World War III — the mark of a "white hat."[2] He highlighted that Hochstein had met Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] more than a dozen times since [[joe-biden|Biden]] took office, and connected Hochstein's brokering of a Hezbollah–Israel ceasefire to the unresolved 2017 episode in which MBS detained Lebanese Prime Minister [[saad-hariri|Saad al-Hariri]] and forced his resignation on Saudi television.[2] He also argued that the obstacle to peace lay with the Kahanist members of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s cabinet — [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] and [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] — who demanded war with both Hamas and Hezbollah and threatened to dissolve the government otherwise.[2]

## Threat to withdraw from mediation (November 2024)

Days before the ceasefire was finalized, Israel's Channel 13 reported that Hochstein had warned Israel's ambassador to the United States, [[michael-herzog|Michael Herzog]], that Washington would pull out of the mediation process entirely if Israel failed to respond positively to the recent American ceasefire proposal with [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[3] The warning was reportedly delivered during Hochstein's visit the previous week, after a two-day trip to Beirut.[3]

Middle East Monitor noted that throughout the year following Israel's invasion of the Gaza Strip and its escalation with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] to the north, Israel had repeatedly foiled attempts to reach a ceasefire on either front, and that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] had most recently demanded freedom to conduct military operations in southern Lebanon as a condition of any deal.[3] Lebanese Parliament Speaker [[nabih-berri|Nabih Berri]] rejected that condition, while Hezbollah Secretary-General [[naim-qassem|Naim Qassem]] said the group was negotiating on two conditions: complete cessation of Israeli aggression and preservation of Lebanese sovereignty.[3]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that he maintained the view that Hochstein was working in earnest to prevent World War III despite a lifelong career in the Washington establishment, and chose the withdrawal-threat story over headlines about Netanyahu "strongly considering" the proposal because he judged it the more accurate account of events.[3] He argued that Israeli assurances amounted to lip service, pointing to Heritage Minister [[amihai-eliyahu|Amichai Eliyahu]]'s statement that any ceasefire would be a "tactical delay" until [[donald-trump|Trump]] re-entered office, and to a video of Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] urging the incoming Trump administration to pressure Palestinians into leaving their lands.[3]

## Badlands assessment

While crediting Hochstein for his efforts toward peace and "his loyalty to President Trump's will," the Brief's commentary cautioned that the resulting peace appeared fragile and unlikely to last, citing continued Israeli bombing of [[beirut|Beirut]] up to the moment of finalization and Netanyahu's stated intent to pivot toward confrontation with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Request to Hezbollah rebuffed (July 2024)

In a July 2024 interview with the Associated Press, [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] deputy leader [[naim-kassem|Naim Kassem]] said Hochstein, acting as White House envoy, had recently requested via intermediaries that Hezbollah pressure [[hamas|Hamas]] into accepting the ceasefire and hostage-exchange proposal put forward by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[4] Kassem said Hezbollah rejected the request, replying that "Hamas is the one that makes its decisions and whoever wants to ask for something should talk to it directly."[4] Badlands commentary treated the rebuff approvingly as an assertion of first principles against Washington's use of intermediaries.[4]

## Role in the November 2024 Badlands framing

In the November 29, 2024 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Hochstein as an example of a career diplomat and lifelong Democrat pressing [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Israel toward peace, consistent with the established Democratic Party platform.[5] In that reading, Netanyahu accepted the pause in expectation that the incoming [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration would prove more hawkish, an assumption the writer argued would instead freeze Israel into a holding pattern while the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] rested and regrouped.[5]

## Israel–Lebanon negotiations, July 2024

By mid-July 2024, Hochstein was reported to be close to brokering a deal between Israel and Lebanon, according to the Lebanese newspaper al-Diyar as relayed by [[i24-news|i24 News]].[6] Hochstein told French officials that Israeli and Lebanese officials had almost completely agreed on terms to end the hostilities, even though the fighting had been between Israel and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] rather than the Lebanese armed forces.[6] Core unresolved disputes involved pushing Hezbollah back from the border and several frontier areas under Israeli control; agreed points included Hezbollah not erecting observation towers along the border and the Lebanese army deploying more extensively in southern Lebanon.[6] Of thirteen territorial disagreements, seven had been resolved, with the Shebaa Farms in the east and segments near Rosh Hanikra in the west still contested.[6]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Hochstein as a lifelong Democrat, former Israeli citizen, and Biden diplomat working to prevent World War III by preventing war between Israel and Hezbollah — a conflict he assessed as more likely to become a US/NATO versus Russia confrontation than anything in Ukraine — and argued that Hochstein was subverting the Deep State from within.[6] He noted that IDF brass had already demanded that politicians in Tel Aviv sue for peace, knowing that a war with Hezbollah, Russia, and Iran could mean the end of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]].[6]

The obstacle, in this reading, was [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s dependence on the most radical elements of Israeli politics — figures associated with [[kahanism|Kahanism]] and the [[smotrich-doctrine|Smotrich Doctrine]] — who would not permit any ceasefire with [[hamas|Hamas]] or Hezbollah because their ideology demands that land.[6] The situation was framed as a showdown between Hochstein and the Jewish supremacist faction, and as part of what the author calls [[multipolar-devolution|Multipolar Devolution]], the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] campaign being waged during what he describes as Trump's second term.[6] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry concluded that the work Hochstein was doing would be finished by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[6]

## Lebanon mission with Brett McGurk (October 2024)

In late October 2024, Hochstein and fellow senior Biden adviser [[brett-mcgurk|Brett McGurk]] were reported to be traveling to Israel on Thursday, October 31 in an effort to close a deal that would end the war in Lebanon and allow displaced civilians on both sides of the border to return to their homes.[7] Axios reported that if the pair struck a diplomatic agreement between Israel and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] during the visit, it would mark the first significant de-escalation of the regional war since the October 7, 2023 [[hamas|Hamas]] attacks.[7]

Israeli and US officials said they believed that after the blows Hezbollah had suffered in the preceding two months — including the assassination of its leader Hassan Nasrallah — the group was finally willing to disconnect itself from Hamas in Gaza, and that an end to the fighting could be reached within a few weeks.[7] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] convened a meeting with several ministers and senior leaders of the Israel Defense Forces and the Israeli intelligence services on the Tuesday night beforehand to discuss the potential deal.[7]

## Backing of a Russia-brokered arrangement (October 2024)

In late October 2024, reporting indicated that a draft ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah was being prepared with the backing of Hochstein and fellow US presidential adviser [[brett-mcgurk|Brett McGurk]], and that Israel had asked Russia to take part in the arrangement.[8] Badlands commentary highlighted the apparent contradiction that Hochstein — frequently accused of advancing an escalatory agenda — was, as a diplomat ostensibly serving the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, helping assemble a settlement that would place Israel's northern-front diplomacy substantially in the hands of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[8] The episode was folded into the broader [[trumps-gambit|"Trump's Gambit"]] framing of converging pressure on [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[8]
