# Lester B. Pearson

**Lester B. Pearson** was Canada's Oxford-educated minister for external affairs and later Prime Minister, presented in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account as a central figure in the British-trained network that gave Canada leverage over Washington.[1]

Quoting Eugene Griffin's 1951 *Chicago Tribune* article *"Canada Offers Fine Field to Rhodes' Wards,"* Ehret notes that when General Douglas MacArthur displeased Britain and Canada by his efforts to win the Korean War, Pearson complained that American-Canadian relations had become "difficult and delicate" — and MacArthur was fired the next day.[1]

Griffin reported that Pearson's foreign office staff was "packed with Rhodes scholars," with 23 among 183 staff officers educated at Oxford on [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholarships]].[1] Pearson himself attended Oxford (St. John's, 1922) on a Massey scholarship endowed by a Canadian millionaire, and other members of the Canadian foreign office were likewise educated in England without being Rhodes Scholars.[1] Among his senior colleagues was Norman A. Robertson, a Rhodes Scholar and Clerk of the Privy Council.[1]
