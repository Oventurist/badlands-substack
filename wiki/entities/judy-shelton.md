# Judy Shelton

Judy Shelton is the economist whom President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] nominated to the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article states that Trump chose Shelton because she was an advocate of bringing back the [[gold-standard|gold standard]], which it dates as spanning from 1873 to 1933.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Her nomination is presented as evidence of Trump's attempt to restore the older American monetary system in the manner of [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Transition role and blocked nomination

Trump chose Shelton as part of his economic transition team after his election, and she had been advocating for the gold standard for a long time.[2] He then nominated her for a seat on the Federal Reserve's Board of Governors.[2] Beyond promoting the gold standard she was also an outspoken critic of the Federal Reserve, which Badlands Media suggests is probably why her nomination was blocked.[2] Shelton has argued for a return to sound money, with the dollar measured in a specific amount of gold or silver, on the grounds that there can be no honest financial system without honest money.[2]

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] places Shelton alongside [[john-allison|John Allison]], the former BB&T chief and [[cato-institute|Cato Institute]] head whom Trump also met during the transition, arguing that these advisers shared two commitments: restoring the gold standard and abolishing the Federal Reserve.[2]

## Sound money advocacy in the 2025 reset debate

Badlands Media's "Trump's Final Offensive" lists Shelton alongside Stephen Moore among [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s financial appointees who have long advocated a gold standard, reduced [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] scope, and decentralised monetary policy.[3] The article argues that the Fed's role is not being ended abruptly but surrounded and replaced through irrelevance, citing reported private gold audits of central bank vaults, executive discussions with [[brics|BRICS]] central banks about currency interoperability, and leaked Treasury memos on a blockchain-based sovereign coin pegged to gold, silver, and rare earths.[3] Answering critics such as economist Nouriel Roubini, who warned that commodity-based currencies could destabilise markets, Shelton wrote in 2024 that "sound money anchored to real value restores confidence; fiat systems breed dependency on unelected officials."[3]
