# George W. Bush

George W. Bush was President of the United States during the Iraq War and the period of the New York Federal Reserve's cash airlift to Baghdad.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## The Iraq money and the Lebanon bunker

The article states that tens of billions of dollars were stolen during the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]], and that an investigation conducted during the Bush presidency discovered over $1 billion — elsewhere given as more than $1.5 billion — in a bunker in Lebanon, money the author says was destined to fund Hezbollah.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Bush is said to have squashed that investigation so that nothing came of it, with the story swept under the rug and forgotten.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article's judgement is blunt: the plan was always to steal the money, and Bush "didn't just turn a blind eye to the theft, he actually stopped an investigation."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It further characterises Bush as one of [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]]'s biggest puppets.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Libya policy

After 9/11, the article notes, [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]] treated Bush's war on terror as a serious threat, voluntarily surrendered his weapons of mass destruction programmes and made peace with Western nations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The Bush administration made a deal with Gaddafi under which Libya would take custody of [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] and any other captured members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Teneo appearances and paid speeches

Bush figures in the America for Sale series as a headline attraction at events staged by the consulting firm [[teneo-holdings|Teneo]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) On September 20, 2012 — days after the [[benghazi-attack|Benghazi]] attack — he spoke alongside [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and former British Prime Minister Tony Blair at an unpublicised reception at the Essex House in New York City organised with the help of [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], an event designed to impress potential Teneo clients with access to three former world leaders on a single stage.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The article treats the line-up as evidence for its claim, drawn from Q drop 299, that [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]] controlled U.S. and U.K. politicians, calling all three men puppets.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

Beginning in May 2011 Bush also appeared on stage with Clinton and [[ubs|UBS]] executive Bob McCann in a Teneo-arranged series of events titled "Revitalizing America," staged while the Swiss bank was under scrutiny for helping wealthy Americans evade taxes.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## The Iraq cash airlift and the Bowen investigation

"Bad Actors" charges Bush with sending tens of billions of dollars in physical cash to Iraq after the 2003 invasion — $12 to $14 billion flown from Andrews Air Force Base to Baghdad aboard C-17s, with a further $5 billion wired — on the authorisation of proconsul [[paul-bremer|Paul Bremer]], and asks why the money was not simply transferred electronically, answering that cash is far harder to trace.[3] The [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] team working on Iraq's reconstruction said it did not know Bremer was flying in the cash and could see no reason for it.[3]

In 2004 Bush appointed his Texas friend [[stuart-w-bowen-jr|Stuart W. Bowen Jr.]] as special inspector general to investigate corruption and waste in Iraq, an appointment Joe Lange interprets as an attempt to make congressional investigations of the missing money go away; Bowen instead pursued the case seriously, tracing $1.2 to $1.6 billion to a bunker in rural Lebanon before being obstructed.[3] Lange writes that Bush "never really wanted to investigate the stolen cash" and that the exercise was a complete whitewash, noting that the Bush administration outside Bowen's office never investigated the reports at all.[3] He concludes that while Bush was constructing a public image of waging war on terrorism he was in fact funding the same terrorist groups, and that both Bush and [[barack-obama|Obama]] were puppets of [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]], who controlled terrorist organisations through his control of the [[cia|CIA]].[3]

## The "War on Terror" vocabulary

Badlands Media argues that Bush's repetition of the word [[terrorism|"terrorism"]] ad nauseam after [[september-11-attacks|9/11]], following [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s 1986 rebranding of the term, completed its transformation from a description of state violence against a population into a synonym for Islamic extremism directed against Western hegemony.4 Bush was informed at 9:30 PM on September 11 by CIA Director [[george-tenet|George Tenet]] that [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]] and [[al-qaeda]] were held responsible for the attacks.4 Many of the recommendations of the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for a New American Century]] were subsequently reflected in his White House's National Security Strategy document of September 2002.4


## Middle Eastern money and the 2017 attack on Trump

"Profiting From War" traces George W. Bush's earliest Middle East connection to the late 1970s and the Texas businessman [[james-bath|James Bath]], who represented in North America the billionaire [[salem-bin-laden|Salem bin Laden]] and the banker and [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] insider [[khalid-bin-mahfouz|Khalid bin Mahfouz]].[5] Bath put $50,000 into Bush's 1979 [[arbusto-energy|Arbusto]] oil partnership, probably using Bin Laden–Bin Mahfouz funds.[5] After several failed oil ventures the business in which he was a major stockholder and chairman was bought out by [[harken-energy|Harken Energy]], which the Wall Street Journal noted in 1991 was surrounded by a "mosaic of BCCI connections," all arising since Bush came on board.[5] In 1990 the inexperienced Harken won a major contract to drill in the Persian Gulf for the government of Bahrain, which Time reporters concluded had been steered to the president's son by Mahfouz or other BCCI players.[5] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] calls Harken "the 'Burisma' for the [[bush-family|Bush family]]" and casts Bush as the vehicle through which his father took payments.[5]

The article also examines Bush's October 2017 speech at a New York policy seminar hosted by his think tank, the [[george-w-bush-institute|George W. Bush Institute]].[5] The [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] described it as an extraordinary if veiled attack on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], suggesting he had promoted bigotry and falsehoods; Bush never named Trump but his target was unmistakable, and the remarks breached the protocol governing post-presidential behavior.[5] Bush celebrated free trade and international engagement as sources of stability and prosperity, and warned that foreign aggressions including cyberattacks, disinformation and financial influence should not be downplayed, calling for the electoral system to be protected from subversion.[5] Lange reads the speech as a coordinated contribution to the Russian collusion narrative, delivered weeks before the Saudi arrests of November 2017 and motivated by fear of Trump's power to declassify records held in presidential libraries.[5]

The article further attributes to Bush the "weapons of mass destruction" rationale for the [[iraq-war|invasion of Iraq]], the origin of [[operation-fast-and-furious|Operation Fast and Furious]], and the precedent of shipping pallets of cash abroad later followed by the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration with Iran.[5]

## Q drop #117: Saudi ties, 9/11 and Halliburton

Chapter 17 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series describes [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]] and both Bush presidents as connected through "a river running deep with corruption," citing frequent Saudi investment in the [[carlyle-group|Carlyle Group]], Saudi financing of Middle East wars, and Saudi investment in [[halliburton|Halliburton]].[6] It emphasizes that [[september-11-attacks|9/11]] occurred under Bush and that dozens of Saudis were permitted to leave the country in its aftermath, and reports the view of many anons that the Saudi royal family led by Alwaleed had a very big hand in funding, planning and executing the attacks.[6]

The chapter traces the consequences of 9/11 to a twenty-year war in Afghanistan and the invasion of [[iraq-war|Iraq]], both justified by claims about weapons of mass destruction and terrorism, and to the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]], which it says further eroded civil liberties under "for your safety" messaging.[6] Answering Q's question about senior officials tied to Halliburton, it names Bush and [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], the latter having served on the company's board.[6] It also notes that [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] served under Bush and may therefore know the truth about the Bush family, Saudi Arabia and 9/11.[6]
