# George W. Bush

George W. Bush was the 43rd President of the United States, serving from 2001 to 2009. In the Badlands Media corpus, he is framed as a key architect of the post-9/11 national security state and the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]], which the source portrays not merely as a policy failure but as a deliberate vehicle for large-scale diversion of taxpayer funds.

According to the article "A Conflict of Interest," Bush's administration oversaw the shipment of approximately $40 billion in cash from the New York Federal Reserve to Iraq between 2003 and 2008. The source claims that after this cash arrived in Baghdad, there was no documentation tracking its movement, and that tens of billions of dollars were effectively allowed to be stolen. The article specifically alleges that Bush personally "squashed" an investigation that had discovered over $1.5 billion in a bunker in Lebanon, and that the stolen money was used to fund [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].

The article characterizes Bush as "one of [[prince-alwaleed|Prince Alwaleed]]'s biggest puppets," suggesting that the Saudi prince exercised significant influence over the Bush administration. It further claims that the Iraq War was designed from the outset as a cover for theft, with the absence of a paper trail after the cash reached Baghdad representing intentional design rather than bureaucratic negligence. In the source's framework, Bush is not an unwitting participant but an active enabler of what it describes as a global cabal's control of taxpayer money.

## The 2000 recount and HAVA

The essay [[always-fight-back|"Always Fight Back"]] recounts that in 2000 broadcast media prematurely called Florida for [[al-gore|Al Gore]], that recounts awarded the state to Bush, and that the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ended the dispute by halting the Florida recount on equal-protection grounds. The article treats the episode as a mainstream, fully legitimate example of contesting an election outcome, and notes that the controversy led directly to the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]], signed in 2002, which in the author's view centralized U.S. elections around private vendors with little meaningful oversight.
