# US Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit

The United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit is the federal appellate court based in New Orleans, Louisiana, with jurisdiction over federal district courts in Louisiana, Mississippi, and Texas. Because of its geographic coverage of much of the southern border, it hears a substantial volume of immigration litigation and is frequently the venue in which federal immigration policy is tested.

The March 26, 2026 Badlands Brief notes that in February 2026 the Fifth Circuit issued a 2-1 decision upholding the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's position that federal law mandates detention in most deportation cases. That ruling was followed the next month by a parallel 2-1 decision from the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-eighth-circuit|Eighth Circuit]], giving the administration two appellate victories on the same statutory question.


## Buenrostro-Mendez v. Bondi (February 2026)

The February 10, 2026 Badlands Brief gave the case name and statutory basis for the court's February ruling on immigration detention. Deciding *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Bondi*, consolidated with *Padron Covarrubias v. Vergara*, the Fifth Circuit reversed two district-court orders that had required bond hearings or release for detained noncitizens, holding that the government may detain certain "applicants for admission" without bond during removal proceedings under 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A). Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] described the outcome as a "crucial legal victory" for [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] attorneys, and [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] framed it as vindication against court orders premised on claims that her department was breaking the law.
