# Kanye West

Kanye West, also known as Ye, is an American rapper, producer and cultural figure whose April 2026 return to live performance was treated by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] as a significant cultural and spiritual event.[1]

## Easter 2026 comeback

West performed a sold-out concert at SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles before roughly 70,000 fans, marking his first major U.S. show in about five years.[1] The performance came months after the release of a song titled "Heil Hitler" and weeks after he issued a public apology for antisemitic remarks.[1] Lauryn Hill joined West on stage during the show, in what was described as their first live collaboration.[1]

The Los Angeles engagement generated about $18 million on Friday night, according to Bloomberg, and $33 million across the two nights — a Friday figure described as the highest revenue ever generated from a single performer's concert.[1]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] emphasized that West staged the two shows on Good Friday and Easter Sunday, "arguably the two most important days on the Christian calendar: the day Christ died; and the day He was resurrected."[1] Commentary focused on the stage design — a giant half-sphere lit to appear as though it were spinning — and on his choice to perform the early hit "Jesus Walks" on Good Friday.[1]

## "Jesus Walks" and the Hollywood thesis

"Jesus Walks" appeared on West's 2004 debut album, released two weeks after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] debuted the first episode of The Apprentice.[1] Badlands read the song's lyrics as a statement that Hollywood despises Jesus to the degree that invoking Him invites radio censorship and career destruction — a claim the outlet extended to the broader [[deep-state|Deep State]] establishment's alleged effort to erase Christ from American culture.[1] The treatment of [[mel-gibson|Mel Gibson]] over The Passion of the Christ, also released in 2004, was cited as a parallel case.[1] West's 2019 album [[jesus-is-king|Jesus Is King]] was described as the most commercially successful gospel album of all time.[1]

## Controversies and Badlands framing

Badlands traced West's escalation to October 2022, when he appeared at Paris Fashion Week alongside [[candace-owens|Candace Owens]] wearing "White Lives Matter" shirts, drawing intense backlash.[1] In the outlet's telling, West then described personal anguish inflicted by a network of "Jewish Hollywood lawyers," some of whom worked for him and allegedly threatened his business interests, before he declared "Defcon Three" against "the Jews" and was widely cancelled.[1] Badlands framed the "White Lives Matter" stunt as an attempt to draw attention to what it called psychological warfare operations aimed at eradicating white populations in North America and Europe, and as an assertion that no identity group should be elevated over another.[1]

West has more recently clarified that he is not a Nazi and offered reconciliation with the Jewish people, but Badlands argued he will be remembered as the personality who "broke the seal on the Israel Question."[1] The piece acknowledged that many critics — often self-identified Christians and Christian Zionists — refuse to extend him grace, while maintaining that his sold-out shows proved his cultural clout had not waned.[1]

A bonus item in the same issue referenced West and his gospel choir performing on CBS's late-night program in October 2019 as part of the Jesus Is King tour.[1]

## Istanbul concert (June 2026)
West drew a massive crowd during a surprise late-night performance near the Bosphorus in Istanbul, described by Turkish media as one of the city's largest recent live music gatherings tied to an international artist.[2] The show followed a string of cancellations as European venues and festival organizers distanced themselves from him over his public remarks and online activity, and was read as evidence of his continued ability to draw large audiences outside Western European cultural institutions — and of [[turkey|Turkey]]'s growing role as a refuge for figures under reputational pressure in Europe and North America.[2]
[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the concert as timed with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "face turn" against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], calling West "one of his most powerful proxies" and arguing the movement is "a cultural revolution and spiritual revival" as much as a political one.[2] He recalled West standing with Trump at Trump Tower before Trump first took office and asserted that West's support cost him billions, his marriage and access to his children before he "went DEFCON 3 on the Jewish elite," quoting the "Heil Hitler" lyric "I still don't get to see my children…So I became a Nazi, yeah b****, I'm the villain."[2] Both men, in his telling, turned heel to shift the [[overton-window|Overton Window]] and later turned babyface.[2] See [[kanye-west-istanbul-concert-2026|Kanye West Istanbul concert (2026)]].[2]

## "DEFCON 3" cancellation (January 2026)

In a postscript to its January 27, 2026 Middle East segment, the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] noted that Ye had cancelled DEFCON 3 and was "now off the rug."[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] speculated that much of what Ye did may have been orchestrated as part of a larger counter-operation to violently shift the [[overton-window|Overton Window]].[3]


## Speculation About the 2025 Inauguration

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote in January 2025 that he would not rule out Kanye West making a dark-horse appearance at [[second-inauguration-of-donald-trump|Donald Trump's second inauguration]], arguing that after years of persecution by Hollywood for supporting Trump, being debanked and losing billions of dollars in personal wealth, West deserved a moment of vindication — though that might not come until later in the story.[4]

## 2016 Trump endorsement and its aftermath

On November 18, 2016 — ten days after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was first elected president — West interrupted his own concert to rant about the result and endorse Trump, declaring, "It's a new world, Hillary. It's a new world, Barack. It's a new world, Jay-Z," and addressing [[jay-z|Jay-Z]] directly: "I know you have killers, please don't send them at my head. Just call me! Talk to me like a man."[5] He said he had "been sent here to give y'all the truth. Even at the risk of my own life," then ended the concert.[5] Days later he was checked into a mental institution by his personal trainer [[harley-pasternak|Harley Pasternak]], whom [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] describes as a former Canadian intelligence agent who has publicly acknowledged working with experimental drugs, and whom West later accused of giving him lithium to make him submissive.[5]

Pasternak was not the only figure to pursue West after the endorsement; [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]] did so aggressively via text messages that West subsequently posted to social media.[5] Badlands argued that West, despite his fame, was never part of "the club" in Hollywood, and highlighted Trump's December 2016 rescue of West — bringing him to Trump Tower for an emotional private meeting — as the possible start of an alliance with cultural significance, later reflected in West calling Trump "the father that he never had."[5] The outlet cited a March 2024 episode in which Jay-Z attended a West concert in Los Angeles seeking a private meeting and was ignored, and speculated that West may have helped Trump map and infiltrate the Hollywood cartel, noting West's ties from the same period to Vladimir Putin, Russian celebrity-artists and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.[5]

## June 2024 Moscow visit

At the end of June 2024, Russian media reported that West had arrived in Moscow for the birthday celebrations of fashion designer [[gosha-rubchinskiy|Gosha Rubchinskiy]], following rumors the previous month that he would perform a concert in the city.[6] The state TASS news agency quoted Russian television presenter Yana Rudkovskaya as saying, "Kanye West is already in Moscow! This is great news, he is staying in the heart of the capital," while Luzhniki Stadium — the rumored concert venue — said it had no information on any performance.[6] Most Western artists had stopped performing in Russia since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine and the resulting US and European sanctions; West had previously expressed admiration for [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and had visited Moscow in the wake of the invasion, prompting TASS to ask the Kremlin in 2022 whether Putin was a fan.[6]

## Saudi Arabia, Yeezy and Vultures

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry assembled a timeline linking West's movements. On December 13, 2023, West announced on X that Rubchinskiy had become the new lead designer for his brand, Yeezy; two days later a video circulated of West telling a room of people he had been meeting with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[6] On December 16 he performed a surprise concert in Saudi Arabia promoting his forthcoming album Vultures, and on December 17 he reactivated his Instagram account after an eight-month hiatus to post an apology letter, written in Hebrew, to the Jewish community for his rhetoric over the prior year.[6] Producer DJ Camper later said he had spent three months living in Saudi Arabia with West and the rapper Ty$ recording Vultures 1, under conditions in which they were not allowed to drink or smoke.[6] Research presented on the Liberty Den placed West at a luxury desert resort in Al Ula developed by the crown prince, a few miles from MBS's winter camp where he hosted world leaders including Senator Lindsey Graham.[6]

The Vultures artwork features the black eagle of the Prussian coat of arms and a landscape painting by Caspar David Friedrich, one of Hitler's favorite artists, while its typography appears inspired by the Norwegian black metal band Burzum, associated with the neo-Nazi movement.[6] Badlands read these choices — combined with West's Kaliningrad and Königsberg-adjacent associations through Rubchinskiy — as deliberate trolling rather than coincidence, summarized in the phrase "Yevolution is real."[6]


## Walking Back Nazi Comments (April 2025)

On Monday, April 21, 2025, Ye disavowed his earlier claims of being a Nazi in a post on X that he later deleted, writing that he had "come to the realization that I'm not a Nazi" while adding that he "can and will say whatever the f*ck I feel like whenever the f*ck I feel like it."[7] Earlier in 2025 he had proclaimed himself "a Nazi" and expressed love for Adolf Hitler following a lengthy series of anti-Semitic posts, and the previous month he had flashed a swastika t-shirt in a TMZ video that he called his "greatest dream" in another since-deleted post.[7] The fallout from these statements cost him hundreds of millions of dollars in lost corporate sponsorships.[7] Badlands commentary speculated that the episode had been an "op" that provoked establishment Republicans into passing hate-speech legislation effectively banning criticism of a specific foreign government, thereby exposing their record on free speech.[7]

## The 2016 Sacramento rant and the Jay-Z allegations

In a December 2024 retrospective, Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry revisited West's November 20, 2016 concert in Sacramento, California, where three songs into the show West stopped the performance to speak about [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election victory over [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[8] West told the crowd that "everybody in Middle America felt a [certain] way, and they showed you how they felt. Feelings matter. It's a new world, Hillary Clinton. It's a new world, Barack. It's a new world, Jay-Z."[8] During the same rant West strongly implied that [[jay-z|Jay-Z]] had threatened his life and may even have hired people to hurt him.[8]

The Badlands commentary argued that while the inclusion of Clinton and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] made obvious political sense, Jay-Z appeared an incongruous third target unless West possessed knowledge of what the writer termed the "pedophile cartel commonly referred to as 'Hollywood.'"[8] The passage was published in the context of a civil lawsuit adding Jay-Z as a defendant alongside [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]].[8]

## Career backstory as told by Badlands

According to the same account, West spent many years as a relatively unknown music producer for major mainstream artists, particularly Jay-Z, becoming a go-to source of hit records because of his musical ability.[8] He spent years attempting to establish himself as a lyricist and rapper but claims to have been stifled by Jay-Z, who held enormous clout over the industry and appeared to prefer keeping West in a subordinate production role.[8]

When West finally released his debut album, *The College Dropout*, in February 2004, one of the five singles chosen to promote it was "Jesus Walks," in which he explicitly decried a music industry he described as controlled by satanists who hate Jesus and promote degeneracy over Christian values.[8]

On October 29, 2019, West appeared on a CBS late-night segment in which celebrities normally ride in a car and sing karaoke with the host; instead, West negotiated to charter a commercial flight filled with the gospel choir assembled for his album [[jesus-is-king|Jesus Is King]], which also toured with him for gospel concerts staged as church services in pursuit of his stated aim of making Christianity appealing to young people again.[8] The Badlands writer observed that only an artist of West's fame and clout could have negotiated such an arrangement with a network, and expressed hope that West was "still around, somewhere."[8]

## Yeezy adult-entertainment reports (April 2024)

In April 2024 a representative for West informed TMZ that the rapper hoped to add an adult entertainment branch to his Yeezy empire, possibly that summer, reportedly in communication with adult film producer Mike Moz, formerly married to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]].[9] The reports came five years after West had said he suffered from an addiction to pornography and expressed regret at how often he watched it, claiming adult entertainment had destroyed his family.[9] Coverage noted that he was more recently thought to have been behind the revealing outfits worn publicly by his wife Bianca Censori.[9]

Badlands commentary treated the announcement as either a sign that West needed help — possibly back on the medication cycles he had accused Harvey Pasternak of imposing on him — or as another instance of his disruptive unpredictability.[9] The writer noted a longstanding thesis that West has a special relationship with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], including the claim that Trump intervened after Pasternak had West institutionalized following his November 2016 concert interruption in which he called out Hillary Clinton and Barack Obama and pledged support for Trump.[9]

The commentary drew a parallel to 2018, when reports of West exploring an entry into the porn industry coincided with his designing wardrobe for the Pornhub awards, his public meetings with Trump, his professed reconnection to Jesus, and work on the gospel album *Jesus is King*, which went to number one and led to a worldwide gospel tour and a campaign to make Christianity appealing to young people.[9] West had also posted publicly that pornography had destroyed his family but that Jesus would heal everything, and called for its removal from all platforms, describing pornography as a product of pedophilia.[9]

The same commentary argued that the antisemitism campaign against West began when he and Candace Owens wore "White Lives Matter" shirts at the Paris Fashion Show in October 2022, a period in which West was also conspicuously wearing and naming Balenciaga apparel just as the Balenciaga photoshoot scandal was unfolding — including the black mask worn during his appearance on Alex Jones's show.[9] The writer pointed to the 2018 song "Violent Crimes" as evidence of West's preoccupation with protecting his daughters from exploitation, and to his repeated public expressions of dread that his mother-in-law would one day exploit them for money and clout.[9] The timing was noted as significant: the announcement came the same day Trump went to trial over hush-money payments to Stormy Daniels, with West's prospective partner being Daniels's ex-husband.[9]
