# Fulton County Board of Registration and Elections

The Fulton County Board of Registration and Elections administers voter registration and elections in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia, the state's most populous county and the center of long-running disputes over the conduct of the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## DOJ records demand (October 2025)

On Thursday, October 30, 2025, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] sent a letter to the Board demanding records related to what Georgia's board of elections has called "unexplained anomalies in vote tabulation and storage related to the 2020 election" in the county.[1] The letter followed the Board's failure to comply with a subpoena issued by the [[state-election-board|State Election Board]].[1]

Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]], writing on behalf of Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]], requested that the Board present for inspection "in its entirety and most original form, all records in your possession responsive to the recent subpoena."[1] The Civil Rights Division framed the request as consistent with its obligations to ensure voting rights are protected, invoking Title III of the [[civil-rights-act-of-1960|Civil Rights Act of 1960]], which empowers the Attorney General to request preserved election records.[1] The letter cited Section 303 of the Act, which provides that records required by Section 20701 to be retained shall, upon written demand by the Attorney General, be made available for inspection, reproduction, and copying at the custodian's principal office.[1]

## Signature verification controversy

Badlands commentary noted that [[signature-verification|signature verification]] records are unlikely to exist because Fulton did not perform signature verification, described as a statutory violation long ignored.[1] Citing January 2024 reporting by [[canncon|CannCon]], the brief recounted that Fulton County officials testified they did not scan any mail-in envelopes with their BlueCrest sorter machines, said they "do not simply have a file of voter signatures" but keep them in different databases for different voters requiring two database checks, and quoted more than 57,000 hours to produce the data — raising the question of how signatures could have been confirmed in the timeframe in which Biden was declared the winner of Georgia.[1] Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] doubted that any incriminating records still exist, reasoning that if compliance means incrimination, the officials will never comply.[1]
