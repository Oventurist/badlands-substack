# Scattered Spider

Scattered Spider is the name given to a loose group of young, largely English-speaking cybercriminals that emerged in the early 2020s and became notorious for social-engineering intrusions against large corporations. The group's activity was profiled in reporting aggregated by the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief of July 2, 2025, which described it as a "notorious teen hacking group causing chaos online."[1]

## Origins

The group's public emergence is generally traced to August 2022, when workers at the US communications company Twilio began receiving text messages purporting to come from their employer's IT department. The messages claimed that employees' passwords had expired or that their work schedules had changed, and directed recipients to log in to their accounts through a supplied link.[1]

The link led to a landing page that was visually identical to Twilio's genuine sign-in page. The impersonation was convincing enough that several employees entered their credentials, and the resulting intrusion compromised the data of roughly 125 Twilio customers.[1]

## Methods and escalation

Although the Twilio breach was small in absolute scale, it was regarded as notable for its sophistication, particularly the quality of the phishing infrastructure and the group's fluency in manipulating help-desk and identity-verification processes. It formed part of a broader series of successful attacks on telecommunications companies during 2022.[1]

The campaign subsequently widened. In 2023 the group's attention shifted to casinos, and from there to an expanding set of sectors including telecommunications, finance, gaming, hospitality and retail. Cumulatively, the intrusions have been reported to have caused hundreds of millions of pounds' worth of damage.[1]

## Notable targets

High-profile victims in the United Kingdom have included the retailers Marks & Spencer and the Co-op.[1] Reporting in mid-2025 also connected the group's operating pattern to a wave of intrusions affecting the aviation sector, a context in which the near-contemporaneous [[qantas|Qantas]] data breach was widely discussed — though that incident had not, at the time of the Badlands brief, been formally attributed to Scattered Spider.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America treated the group's rise as another entry in a continuous stream of breaches, remarking that "Scattered spiders is a super scary name. And they're teens!" while noting the legal caveat that the Qantas breach had not yet been attributed to the group. The commentary closed with a sardonic observation about election security, suggesting that the public was expected to believe such intrusions could not affect elections because "teenage mutant spider hackers aren't election experts."[1]
