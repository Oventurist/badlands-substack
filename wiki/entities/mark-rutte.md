# Mark Rutte

Mark Rutte is the Secretary-General of [[nato|NATO]] and the alliance's chief interlocutor with Washington during the 2026 crisis over the U.S.-Israeli war against [[iran|Iran]]. He appears in the April 6, 2026 Badlands Brief, "Jesus Walks", which reports that Rutte was expected to travel to Washington to meet [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in an effort to ease tensions within the alliance.

The dispute Rutte was tasked with defusing arose after the February 28, 2026 U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran. European member states questioned the legality of the war and declined to contribute militarily, including refusing to deploy naval forces to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]]. Trump publicly criticised the allies' reluctance and questioned whether the United States should remain committed to NATO — a withdrawal that, as the source notes, would require the approval of [[congress|Congress]]. Officials and analysts quoted in the Brief describe the resulting divisions as a significant crisis for the alliance's cohesion and future role, and Rutte's Washington visit is presented as the principal diplomatic response to it.

## Tariff pause, January 2026

The January 26, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the transatlantic controversy over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threatened tariffs and his pressure campaign over [[greenland|Greenland]] eased mid-week when Trump announced what he described as a "framework of a future deal" with Rutte as [[nato|NATO]] Secretary General, temporarily pausing tariff actions that had rattled global markets and alarmed European diplomats.

## Ankara summit: "Trump was right" (July 2026)

The July 8, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that Rutte, in remarks at the NATO summit in Ankara, urged alliance members to acknowledge that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was correct in demanding higher defense spending and greater burden-sharing from European allies, crediting Trump with advancing a long-standing U.S. goal of equalizing defense contributions between America and Europe and describing the period as transformational for the alliance. He praised Trump personally, stating that what the president is doing for NATO is "great news," and highlighted that over the past two years the 31 non-U.S. NATO members have committed $250 billion in new defense investments and programs, with additional pledges expected. He also pointed to European support for the U.S.-led military campaign against [[iran|Iran]] — basing, airfields and power projection capabilities that enabled the operation — while acknowledging Trump's frustration that some allies were slow to offer public backing, and stressed the need for a sustainable alliance in which Europeans and Canadians take on a larger share of the load even as Washington reviews its troop presence in Europe.

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] notes that Rutte invoked [[dwight-eisenhower|Dwight D. Eisenhower]] in relation to Trump's push, and that he also cited the kinetic and economic campaigns of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], plus [[iran|Iran]] and [[north-korea|North Korea]], as proof of the premise "that we are moving from a hegemonic world to a multipolar one, and that the only language of negotiation in that world is power" — the quintet Burning Bright calls the [[new-axis|New Axis]].
