# Children's Health Defense

Children's Health Defense (CHD) is the health advocacy organization founded and long chaired by [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], focused on vaccine safety, pharmaceutical accountability and medical freedom.[1]

## Reform Pharma initiative

At its second annual conference, held in early November 2023, CHD announced the debut of [[reform-pharma|Reform Pharma]], described as the organization's latest initiative.[1] The stated aim of the endeavor is to restore the integrity of healthcare by identifying and dismantling the avenues that permit corruption to seize hold of and dominate the healthcare system.[1] Among the changes the initiative seeks to effect are the separation of pharma and state, the prohibition of [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]], accountability measures, vaccine manufacturer liability, and a restoration of medical freedom.[1]

## Supporting claims cited at launch

The launch was accompanied by figures marshaled against the pharmaceutical industry: fines totaling more than $100 billion since 2000 for offenses including fraud, unsafe product claims, and off-label or unauthorized medical product advertising; up to 220,000 annual deaths on "properly prescribed" medications; roughly 2.7 million hospital admissions and around 81 million adverse reactions per year attributed to prescription medication; and 26,789 petitions filed with the [[national-vaccine-injury-compensation-program|National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program]] since 1988, only a small portion of which resulted in compensation despite payouts exceeding $5 billion.[1]

## Coverage

The announcement was reported by [[vigilant-news|Vigilant News]] and carried in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of November 10, 2023, where [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that although he supported [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for 2024, he welcomed a candidate offering concrete measures rather than talking points, while cautioning that whether anything would come of the initiative remained to be seen.[1]

## Texas measles death records review (March 2025)

In March 2025 Children's Health Defense obtained access to the medical records of a six-year-old Texas girl with measles who had died, at the specific request of her family, who asked that the findings be shared as a warning to other parents.[2] The review was presented on CHD.TV by Polly Tommey, CHD Chief Scientific Officer Brian Hooker, [[pierre-kory|Dr. Pierre Kory]] and Dr. Ben Edwards.[2]

Hooker explained that the girl had actually been recovering from measles: her pediatrician had given cough syrup and recommended Tylenol, and after the rash faded she developed cough and breathing problems, leading her parents to take her to the emergency room where antibiotics were administered.[2] She showed no progress after two and a half days, entered the ICU, and died 36 hours later.[2] Kory, an expert in emergency care and pulmonology, reviewed the records and concluded that while the case was tragic, "she did not die of measles by any stretch of the imagination. In fact, she died of a pneumonia."[2]

## 2023 FOIA lawsuit against the FDA

On January 26, 2023, Children's Health Defense filed suit in federal court in Washington against the [[food-and-drug-administration|U.S. Food and Drug Administration]], alleging that the agency violated federal law by withholding the results of key COVID-19 vaccine safety analyses.[4] The suit sought the raw results of the FDA's analyses of reports submitted to the [[vaccine-adverse-event-reporting-system|Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System]], the post-vaccination adverse-event reporting system the FDA operates jointly with the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]].[4]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that regulatory agencies, rather than manufacturers, were the most critical link in the pandemic chain, since the bulk of their funding comes from the companies whose products they approve, and expressed hope that a wave of litigation would reach the regulators as well as the firms.[4]

## Lawsuit against the CDC (April 2025)

The nonprofit, formerly led by Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], sued the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] in April 2025, accusing the agency - then under Kennedy's own oversight - of pressuring doctors to administer COVID-19 vaccines to disadvantaged children through the federal [[vaccines-for-children-program|Vaccines for Children]] program.[5] The suit sought to force the CDC to abandon a "scientifically untethered" vaccination policy.[5] Its lead plaintiff was Dr. Samara Cardenas, an Anaheim pediatrician who said her refusal to administer COVID-19 shots on medical judgment cost her nearly 2,000 Medicaid patients and closed her practice; the complaint, filed April 25 in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California, alleged that the CDC "punish[es] ethical physicians" and promotes an "unnecessary mass vaccination" campaign targeting the nation's poorest children.[5]
