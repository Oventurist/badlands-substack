# Wuhan Institute of Virology

The Wuhan Institute of Virology is a Chinese research laboratory in Wuhan that is at the center of the COVID-19 lab-leak controversy.[1]

In his opening statement to the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] on June 3, 2024, former [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|NIAID]] director [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] denied trying to silence officials and scientists who believed the virus had been created in, or had escaped from, the institute.[1] Fauci called the accusation that he had influenced scientists to change their minds by bribing them with millions of dollars in grant money "absolutely false and simply preposterous," stating he had no input into the content of the published paper, and rejected the charge that he had tried to cover up a possible laboratory origin.[1]

Badlands commentary characterized the hearing as full of spin and long-debunked narratives, with Democratic members defending the prevailing account and the "Fauci didn't know" defense dominating the proceedings.[1]

## Chinese denials of gain-of-function work (February 2025)

On Wednesday, February 12, 2025, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman [[guo-jiakun|Guo Jiakun]] responded to US media reports that [[usaid|USAID]] had used American taxpayer money to fund gain-of-function studies of coronavirus at the institute, allegedly causing the COVID-19 pandemic.[2] Guo said China opposed political manipulation of origins-tracing and urged the United States to reflect on itself rather than shift blame.[2]

He stressed that China had stated many times that the Wuhan Institute of Virology had never engaged in gain-of-function studies of coronavirus and had never designed, made, or leaked COVID-19, and said it was extremely unlikely that the pandemic was caused by a lab leak — the authoritative conclusion, he said, of the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]]-China joint mission.[2] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] rejected both mainstream explanations, saying the [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]] and the wet market theory were both made up.[2]
