# Bill Cassidy

Bill Cassidy is a Republican United States Senator from Louisiana.[1]

## Friction with the Trump agenda pace

In June 2026, Cassidy was among the Senate Republicans identified in reporting on tensions with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the pace and strategy of advancing the administration's 2026 agenda.[1] Alongside Senators [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] and [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]], Cassidy pushed back on the expectation that senators simply approve proposals without changes or extended debate, citing Senate procedures and narrow majorities as constraints, while Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] emphasized working through the legislative process.[1] Despite the disagreements, the senators involved were described as generally supportive of many of Trump's policy goals.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed this resistance through the lens of change management, arguing that in a transformational change program one moves fast and breaks things, and that resisters such as Thune and his "old guard collaborators" were deliberately destabilized — discombobulated — and attempting delay to stop what was coming.[1]

## HELP Committee chairmanship and the McMahon nomination (2025)

As chair of the [[senate-help-committee|Senate HELP Committee]], the Louisiana Republican presided over the February 20, 2025 party-line vote that advanced [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]]'s nomination as Education Secretary to the Senate floor.[2] Cassidy said that if confirmed McMahon would face "the tall task of reforming a Department of Education that has lost its purpose," argued that for four years the [[department-of-education|department]] had "focused on everything but student learning with bureaucracy and red tape standing in the way of student success," and said the country needed "a strong leader at the department who will get our education system back on track," adding that McMahon had "demonstrated a strong vision for the Department of Education."[2]
