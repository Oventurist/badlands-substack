# Douglas Macgregor

Colonel Douglas Macgregor is a retired U.S. Army officer who served as a senior military advisor during the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and is frequently cited in Badlands Media commentary as an insider source on that period's personnel decisions.[1]

## Account of the Bolton appointment

Macgregor is cited by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] for the claim that President Trump wanted nothing to do with [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] until Israeli tycoon [[sheldon-adelson|Sheldon Adelson]] asked that Bolton be brought onboard, at which point Trump became receptive.[1] This account anchors the Brief's argument that Bolton's April 2018 – September 2019 tenure shaped the first administration's Venezuela policy, including the sanctions imposed on [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] and others in the Maduro circle.[1]

## Comments on a possible Netanyahu tribunal

In August 2024, Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited recent remarks by Colonel Macgregor as grounds for believing that an actual war crimes tribunal for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and his allies is a real possibility, describing the prospect as making the situation more dangerous and existential than previously assessed.[2]

## Assessment of an Israeli strike on Iran (October 2025)

In October 2025, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Macgregor as holding that all signs pointed toward a provocation or attack against Iran from Israel.[3] Macgregor was described as believing Israel would likely use a detachment of special forces in its next aggression against Iran, comparable to operations conducted in the DR Congo and Venezuela, all of which had failed.[3] That assessment was presented as the strategic backdrop to the [[syria-turkey-counterterrorism-cooperation-2025|Syria–Turkey counterterrorism cooperation]] announced the same weekend, with Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] framed as moving to subvert such an operation.[3]

## South Korea as a CIA client state

In commentary published in the July 8, 2025 Badlands News Brief, contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited an explanation Macgregor had given over six months earlier that [[south-korea|South Korea]] has been a CIA puppet state since the end of the Korean War.[4] On that account, the South Korean government has long engaged in a "slap-tickle fight" with North Korea that sustained a constant existential threat from the ruling Kim dynasty, which the military and defense sector exploited to extract wealth from the US taxpayer.[4] The argument was used to frame President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 25 percent tariff on South Korean goods as especially telling.[4]

## Napolitano interview on Bolton (October 2025)

Four days before [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] was indicted in October 2025, Macgregor told [[andrew-napolitano|Judge Napolitano]] that it was [[sheldon-adelson|Sheldon Adelson]] who convinced President Trump to bring Bolton into the first administration.[5] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the timing as signal and as proof of "drafting" — Trump using the inertia of the NeoCon and Zionist agenda to advance the white hat plan — in commentary on the [[indictment-of-john-bolton|indictment of John Bolton]].[5]

## Assessment that the Abraham Accords were dead (2024)

For roughly six months before October 2024, Macgregor argued publicly that the [[abraham-accords]] were dead and that he did not see them being revived, even as Arab partners kept Israel well-provisioned and intercepted enemy missiles overhead.[6] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he had initially dismissed the assertion as narrative, but revised his view after hearing foreign ministers of the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation]] — including Saudi FM [[faisal-bin-farhan]] — voice exasperation with Israel at the [[united-nations-general-assembly]], concluding that "Macgregor is on to something."[6]

## Commentary on the South Korean martial law crisis (December 2024)

In December 2024, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Macgregor as the authority to whom skeptical readers should defer regarding events in [[south-korea|South Korea]], where President [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]]'s short-lived martial law declaration had led to arrests of military leaders and a travel ban on the president.[7] The commentary paired Macgregor's assessment with speculation that the [[cia|CIA]] had become nervous about developments involving Russia, China, and [[north-korea|North Korea]].[7]

## Little Bighorn analogy for Israel (October 2024)

In October 2024, Macgregor appeared on Judge Andrew Napolitano's program and compared [[israel|Israel]]'s regional position to the events preceding the Battle of the Little Bighorn, "Custer's Last Stand."[8] He recounted how Colonel George Custer rode into the Black Hills — the Badlands — hunting Indians and unwittingly united the Sioux and Cheyenne, tribes previously at perpetual odds, by giving them a common enemy; led by Crazy Horse and Sitting Bull they ambushed and annihilated his forces to the last man.[8] Macgregor identified Custer's fatal errors as pompous arrogance, a view of the natives as vermin to be exterminated, going out looking for trouble, and vastly underestimating an enemy he assumed existed only in small scattered pockets.[8]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who said he completely agreed with Macgregor, extended the analogy to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s posture toward the peoples of the Middle East, and described the alliance now materializing there as something the world had never seen — not a caliphate nor an empire, and with no formal governing body over its factions, but a coalition of sovereign Muslim nation-states cooperating against a common enemy.[8]

## Assessment of the Gaza campaign (May 2024)

In interviews with Judge Andrew Napolitano in late May 2024, Macgregor argued that [[hamas|Hamas]] had "made whole" all of its personnel losses sustained since October 7th, thanks to what he characterised as the IDF's clumsy military campaign, which had proven to be a major recruiting tool for the group.[9] He said there was no military plan for preventing a Hamas resurgence in areas deemed "cleared" of hostiles, resulting in the return of Hamas militants to northern Gaza.[9]

Macgregor further argued that the chosen strategy — indiscriminate bombing of urban environments — is the opposite of what a discerning commander would do against guerrillas, since the ruins of a bombed-out city give an enormous advantage to defending infantry who can hide and ambush armor columns forced through limited corridors.[9] He also pointed to activity in the West Bank going unreported to Western audiences by corporate media and governments, and told Napolitano that he believed the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] are likely dead.[9]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited these assessments in rebutting [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]]'s "Finish Them!" artillery-shell inscription, arguing that destroying an enemy and achieving victory are almost never the same thing where lasting peace is the endgame.[9]

## August 2023 Tucker Carlson interview

On August 21, 2023, Macgregor appeared on episode 18 of [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s program on X, titled "Into the abyss," to argue that the war in Ukraine had to end immediately.[10] In the interview the retired colonel said the U.S. Armed Forces had "lost a sense of what is right," that morale and discipline had fallen to levels comparable to the late 1970s, and that existing cracks of disunity risked widening into massive "fissures" under the pressure of the war.[10] He also stated plainly that Ukraine was losing the war badly.[10]

The episode aired hours after Carlson posted video of himself meeting a Serbian diplomatic delegation, which prompted [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]] to call Carlson "a traitor to the US" on Twitter.[10] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] anticipated that Kinzinger would attack the decorated combat veteran as well, argued that Kinzinger's own participation in the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] amounted to treason, and described establishment figures as projecting their crimes onto those telling the truth — a tactic she said was no longer working now that roughly two-thirds of the public was alert to government corruption.[10]
