# Mark Epstein

Mark Epstein is the brother of financier and convicted sex offender [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], and has publicly pressed New York authorities for records related to his brother's 2019 death in federal custody.[1]

## Search for the pre-hospital care report

In a video shared on social media by former Fox News host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] in late December 2023 — an apparent preview of a forthcoming interview — Mark Epstein was recorded attempting to obtain the pre-hospital care report (PCR) written by the EMS team that moved his brother's body out of the cell.[1] In the exchange he asks an employee, "But as of right now, you're telling me you can't find the PCR report?" and the employee replies, "Not for the 10th of August 2019... It's not in the fire department database. I don't know why."[1]

Carlson framed the episode as a reason for skepticism about the official ruling that Jeffrey Epstein died by suicide, asking why so few records were available from that night if the government's account were true.[1] The exchange was reported by Caden Pearson of [[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] and amplified by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]].[1]

## Tucker Carlson interview (January 2024)

In an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] aired in early January 2024, Mark Epstein described his struggle to obtain basic documents related to his brother's death in federal custody and criticized the handling of the body, the unavailability of key records, and what he called the absence of a thorough investigation by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[2] He did not appear on camera for the interview.[2]

He stated that the autopsy showed his brother had been dead for at least two hours before he was found, and that protocol required staff to leave the body in place and call the medical examiner's office to take photographs and perform initial testing — "but that wasn't done."[2] He recounted that after the autopsy, both the city pathologist and Dr. Michael Baden "came out of the autopsy and they said this doesn't look like a suicide. It looks more like a homicide," pointing to broken neck bones more consistent with strangulation or physical attack than with hanging, and to the absence of physical signs expected on a body that had hung for an extended period.[2] Describing the autopsy photographs, he said the ligature mark "is in the middle of his neck and goes straight back as if someone put a rope around his neck and strangled him like Carlo in The Godfather in the car."[2]

The interview aired as unsealed court documents naming [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s associates — including former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] — dominated national coverage, and [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] observed that the "eliteness" of that list meant many people stood to benefit from Epstein's perpetual silence.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the account was a valuable first-hand refresher on the case and quoted Carlson's remark that "anyone who could murder someone in a federal detention facility obviously has a lot of power," adding that while she was not confident the full truth would emerge, she was not blackpilled — Clinton's presence on Epstein's plane had once been dismissed as conspiracy theory and was now accepted fact.[2]
