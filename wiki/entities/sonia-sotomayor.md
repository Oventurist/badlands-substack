# Sonia Sotomayor

Sonia Sotomayor is an Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] and a member of the Court's liberal wing. She appears in the April 17, 2026 Badlands Brief as the subject of a rare public apology issued to a fellow justice.

## The 2026 immigration dissent and apology

The Brief reports that Sotomayor apologized publicly to Justice [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]] after criticizing him sharply in a dissent involving an immigration case, acknowledging that her remarks had crossed into personal territory rather than remaining focused on legal reasoning. The dispute arose from a Supreme Court decision permitting the Trump administration to proceed with contested immigration enforcement actions; Sotomayor's dissent accused the majority, implicitly including Kavanaugh, of enabling harmful policy outcomes. Her apology clarified that she stood by her legal objections while regretting the tone directed at a colleague.

The remark that prompted the apology was quoted as: "I had a colleague in that case who wrote, you know, these are only temporary stops. This is from a man whose parents were professionals. And probably doesn't really know any person who works by the hour." Kavanaugh reportedly accepted the apology. The source treats the episode as notable given the Court's tradition of institutional decorum and its avoidance of direct personal criticism between justices.

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterizes the incident as "a mask slip moment" revealing that the Court's decorum is false, and argues that Sotomayor "is not an impartial arbiter of justice" but "an activist and an anti-constitutional radical" whose disdain for Kavanaugh surprises no one. She finds the story's saturation coverage across every network — including [[cnn|CNN]] — more interesting than the story itself, noting that it arrives while the Court has major merits cases pending on tariffs, independent-agency removal, Louisiana redistricting, Hawaii gun regulations, transgender sports bans, campaign-finance limits, and preemption and immigration issues. This assessment is contributor opinion rather than reporting.
