# Benghazi Attack

The September 11, 2012 attack on U.S. facilities in Benghazi, Libya killed Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]], information management officer Sean Smith, and former Navy SEALs [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]], in an extended assault by more than 100 Islamist militants.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Background

After Muammar Gaddafi was overthrown, Benghazi became a safe haven for Al Qaeda training camps and the focal point for funding and supplying jihadists fighting Americans in Iraq and Afghanistan, with the new government controlled by Al Qaeda elements such as [[abdelhakim-belhadj|Abdelhakim Belhadj]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Investors Business Daily reported that al-Qaida in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM), using heavy arms seized from the toppled Libyan army, hit the American consulate and assassinated the ambassador and three of his aides — the first American ambassador to die in the line of duty in 33 years.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Timeline

Three minutes after the first wave of assault at the Special Mission Compound (roughly 3:42 p.m. in Washington), senior diplomats at the U.S. Embassy in Tripoli learned of the attack; [[africom|AFRICOM]] learned of it within thirty minutes.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The [[state-department|State Department]]'s Diplomatic Command Center was notified immediately, but the notice took about an hour to reach the [[pentagon|Pentagon]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) By 5 p.m. President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], the Secretary of Defense, the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs and the National Security Advisor met at the [[white-house|White House]], where Obama directed that all resources be used to save lives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) [[leon-panetta|Leon Panetta]] testified he identified three distinct capabilities by 6 p.m. and ordered deployment by 7 p.m.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) At 7:30 p.m. a deputies meeting led by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] emphasized that deployment into Libya required Libyan government approval.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The attack lasted more than seven hours; no U.S. help arrived, and a Libyan militia of ex-Gaddafi soldiers rescued the Americans trapped at the CIA annex while the administration watched via drone feed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Aftermath

The [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee]] report opened with a Diplomatic Security Agent's plea, "If you guys don't get out here, we're all going to f—-ing die," and contrasted the heroism of GRS personnel and Team Tripoli with the [[dereliction-of-duty|dereliction of duty]] of leaders in Washington.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article argues the administration's weeks-long focus on a protest over an online video was a red herring concealing the weapons pipeline, and that 37 Americans were deliberately left without rescue.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## 2026 extradition of Zubayar Al-Bakoush

On February 6, 2026, U.S. authorities transported Libyan national [[zubayar-al-bakoush|Zubayar Al-Bakoush]] to Virginia, and he appeared in federal court in Washington, D.C., where he was ordered detained pending further proceedings.[2] An unsealed eight-count indictment in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia charges him with murder, attempted murder, arson, terrorism, conspiracy and material support for terrorism over his alleged role in the assault on the U.S. diplomatic mission.[2]

The Brief restated that the September 11, 2012 attack killed Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|J. Christopher Stevens]], State Department information officer Sean Smith, and CIA security contractors [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]].[2] Al-Bakoush is the third person charged in U.S. courts in connection with the attack, after [[ahmed-abu-khatallah|Ahmed Abu Khatallah]] and [[mustafa-al-imam|Mustafa al-Imam]] were convicted; a further suspect, Ali Awni al-Harzi, was killed in a 2015 airstrike in Iraq.[2] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and U.S. Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]] used public remarks to emphasize continued pursuit of those responsible.[2]

### Commentary on accountability

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the arrest — the first Benghazi-related arrest in over eight years — was "too little too late" for survivors, writing that "one more foreign arrest isn't justice — it's paperwork."[2] Annex contractor [[john-tiegen|John "Tig" Tiegen]] called the case "a waste of time and resources" and insisted that "Benghazi wasn't a criminal act, it was an attack by an enemy force," urging that accountability be directed at U.S. officials.[2] The Brief noted that no senior political appointees were terminated or formally sanctioned and no employees fired over Benghazi.[2]

The piece also tied the news to [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s expected testimony before [[congress|Congress]] on the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] scandal, noting that Benghazi was one of the founding scandals involving her private email server, and that current CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] had questioned then-FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] about that server during his own time in Congress.[2]

## The video narrative and its purpose

Badlands contributor Joe Lange argues that the "protest over an online video" explanation advanced for weeks after the attack was planned in advance rather than improvised.[3] [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] issued the first statement linking an [[innocence-of-muslims|anti-Islam video]] to the assault at 10:07 p.m. on September 11, 2012, before updating President [[barack-obama|Obama]]; [[susan-rice|Susan Rice]], then UN ambassador and national security adviser, then carried the claim across the Sunday news programs.[3]

The evidence cut against the narrative from the outset. U.S. officials knew within 24 hours that it was terrorism, and personnel on the ground in Libya reported a direct assault rather than a protest gone awry.[3] Later that evening a mortar strike killed former Navy SEALs [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]], permanently maimed another CIA contractor and severely injured diplomatic security agent David Ubben, all of whom were defending the CIA annex; the accuracy of the strike — three of five rounds on target from more than half a mile away in darkness in under a minute — required military training and premeditation according to multiple military and intelligence professionals.[3] Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|J. Christopher Stevens]] and Foreign Service information management officer Sean Smith also died.[3] Survivor [[kris-paronto|Kris "Tanto" Paronto]] said the attackers knew when the ambassador would be there and knew there would be no response, exploiting a vulnerable high-value target, and that he first heard of the film about 36 hours afterward.[3] Judicial Watch obtained documents showing the administration continued to withhold the full contents of a "media strategy" discussion held weeks after the attack.[3]

Lange's conclusion is that the video story existed not to conceal the fact of terrorism — which was quickly evident — but to create a pretext for arresting [[nakoula-basseley-nakoula|Nakoula Basseley Nakoula]] and to fold Benghazi into the [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]] narrative, advancing the speech restrictions embodied in [[un-resolution-16-18|UN Resolution 16/18]].[3]
