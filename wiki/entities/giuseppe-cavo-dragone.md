# Giuseppe Cavo Dragone

Admiral Giuseppe Cavo Dragone is an Italian naval officer who serves as chair of the NATO Military Committee, the alliance's senior uniformed post and its top military advisory role.[1] In that capacity he functions as the principal military adviser to the North Atlantic Council and is a public voice for [[nato|NATO]]'s collective posture toward [[russia|Russia]].[1]

## Call for a "more aggressive" posture toward Russia

In an interview with the *Financial Times* published on Sunday, 30 November 2025, Dragone said that NATO member states had been weighing options for responding to what he characterized as Russia's "hybrid war" against the alliance.[1] "We are studying everything … being more aggressive or being proactive instead of reactive is something that we are thinking about," he said.[1]

Dragone went further, suggesting that a "pre-emptive strike" could be framed as a "defensive action," while conceding that such a doctrine would sit "further away from our normal way of thinking and behavior."[1] According to the *Financial Times*, diplomats from Eastern European member states had been especially vocal in demanding tougher measures against Russia, including retaliatory cyberattacks.[1] Dragone noted that NATO's decision-making in this area remained constrained by legal and ethical concerns as well as by questions of jurisdiction.[1]

## Reception in independent media

Badlands commentary treated Dragone's remarks with derision, arguing that an aggressive posture toward Russia had been NATO's organizing premise since its founding and certainly since the 2022 invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1] The Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry sarcastically framed the statement as NATO "playing opossum" for four years and only shifting gears once the Ukrainian military position had deteriorated, Ukrainian officials had resigned amid corruption exposure, and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] appeared close to announcing a peace agreement.[1] The timing of the commander's escalation talk — arriving as negotiations advanced — was read as evidence that neoconservative elements within the alliance were attempting to sabotage a settlement rather than respond to any new Russian threat.[1]
