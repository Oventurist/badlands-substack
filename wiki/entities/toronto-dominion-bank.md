# Toronto-Dominion Bank

Toronto-Dominion Bank (TD Bank) is a major Canadian banking group whose United States operations became the centerpiece of one of the largest anti-money-laundering enforcement actions in American history. In October 2024 the bank pleaded guilty to multiple criminal charges and paid roughly $3 billion in fines and other penalties to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and financial regulators for failing to monitor money laundering conducted through its systems by fentanyl traffickers and other criminal actors.[1] The resolution was reported in contemporaneous coverage under the headline "TD Bank Pleads Guilty to Bank Secrecy Act and Money Laundering Conspiracy Violations in $1.8B Resolution," reflecting the differing components of the overall penalty package.[1]

## Fentanyl trafficking and compliance failure

The government's case rested on the finding that the Canadian bank had effectively chosen profits over compliance with the [[bank-secrecy-act|Bank Secrecy Act]], allowing fentanyl and narcotics trafficking operations to make use of its banking services.[1] A federal probe into the bank's role in laundering illicit fentanyl profits had been publicly announced in May 2024, months before the guilty plea.[1]

## Wider implications for US banks

Following the TD resolution, the money-laundering investigation appeared to expand to institutions inside the United States, with [[bank-of-america|Bank of America]] disclosing in an October 2024 filing that it was in discussions with several federal regulators over its own Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering, and sanctions compliance programs.[1] Investigative journalist Sam Cooper and former [[state-department|State Department]] investigator David Asher had earlier framed the TD case as the leading edge of a broader inquiry, with Asher predicting, "We'll see which one of the big four US banks gets named next."[1]

Badlands Media commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the TD case in a sequence alongside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s December 2017 executive order blocking the property of persons involved in serious human rights abuse or corruption, his December 2023 declaration of "war on cartels," and his October 2024 proposal to seize cartel assets, posing the rhetorical questions "Who controls the cartels? Who controls the banks? What is the Deep State?"[1]

## Record short interest (April 2023)

In early April 2023, the Financial Post reported that the largest short position against any bank anywhere in the world was not in Switzerland or Silicon Valley but in Canada, where short sellers had built roughly US$3.7 billion of bearish bets against Toronto-Dominion Bank, the country's second-largest lender.[2] According to an analysis by S3 Partners, that made TD the most heavily shorted financial institution globally, ahead of France's BNP Paribas SA and [[bank-of-america|Bank of America Corp]].[2] The short interest had risen in the weeks following the March 2023 banking turmoil.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] sponsor and financial analyst [[kirk-elliott|Dr. Kirk Elliott]] commented that it was hard to find bright spots, trust or confidence in traditional financial markets where layoffs and uncertainty had become the norm, and that once-trusted institutions were now objects of scrutiny.[2] He linked the banking news to reports that four billionaires had been subpoenaed in connection with the [[us-virgin-islands|U.S. Virgin Islands]] legal action against [[jpmorgan-chase|JP Morgan]] over [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], and said investors and ordinary people alike were increasingly turning toward gold and silver as trust in the system eroded.[2]
