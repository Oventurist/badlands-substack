# Bob Good

Bob Good is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from Virginia (R-VA).[1]

## Montpelier oversight letter

In May 2023, Good joined Rep. [[eli-crane|Eli Crane]] (R-AZ) in demanding answers about what the two lawmakers described as "woke" influences at President [[james-madison|James Madison]]'s [[montpelier|Montpelier]] estate and other historical sites.[1] The pair flagged concerns about how Montpelier exhibits discuss slavery and the country's founding documents, and sought answers from the head of the [[national-trust-for-historic-preservation|National Trust for Historic Preservation]] about whether federal funds had been used to support the exhibits.[1]

In a letter to Jay Clemens, interim president of the National Trust, obtained by the [[washington-examiner|Washington Examiner]], Good and Crane wrote: "We urge you to focus on the true life and history of James Madison, rather than succumbing to dishonest, 'woke,' and radical anti-American prejudices. We look forward to your prompt response."[1] They underscored that the trust was established to "preserve and protect our nation's history, as well as perpetuate the American story," and expressed befuddlement that the "sole exhibit focused on the Constitution" advanced the notion that the document is a "pro-slavery document."[1]

The two representatives further objected that "the only exhibit for children aims to teach them about race and slavery and includes a children's book with graphic images and 'imagination exercises' for whites and blacks that encourages children to imagine themselves as the aggressor and to examine their own capacity for evil."[1] They sought answers regarding the lack of exhibits concentrated on Madison's achievements, the lack of exhibits explaining the importance of the Constitution, and the "restricted funds" used for operating expenses.[1]

Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the letter as a rare instance of legislative pushback against what she characterized as an ongoing rewrite of American history, asking why only two US legislators were pressing the issue.[1]
