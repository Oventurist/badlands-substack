# Ray Valenzuela

Ray Valenzuela is a [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] elections official who appeared as the defense's sole witness in the second phase of [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]'s 2022 election challenge trial.[1]

## Role in Lake v. Hobbs

After the plaintiffs rested their case on May 18, 2023, the defendants in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]] immediately moved for summary judgment without presenting a defense.[1] Following what Badlands Media described as an impassioned back-and-forth, the judge declined the motion, and the defense then called Valenzuela as its only witness.[1] Attorney Liddy conducted the direct examination, with Lake's attorneys scheduled to cross-examine Valenzuela the following day.[1]

## May 2023 trial testimony

Rey Valenzuela, Maricopa County Director of Elections for mail-in voting and election services, was the defendants' star and only witness in the May 2023 Count III trial of [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], although he was first called by the plaintiffs.[2] He has worked in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] elections for over 30 years, verified signatures for more than 20, and helped design the county's [[signature-verification|signature verification]] process.[2]

On the first day, Valenzuela testified that it was not at all uncommon to approve ballots in two to four seconds, testimony that conflicted with plaintiff whistleblowers' accounts of the county's training.[2] He dismissed that conflict by characterizing the documented process as suggestive rather than required and by saying the whistleblowers simply did not understand how signature verification was supposed to work.[2] He also affirmed that signatures were approved at two other locations without observers present, saying indignantly that he was not going to have someone standing over his shoulder while he approved signatures alone in the Clerk and Recorder's office.[2]

Expert testimony from [[erich-speckin|Erich Speckin]] that more than 45,000 signatures had been approved through a batch upload forced Valenzuela to correct his prior testimony that batch files were not used in Maricopa's signature verification.[2] After the court denied the defense's motion for a directed verdict, Valenzuela — having been called as a fact witness — returned to the stand as the county's expert witness.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that he contradicted himself so many times over the three days that Americans should have zero confidence that statutory signature verification is taking place, and noted that he claimed at one point, without support, to have personally reviewed "millions" of signatures before walking the statement back when pressed.[2]
