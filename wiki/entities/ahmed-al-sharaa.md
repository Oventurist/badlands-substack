# Ahmed al-Sharaa

Ahmed al-Sharaa is the Syrian leader referenced in July 2026 Badlands commentary on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s regional diplomacy, and is also known by the nom de guerre [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Abu Mohammad al-Jolani]].[1]

## Trump's regional diplomacy (July 2026)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that in a single week Trump publicly highlighted his relationships with Muslim leaders in the region, naming both Türkiye's [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdoğan]] and Syria's al-Sharaa.[1] He argued that these "bromances" with Middle Eastern Muslim leaders were unlikely to please the IDF and [[mossad|Mossad]], and formed part of the motive structure he saw behind reported intelligence about an [[iran|Iranian]] plot to assassinate Trump.[1]

## Ben-Gvir assassination call and the Jolani question (March 2026)

Israeli National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] made public statements calling for the assassination of al-Sharaa, formerly known as Mohammed Abu al-Jolani.[2] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued this was awkward in light of [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]]'s disclosure that the [[cia|CIA]] worked directly with [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] and [[islamic-state|ISIS]] in Syria on Israel's behalf, given that Jolani had once been a leader in both organizations — which he suggested explains why the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government was initially thrilled at his taking power in December 2024.[2]

He speculated that Israel entered the operation to overthrow [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] believing Jolani was working for them, only to find him aligned with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdoğan]], and that Israeli expectations of annexing large portions of southern [[syria|Syria]] went unfulfilled.[2] Reports of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] raising the Israeli flag over towns in southern Syria were cited as evidence that Israel intended to take the land regardless, in pursuit of [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]].[2]

## Proposed Syrian role against Hezbollah (June 2026)

At the [[2026-g7-summit|G7 summit]] in June 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly criticized [[israel|Israel]]'s military campaign against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and suggested that Israel allow [[syria|Syria]], under al-Sharaa, to take the lead against the group.[3] Trump described the Syrian leader as capable and asserted that Syrian forces could accomplish the task more effectively and with fewer civilian casualties, saying that if Israel could not do the job without killing everyone else, "Syria will do the job."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Trump praised al-Sharaa — still widely referred to by his former nom de guerre Jolani — as extremely cooperative and as having done everything Trump asked of him, while claiming to have installed him.[3] He argued that elevating al-Sharaa's stature in this way, alongside Trump's rebuke of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] as reckless or incompetent, was one of the most damaging things Trump could publicly say about Israel at that moment.[3]

## Trump's endorsement and Syria's delisting (July 2026)

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced, while seated next to al-Sharaa at the [[2026-ankara-nato-summit|NATO summit in Turkey]], that he would remove [[syria|Syria]] from the [[state-sponsors-of-terrorism-list|State Sponsors of Terrorism list]], praising the Syrian president for having "done a great job."[4] The administration notified [[congress|Congress]], beginning a mandatory 45-day review period, and the move followed earlier steps to unwind parts of the US sanctions program against Syria.[4] Badlands commentary described Trump's glowing review of al-Sharaa as infuriating hostile media voices such as [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]], and framed the pivot as bringing Syria and Turkey deeper into Trump's orbit along the lines of the "Venezuela Model."[4]

## January 2026 Moscow summit with Putin

On Wednesday, January 28, 2026, Sharaa met Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Moscow for talks covering Syria's reconstruction; bilateral cooperation in trade, healthcare, construction, industry, and sports; the future of Russia's military presence at bases including Khmeimim and Tartus; and restoring Syria's territorial integrity, including reestablishing government control over territories east of the Euphrates River.[5]

The summit followed a partial Russian drawdown: five Syrian sources cited by Reuters said Russia had begun withdrawing troops and equipment from Qamishli airport in northeastern Syria the previous week, described as a redeployment rather than a full exit, with the Kremlin expected to retain its main air base and Mediterranean naval facility.[5] The same week, Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] spoke by phone with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] about developments in Syria, Gaza, bilateral relations, trade, and defense industry cooperation, emphasizing full implementation of the Syria ceasefire.[5]

Badlands commentary framed Sharaa's relationship with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] as symbolically important, noting that after Sharaa seized control of Syria in December 2024 — which the writer suggested occurred with some degree of Assad's consent — MBS paid off Syria's debt to the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]], averting a distressed asset sale.[5] Gulf states are now investing billions in Syria's natural gas infrastructure and coordinating with Erdoğan to bring Syrian tribes into negotiations, with regional stability rather than return on investment cited as the goal.[5]

## Credit to Erdoğan for sanctions relief (July 2026)

In late July 2026, al-Sharaa publicly credited Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] — along with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] — for mediating with [[donald-trump|President Trump]] the previous year to lift most U.S. sanctions on [[syria|Syria]] following the fall of the Assad regime.[6] He noted that Syria's remaining designation as a state sponsor of terrorism continues to pose an economic obstacle, and said Damascus has provided assurances on counterterrorism in support of that designation's full removal.[6]

## January 2026 Kurdish framework and the ISIS question

In January 2026, Kurdish authorities and the Syrian government reached a framework agreement addressing security and governance arrangements in Kurdish-controlled areas; the deal was described as preliminary and no implementation timeline was publicly released.[7]

In the same period, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] drew a connection between al-Sharaa — once a public leader of [[isis|ISIS]] — and [[nouri-al-maliki|Nouri Al-Maliki]], whom the [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] alleged helped create the group, speculating that both men may once have worked for one side and now work for the other.[7]

## December 2024 remarks on Russia

In an interview with Al Arabiya at the end of December 2024, al-Sharaa said the new Syrian authorities led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] would like to remain on good terms with Russia, and suggested his government would not necessarily seek the withdrawal of Moscow's military installations from the country.[8] He argued that Syria has "strategic interests" in maintaining good relations with "the second most powerful country in the world," and said, "We don't want Russia to exit Syria in a way that would not be befitting for its long-standing relations" with the Middle Eastern nation.[8] The HTS chief emphasized that the new authorities in Damascus wanted to avoid conflicts with foreign powers.[8] The remarks bore directly on the future of the [[khmeimim-air-base|Khmeimim]] and [[tartus-naval-base|Tartus]] facilities, whose status had been in doubt since [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] resigned and fled to Moscow.[8]

Badlands commentary noted the resulting realignment: both the [[state-department|State Department]] and the Israeli Foreign Ministry had begun turning on the very fighters they were said to have backed, with Israeli Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] calling al-Sharaa's outfit a "gang" and his government illegitimate only weeks after [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] hailed him as the liberator of Syria, while the State Department formally unmasked his background and terror ties.[8] The same analysis predicted that al-Sharaa's rule would prove brittle and short-lived as [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]]'s Turkey pushed south toward Damascus.[8]

## Palmyra attack coverage

Coverage of the December 2025 [[palmyra-attack-on-iowa-national-guard-members|Palmyra ambush]] that killed two Iowa National Guard members noted that Syria's new government is headed by al-Sharaa, described as a former al-Qaeda terrorist.[9] Syrian Interior Ministry spokesman Nour al-Din al-Baba, speaking for that government, characterized the attack as a major security breach while defending the overall record of Syrian security forces since the fall of Bashar Assad.[9]

## Currency redesign and the Goznak deal (2025)

In August 2025 Reuters reported that Syria under al-Sharaa would print the banknotes for its redesigned and revalued currency in Russia.[10] Damascus had decided to give the severely devalued Syrian pound a makeover and remove two zeroes from the currency in order to ease transactions and restore confidence; the pound had lost over 99% of its value since 2011, falling from 50 to around 10,000 per US dollar.[10] Central Bank Governor Abdelkader Husrieh announced the plan for a new currency aimed at "strengthening customer confidence" and easing foreign transactions, calling the move a "necessity."[10] According to two bankers and another source, Damascus reached a deal with the Russian state-owned firm [[goznak|Goznak]] — which had printed Syrian currency under [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar Assad]] — with the agreement reportedly finalized during Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaiban's visit to Moscow in late July.[10]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the arrangement as puzzling on its face, given [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s close ties to Assad, but argued it runs parallel to [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s Azerbaijan–Armenia peace deal and reported plans for Turkish gas transit to Syria, collectively subverting Israeli efforts to balkanize Syria in service of [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]].[10]

## Kremlin visit (October 2025)

On Wednesday, October 15, 2025, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] hosted al-Sharaa at the Kremlin, praising the two countries' deep historical ties and friendly relations; it marked the first visit of a Syrian leader to Russia since the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar Assad]]'s government late the previous year.[11] Al-Sharaa said Moscow would play a significant role in his country's transition to a "new Syria" and vowed to honor all past commitments, adding that [[syria|Syria]] continues to "build on the many achievements" that cooperation with Russia has facilitated.[11] Putin said diplomatic ties between the two states "have always been friendly" since their establishment in 1944 and that throughout the decades Moscow had "always been guided by one thing – the interests of the Syrian people."[11] Al-Sharaa, who once led the Islamist group Hayat Tahrir al-Sham under the nom de guerre Abu Mohammad al-Julani, rose to power following Assad's departure.[11]

Reuters reporting cited by Badlands indicated that al-Sharaa intended to use the talks to formally request that Moscow hand over Assad to face trial over alleged crimes against Syrians — a request Russia was unlikely to grant — and to seek economic concessions including resumed wheat supplies on favourable terms and compensation for war damage, as well as Moscow's backing to resist Israeli demands for a wider demilitarised zone in southern Syria, possibly including the redeployment of Russian military police as a guarantor against further Israeli encroachment.[11]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the Damascus government wants Assad in order to humiliate and execute him, alleged that al-Sharaa was deployed by the [[state-department|US State Department]] and Mossad to overthrow the Syrian government, and predicted Putin would not surrender Assad because doing so would betray a longtime ally and signal to the emerging "Sovereign World Order" that Putin's word as a negotiator is worthless — invoking [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 1987 book The Art of the Deal on integrity as a negotiator's most valuable asset.[11]

## Israeli calls for his elimination, July 2025

On July 15, 2025, Israeli Diaspora Affairs Minister [[amichai-chikli|Amichai Chikli]] publicly called for al-Sharaa's elimination amid ongoing Israeli strikes on the Syrian regime and while diplomatic efforts toward a security agreement between Israel and Syria were still under way.[12] Chikli described him as "Al-Qaeda in a suit and tie," rejected his legitimacy as a leader, and invoked the massacre of the [[alawites|Alawites]] and the treatment of the [[druze]] as justification for fighting what he called "the terror regime in Syria."[12]

Badlands commentary from [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the Israeli government had been elated when al-Sharaa, then known as Abu Mohammed al-Jolani, overthrew [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in December, and theorized that he had been trained and deployed by [[mossad]] for that purpose before accepting a better offer from [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]] and Turkey — thereafter defying Israeli demands for control of Damascus and southern Syria and for a corridor between the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] and the Euphrates River.[12] The same commentary speculated that Assad would eventually return as Syria's "savior."[12]

## Response to Israeli strikes on Damascus (July 2025)

After powerful Israeli airstrikes on Damascus in July 2025, Sharaa accused [[israel|Israel]] of seeking "chaos and destruction," saying in his first televised statement that protecting Druze citizens and their rights is "our priority."[13] Israel had vowed to destroy Syrian government forces attacking [[druze|Druze]] communities in southern Syria.[13] Addressing Druze citizens, Sharaa said "we reject any attempt to drag you into hands of an external party."[13] Directing his remarks at Israel, he added: "We are not among those who fear the war. We have spent our lives facing challenges and defending our people, but we have put the interests of the Syrians before chaos and destruction," and said the Syrian people are not afraid of war and are ready to fight if their dignity is threatened.[13]

Badlands commentary linked the strikes to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s domestic position, arguing that bombing [[syria|Syria]] served to shore up a coalition destabilized by the departures of [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] and [[shas|Shas]] from the government.[13]

## Revocation of the HTS terrorist designation (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, the United States formally revoked the foreign terrorist organization designation for [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]], the group Sharaa led, nearly two months after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met with him in his capacity as Syria's self-declared interim president.[14] ZeroHedge described the sequence as not merely ironic but scandalous, noting that Trump met a US-designated terrorist during his Gulf tour while in Saudi Arabia, and that Sharaa's standing as the "former" al-Qaeda in Syria figure who helped overthrow [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] appeared to be all that Washington cared about.[14] The [[state-department|State Department]] memo effecting the change was signed by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and revoked the designation of the al-Nusra Front, also known as Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham.[14] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] revisited the suspicion that Sharaa, under his nom de guerre al-Jolani, may always have been a well-trained television actor now under new management, or alternatively a CIA- or Mossad-deployed operator who had been offered a better deal by Trump and the Saudis.[14]

## U.S. backing amid Israeli strikes (December 2025)

Supporting al-Sharaa's efforts to stabilize Syria and encouraging him to enter a peace process with Israel were described in December 2025 as key elements of the Trump administration's Middle East strategy, with Trump and his team repeatedly siding with Syria's government in disputes with Israel — the only country in the region for which that was the case.[15] Trump posted on Truth Social in support of al-Sharaa and said he was "very satisfied" with Syria's performance under the former Islamist rebel leader, who had made a historic visit to the [[white-house|White House]] in November 2025.[15] Badlands commentary speculated that Israel might move against al-Sharaa, noted his cooperation with the United States and Turkey in bombing ISIS locations, and cited rumors of Russian redeployment in southern Syria.[15]
