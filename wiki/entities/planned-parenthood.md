# Planned Parenthood

Planned Parenthood is one of the United States' leading providers of healthcare for women, and the defendant in litigation that could potentially bankrupt the organization.[1]

## Fifth Circuit litigation

As of October 2024, a lawsuit described as one of the most consequential cases moving through the American legal system was working its way toward the U.S. Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals, seeking to strike another blow to abortion rights and possibly to bankrupt Planned Parenthood.[1] The case was filed by an anti-abortion activist tied to [[leonard-leo|Leonard Leo]], and has been heard at every level — from the lower courts up to the Fifth Circuit — by judges who are themselves linked to Leo.[1]

Coverage of the case situates it within the wider architecture of the [[federalist-society|Federalist Society]], the conservative legal group Leo built into a political powerhouse and which supplied the judicial appointments that produced the Supreme Court majority responsible for erasing federal abortion protections in 2022.[1] The suit is therefore presented less as an isolated action than as the product of a MAGA-aligned legal network operating across multiple tiers of the judiciary.[1]

## South Carolina Medicaid exclusion (2025)

On June 26, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruled 6–3 that Planned Parenthood and a South Carolina patient lacked standing to challenge the state's exclusion of the organization from its [[medicaid|Medicaid]] program because it provides abortions.[2] Writing for the majority, Justice [[neil-gorsuch|Neil Gorsuch]] held that the Medicaid Act's "any qualified provider" requirement does not create a clear and unambiguous individual right enforceable through private lawsuits.[2] The decision ended the suit brought by patient Julie Edwards; Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]] dissented, joined by Justices [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] and [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]].[2] The case is treated in detail at [[south-carolina-medicaid-planned-parenthood-case]].[2]

## Closure of the Houston facility (2025)

In October 2025 Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] announced arrests of eight people accused of operating illegal abortion clinics in the Houston area, and reporting noted that the arrests followed the recent closure of a major Planned Parenthood facility in Houston, once one of the largest abortion clinics in the country.[3] Badlands commentary welcomed the Texas closure alongside the arrests, and argued — while citing Guttmacher-derived figures suggesting that in all but roughly 0.15% of cases in Florida abortion is an elective procedure — that the overwhelming majority of abortions nationally are elective rather than emergency interventions.[3]

## 1916 founding in the Badlands account

Badlands Media commentary states that 1916 gave America Planned Parenthood, started by a group of racists seeking to control the population and eliminate the gene pool of groups they viewed as inferior.[6] It asserts that clinics were set up mostly in Black neighborhoods in an attempt to reduce or eliminate Black people from American society, and the author offers the opinion that legal abortion became the greatest Satanic child sacrifice system the world has ever known.[6] The founding is counted among the institutional developments of the 1910s that the article calls the decade Americans became slaves.[6]

## Origins and Rockefeller funding

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] traces Planned Parenthood to the [[american-birth-control-league|American Birth Control League]] founded by [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]], whose clinical birth control studies were financed by the Rockefeller-controlled [[bureau-of-social-hygiene|Bureau of Social Hygiene]].[7] He notes Sanger's advocacy of limiting "dysgenic stocks" and "slum dwellers."[7]

The organization remained funded by the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] into the late 1960s.[7] In 1969 its national medical director, Dr. [[richard-day|Richard Day]], told a Pittsburgh gathering of pediatric physicians attended by Dr. [[lawrence-dunegan|Lawrence Dunegan]] that hard-to-cure diseases would be created in the future and that cures for nearly all cancers had already been developed but were being hidden at the [[rockefeller-institute|Rockefeller Institute]] so that populations would not increase.[7]
