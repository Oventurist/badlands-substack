# Jill Wine-Banks

Jill Wine-Banks is an American attorney, former Watergate prosecutor, and legal commentator who appears as a guest analyst on [[msnbc|MSNBC]]. She became a recurring presence on cable television during the criminal prosecutions of former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], where she was routinely asked to compare Trump's legal exposure to the Watergate-era cases she worked on as a young government lawyer.[1]

## August 2023 remarks on Trump's Fulton County surrender

On August 21, 2023, Wine-Banks appeared on MSNBC opposite host Alicia Menendez, who asked her how Trump's impending surrender in Georgia would differ from his earlier indictments. Wine-Banks replied that the Georgia process would be "very different because they have said that they are going to do mug shots and fingerprints," and that Trump would be "turning himself in at the Fulton County Jail, not in a federal building, not in a clean, nice environment."[1] She characterized the [[fulton-county-jail|Fulton County Jail]] as, "from what we hear from the press, a really dirty, dangerous, scary place."[1]

Wine-Banks then drew on her Watergate experience, saying that "some of the Watergate defendants, when they were put in the D.C. jail, really freaked out," to the point that prosecutors "had to move them to a Army base where they could be housed without being in fear at all times so that they could cooperate and testify." She suggested Trump's own "experience of being in a real jail" would produce "a very different picture."[1]

## Reception in independent media

The segment was covered by Summit News under the headline that the "MSNBC talking head delights at thought of Trump in a 'really dirty, dangerous, scary' jail," framing her tone as anticipatory rather than analytical.[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the clip as an illustration of what the outlet's contributors describe as a quasi-religious animus toward Trump, comparing the commentary to a scene from Frank Peretti's novel *This Present Darkness* in which a demon stirs a sleeping academic's brain.[1] The same commentary argued that Wine-Banks' description of the Atlanta jail as filthy and terrifying was an unintended indictment of the criminal justice system itself, raising the question of where taxpayer money for jail conditions and justice reform had gone, and predicted that the spectacle of a mug shot would accelerate rather than dampen the [[great-awakening|great awakening]] among Trump's supporters.[1]
