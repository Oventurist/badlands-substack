# Neuralink

Neuralink is the brain-implant company founded by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], developing surgically implanted neural interface devices. Its research program has relied on animal subjects, including primates, and that testing became the basis of a federal inquiry reported in February 2023.[1]

## Department of Transportation investigation (2023)

On Thursday, February 9, 2023, the [[us-department-of-transportation|U.S. Department of Transportation]] confirmed that it was investigating Neuralink over the potentially illegal movement of hazardous pathogens.[1] A Department spokesperson disclosed the probe to Reuters after the [[physicians-committee-for-responsible-medicine|Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine]] (PCRM), an animal-welfare advocacy group, wrote to Secretary of Transportation [[pete-buttigieg|Pete Buttigieg]] earlier the same day to alert the agency to records it had obtained.[1]

PCRM said it had obtained emails and other documents suggesting unsafe packaging and movement of implants removed from the brains of monkeys, and that these implants may have carried infectious diseases in violation of federal law.[1] The Department of Transportation spokesperson said the agency took PCRM's allegations "very seriously."[1]

Notably, PCRM did not identify any harm resulting from the incidents, stating instead that Neuralink's actions "may pose a serious and ongoing public health risk."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] argued that the investigation appeared politicized, coming shortly after the release of the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] under Musk's ownership of Twitter.[1] He suggested that if a hazardous-materials packaging complaint was "the best they can come up with" against Musk, his opponents likely lacked anything more substantial, while acknowledging that animal testing is itself "horrific and inhumane."[1] He characterized the allegations as "all conjecture and what-ifs," but sufficient to launch a probe likely to function as an assault on Musk's credibility and reputation.[1]

## First human implant (2024)

Neuralink implanted its first human patient, [[noland-arbaugh|Noland Arbaugh]], in January 2024, and in March 2024 the company published a nine-minute video showing his progress with the device.[2] Arbaugh, a 29-year-old man paralyzed from the shoulders down in a diving accident eight years earlier, was shown playing chess on a computer using the mind-controlled chip, as well as playing the video game Civilization and taking Japanese and French lessons.[2] Neuralink described the trial as a complete success; doctors detected no cognitive impairment and Arbaugh was discharged within a day of the procedure.[2] Arbaugh said he could move his computer mouse cursor with his thoughts and cautioned that "there is a lot of work to do, but it has already changed my life. I don't want people to think that this is the end of the journey."[2]

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that he was torn about the technology, recognizing its potential to help patients with Parkinson's, epilepsy, Alzheimer's, blindness or paralysis while worrying about what happens if such devices fall into the wrong hands, become smarter than humans, become sentient, or transmit harmful signals.[2] He concluded that there were "far too many red flags and what ifs" for him to be comfortable with it, invoking Terminator and iRobot as cautionary references.[2]
