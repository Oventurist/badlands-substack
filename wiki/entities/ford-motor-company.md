# Ford Motor Company

Ford Motor Company is an American automaker that, in May 2026, announced it was exploring how its commercial vehicle lines could supply North American and European militaries — a move Badlands commentators read as an early marker of a World War II–style industrial mobilization.[1]

## Defense pivot

Ford said it was assessing how products such as its F-Series and Ranger trucks, along with its Pro Power Onboard generation technology, could "quickly and cost-effectively" provide militaries with mobility, transport, and field-power solutions.[1] The company explicitly invoked its World War II record of producing hundreds of thousands of aircraft, trucks, and engines.[1]

The announcement aligned with broader talks between the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and automakers — including General Motors and Oshkosh — aimed at converting idle civilian production capacity into defense output.[1] Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] characterized the shift as placing the U.S. industrial base on a "wartime footing" amid stockpiles depleted by conflicts across Eurasia.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Ford's move signals the revival of Detroit's "Arsenal of Democracy" identity, but oriented toward "sovereign power projection along kinetic, technological and energetic axes" rather than "abstract globalist adventures."[1] In this reading the productive capacity always existed and was suppressed by an inverted narrative; the apparent great-power confrontation among Trump, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] serves as the cover story that makes reindustrialization politically possible.[1]

## 2023 UAW strike

Ford was one of three automakers targeted by the [[2023-united-auto-workers-strike|2023 United Auto Workers strike]], which shut down one assembly plant at each of the Detroit Three.[2] On September 16, 2023, the [[united-auto-workers|UAW]] said negotiators for the union and Ford had held "reasonably productive discussions" toward a new contract — a more positive assessment than the union offered of the other talks.[2] Two days earlier, chief executive [[jim-farley|Jim Farley]] had said the union's proposals would push the company into bankruptcy and that there was "nothing going on" in negotiations.[2]

## 2023 electric-vehicle retreat

In its third-quarter 2023 earnings call Ford signalled that it would "adjust" production of its all-electric vehicles and delay roughly $12 billion in EV investments, citing softening demand for higher-priced premium electric models.[3] In December 2023 a memo to suppliers reported by Automotive News showed the company halving its 2024 output target for the [[ford-f-150-lightning|F-150 Lightning]], from about 3,200 trucks a week to roughly 1,600 built at the Rouge Electric Vehicle Center in Dearborn, Michigan.[3] A company spokeswoman said only that Ford would "continue to match production with customer demand."[3] Because Farley had presented the electric F-150 in May 2021 as "a test for adoption for electric vehicles," commentators including [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] treated the cut as Ford failing its own stated benchmark.[3]
