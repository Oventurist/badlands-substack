# Donald Wakeford

Donald Wakeford is a prosecutor in the Fulton County District Attorney's office who argued for the state in the Georgia racketeering case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|State of Georgia v. Trump, et al.]][1]

## First Amendment argument

At the March 28, 2024 pretrial hearing before Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], Wakeford countered Trump lead counsel [[steve-sadow|Steve Sadow]]'s claim that the indictment criminalized protected political speech.[1] Wakeford argued that Trump's statements were not shielded by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] because they were integral to criminal activity.[1] "It's not just that they were false. It's not that the defendant has been hauled into a courtroom because the prosecution doesn't like what he said," Wakeford said, adding that Trump remained free to express opinions and make legitimate protests.[1] What Trump was not allowed to do, he argued, was "employ his speech and his expression and his statements as part of a criminal conspiracy to violate Georgia's RICO statute, to impersonate public officers, to file false documents, to make false statements to the government."[1]

Wakeford also pointed out that similar arguments had been raised and rejected in the federal election case brought by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], citing U.S. District Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]]'s December ruling that "it is well established that the First Amendment does not protect speech that is used as an instrument of a crime."[1]
