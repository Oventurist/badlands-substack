# Los Angeles Department of Water and Power

The **Los Angeles Department of Water and Power** (LADWP) is the municipal utility that supplies water and electricity to the city of Los Angeles. It became the subject of intense scrutiny after the January 2025 [[palisades-fire|Palisades Fire]].[1]

## Leadership and compensation

On the orders of Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]], the city maxed out its budget to "attract private-sector talent," hiring [[janisse-quinones|Janisse Quiñones]] as chief executive in May 2024 on a $750,000 salary, almost double that of her predecessor.[1]

## Palisades Fire failures

Los Angeles Fire Department insiders alleged that LADWP left a reservoir near the Pacific Palisades disconnected and allowed fire hydrants to remain broken for months, resulting in firefighters running out of water during the Palisades Fire.[1] The reporting framed these as institutional failures known to utility leadership well before the fire began.[1]

## Litigation by Palisades homeowners

In late April 2025, two federal judges who lost their Pacific Palisades homes in the January firestorm joined hundreds of their neighbors in suing LADWP, claiming the utility failed to properly prepare for the wildfire and to respond when it broke out.[2] U.S. District Judge [[dean-pregerson|Dean Pregerson]], who sits on the Central District of California, and [[vijay-gandhi|Vijay "Jay" Gandhi]], a former magistrate judge on the same court, filed the lawsuit along with their families in Los Angeles Superior Court.[2] The suit alleges that the Palisades conflagration "was caused by both LADWP's water and power assets, specifically empty reservoirs and energized powerlines."[2]

The complaint cites Los Angeles Times reporting that LADWP's Santa Ynez Reservoir in the Palisades sat empty during the firefight, having been closed months earlier for repairs.[2] It further alleges that "despite dire warnings by the [[national-weather-service|National Weather Service]] of a 'Particularly Dangerous Condition-Red Flag Warning,' of 'critical fire weather' which had the potential for rapid fire spread and extreme fire behavior, the LADWP was unprepared for the Palisades fire."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued there was a not insignificant chance that suing the utility would lead back to the government, but expressed skepticism the case would reach discovery, predicting instead a settlement that would seal the secrets of public-private-partnership utility governance in private action.[2]
