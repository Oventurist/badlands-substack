# Bahrain

Bahrain is a Gulf state that became a target of Iranian action during the June 2026 escalation surrounding the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

The Guardian, cited in the Badlands Brief, reported that a residential building in Muharraq, Bahrain, was struck by an Iranian drone, according to Bahrain's Interior Ministry.[1] The strike came amid several days of renewed hostilities that followed an earlier memorandum of understanding intended to end the U.S.–[[iran|Iran]] conflict, with differing interpretations of the agreement over the Strait of Hormuz cited as a contributing factor.[1]

Bahrain had also been among the states represented at a meeting in the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] convened by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], alongside officials from the UAE and [[kuwait|Kuwait]].[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Iran subsequently struck targets specifically in Kuwait and Bahrain — the two countries he characterized as having colluded to subvert [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s peace framework — and argued that Iran appeared to be acting as an enforcer on Trump's behalf against opponents of his peace plan.[1]

## Russia ties and 2024 diplomacy

In May 2024 King [[hamad-bin-isa-al-khalifa|Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa]] travelled to Moscow for a two-day working visit, agreeing with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to step up trade relations and cooperate on resolving the crisis in the Middle East.[2] The two signed documents on pharmaceutical and medical cooperation, transport and transit, cultural exchange and environmental memoranda.[2] Putin noted that bilateral trade remained at an early stage while investment was stronger, with about 30 large projects worth roughly $500 million underway.[2] Putin invited Bahrain to the upcoming [[brics|BRICS]] summit; Bahrain had applied for membership in 2022 but was not among the states invited to join in August 2023.[2]

Bahrain is a small island state of about 1.5 million people in the Persian Gulf off the Saudi coast near Qatar, with a population split between Sunni and Shia and a slight Shia majority; the House of Al Khalifa has ruled since 1784.[2] During the 2011 Arab Spring the government was criticized for its response to protests, after which the Al Khalifa turned to Saudi Arabia and the UAE for support and protection.[2] Hamad hosted the 33rd Arab Summit in 2024, which issued a collective pact calling for Israel's total withdrawal from Palestine, and announced plans for an international peace conference aimed at fulfilling the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] and a two-state solution, as well as an intention to restore ties with [[iran|Iran]].[2] Badlands commentary treated these moves as indicators of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and of Sunni-Shia rapprochement.[2]
