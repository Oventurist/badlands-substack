# Harry Truman

Harry Truman was the President of the United States who signed the legislation that became the [[marshall-plan|Marshall Plan]].[1]

## Role in the Marshall Plan

On December 19, 1947, Truman sent [[congress|Congress]] a message that followed Secretary of State [[george-marshall|George Marshall]]'s ideas for providing economic aid to Europe.[1] Congress overwhelmingly passed the Economic Cooperation Act of 1948, and on April 3, 1948, Truman signed the Economic Recovery Act of 1948, which became known as the Marshall Plan.[1] Over the following four years Congress appropriated $13.3 billion for European recovery under the program.[1]

## Interpretation in the Badlands account

In [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s reading, the aid Truman authorized was not primarily humanitarian. He notes that the UK, France and West Germany — the countries he considers most controlled by the [[cabal|Cabal]] — received 55% of the funds while the Eastern Bloc received nothing, and argues the program's real functions were the consolidation of power, the creation of [[nato|NATO]], and the seeding of offshore [[eurodollar|Eurodollar]] accounts.[1]
