# Florence Pan

Florence Pan is a federal appellate judge in Washington, D.C., appointed by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], who served on the three-judge panel that heard [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s appeal asserting immunity from criminal charges arising out of his effort to overturn the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## Trump immunity appeal

At the January 9, 2024 oral argument, the panel — Pan, fellow Biden appointee [[michelle-childs|Michelle Childs]], and George H.W. Bush appointee [[karen-henderson|Karen Henderson]] — strongly suggested it would reject Trump's immunity claim, probing the implications of a rule under which a president could not be prosecuted absent prior impeachment and conviction by Congress.[1] Despite that inclination, the judges appeared divided over how to shape their decision, and their ruling was expected to prompt a further appeal to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] determining whether Trump's Washington, D.C. criminal trial would proceed in 2024.[1]

The charges at issue were brought by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], who alleged that Trump had tried to disenfranchise American voters and defraud the nation; Trump argued that the conduct charged consisted of official acts as president.[1]
