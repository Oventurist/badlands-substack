# Moon Jae-in

Moon Jae-in is a former president of South Korea whose published memoirs became a reference point in Badlands coverage of Korean diplomacy.[1]

According to Badlands reporting on those memoirs, Moon claimed that [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] had been "desperate" to rid [[north-korea|North Korea]] of its nuclear weapons since the historic 2018 summit with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Moon quoted Kim as saying he had a daughter and did not want her generation to live with the burden of nuclear weapons, and described Kim as having "sincerely explained his commitment to denuclearization."[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the account in arguing that "peace was always an option" and that the inducement capable of delivering it is the promise of prosperity.[1]

## "From the Periphery to the Centre"

Moon's memoir, released in May 2024 under the title "From the Periphery to the Centre," outlined in detail his interactions with the North Korean leader.[2] Moon, who led South Korea for five years from 2017, was instrumental in brokering two high-profile summits between [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] and then-United States president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] aimed at securing Pyongyang's denuclearization in exchange for sanctions relief.[2] After the second summit collapsed in 2019, diplomatic outreach was abandoned and inter-Korean relations fell to one of their worst points in years, as Kim doubled down on weapons production and drew closer to Moscow.[2]

In the book Moon wrote that "Kim said he would forsake nuclear weapons if there was a guarantee of regime survival," adding that he felt the young leader was "very honest."[2] He recorded Kim's stated reasoning: "I have a daughter and I do not wish her generation to live with nuclear weapons… Why would we continue to live in difficulty, under sanctions, with nuclear weapons if our security can be guaranteed?"[2]
