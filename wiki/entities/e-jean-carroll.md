# E. Jean Carroll

E. Jean Carroll is an American writer who brought civil litigation against [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], winning an $83.3 million defamation judgment that became one of the highest-profile civil cases connected to Trump's post-presidency years.[1]

## Judgment and appeal

In May 2026 a federal appeals court temporarily halted Carroll from collecting the $83.3 million defamation judgment awarded against Trump while his appeal moved forward. The ruling paused enforcement of the payout as appellate judges reviewed challenges tied to the underlying trial, the damages award, and the legal procedures surrounding the case.[1]

Trump has continued to deny Carroll's allegations, arguing that the verdicts were politically motivated and legally flawed, while Carroll's legal team has sought to preserve the judgment and defend the jury's findings.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the stay as good news for Trump and, should the verdict be overturned, for justice generally, calling the trial "one of the most egregious abuses of process and power that we've ever seen."[1] She argued Carroll's account never made sense "from the date to the dress," and criticized the presiding judge, Lewis Kaplan, as "a political hack whose story is still being written," urging that accountability for everyone involved is the first step toward restoring public trust.[1]

## DOJ perjury investigation (May 2026)

In May 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] reportedly opened a criminal investigation into Carroll, the former Elle magazine columnist whose accusations of sexual assault against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] produced a $5 million civil judgment.[2] CNN, citing multiple sources familiar with the matter, reported that investigators were examining whether Carroll committed perjury during the lawsuits, with the probe led by US Attorney for the Northern District of Illinois Andrew Boutros.[2] Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] recused himself from the investigation because of his prior role representing Trump in the Carroll case.[2]

## $83.3 Million Defamation Verdict (January 2024)

In late January 2024 a jury ordered former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to pay $83.3 million in Carroll's second civil defamation trial.[3] Carroll had accused Trump of sexually assaulting her in the mid-1990s in a dressing room at the Bergdorf Goodman department store in Manhattan; although the alleged attack occurred decades earlier, she sued under New York's Adult Survivors Act, which allows victims to bring civil damages claims beyond the ordinary statute of limitations.[3] Trump responded on Truth Social, calling the award "absolutely ridiculous" and raging that the legal system was "out of control."[3]

## "Price Is Right" appearance with Maddow (January 2024)

Following the $83.3 million award, Carroll appeared on [[msnbc|MSNBC]] on Monday, January 29, 2024, where host [[rachel-maddow|Rachel Maddow]] asked how she would spend "Trump's money" to help "women's rights."[6] Carroll replied in what [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] likened to a "Price Is Right" winner's routine: "First thing Rachel, you and I are gonna go shopping, we're gonna get completely new wardrobes, new shoes…Rachel what do you like, Penthouse? You want France? You wanna go fishing in France? It's yours Rachel."[6] Her lawyer interjected, "That's a joke," and Maddow replied that "if me fishing in France could do something for women's rights, I would take the hit."[6] The coverage noted that Carroll's case had been bankrolled by Trump's political opponents, that she could not recall basic facts of her own claim, that she had once described rape as "sexy," and that her "off the rails" tweets were excluded from the jury's view.[6]

## Amendment of the 2019 defamation suit (June 2023)


On Tuesday, June 13, 2023, a judge ruled that Carroll could amend her original defamation lawsuit against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to include additional comments he made about her during a [[cnn|CNN]] town hall.[4] The town hall took place one day after a Manhattan jury found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation and awarded Carroll $5 million; the jury did not find him liable for rape, and Trump denied the allegations and appealed.[4] At the town hall Trump called the case "fake news" and said he did not know Carroll.[4] The amended suit was her initial 2019 action — separate from the case that produced the $5 million award — which had been stalled while courts considered whether Trump was immune from liability for statements made while he was president.[4]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], argued that the proceeding illustrated the difference between law and justice, contending that in a truly lawful system only acts that violate rights and cause demonstrable harm are actionable, and that malice is not a valid basis for initiating a legal action.[4] He characterized Carroll as vexatious, pursuing damages absent material proof of harm, and framed the suit as "either a grift, an attempt to harm Trump, or both."[4] He added that reputational damage arising from truthful public speech is, as a matter of law, self-incurred, and closed by urging readers to treat the episode as preparation for redesigning American legal institutions after the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]].[4]

## May 2023 verdict

On May 9, 2023, a New York City jury in U.S. District Court in lower Manhattan found [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] liable for sexual abuse and defamation in Carroll's civil case, but declined to find him liable for rape as she had alleged.[5] After less than three hours of deliberation, the jury awarded Carroll $20,000 in punitive damages on a battery claim and roughly $5 million in compensatory damages for defamation, a result Trump denounced as "an affront to justice" while vowing to appeal.[5] Trump had long denied the allegation, which dated to the mid-1990s, and accused Carroll of using false claims to promote her book, telling The Hill in June 2019: "Number one, she's not my type. Number two, it never happened."[5] The verdict was covered by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and excerpted in the *[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]]*, where [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] situated it within a broader [[lawfare|lawfare]] campaign against a former head of state.[5]

## Outside funding disclosure (April 2023)

In April 2023 The National Pulse reported that Democratic mega-donor [[reid-hoffman|Reid Hoffman]], the billionaire founder of LinkedIn, was bankrolling Carroll's litigation against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[7] Carroll had previously denied receiving outside funding, but her attorney [[roberta-kaplan|Roberta Kaplan]] conceded to Trump lawyer [[alina-habba|Alina Habba]] that the former *Elle* columnist "remembered just recently that she had been told at some point about outside funding by a nonprofit and that it was irrelevant to her claim anyway."[7] Habba then wrote to Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]], who was overseeing the suit, raising significant concerns about "the plaintiff's bias and motive in commencing the litigation," and cited Hoffman's prior contribution of more than $600,000 to the legal defense fund of Bean LLC, better known as [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]].[7]
