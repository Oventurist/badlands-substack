# Jen Easterly

Jen Easterly is a career intelligence official who served as director of the U.S. [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] (CISA), the federal body responsible for the security of American critical infrastructure, including election systems.[1] She succeeded [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]], who was removed after the 2020 election, and was sworn in as CISA director in July 2021.[1]

## Background and career

Before joining CISA, Easterly served as special assistant to President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] from October 2013 to February 2016, and as the National Security Council's senior director for counterterrorism.[1] Following her White House service and prior to her CISA appointment, she was global head of cybersecurity for [[morgan-stanley|Morgan Stanley]].[1]

Badlands Media's commentary emphasizes that Easterly held the top cyber role at Morgan Stanley in January 2021, when a data breach at Guidehouse — a Morgan Stanley technology vendor storing corporate data — exposed sensitive client information including Social Security numbers.[1] In that role, the outlet argues, Easterly would have had oversight responsibility for the security of the vendor ecosystem in which the breach occurred.[1] Five months later she was appointed to head security for U.S. critical infrastructure, while Morgan Stanley went on to experience additional cybersecurity scandals.[1]

## Statements on election security

Roughly a month before Election Day 2024, Easterly told the [[associated-press|Associated Press]] that state and local election officials had made so much progress securing voting, ballot-counting and other election infrastructure that the system was more robust than it had ever been, and that as a result there was "no way" [[russia|Russia]], [[iran|Iran]] or any other foreign adversary would be able to alter the results.[1] "Malicious actors, even if they tried, could not have an impact at scale such that there would be a material effect on the outcome of the election," she said in a Wednesday interview.[1] Her reassurance came as intelligence officials warned of escalating foreign efforts to influence voters, deepen partisan divides and undermine faith in U.S. elections, and stood in contrast to doubts held by millions of Americans — especially Republicans — since the 2020 election.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that actual cyber experts do not make absolute statements about security.[1] The critique holds that threat levels evolve faster than threat-assessment capabilities, that bureaucracy, politics and corruption have displaced sober judgment, and that U.S. elections lack even acceptable password-management practices.[1] On that view, no credible security expert examining U.S. election system architecture, the vendor landscape and the testing ecosystem would conclude that there is "no way" results could be breached or altered.[1] The commentary situates Easterly's assurance in a lineage running back to Krebs, who produced the "safest and most secure election in history" formulation the Brief characterizes as a bold lie.[1]

## Rescinded West Point appointment (2025)

On Tuesday, July 29, 2025, the United States Military Academy announced that Easterly, an academy alumna, had been named the next Robert F. McDermott Distinguished Chair in [[west-point|West Point's]] Department of Social Sciences — a position created in 1943 to bring a leading scholar, practitioner or expert in fields such as economics, political science or international relations to the academy.[2] In a LinkedIn post that was later deleted, the academy welcomed the former CISA director after "an extraordinary career of service in the public and private sectors."[2]

The appointment was abruptly ended the following day. On Wednesday, July 30, Secretary of the Army [[dan-driscoll|Dan Driscoll]] announced in a memorandum and a post on X that the position would be rescinded and that a full review of the academy's hiring practices would be conducted.[2]
