# Scott Stapp

Scott Stapp is the frontman of the rock band Creed, cited by Badlands Media in September 2024 as an example of a mainstream, non-political cultural figure publicly turning against establishment power.[1]

## Texas concert monologue

During a performance in Texas in mid-September 2024, Stapp halted the concert to deliver an impassioned anti-establishment speech, urging Americans to stand up for their rights and fight back against the abuses of what he called the "powers that be."[1] He also advocated for the United States as a Christian nation, "built upon the Bible and the word of God."[1] Stapp named no political candidates or parties, appearing instead to speak against establishment power in general.[1]

He told the crowd: "They want us divided. They want us separate. They want us compartmentalized in our little niches, in our own little groups… to keep us distracted from holding them accountable."[1] Later in the monologue he urged Americans to "start reminding them what our Constitution says, we start reminding them what our Bill of Rights says. We remind them that we are a Constitutional Republic built upon the Bible and the word of God, and not a 'democracy.'"[1] The remarks were reported by [[breitbart|Breitbart]].[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] presented Stapp's remarks as evidence that "the awakening is accelerating and is accompanied by increasing courage," welcoming the emergence of mainstream voices with large platforms speaking bluntly about the regime.[1] Noting having seen Creed perform at Red Rocks in 1998 and that the band had always been loosely associated with conservative values, she described Stapp's open discussion of "them" wanting to divide the public as both refreshing and undeniably true.[1] She linked the moment to [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s framing of a "crisis cascade crescendo" accompanied by deep state panic patterns, arguing that the counter to engineered division is uniting across externally agitated divides and exposing the underlying psychological operations.[1] Her conclusion was that panic would continue as more ordinary citizens of every stripe stood up and spoke out.[1]
