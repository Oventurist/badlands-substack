# Venezuela

Venezuela is a South American state that in July 2026 announced its withdrawal from the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]], a decision the United States welcomed and used as a rallying point to urge other countries to follow suit.[1]

## ICC withdrawal

The ICC has been examining the Venezuelan government since [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] took office in 2014.[1] After receiving formal referrals in 2018 from governments Badlands characterized as NeoCon-aligned, the court opened formal investigations that continue to this day, with additional referrals as recently as 2025 — including one from Ecuador, the country Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] has identified as the new home base of the cocaine cartels.[1] The court denied all Venezuelan requests to end its inquiries, which Badlands read as evidence that it intended to indict Maduro and probably interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], and to use its clout to bring Venezuela under UN control.[1]

The withdrawal was announced by Rodríguez after a group of Zionist congressmen traveled to Caracas to meet with her over the weekend preceding July 27, 2026, prompting allegations that she was acting on behalf of [[israel|Israel]].[1] The [[state-department|State Department]] subsequently welcomed the withdrawal and called on other countries to do the same, in a statement reported by Anadolu Agency.[1]

## The "Venezuela model" (2026)

By April 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was invoking Venezuela as the template for U.S. operations in [[iran|Iran]], writing on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that "just like the results in Venezuela, which the media doesn't like talking about, the results in Iran will be amazing," and that the Iranian campaign was "being perfectly executed, on the scale of Venezuela, just a bigger, more complex operation."[2] The Atlantic reported that Venezuela was going well, while [[cnn|CNN]] reported that no genuine regime change had occurred in Iran.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the key tell in Trump's statement was the Venezuela reference rather than the regime-change rhetoric, framing Caracas as proof of the [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] thesis: continuity beneath the optics, sovereign control preserved, and oil flowing on terms serving a multipolar reordering rather than the old scarcity cartel.[2] He cited the U.S. [[state-department|State Department]]'s reference to "political reconciliation" following the re-opening of the U.S. Embassy in Caracas as evidence of that codification.[2]

## The "Venezuela Model" in Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the American operation in Venezuela as the template for later actions, coining the label [[venezuela-model|Venezuela Model]].[3] In his account the operation removed the visible lightning rod in the form of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], cut off cartel pipelines said to feed globalist proxies, installed continuity with a sovereignty-aligned hand at the wheel, and moved straight into energy cooperation and stability narratives — without occupation or ground troops.[3] He contrasted this with the NGO-backed regime change scenario centered on [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] that he said globalist actors had hoped for.[3] In March 2026 the model was invoked as the framework President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was following in [[iran|Iran]].[3]

## Normalization with Washington (February 2026)

By February 2026 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] was publicly celebrating its relationship with Caracas. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said relations between Venezuela and the United States had been "to put it mildly, extraordinary," that Washington was "dealing very well with President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodriguez]], and her Representatives," and that oil was starting to flow with "large amounts of money, unseen for many years" soon to help the Venezuelan people.[4] He credited Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and other US representatives, while warning that no one lacking [[state-department|State Department]] approval — specifically naming businessman [[harry-sargeant-iii|Harry Sargeant III]] — was authorized to act on behalf of the United States.[4]

Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] toured Venezuelan oil fields the same week to "explore ways to boost oil production, update infrastructure, and unlock the country's enormous economic potential," arguing that stronger commerce would benefit Americans and Venezuelans alike.[4] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted the euphoric rhetoric with earlier calls for war, remarking that "all Venezuela needed was to get discombobulated to be set straight" and noting the silence of pro-war commentators.[4] The same week, the United States repositioned the [[uss-gerald-r-ford|USS Gerald R. Ford]] carrier strike group away from Venezuela toward the Middle East.[4]

## Institutional reshuffle and amnesty (February 2026)

Later in February 2026, Attorney General [[tarek-william-saab|Tarek William Saab]] resigned after nearly nine years and was immediately appointed acting Ombudsman by the ruling party-dominated [[national-assembly-of-venezuela|National Assembly]], with [[larry-devoe|Larry Devoe]] of the National Council of Human Rights named acting Attorney General.[5] The legislature formed a 13-member commission with a 30-day deadline to define the permanent heads of both offices, and human rights organizations condemned Saab's interim appointment as a setback for justice reform.[5] In the same period the Assembly approved a limited [[venezuela-amnesty-law|amnesty law]] after a second debate headed by [[jorge-rodriguez|Jorge Rodríguez]], brother of President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], reportedly freeing hundreds of political protestors while excluding homicide and other violent crimes.[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned the logic of a supposedly overthrown regime's succession plan being followed and its next-in-line members running the new government, tying Jorge Rodríguez to [[ralph-pezzullo|Ralph Pezzullo]]'s book *Stolen Elections* and asking whether the public "rewrite" of the Venezuela election fraud story was meant to get ahead of — and undermine — the country's restoration.[5] She also asked where opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] had gone.[5]

## Joint operation against Tren de Aragua (June 2026)

In June 2026 the Venezuelan government confirmed its participation in a U.S. military operation that killed Héctor Rusthenford Guerrero Flores, known as "Niño Guerrero," the leader of [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]], saying clashes during the mission in Bolívar state resulted in his death.[6] Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] described the strike on a Tren de Aragua compound in Venezuela as evidence of cooperation between the United States and Venezuela against what he called narco-terrorist groups.[6]

Tren de Aragua originated in a prison in Venezuela's Aragua state and expanded beyond the country's borders during the mass migration of Venezuelans driven by the nation's economic crisis, with authorities in several Latin American countries linking the group to violent criminal activity across the region.[6] Badlands commentary argued the gang had never worked for President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] and was instead created by the [[cia|CIA]] to destabilize his government; it also cited Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s claim that his predecessor Iván Duque had collaborated with the American Deep State and intended to use Colombia as a staging ground for an invasion of Venezuela.[6]

## Post-capture reforms and annexation talk (May 2026)

Following the January 2026 U.S. military operation that captured Venezuelan leader [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], President [[donald-trump|Trump]] floated the idea of annexing Venezuela as America's 51st state, citing its vast oil reserves — valued at around $40 trillion — and claiming strong local support with the remark "Venezuela loves Trump."[7] Post-capture reforms boosted oil output to over 1 million barrels per day, with new [[chevron|Chevron]] deals and a $2 billion U.S. oil-supply agreement in place, and an interim government led by Maduro's former vice president was installed.[7]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the changes as measurable "renormalization" achieved only four months into Trump's 2026 cycle, arguing the speed of the transformation signaled intent rather than chaos, and read the 51st-state proposal as both a troll and a narrative precision strike underscoring who holds leverage in the hemisphere.[7]

## 2026 opening to Chinese and Indian oil investment

Around January 31-February 1, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters aboard Air Force One that the United States welcomes Chinese and Indian investment in Venezuela's oil sector, stating that [[china|China]] "is welcome" to make deals in the country and that [[india|India]] is "coming in" to buy Venezuelan oil.[8] Venezuela holds the world's largest proven oil reserves, though production has been constrained for years by sanctions and political instability; Trump characterized the approach as permitting third-country investment and purchases as part of a broader energy realignment.[8] The opening was tied to the [[2026-us-india-tariff-agreement|2026 US-India tariff agreement]], under which India agreed to shift crude purchases away from Russia and toward US and Venezuelan supply.[8]

## Earthquake relief and the Machado dispute (July 2026)

After deadly [[2026-venezuela-earthquakes|earthquakes]] struck the country, the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration criticised opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]]'s efforts to return, with a senior [[white-house|White House]] official calling the move "grotesque political opportunism."[9] Some reports indicated US officials support her eventual return but believe doing so amid the humanitarian crisis could undermine relief operations and destabilise the country; the administration said its immediate priority was earthquake response, including ongoing US search-and-rescue operations and humanitarian assistance.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] welcomed the shift in tone, describing Machado as "literally a creation of [[usaid|USAID]]," dispatched by the Bush administration to topple [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]], and arguing that honest official language shifts the [[overton-window|Overton Window]].[9]

## Travel advisory and the "super soldiers" narrative (January 2026)

In January 2026 the [[state-department|State Department]] maintained its Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisory for Venezuela and reiterated that US citizens and lawful permanent residents in the country should depart immediately, citing security risks and the limited ability of the US government to provide emergency assistance.[10] No official confirmation was issued regarding claims of "energy weapons and super soldiers" described in a viral video amplified by White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] over the same weekend.[10]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the circulating account of an American raid on [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] as a narrative deployment whose operational truth was secondary to its effect.[10] The account described elite Delta operators moving through Caracas at night, swarms of autonomous drones silencing radar arrays, non-lethal sonic emitters that ruptured sinuses and induced immediate surrender, and precision fire dropping hundreds of regime loyalists while leaving twenty American operators untouched, corroborated by a surviving Venezuelan guard whose trembling interviews spread across platforms.[10]

Whether read through the Bicameral Mindset — as layered narrative with minimal kinetic actuals — or as fragments of operational truth bleeding through, Burning Bright argued the translation was identical: the United States can reach into the heart of a regime, extract or eliminate its head, and leave its security apparatus shattered while projecting zero vulnerability.[10] In this framing the story's teeth, once bared, changed the calculus of every observer and would-be actor, and supplied the pressure behind simultaneous US moves on [[cuba|Cuba]] and [[greenland|Greenland]].[10]

## Senate war powers resolution blocked (January 2026)

On January 14, 2026 the [[united-states-senate|US Senate]] voted 51–50 to block a bipartisan [[venezuela-war-powers-resolution|war powers resolution]] that would have required President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to obtain congressional approval before further military action in or against Venezuela, with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] casting the tie-breaking vote.[11] The measure, led by Sen. [[tim-kaine|Tim Kaine]], had advanced on January 8, 2026 by a 52–47 procedural vote with support from several Republicans, but Senators [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] and [[todd-young|Todd Young]] reversed course on final passage; the resolution did not become law and Democrats decried the outcome.[11] Badlands analyst [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] separately described the months of teased war with Venezuela as a "rugpull," part of a pattern in which Trump escalates the narrative of a kinetic conflict without delivering one.[11]

## Caribbean deployment and the drug war (August 2025)

In August 2025 the United States sent air and naval forces to the southern Caribbean Sea in what the [[white-house|White House]] described as part of a broader campaign against drug trafficking groups operating in Latin America, particularly those linked to Venezuela.[12] Bolivian President [[luis-arce|Luis Arce]] criticized the deployment as an attempt to control the region for its natural resources rather than a genuine effort against narcotics trafficking.[12] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued instead that the tension between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] — including a $50 million [[state-department|State Department]] bounty on Maduro — was staged conflict giving Maduro cover while both men pursued the cartels and corrupt commodity-trading executives.[12]


## US military pressure (September 2025)

By mid-September 2025 Washington had deployed three warships and roughly 4,000 troops to the Southern Caribbean, describing the mission as targeting drug cartels.[13] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] declined to rule out striking mainland Venezuela, telling reporters in Morristown, New Jersey, "We'll see what happens," and accusing the country of "sending us their gang members, their drug dealers, and drugs."[13]

President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] insisted Venezuela had already dismantled its major trafficking networks and vanquished prominent gangs, telling RT that Washington was using the anti-narcotics campaign as a pretext for gaining access to the country's vast natural resources as part of a broader "war plan."[13] Relations had been tense for years, with Washington refusing to recognize Maduro's 2018 reelection, imposing sweeping sanctions and backing the opposition.[13] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Trump and Maduro were in fact cooperating against a transnational corporate cartel including firms such as [[glencore|Glencore]], and were pivoting to dismantle drug cartels whose origins he traced to the [[united-fruit-company|United Fruit Company]].[13]

## US escalation and opposition support for strikes (November 2025)

By November 2025 the United States had assembled a substantial military presence off Venezuela's coast, with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] having deployed a naval armada to the western Caribbean and US forces striking alleged drug-smuggling vessels since September; Washington accused President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] of cartel ties and labeled him a "narcoterrorist."[14] Media reports indicated the naval presence was expanding, with analysts suggesting the mission could extend beyond counter-narcotics, while Trump denied planning direct strikes inside the country but reportedly reviewed a list of potential targets.[14]

Opposition figure and 2025 Nobel Peace Prize laureate [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] said the buildup could help bring about regime change and that "the escalation that's taking place is the only way to force Maduro to understand that it's time to go."[14] Badlands commentary criticized her claim that Hamas was operating inside Venezuela as a pretext for bombing and framed her elevation as a regime-change effort linked to the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]].[14]

## Oil tanker blockade and great-power backing (December 2025)

[[china|China]] and [[russia|Russia]] expressed support for Venezuela in December 2025 as it confronted a US blockade of sanctioned oil tankers, while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] continued to ramp up his pressure campaign on President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolas Maduro]].[15] Amid reports of slowing activity at Venezuelan ports, Trump again called for Maduro to leave power and reiterated that the United States would keep or sell the oil it had seized off the Venezuelan coast in recent weeks.[15] After the [[2025-venezuela-oil-tanker-blockade|blockade]] of all sanctioned tankers entering and leaving Venezuela was announced, tanker loading at the country's ports slowed, with most ships moving oil cargoes only between domestic ports.[15] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] asked whether the US and Russian militaries were about to repeat in Venezuela the kind of pincer movement he believed they had executed against ISIS in Syria in December 2024.[15]

## Terrorist designation and prospective Trump–Maduro talks (November 2025)

In late November 2025 the U.S. Treasury designated the [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel of the Suns]] — a purported criminal network alleged to operate within Venezuela's security services — as a foreign terrorist organization, alleging that President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolas Maduro]] heads the group.[16] Axios nonetheless reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] planned to speak directly with Maduro, a turn in his "gunboat diplomacy" suggesting missile strikes or ground operations were unlikely in the near term, even as U.S. forces continued striking boats alleged to be shipping drugs.[16] Separately, Trump appeared on Truth Social to endorse the discredited theory that Venezuela's leadership controls electronic voting software worldwide and caused his 2020 defeat, a claim ruled false by a judge in 2023 and central to the [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting]] defamation suit that Fox News settled for $787 million.[16]

## Rubio warning over Essequibo (March 2025)

In late March 2025, Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] warned in Georgetown that there would be "consequences" if Venezuela used forceful actions to assert claims to resource-rich territories controlled by neighboring [[guyana|Guyana]], calling Caracas a "narco-trafficking regime" making "illegitimate territorial claims."[17] Venezuela has intensified its claims to the [[essequibo-dispute|Essequibo region]], which makes up about two-thirds of Guyana and contains oil-rich offshore waters where [[exxonmobil|ExxonMobil]] leads production.[17] Rubio pointed to the size and power of the US Navy without specifying what action the Trump administration might take.[17]

Badlands commentary connected the standoff to high-profile arrests of oil executives worldwide in corruption probes involving Venezuela, probes launched by [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in March 2017 after he received a "laptop from hell" from the scorned ex-wife of one of his oil executives, and speculated that Maduro and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might be cooperating to squeeze the players involved in Guyana and Venezuelan oil graft.[17]

## December 2025 blockade and "oil rights" dispute

In mid-December 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced a naval blockade on sanctioned tankers carrying Venezuelan crude and branded the government in Caracas a "foreign terrorist organization," accusing it of stealing US oil and other assets.[18] Speaking to reporters on December 17, Trump said "They took our oil rights. We had a lot of oil there... They threw our companies out, and we want it back," blaming earlier presidents for allowing Caracas to seize assets once held by American companies.[18]

President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolas Maduro]] responded in a televised address the same day, accusing Washington of seeking to overthrow his government, install a "puppet government" that "would not last even 47 hours," and turn the country into a colony to seize its natural wealth.[18] [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] reported that members of [[congress|Congress]] had been briefed that a war with Venezuela was imminent and would be announced in Trump's December 17 primetime address, which in the event made no mention of the country.[18]

Badlands analysis stressed that the ownership picture behind the "oil rights" rhetoric is more tangled than the official story: [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] has held no Venezuelan oil rights since 2007 and instead operates in neighboring [[guyana|Guyana]], which shares territorial waters with Venezuela; the Venezuelan rights sit with [[citgo|Citgo]], which acquired [[hess-corporation|Hess]] in July 2025 and is itself owned by the state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] — until a US court order of December 2, 2025 forced a sale to satisfy debts.[18] Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] noted that the assets Trump described as stolen were oil company projects nationalized by Venezuela two decades earlier.[18]

## B-1 bomber flights and strike authority debate (October 2025)

On Thursday, October 23, 2025, the United States flew Air Force [[b-1-lancer|B-1 bombers]] near Venezuela, stepping up pressure on President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] only days after other American warplanes carried out an "attack demonstration" near the country.[19] Two B-1 Lancers took off from Dyess Air Force Base in Texas and flew near Venezuela while remaining in international airspace, according to a U.S. official and flight-tracking data; the aircraft were not moved to bases in the region because they can reach anywhere in the Caribbean from the continental United States.[19] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] denied the report, saying: "No, that's not accurate. But we're not happy with Venezuela."[19]

The same day, Trump insisted he could continue launching strikes against alleged drug traffickers abroad without [[congress|Congress]] first passing a declaration of war, saying "I'm not going to necessarily ask for a declaration of war" and that "we're just going to kill people that are bringing drugs into our country."[19] He suggested his administration would soon begin targeting those deemed cartel members inside countries like Venezuela, in addition to continuing strikes on alleged drug boats in international waters, while saying he would notify Congress before any operations on land.[19]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that the Venezuela war story was going badly for the warmongers and for those who believe it is America's business to dictate who leads a faraway country.[19] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump's frequent posting was evidence against imminent escalation rather than for it, writing that whenever readers worry about "fake WW3 or fake nukes," they should look at Trump's timeline: "He feels fine."[19] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said the warning to drug runners was adequate and asked what the penalty should be for those who run policy and legislation on behalf of the cartels.[19]

## 2024 election crisis

Venezuela's July 2024 presidential election produced an immediate legitimacy crisis: the government-controlled electoral authority proclaimed [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] the winner, while opposition candidate [[edmundo-gonzalez-urrutia|Edmundo González Urrutia]] and [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] said they could prove their own victory using 73.2% of the polling-station tallies.[20] Thousands of protesters took to the streets; at least nine Latin American countries withheld recognition of Maduro's third term; and the [[white-house|White House]] said it was reviewing available data and saw "signs" the results did not reflect the will of the people.[20] The national coordinator of [[voluntad-popular|Voluntad Popular]], [[freddy-superlano|Freddy Superlano]], was detained, while the top military leadership in Caracas publicly reaffirmed its loyalty to Maduro.[20]

Newsweek framed the disputed result as trouble for U.S. Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], predicting a surge in migration from Venezuelans fleeing the regime and renewed scrutiny of Biden-Harris policy toward a country that had been an important oil supplier to the United States before sanctions as well as a source of rare earth elements.[20]
