# Cantor Fitzgerald

Cantor Fitzgerald is the financial services firm formerly led by Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] and now run by his sons.[1]

## Tariff refund controversy

After the [[supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-2026|Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling]] striking down tariffs imposed under the [[international-emergency-economic-powers-act|International Emergency Economic Powers Act]], online and media claims circulated that Cantor Fitzgerald had profited from the decision.[1] The firm rejected those claims according to multiple news outlets.[1]

The allegations traced to a July 2025 WIRED report that a Cantor Fitzgerald subsidiary had developed a way for clients to buy rights to potential tariff refunds at steep discounts, citing an internal letter indicating the firm was willing to trade such rights at roughly 20 to 30 percent of duties paid and had "already put a trade through" on about $10 million of positions.[1]

Cantor Fitzgerald flatly denied executing any tariff-linked transactions or taking risk on the legality of tariffs, calling the suggestions false.[1] A spokesperson told Semafor that the product had been discussed internally but the company ultimately decided not to offer it, citing political sensitivities.[1] The episode fed a wider debate over potential conflicts of interest involving Lutnick, who stepped down from the firm to become Commerce secretary, and prompted questions from lawmakers including Senators Elizabeth Warren and Ron Wyden.[1]

## Gold Protected Bitcoin Fund

On Monday, September 8, 2025, Cantor Fitzgerald debuted the Cantor Fitzgerald Gold Protected Bitcoin Fund, a vehicle designed to give investors exposure to [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]]'s upside while using gold to provide downside protection.[2] The fund had been announced in May at the Bitcoin 2025 conference in Las Vegas and was aimed at investors wary of Bitcoin's volatility.[2] Cantor said the structure "minimizes the risk of short-term volatility and reduces the impact of correlation spikes while continuing to benefit from the long-term upside trend of Bitcoin."[2] [[bill-ferri|Bill Ferri]], Global Head of Cantor Fitzgerald Asset Management, described it as a five-year strategy in which "gold provides a safety net that historically performs well when markets decline," adding that "with risk assets at or near all-time highs, timing and protection matter."[2]

Badlands host [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the firm founded and long led by Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] had launched what he called a "signal-soaked Trojan Horse into the financial world," and asked whether gold-insured Bitcoin could serve as a template for the "Global Economic Reordering" referenced by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]], concluding that central banks were "on notice."[2]
