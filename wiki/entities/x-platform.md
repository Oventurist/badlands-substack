# X (formerly Twitter)

X, the social media platform formerly known as Twitter, was purchased by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] with the intention of operating it as a free speech platform of the kind it had been at its founding.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) Badlands Media treats X, alongside [[rumble|Rumble]], as one of the principal targets of contemporary advertiser-pressure campaigns.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Before and after the acquisition

Under prior ownership, the article notes, pressure groups such as the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] and [[media-matters|Media Matters]] could obtain content removals from Twitter, Facebook and Google with little more than a well-crafted and intimidating letter.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The platform of that era also carried bigoted and mean-spirited commentary, though it was typically directed at Christians or supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The author accepts that a genuinely open platform will surface opinions users find reprehensible, but argues that the alternative documented by the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] — federal government and intelligence agency collusion with big tech to determine permissible public conversation — is far more dangerous.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## The advertising controversy

In November 2023 Media Matters published a report claiming that advertisements for Apple, Bravo, IBM, Oracle and Xfinity had been placed next to pro-Nazi content on X, and that similar placements had appeared alongside pro-Hitler, Holocaust-denial, white nationalist, pro-violence and neo-Nazi accounts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The report also quoted CEO Linda Yaccarino's assurance that brands were "protected from the risk of being next to" potentially toxic content.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

Badlands Media argues the screenshots were manufactured: the offending posts had negligible view counts, and researchers reportedly refreshed the feeds of these fringe accounts repeatedly until a major brand's advertisement appeared, raising the question of how many of the few views recorded came from Media Matters staff themselves.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The dispute produced Musk's "thermonuclear lawsuit."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Musk's "collective consciousness" vision (2025)

Speaking to the investor and entrepreneur Nikhil Kamath on the People of WTF podcast in late November 2025, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] laid out a vision for X that went well beyond microblogging, describing the platform formerly known as Twitter as an experiment in global communication, commerce and consciousness.[2] "We're building a global town square," Musk said, framing X not merely as a social network but as the digital foundation of humanity's collective mind.[2]
The ambition was described as both philosophical and practical: in a world of fragmentation and noise, Musk wants X to become a place where language barriers dissolve, conversations deepen, and the boundaries between media, messaging and money disappear.[2]

Badlands commentary responded to the framing on metaphysical grounds, with Chris Paul questioning the premise that the purpose of soul and consciousness is to maximize pleasure and minimize pain temporarily before vanishing, and asking what system would generate such an idea and desire the world it necessitates.[2]
