# Fani Willis

Fani Willis is the Fulton County, Georgia district attorney who obtained a sweeping state indictment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and co-defendants over efforts to contest the 2020 presidential election result in Georgia.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## The Fulton County indictment

Willis's indictment contains 41 counts, and according to the article only a handful of those counts are not built on alleged "false statements" or on subsequent acts said to have arisen from those false statements — including the solicitation-of-violation-of-oath counts, which depend on the prior existence of the alleged falsehoods.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

Count 1 is a RICO charge that enumerates 161 separate "acts" said to demonstrate an organized criminal enterprise.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The article characterizes a significant portion of those acts as ordinary conduct — tweets, or the sorts of conversations officials would naturally have if they were investigating [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud]] — arguing that what renders them criminal in the indictment's telling is solely the presumption that they were undertaken fraudulently by people intending to overturn the election.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Legal critique

Applying the elements-of-the-cause framework the article borrows from defamation law, the author argues that being wrong is not a crime: only knowing dishonesty would satisfy the charges.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) Because Trump has, in the author's assessment, never contradicted himself on the 2020 election, proving him a liar will be difficult.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Speculation about motive

Together with federal special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], Willis is the subject of the article's central question: whether these prosecutions were "crafted to give Trump precisely the ammo he needs" by creating a courtroom in which suppressed election-fraud evidence must finally be heard.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The author frames this as speculation rather than assertion, noting that the indictments also shift public optics in Trump's favor by making a billionaire into a mugshot-bearing target of what supporters see as a corrupt system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Biden Justice Department grants

In February 2026, Willis returned to the headlines after reports confirmed that the Biden [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] had awarded approximately $18 million in federal grants to her office during the period in which she was prosecuting former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Georgia.[2] The funds were distributed through DOJ grant programs related to violence reduction and victim services, and public records did not show that the grants were specifically earmarked for the Trump prosecution.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] speculated that the regime "had a good thing going" with a friendly jurisdiction keen to pursue frivolous claims, keep the narrative going, and "make the process the punishment," and argued that the arrangement unravelled only when Willis hired her boyfriend, "abandoned discretion," and drew a national spotlight onto the operation.[2]

## Aftermath of the Fulton County case

Badlands has described [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|State of Georgia v. Trump, et al.]] as having been manufactured by Willis together with her lover, special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]]; the case was dismissed on November 25, 2025.[3] Its record continued to generate disclosures after dismissal, most notably the unsealed special grand jury testimony of former Senator [[david-perdue|David Perdue]], taken by Wade, alleging that Governor [[brian-kemp|Brian Kemp]] had shut down Georgia's 2020 election investigation.[3]

## One-year anniversary of the indictment (2024)

In August 2024, a year after a Fulton County grand jury accused [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and others of illegally trying to overturn the 2020 presidential election in Georgia, the [[associated-press|Associated Press]] reported that the case had stalled with no chance of reaching trial before the end of that year.[4] The AP recounted that Willis had secured the indictment using Georgia's anti-racketeering law to allege that Trump and 18 others participated in a wide-ranging scheme to subvert the will of the state's voters, in what was the fourth and most sprawling of the criminal cases against the former president.[4] Willis's team notched early victories, but explosive allegations raised by one of Trump's co-defendants earlier that year caused delays that observers said could derail the prosecution outright.[4]

Trump and other defendants appealed Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]]'s ruling, and the appeals court barred McAfee from taking any further action in the case while the appeal was pending.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the AP was "lamenting the impending death of Fani's RICO" and that the absence of actual news — the anniversary itself being the hook — proved the prosecution was stalled indefinitely.[4] She argued the broader [[lawfare]] campaign against Trump had failed spectacularly, with wins backfiring by making Trump more popular and "walls closing in" cases dismissed or frozen, to the point that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] had to shift resources to defend against Trump's $100 million tortious abuse suit over the classified documents raid.[4] Those who took plea deals in the Fulton County case, she added, "must be drowning in regret."[4]

## Disqualification motion and Wade's resignation (March 2024)

On March 15, 2024, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] declined to disqualify Willis outright from the Fulton County election interference case but ruled that either she or special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] had to step aside because of a "significant appearance of impropriety" arising from their romantic relationship.[5] Wade resigned hours later, which under the terms of the order allowed Willis and the remainder of her team to remain on the case.[5] In his resignation letter Wade said he was stepping down "in the interest of democracy," noting that the court had found the defendants failed to prove an actual conflict of interest.[5] Willis, accepting the resignation, praised Wade "for the professionalism and dignity you have shown over the last 865 days" and said she would always remind everyone that he had been brave enough to take on the prosecution.[5]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued the ruling left Willis in the most difficult of positions after McAfee had already severed six charges tied to the [[brad-raffensperger|Raffensperger]] phone call, gutting a [[election-fraud-rico|RICO]] case that was already thinly woven; she could either proceed with a crippled prosecution or start over with a new grand jury and indictment.[5] He added that the evidentiary record built during the disqualification hearings — testimony pointing toward conflict of interest, unethical behavior and possible perjury — gave an ethics board, the Supreme Court of Georgia and the State Bar serious material to examine.[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the order as a genuine setback rather than the total victory many commentators expected, praising McAfee for being "the first court in a while that acknowledged public trust at all."[5] She reported that Willis had struggled to find a prosecutor from the case's inception, was roughly $700,000 out of pocket, and might have to return to Fulton County for more funding at a time when she faced a Georgia Senate inquiry into her use of public funds — an inquiry premised on the observation that Willis had secured funding to hire 55 employees and then, two weeks later, hired her boyfriend and seemingly no one else.[5] Willis also faced a state ethics inquiry, a Georgia Bar complaint and a congressional inquiry, forums McAfee's order itself identified as the appropriate fact finders for the corruption allegations.[5] Ashe added that she did not want the case dismissed before the evidence was heard, since defendants such as [[harrison-floyd|Harrison Floyd]] were pursuing an affirmative defense premised on proving that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won the 2020 election, making the televised trial what she called the best opportunity to litigate [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud]].[5]

## January 2024 Fulton County cyberattack

In late January 2024 a [[2024-fulton-county-cyberattack|cybersecurity incident]] struck government systems in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia, taking down desktop phones, the county intranet and server-dependent devices across all departments, including Willis's district attorney's office as she prosecuted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on election interference charges.[6] Board of Commissioners Chairman [[robb-pitts|Robb Pitts]] confirmed the outage affected the county's phones, courts and tax systems and that law enforcement was investigating, with no time frame for restoration and no known transfer of sensitive information at that point.[6]

## Appellate review of the disqualification bid (May 2024)

On 8 May 2024 the [[georgia-court-of-appeals|Georgia Court of Appeals]] agreed to hear [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s bid to disqualify Willis from the election interference case, following Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]]'s March refusal to remove her after revelations of her romantic relationship with a member of her legal team.[7] No trial date had been set in the case at that point.[7] Trump attorney Steve Sadow said the former president looked forward to telling the appeals court "why the case should be dismissed and Fulton County DA Willis should be disqualified for her misconduct in this unjustified, unwarranted political persecution."[7] Commentators described the appellate court's decision to take up the matter as the latest setback for Willis's high-profile prosecution of the presumptive Republican presidential nominee.[7]

## Court order on the Wade misconduct allegations (January 2024)

In January 2024, Judge [[scot-mcafee|Scot McAfee]] ordered Willis to answer in writing, by February 2, accusations that she had hired her romantic partner [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] as a top prosecutor on the Georgia case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and scheduled a hearing on the matter for February 15.[8]

The accusation was first made in a court filing by attorney and Trump co-defendant [[mike-roman|Mike Roman]], who argued that Wade's involvement rendered the indictment "fatally defective" and asked McAfee both to dismiss the charges and to bar the Fulton County District Attorney's office from further involvement in the case.[8] The 127-page filing described Wade as a private attorney in the midst of a divorce who had been hired without proper approval and who had "little to no experience trying felony cases, much less complex RICO actions."[8]

According to the filing, Wade collected nearly $700,000 from Fulton County taxpayers, funds with which he allegedly took Willis on lavish vacations, and billed taxpayers $2,000 for time spent speaking with the Biden White House about prosecuting the sitting president's political opponent.[8]

## February 2024 disqualification hearing

In late February 2024, Willis and Special Prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] were embroiled in a fight with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s legal team over Wade's cellphone records, which Trump's attorneys argued could establish when their romantic relationship began.[9] Both Willis and Wade had testified that they "never" spent the night together at Willis' condominium in Hapeville.[9] On Monday, February 26, Trump's team filed a reply in Fulton County Superior Court to the state's response, stating that the investigator hired to analyze the records would offer no "opinion testimony" and would only summarize data produced by AT&T under a "lawfully issued subpoena" along with information on cell tower locations; the reply cited cases where similar evidence had been used, including in Georgia, and noted that the state had not addressed the analysis of two specific dates.[9] Acceptance of the new evidence was expected to extend the evidentiary hearing on [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]]'s motion to disqualify her.[9]

That same evening, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] ruled that certain communications between Wade and his former law partner [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] were not protected by attorney-client privilege, clearing Bradley to testify about the relationship; a spokesman for Willis said the DA's office could not comment except in court.[9] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the new evidence and Bradley's testimony appeared to prove that Willis and Wade had perjured themselves on the stand and in their representations to the court, describing perjury by the top prosecutor in the jurisdictional county and the lead prosecutor in the case as a "career-ending, international scandal."[9] Summations in Roman's motion were scheduled for Friday, March 1, 2024.[9]

## Alleged collusion with the January 6 Committee

Among the preliminary findings released in March 2024 by the [[house-administration-subcommittee-on-oversight|House Administration Subcommittee on Oversight]] was the conclusion that the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] colluded with Willis on her prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[10] The finding appeared alongside allegations that the committee hid transcribed interviews and withheld evidence used in its prime-time hearings.[10]

## Georgia Senate Investigative Panel (January 2024)

On Friday, January 26, 2024, the Republican-led Georgia Senate approved a resolution establishing a special committee to investigate Willis, the Fulton County District Attorney who had brought an election interference case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and eighteen co-defendants the previous year.[12] The 30-19 vote fell along party lines.[12] The resolution cited misconduct allegations that "include the ongoing expenditure of significant public funds for the purpose of hiring a special assistant district attorney with whom District Attorney Willis had, and may yet have, an ongoing romantic relationship."[12] Under the resolution the panel would consist of no more than nine members, including at least three Democrats.[12]

## Ellis plea, October 2023

In October 2023 [[jenna-ellis|Jenna Ellis]], a former Trump attorney arrested in the RICO case brought by Willis's office, pleaded guilty in Georgia state court to one count of aiding and abetting false statements in writing, admitting to intentionally interfering in the state's election process.[13] Like other former Trump counsel who resolved their cases, Ellis avoided jail, receiving five years of probation, $5,000 in restitution to the Georgia Secretary of State, 100 hours of community service, an obligation to write an apology letter to Georgia voters, and a requirement to testify truthfully in future hearings.[13]

## Appellate disqualification (December 2024)

On December 19, 2024, the [[georgia-court-of-appeals|Georgia Court of Appeals]] disqualified Willis from prosecuting the case against President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his alleged co-conspirators over their efforts to overturn the 2020 election.[14] The long-awaited decision, in a state criminal case already on hold, raised questions about whether the prosecution could move forward: the court found that Willis's office could not prosecute the case, so a new special prosecutor would have to be appointed for it to continue.[14]

The appeals court held that a "significant appearance of impropriety" was enough to potentially taint the case in the public eye, writing that "while we recognize that an appearance of impropriety generally is not enough to support disqualification, this is the rare case in which disqualification is mandated and no other remedy will suffice to restore public confidence in the integrity of these proceedings."[14] It declined, however, to dismiss the sprawling racketeering conspiracy case entirely, stating it could not conclude that the record supported "the extreme sanction of dismissal of the indictment."[14] Willis indicated she would continue fighting to stay on the case, with her team asking the state Supreme Court to review the decision.[14] Trump and several co-defendants had sought her disqualification because of her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], arguing she financially benefited from vacations he covered.[14]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote in [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that Willis's services were "no longer needed in the lawfare against President Trump and the 18 other defendants," emphasizing that the disqualification extended to her whole office and that Fulton County taxpayers would have to start over with a new prosecutorial team after the extraordinary cost of the litigation.[14] She questioned whether continued pursuit served the people of Fulton County given unsolved crime in Atlanta, called every further move "a waste of public resources, a fraud upon the court, and an abuse of her statutory power," and said that when Willis appeared before the Georgia Supreme Court she "needs a spanking."[14]

## February 2024 disqualification hearing

On Thursday, January 18, 2024, Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], who oversees *The State v Donald John Trump*, issued an order setting a February 15 hearing on allegations that Willis had an improper romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], whom she hired to assist her investigation and subsequent indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[15] The hearing was scheduled for 9:30 a.m., with prosecutors given until February 2 to file a response.[15]

Willis, the locally elected district attorney, had issued dozens of indictments in August 2023 accusing Trump and his allies of trying to overturn Georgia's 2020 election results.[15] Those indictments alleged that Trump and his allies suggested Georgia's Republican secretary of state, [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]], could find enough votes for Trump to win the battleground state; that they harassed an election worker who faced false claims of fraud; and that they attempted to persuade Georgia lawmakers to ignore the will of voters and appoint a new slate of electors favorable to Trump.[15]

Commenting in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the Georgia elections case and the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago]] case were following similar trend lines, with the court system turning its slow, steady ire onto the prosecutorial teams rather than Trump, largely over perceived and alleged process violations.[15] He restated his view that in 2024 "Trump's Trial(s) are his campaign," and that the informational and reputation-destroying chaos of four simultaneous trials — an intentional feature of what he calls the Deep State's weaponized [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] — was backfiring.[15]

## December 2024 order to release RICO communications

In December 2024 a local judge ordered Willis to release all communications between her office, Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s office, and the January 6th Committee regarding her RICO case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his allies, after she was found to have violated federal law by withholding them.[16] The Tuesday order, obtained in litigation brought by [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]], directed her to "conduct a diligent search of her records for responsive materials within five business days of the entry of this Order" and, within the same period, to provide the plaintiff with copies of all responsive records not legally exempted or excepted from disclosure.[16] If Willis could not find the records, she was mandated to follow court-ordered procedures to "provide an explanation why such correspondence does not exist."[16]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Willis had won reelection as Fulton County District Attorney in November 2024 and was now ordered to release records of what she called Willis's "Trump-deranged collusion" with Smith.[16] She recalled that Willis had previously lied about visiting the White House, a fact that emerged during her disqualification hearing before Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], who also won his own election in the first half of the year, and argued that the communications should shed light on "the actual RICO happening down in Georgia."[16]

## House Judiciary contempt threat (2024)

In March 2024 the [[house-judiciary-committee|U.S. House Committee on the Judiciary]] put Willis on notice that it expected her cooperation with its investigation into allegations that she misused federal funds during the Georgia election interference investigation.[17] A subpoena dated February 2 and issued by Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] said Willis had "failed to comply voluntarily" with the committee's requests for documents concerning her office's receipt and use of Department of Justice grants.[17] Jordan further accused Willis of terminating an employee who had tried to stop the Fulton County District Attorney's Office from spending a federal grant on travel, computers and "swag" tied to a Center of Youth Empowerment and Gang Prevention.[17] A spokesperson for the district attorney called the allegations false, said the dismissed employee was a holdover from the previous administration terminated for cause, and dismissed any potential suit over the firing as "baseless litigation."[17]

## Georgia court order on Trump-related record searches (August 2025)

In August 2025 a Georgia state court ordered Willis to provide new information and possibly conduct another search for Trump-related records after her affidavit failed to show whether devices belonging to former Fulton County special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] or chief investigator Michael L. Hill had been searched; both were involved in evidence gathering and likely met with the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]].[18] The order stemmed from a [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] lawsuit claiming Willis falsely denied having records responsive to an earlier Georgia Open Records Act request for communications with Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s office or the January 6 Committee.[18]

A March 7, 2025 order had required Willis to turn over 212 pages of records and submit an affidavit explaining how they were located and why they were initially withheld; the documents were found after at least a fifth search of her office, and Judicial Watch was awarded $21,578 in attorney's fees and costs, paid ten days after the court deadline.[18] The new order acknowledged that Willis submitted the affidavit and 212 pages of emails, texts, correspondence, and search protocols, but identified gaps: no search protocol for Investigator Hill, uncertainty over whether Wade's materials were reviewed, and incomplete detail on Willis' own search.[18] The court directed Willis to submit the missing search protocols for Hill and herself, clarify whether Wade's materials were searched, and submit any additional records uncovered for in-camera review, where they would remain pending the court's assessment.[18]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the ruling, writing that it was "great news that the Fulton County DA is back in the hot seat" while cautioning readers not to hold their breath on compliance, and characterizing Willis as someone who "wields power without any regard for norms, rules, or ethics."[18] She likened a "reboot of Fani!" to a revived television series with old characters and new storylines, noting that the missing files relate to Willis' team working with the J6 Committee in what she described as the overarching conspiracy to "get Trump."[18]

## Bradley's February 27, 2024 testimony

At the disqualification hearing on Tuesday, February 27, 2024, key witness [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] — the former divorce lawyer and law partner of [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] — testified that he had no knowledge of when the romance between the two prosecutors began, a potential setback for [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his co-defendants who had expected him to contradict Willis's account.[19] Willis and Wade maintained that the relationship began only after she hired him in November 2021, and Trump's lawyer accused them of lying.[19]

Defense attorneys attacked Bradley's credibility by reading aloud January text messages in which he told [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]] that the romance had begun before Nov. 1, 2021, answering "Absolutely" when asked whether it predated the hiring.[19] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that Bradley was impeached several times on his recollection and that the movants established he had reviewed and edited Merchant's motion and pronounced it "looks good," effectively approving its allegations.[19] Trump attorney [[steve-sadow|Steve Sadow]] led the cross-examination, and the hearing was scheduled to resume that Friday at 1 p.m. ET.[19]

## "The train is coming" remarks

On Saturday, March 23, 2024, while attending an Atlanta-area Easter basket giveaway, Willis told CNN that "the train is coming," referring to her team's preparation of the case against Donald Trump and his 14 co-defendants.[20] Having avoided disqualification from the election subversion case after more than two months of hearings over her romantic relationship with her lead prosecutor, she said she had continued working on the matter throughout: "While that was going on, we were writing responsive briefs, we were still doing the case in a way that it needed to be done. I don't feel like we've been slowed down at all. I do think there are efforts to slow down this train, but the train is coming."[20] She added that her office was "not going to miss or skip a beat because of all the noise or distraction on one case."[20]

The remarks came as the district attorney's office still faced legal hurdles; on Wednesday, March 20, 2024, Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] granted requests from Trump and his co-defendants to appeal the decision allowing Willis to continue prosecuting the case.[20] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that McAfee had floated in his disqualification order the idea of gagging the district attorney's office, and that Willis had previously been disqualified from prosecuting cases after publicly discussing the guilt of the accused.[20] Trump attorney [[steve-sadow|Steve Sadow]] responded to the interview on X, writing: "Apparently Judge McAfee's warning to Willis in his disqualification order about talking about the case in a public forum is simply being ignored. Does that surprise anyone??"[20]

## Wade's White House meetings (January 2024)

In January 2024, reporting revealed two 2022 meetings between [[white-house|White House]] employees and [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], the special prosecutor Willis had appointed to her Georgia election-interference case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and 18 other defendants, prompting calls for the White House to disclose details.[21] The meetings surfaced in a 127-page filing by co-defendant [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]] seeking dismissal on the ground that Willis "never had legal authority to appoint the special prosecutor," and alleging that she and Wade maintained a "clandestine personal relationship during the pendency of this case" while both profited "at the expense of the taxpayers."[21] Included in the filings was a bill Wade sent to the Fulton County district attorney's office requesting two $2,000 payments under the project title "anti-corruption special prosecutor."[21]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Willis was possibly involved in a RICO conspiracy while prosecuting her political opposition for RICO, describing the billing arrangement as "ballsy," and echoed [[bernard-kerik|Bernie Kerik]]'s question of who was directing the Willis investigation — the White House or the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]].[21]

## 2020 Facebook posts on election irregularities

In late August 2023, newly discovered Facebook posts written by Willis in November 2020 were reported to show the Atlanta-area district attorney publicly commenting on the presidential election results, including one post advancing theories of election irregularity similar to those for which she had indicted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and eighteen associates.[22] The posts were surfaced by independent journalist [[collin-rugg|Collin Rugg]], who asked whether the "unbiased" and "fair" district attorney should indict herself, and were written up by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]].[22]

One post, dated November 4, 2020, referenced the Fulton County "water leak" episode: "Georgia could determine who is our next president. A TEAM of lawyers needs to watch them count every single VOTE. They can start in Fulton where we are having water leaks. What ballots are they throwing out? Georgia lets give an honest accounting. No stunts!"[22] The Gateway Pundit account held that Willis had opened her February 2021 investigation of Trump on the basis of his call to Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]], a call it said was misrepresented to [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] by [[jordan-fuchs|Jordan Fuchs]] and Raffensperger — a report the Post later corrected — and that Willis's team proceeded with the indictment despite knowing this.[22]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that Willis should indict herself but would not, while nonetheless painting a target on other officials guilty of the same conduct.[22] Summarizing the substance of the charges, he described the claim against Trump as being that he knew the 2020 election was legitimate yet used his speech to spread false claims of fraud and conspired with others to overturn the result.[22] Deschamps argued the allegation had no basis, since Trump never acknowledged the validity of the election and substantial evidence indicated the contest was at minimum of poor quality — citing more than 300,000 people improperly on the Georgia voter rolls whose ballots were never traced.[22]

He turned the logic of the prosecution around: claiming there is no evidence of wrongdoing when no one bothered to look is itself an indictment of the officials charged with vetting elections, from poll workers to the vice president, then [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]].[22] Gatekeepers who certified while aware of fraud or bad faith, he wrote, are guilty of conspiracy, and their abandonment of a duty they were explicitly charged with amounts to criminal — even "seditious" — negligence.[22] In his reading, Trump was willingly submitting to the process in order to educate the public about the actual crimes committed with respect to the 2020 election, so that "these corrupt actors will have given the people the eyes to see their crimes."[22]

## February 2024 Shafer disqualification filing

On Tuesday, February 13, 2024, co-defendant [[david-shafer|David Shafer]], a former chairman of the Georgia Republican Party charged with racketeering over efforts to help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] overturn the 2020 election, submitted additional court filings supporting the motion to disqualify Willis and special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] from prosecuting Trump and his 18 co-defendants.[23] The new filing accused Wade of having lied in his divorce proceedings, presenting evidence that he had updated his answers to two interrogatories asking whether he had sexual relations with anyone besides his spouse during the marriage.[23] Wade had answered "none" to both on May 30, 2023, but revised his responses the previous month after allegations of an "improper relationship" with Willis surfaced, changing his answer to assert privilege under O.C.G.A. Sec. 24-5-505.[23] Georgia law provides that no party is required to testify on matters that "incriminate" or "bring infamy, disgrace, or public contempt upon" parties, witnesses or their family.[23]

## Defense claims of deliberate venue selection

In August 2023, Trump attorney [[alina-habba|Alina Habba]] told [[newsmax|Newsmax]] that Willis had brought charges in state court that belonged in federal court, and that she "did it on purpose so that if he is president, he can't pardon himself if he's convicted."[24] Habba said the defense would probably seek removal to federal court and a change of venue, and argued that election-related charges against a president should "100%" be heard federally.[24] Asked whether any of the eighteen co-defendants charged alongside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might cooperate against him, she said no.[24] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the venue fight to [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]]'s parallel removal motion and predicted the dispute would ultimately reach the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[24]

## February 2024 disqualification proceedings

On February 12, 2024, Fulton County Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] ruled that an evidentiary hearing on allegations against Willis would go forward that Thursday, saying it was "possible" the misconduct allegations "could result in disqualification" if evidence showed she had financially benefited from the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Georgia election interference case]].[25] Willis, special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] and a number of employees of the district attorney's office had been subpoenaed to testify by Trump co-defendant [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]], who sought both dismissal of the charges against him and Willis's disqualification on the grounds of an alleged romantic relationship with Wade that produced financial gain for both.[25] McAfee observed that the state had admitted a relationship existed, leaving the existence and extent of any financial benefit as the open question.[25]

Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] wrote that the Georgia case was "floundering" as allegations mounted against Willis over her intimate relationship with her subordinate special prosecutor, part of a broader collapse of the cases against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[25]

## Dismissal of the Georgia RICO case (November 2025)

In late November 2025, the Georgia racketeering prosecution Willis had brought against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and co-defendants was dismissed in its entirety.[26] Trump announced the dismissal in a Truth Social post celebrating that "LAW and JUSTICE have prevailed in the Great State of Georgia," describing the case as an illegal, unconstitutional and un-American hoax perpetrated by Willis and [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] at the direction of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and his "handlers."[26]

Trump's statement recapitulated the grievances that had dogged the prosecution: that Willis had unlawfully hired her romantic partner to prosecute a former president despite his inexperience with RICO cases, that she had misrepresented the nature and timeline of the relationship, that she paid him substantial sums drawn from Georgia taxpayers, and that the pair took repeated Caribbean cruises and vacations together.[26] He also alleged campaign finance violations and asserted that the overriding motive was to "GET TRUMP."[26] He predicted that the few remaining Democrat "witch hunts" would meet a similar end, and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] flagged the story for coverage on Badlands Daily.[26]

## Cell phone records filing (February 2024)

In February 2024, attorneys for Donald Trump filed cell phone records and geolocation analysis that appeared to contradict Willis's sworn account of when her romantic relationship with Special Prosecutor Nathan Wade began.[27] Willis and Wade had stated, in sworn filings and testimony, that the "personal relationship" started in 2022; a February 2, 2022 filing submitted a Wade affidavit reading, "In 2022, District Attorney Willis and I developed a personal relationship."[27] Records subpoenaed from AT&T covering January 1 to November 30, 2021 were analyzed by an investigator whose geolocation tool placed Wade at Willis's South Fulton condominium during that period, indicating the relationship predated Wade's November 1, 2021 appointment.[27] Ashe in America wrote that if the process were just, the data "would be the final nail in the coffin of Fani's foray into complex RICO litigation," noting that mainstream legal analysts who had been confident Willis would survive shifted to cautious "here's what we know" coverage over the weekend.[27] See [[willis-wade-cell-phone-records-filing|the Willis–Wade cell phone records filing]] for detail.[27]

## May 2024 Democratic primary victory

Willis won the Democratic primary in her bid for reelection as Fulton County District Attorney in May 2024, defeating progressive attorney Christian Wise Smith and setting up a general election contest against Republican [[courtney-kramer|Courtney Kramer]] in the fall.[28] After the victory she told reporters that voters had sent a message that 'people want a DA that is just, that treats everybody equally and that works hard, and they know that they have that in me.'[28] Her public profile had been boosted by the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Trump election interference case]] and by racketeering cases against well-known rappers, though on election night she emphasized her record against violent crime and gang members alongside second-chance programs for first offenders and interventions for at-risk youth.[28] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that her race was called within about 30 minutes and that, unlike Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], her electability remained an open question heading into the fall.[28]

## August 2023 arraignment scheduling and surrenders

In August 2023, Willis sought to hold the arraignments of all defendants in the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Georgia case]] during the week following Labor Day, and Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] authorized cameras in his courtroom through September 8, so that the proceedings would become the first televised criminal proceedings involving [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[29] She had charged Trump the previous week alongside 18 co-defendants over their alleged efforts to overturn the 2020 election results.[29] On Tuesday, August 22, 2023, [[scott-hall|Scott Hall]] — charged by Willis over the [[coffee-county-voting-systems-breach|Coffee County voting systems breach]] — became the first co-defendant to surrender at the Fulton County jail, having signed a bond agreement with her the day before.[29] Willis also set a Friday noon deadline for surrenders, prompting [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]] to ask a federal judge to bar her from seeking his arrest before his removal hearing; the court ordered her to respond in writing by 3 p.m. that Wednesday.[29]

## Conspiracy allegation against Joycelyn Wade and Mike Roman

In a court filing reported on January 23, 2024, Willis accused Joycelyn Wade — the estranged wife of special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], with whom Willis was alleged to have had an affair — of "interfering" in her prosecution of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and of "conspiring" with Trump co-defendant [[mike-roman|Mike Roman]] in an effort to get the charges against Roman dismissed.[30] The Wades had been married for 26 years.[30] Earlier that month a Georgia judge gave Willis until February 2 to respond formally to the allegations that she had an affair with Nathan Wade, the lawyer she hired to oversee the election-fraud case against Trump and 14 alleged co-conspirators.[30] Roman, who worked for Trump's 2020 campaign, was the defendant whose filing raised the affair allegations and sought both Willis's disqualification and dismissal of the charges against him.[30]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Willis's theory rested entirely on timing — that the disclosures had to be a conspiracy because they damaged her case against Trump and helped Roman — and called the argument flimsy, noting that Nathan Wade's divorce case was proceeding in parallel with the RICO prosecution and that the pace of disclosure there was being driven by the court rather than by the defendants.[30] She questioned whether Willis had any evidence that Roman knew of the affair before the divorce filings made the details public, characterized the claim as a "Hail Mary" given that two judges and the Georgia Senate were examining the matter, and observed that Willis did not appear to be receiving special treatment as a public official despite prosecuting Trump.[30]

## Appeals Court Cancels Disqualification Arguments (2024)

On November 18, 2024, the Georgia Court of Appeals canceled oral arguments scheduled for December 5 in President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s bid to disqualify Willis from prosecuting him, issuing a one-sentence order with no explanation that postponed the hearing "until further order of this Court."[31] The cancellation came less than two weeks after Trump's election victory.[31] Trump had sought to derail the Fulton County prosecution over his attempt to overturn the 2020 Georgia election result by challenging Willis's relationship with [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], the top prosecutor she hired to oversee the case; the trial judge allowed the case to proceed on the condition that Wade resign, which he did.[31] The appeals court had been set to hear arguments from Trump and eight co-defendants that Willis could not continue to oversee the prosecution.[31]

## March 2024 partial dismissal ruling

On March 13, 2024, Judge Scott McAfee quashed six counts of Willis's Fulton County racketeering indictment, including three against Donald Trump, holding that prosecutors had failed to provide enough detail about the alleged crimes; the remainder of the indictment, including the RICO count and ten counts against Trump, survived, and McAfee indicated prosecutors could seek a new indictment to reinstate the dismissed charges.[32] The ruling landed while Willis was already fighting an effort to remove her from the prosecution over her romantic relationship with a colleague, and marked the first time any charges in Trump's four criminal cases had been dismissed.[32]

Legal commentators split over what the decision portended. Norm Eisen suggested it indicated McAfee was leaning toward keeping Willis on the case, though he conceded the lean was slight and the question essentially a toss-up, while CNN analyst Elie Honig called it an embarrassing setback in a long line of them dating to the case's grand jury phase.[32] Badlands writer Ashe in America framed the pending disqualification decision as certain to be both captivating and divisive in an election year, given what she characterized as the corruption and "punishment for profit" that had come to light regarding Willis.[32]

## February 2024 admission of the relationship

On Friday, February 2, 2024, Willis formally acknowledged a "personal relationship" with [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], the prosecutor she had hired to manage the Georgia election interference case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], while arguing that the relationship was not a reason to disqualify her or her office from the prosecution.[33] The admission came almost a month after allegations of an "improper, clandestine personal relationship" surfaced in a motion from one of Trump's co-defendants, [[mike-roman|Michael Roman]], a former Trump campaign official; that motion sought to disqualify both prosecutors and Willis's entire office, an outcome that would likely have sown chaos for an unprecedented racketeering prosecution of a former president.[33] Roman's filing alleged that Willis had hired her "boyfriend" as a special prosecutor, granted him lucrative contracts despite his being underqualified, and then benefited by taking vacations that Wade paid for.[33]

Willis's filing responded that "while the allegations raised in the various motions are salacious and garnered the media attention they were designed to obtain, none provide this Court with any basis upon which to order the relief they seek," and asserted that the relationship "has never involved direct or indirect financial benefit" to her.[33] The submission included an affidavit from Wade stating that the relationship began only after he had been hired, along with copies of Willis's email traffic with Delta showing travel arrangements to and from Miami and indicating she had purchased plane tickets for both of them.[33]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the episode "an absolute disaster" for Willis, noting that even *The Root* — which had given her the top spot at its Root 100 ceremony — wrote that she "absolutely should've known better than to put herself in this position," and that the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] was keeping her at arm's length.[33] She argued that copies of email traffic are not proof of payment and that Willis would have produced proof of payment if it existed.[33] At that point the embattled prosecutor faced a state legislative inquiry, a federal legislative inquiry, and an examination of the alleged facts by RICO case fact finder Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] scheduled for February 15.[33] Ashe contended that Willis's admissions came nowhere near resolving the matter, instead raising new questions about perceived corruption and introducing material disputes of fact that the court must hear from both sides, and observed that an innocent prosecutor would presumably want her side examined rather than seeking to vacate the hearing.[33] She added that a Republican prosecutor in the same position while charging [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would have faced wall-to-wall coverage and been forced to resign in disgrace, and concluded that no decision should be made before the court, legislators, and public could examine the facts, because "the public trust demands it."[33]

## Federal grant reporting inconsistencies, April 2024

In April 2024 the Washington Free Beacon reported that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] had uncovered "inconsistencies" in the Fulton County district attorney's use of federal grant funds.[34] The finding came two years after Willis fired a whistleblower who had warned that her office was attempting to misuse a $488,000 federal grant to pay for "swag," computers, and travel; a department spokeswoman said the Office of Justice Programs had "noticed some inconsistencies in what Fulton County has reported to [the Federal Subaward Reporting System]" and was working with the county to correct its reporting.[34] The grant had been earmarked for a Center for Youth Empowerment and Gang Prevention in Atlanta; it ended in September 2023 and the center never opened.[34]

## February 2024 disqualification testimony

On February 15, 2024, Willis took the witness stand in the [[fani-willis-disqualification-hearing|disqualification hearing]] and forcefully pushed back against what she described as "lies" about her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], in a proceeding that threatened to upend one of four criminal cases against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[35] She had originally fought to stay off the stand but agreed to testify after a previous witness said the relationship began earlier than the pair had claimed.[35]

Her testimony grew heated under questioning by defense attorney [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]], with Willis at one point raising papers and shouting, "It's a lie!"[35] She told the court, "Do you think I'm on trial? These people are on trial for trying to steal an election in 2020. I'm not on trial no matter how hard you try to put me on trial," and separately said that "Merchant's interests are contrary to democracy."[35] She was expected to resume testifying the following day.[35]

Willis testified that she paid rent on an off-book condominium sometimes in cash and mostly by CashApp in variable amounts, and that she spent time there with Wade because her own home was unsafe given her record on gang crime rather than to conceal a relationship.[35] She said she had reimbursed Wade in cash for vacations he paid for, leaving no records, and that she had not disclosed the relationship to anyone in government because she considered herself a private person without a duty to disclose.[35] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the day as the county's top law enforcement official explaining away evidence of an improper relationship under oath, noting that even CNN appeared to be distancing itself from her.[35]

## Coordination with the House January 6 Committee

Reporting in January 2024 revealed that Willis's Fulton County team had received an early boost from the House [[january-6-committee|January 6 select committee]] while investigating [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over his actions related to the 2020 election.[36] In mid-April 2022, committee staff quietly met with attorneys working on the Fulton County case, around the time Willis was preparing to convene a special grand jury — the investigation in which she employed her alleged paramour [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], who separately coordinated with the Biden White House on the case.[36] According to Politico, committee attorneys allowed the district attorney's team to review, but not keep, a "limited set of evidence" the panel had gathered.[36] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] framed the arrangement as collusion, noting that Willis had coordinated with both the committee and the White House in assembling her case against the former president.[36]

## The McAfee donation disclosure (February 2024)

In February 2024 it emerged that Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], who was overseeing the Fulton County case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], had donated $150 to Willis's campaign in June 2020, while he was still an assistant U.S. Attorney and before his appointment to the bench.[37] McAfee had also formerly worked under Willis when she led the complex trial division in the Fulton County District Attorney's Office.[37] The report surfaced as McAfee prepared to decide whether Willis should be disqualified over allegations that she financially benefited from appointing her romantic partner [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] to the case.[37]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the pending disqualification question as the most urgent matter before the court, framing it as one of impropriety and conflicts of interest, and characterized the Willis–Wade arrangement as an "all-cash-all-luxury-but-totally-legit relationship."[37] She noted that Willis had been allowed to speak at length on the stand, a latitude that in her view only undercut Willis's claims not to be corrupt.[37] Ashe added that watching Willis's televised hearing had made it easier for her to recognize the same patterns — trips, gifts and large cash payments — in Ukrainian public corruption investigations she read about the following day, and observed pointedly that Nathan Wade had invoiced the [[white-house|White House]].[37]

## Bond terms for the nineteen defendants (August 2023)

On August 21, 2023, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] set bonds in Willis's election interference prosecution: $200,000 for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], $100,000 each for [[john-eastman|John Eastman]] and [[kenneth-chesebro|Kenneth Chesebro]], $50,000 for [[ray-smith-iii|Ray Smith III]], and $10,000 for [[scott-hall|Scott Hall]].[38] Willis's office required all nineteen defendants to appear at the [[fulton-county-jail|Fulton County Jail]] for processing by noon that Friday, and every bond agreement barred acts of intimidation against codefendants or witnesses, with Trump's specifying "no direct or indirect threat."[38]

## Timpson whistleblower reporting

In late January 2024 the Washington Free Beacon reported that Willis had fired the employee who warned her about the mishandling of federal funds, while never denying the substance of her allegations.[41] Less than a year into her tenure as Fulton County district attorney, in 2021, Willis met with [[amanda-timpson|Amanda Timpson]], an office employee responsible for providing nonviolent juvenile offenders with alternatives to the juvenile court system.[41] In a recorded November 19, 2021 conversation, Timpson said she had been demoted after trying to stop a top Willis campaign aide, [[michael-cuffee|Michael Cuffee]], from misusing a $488,000 federal grant earmarked for a Center of Youth Empowerment and Gang Prevention on "swag," computers, and travel.[41] Willis responded, "I respect that is your assessment. And I'm not saying that your assessment is wrong," later apologizing and saying Cuffee had "failed" her administration.[41]

Less than two months afterward Willis abruptly terminated Timpson and, according to Timpson, had her escorted from the office by seven armed investigators.[41] After Timpson filed a whistleblower complaint alleging wrongful termination, Willis's office described her as a "holdover from the prior administration" terminated for "failure to meet the standards of the new administration."[41] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Willis had "99 problems, the least of which is her failing case against President Trump," argued that the standards of the new administration appeared to consist of bending and breaking the law, and pointed to articles of impeachment filed against Willis as a possible remedy.[41]

## Bradley–Merchant text messages (February 2024)

Hundreds of text messages obtained by CNN and reported on February 28, 2024 showed that [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]], a key witness in the push to disqualify Willis from the Georgia election case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], had been far more deeply involved in that effort than previously known.[42] The 413 texts between Bradley and [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]], an attorney for one of Trump's co-defendants, revealed months of communication and underscored the extent to which Bradley assisted Merchant's pursuit of evidence for claims that Willis and her top prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] had an improper romantic relationship.[42] The messages raised questions about Bradley's credibility and the degree to which Merchant relied on claims she could not substantiate elsewhere, and shed light on why his testimony fell short of defense expectations for their star witness.[42]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that attorney Phil Holloway broke the texts publicly on Wednesday and that the takeaway was that Bradley, Wade and Willis had perjured themselves, citing Bradley's account that the affair began in 2020 "when she left the DA's office and was a judge in South Fulton."[42] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], [[just-human|Just Human]] and [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] worked through the messages on that night's [[devolution-power-hour|Devolution Power Hour]].[42]

## February 2024 evidentiary hearing testimony

The February 16, 2024 session of the disqualification hearing did not produce the expected dramatic conclusion: Willis was not recalled by her attorneys for further cross-examination.[45] Instead the court heard from former Georgia Governor [[roy-barnes|Roy Barnes]], who testified that he had been offered the special prosecutor role ultimately given to [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] but declined it because he did not want the attention that came with the case.[45] Willis's father, [[john-floyd|John Floyd]], then testified that he had known of prior romantic relationships in his daughter's life but that she had concealed her relationship with Wade from him.[45]

The bulk of the day was consumed by the testimony of [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]], Wade's business partner and divorce lawyer, punctuated by repeated objections on grounds of attorney–client privilege.[45] On cross-examination, Willis's attorney [[anna-cross|Anna Cross]] sought to impeach and discredit Bradley — a move [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called puzzling given that he had spent the day protecting Wade's account — and which may have forfeited the privilege Bradley had claimed, opening the door for the defense to compel further answers.[45] The impeachment included a public accusation that Bradley had committed sexual assault while working with Wade, and an additional witness on nightclub conduct who was excluded as irrelevant; Ashe in America described it as a "public humiliation ritual."[45] The court recessed with the timing of the conclusion undetermined.[45]

Analyzing the hearing on "Fox News Sunday," [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] called the facts "astonishing," said the judge had "heard enough," and suggested he might ask Willis and Wade to step aside and could additionally refer both to the state bar over the allegation of false statements.[45]

## Second round of the disqualification fight (May 2024)

Over the weekend of May 18-19, 2024, Fulton County Superior Court began sending thousands of pages of documents to the Georgia Court of Appeals, teeing up a second round of fighting over Willis's leadership of the election interference case involving [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and others.[46] The office of Ché Alexander, clerk of Superior and Magistrate Courts, transmitted transcripts and other records from three of the nine defendants seeking Willis's removal — [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]], [[david-shafer|David Shafer]] and Cathy Latham — with documents for a fourth, Bob Cheeley, apparently in the process of being sent; the appeals court requires the records in order to docket the case and assign judges.[46]

## June 2024 appellate pause of the Georgia case

On June 5, 2024, the [[georgia-court-of-appeals|Georgia Court of Appeals]] paused proceedings in the state 2020 election subversion case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] while it reviewed appeals, including whether Willis could remain on the case as Fulton County District Attorney.[39] The pause pushed the case past October, when the appeals court was scheduled to hear oral arguments on Trump's bid to disqualify her, making a trial before the November 2024 election unlikely.[39]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recounted that [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]]'s disqualification motion earlier that year had produced witness and documentary evidence that Willis secured funding from the Georgia legislature to reduce her case backlog — including processing long-delayed rape kits — and then used it both to pursue Trump and to elevate her standard of living.[39] Willis hired her boyfriend [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], who had no relevant experience, and obstructed transparency into his contracts and fee arrangements; Wade allegedly hid roughly $700,000 in income from his wife during their divorce and is accused of misusing client fee accounts.[39] The pair took numerous vacations they claimed were paid in cash, a claim convenient for asserting costs were split equally and no state money was used.[39] Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] declined to disqualify Willis but forced a choice between herself and Wade, who resigned shortly afterward; in later hearings Fulton County was still said to be obstructing discovery.[39] Ashe in America called the appellate review a new "season" of the Willis and Wade saga, while RICO defendant [[harrison-floyd|Harrison Floyd]] pushed back on [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]]'s caution that the pause should not be treated as a win.[39]

## Appeal of the disqualification ruling (March 2024)

Days after Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] ruled on Friday, March 15, 2024 that Willis could stay on the Georgia prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his co-defendants, Trump and several co-defendants moved for a certificate of immediate review so they could appeal the decision.[40] Because there is no automatic right to appeal at that stage, McAfee would have had to grant permission within ten days and the state appeals court would then have had to agree to hear the matter — a sequence that could add still more delay to a prosecution without a trial date.[40] Commentators noted that McAfee had signalled openness to an appeal in a separate ruling dismissing some counts but gave no such signal in the disqualification order.[40] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that [[msnbc|MSNBC]]'s coverage of the appeal was unusually subdued, which she read as the outlet coming to terms with the case's likely fate.[40]

## Trump surrender deadline (August 2023)

Willis brought the Georgia racketeering and conspiracy indictment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and eighteen of his associates, and gave those charged until noon on August 25, 2023 to hand themselves over or risk arrest.[43] Trump surrendered at the [[fulton-county-jail|Fulton County Jail]] on the evening of August 24 and was released on a $200,000 bond about twenty minutes later, after being fingerprinted and photographed.[43] See [[2023-trump-fulton-county-booking|2023 Trump Fulton County booking]].[43]

## House GOP probe into J6 committee collusion (March 2024)

Republican congressional investigators opened an inquiry in March 2024 into "collusion" between the Democrat-run House January 6 committee and Willis during her prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[44] The House Administration Oversight Subcommittee chaired by Rep. [[barry-loudermilk|Barry Loudermilk]] said in a status report released Monday, March 11 that it had discovered a letter dated December 17, 2021 in which Willis asked the select committee for any records relevant to her investigation of Trump's efforts to challenge the 2020 election, including "recordings and transcripts of witness interviews and depositions, electronic and print records of communications, and records of travel."[44] The subcommittee expressed concern that the panel led by Reps. [[bennie-thompson|Bennie Thompson]] and [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] may have shared evidence such as video depositions with Willis's office while withholding it from Republicans who took control of the chamber in 2023.[44]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Willis by then faced prosecutorial disqualification, a state Senate hearing, a possible state ethics probe and the House investigation, and cautioned that although these might look like political theatrics, Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] was operating in a real courtroom with real consequences and had said he was on track to rule that week on disqualification.[44] Citing evidence of "prosecution for profit," including sworn testimony that the case netted Willis and [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] several hundred thousand dollars, she wrote she would be stunned if both were allowed to remain, arguing that the appearance of impropriety matters as much as an actual conflict when a novel legal theory is levied against a former president.[44]

## Funding, hiring and misconduct allegations (March 2024 Senate testimony)

On March 6, 2024, defense attorney [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]] testified before the [[georgia-senate-committee-on-special-investigations|Georgia Senate Committee on Special Investigations]], presenting a case built largely on open records and her own investigation that Willis had been deceptive in obtaining the county funding used to hire [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[47] Prior to September 2021 Willis funded her office's special prosecutors through the State Seizure Fund, which ran out; on September 15, 2021 she requested $780,000 to hire 55 new employees, four days after cell data indicated Wade spent the night at her condominium.[47] The business rationale given to Fulton County was clearing the backlog of cases from the Covid shutdowns — it did not mention hiring special prosecutors or contractors, and made no reference to the election interference RICO case.[47] Additional 2022 funding not to exceed $5 million followed, after which Willis claimed discretionary authority to spend the money as her office saw fit.[47]

Two weeks after the funding request, on November 1, 2021, Willis hired Wade as special prosecutor at a $15,000 monthly fee cap.[47] Merchant testified that Willis would not have been permitted to hire Wade as an employee because of their personal relationship, and instead undertook "a process that circumvented county rules to hire him anyway."[47] Willis was the sole reviewer and approver of Wade's billings, which exceeded $700,000 — several times what the experienced prosecutors [[anna-cross|Anna Cross]] and [[john-floyd|John Floyd]] billed — and were never questioned or cut despite block billing and repeated overruns.[47]

Merchant also alleged obstruction and witness tampering. She testified that the District Attorney's office was obstructing her open records requests, refusing to produce Wade's badge access logs on the claim that he never had a badge, and that the county stopped publishing his payable checks once her disqualification motion was filed, potentially in violation of state sunshine laws.[47] She alleged that in September 2023 Willis telephoned [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] — in the presence of Cobb County prosecutor [[cindi-lee-yeager|Cindi Lee Yeager]] — and said, "They are coming after us. You don't need to talk to them about anything about us."[47] Bradley was subsequently contacted by attorney [[gabe-banks|Gabe Banks]], whose wife reportedly works for Willis, in what Bradley described as a "first shot over the bow."[47]

On the misconduct question, Merchant pointed to Willis's January 14, 2024 "church speech" — a roughly 35-minute address in which she publicly condemned the accused and framed criticism of her office in racial terms — and to her cooperation with the authors of [[find-me-the-votes|Find Me the Votes]], to whom she gave unfettered access and on-the-record quotes about the pending case.[47] Merchant testified that Willis had previously been disqualified from prosecuting cases for similar public statements about defendants.[47] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] should toss the case, sanction the offending parties and refer Willis and Wade to the Georgia Bar, while the Senate committee should recommend criminal charges.[47]
