# J. Christopher Giancarlo

J. Christopher Giancarlo is an American lawyer and former regulator who chaired the [[commodity-futures-trading-commission|Commodity Futures Trading Commission]] and founded the [[digital-dollar-project|Digital Dollar Project]]. He is known in cryptocurrency circles as "Crypto Dad," a nickname earned through his testimony before [[congress]] and used as the title of his book. In [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s article "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?" he is presented, alongside [[jared-kushner]], as one of the two central figures behind the American push for a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]].

## Career

Quoting Wikipedia, the article records that Giancarlo served as acting CFTC chair from the inauguration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on January 20, 2017; that Trump nominated him as full-time chairman on March 14, 2017; and that the U.S. Senate confirmed him on August 3, 2017. The same source describes him as a [[blockchain]] technology advocate and a contributor to global discourse on cryptocurrencies and digital assets, notes his founding of the nonprofit Digital Dollar Project "to advance exploration of a United States Central Bank Digital Currency," and identifies him as a former director of the financial services company BlockFi. Under his CFTC oversight the [[bitcoin]] futures market launched in December 2017.

## Advocacy inside the Trump administration

CNBC's reporting on the FOIA-released correspondence of Treasury Secretary [[steven-mnuchin|Steven Mnuchin]] places Giancarlo among the "crypto progressive voices" advising the Secretary. A July 2018 exchange shows him, via his executive assistant, pressing for an in-person meeting. Asked later about a White House meeting, Giancarlo said he could not recall the specific request but confirmed that throughout 2018 he "routinely apprised Secretary Mnuchin of ongoing operation and supervision of the Bitcoin Futures market launched under CFTC oversight in December 2017."

## Philosophy of digital money

The article quotes at length from a November 2021 Forbes interview in which Giancarlo summarized the lesson of writing his book: "money is too important to be left to central bankers." He drew the analogy to French prime minister Georges Clemenceau's remark at the end of World War I that "war is too important to be left to the generals." A free society, he argued, "has so much to gain from the modernization of our financial system that could be brought about by crypto. But it has so much to lose if it doesn't have a say in what values are going to be encoded in the future of money," whether non-sovereign money of the kind represented by Diem or sovereign money in the form of a digital dollar. The values he named — "free enterprise, free economic activity, individual privacy, free of censorship by governments, for legal activities, people need to know that their affairs are private" — he described as the stakes of "the fight for the future of money," and as the reason the Digital Dollar Project was launched.

Lange presents these values as precisely what Trump is said to be fighting for behind the scenes in the design of an American digital dollar.
