# David Solomon

David Solomon is Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]].[1]

## Meeting with the Treasury Secretary

Sources cited in Badlands' 2026 coverage of the [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]] grand jury investigation reported that Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] met with Solomon earlier in the year in New York.[1] Bessent reportedly delivered a direct message urging the firm's cooperation with investigators and warning of potential scrutiny over the role of Goldman Sachs' donor-advised philanthropy fund in moving approximately $110 million of Singham's money.[1]

## Position on ESG and fossil fuels (September 2023)

In September 2023, Solomon stood firm in his support for maintaining Goldman Sachs' relationships with fossil fuel companies, declining calls to sever those ties.[2] "Traditional energy companies are hugely important to the global economy, they are hugely important to Goldman Sachs. We are all going to continue to finance traditional companies for a long time," he said, adding that "we recognize there needs to be a transition over time but that transition is going to take time."[2] The remarks came as the firm's earnings were under close scrutiny and as [[esg|ESG]] investing faced mounting criticism, including a $25 million [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] fine against a Deutsche Bank subsidiary over ESG misstatements.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the statement should not be read as a genuine break with ESG, noting that Goldman Sachs had itself been fined millions by the SEC in 2022 for misreporting and mismanaging its ESG investment vehicles, and that Solomon and other firm leaders had since framed the ESG transformation as requiring "common sense" while keeping the transformation itself as the goal.[2] She cited a market-sizing study naming [[blackrock|BlackRock]] and Goldman Sachs among the top contributors to ESG's projected growth through 2030, and suggested Goldman needed fossil fuel revenue to subsidize its green agenda.[2] She likened the posture to that of [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]], who had earlier appeared to rebuke ESG's impact on business performance.[2]
