# Club for Growth

The Club for Growth is an American conservative political advocacy and fundraising organization that backs Republican candidates aligned with free-market and free-trade economics. It became a target of public criticism from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in January 2023.[1]

## Trump's "globalist group" attack

Shortly after midnight on Monday, January 30, 2023, Trump posted on TruthSocial calling the organization "The Club For No Growth," describing it as "a GLOBALIST group that I have been taking to the cleaners for years."[1] He said he had worked with the group for a period of time but that it "couldn't get away from China, Europe, Asia, and part's unknown," contrasting its orientation with his own position that he is "America First all the way."[1]

In the same post Trump extended the charge to Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], reviving the nickname "Ron DeSanctimonious" he had coined in November 2022 and asserting that DeSantis "is also a Globalist, & so are his donors."[1] Trump added that [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] — whom he labelled "Low Energy" — had been standing next to DeSantis the previous week, telling readers to "Check PAST!"[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] read the episode as possibly a deliberate manoeuvre rather than a genuine feud, suggesting the attack was designed to draw fake-news media attention onto a non-story about DeSantis's presumed 2024 ambitions, at a time when DeSantis had made no statement about running.[1]

## Ties to Win It Back PAC

The Club for Growth maintained close ties to [[win-it-back-pac|Win It Back]], a political action committee that spent heavily against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during the 2024 Republican primary.[2] Win It Back devoted more than $4 million to depressing Trump's support in Iowa and nearly $2 million more in South Carolina, but in a September 28, 2023 donor memo obtained by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] its head, [[david-mcintosh|David McIntosh]], conceded that after testing more than forty anti-Trump advertisements "all attempts to undermine his conservative credentials on specific issues were ineffective."[2] The disclosure was cited by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators as evidence that establishment-aligned donor networks were squandering resources against the party's dominant candidate.[2]
