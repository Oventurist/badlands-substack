# Burning Man

**Burning Man** is the annual festival held at Black Rock City in the Nevada desert.[1]

## Destruction of the "Orgy Dome" (August 2025)

Over the weekend of August 23–24, 2025, Burning Man's "Orgy Dome" — described as one of the festival's most notorious attractions — was blown away and wrecked after a massive windstorm swept through the Nevada campsite.[1] The venue, characterized as a sprawling, air-conditioned private tent outfitted with beds and mood lighting where consenting Burners could freely get together, was left ravaged by high winds during the storm in Black Rock City on the Saturday, as organizers shared on social media the following Monday.[1] "Our build team worked so hard this past week to *erect* our lovely space," the group posted on Instagram, accompanied by a video showing what little remained of the sand-swept structure.[1] The New York Post summarized the episode as "easy come, easy go," and the Badlands News Brief carried it as a bonus item, referencing it in the edition's title.[1]

## The 2023 "Mudpocalypse"

Over the Labor Day weekend of September 2–3, 2023, heavy rain turned the Black Rock playa to mud and trapped roughly 73,000 attendees in what ZeroHedge called a "toxic" Nevada lake bed, an episode dubbed the "Mudpocalypse."[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] declined to write a full commentary, saying the memes made themselves and that it was "okay to poke fun at 73,000 trendy intellectuals constantly bloviating about the dangers of Climate Change trapped in a mug pit due to a fundamental lack of understanding of how the world works."[2]

## Homicide and sexual assault arrests (2025)

Following the close of the 2025 festival, the Pershing County Sheriff's Office announced two arrests, including one man accused of sexual assault and another accused of firearms and drug trafficking offenses.[3] A female participant reported on Monday, September 1, that she had been sexually assaulted during the weeklong gathering in Nevada's Black Rock Desert.[3] "The victim cooperated with an interview and provided a very distinct description of the male suspect, which is sometimes very difficult to obtain at a Festival such as Burning Man," Sheriff Jerry Allen said, adding that a search of the remaining festival area began to locate the suspect.[3] Deputies arrested Alessandro Boshant, 34, of Southern California, on suspicion of sexual assault and burglary, with bail set at $640,000.[3] In a separate case, a traffic stop near the Esplanade for a permit violation turned up two firearms, large quantities of several illicit substances, and evidence of intent to distribute.[3]

Separately, authorities investigated the death of a man found "lying in a pool of blood" as thousands watched the wooden Man effigy begin to burn on August 30, 2025, treating it as a homicide committed between roughly 8 and 9:30 p.m. local time.[3] The event drew some 70,000 participants known as "Burners" to the Black Rock Desert to build an entire city from scratch over the week ending Labor Day weekend, and the sheriff's office appealed publicly for witnesses.[3] The festival ends with two large-scale burns on the two nights before Labor Day: a 100-foot-tall wooden Man effigy loaded with fireworks, followed by the burning of the "Temple."[3] The victim, found after a participant flagged down a sheriff's deputy and confirmed deceased by the sheriff's office and Bureau of Land Management rangers, remained unidentified as of September 1 and was described as a white male roughly 35 to 40 years old, about six feet tall and 200 pounds with short brown hair and facial hair.[3]

## "Is Burning Man Demonic?" and Badlands commentary

Days before the 2025 arrests, former vice-presidential candidate [[nicole-shanahan|Nicole Shanahan]] published an insider account of Burning Man from her perspective as a newly baptized Christian, titled "Is Burning Man Demonic?", which Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recommended in full.[3] Shanahan wrote that there are constant orgies, staggering drug consumption, frequent overdoses that rarely interrupt a party, ubiquitous nudity, and that sexual assault, rape and tragic, often preventable deaths occur at the festival, alongside open practice of magic, the summoning of spiritual entities as entertainment, self-proclaimed "healers," and occult symbols and ceremonies so frequent they become ingrained in daily life.[3] She recounted deaths across her years of attendance, including a young woman killed after being sucked into the undercarriage of an art car, a man who hurled himself into the fire, and a serious impalement injury during an amateur construction attempt, noting that organizers try to mitigate such tragedies but they recur annually.[3]

Ashe in America argued that the festival's dark side was less publicized before what Badlands calls the golden age because wealthy underwriters "handled" the stories and kept the public unaware, and contended that most people would not support what she characterized as a hedonistic and demonic ritual in which wealthy elites engage in literal sacrifice and extrajudicially determine acceptable levels of death, and would want elites who cover up murder and sexual assault imprisoned.[3]
