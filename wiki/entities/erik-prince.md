# Erik Prince

Erik Prince is the founder of the private military company Blackwater and a recurring figure in U.S. backchannel diplomacy with Venezuela.[1]

## Venezuela backchannel

Prince secretly traveled to Caracas in November 2019 to meet [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]]; mainstream outlets speculated at the time that the trip represented an attempt by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to establish a backchannel with the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] regime, despite the administration's public support for opposition leader Juan Guaidó.[1]

## 2026 intervention

In January 2026 Prince appeared on Steve Bannon's show to argue that the United States must not repeat the mistakes it made in Iraq, and that Delcy Rodríguez should be allowed to contract directly with whatever private military contractor might be needed to shore up Venezuelan security.[1] Within an hour or two of that appearance, Trump publicly invoked the same Iraq analogy when asked why he favored Rodríguez over [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]].[1]

## November 2019 Caracas meeting

In November 2019, Prince secretly traveled to Caracas to meet with [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] on behalf of President [[donald-trump|Trump]], a trip later reported by The Guardian as an "outreach" to the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] government.[2] Badlands commentary places this meeting immediately after the firing of [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] — who is credited with the sanctions on Rodríguez — and notes that Prince had previously offered to provide 5,000 mercenaries for a ground invasion of Venezuela.[2]

Writing in May 2026 after the Trump administration directed federal prosecutors to stand down on Rodríguez, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the secret Prince meeting as evidence that Rodríguez "was never a target" and that the earlier sanctions and investigation may have been misdirection.[2]

## Warning against a ground invasion of Iran

During the March 2026 escalation, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] recalled that a ground invasion was "the one thing that Erik Prince said we should never do," citing the warning in response to remarks by retired Israeli general [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] raising the prospect of Israeli or American ground forces entering [[iran|Iran]].[3]

## Commentary on the Iran war (March 2026)

Following the opening of major U.S. combat operations against [[iran|Iran]], Prince appeared on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s show on March 1, 2026 and advised President [[donald-trump|Trump]] never to contemplate the use of ground troops.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Prince's appearance "perfectly captured" his own initial reaction to news that the United States was at war with Iran.[4]

## The 2016 transition and the Trump Tower meeting

Badlands Media's [[purple-revolution|Purple Revolution]] analysis raises Prince, founder of the private military company [[blackwater|Blackwater]], as evidence that the incoming [[donald-trump|Trump]] team distrusted the US military.[5] Prince at first denied having any role in Trump's transition team, then later admitted to being present at the Trump Tower meeting with Donald Trump Jr. in August 2016.[5] The article asks why the Trump team would seek advice from someone in the private military space rather than from the US Military, and answers that the military may have been too compromised.[5]

It further notes that Trump appointed Prince's sister [[betsy-devos|Betsy DeVos]] as Secretary of Education and pardoned four former Blackwater employees convicted over a 2007 shooting in Iraq that killed 17 people, while observing that Blackwater's US government contracts declined sharply after Barack Obama took office.[5]

## CIA asset, Syria, and the "think double" reading

The Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" identifies Prince and [[blackwater|Blackwater]] as the "special contractors" that Q described as having shadowed Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] during his May 2013 trip into Syria, providing his security and escorting him into and out of the country at a time when the United States had no diplomatic relations with Damascus.[6]

Quoting Politico and a January 2010 Vanity Fair profile, the article recounts that Prince had for six years led what the magazine called a "double life": publicly serving as Blackwater's CEO and chairman while privately doing the CIA's bidding, helping craft, fund and execute operations ranging from inserting personnel into "denied areas" to assembling hit teams targeting al-Qaeda members and their allies.[6] Blackwater gleaned more than $1.5 billion in government contracts between 2001 and 2009, acting among other things as an overseas Praetorian guard for CIA and State Department officials.[6] Prince said afterward that he was "thrown under the bus," faced charges, and was targeted with lawsuits intended to bankrupt him — a pattern the author compares to the treatment of General Michael Flynn.[6]

Author [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] rejects the Vanity Fair "double life" as the meaning of Q's instruction to "think double," arguing instead that Prince had flipped: no longer working for the CIA, he had become a double agent handing over evidence of McCain's treason and one of [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s biggest supporters.[6] Because Blackwater shadowed McCain everywhere, the article treats Prince as a witness who knows the identity of every person in the disputed Syria photographs, including the man it identifies as [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]].[6]
