# Agha Hasan Abedi

Agha Hasan Abedi was the founder and head of the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]] (BCCI) and a close adviser to General [[muhammad-zia-ul-haq|Zia]] of [[pakistan|Pakistan]].[1]

## Role as intermediary between the CIA and Pakistan

Badlands Media, citing WhoWhatWhy, describes Abedi as one of the foreign figures with whom [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] Director [[william-casey|William Casey]] dealt directly and secretly, outside regular agency channels, while pursuing his own plans for [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] in the 1980s. Casey's Afghan initiatives aroused concern among the CIA's professional operatives and analysts, including his deputy directors Bobby Ray Inman and John McMahon, but he continued making high-level decisions about the Afghan campaign in secret meetings with foreigners.[1]

Abedi arranged repeated meetings between Casey and General Zia in Pakistan, as well as meetings with Saudi intelligence chiefs [[kamal-adham|Kamal Adham]] and [[turki-al-faisal|Prince Turki al-Faisal]] — both of whom were BCCI shareholders.[1]

## Significance

Through Abedi's bank, Badlands Media argues, Casey was able to conduct off-the-books operations including the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] arms deal and, on a far larger scale, support to the [[mujahideen|Afghan mujahideen]] resistance against the Soviet invaders. The article characterizes the relationship not as a mere backdoor arrangement with a bank but as a multi-billion-dollar backdoor operation with a criminal institution accused by its own insiders of global involvement with drug shipments, smuggled gold, stolen military secrets, assassinations, bribery, extortion, covert intelligence operations, and weapons deals.[1]
