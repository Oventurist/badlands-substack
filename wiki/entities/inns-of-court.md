# Inns of Court

The inns of court are the professional legal societies of London, of which the [[middle-temple|Middle Temple]] was one of four, and which Badlands Media identifies as a channel of continuing British legal influence over the United States after independence.[1]

## Description in Badlands Media

A February 16, 2023 Badlands Media essay describes an "inn" of the [[british-crown|Crown]] as, roughly in today's language, an institution that acts simultaneously as a law school and as a source of original law.[1] The essay states that the inns of court in England were responsible for creating much of what is recognized today as [[equity-jurisprudence|equity jurisprudence]], including the formation of chancery courts.[1]

## Significance for the American legal system

Because many jurists and attorneys working in early America were trained at the Middle Temple, the article argues that inn-derived doctrine entered American practice directly rather than being displaced by the Revolution.[1] It cites the [[delaware-court-of-chancery|Delaware Court of Chancery]], whose general equity jurisdiction is measured by that of the High Court of Chancery of Great Britain as it existed before the separation of the American colonies, as proof that eighteenth-century British equity law was imported into Delaware and operates there to this day.[1] The essay treats this continuity as one leg of its broader argument that the legal groundwork for [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] was laid by imported British law.[1]
