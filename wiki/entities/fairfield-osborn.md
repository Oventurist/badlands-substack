# Fairfield Osborn

Fairfield Osborn is described by Matthew Ehret as a leading British fascist who spoke at the 1932 [[third-eugenics-conference|Third Eugenics Conference]] in New York.[1]

In his address Osborn argued that [[eugenics|eugenics]] "aids and encourages the survival and multiplication of the fittest; indirectly, it would check and discourage the multiplication of the unfitted."[1] He complained that in the United States "there are millions of people who are acting as dragnets or sheet anchors on the progress of the ship of state," noting that unemployment fell mainly on "the less competent," and concluded that keeping such people alive in hope of future employment was "another instance of humane civilization going directly against the order of nature and encouraging the survival of the un-fittest."[1]
