# Guinea-Bissau

Guinea-Bissau is a West African state that became the focus of a regional constitutional crisis after its military seized power on November 26, 2025, three days after disputed elections.[1]

## The November 2025 coup

The coup followed elections in which ousted President Umaro Sissoco Embalo and his main challenger, Fernando Dias da Costa, both claimed outright victory. Soldiers swore in Major-General Horta Inta-a as interim president under a Military High Command installed to run the country, and later appointed Ilidio Vieira Te, a civil servant and former finance minister, as prime minister.[1]

## Transitional charter

In December 2025 the military government published a 29-article transitional charter laying out a 12-month framework culminating in presidential and legislative polls at the end of the period. Under the charter, a 65-member National Transition Council including ten senior military officers would act as a legislative body. The document also proposed sweeping legal and institutional changes, including revisions to the suspended constitution and changes to electoral bodies.[1]

## Regional response

[[ecowas|ECOWAS]], West Africa's main political and economic bloc, rejected the proposed transition roadmap, demanded a swift return to constitutional rule, and warned that it could impose sanctions.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the episode as a continuation of the regional "revolt against ECOWAS," noting that [[nigeria|Nigeria]] heads the bloc and is therefore where the power controlling the region resides — a point he connected to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s recent threat to enter Nigeria "guns-a-blazing."[1]
