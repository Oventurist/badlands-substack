# Scott Green

Scott Green is a Washington lobbyist with close ties to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] who, according to a 2020 [[politico|Politico]] investigation summarized in Badlands Media commentary, purchased Virgin Islands property from [[james-biden|James Biden]] and later extended him a private mortgage.[1]

## Career

After working as a probation officer following college, Green accepted an offer from Biden in the early 1980s for a job on the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], where he worked for a decade in roles including staff assistant and senior advisor.[1] He then moved to the private sector, co-founding the Lafayette Group in 1994 — a lobbying and consulting firm focused on law enforcement and national security, later headed by his son.[1]

Government contracts for niche support services to law enforcement and national security agencies were central to the firm's business.[1] In the Senate, Biden over the years proposed appropriations for many of the niche areas in which the firm specialized, including "interoperable communications" for first responders and "fusion centers" for processing threat-related intelligence.[1] From 2012 to 2018 the Lafayette Group received contracts tied to at least one government program championed by Biden, the Nationwide Public Safety Broadband Network, taking in more than $10 million in contracts for work related to the network now called FirstNet.[1]

Another of Green's lobbying clients, the non-profit Drug Abuse Resistance Education program (D.A.R.E.), benefited from Biden's support across more than two decades, both before and after Green's land purchase; Biden ensured its inclusion in the [[crime-bill-1994|1994 Crime Bill]], making it eligible to compete for hundreds of millions of dollars in federal funds.[1]

## Water Island land deal

In 2005 James Biden bought an acre on [[water-island|Water Island]] for $150,000 and split it into three parcels; the next year Green bought one parcel for what the entire property had cost.[1] Green subsequently provided James Biden a mortgage loan on the remaining parcels.[1] It is not clear why Green paid the price he did, or why James Biden sought a loan from a lobbyist rather than a bank, and the loan terms were not disclosed in property records.[1] Badlands writer [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cites the arrangement as evidence of a broader pattern of Biden-family financial dealings with parties interested in influencing the future vice president.[1]
