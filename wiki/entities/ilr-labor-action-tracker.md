# ILR Labor Action Tracker

The ILR Labor Action Tracker is a labor-dispute data project housed at Cornell University's School of Industrial and Labor Relations, directed at the time of reporting by Johnnie Kallas, a PhD candidate at the school.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Role in reporting on the 2023 strike wave

The Tracker's figures supplied the quantitative backbone for coverage of the 2023 [[summer-of-strikes-2023|Summer of Strikes]]. As relayed via CNBC, its data recorded 312 strikes involving roughly 453,000 workers in 2023 to that point, against 180 strikes involving 43,700 workers over the same period two years earlier — a comparison that establishes both a modest rise in the number of disputes and a roughly tenfold increase in the number of workers involved.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Those numbers are what allow the year's labor unrest to be characterized as unprecedented in recent memory rather than merely noisy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Significance

By aggregating actions across industries — from small workplaces of dozens to healthcare systems mobilizing tens of thousands — the Tracker provides the basis for treating disparate walkouts at [[amazon|Amazon]], [[starbucks|Starbucks]], [[kaiser-permanente|Kaiser Permanente]], the [[writers-guild-of-america|Writers Guild of America]], and the [[united-auto-workers|United Auto Workers]] as a single phenomenon rather than as unrelated disputes.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)
