# Andrew McCabe

Andrew McCabe is a former [[fbi|FBI]] deputy director who moved into a media career as a [[cnn|CNN]] commentator, and who in May 2023 publicly linked the [[sai-varshith-kandula|U-Haul incident]] at the [[white-house|White House]] to the January 6 Capitol riot.[1]

## The U-Haul commentary

Following the May 22, 2023 incident in which a U-Haul truck struck White House barricades, McCabe said on CNN: "And I think you have to draw a line from this apparent attack on the White House by someone bearing a Nazi flag to at least some of the people, it's hard to say how many, but some of the people involved in the January 6th attack on the Capitol."[1]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] singled out the remark, emphasizing that it was "that Andrew McCabe" and that he "did use his new career in media to link the alleged brown white supremacist U-Haul driver to January 6 defendants," concluding that "FBI psy-op habits die hard."[1] Her broader argument was that the subsequent downgrading of Kandula's charges to a single count of depredation of federal property undercut the narrative McCabe had advanced.[1]

## Commentary on the Mohn case

As a [[cnn|CNN]] law enforcement analyst and former [[fbi|FBI]] deputy director, McCabe commented on the late-January 2024 arrest of [[justin-mohn|Justin Mohn]], who was charged with murder after posting a video displaying his decapitated father's head with a political rant.[3] McCabe said the "bigger picture is extremely concerning" as the country moved into what he expected to be an incredibly heated political season.[3]

## The criminal inquiry into Jeff Sessions

The July 2025 Badlands article "Shall We Play a Game?" by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] devotes extended attention to McCabe's role in the Russiagate period.[5] Citing Business Insider's account of ABC News reporting, the article states that as FBI deputy director McCabe oversaw a criminal inquiry into Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] over whether Sessions "lacked candor" in testifying to Congress about his contacts with Russian officials.[5] Sessions had told his January 2017 confirmation hearing that he did not discuss the 2016 election with any Russian official; the Washington Post later reported that he had met twice with Russian ambassador Sergey Kislyak during the campaign and transition, meetings Sessions acknowledged while maintaining they did not touch the election.[5] Lange argues the contacts were not illegal and that the leak was engineered to remove Sessions from the board.[5]

The inquiry was authorised after Democratic senators Patrick Leahy and Al Franken asked the FBI to investigate Sessions's Russian contacts, and McCabe and Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]] are reported to have briefed lawmakers on it in a classified setting.[5] Lange emphasises that McCabe and Rosenstein were thereby secretly investigating their own superior, and that Sessions was reportedly unaware of the inquiry when he fired McCabe.[5]

## Firing, "lack of candor," and the treason framing

McCabe was forced out of the FBI amid an [[department-of-justice-office-of-inspector-general|Office of the Inspector General]] investigation into his approval of unauthorised media disclosures in October 2016 relating to the bureau's [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] email probe; Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]] concluded he had not been forthcoming, and Sessions cited OIG and FBI OPR findings that McCabe had made an unauthorised disclosure and "lacked candor — including under oath — on multiple occasions."[5] Lange presents this as a boomerang: Sessions dismissed McCabe for the very offence McCabe had investigated him over.[5] McCabe responded with a Washington Post op-ed denying any lack of candor and denying that he had knowingly misled investigators, a denial Lange links to 18 U.S. Code § 2385, quoted in Q drop 3638, which criminalises "knowingly" advocating the overthrow of the government.[5]

The article further characterises McCabe as a Clinton-aligned figure, noting that political groups controlled by Clinton ally [[terry-mcauliffe|Terry McAuliffe]] gave close to $700,000 to his wife's unsuccessful run for the Virginia legislature, and that colleagues accused him of shutting down the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] investigation.[5] Q drop 4777 of September 28, 2020 is cited for the prompt "Think McCabe's wife. She wasn't supposed to lose."[5] Former FBI general counsel [[james-baker-fbi|James Baker]] later told Congress that McCabe had been serious about secretly recording President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and using the tapes to remove him.[5]

## The obstruction-of-justice witness plan

In "Setting the Stage," Joe Lange identifies McCabe as the architect of the enemy's original plan to charge Trump with obstruction of justice.[6] VOX reported that shortly after the appointment of special counsel [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] in May 2017, McCabe — then acting FBI director — told several of the bureau's highest-ranking managers that they should consider themselves possible witnesses in any investigation into whether Trump had obstructed justice.[6] McCabe told colleagues he too was a potential witness in the probe of whether Trump broke the law by trying to thwart the Russia investigation.[6]

A senior law enforcement official quoted in the same report described the intended effect: the former FBI director would testify, followed by the acting director, the chief of staff to the FBI director, the bureau's general counsel and others in succession, so that the confrontation would read not as Trump's word against [[james-comey|Comey]]'s but as "the Federal Bureau of Investigation versus Donald Trump."[6] Lange argues that this planned narrative collapsed for three reasons: [[david-bowdich|David Bowdich]] was a plant inside Comey's FBI holding evidence of the conspiracy, Trump held the power to [[declas|declassify]], and Mueller had already cut a deal.[6] McCabe stepped down as deputy director in January 2018 amid allegations of bias and misconduct over the Clinton email investigation, and was replaced by Bowdich.[6]

## The "special" and the Clinton email investigation

"[[the-biggest-boomerang-in-history|The Biggest Boomerang In History]]" lists McCabe alongside [[james-comey|James Comey]] as leadership of the [[fbi|FBI]] arm of the conspiracy [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] describes, and notes that neither recused himself despite what Lange calls an obvious conflict of interest in investigating the Clintons.[7]

Reporting in The Hill quoted in the article records that lawmakers on the House Judiciary Committee attending a December 21 closed-door briefing by McCabe, then FBI Deputy Director, say he confirmed that the investigation and charging decisions in the [[hillary-clinton-private-email-server|Clinton email server]] case were controlled by a small group at Washington headquarters rather than by the field office where the conduct occurred — the server being based in New York.[7] In normal practice the relevant field office investigates and recommends charging decisions; here the hierarchy controlled both from Washington, an arrangement known in bureau parlance as a "special."[7]

Lange presents this centralisation as the reason FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] is decentralising power away from the Washington headquarters and returning employees to field offices around the country, so as to take politics out of the bureau.[7] He promised further treatment of McCabe in a subsequent article.[7] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.

## Uranium One supervision

Documents cited in "The Biggest Cover-up in American History" indicate that the FBI's investigation of [[vadim-mikerin|Vadim Mikerin]] and the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]] was supervised by then-Assistant FBI Director McCabe alongside then-U.S. Attorney [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]].[8] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] highlights that McCabe, who he says later tried to trap and frame [[donald-trump|Trump]] using the [[steele-dossier|Dossier]] and FISA warrants, was thus already embedded in the earlier scandal — which he presents as evidence that the collusion narrative was a cover-up by the same officials rather than a coincidence.[8]
