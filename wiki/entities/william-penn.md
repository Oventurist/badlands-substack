# William Penn

William Penn (1644–1718) was the founder of the Pennsylvania Colony and, in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], one of the key leaders of the Leibniz and Jonathan Swift networks in America.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The grandfather of [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]] was a close friend of Penn, and West himself came from Swarthmore, Pennsylvania.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Development rather than extraction

Having acquired a large territory, Penn committed himself not to wealth extraction as the British Empire expected, but to developing the culture, economy and population of the colony along the lines earlier set forth by John Winthrop Jr and John Winthrop.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The historian [[graham-lowry|Graham Lowry]] treats Penn's work at length in *How the Nation Was Won*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## The 1682 treaty

Penn held sole dictatorial authority over Pennsylvania under the Crown Charter and was under no obligation to sign a treaty with, or pay anything to, the Delaware natives; he could have invoked the "might makes right" principle of empire as had been done many times before and since.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Instead he negotiated the unprecedented [[penn-treaty-of-1682|Penn Treaty of 1682]], paying the Delaware people in a variety of ways and guaranteeing them total freedom to use the land as they saw fit even after European settlement.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The treaty was renowned as one of the only such agreements to endure and was honoured for many generations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) West's 1771 painting commemorates the respectful negotiations preceding the signing, with textiles and goods rather than alcohol or guns being offered beneath the famous cedar tree.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
