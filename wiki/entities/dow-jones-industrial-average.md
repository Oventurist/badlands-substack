# Dow Jones Industrial Average

The Dow Jones Industrial Average (commonly "the DOW") is a United States stock index comprising the 30 largest publicly traded companies in America.[1] "Publicly traded" means that shares of the company can be bought on a stock market exchange, and that the stockholders own the company.[1]

## Role as an economic indicator

In a February 2023 Badlands Media commentary, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used the DOW as the starting point for a broader argument that stock indices are poor proxies for the health of the American economy.[1] Because the DOW tracks only 30 firms, it is a narrower and less representative gauge than the [[sp-500|S&P 500]], which covers the 500 largest publicly traded companies and can therefore be inferred to be a better overall representation of the stock market.[1]

Carlson's central claim is that indices such as the DOW, the S&P 500 and the [[nasdaq|NASDAQ]] together describe roughly 0.1% of American businesses, since most US companies are private and small businesses made up about 99.9% of US businesses in 2022.[1]
