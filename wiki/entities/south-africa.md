# South Africa

South Africa appears in the article as a recurring node in the sanctions-evasion economy described by the author, first as an apartheid-era pariah state buying embargoed oil and later as the original corporate home of the mining company [[uranium-one|Uranium One]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Apartheid-era sanctions and Marc Rich

Under apartheid, South Africa was subject to international sanctions, a status the article says made it a natural customer for the commodities trader [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Rich bought Iranian oil in 1979, while [[iran|Iran]] was under an oil embargo tied to the American hostage crisis, and sold that oil on to sanctioned South Africa, pocketing the spread between the depressed purchase price and the premium a cut-off buyer would pay.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Washington Post is quoted listing apartheid-era South Africa among the compendium of rogue states Rich traded with.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Citing ForeignPolicy.com, the article notes that Rich pioneered commodity swaps, specifically the uranium-for-oil deals he brokered in the 1980s between apartheid South Africa, Islamic Iran and Soviet Russia.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) This makes South Africa a source as well as a destination in the author's account of how uranium reached nuclear programs in sanctioned states.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Uranium One's South African origin

In 2007, [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy]] — the company [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]] had built on Kazakh uranium stakes — merged with Uranium One, described by the New York Times as a South African company with assets in Africa and Australia, in a transaction valued at about $3.5 billion.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The merged entity retained the Uranium One name and was controlled by UrAsia investors including [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The article treats Uranium One's South African origin as a "key data point," asking whether it is coincidence that Marc Rich had deep ties to commodity trading in South Africa and expertise in uranium-for-oil swaps, and answering that it is not.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Trump–Ramaphosa White House meeting (May 2025)

On May 21, 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] confronted President [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]] at the [[white-house|White House]] with a video montage alleging discrimination and violence against white farmers, after a journalist asked what would convince Trump that there is "no white genocide in South Africa."[2] The footage included clips of South African opposition figures making inflammatory remarks and images purportedly showing the graves of white farmers.[2]

Badlands commentary highlighted the dynamic of South Africa simultaneously serving as the petitioner at the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and the Israeli government over Gaza while standing accused of atrocities at home, and noted the country's [[brics|BRICS]] membership and deepening alignment with Russia.[2] The same commentary described South Africa as a longtime hub of regime and regime-change activity, calling its current geopolitical alignment an enigma.[2]
