# Mouaz Moustafa

Mouaz Moustafa (rendered "Mouza Moustafa" in parts of the article) is a Washington, D.C. lobbyist who headed advocacy organisations for both the Libyan and Syrian insurgencies. The Badlands account presents him as the connective tissue between [[congress|Congress]]'s appropriations and the armed groups the United States was nominally at war with.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Background

Moustafa was born and raised in Damascus, Syria, before moving to the United States as a teenager.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He served as Executive Director of the Syrian Emergency Task Force (SETF), Political Director of United For a Free Syria (UFS), and a board member of the Coalition for a Democratic Syria (CDS).[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) During the Libyan revolution he was Executive Director of the Libyan Council of North America (LCNA) and Political Director of the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] (LETF), working closely with top officials of the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Syria and ISIS

The article characterises the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] — ostensibly a lobbying group raising money for a Syrian rebel army fighting [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] — as in reality a lobbying group for [[isis|ISIS]], through which Congress channelled taxpayer money to the organisation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Moustafa is described as the go-between linking [[john-mccain|Senator John McCain]] to ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi, and as the man who chaperoned McCain's 2013 trip into Syria to meet ISIS leaders, with the full approval of President [[barack-obama|Obama]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## The Libyan precedent

The article stresses that Syria was not the first instance of this arrangement: before lobbying for ISIS, Moustafa ran the similarly named Libyan Emergency Task Force on behalf of the armed groups seeking to overthrow [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Both he and McCain were heavily involved in the Libyan campaign two years before turning to Assad — described as "a similar name, and the same playbook."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article's broader claim is that such [[lobbying|lobbying]] groups are established by the enemy specifically to extract and redirect American taxpayer money to the groups the United States is fighting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Resurfacing during the 2024 Syrian offensive

Moustafa's name resurfaced in December 2024 amid the [[2024-hts-aleppo-offensive|HTS offensive]] against [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], after a Max Blumenthal post was cited in ZeroHedge coverage of Russian accusations that Ukraine was backing [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]].[2] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described Moustafa as a name that brings the narrative full circle, identifying him as both a [[cnn|CNN]] correspondent and the field guide for the late Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] during his 2013 trip to Syria to meet with [[isis|ISIS]] and help coordinate support for their fight.[2]

The commentary pointed to a 2013 ABC News segment by George Stephanopoulos and to photographs from the trip in which Moustafa is seated to McCain's left while [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]] — the ISIS leader later killed in a 2019 raid involving the military dog Conan, ordered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — is visible in the background.[2] After al-Baghdadi's death, the commentary noted, CNN hosted Moustafa to lament the killing and to call Assad a puppet of [[vladimir-putin|Putin]].[2] The writer argued the episode showed that mainstream media had been straightforward all along about its support for ISIS, and treated the fawning coverage of McCain by his purported political rivals as an illustration of [[uniparty|Uniparty]] dynamics.[2]
