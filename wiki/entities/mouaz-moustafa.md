# Mouaz Moustafa

Mouaz Moustafa (rendered "Mouza Moustafa" in the article) is a Washington, D.C. lobbyist presented by Badlands Media as the connective tissue between U.S. politicians and the armed groups the United States funded in Libya and Syria.[1]

## Background

According to a biography at LPR.com, Moustafa was born and raised in Damascus, Syria before moving to the United States as a teenager.[1] He is Executive Director of the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|SETF]], Political Director of United For a Free Syria (UFS), and a board member of the Coalition for a Democratic Syria (CDS).[1] He also worked with the Libyan Revolution, during which he served as Executive Director of the Libyan Council of North America (LCNA), Political Director of the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] (LETF), and worked closely with top officials within the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]].[1]

## Alleged role

The article asserts that the Syrian Emergency Task Force was in reality a lobbying group for [[isis|ISIS]], and that Moustafa acted as the go-between for Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi, chaperoning McCain into Syria in 2013 to meet ISIS leaders.[1] Before that, it argues, he was a lobbyist for the Libyan insurgents seeking to overthrow [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] under the near-identical Libyan Emergency Task Force banner — "a similar name, and the same playbook."[1] The article uses this pairing to argue that lobbying is one of the principal mechanisms by which enemies of the United States extract taxpayer money, with [[congress|Congress]] appropriating funds that reach the groups it is nominally at war with.[1]
