# Jesuits

The Society of Jesus, or Jesuits, appears in the Badlands Media essay [[a-new-american-renaissance|A New American Renaissance]] as the principal adversary of the American republican networks in colonial North America. [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes the Jesuits as the effective controllers of the French territories of the Americas, credited with doing "the most work in organizing a lot of alliances with different native bands" and with "an immense amount of damage manipulating these groups against each other, and sometimes against colonists."

The article argues that the great danger for English republicans before the Battle of Quebec was that the [[iroquois-confederacy|Mohawks and Iroquois]] would ally with the Jesuit-run French; it credits [[william-johnson|William Johnson]] with reversing that outcome and thereby tipping the battle. In discussing the [[penn-treaty-of-1682|Penn Treaty of 1682]], Ehret reads its fourth article — forbidding either side from believing false rumors about the other — as an explicit countermeasure to "Jesuitical intelligence operations" that spread slander and gossip to provoke violence between settlers and natives, and that he claims deployed manipulated native groups from Canada and Quebec to conduct massacres.

The essay cites three supporting sources for this reading: [[cynthia-chung|Cynthia Chung]]'s *The Shaping of a World Religion: From Jesuits, Freemasons and Anthropologists to the Ghost Dance Religion*; Friedrich Schiller's 1792 study *The Jesuit Government in Paraguay*; and [[samuel-morse|Samuel F. B. Morse]]'s 1841 *Foreign Conspiracy Against the Liberties of the United States*, which described Prince Metternich's Holy Alliance deploying "hundreds of Jesuits and priests... who have a complete military organization through the United States" in order to undo the American Revolution.
