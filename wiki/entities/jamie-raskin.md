# Jamie Raskin

Jamie Raskin is a Democratic member of the U.S. House of Representatives from Maryland and a prominent voice on oversight matters.[1] Following the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s release of more than 3 million pages of previously undisclosed [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] investigative files in early 2026, Raskin described the process as a potential cover-up.[1]

He pointed to over 10,000 redactions in the released material and to the halving of released pages relative to the more than 6 million pages initially identified as responsive, with the remainder deemed duplicates, non-responsive, or withheld.[1] His criticism paralleled that of victims' advocates and attorneys, who argued the release failed to address key questions including Epstein's lenient 2007 plea deal, potential immunity arrangements, and government failures in investigating decades of abuse.[1] The release had been framed by Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] as the final phase of compliance with the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]].[1]

## Response to the 2024 Trump ballot ruling

In March 2024, following the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]]'s unanimous decision that Colorado could not disqualify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from its primary ballot, Raskin — then ranking member of the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] — told Axios he was already drafting legislation in response, saying "Congress will have to try and act" and "I'm working on it — today."[2] The ruling had held that only Congress may enforce the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]]'s Section 3 insurrection bar with respect to federal office.[2]

Raskin, a former member of the January 6 select committee, pointed to a 2022 bill he had introduced with Rep. [[debbie-wasserman-schultz|Debbie Wasserman Schultz]] creating a pathway for the Justice Department to sue to keep candidates off the ballot under the Fourteenth Amendment, saying "We are going to revise it in light of the Supreme Court's decision."[2] He suggested pairing the bill with a concurrent resolution formally declaring January 6 an "insurrection" and those involved to have "engaged in insurrection."[2] Axios noted the measure faced almost certain stonewalling in a House where even moderate swing-district Republicans such as Rep. [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] cheered the ruling.[2]

Badlands commentary characterized the effort — described as a joint push with Rep. [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] — as a coping mechanism unlikely to reach the floor under Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[2]

## February 2025: Remarks on Elon Musk

In February 2025 Raskin drew criticism from Badlands commentators over remarks about [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] made amid a wave of threats and vandalism directed at [[tesla|Tesla]] owners and dealerships, including notes left on vehicles in Arcata, California by a group calling itself [[students-against-nazi-extremism|Students Against Nazi Extremism]].[3] The hosts argued that Raskin was being overtly xenophobic about a successful legal immigrant and that his rhetoric amounted to dog-whistling supporters toward further acts of violence against Tesla and possibly Musk himself; they also described him as appearing demoralized and defeated in the clip in question.[3]
