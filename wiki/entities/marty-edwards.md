# Marty Edwards

Marty Edwards is the Deputy Chief Technology Officer for [[operational-technology|Operational Technology]] (OT) and the [[internet-of-things|Internet of Things]] (IoT) at [[tenable|Tenable]], a cybersecurity exposure management company.[1] He testified at the February 6, 2024 hearing of the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Committee on Homeland Security]]'s Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee on the water sector.[1]

## Background

At Tenable, Edwards works on "furthering government and industry initiatives to improve critical infrastructure security."[1] In 2022 he served as a working group lead in the [[biden-administration|Biden administration]]'s National Security Telecommunications Advisory Committee (NSTAC).[1] Earlier he was a program manager focused on cybersecurity at the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]]'s [[idaho-national-laboratory|Idaho National Laboratory]], and he was the last and longest-serving director of the Department of Homeland Security's [[ics-cert|Industrial Control Systems Cyber Emergency Response Team]] (ICS-CERT), a function later absorbed into the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]].[1]

## Testimony

Edwards's nightmare scenario centered on hardware monoculture across sectors. He noted that programmable logic controllers and similar OT devices are reused widely: "you have the same box in a water treatment plant that you do in an electrical substation that you do in a manufacturing plant."[1] His concern was "some type of malware or ransomware that holds all of those devices hostage or makes them inoperable," compounded by the fact that "we just simply do not have the supply chain capacity to replace all of them in any reasonable amount of time."[1] The Badlands account used this observation as the hinge of its broader argument that the vulnerabilities exposed in water apply equally to energy, banking, communications, and elections.[1]
