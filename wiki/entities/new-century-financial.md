# New Century Financial

**New Century Financial** was an American mortgage lender that in March 2007 was the country's second-largest subprime lender when it collapsed.[1] Its failure is widely recalled as the catalyst that ushered in the global financial crisis, leading within a year to the collapse of Bear Stearns and eventually of Lehman Brothers.[1]

## Invoked as a 2023 benchmark

In April 2023 the company's name resurfaced as a benchmark for stress in the American banking system.[1] Analysis of the Federal Reserve's H.8 statement showed the biggest drop on record in bank loans and leases in the final two weeks of March 2023, and a breakdown of small-bank lending by subcomponent revealed that while the plunge in commercial and industrial loans had moderated to $6.9 billion from $25 billion the week before, the largest decline had shifted to real estate loans — one of the anchor pillars of the small-bank sector.[1] Small banks recorded an $18.7 billion decline in real estate loans in the latest week, following a $19.2 billion drop the week before, for a combined $37.8 billion plunge in the second half of March.[1] That figure was reported as the biggest since the collapse of New Century Financial in March 2007, a comparison drawn as [[morgan-stanley|Morgan Stanley]] projected 40% downside in commercial real estate.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] sponsor and financial analyst [[kirk-elliott|Kirk Elliott]] responded that "we've seen structural problems in our global financial system before, and now those same symptoms are beginning to present in our current crisis," adding that while mainstream economists argued over whether a recession had arrived, "the wise are preparing for a cascade of economic disasters from banking failures to world wars."[1]
