# Newsweek

Newsweek is an American news magazine. In October 2023 it published an exclusive report, referenced in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], stating that the [[fbi|FBI]] was targeting supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the 2024 election neared.[1]

The report described the Bureau's creation of a threat category known as [[anti-government-anti-authority-violent-extremism|anti-government and anti-authority violent extremism]], covering individuals deemed a threat who do not fall under anarchist, militia, or sovereign citizen groupings, and cited more than a dozen current and former anonymous government officials saying the program often investigates Trump supporters.[1] The FBI issued a statement to news outlets calling the report false and denying that it targets individuals solely for their political beliefs.[1]

## Coverage of Tim Walz's military service (August 2024)

In August 2024, following [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s selection of [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] as her running mate, Newsweek published a combat veteran's opinion essay arguing that Walz's departure from the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] before his battalion's Iraq deployment was "unforgivable."[2] The piece, excerpted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], stated that Walz served 24 years as an enlisted soldier, deployed to Italy and Norway, and retired after learning his battalion would deploy to Iraq despite assuring his troops he would join them.[2] It further alleged that Walz left before completing the schooling required to hold the rank of command sergeant major while continuing to use the title in official biographies.[2] The essay is discussed further at [[tim-walz-military-service-controversy|the Tim Walz military service controversy]].[2]

## Canada favorability coverage (2025)

In late October 2025, Newsweek published an article reporting that polls showed Americans viewing Canada more favorably than President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], amid an intensifying feud between Trump and Canadian political leaders.[3] The piece noted that the US and Canada, sharing a border more than 5,000 miles long, had been key allies for decades but that the relationship had strained following Trump's tariffs and remarks about annexing Canada as the 51st state, and that favorable attitudes toward Canada had remained high and bipartisan while Trump's favorability was more sharply divided along partisan lines.[3]

Badlands commentary criticized the article for going six paragraphs before citing a specific poll — a [[gallup|Gallup]] survey from the previous February finding 89 percent of Americans felt warmly toward Canada against 10 percent unfavorable — and for later quoting a CNN polling analyst.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the piece as unexpectedly obvious Canadian propaganda.[3]

## June 2024 coverage and Badlands criticism

In June 2024 Newsweek published several stories that drew sustained criticism in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[4] One reported that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had suffered an "abysmal polling week," citing three surveys showing him behind [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] — including a Fox News poll of 1,095 registered voters giving Biden 50 percent to Trump's 48, a three-point shift from May and the first time since October that Biden had led in a Fox News survey.[4] Commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the piece admitted in its eleventh paragraph that other polls showed Trump leading, and that the outlet contacted the Trump campaign outside business hours, which she read as an attempt to avoid a response.[4]

Newsweek also carried the report on Harvard professor [[matthew-bunn|Matthew Bunn]]'s warning that the risk of nuclear war had not been so high since the Cuban Missile Crisis, and a companion story on China using state-sanctioned hacking contests to recruit young hackers for cyberattacks on the United States.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the pairing as a coordinated narrative deployment intended to prime audiences to expect a nuclear or infrastructure crisis.[4]
