# Nancy Mace

**Nancy Mace** is a Republican member of Congress from South Carolina who ran unsuccessfully for governor of the state in 2026. Badlands coverage treats her as an archetype of the nominally MAGA-aligned politician whose loyalty collapsed once President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] withdrew his support.[1]

## Badlands assessment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that years earlier Mace had done "a bunch of shady things" demonstrating she could not be trusted and was not a true populist leader, describing her as recruited to lure men back into the Uniparty.[1] He singled out a congressional hearing she convened in 2025 in which she said a nude video of her had been leaked by her ex-fiancé, and then displayed enlarged printed images of her nude silhouette on boards, with a yellow circle placed around her body so the audience could easily find it.[1]

Her ex-fiancé Patrick Bryant categorically denied her claims and promised to take steps to clear his name, while the four other men she identified in the hearing and accused of spreading the video filed a defamation lawsuit against her to prove their innocence.[1] The Brief added that Mace's social media activity consisted largely of selfies with platitudes about fighting corruption in Washington.[1]

## 2026 gubernatorial primary

Trump endorsed one of Mace's primary opponents in the six-candidate field for South Carolina governor.[1] Mace finished last with 12% of the vote and failed to advance to the run-off scheduled for June 23.[1] She then took to X vowing to seek revenge against Trump for "ending her political career," and later held a press conference endorsing [[alan-wilson|Alan Wilson]] in the run-off, saying she wanted to help him defeat Trump-endorsed Lieutenant Governor [[pamela-evette|Pamela Evette]].[1]

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry cited the speed of her turn against Trump and MAGA — "after spending years grifting off of Trump's name and policy platform" — as justification for Trump's disruptive behaviour, which he characterised as deliberately breaking the minds of rivals who had anointed themselves his successors.[1]

## Epstein files document review (2026)

In February 2026 Mace joined the congressional pressure campaign over the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]]'s production under the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]].[2] In a Facebook post written after reviewing documents at the department, she said: "Many of the co-conspirator documents are missing or still redacted. I was unable to view critical information. Memos and attachments drafted by the DOJ on potential co-conspirators remain hidden. The excuse? This is how they received the data."[2] She added that "these girls were young. They were groomed. Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell are p*dophiles. The American people deserve every page. Every name. And every truth the government is holding onto. I'll be back tomorrow for more."[2] Her intervention paralleled criticism from Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]], who said he lacked confidence in Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]].[2]

## Opposition to ground troops in Iran (2026)

As the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]] escalated in 2026, Mace was among Republican lawmakers raising concerns about its trajectory, warning against deploying U.S. ground troops in [[iran|Iran]] and cautioning that prolonged uncertainty could weaken support among both [[congress|Congress]] and the public.[3]

## Settlement disclosure vote (2026)

On June 30, 2026, Mace was the sole member not voting yes on Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]]'s resolution requiring disclosure of House members who used taxpayer funds to settle sexual misconduct allegations, casting a "present" vote in the 420-0 tally.[4] She described the timing as "political theater," noting that her earlier efforts had already compelled related materials through subpoena.[4]

## Confrontation with Hunter Biden (January 2024)

When [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] made a surprise appearance at his [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] hearing on January 10, 2024, Mace tore into the president's son, asking "who bribed Hunter Biden to be here?"[5] She continued: "You are the epitome of white privilege. Coming in to the Oversight Committee, spitting in our face, ignoring a congressional subpoena to be deposed. What are you afraid of? You have no balls to come up here," and added that she thought Hunter Biden "should be arrested right here, right now, and go straight to jail."[5] The exchange came as the Oversight and Judiciary Committees moved to hold him in contempt for skipping a closed-door deposition tied to the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[5]

## ABC defamation settlement fallout (December 2024)

Mace was the interviewee in the March 10, 2024 edition of ABC's *This Week* that gave rise to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s defamation suit against [[george-stephanopoulos|George Stephanopoulos]] and [[abc-news|ABC News]].[6] After playing a clip of Mace discussing having been a victim of rape, Stephanopoulos asked how she squared her endorsement of Trump with that testimony, asserting that "judges and two separate juries have found him liable for rape and for defaming the victim of that rape" — a claim he repeated ten times despite the jury in the [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] case having found Trump liable for sexual abuse rather than rape.[6] The suit settled in December 2024 with ABC agreeing to pay $15 million to a future presidential foundation and museum plus $1 million in attorney fees, along with a published statement of regret naming the Mace interview.[6]
