# 118th Congress

The **118th Congress** convened in January 2023 with a narrow Republican majority in the House of Representatives, and its organization — above all the protracted contest for the speakership — became a central example in Badlands Media contributor [[jb-white|J.B. White]]'s "The Schedule F Imperative - Part 2" (March 13, 2023).[1]

## The speakership fight

White argued that the proceedings of the 118th Congress carried "exponential strategery" intimately associated with what he called the Schedule F Imperative.[1] He wrote that all that was required of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] before the Congress organized was verification of his aspiration to be Speaker, a certain level of decency and love for America, and a willingness not to attack the 45th president.[1] In White's telling, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] tested McCarthy through loyal Republican House members who "proved their mettle" before a watching nation, and McCarthy passed "the man test, the patriot test, and the Speakership test" — which required him to fail what White called the Charlie Brown test.[1] He described the [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election|fifteen-ballot election]] as the proper use of leverage in "a world-historic and absolutely unique legislative chamber designed to force legitimate compromise," and as an "absolutely Trumpian moment."[1]

## Position of the Freedom Caucus

White contended that through the stealthy application of leverage to congressional rules, the [[house-freedom-caucus|Freedom Caucus]] came to occupy a controlling position in what he called the action chamber of the federal legislative branch, the House of Representatives, and was therefore positioned to force the deliberative chamber — the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] — to take up matters of importance to the MAGA movement, such as [[schedule-f|Schedule F]].[1] He observed that a transformation of the House takes only two years, while real transformation of the Senate takes four to six, pointing to the 2024, 2026 and 2028 election cycles.[1]

## Legislative branch as primary target

More broadly, White argued that Team Trump made knowing the legislative branch — as both the lawmaking and the appropriations body — "job number one," because "it all starts with the legislative branch" and "literally everything we care about hovers around them."[1] He tied this to [[sun-tzu|Sun Tzu]]'s maxim on knowing the enemy and oneself.[1]

## McCarthy's speech

White quoted at length from McCarthy's post-election address to the House, in which the new Speaker praised Abraham Lincoln and then expounded on Emanuel Leutze's painting *Washington Crossing the Delaware*, painted in 1850–51 by a German immigrant who hoped to inspire a revolution at home based on American values.[1] McCarthy described the boat's occupants — a Scotsman, an African American, a woman rowing hardest, a Native American, a farmer, and a thirteenth person visible only as a hand — as the immigrant painter's vision of who belonged in the boat, closing on the theme of "a more perfect union" and letting everybody in the boat.[1] White, writing as an African American, read the outstretched hand as representing the presence of God and the motto "where we go one, we go all."[1]
