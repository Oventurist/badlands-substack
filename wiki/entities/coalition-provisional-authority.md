# Coalition Provisional Authority

The Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA) was the transitional governing body established by the United States and its coalition partners to administer Iraq following the 2003 invasion. Among its functions was the handling of Iraqi state funds and the distribution of large quantities of United States currency inside the country.

## Role in the Badlands corpus

In the Badlands Media article A Conflict of Interest, the CPA is the institution through which, according to a 2011 CNBC investigation quoted at length, roughly $40 billion in American cash passed after arriving in Baghdad. The article recounts that the money was palletised at a [[federal-reserve|New York Federal Reserve]] compound in East Rutherford, New Jersey, trucked to Andrews Air Force Base and flown by C-17 to Baghdad International Airport between 2003 and 2008. In the first two years alone the shipments included more than 281 million individual bills weighing 363 tons; a typical pallet held 640 bundles of a thousand bills each and weighed 1,500 pounds, with gold seals for $100 bills, brown for $50 and purple for $20.

The article's central claim about the CPA concerns the break in the chain of custody. It notes that documentation was rigorous while the money was in the hands of the Federal Reserve and Air Force officers, but that on the Baghdad side every flight was met by a single previously unknown CPA official, identified only by the first name Basel — a naturalized American citizen of Lebanese descent born in Saudi Arabia, formerly a translator — who arranged transport of the cash to the [[central-bank-of-iraq|Central Bank of Iraq]] over the seven-mile stretch of Route Irish without military escort. The article argues that the absence of any paper trail after this point was deliberate, and that the CPA arrangement provided the cover under which tens of billions of dollars were diverted, including the sum later discovered in a bunker in [[lebanon|Lebanon]].
