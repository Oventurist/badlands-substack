# Merck & Co.

Merck & Co. is a major American pharmaceutical manufacturer whose COVID-19 antiviral pill, [[lagevrio|Lagevrio]], became the subject of a February 2023 scientific controversy after researchers linked the drug to the emergence of new, transmissible mutations of the SARS-CoV-2 virus.[1]

## Lagevrio mutation study

A preprint study by researchers in the United States and at the Francis Crick Institute, Imperial College London and other British institutions identified mutations linked to Lagevrio in viral samples taken from dozens of patients.[1] As reported by [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]], the finding underscored the risk of therapies that work by intentionally altering a pathogen's genetic code, and prompted concern among some researchers that the drug could generate more contagious or more dangerous variants of a virus that had killed more than 6.8 million people worldwide over the preceding three years.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the finding should be "filed under 'surprising to no one,'" citing what he described as Merck's troubled history of dishonesty — including allegations that the company manipulated the science surrounding its drugs Vioxx and Gardasil — while conceding the same criticism could be levelled at the pharmaceutical industry generally.[1] DeLarme framed the central open question as whether the drug's virus-mutating properties were "a happy accident, or by design."[1]

## 2025 nongovernmental vaccine coalition

Industry groups with longstanding ties to Merck, alongside those tied to Sanofi and Pfizer, joined a private coalition in 2025 aimed at bypassing the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices after Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. dismissed the committee's members over conflict-of-interest concerns.[2] The coalition, working with the University of Minnesota's Vaccine Integrity Project, planned to order vaccines directly from manufacturers and follow medical-association recommendations rather than ACIP's.[2]
