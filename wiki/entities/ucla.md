# University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA)

The University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) is a public research university that became one of the focal points of the spring 2024 wave of pro-Palestinian campus demonstrations in the United States.[1]

## The 2024 "liberated zone" encampment

In late April and early May 2024, far-left activists declared portions of the UCLA campus a "liberated zone" and refused entry to many students who were not affiliated with the pro-Gaza protest movement.[1] Protesters erected barriers, posted guards, and created makeshift checkpoints at which a special wristband was required in order to pass.[1] Students who were not deemed "allies" were effectively barred from attending classes, or at best forced to take longer routes around the barricades.[1]

Commentators compared the encampment to the Seattle CHAZ (Capitol Hill Autonomous Zone) of 2020, noting that the selective exclusion of students is not a protected form of protest or free speech — a point cited as the reason many other campuses had police remove demonstrators.[1] UCLA, by contrast, was described as effectively supporting the activists and allowing them to take control of contested areas of campus.[1]

## Violence and the absence of police

The barricades and the selective barring of students produced predictable anger, and rival groups engaged the protesters in what was described as an all-out brawl.[1] Weapons were used and firecrackers were reportedly fired at activist tents.[1] It was not immediately clear whether the fighting was strictly between pro-Gaza and pro-Israel factions, or whether it simply reflected students enraged by the general blockade; no substantial police presence was visible during the initial clashes.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the UCLA encampment as part of a nationwide pattern of "state-sanctioned rebellion," describing the protest wave as a reboot of the 2020 "Summer of Love" in which [[antifa]]-style activists exchanged black bloc attire for keffiyeh and makeshift thobes used to conceal their faces while destroying property and harassing passersby.[1] She noted students participating in the Muslim call to prayer and privileged demonstrators demanding humanitarian aid while occupying Ivy League campus buildings, and warned that the real question was what would happen when the activists left campus.[1] The episode is treated within the [[2024-pro-palestinian-campus-protests]] as an emblematic case.[1]

The UCLA clashes also formed part of the political backdrop to the House of Representatives' passage of the [[antisemitism-awareness-act]] on the same day.[1]

## May 2024 LAPD clearance of the campus encampment

On the night of Thursday, May 2, 2024, the [[lapd|Los Angeles Police Department]] moved onto the UCLA campus to end what were described as illegal encampments set up by pro-Palestinian agitators, and in the course of arrests officers removed face coverings from some of those detained, exposing their faces to news cameras.[2] Coverage by Modernity News argued that the COVID-era face mask had been adopted wholesale by protesters as a means of concealing identity while signalling political affiliation, and noted that some of those arrested tried to hide their faces once unmasked.[2]

The report highlighted that the first person unmasked in the footage had been part of a group that complained the previous day about insufficient police protection, despite advocating for the abolition of the police. In remarks the outlet characterised as a word salad, she claimed that UCLA was "founded on colonialism" and criticised its alleged involvement with foreign wars, Zionism, and police brutality against black and brown people.[2]

Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the campus movement's method of ending what it called genocide in Gaza was to make America resemble Gaza, drawing a parallel with the 2020 unrest over systemic racism. She contended that the protesters were among the most privileged young people in the country, likely operating under the direction of paid professional organisers rather than students, which she offered as the explanation for their efforts to conceal their faces. She was equally sceptical of the counter-protesting fraternity students who raised large sums overnight, and described the whole episode as suspiciously inorganic — potentially narrative seeding or a diversion — while predicting a move from campuses to city occupations over the summer.[2]
