# Hungary

Hungary is a Central European state whose 2026 change of government produced continuity rather than reversal on migration policy.[1]

[[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]], set to replace [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] after a landslide 2026 election victory, signaled that Hungary would maintain a strict anti-immigration stance, including keeping border barriers and rejecting broader [[european-union|EU]] migration frameworks.[1] Although he campaigned on restoring the rule of law and reengaging with the European Union, Magyar said explicitly that Hungary would not adopt the EU's migration pact and would continue prioritizing national border enforcement.[1]

Under Orbán, Hungary's immigration posture centered on hard border controls, opposition to asylum quotas, and framing migration as a civilizational and security issue — positions that reshaped domestic politics and the country's relationship with Brussels.[1] Analysts noted that while Magyar represents a shift on governance, corruption, and EU relations, immigration policy appears among the least changed areas, suggesting broad internal consensus on limiting illegal migration.[1] The outcome amounts to a hybrid transition in which Budapest moves away from Orbán's centralized political system while retaining core nationalist border policies, signaling continuity on Europe's migration pressure points.[1]


## 2026 parliamentary election

In Hungary's April 2026 parliamentary election, [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] conceded defeat, calling the result "clear" and "painful," as early counts showed opposition leader [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]] heading for a decisive victory.[2] With roughly 99% of votes counted, Magyar's [[tisza-party|Tisza Party]] was projected to secure a two-thirds supermajority, taking 138 seats and crossing the 133-seat threshold needed for constitutional changes — the most significant political shift in the country in over a decade.[2] Orbán had governed for sixteen years and was a close ally of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], facing his strongest electoral test since 2010.[2]

[[european-union|EU]] Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] wrote on X that "Hungary has chosen Europe," calling it a country reclaiming its European path.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the outcome as a "Hungarian Shatterpoint" arriving after EU–NATO pressure over Ukrainian aid, energy deals, and Orbán's refusal to join sanctions campaigns, and framed the choice facing Hungarians as one between the sovereign future Orbán represented and managed decline under Brussels' renewed embrace.[2]

## April 2026 election and Russia scandals

Hungary's [[2026-hungarian-parliamentary-election|April 12, 2026 parliamentary election]] became a geopolitical flashpoint framed as an existential test of the MAGA movement's global nationalist model.[3] The campaign's final stretch was dominated by scandals, including leaked audio of Hungary's foreign minister coordinating with [[russia|Russia]]'s top diplomat to lift EU sanctions, espionage charges against a journalist who exposed Russian operatives, and the discovery of explosives near a Serbia–Hungary gas pipeline.[3] Hungary under [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] was described as Moscow's closest ally inside [[nato|NATO]] and the [[european-union|European Union]], having long blocked Ukraine aid and EU sanctions, with its government accused of leaking information to Russia.[3] Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] traveled to Budapest to reinforce Hungary as a key U.S. partner on migration, energy, technology and defense.[3]

## Die Welt distrust campaign (2023)

In March 2023 the German newspaper Die Welt published an opinion piece by Clemens Wergin arguing that [[nato|NATO]] should not trust Hungary or [[turkey|Turkey]] within the alliance, noting that Hungary wanted EU money in exchange for approving the NATO membership of Sweden and Finland while Turkey continued to block Sweden's accession.[4] Wergin claimed both countries had developed "unseemly" ties to [[russia|Russia]] and asked whether NATO should keep sharing sensitive data with them, writing that "in their turn toward authoritarianism, Ankara and Budapest have also distanced themselves significantly from the community of values for which NATO stands."[4] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the piece as globalist outlets piling on nations sitting on the sidelines of the emerging [[multipolar-world|multipolar world order]], and argued that such pressure only accelerated the realignment.[4]

## Euro adoption ruled out, 2025

On October 6, 2025, Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]] said Hungary would not adopt the euro, calling the [[european-union|EU]] a bloc "in the process of disintegration."[5] Although EU members other than Denmark, which secured an opt-out, are formally obliged eventually to join the Eurozone, seven of the 27 member states still use national currencies.[5] A [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] contributor who visited Hungary in 2017 recalled prices there being far cheaper than in Western Europe, attributing the lingering effects to the hyperinflation Hungary suffered at the end of World War II.[5]
