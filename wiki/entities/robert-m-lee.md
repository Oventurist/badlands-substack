# Robert M. Lee

Robert M. Lee is the chief executive officer and co-founder of [[dragos|Dragos, Inc.]], a firm that describes itself as an "industrial cybersecurity technology and services provider."[1] He was one of four expert witnesses who testified on February 6, 2024 before the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Committee on Homeland Security]]'s Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee at a hearing entitled ["Securing Operational Technology: A Deep Dive into the Water Sector"](https://www.youtube.com/live/LNcX7MCJfnU).[1]

## Background

Lee holds advisory roles with numerous governments and international organizations, including the United States [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]], Singapore's Cyber Security Agency, and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s cybersecurity committees on oil and gas and on electricity.[1] He is a veteran of the United States Air Force and of the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], a background he invoked during the hearing when he told Representative [[carlos-gimenez|Carlos Gimenez]] that he "would take one of ours for 50 others any day" in response to the congressman's point about the manpower advantage of Chinese cyber forces.[1]

## Testimony on operational technology risk

Lee framed the distinction between information technology and [[operational-technology|operational technology]] in stark terms during the hearing: "If you steal from IT, you steal somebody's data. You target OT, you kill people. You need to treat that differently."[1] Asked to describe his nightmare scenario for the water sector, he emphasized attacks at scale, warning that an adversary "can very quickly deny drinking water" and that "denying access to our communities or even manipulating chemical levels in that, at scale, is a scary scenario that we have to prepare for."[1]

Lee was the most direct of the witnesses in criticizing the [[biden-administration|Biden administration]]'s sector-specific cybersecurity "sprints." Asked by Representative [[rob-menendez|Rob Menendez]] what results those efforts produced, Lee replied that water utilities "were pushed very strongly to a government specific answer that didn't actually meet a lot of what they're trying to accomplish, with no resourcing behind it."[1] The Badlands account read this as evidence that particular government-favored technologies and vendors were being pressed on water providers in response to a central agenda rather than sector needs, and speculated about non-competitive contracting.[1]

## Position on digitization and manual fallback

In an extended exchange with Representative Gimenez over [[quantum-computing|quantum computing]], [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]], and the merits of closed intranet or manual systems, Lee argued that reversion is no longer technically possible: "you can no longer operate manually disconnected or in an intranet. And unfortunately, that's just reality."[1] He allowed that hardening selected "strategic sites" was "a great idea" but said the approach could not be scaled across the more than 50,000 water companies in the United States, particularly while the sector still faces roughly a trillion dollars of infrastructure upgrades needed simply to deliver clean water, and while some water utilities share a single IT contractor among six companies.[1] He asserted there were "more efficient ways to get to a more resilient system" than building non-digital redundancy, but did not elaborate — an omission the article highlighted.[1]
