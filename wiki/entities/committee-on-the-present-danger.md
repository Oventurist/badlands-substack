# Committee on the Present Danger

The Committee on the Present Danger was an American pressure organisation led by financier [[paul-nitze|Paul Nitze]].[1] In 1950 Nitze used the Committee to spearhead the passage of [[nsc-68|NSC-68]], which first justified the notion that the United States should maximise its build-up of nuclear warheads on the supposition that it was in a moral equivalent of war with Russia.[1]

Throughout the 1960s, saner forces pushed back against the Committee's programme, resulting in the nuclear test ban treaty, the Open Skies Treaty, the Space Treaty and other trust-building measures, followed by the 1972 [[salt-i|SALT]] agreement.[1]

The Committee's outlook was revived in 1976 with the formation of [[team-b|Team B]], the think tank assembled to produce a hardline counter-estimate of Soviet intentions and, by 1978, to complete what [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]] and [[elizabeth-gould|Elizabeth Gould]] describe as the Trilateral-run coup of U.S. intelligence.[1]
