# Netflix

Netflix is a streaming company that appears in Badlands Media's account as an advertiser targeted in a [[media-matters|Media Matters]] campaign against [[rumble|Rumble]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

A March 2023 Media Matters article was headlined "Ads for Netflix are appearing next to Holocaust denial videos on Rumble."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) Rumble's traffic data reportedly showed that the Media Matters employee was the only human to view the instance of the video carrying the Netflix advertisement, and that the video had received essentially no engagement before the article's publication on 14 March.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) Despite the weakness of the underlying claim, Netflix pulled its advertisements from Rumble out of fear of being labeled antisemitic, achieving the campaign's intended result.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Warner Bros. Discovery merger and 2026 waiver

By February 2026 Netflix held a merger agreement with [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]].[2] That month Netflix granted Warner a waiver permitting it to reopen takeover talks with [[skydance|Skydance]]-owned [[paramount-global|Paramount]], a development disclosed in a Warner regulatory filing; Warner continued to back the Netflix merger agreement while pursuing the Paramount discussions.[2] Because Paramount holds broadcast assets and licenses, including [[cbs|CBS]], any resulting deal would require regulatory approval.[2]

## Bid for Warner Bros. Discovery (December 2025)

On December 5, 2025, Netflix announced an $83 billion deal to acquire a large portion of [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]], in an agreement approved by the boards of both companies; the target's cable channels, including CNN, were not part of the transaction.[3] Three days later [[paramount-global|Paramount]] launched a hostile counter-bid directly to Warner shareholders, offering $30 per share in cash for the entire company and characterizing the Netflix agreement as an inferior proposal facing a challenging regulatory path.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described a Netflix victory as producing a hyper-centralized media behemoth fusing the streaming platform with Warner's intellectual-property library, accelerating what he called cultural inversion through fewer voices and larger echo chambers.[3]

## The Alexander the Great controversy (February 2024)

In February 2024 Netflix's docudrama "The Making of a God" drew a formal objection from the Greek government for portraying [[alexander-the-great|Alexander the Great]] as homosexual, along with his main companion in the show.[4] Greek Culture Minister [[lina-mendoni|Lina Mendoni]] said the series was full of historical inaccuracies that showed "the negligence of the director and the lack of quality of the script," and argued that "there is no mention in the sources that this friendship goes beyond the boundaries of friendship as defined by Aristotle."[4] An opinion piece in the daily Eleftheros Typos called the portrayal a "distortion of the truth" and blamed [[oliver-stone|Oliver Stone]]'s 2004 film Alexander for launching a propaganda campaign about Alexander's sexuality.[4]

[[dimitris-natsios|Dimitris Natsios]] of the [[niki-party|Niki]] party called the series "deplorable, unacceptable and unhistorical," and asked whether Greece would act against Netflix; Mendoni replied that such a move would be unconstitutional under protections for artistic freedom dating to the early 19th century.[4] Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] cited the series as an operation within a [[neo-marxism|Neo-Marxist]] strategic objective to [[queering-history|queer history]].[4]

## The Mystery of Marilyn Monroe

In 2022 Netflix released The Mystery of Marilyn Monroe: The Unheard Tapes, which presented [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]] as the purported organizers of the actress's murder.[5] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] wrote that films of this kind have helped cure millions of Americans of their romantic memories of the Kennedy brothers, and that the documentary drew on some 650 interviews collected by [[anthony-summers|Anthony Summers]] which professor Sarah Churchwell described as often repeating second- or third-hand accounts from sources who were not dispassionate.[5] [[mike-rothmiller|Mike Rothmiller]] played a key role in Netflix films on [[marilyn-monroe|Marilyn Monroe]] and other historical topics.[5] Ehret cautioned readers not to be confused about the nature of Netflix or its founder and president, a grand-nephew of [[edward-bernays|Edward Bernays]].[5]
