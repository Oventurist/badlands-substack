# Ukrainian Orthodox Church

The Ukrainian Orthodox Church is at the center of a religious and geopolitical dispute between Moscow and Kyiv that Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] invoked in April 2026 when he accused parts of the European Union of promoting "rampant Satanism."[1]

## Persecution claims

Lavrov stated that "the Ukrainian Orthodox Church has been persecuted for over a decade now, with churches [forcibly] taken over, vandalized and clergy and parishioners harassed."[1] He further charged that the Ukrainian Ministry of Culture used a bureaucratic formula to conceal what he called legalized blasphemous practices, and that several European countries had turned a blind eye to these developments or directly supported them.[1] The remarks centered on tensions over the [[kyiv-pechersk-lavra|Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra]], where Ukrainian authorities have taken action against clergy tied to Moscow.[1]

## Competing framings

[[russia|Russia]] has framed the Ukrainian measures as religious persecution, while [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and its allies argue they are national security actions taken amid the ongoing war.[1] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the dispute as evidence of a spiritual war, writing that the Kremlin reports systematic persecution of the Russian Orthodox Church and its usurpation by the Ukrainian Orthodox Church.[1]


## Prosecution of Metropolitan Jonathan of Tulchin

In August 2023 a senior clergyman of the church, [[metropolitan-jonathan-of-tulchin|Metropolitan Jonathan of Tulchin]], was sentenced to five years in prison after being accused of "justifying" Russian aggression against Ukraine, and was to see property under his oversight confiscated.[2] Although the church has frequently criticized the Russian invasion, it has not broken communion with the Moscow Patriarchate, and on that basis its clergy and monastics have been treated by the Ukrainian state as sympathetic to Moscow.[2] Ukrainian authorities have seized several churches and monasteries on accusations of cooperating with Russia, while other clergy members have faced jail time, house arrest, or harassment by mobs of far-right Ukrainian nationalists, most often for merely calling for peace between the two countries.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] followed a policy of "nothing about Ukraine without Ukraine," Washington was effectively endorsing the persecution.[2]
