# Church of the Holy Sepulchre

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre is a Christian church complex in the Old City of Jerusalem, traditionally recognized as the site of both the crucifixion and the burial of Jesus. It is one of Christianity's holiest sites and, according to the Badlands Brief edition ["Golgotha & The Metaverse"](https://badlands.substack.com/p/badlands-brief-52b), typically draws up to four million visitors annually.

## Layout and tradition

As described in the March 2026 Brief, the church encloses both the traditional site of the crucifixion and the empty tomb within a single large complex, the two locations separated by only a few dozen meters. Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] anchored the tradition in John 19:41: "At the place where Jesus was crucified, there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb, in which no one had ever been laid."

## 2026 closure

In March 2026, church authorities closed the site to the public, citing security concerns ahead of Easter. The closure was described as rare and precautionary, occurring amid the escalating [[iran|Iran]]–[[israel|Israel]] war and heightened security risks in Jerusalem. The Brief emphasized that the church "is almost never closed," and that the shutdown came only weeks before Easter.

Commentator [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the previous closure occurred in 2018, when the church shut its doors in protest after the Jerusalem municipal government imposed taxes it had not previously faced. He cited that history as evidence of longstanding "animus between the Jerusalem (Israeli) government and the church," and read the 2026 closure as one front in what he described as a spiritual dimension of the broader conflict.

## Palm Sunday 2026: clergy barred

On March 29, 2026, Israeli police blocked senior Catholic leaders — including Cardinal [[pierbattista-pizzaballa|Pierbattista Pizzaballa]], the Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem, and the Custos of the Holy Land — from entering the church to celebrate a private [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] Mass limited to a handful of clergy and closed to the public. Catholic authorities said it was the first time in centuries that the church's senior leadership had been unable to conduct Palm Sunday services at the site.

Israeli authorities cited security conditions arising from the war with [[iran|Iran]]: missile threats, limited shelter availability and restricted emergency access in the Old City. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said holy sites of all three faiths had been "repeatedly targeted" by Iranian missile strikes and that in one strike "missile fragments crashed meters from the Church of the Holy Sepulcher," insisting there was "no malicious intent whatsoever." The [[latin-patriarchate-of-jerusalem|Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem]] called the measure "manifestly unreasonable and grossly disproportionate." After criticism from Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] and U.S. Ambassador [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], Netanyahu instructed authorities to allow access.

## Escalation timeline

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] traced a year-by-year narrowing of access at the church: full public closure during Holy Week in 2020 under [[covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19]] restrictions; limited reopening and controlled attendance in 2021; Israeli police entry limits in the Old City in 2022; barriers and controlled entry in 2023; and sharply reduced pilgrimage under war-related security posture in 2024. She argued that in none of those years were clergy themselves denied access, making 2026 a categorical break. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] added the property-tax dispute and the freezing of church bank accounts to the same sequence.
