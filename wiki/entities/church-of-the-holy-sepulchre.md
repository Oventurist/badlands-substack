# Church of the Holy Sepulchre

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre is the Jerusalem church traditionally recognized as the site of Jesus's crucifixion and burial.[1] The complex is said to contain both Golgotha, the place of the crucifixion, and the empty tomb, which sit only a few dozen meters apart inside one large structure.[1] It is one of Christianity's holiest sites and typically draws up to four million visitors annually.[1]

## March 2026 closure

In March 2026 the church was closed to the public, with church authorities citing security concerns ahead of Easter.[1] The closure was described as rare and precautionary, occurring amid heightened regional instability and security risks in Jerusalem during the escalating [[iran|Iran]]–[[israel|Israel]] conflict.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the church is almost never closed and that its doors shut due to the war only weeks before Easter, quoting John 19:41 — "At the place where Jesus was crucified, there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb, in which no one had ever been laid" — and adding that "Good thing Jesus is alive, otherwise this would be very sad."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the closure a crazy development in the weeks leading up to Easter, noting animus between the Israeli government in Jerusalem and the church, and recalling that the last closure in 2018 was a protest by the church after the Jerusalem municipal government imposed taxes it had never before faced.[1] He argued that the Israeli government is "drunk on its own hubris" and, if left unchecked, has the ego to take control of the Middle East and create a [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]].[1] He framed the closure as part of a spiritual dimension of the conflict between the People and the [[deep-state|Deep State]], in which the enemy seeks to remove Jesus from people's lives — citing [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] characterizing the phrase "Christ is King" as antisemitic — and urged readers to "plant our flag and prepare to die on this hill."[1]

## Palm Sunday 2026 clergy exclusion

On March 29, 2026, [[israeli-police|Israeli police]] blocked senior Catholic leaders from entering the church, preventing them from celebrating a private [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] Mass; the group included Cardinal [[pierbattista-pizzaballa|Pierbattista Pizzaballa]], the Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem.[2] Catholic authorities said the incident marked the first time in centuries that top church leaders were unable to conduct Palm Sunday services at the site, and noted the service had been limited to a small number of clergy and closed to the public.[2] Israeli authorities cited security concerns tied to the conflict with Iran, including missile threats, limited shelter availability and restricted emergency access in the Old City.[2] The [[latin-patriarchate-of-jerusalem|Latin Patriarchate]] called the move "a manifestly unreasonable and grossly disproportionate measure" impeding freedom of worship, and after criticism from U.S. and European officials [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] instructed authorities to allow access.[2]

Netanyahu said Christian, Jewish and Muslim holy sites had been "repeatedly targeted" by Iranian missile strikes, that in one strike fragments "crashed meters from the Church of the Holy Sepulcher," and that the decision carried "no malicious intent whatsoever."[2] U.S. ambassador [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] said the barring of the Patriarch was difficult to justify given that the four Catholic representatives fell well below the Home Front Command's 50-person limit.[2] Italian leader [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] said the church "must be preserved and protected for the celebration of sacred rites."[2]

Badlands writers charted a year-by-year tightening of access at the church from the 2020 COVID closure through the 2024 war-related security posture, and argued that never before had clergy themselves been denied entry.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] added that not even the Muslim rulers who controlled Jerusalem for centuries imposed a property tax on the Church or blocked clergy from the site, and accused [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionists]] of excusing the Israeli government's conduct.[2]
