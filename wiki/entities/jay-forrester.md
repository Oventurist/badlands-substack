# Jay Forrester

Jay Forrester was the MIT systems theorist who, with [[dennis-meadows|Dennis Meadows]], gave nominal leadership to the team of statisticians whose two-year study produced the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s 1972 report [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]].[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] notes that Forrester died in 2016.[1]

## The modelling method and its critics

The study introduced the environmental computer modelling that shaped subsequent planetary-scale ecological debate, and, as a celebratory retrospective quoted by Ehret put it, more than any other book introduced the concept of anthropocentric climate change to a mass audience.[1] [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] attacked the method directly, arguing that Forrester and Meadows greatly understated known quantities of natural resources, and that their use of systems of simultaneous linear equations built into the projections the assumption that absolutely no technological progress was occurring in society.[1] Since technological progress — including fundamental redefinitions of what "natural resources" means — had been the outstanding feature of European civilization for five hundred years, LaRouche concluded that the study depended on the assumption that such progress had come to a sudden, absolute stop.[1]

## Funding context

The Club of Rome–MIT project began at Montebello, Quebec in 1971 with Canadian taxpayer money allocated by [[pierre-trudeau|Pierre Trudeau]], and afterwards drew its funding from the Volkswagen Foundation.[1]
