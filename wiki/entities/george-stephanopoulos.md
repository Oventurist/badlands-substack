# George Stephanopoulos

George Stephanopoulos is an ABC News anchor who conducted the July 2024 damage-control television interview with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that followed Biden's widely criticized debate performance against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The interview aired on a Friday and was framed by the Biden campaign as an opportunity to reassure Democrats about the president's mental acuity.[1]

## The July 2024 Biden interview

Badlands Media characterized Stephanopoulos as "ABC's lobber of softballs for the evening," arguing that the questioning was deliberately gentle and that the exercise nonetheless failed on its own terms.[1] Asked whether he understood how badly the debate was going while it was underway, Biden delivered a meandering answer in which he repeatedly assigned blame to himself — "nobody's fault but mine" — while citing New York Times polling numbers and asserting that Trump "lied 28 times."[1]

The interview produced two moments that drew the most commentary in alternative media: Biden's claim that he had "put NATO together," despite [[nato|NATO]] having been founded in 1949 when Biden was six and a half years old, and his repeated boast — made at least three times — that he had "expanded NATO."[1] Badlands treated the latter as an admission of responsibility for the [[russia-ukraine-war|Russia-Ukraine war]], on the view that NATO expansion is the proximate cause of the conflict.[1]

## Reception and aftermath

Rather than stabilizing Biden's position, the Stephanopoulos interview accelerated Democratic defections. Reporting cited by ZeroHedge noted that donors including [[reed-hastings|Reed Hastings]] and [[bill-harris|Bill Harris]] reiterated or hardened their calls for Biden to step aside after watching it, and Axios described congressional Democrats as "further deflated" by the broadcast.[1] Badlands Media dissected the full 22-minute interview on [[the-liberty-den|The Liberty Den]].[1]

## Trump defamation suit and the December 2024 settlement

During a contentious March 10, 2024 interview with Representative [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] on ABC's *This Week*, Stephanopoulos asserted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been found "liable for rape" in a civil case, telling Mace: "You've endorsed Donald Trump for president. Judges and two separate juries have found him liable for rape and for defaming the victim of that rape."[2] He repeated the claim ten times during the exchange, although the jury in the case brought by [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] had in fact found Trump liable for "sexual abuse," a distinct category under New York law.[2]

Trump sued Stephanopoulos and [[abc-news|ABC News]] for defamation, and on Saturday, December 14, 2024 a settlement was publicly filed that avoided a costly trial.[2] Under its terms ABC News agreed to pay $15 million as a charitable contribution to "a Presidential foundation and museum to be established by or for Plaintiff," plus $1 million in Trump's attorney fees.[2] Stephanopoulos and the network were also required to issue statements of "regret" as an editor's note appended to the March 10, 2024 online article, reading: "ABC News and George Stephanopoulos regret statements regarding President Donald J. Trump made during an interview by George Stephanopoulos with Rep. Nancy Mace on ABC's This Week on March 10, 2024."[2] An ABC News spokesperson said the network was "pleased that the parties have reached an agreement to dismiss the lawsuit on the terms in the court filing."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the statement of regret "didn't go far enough, but $15M should cover the deficit," describing the outcome as part of a trend in which "legal outcomes are starting to feel like justice."[2]
