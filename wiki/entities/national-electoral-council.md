# National Electoral Council (Venezuela)

The National Electoral Council (Consejo Nacional Electoral) is Venezuela's electoral authority, responsible for administering and certifying national elections.[1] It became the focus of an international dispute after it declared incumbent [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] the winner of the July 28, 2024 presidential election on the following Monday.[1]

## 2024 election dispute

Pressure on the council intensified as multiple governments — including close allies of Maduro — demanded that it release detailed vote counts, as it had done in previous elections.[1] Hours after the council's declaration, the main opposition coalition said it held contrary evidence in the form of more than two-thirds of the tally sheets printed by each electronic voting machine after polls closed.[1] The stakes rose further when the United States recognized opposition candidate [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]] as the victor, with Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] citing "overwhelming evidence."[1]

Badlands commentary questioned the reflexive Western consensus on the council's alleged fraud, framing the episode as an information-war "reverse indicator" rather than a settled factual matter.[1]

## Disputed 2024 results and the AP analysis

On Friday, August 2, 2024, the council published updated results based on 96.87% of tally sheets showing [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] with 6.4 million votes and [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]] with 5.3 million.[2] Council president [[elvis-amoroso|Elvis Amoroso]] attributed the delay in updating results to "massive attacks" on the body's "technological infrastructure."[2] An Associated Press analysis of nearly 24,000 opposition-released tally sheets — 79% of voting machines, 10.26 million votes decoded from QR codes — instead showed González with 6.89 million votes and Maduro with 3.13 million, casting serious doubt on the council's official declaration.[2]
