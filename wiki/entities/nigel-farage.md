# Nigel Farage

Nigel Farage is a British politician and broadcaster, the former leader of the Brexit Party, who in February 2024 became one of the most prominent international voices predicting that the Democratic Party would remove [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] from the 2024 presidential ticket.[1]

## Commentary on Biden's fitness

Speaking to GB News, Farage said flatly that Biden suffers from "senile dementia," adding that he had "been saying that for some months."[1] He framed the issue as one of global consequence: "he's not just the leader of America, he's the leader of the free world and under his tenure many of these awful things around the world have happened…he is totally incapable of the job."[1]

Farage's remarks followed the release of the [[hur-report|Hur report]], in which special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]] described Biden's memory as so poor that he could not recall when his vice presidency began or ended, nor recall within several years when his son Beau died.[1]

## Prediction of a Biden replacement

Farage predicted that there would be no Trump–Biden run-off, arguing that Democrats would "ditch him before the Democrat convention takes place in July" because Biden had become "just too embarrassing," and noting the president's plummeting approval ratings.[1]

Badlands Media commentary treated the prediction as plausible but not inevitable. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disagreed with Farage's timeline, leaning instead toward a contested [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]] producing a [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]], [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]], or [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] candidacy, while noting that other Badlands writers believed the party would be stuck with Biden — read as [[devolution|devolution]] proof.[1]

## Return to frontline politics and the 2024 election

Farage returned from political retirement in 2024 to lead [[reform-uk|Reform UK]], a populist party surging in the polls ahead of the July 4 general election on the strength of anti-immigration sentiment, splitting the right-wing vote and putting [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]]'s Conservatives on course for a historic defeat to [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]]'s Labour Party.[2] Badlands commentary described him as "the Donald Trump of geopolitics before Donald Trump was a politician," recalling his 2010 European Parliament speech telling its president Herman van Rompuy that he had "the charisma of a damp rag, and the appearance of a low-grade bank clerk."[2] The same commentary recalled that while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] campaigned for president in 2016, Farage campaigned for [[brexit]] — characterized as the flagship movement of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and a referendum for British sovereignty against the European Union — after which he retired believing his life's work of sovereignty achieved.[2] The Brexit vote took place on June 23, 2016, a date the writer noted as significant, arguing such coincidences are never really coincidences.[2]

## Ukraine war remarks

In a BBC interview in June 2024 Farage stood by earlier comments blaming the expansion of [[nato|NATO]] and the European Union into eastern Europe for Russia's invasion of Ukraine, saying "it was obvious" that more eastern European countries joining the pacts would provoke Vladimir Putin.[2] Sunak called the statement "completely wrong" and warned that "this kind of appeasement is dangerous for Britain's security, the security of our allies that rely on us and only emboldens Putin further."[2] Home Secretary [[james-cleverly|James Cleverly]] said Farage was "echoing Putin's vile justification for the brutal invasion of Ukraine," Starmer called the remarks "disgraceful," and shadow defense secretary John Healey labelled Farage a "Putin apologist."[2]


## Badlands commentary during the 2024 riots

In its coverage of the August 2024 disorder across British cities, Badlands Media concluded that "what Britain needs is Nigel Farage; just like America needs President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]," presenting him as the domestic counterpart to Trump in a contest against what the outlet describes as the globalist hegemony administered from London.[4] See [[southport-riots|the Southport riots]].[4]

## Mar-a-Lago meeting with Elon Musk (December 2024)

On Monday, December 16, 2024, Farage met [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] at [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Mar-a-Lago club in Florida, alongside [[reform-uk|Reform UK]]'s new party treasurer [[nick-candy|Nick Candy]], amid rumours that Musk was preparing to donate $100 million to the party — which would be by far the largest donation in British electoral history.[3] Farage and Candy described the hour-long meeting as "great," said they had "learned a great deal about the Trump ground game," and promised "ongoing discussions" with the Tesla boss on other areas, adding: "We only have one more chance left to save the West and we can do great things together."[3] They thanked Trump for the use of Mar-a-Lago for what they called a historic meeting, declaring "the special relationship is alive and well."[3]

According to The Sunday Times, businessmen and Conservative Party officials believed Musk might make the donation as a "f*** you Starmer" payment amid his feud with Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]].[3] Farage had previously described Musk as "very supportive," saying: "He thinks that if Reform do well in the UK, we can bring about the same kind of change that he intends to do with Donald Trump in America."[3] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the meeting as a strong signal for what he called the Sovereign Alliance, noting Musk was offering a war chest to the man who spearheaded Brexit so he could "finish the job."[3]
