# Elizabeth Bruenig

Elizabeth Bruenig is an American journalist and staff writer at *[[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]]*. In the Badlands corpus she is the author of the February 2026 fictional measles essay that became the subject of a fabrication controversy.

## The measles fiction controversy (February 2026)

The February 20, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that *The Atlantic* published a piece by Bruenig written from the perspective of a mother whose two unvaccinated children fell ill with measles, in which the 11-month-old child dies. The piece read as a first-hand account but was a work of fiction: an editor's note at the end stated that "this story is based on extensive reporting and interviews with physicians, including those who have cared directly for patients with measles." [[laura-hazard-owen|Laura Hazard Owen]] of [[niemanlab|NiemanLab]] was the first to flag publicly that the story was fiction, and multiple journalists questioned the piece's truthfulness before the disclaimer was added.

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argues that Bruenig constructed a "composite narrative character" from medical literature and physician interviews — an invented dead child — and that presenting such emotionally manipulative fiction as journalism is propaganda rather than reporting, invoking the [[new-journalism|New Journalism]] movement and the magazine's earlier [[pandemic-amnesty|"Pandemic Amnesty"]] plea. The commentary also notes Bruenig reportedly has two daughters of her own, and concludes that she "was so unbelievable as an 'anti-vaxxer' that even the other vax-maxxing wine moms called her out."
