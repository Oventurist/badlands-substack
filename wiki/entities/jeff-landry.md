# Jeff Landry

Jeff Landry (R) is the Governor of Louisiana. He entered the Badlands Media coverage record on January 1, 2025, when he issued a State of Emergency for Louisiana within hours of a vehicle-ramming attack on Bourbon Street in the French Quarter of New Orleans.[1]

## Emergency declaration after the French Quarter attack

A press release from Landry's office explained that the State of Emergency would "allow for the utilization of all available state resources ahead of the Sugar Bowl, Super Bowl LIX and Mardi Gras" — three large public events scheduled for New Orleans in the weeks following the attack.[1]

Landry framed the declaration in terms of a standing commitment: "From day one, my commitment to the people of this state has been that we will not look the other way," he said in a statement. "We will not allow heinous acts of crime such as the one this morning to dampen the Louisiana spirit and ruin our way of life."[1] He added that his administration would "not tolerate one of the jewel cities" in Louisiana "to remain with these conditions," and described the declaration as "a necessity in order to ensure that every single person, place, and street across the city of New Orleans is the most safe and secure in the world."[1]

The declaration was reported by Breitbart and aggregated in the Badlands News Brief covering news from December 31, 2024 and January 1, 2025, alongside the federal response to the same attack.[1] The attack itself is covered at [[new-orleans-bourbon-street-attack]].

## Nitrogen hypoxia and the Hoffman execution

As governor of [[louisiana|Louisiana]], Landry signed a bill in 2024 making nitrogen hypoxia a legal alternative to lethal injection, reopening a path to executions in a state that had not carried one out since 2010.[2] On March 18, 2025, Louisiana used the method to execute Jessie Hoffman Jr., convicted in the 1996 murder of a woman abducted from a New Orleans parking garage; officials said the gas flowed for 19 minutes in what one described as a "flawless" execution.[2] Landry declined to intervene after a state district court judge rejected a defence request for a temporary pause, and the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] denied a final application 5-4.[2] The case is treated in detail at [[louisiana-nitrogen-gas-execution|the Louisiana nitrogen gas execution]].[2]

## Surgical castration law (2024)

On June 18, 2024, Landry signed into law Senate Bill 371, allowing Louisiana judges to sentence people convicted of aggravated sex crimes against victims under the age of 13 to undergo surgical castration.[3] The bill, sponsored by state Senator Regina Ashford Barrow, applied to offenders aged 17 and older and was expected to take effect on August 1, 2024.[3] The measure is treated in detail at [[louisiana-surgical-castration-law|the Louisiana surgical castration law]].[3]

## 2023 election as governor

Landry, then Louisiana attorney general, was projected by the Associated Press to win the state's gubernatorial race on Saturday, 14 October 2023, handing Republicans control of the top executive office for the first time since 2016.[4] He competed against more than a dozen candidates in Louisiana's jungle primary, and because he took more than half the vote — reported at roughly 51 percent — he won outright rather than advancing to a November runoff, stunning observers who had expected a second round against Democrat Shawn Wilson.[4] A hard-right Republican backed by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Landry succeeded term-limited Governor [[john-bel-edwards|John Bel Edwards]], the only sitting Democratic governor in the Deep South, and his win was read as momentum for the party's conservative populist wing heading into Kentucky and Mississippi races in November.[4] The contest is treated in detail at [[2023-louisiana-gubernatorial-election|the 2023 Louisiana gubernatorial election]].[4]
