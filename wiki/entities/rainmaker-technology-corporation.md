# Rainmaker Technology Corporation

Rainmaker Technology Corporation is a cloud-seeding company that became the focus of viral speculation following the [[2025-central-texas-floods|2025 Central Texas floods]].[1] The company is led by [[augustus-doricko|Augustus Doricko]].[1]

## Role in the 2025 Texas flood controversy

After the Fourth of July weekend flooding, theories about [[weather-modification|weather modification]] circulated widely online, with the most viral of them implicating Rainmaker.[1] On the Saturday following the disaster, Doricko posted on X that "To ensure safety and prevent any risk of flooding from cloud seeding, Rainmaker suspended operations in accordance with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulations suspension criteria."[1]

In the same thread, Doricko addressed the question of whether Rainmaker had conducted operations that could have affected the floods, answering that it had not.[1] He said the last seeding mission before the July 4 event occurred during the early afternoon of July 2, when a brief mission was flown over the eastern portions of south-central Texas and two clouds were seeded.[1] Those clouds persisted for about two hours before dissipating between 3:00 and 4:00 p.m. CDT — more than 24 hours before the storm complex that produced the flooding rainfall developed.[1] Doricko noted that natural clouds typically have lifespans of thirty minutes to a few hours, with even the most persistent storm systems rarely maintaining the same cloud structure for more than 12 to 18 hours.[1]

## Denial of involvement in the 2025 Texas floods

After a series of deadly flash floods struck central Texas on Friday, July 4, 2025, Rainmaker chief executive [[augustus-doricko|Augustus Doricko]] publicly stated that his company — which works with Texas farmers during droughts — "unequivocally had nothing to do with the flooding."[2] More than 80 people died and many remained missing as the Guadalupe River surged by 20 to 26 feet within 90 minutes, causing widespread devastation and mass evacuations; the [[national-weather-service|National Weather Service]] activated flood warnings across the state, and flooding is the second-deadliest U.S. weather hazard after extreme heat by NWS data.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary was scathing. [[canncon|CannCon]] mocked Doricko for likening "moving forward" after 109 deaths to football, writing that any presidential aspirations were finished.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] added that critics of [[weather-modification|weather modification]] skeptics "were against man made climate change before they were for man made climate change."[2]
