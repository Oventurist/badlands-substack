# James Bredar

James Bredar is a U.S. District Judge, an appointee of former President Barack Obama, who issued rulings in 2025 blocking the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] from terminating thousands of probationary federal employees.[1]

## Probationary employee litigation

Bredar had previously ordered officials to temporarily reinstate probationary employees fired at 18 agencies, regardless of where those employees physically worked.[1] On April 1, 2025, he indefinitely barred the administration from terminating the employees but declined to extend the order nationwide.[1] The replacement preliminary injunction covered two additional agencies — the [[department-of-defense|Defense Department]] and the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] — while simultaneously limiting reinstatements to employees whose "duty station" lay within Washington, D.C., and the 19 states that had sued.[1]

Explaining the narrowing, Bredar wrote that "[o]nly states have sued here, and only to vindicate their interests as states. They are not proxies for the workers," and added that each presumably well-informed state was entitled to decide for itself whether to seek relief, so that it would be inappropriate for the court to fashion relief effectively overruling decisions reserved to non-party states.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] situated the ruling within a wider pattern of district-court [[judicial-lawfare-against-the-trump-agenda|judicial lawfare against the Trump agenda]], citing [[article-iii-project|Article III Project]] founder [[mike-davis|Mike Davis]]'s warning that "widespread judicial sabotage from activist judges is out of control" and that the judiciary cannot afford to lose its legitimacy.[1] She argued that appeals from such district-court rulings usefully clarify legal authorities and establish precedent, noting a [[norm-eisen|Norm Eisen]]-affiliated Just Security litigation tracker counting 159 cases filed against the administration.[1]
