# Project for the New American Century

The Project for the New American Century (PNAC) is a neoconservative think tank that Badlands Media identifies as the intellectual architecture behind decades of U.S. regime-change policy, including in Syria.[1]

## Founding and membership

PNAC was created in 1997 by [[william-kristol|William Kristol]] and [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]], and had strong ties to the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]], described as a storied neocon hotbed.[1] Its ranks included [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], [[paul-wolfowitz|Paul Wolfowitz]], [[john-bolton|John Bolton]], and [[donald-rumsfeld|Donald Rumsfeld]], among others.[1] Of the 25 original signatories, 14 were given positions in the administration of George W. Bush.[1]

## Doctrine

PNAC's vision of a "New American Century" encompassed the takeover of countries including Libya, Iraq, Iran, and Syria in pursuit of [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]] — the pretext, per the article, used by the United States to effect regime change worldwide in the name of "Democracy."[1] Its report *Rebuilding America's Defenses* stated that "The American peace has proven itself peaceful, stable, and durable. Yet no moment in international politics can be frozen in time: even a global Pax Americana will not preserve itself."[1] The report concluded that the global order "must have a secure foundation on unquestioned U.S. military preeminence," and observed that "the process of transformation is likely to be a long one, absent some catastrophic and catalyzing event — like a new Pearl Harbor" — language published years before the September 11 attacks.[1]

## Relevance to Syria

The article situates PNAC alongside General Wesley Clark's 2007 account of a post-9/11 [[pentagon|Pentagon]] memo outlining a plan to "take out seven countries in five years," starting with Iraq and then Syria, Lebanon, Libya, Somalia, Sudan, and finishing with Iran.[1] Taken together, these are presented as evidence that the neoconservative faction had targeted Syria since at least the 1990s, and that the fall of Assad fits a long-standing template rather than a spontaneous uprising.[1] PNAC's ambitions are also tied to the post-Cold War expansion of American media conditioning, from the CIA's Mighty Wurlitzer and [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]] onward.[1]

## Founders and the road to Iraq

Badlands Media's August 2023 "Deep State Spotlight" on [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]] identifies PNAC as the brainchild of [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]] — Nuland's husband — and [[william-kristol|William Kristol]], and describes it as a neoconservative think tank with strong ties to the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]].[2] Its policy document [[rebuilding-americas-defenses|Rebuilding America's Defenses]] is characterized there as openly advocating total global military domination.[2]

According to that profile, PNAC was the mid-1990s think tank that laid the groundwork for the brand of [[neoconservatives|neoconservatism]] that has shaped global affairs in the decades since, relentlessly pressuring then-President Bill Clinton to adopt a more aggressive posture toward Iraq and planting the seeds of war with Iraq and Iran well before the September 11 attacks provided the pretext its members wanted.[2] Many PNAC figures went on to hold the highest-level positions in the [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] administration, a cohort the article dryly summarizes as "all Yale boys together" given their shared [[yale-university|Yale]] background.[2] Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] compares this pre-9/11 agitation to what he characterizes as the liberal establishment's desire for a domestic war on MAGA Republicans before January 6, arguing that the [[deep-state|deep state]] reuses a small number of plays.[2]
