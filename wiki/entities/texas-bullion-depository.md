# Texas Bullion Depository

The Texas Bullion Depository is a state-run precious-metals storage facility in Texas, cited by Badlands Media as an early and influential example of a U.S. state taking physical custody of gold outside the federal financial system.[1]

## Establishment and function

The Texas government moved years in advance to protect the gold and silver of its citizens by building its own bullion depository, which opened in 2018.[1] Citizens can store their precious metals in the secure state government facility, and the arrangement also allows the state of Texas to keep its own emergency gold reserves within its borders.[1]

## Significance

Because the state has full control over its reserves, Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues Texas is more independent of and less controlled by the federal government — one reason, he writes, that residents call it "the Republic of Texas."[1] Tennessee subsequently followed the Texas model, with a 2023 bill filed in its House to establish a comparable precious-metals depository, reported by the [[tenth-amendment-center|Tenth Amendment Center]] as a step that could undermine the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s monopoly on money.[1] The depository is presented as part of a broader state-led push toward a [[gold-standard|gold standard]].[1]
