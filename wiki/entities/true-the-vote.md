# True the Vote

True the Vote is a Houston-based election-integrity organization that grew out of the Tea Party group King Street Patriots. In addition to running a poll-watcher campaign, the organization advocates for restrictive election laws such as voter identification requirements.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

True the Vote was the better-known partner of Verify the Vote, the group for which [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] worked before being hired as an assistant attorney general focused on election integrity in the [[arizona-attorney-generals-office|Arizona Attorney General's Office]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) *Talking Points Memo* drew the connection in its August 22, 2019 article on Wright's hiring, an account [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] cited approvingly, calling Wright "our kind of gal."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Fair Fight litigation (2021–2024)

True the Vote was the defendant in a federal lawsuit brought by [[fair-fight|Fair Fight]], the liberal voting-rights group, over the organization's effort to challenge the eligibility of hundreds of thousands of voters in the Georgia U.S. Senate runoff elections of early 2021.[2] On January 2, 2024, Judge Steve C. Jones of the United States District Court for the Northern District of Georgia ruled in a 145-page opinion that the challenges did not violate the clause of the [[voting-rights-act-of-1965|Voting Rights Act]] outlawing voter suppression, writing that the court "maintains its prior concerns" about the group's methods but that Fair Fight had failed to show the efforts were illegal.[2] The court found no violation of Section 11(b) by any named defendant and directed judgment to be entered for the defendants.[2]

The decision applied only within Jones's northern Georgia district and was assessed by *The New York Times* as unlikely to change a status quo in which right-wing election groups had already generated thousands of voter-registration challenges nationwide, though it was expected to encourage conservative activists hunting for voter fraud during the 2024 presidential election.[2] Election officials and voting-rights groups warned that mass challenges could intimidate voters away from the ballot box.[2] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the ruling as a First Amendment victory and quoted the court's finding that the group's two efforts were "aimed at the same purpose (i.e., reduce voter fraud and pursue election integrity)," while noting the court's pointed criticism of the credibility of [[catherine-engelbrecht|Catherine Engelbrecht]] and its evident dislike of both Engelbrecht and [[gregg-phillips|Gregg Phillips]].[2]

## Georgia ballot harvesting presentation (2021)

In May 2021, True the Vote presented findings on alleged ballot harvesting in Georgia to then-[[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|GBI]] Director [[vic-reynolds|Vic Reynolds]].3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator) According to a follow-up letter from Reynolds, the data identified "279 cellphones which had made multiple trips to within 100 feet of a voter drop box," with accompanying documents tying those phones to various organizations through geolocation.3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator) Reynolds wrote that no witness statements or names of potential defendants had been supplied, that a claimed validating source had never been produced despite "repeated requests," and that the data therefore did not rise to probable cause.3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator)

Founder [[catherine-engelbrecht|Catherine Engelbrecht]] rejected that characterization in a statement to Badlands Media, saying the organization met Reynolds and his team only once — in the Governor's office in early 2021 — and that its later attempts to re-engage the GBI went unanswered.3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator) She said the FBI offered to host a secure database so the GBI and other agencies could access the material and preserve chain of custody, while making clear it was not itself investigating.3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator) Engelbrecht said the group first learned of Reynolds's letter when it appeared in the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, and that "at no point was there a serious effort to ask the kinds of questions a real investigation requires."3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator)

Former Senator [[david-perdue|David Perdue]] testified that the same evidence — video, cell phone data, testimony and bank records — was viewed by Reynolds, who called it compelling enough to investigate before declining to act.3(https://badlands.substack.com/p/grand-jury-testimony-from-senator)
