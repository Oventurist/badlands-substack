# William Burns

William Burns is the Central Intelligence Agency director under Joe Biden and the author of the February 2008 diplomatic cable that gives the article "A Bright Red Line" its title.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) At the time of writing he was serving in the intelligence and diplomatic community in Moscow, and the cable was never intended for public consumption; it became available through the efforts of [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## The 2008 cable

Burns wrote that the NATO aspirations of Ukraine and Georgia "not only touch a raw nerve in Russia, they engender serious concerns about the consequences for stability in the region," with Russia perceiving encirclement, efforts to undermine its regional influence, and unpredictable consequences for its security interests.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) He reported expert concern that strong divisions inside Ukraine over membership — with much of the ethnic-Russian community opposed — could produce a major split involving violence or, at worst, civil war.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The cable's central judgment was that "Ukrainian entry into NATO is the brightest of all redlines for the Russian elite (not just Putin)."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article reads this as meaning there is nothing that could provoke Russia more, and that such a step would effectively be treated as a declaration of war.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Burns further wrote that in more than two and a half years of conversations with key Russian players, "from knuckle-draggers in the dark recesses of the Kremlin to Putin's sharpest liberal critics," he had "yet to find anyone who views Ukraine in NATO as anything other than a direct challenge to Russian interests."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Badlands cites this line as evidence that opposition to NATO encroachment is not merely the Putin government's position but a broadly Russian one, and that the notion of a pro-Western Russian constituency rallying behind [[alexei-navalny|Alexei Navalny]] is a myth.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

On the consequences of a premature Membership Action Plan offer, Burns predicted it would be seen "not as a technical step along a long road toward membership, but as throwing down the strategic gauntlet," that Russian-Ukrainian relations would go into a deep freeze, that Moscow would contemplate economic measures ranging from immediate gas price increases to world market levels to a clampdown on Ukrainian workers in Russia, and that it would "create fertile soil for Russian meddling in Crimea and eastern Ukraine."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Significance

The article treats the cable as a warning delivered to an incoming administration that outwardly cultivated the appearance of wanting a workable relationship with Russia, and as proof that Washington knew for at least fifteen years what continuing down this path would produce.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Because such internal documents were never meant for public scrutiny, Badlands argues they are free of the usual propaganda and deal in geopolitical fact.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Lead negotiator of the Iran Deal

A later Badlands piece by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] names Burns as the lead negotiator of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Nuclear Deal]], alongside [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] at the policy level — the two described as "certified swamp creatures in high places."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Burns is listed as a member of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], the [[carnegie-endowment-for-international-peace|Carnegie Endowment for International Peace]] and the board of the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], and as an Epstein associate.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) As former Deputy Secretary of State he headed a secret negotiating team that met high-level Iranian representatives first in 2008 under [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]], and then in earnest in 2013 when the [[barack-obama|Obama]] and Rouhani governments revived the talks.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) He would later become [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s [[cia|CIA]] director.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Carnegie and Chinese money

DeLarme reports that Burns and the Carnegie Endowment made millions off China.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) While serving as president of the Carnegie board, Burns welcomed onto it a Chinese businessman named Zhang Yichen, who had links to two CCP entities — the Center for China Globalization and the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — and who donated between $750,000 and $1.5 million to Carnegie.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Carnegie also received millions from the China-U.S. Exchange Foundation, described as responsible for United Front work in the United States, to build the Carnegie-Tsinghua Center in Beijing.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) The 2008 Russia cable is cited in the later article as evidence that Burns belongs to the very same [[foreign-policy-establishment|foreign policy establishment]] conducting a modern cold war and an active proxy war against Russia.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## November 2023 secret visit to Ukraine

In mid-November 2023, while serving as CIA Director, Burns made a secretive, unannounced visit to Ukraine for urgent meetings with President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and top Ukrainian military and intelligence officials.[3] The trip preceded by days a separate unannounced visit to Kyiv by Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] on 20 November, and commentary in Asia Times concluded that the flurry of senior American visits reflected how badly the war was going on the battlefield.[3] Badlands framed the [[november-2023-us-emergency-visits-to-ukraine|episode]] as evidence that the collapse of the Ukraine proxy war was dragging the [[cia|CIA]] and the Biden administration down with it.[3]

## Epstein calendar meetings (2014)

In April 2023, leaked documents including [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s private schedules — material not contained in Epstein's "black book" of contacts or his flight logs — revealed that Burns had three meetings with Epstein in 2014, while Burns was [[barack-obama|Obama]]'s deputy secretary of state and after Epstein had been convicted of child sex exploitation.[4] The two first met in Washington before Burns visited Epstein at his Manhattan townhouse.[4] The Wall Street Journal reported the calendar, which also listed a top [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] lawyer and [[noam-chomsky|Noam Chomsky]] among Epstein's contacts.[4]

Burns, who became [[cia|CIA]] Director under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in 2021, met Epstein while preparing to leave government service, according to agency spokeswoman Tammy Kupperman Thorp.[4] "The director did not know anything about him, other than that he was introduced as an expert in the financial services sector and offered general advice on transition to the private sector," she said, adding that "they had no relationship."[4] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] cited Burns's appearance on the calendar as evidence that Epstein's [[blackmail-operations|blackmail network]] reached "as high as they get."[4]

## Named in Epstein schedules reporting (2023)

A [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] investigation reported in May 2023, and summarized by [[the-independent|The Independent]], identified Burns — the sitting director of the [[cia|CIA]] — among the figures who met [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] after Epstein's 2008 sex conviction, based on schedules and emails covering 2013 to 2017 that had not previously been reported.[5] Burns's name did not appear in Epstein's public "black book" or in the flight logs of his private jet.[5]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] highlighted that Burns did not join the CIA until after the 2014 meeting in question, treating the sequence as significant evidence for reading the Epstein–[[ghislaine-maxwell|Maxwell]] network as an influence operation aimed at placing compromised figures in senior positions — "infiltration instead of invasion."[5]

### COVID Discovery Team whistleblower letter (September 2023)

On 12 September 2023, House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic chairman [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] and House Intelligence Committee chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] wrote to Burns as [[cia|CIA]] director requesting all documents, communications and pay information concerning the agency's COVID Discovery Team by 26 September.[6] The letter followed whistleblower testimony alleging that the agency had offered payments to six analysts to abandon their assessment that SARS-CoV-2 likely originated in a Wuhan laboratory.[6]

## Second Gaza hostage deal talks (January 2024)

In January 2024, three sources told Reuters that Burns and his Israeli counterpart would meet Qatari officials within days for talks on a second potential Gaza hostage deal.[7] Burns and [[mossad|Mossad]] chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]] were to meet Qatari Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani in Europe that weekend, with Egyptian intelligence chief [[abbas-kamel|Abbas Kamel]] also participating.[7] The [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration had been trying to facilitate the release of more than 100 remaining hostages taken during the [[hamas|Hamas]] attack of October 7, though a considerable distance remained between the two sides' demands; the same channel had helped broker a short-lived truce in November 2023 that freed more than 100 hostages.[7]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that "where the CIA goes, trouble tends to follow" but that lately the reverse seemed truer — the agency being dispatched to problem zones after the administration failed to produce results — a pattern he said first became apparent during the Kabul withdrawal, when a similar [[cia|CIA]] visit preceded the gate detonation that killed US service members.[7] He added that Burns's own admission that the agency was effectively flying blind on the directional plans of nations such as China and Russia supported the view that the Deep State had become "an archipelago of independently-acting — and ass-covering — criminals rather than a masterful, decentralized spy network."[7]

## Doha Gaza talks (July 2024)

As director of the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], Burns met senior Qatari, Egyptian and Israeli officials in Doha on Wednesday, July 10, 2024, seeking to close the remaining gaps in the framework of the Gaza hostage and ceasefire deal and move to detailed negotiations, according to sources with direct knowledge cited by [[axios|Axios]].[8] Israeli and U.S. officials were described as more optimistic than ever about the prospects of an agreement, with the [[white-house|White House]] pressing all parties to close a deal that could secure the release of 120 hostages and end the nine-month war in Gaza, or at minimum produce a 42-day pause and the release of some hostages including American citizens.[8] A key remaining gap centred on [[hamas|Hamas]]'s demand for written commitments from the United States, Egypt and Qatar that negotiations over the deal's second phase would continue without time limit while the first phase was underway.[8] Such a deal was expected to deliver a significant foreign policy win for President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as he fought to restore trust in his leadership.[8]

## December 2023 Gaza hostage diplomacy

On Monday, December 18, 2023, Burns met in Poland with [[david-barnea|David Barnea]], director of Israel's [[mossad|Mossad]] intelligence agency, and Qatari Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Mohammad bin Abdulrahman al Thani]], in the latest effort to secure the release of dozens of hostages still held captive in Gaza, according to a U.S. official familiar with the talks.[9] The same group had met twice the previous month, alongside Egyptian intelligence officials, in Doha, [[qatar|Qatar]].[9]

The meeting followed bilateral talks between Barnea and al Thani the preceding Friday, which marked the first attempt by both sides to restart hostage negotiations after the week-long ceasefire between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] collapsed on December 1.[9] Burns and Egyptian intelligence leaders were briefed on those talks.[9] U.S. and Israeli officials said earlier rounds had stalled over an impasse concerning the remaining female hostages, whose release Israel insisted upon before considering other categories including elderly and infirm men; Hamas claimed the women it continued to hold were IDF soldiers, a characterization the United States and Israel denied.[9]

## May 2024 emergency mission to Doha

On Sunday night, May 5, 2024, Burns travelled to Doha for an emergency meeting intended to salvage the Gaza hostage deal, as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] prepared for a military operation in Rafah and [[hamas|Hamas]] fired rockets at the Kerem Shalom Crossing, injuring ten people.[10] His meeting with Qatari Prime Minister Mohammed Al-Thani was, according to an official briefed on the talks, "aimed at exerting maximum pressure on Israel and Hamas to continue negotiating."[10]

Prospects for a ceasefire appeared slim: Hamas reiterated its demand for an end to the war in exchange for freeing the hostages, and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] flatly ruled that out, insisting the IDF must be free to destroy the group, including in Rafah.[10] One official briefed on the talks told Reuters that "the latest round of mediation in Cairo is near to collapse," and a Hamas delegation left Cairo after a day of talks with Burns and with Egyptian and Qatari officials, though it was expected to return.[10] The framework under discussion would have seen 33 of the 132 remaining hostages released in exchange for a 40-day pause in its first phase.[10]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] questioned why the [[cia|CIA]] rather than the [[state-department|State Department]] was taking point in peace talks, recalling the January 2023 "secret meeting" between Burns and Ukrainian officials reported by the *Washington Post*, which produced no negotiated ceasefire and instead the assessment that "Russia will only negotiate if it feels threatened" — a line he blamed for the media build-up to a Ukrainian spring offensive that never materialised.[10] He argued the episode illustrated that the CIA and the State Department had become functionally synonymous, with the agency performing diplomacy, and characterised Netanyahu's posture as subversion.[10]

## Ceasefire diplomacy at the time of the Haniyeh killing

As CIA Director, Burns was in Rome on Sunday, July 28, 2024 to meet senior Israeli, Qatari and Egyptian officials in the latest round of talks aimed at a temporary ceasefire and hostage-release deal, days before the assassination of [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] in Tehran complicated that effort.[11]

## November 2023 Middle East shuttle mission

In early November 2023 the White House dispatched Burns to Israel, where he arrived on Sunday, November 5 for top-level meetings, with onward stops planned in Qatar, Jordan and the United Arab Emirates.[12] The trip ran in parallel to Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]]'s regional shuttle, which had most recently included a meeting with Palestinian Authority President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] and which publicly sought a "humanitarian pause" rather than a full ceasefire.[12] As in the Ukraine war, Burns was described as the official sent to crisis points to relay messaging the administration did not wish to discuss publicly, potentially including mediation to free hostages or work toward a ceasefire agreement centered on their release.[12] The mission coincided with Iranian foreign minister [[hossein-amir-abdollahian|Hossein Amir-Abdollahian]]'s claim that "the Americans sent us a message in the past three days saying they are seeking a ceasefire," alongside a warning that Americans would be "hit hard" if no ceasefire were reached.[12]
