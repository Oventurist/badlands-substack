# William Burns

William Burns is the U.S. diplomat and intelligence official who serves as CIA director under Joe Biden and who, in February 2008 while working in the intelligence community, authored the classified Moscow cable that supplies "A Bright Red Line" with its title and central evidence.[1]

## The 2008 Moscow cable

The cable, never intended for public consumption but made accessible through the efforts of [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]], set out in explicit terms how Ukraine's admission into [[nato|NATO]] would be viewed in Russia — not only by the [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] government but by the Russian population as a whole.[1]

Burns wrote that Ukrainian and Georgian NATO aspirations "not only touch a raw nerve in Russia, they engender serious concerns about the consequences for stability in the region," noting that Russia perceives encirclement and efforts to undermine its regional influence, and fears unpredictable and uncontrolled consequences affecting its security interests.[1] He reported expert concern that strong divisions inside Ukraine over NATO membership, with much of the ethnic-Russian community opposed, could lead to a major split involving violence or, at worst, civil war.[1]

The cable's key passage states that Ukrainian entry into NATO is "the brightest of all redlines for the Russian elite (not just Putin)."[1] The article reads this as meaning that no action could provoke Russia more, and that Burns made clear such a step would essentially be seen as a declaration of war.[1]

Burns further wrote that in more than two and a half years of conversations with key Russian players, "from knuckle-draggers in the dark recesses of the Kremlin to Putin's sharpest liberal critics," he had yet to find anyone who viewed Ukraine in NATO as anything other than a direct challenge to Russian interests.[1] The article treats this as disproving the notion of a pro-Western Russian constituency rallying behind figures such as Alexei Navalny.[1]

On consequences, Burns predicted that a Membership Action Plan offer would be seen not as a technical step but as "throwing down the strategic gauntlet," that Russian-Ukrainian relations would enter a deep freeze, that Moscow would contemplate economic measures from immediate gas price increases to world market levels to a clampdown on Ukrainian workers in Russia, and that it would "create fertile soil for Russian meddling in Crimea and eastern Ukraine."[1]

## Significance in the article's argument

Because the cable was an internal document, the article argues its contents are void of typical propaganda and deal largely in geopolitical fact.[1] It concludes that Washington has known for at least fifteen years exactly what continuing down this path would produce, and pressed on regardless until a breaking point was reached.[1] The cable is characterized as Burns's warning to an incoming administration that at least outwardly cultivated the appearance of wanting a tenable relationship with Russia.[1]
