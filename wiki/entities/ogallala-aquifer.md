# Ogallala Aquifer

The Ogallala Aquifer is the gigantic underground water body that supplies the High Plains basin of the United States and is cited in Badlands Media commentary as the clearest illustration of the finite nature of groundwater as a drought remedy.[1]

## Depletion

Underground aquifers take thousands of years to replenish, and have historically served as the water source of last resort for drought-stricken regions.[1] The Ogallala has dropped by 19 feet in only 17 years, evidence that such sources "are not going to be there forever."[1] The wider drawdown of U.S. aquifers over recent decades is described as posing a major threat to the drinking water supply and creating a calamitous situation for citizens and food production alike.[1]

## Role in the NAWAPA proposal

Replenishment of the Ogallala was one of the advertised benefits of the [[north-american-water-and-power-alliance|North American Water and Power Alliance]], whose southern distribution circuit was projected to double irrigated farmland from 21 million to 44.3 million acres, refill depleted aquifers such as the Ogallala, and reverse desertification across the southwestern United States and northern Mexico.[1]
