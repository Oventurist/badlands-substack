# King Fahd

King Fahd of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] was, according to the New York Times, party to a 1981 arrangement under which he and other top Saudi officials agreed to aid anti-Communist resistance groups around the world as part of the deal allowing the kingdom to purchase sophisticated American AWACS radar planes.[1] The disclosure of the arrangement, confirmed by present and former United States officials familiar with the sale, showed for the first time one of the origins of Saudi support for anti-Communist groups and the King's personal involvement.[1]

As an example of the arrangement, Fahd and other officials tried in 1984 and 1985 to funnel $15 million to the Nicaraguan rebels; an American businessman said he declined direct requests from the King and other Saudi officials to act as a conduit for money that was to be routed through [[richard-secord|Richard Secord]] and [[albert-hakim|Albert Hakim]].[1] Congressional investigations of Iran-Contra later uncovered a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] bank account in Switzerland holding $250 million donated by the Saudis for the Afghan guerrillas, and the Senate Intelligence Committee heard testimony about roughly $30 million the Saudis donated for the Contras — funds investigators were unable to trace.[1]

Joe Lange argues that nobody in the [[shadow-government]] had a closer relationship with King Fahd than [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]], and that Bush was behind the covert funding scheme that the [[awacs-sale-to-saudi-arabia|AWACS sale]] concealed.[1]
