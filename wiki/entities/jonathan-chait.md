# Jonathan Chait

Jonathan Chait is an American political columnist who writes for [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]]. In July 2026 he authored a column examining the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]]'s renewed call to abolish the [[united-states-senate|US Senate]], arguing that the chamber is structurally undemocratic because every state receives equal representation regardless of population, and that the Senate has become a major obstacle to advancing legislation favored by national popular majorities.[1]

## The Atlantic Senate column

Chait's piece revisited longstanding criticisms of the Senate, including its role in slowing or blocking federal legislation, confirming judicial nominees, and giving disproportionate influence to smaller states. It presented arguments that constitutional reform should be considered, while acknowledging that abolishing the Senate would require an amendment approved through the Constitution's demanding amendment process.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she had scanned Chait's recent writings and concluded that he is "either a card carrying member of the DSA or a closeted one," and characterized him at minimum as a DSA apologist and socialist healthcare advocate with what she called "a scorching case of Trump Derangement Syndrome."[1] She argued that Chait's proposal effectively amounts to wanting a second House of Representatives, and questioned whether he had read the Constitution.[1]

The Badlands reading treats Chait's column as revealing rather than persuasive: in this framing, the fact that the Senate is still described as a barrier to the progressive agenda — "despite all its transformation and ineffectiveness" — is presented as an argument in the Senate's favor.[1] Ashe in America proposed going further in the opposite direction by repealing the [[seventeenth-amendment|17th Amendment]] to restore the Senate as a body representing the states rather than the parties.[1]
