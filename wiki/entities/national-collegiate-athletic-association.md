# National Collegiate Athletic Association

The National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) is the governing body for United States college athletics, which in March 2024 became the defendant in a class-action lawsuit brought by female athletes over its policies permitting transgender athletes to compete in women's events.[1]

## Female athletes' lawsuit (2024)

Over a dozen female athletes sued the NCAA for letting transgender athletes compete against them and use female locker rooms in college sports.[1] At the center of the case is [[lia-thomas|Lia Thomas]], who won the 500-yard freestyle title at the 2022 NCAA Division I Women's Swimming and Diving Championships while a student at the [[university-of-pennsylvania|University of Pennsylvania]].[1] The suit states that the NCAA and Georgia Tech, which hosted the 2022 event, knowingly violated [[title-ix|Title IX]], the federal statute guaranteeing equal opportunity for men and women in college education and sports.[1]

Described as the first federal action of its kind, the lawsuit seeks to change the association's rules so that any biological males are ineligible to compete against female athletes.[1] It further demands that the NCAA revoke all awards given to trans athletes in women's competitions and "reassign" them to their female contenders, and asks for "damages for pain and suffering, mental and emotional distress, suffering and anxiety, expense costs and other damages due to defendants' wrongful conduct."[1]
