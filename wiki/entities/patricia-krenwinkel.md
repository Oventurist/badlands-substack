# Patricia Krenwinkel

**Patricia Krenwinkel** is a former follower of cult leader [[charles-manson|Charles Manson]] and one of the perpetrators of the 1969 Tate-LaBianca murders.[1] Now 77, she was convicted of seven counts of first-degree murder for her role in the killings, which included the murder of pregnant actress Sharon Tate.[1] She has been incarcerated since 1971 and is currently the longest-serving female inmate in California, having spent over 54 years behind bars.[1]

## Parole proceedings

In October 2025, California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] denied Krenwinkel parole, overruling a parole board's recommendation for her release.[1] It was the second time Newsom had done so, the first being in 2022; the board had recommended her release a second time nearly five months before the 2025 denial.[1]

Over the decades, Krenwinkel pursued degrees, maintained a clean prison record, and expressed remorse for her crimes, according to the parole board.[1] Her legal team highlighted her personal transformation, labeling her a low risk to society and emphasizing "the abuse she suffered" under Manson's control, and a 2017 investigation acknowledged her history of victimization in support of her rehabilitation efforts.[1]

Newsom nonetheless concluded that Krenwinkel still posed an "unreasonable danger to society," citing her limited insight into her past actions and a tendency to externalize blame, and arguing that her rehabilitation was outweighed by lingering psychological deficits.[1] He acknowledged her age and the circumstances surrounding the original crimes but asserted that public safety must remain the priority.[1]
