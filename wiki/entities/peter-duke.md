# Peter Duke

Peter Duke is a media figure and commentator in the independent/alternative media space whose analysis of political symbolism became the subject of an extended critique by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] in the November 15, 2025 Badlands Media essay "Don't Be Symbol-Minded."[1] Ehret described Duke as a man he likes very much and considers a friend, while calling the interview Duke gave to [[hrvoje-moric|Hrvoje Moric]] on Geopolitics & Empire "extremely disappointing."[1]

## Positions attributed to Duke

Ehret credited Duke with two points of agreement: an appreciation for the Executive Intelligence Review research on the subject of Lord Palmerston's Zoo, which Ehret said everyone should watch in full, and an appreciation for the concept of [[epistemological-warfare|epistemological warfare]] as the principled battleground shaping history.[1]

Against those, Ehret identified four "epistemological blindspots" in Duke's presentation.[1] The first was Duke's thesis that [[neuro-linguistic-programming|Neuro-Linguistic Programming]] techniques are a substitute for needing to understand any particular topic debated within the information war — the claim being that NLP allows a mind to appreciate structures of logical argumentation and thereby detect holes or fallacies without serious study of the subject matter.[1] The second was Duke's related belief that [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] and AI can produce summaries of complex writings and books in order to save time; Ehret argued that Duke drew no distinction between "thumbnail pseudo knowledge" and true earned knowledge obtained by actively reading source texts.[1]

The third blindspot was the thesis that the appearance of the [[phrygian-cap|Phrygian cap]] on revolutionary banners and flags throughout the 18th and 19th centuries indicates that evil mystery cults have fully controlled every revolution against those same cults from 1776 to the present.[1] The fourth, presented as a corollary of the third, was the conclusion that every nation wielding significant power today is equally committed to global enslavement, depopulation and a dystopian digital hellscape.[1]

## Symbol evidence presented

In his presentation Duke catalogued American state and federal insignia bearing the Phrygian cap, including the states of Iowa, New Jersey, North Carolina, Idaho, Arkansas, Hawaii, New York and West Virginia, as well as the [[united-states-senate|US Senate]], the 1867 Liberty Dollar and the 1783 Libertatis Medal designed by [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]].[1] He also showcased Latin American and Caribbean republics using the cap: Uruguay, Cuba, Argentina, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Paraguay, the City of Rio de Janeiro and Santa Catarina in Brazil.[1]

Ehret did not dispute the empirical catalogue but argued that a purely pattern-recognition reading of it — the kind he attributed to AI systems such as ChatGPT and Grok — leads inevitably to "black pilled" conclusions, and that symbols have always been contested rather than fixed in meaning.[1]
