# Southern Air Transport

Southern Air Transport was a CIA-affiliated air carrier central to the logistics of the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] operation.[1]

After the scandal erupted in 1986, aircraft linked to the carrier were relocated to Ohio bases servicing the empire of [[les-wexner|Les Wexner]], with accounts from local investigators identifying [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] in the transfer.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described this evolution — from arms-related assets to private luxury fleets — as mirroring how covert infrastructure sometimes transitions into elite private use, and faulted the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] for avoiding the episode entirely in its December 2025 investigation.[1]

## Origins and CIA purchase

Southern Air was born in Miami in 1949 as a small charter operation hauling cargo to the Bahamas; eleven years later it still had only three planes and plenty of debts.[2] In 1960 it found an eager buyer: the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], looking to obtain airlines that could do covert work under legitimate cover, purchased Southern Air for $300,000 and extended its operations into the Far East and Latin America.[2] Joe Lange likens this pattern of a tiny company suddenly expanding into key trafficking regions to [[george-hw-bush|Bush]]'s [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]] drilling company.[2]

## Iran-Contra operations

The Los Angeles Times reported that when US officials wanted to ship arms to Iran they called Southern Air Transport, the "worldwide charter specialists" with "oversized cargo capabilities" and "remote site delivery"; when the contras in [[nicaragua]] needed supplies ferried from Portugal to air bases in Central America they dialed the same number; and when Americans running guns to the contras needed mechanics for their dilapidated planes, they used Southern Air too.[2] By late 1986 the House and Senate intelligence committees and a federal grand jury were issuing subpoenas to the Miami-based airline, once owned by the CIA and by then caught up in virtually every thread of scandal tightening around the [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] administration.[2]

In fiscal 1984 the carrier earned $17 million from military hauls, with the contracted total rising to $44 million for fiscal 1987, according to airlift command spokesman Lt. Jerry Todd.[2] Contracted routes included flights into [[ilopango-air-base|Ilopango Air Base]] in El Salvador — a staging area for the contras where the company also stationed mechanics to service the gunrunning planes.[2] Company flight logs subpoenaed by federal investigators showed that in the early to mid-1970s Southern Air had shuttled cargo for the Shah of Iran's air force, its highest-paying client other than the U.S. government, at a time when [[richard-secord|Richard Secord]] was chief U.S. military adviser to that air force.[2]

It was a Southern Air C-123K, loaded with 10,000 pounds of ammunition, that Nicaraguan forces shot down on October 5, 1986, killing former CIA proprietary pilot William J. Cooper — who carried a Southern Air identification card — and capturing [[eugene-hasenfus|Eugene Hasenfus]].[2] The [[justice-department|Justice Department]] admitted holding back its probe of the airline for ten days in late October and early November at the request of national security adviser [[john-poindexter|John Poindexter]], who said the investigation would interfere with arms shipments to Iran and the possible release of American hostages in Lebanon.[2]
