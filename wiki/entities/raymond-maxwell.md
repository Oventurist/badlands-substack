# Raymond Maxwell

Raymond Maxwell is a former United States diplomat who served as Deputy Assistant Secretary at the [[state-department|State Department]] under Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] at the time of the September 11, 2012 [[benghazi-attack|attack on the U.S. mission in Benghazi]], and who later came forward as a whistleblower before the [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee on Benghazi]].[1]

## Position and responsibilities

At the time of the attack Maxwell was a leader in the State Department's Bureau of Near Eastern Affairs, the bureau charged with collecting the emails and documents relevant to the Benghazi probe.[1] That role placed him at the center of the document-production process on which every subsequent inquiry depended.[1]

## Allegation of a document "separation" session

As the House Select Committee on Benghazi prepared for its first hearing in September 2014, Maxwell came forward with the allegation — reported by the Daily Signal — that confidants of Hillary Clinton had taken part in an operation to "separate" damaging documents before they were turned over to the [[accountability-review-board|Accountability Review Board]] investigating the security lapses surrounding the attacks.[1] According to Maxwell, the after-hours session took place over a weekend in a basement operations-type center at State Department headquarters in Washington, D.C.[1] He said he had not been invited to the endeavor but heard about it and decided to check it out on a Sunday afternoon; he testified that two of Clinton's closest aides at the State Department, [[cheryl-mills|Cheryl Mills]] and [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], were overseeing the operation.[1] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the episode as direct evidence of a cover-up rather than of the "mistakes" the official investigations concentrated on.[1]

## Scapegoating and administrative leave

Maxwell, 58 at the time of his disclosure, had strongly supported President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and personally contributed to his presidential campaign, but soured on both Obama and Clinton after Benghazi, saying he had nothing to do with security and had been sacrificed as a scapegoat while higher-up officials directly responsible escaped discipline.[1] He spent a year on paid administrative leave with no official charge ever levied against him; the State Department ultimately cleared him of wrongdoing and reinstated him, and he retired a short time later, in November 2013.[1]

## Why he stayed silent

Maxwell had testified in two earlier Benghazi investigations roughly two years before without ever mentioning the basement document session.[1] The Badlands account attributes his silence to long experience at the State Department: he judged the earlier inquiries to be a "whitewash" designed to make the scandal go away and did not want to risk his life on a sham process.[1] What changed, in this telling, was the launch of the new House investigation led by [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]], a former prosecutor whom the article credits with asking the right questions and with producing the only genuine bombshells to emerge from any Benghazi inquiry.[1]

These characterizations reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
