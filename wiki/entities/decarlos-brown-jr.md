# DeCarlos Brown Jr.

DeCarlos Brown Jr. is an American man charged with the August 22, 2025 murder of [[iryna-zarutska|Iryna Zarutska]], a 23-year-old Ukrainian refugee stabbed in Charlotte, North Carolina.[1]

## Criminal history

Brown, 34 at the time of the killing, had been arrested fourteen times over more than a decade and had been diagnosed with schizophrenia.[1] He was previously convicted of felony larceny, robbery with a dangerous weapon, and communicating threats, receiving a six-year prison sentence in 2015 for incidents dating to 2013 and 2014.[1] Released in 2020, he was charged months later with assaulting his sister.[1] In January 2025 he was arrested for alleged "misuse of the 911 system" and released after signing a written promise to appear, on documents signed by magistrate judge [[teresa-stokes|Teresa Stokes]].[1]

## Media handling of the attack video

Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the video of the attack, released in September 2025, as gruesome and its rollout as shameful — noting that legacy media reported nothing, social media circulated selective clips and stills, and Alex Jones's Infowars published the full footage.[1] Ten members of [[us-congress|Congress]] subsequently demanded Stokes's removal over her handling of Brown's earlier release.[1]

## Mother's account and mental health failures

Brown's mother, [[michelle-dewitt|Michelle Dewitt]], spoke publicly in September 2025, saying local authorities had failed her son by turning him away when the family sought help for his schizophrenic fantasies.[2] Dewitt, 51, said Brown should never have been left to walk the streets a free man given his rapidly deteriorating mental health, stating "He shouldn't have been released" in reference to his January arrest and release on a written promise to appear.[2] She recounted being told at mental health intake either "We don't have enough space" or being asked "Is he trying to kill himself or someone else?" — and that a negative answer left the family with no option but to seek a court order.[2] Dewitt said her son had become convinced someone was controlling him with a "chip" in his brain, and reporting noted that during his January arrest for misuse of the 911 system he told police that "man-made" materials inside him were controlling his body.[2]

Harrowing video appeared to show [[iryna-zarutska|Zarutska]] board the train and sit in front of Brown, who moments later allegedly drew a knife and stabbed her several times; the 34-year-old was charged with first-degree murder over the unprovoked attack and subsequently faced federal charges as well.[2]

## Commentary and competing theories

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Dewitt's account accurately described life with a schizophrenic loved one and used it to indict the [[united-states-mental-health-system|United States mental health system]] as bureaucratic, immoral, and a public safety threat.[2] She noted theories that the killing was a skilled hit with a geopolitical motive — Brown's talk of implanted "materials" evoking MK Ultra and Jason Bourne-style speculation — but concluded that schizophrenia best fit the evidence, including audio of Brown after his arrest.[2] She nonetheless argued Brown should receive the death penalty, quoting his statement that "whoever was controlling the material lashed out at her" and asking at whom else such individuals would lash out.[2]
