# Office of Special Counsel

The Office of Special Counsel (OSC) is an independent federal investigative and prosecutorial agency whose leadership requires Senate confirmation. In 2025 it became the focus of a confirmation fight when President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee to head the agency, [[paul-ingrassia|Paul Ingrassia]], withdrew before his hearing.[1]

## 2025 leadership nomination fight

Ingrassia withdrew his nomination to lead the office on Tuesday, October 21, 2025, after reports surfaced of racially charged text messages he had allegedly sent.[1] The withdrawal came two days before the [[senate-homeland-security-and-governmental-affairs-committee|Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee]] — the panel with jurisdiction over OSC nominations — was set to consider his confirmation, and Ingrassia cited a lack of sufficient Republican votes as his reason for stepping aside.[1] The episode left the agency's permanent leadership unresolved and illustrated the degree to which committee-level Republican support, rather than floor arithmetic alone, governs the fate of second-tier executive-branch nominees.[1]

## 2025 investigation of Jack Smith

The OSC's Hatch Act Unit opened a review in 2025 into former DOJ special counsel Jack Smith over alleged political targeting of Donald Trump, according to an internal email from Senior Counsel Charles Baldis reviewed by the New York Post.[2] The review followed a formal complaint from Senator Tom Cotton alleging Smith timed legal proceedings to influence the 2024 election.[2]
