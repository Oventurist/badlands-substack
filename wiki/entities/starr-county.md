# Starr County

Starr County is a heavily Mexican American county on the Texas border with Mexico that became a widely cited case study in the rightward drift of Latino voters during the 2024 presidential campaign.[1]

## Political shift

A longtime Democratic stronghold, Starr County has been shifting steadily to the right, a trend reporters examined in the summer of 2024 after President Joe Biden exited the race. The county differs from other border communities in some respects — notably its relative dearth of recent migrants — but it illustrates how being Latino has become far less predictive of how a person will vote.[1]

Observers described the area as working class, with politics resembling much of rural America: a reverence for law enforcement and the military, a sense of economic instability, and a nagging suspicion that liberal elites in Hollywood and on Wall Street regard local residents as ignorant hicks. In [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], residents reportedly saw a man offering something different, with one local figure, García, telling a reporter, "People tell me they're going to vote for him... Trump is going to win."[1]

## National significance

The shift observed in Starr County fed into a broader question of whether Democrats were losing their long-standing advantage among Latino voters. Polling in July 2024, before Biden's withdrawal, found his support among Latinos had fallen below 50 percent, and even after [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] won the nomination surveys indicated she was likely to win no more than 58 percent of Latino voters — far below historic Democratic margins.[1]

Analysts stressed that Trump did not need to win a majority of Latino support to retake the White House. Peeling off enough of the roughly 36 million Latino voters, especially in hotly contested swing states such as Arizona and Nevada, could prove to be the margin of victory.[1]
