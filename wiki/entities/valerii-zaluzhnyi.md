# Valerii Zaluzhnyi

**General Valerii Zaluzhnyi** served as commander in chief of the Ukrainian armed forces during the [[russia-ukraine-war|Russia–Ukraine war]] and was a widely popular figure among the Ukrainian public and military.[1]

## Rift with Zelensky, 2024

In late January and early February 2024, Ukrainian and American media reported a growing rift between Zaluzhnyi and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], suggesting that Zelensky intended to dismiss the general and had already informed him of his imminent firing in a recent meeting.[1] Some reports indicated that Zelensky asked Zaluzhnyi to resign and that the general refused.[1]

On Sunday, February 4, 2024, Zelensky publicly confirmed to Italian broadcaster RAI TV that he was considering the dismissal, saying the Ukrainian people wanted "a reset" and that "a new beginning is necessary" after more than a year of brutal stalemate against Russian forces in eastern Ukraine.[1] Zelensky framed the contemplated change as broader than one individual, saying he had "something serious in mind, which does not concern a single person but the direction of the country's leadership," and clarifying that he meant a "replacement of a series of state leaders, not just in a single sector like the military."[1]

## Dismissal, February 8, 2024

Zelensky announced Zaluzhnyi's dismissal on Thursday, February 8, 2024, the biggest military shakeup since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion nearly two years earlier, and named [[oleksandr-syrskyi|Oleksandr Syrskyi]] — Commander of Ukrainian Land Forces since 2019 — as his replacement.[3] The move followed tensions between Zelensky and his hugely popular military chief after the failure of Ukraine's much-vaunted counteroffensive, amid a renewed Russian onslaught, manpower and ammunition shortages, and US aid stalled in [[us-congress|Congress]].[3]

In a Telegram post sent shortly before the formal announcement, Zelensky said he had met with Zaluzhnyi and "discussed what kind of renewal the Armed Forces of Ukraine need," adding that "the time for such a renewal is now."[3] Zaluzhnyi posted on his own Telegram channel that "the tasks of 2022 are different from those of 2024."[3]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the timing as an information-war maneuver, calling it "serendipitous" that Zelensky fired his top general just as Russia returned to the center of the news cycle through the [[tucker-carlson-putin-interview|Tucker Carlson–Putin interview]], and arguing that the Media Industrial Complex was "freaking out because their unseen masters are freaking out."[3]

## Ambassador to the UK and neo-Nazi "role models" remarks (August 2025)

By August 2025 Zaluzhny, retired from the military, was serving as Ukraine's ambassador to the United Kingdom and was widely seen as a potential successor to [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]].[2] Described as one of the country's most popular public figures, polling suggested he would likely defeat Zelensky if presidential elections were held, and Western governments were reportedly courting him as a possible future leader.[2]

In an interview published in late August 2025, Zaluzhny called for education programs that highlight members of the neo-Nazi [[azov-battalion|Azov]] military unit as role models.[2] He praised the Soviet Union's approach to memorializing historic figures and suggested Ukraine adopt a similar model using fighters from the regiment — which is accused of war crimes and recognized as a bastion of militarized neo-Nazism — as examples of proper behavior.[2] "It's very important for the military-patriotic education to know who did what and what came out of it," he said, adding that "Soviet propaganda did it right" and that he had once argued the point with NATO specialists.[2] Ukraine, he said, should "set a goal of what it wants from its children in 10 years," arguing that promoting Azov's "heroism" would be beneficial.[2]

## Role in the Nord Stream sabotage plot

An August 2024 Wall Street Journal report on the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream pipeline sabotage]] identified Zaluzhniy, then Ukraine's army chief, as having given initial backing to the plan to destroy the pipeline.[4] The scheme reportedly emerged from a night of celebration among Ukrainian businessmen and senior military officers, was funded with roughly $300,000, and involved six Ukrainian-backed divers who chartered a 50-foot boat from Germany into the Baltic Sea to plant explosives.[4] President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] initially approved the operation before attempting to halt it following an intervention by the [[cia|CIA]] in June 2022.[4]
