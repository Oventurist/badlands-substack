# United States Postal Service

The United States Postal Service (USPS) is the federal agency responsible for mail delivery throughout the United States. In [[a-seismic-shift|A Seismic Shift]], [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats it as a pivotal institution in what he describes as a plan by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to return power to the American people, arguing that the Postal Service will serve two functions: protecting elections and transforming the banking system.

## Constitutional service obligation

Lange emphasises that, unlike private banks, the Post Office carries what he calls a constitutional public service obligation to all Americans. It serves the whole population rather than catering to the wealthy, and its nationwide footprint tracks population centres rather than commercial activity. Where private banks concentrate where profit is available, the Postal Service operates more than 30,000 retail locations reaching every community in the country.

## History of postal banking

From 1911 to 1967 the agency operated the [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]], a no-cost, interest-bearing public savings option that became one of the largest depository institutions in the country and a safe harbour during the Great Depression. The article argues the system was killed by the introduction of federal deposit insurance for private banks.

## Proposals to revive financial services

The Postal Service [[office-of-inspector-general|Office of Inspector General]] issued a white paper, reported by Postal-Reporter.com, examining a return to financial services. Without making an official recommendation, it suggested the agency could first expand products likely permitted under current law — payroll check cashing, electronic domestic money transfers between post offices, international money transfers, walk-up bill pay, money orders, ATMs and gift cards — subject to approval by the Postal Regulatory Commission. Small loans, reloadable prepaid cards and checking or savings accounts were judged likely to fall outside its current legal authority, though expanded authority could yield significantly more revenue and a greater impact on financial inclusion. The OIG also considered the Postal Service becoming a licensed bank, which it called the most time-consuming, challenging and risky approach but also the one with the highest potential benefit to the financially underserved.

Cheddar News is cited for the argument that postal banking would help consumers avoid predatory nonbank options such as payday lenders and check-cashing companies. FDIC survey data quoted in the article found 6.5 percent of U.S. households (8.4 million) unbanked and 18.7 percent (24.2 million) underbanked. [[mark-dimondstein|Mark Dimondstein]] of the [[american-postal-workers-union|American Postal Workers Union]] and the [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] proposal associated with [[morgan-ricks|Morgan Ricks]] both envisage the Postal Service as the physical branch network for a public money-and-payments system. The [[american-bankers-association|American Bankers Association]] opposes this, arguing it would extend well beyond the agency's core competencies and represent "a seismic shift in our national banking infrastructure."

## The Board of Governors and Trump

Citing Time magazine, the article recounts that the [[usps-board-of-governors|USPS Board of Governors]] fell below quorum by 2014, that none of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s seven nominees received a Senate vote, that by 2016 only James Bilbray remained, and that the board was completely empty when Trump took office in January 2017 — an opportunity Lange says Trump quickly seized. Trump's appointees restored a quorum by August 2019 and in May selected [[louis-dejoy|Louis DeJoy]] as Postmaster General, succeeding Megan Brennan.

## Blockchain voting patent

Business Insider reported in 2020 that the Postal Service had filed a patent for a "reliable voting system" using blockchain technology to improve the security and transparency of election results, under which voters would receive a unique code in the mail to verify their identity and vote online, with the record stored on a blockchain to prevent tampering. Lange argues the patent's real purpose is citizen verification and securing the voter rolls, and predicts it will accompany a national voter ID regime.

## As an argument against income tax

The Badlands Media essay [[a-week-to-remember|A Week to Remember]] cites the Postal Service in questioning the standard justifications for the [[federal-income-tax|federal income tax]]. If income tax pays to fix roads and run the postal service, the article asks, "why are we also paying gas tax, car tabs and postage?"

It also recalls [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s position "that Amazon should deliver their own packages, and our taxes shouldn't be used to subsidize them through the USPS," observing that Amazon now runs a large delivery fleet of its own "and the company seems to be doing just fine."
