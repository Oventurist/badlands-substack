# United States Postal Service

The United States Postal Service (USPS) is the federal institution responsible for mail delivery in the United States. In Badlands Media's analysis it is a central piece of a plan attributed to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to transfer power from private banks back to the people, serving two purposes: protecting elections and transforming the banking system.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Constitutional service obligation

Unlike private banks, the Post Office is described as having a constitutional public service obligation to all Americans; it serves the whole population rather than catering to the wealthy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Where private banks are focused entirely on profit, public banking institutions are framed as focused on serving the people.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The USPS operates over 30,000 separate post offices nationwide, a footprint that tracks population centers rather than commercial activity and therefore reaches communities that commercial banks ignore.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Postal banking history and proposals

The USPS previously operated the [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]] from 1911 until 1967, a no-cost, interest-bearing public savings option available to all Americans.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Contemporary proposals would restore that role. The [[usps-office-of-inspector-general|USPS Office of Inspector General]] produced a white paper examining what financial products the Postal Service could offer, ranging from payroll check cashing, electronic domestic and international money transfers, walk-up bill pay, money orders, ATMs and gift cards under current law, to small loans, reloadable prepaid cards and checking or savings accounts that would require expanded legal authority.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The OIG also examined the possibility of the Postal Service becoming a licensed bank, which it called the most time consuming, challenging and risky approach, but also the one with the highest potential benefit for the financially underserved.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

[[mark-dimondstein|Mark Dimondstein]], president of the [[american-postal-workers-union|American Postal Workers Union]], has argued in The Hill that postal banking as an essential public service is an old idea whose time has come again.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Scholarly proposals would have the Federal Reserve partner with the Postal Service to use its branch network to service [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]], creating a robust public system for money and payments.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The [[american-bankers-association|American Bankers Association]] opposes this, arguing that requiring the USPS to provide banking services would extend well beyond its core competencies and raise regulatory and consumer protection questions.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Elections and blockchain

The USPS filed a patent for a "reliable voting system" that would use blockchain technology to improve the security and transparency of election results.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The patent proposes sending voters a unique code in the mail which they can use to verify their identity and cast a vote online, with that information stored on a blockchain to ensure votes are not tampered with.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media argues the real purpose is not mail-in voting but citizen identity verification and securing voter rolls on a tamper-proof ledger, anticipating a U.S.-owned voter ID law and a cleansing of the voter rolls.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Trump-era governance

When Trump took office in January 2017, the nine-member [[usps-board-of-governors|USPS Board of Governors]] was completely empty, allowing him to fill the entire board; that board in turn selected [[louis-dejoy|Louis DeJoy]] as Postmaster General.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media treats this sequence as deliberate rather than coincidental, part of a behind-the-scenes restructuring of a "once-obscure" governing body into a powerhouse setting priorities for years to come.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Amazon delivery subsidy

In *A Week to Remember*, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cites the Postal Service as an example of a public function Americans are told their income taxes fund, asking why they also pay postage if that were true.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He recalls [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] telling [[amazon|Amazon]] to deliver its own packages rather than have taxpayers subsidise the company through the USPS, and notes that Amazon's own delivery fleet is now ubiquitous and the company is doing fine.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Proposed ballot mail tracking rules (2026)

In June 2026 the USPS proposed new regulations governing the handling of mail-in and absentee ballots for federal elections, citing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[executive-order-14399|Executive Order 14399]] on election integrity.[3] The proposal would establish new tracking, reporting, and processing requirements for ballots sent through the mail.[3]

Under the proposed rule, ballots for federal general, special, and runoff elections would be required to use USPS-approved envelope designs featuring the official Election Mail logo and unique Intelligent Mail barcodes on both outbound and return envelopes.[3] Election officials would also be required to submit ballot-mailing and participation data through a new Federal Ballot Mail Portal, including information used to associate mailed ballots with official election records and to support reconciliation of ballot mailings.[3]

USPS said the proposal would create a standardized process for tracking and verifying election mail, providing greater visibility into ballots moving through the postal system, and stated that the changes are intended to support the objectives outlined in Executive Order 14399 while improving handling and monitoring of election mail.[3] The rule would additionally establish a verification process before USPS accepts outbound ballot mailings from election officials, with non-compliant mailings returned for correction.[3]

## Mail-in ballot rulemaking (2026)

In May 2026 USPS chief executive [[david-steiner|David Steiner]] joined [[white-house|White House]] interagency talks with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] on expanding federal involvement in voter verification and mail-in ballot rules.[4] President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s March executive order [[ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections|"Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections"]] calls on the Postal Service to draft rules requiring states to provide voter registration lists in order to process mail-in ballots.[4] USPS said it was reviewing the executive order and working on draft regulations.[4]

## Litigation over the election executive order (July 2026)

In July 2026 a federal appeals court blocked key portions of President Trump's election administration executive order in 23 Democrat-led states, among them the provision instructing the Postal Service to establish new standards for mail-in ballots.[5] Twenty-three states had sued on the theory that the administration could not govern the Postal Service in this manner, a claim Badlands commentary described as a conflict over authorities and established decision rights.[5] The Brief argued that states plainly hold no decision rights over federal executive agencies and asked, if the President is also denied them, who is constitutionally authorized to exercise the Postal Service's decision rights.[5] The dispute was expected to reach the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] dangerously close to the midterm elections.[5]

## 2020 ballot transport allegations and the final report

After the 2020 general election, the Postal Service became the focus of whistleblower allegations that completed ballots had been trucked across state lines.[6] Contract driver [[jesse-morgan|Jesse Morgan]] said he hauled a trailer of completed ballots from New York to Pennsylvania on October 21, 2020, and that the trailer disappeared after he parked it at the post office in Harrisburg.[6] The [[amistad-project|Amistad Project]] of the [[thomas-more-society|Thomas More Society]] presented sworn declarations claiming multi-state illegal efforts by USPS workers to influence the election in at least three of six swing states, with over 300,000 ballots at issue in Arizona, 548,000 in Michigan, 204,000 in Georgia and over 121,000 in Pennsylvania.[6] Attorney [[phill-kline|Phill Kline]] said 130,000 to 280,000 completed ballots were shipped from Bethpage, New York, to Lancaster, Pennsylvania, where both ballots and trailer vanished.[6]

The Postal Service investigated the incident; its inquiry reportedly concluded in June 2022, but the report was not posted to the agency's website until December 2022 and was later removed.[6] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the report's findings confirm Morgan's account that USPS transported ballots across state lines, that the document is so heavily redacted that its full findings cannot be known, and that it states it was submitted to federal prosecutors.[6] She added that Attorney General [[bill-barr|William Barr]] had shut down a Senate-led investigation of the incident and that the story was subsequently memory-holed.[6]
