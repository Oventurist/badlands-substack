# Qatar

Qatar is identified in the article as both a financier of the Syrian arms pipeline and the conduit by which American Stinger missiles reached the Taliban.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The World Socialist Web Site account cited in the article names Qatar, Saudi Arabia and [[turkey|Turkey]] as the paymasters of the [[cia|CIA]]-directed smuggling network supplying Syrian rebels.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) According to the New York Post, Special Operations sources believed the Stinger fired at a U.S. Army CH-47 in Kunar province on July 25, 2012 came from the same lot the CIA turned over to the Qataris in early 2011 — weapons that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s [[state-department|State Department]] had intended for anti-Gaddafi forces in Libya.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Those sources believed Qatar delivered between 50 and 60 of those Stingers to the Taliban in early 2012, along with an additional 200 SA-24 Igla-S surface-to-air missiles.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The Post also noted that Qatar was expected to hold five Taliban commanders released from Guantanamo for a year before allowing them to travel to Afghanistan, and asked how the Qataris could be trusted with that arrangement given the weapons transfers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article summarizes the episode in one word: treason.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Base for Belhaj and Al-Qaeda financing

"A Conflict of Interest" adds that when [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] was released from Libyan prison in 2009 he travelled to Qatar to take command of the elite [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]] as the 2011 insurrection against the Libyan regime began.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It describes Qatar as one of the biggest funding sources for [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]].2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

In June 2017, following the [[qatar-diplomatic-crisis-2017|Qatar diplomatic crisis]], Belhaj was placed on a terrorist watchlist on suspicion of terrorism-related activities conducted with Qatari support, a claim backed by Egypt, the UAE, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia and Libya's Tobruk government.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article states that with President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s approval, Saudi Arabia and other Arab countries blockaded Qatar precisely to cut off that funding.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)


## 2026 Doha talks venue
Doha served as the announced venue for US-[[iran|Iran]] discussions in late June 2026.[3] The [[white-house|White House]] said Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] would travel there to advance implementation of the two countries' memorandum of understanding, while Iran's Foreign Ministry said only that a delegation would attend technical discussions and denied that negotiations were scheduled.[3]

## Backchannel role in the 2026 US–Iran agreement

Following the early remote signing of the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|US–Iran memorandum of understanding]] in June 2026, media coverage focused on Doha's role in facilitating the diplomacy.[4] [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] reported that Qatar's longstanding ties with both Washington and Tehran helped provide communication channels before and during the conflict, that Qatari officials had been working to broker contacts with the Trump administration before hostilities began, and that the country's unique relationships made it particularly well positioned to facilitate mediation.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that this confirmed years of his own speculation, tracing the dynamic to the 2017 "soft war" between [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the [[arab-league|Arab League]] on one side and Qatar on the other, in which President [[donald-trump|Trump]] backed Saudi Arabia while simultaneously meeting Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Sheikh Tamim]] and promising to help resolve the rift.[4] In his reading, the rift pushed Qatar away from the Arab League and forced Doha to make peace with its longtime rival in Tehran, positioning it later as the backchannel between Saudi Arabia and [[iran|Iran]] and as the broker of the November 2023 restoration of Saudi–Iranian relations after the October 7 attack.[4]

The same commentary revisited the Qatari proposal of 2010 to run a gas pipeline through [[syria|Syria]] into Europe, which [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]] rejected because it would have competed with an Iranian–Russian pipeline, and alleged that Qatar then worked with [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[barack-obama|Obama]] to create [[isis]] to overthrow Assad.[4] It further asserted that Qatar has been branded a sworn enemy of [[israel|Israel]], that accusations of Qatari funding for the October 7 attack in fact trace to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s use of money secured from Qatar on humanitarian grounds after [[hamas]] lost Palestinian Authority funding, and that Israel fired a missile into a residential neighborhood in Doha in September 2025, the day before the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[4] In response, Trump signed a security agreement with Qatar pledging a US military response should Israel repeat such a strike.[4]

Qatar also gifted the United States one of its 747 aircraft to serve as [[air-force-one|Air Force One]] after [[boeing|Boeing]] failed to deliver Trump's ordered replacement on time — described in the commentary as a symbolic gesture defining the evolving American relationship with the Arab world.[4]

## Role in Phase Two of the Gaza plan (January 2026)

Special Envoy for Peace Missions [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] credited Qatar, alongside [[egypt|Egypt]] and [[turkey|Turkey]], with finalizing the particulars of Phase Two of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[comprehensive-plan-to-end-the-gaza-conflict|20-Point Plan to End the Gaza Conflict]], announced in January 2026 as a move from ceasefire to demilitarization, technocratic governance and reconstruction.[5] Badlands analyst [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that most of the Israeli political establishment is opposed to Qatar holding a leadership role in the International Stabilization Force, while Trump specifically wants Qatar involved, both countries having recently demonstrated a close relationship with the President.[5] He attributed Israeli hostility to Qatar's position at the center of a major internal Israeli political scandal over the funding of [[hamas|Hamas]] in the period leading up to October 7 and the extent of the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government's involvement.[5]

## Mediation in the 2023 Gaza war

In October 2023 Qatari mediation with [[hamas|Hamas]] produced the release of four hostages during the period in which Israel, at Washington's request, was delaying its planned ground invasion of Gaza.[6]

## July 2025 White House Gaza talks

On July 8, 2025, a group of senior Qatari officials flew to the White House to meet President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Mideast envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] before [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] returned for the second day of his visit, in a session reported to have lasted over three hours.[7] Witkoff had been scheduled to fly to Doha that night to join proximity talks between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] but delayed the trip in order to remain in Washington and assist the negotiations, with reports indicating that a significant amount of progress still needed to be made.[7] Badlands commentary speculated that the Qatari delegation and the American side were wargaming scenarios for dealing with an increasingly belligerent Israeli government.[7]

## Trump's May 2025 visit

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] visited Doha in May 2025 as part of his Gulf tour, and Qatar announced plans to invest $10 billion in a US military facility.[8] Speaking at a business round table in Doha on the third day of the tour, Trump said his administration was "getting close to maybe doing a deal" with [[iran|Iran]] over nuclear weapons and that "Iran has sort of agreed to the terms," adding that "we're in very serious negotiations with Iran for long-term peace" — talks that were being held behind closed doors in [[oman|Oman]].[8] It was during the same Qatar stop that Trump publicly recounted telling [[apple|Apple]] chief executive [[tim-cook|Tim Cook]] he had "a little problem" with the company's plans to build iPhones in [[india|India]].[8]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] included Qatar among the "problem" regions — with Iran and Syria — where he argued a renewed American peace-through-strength posture would take root, "all the better to keep 'allies' like Israel in check."[8]

## Role in the 2025 Gaza plan negotiations

Qatar was a central broker in the diplomacy around President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s late-September 2025 Gaza peace plan, a role Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] denounced as "re-empowering Qatar as a central broker."[9] Qatar said it was "satisfied" with security guarantees obtained after a call with Trump and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[9] Arab mediators from [[egypt|Egypt]], Qatar, and [[turkey|Turkey]] held talks with [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders in Doha about the plan, which the source described as productive.[9]

## Dispute with the Shin Bet over October 7 funding (March 2025)

On Wednesday, March 5, 2025, Qatar publicly lambasted a probe by Israel's [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] security agency that cited the Gulf state's money to Gaza over the years as having contributed to [[hamas|Hamas]]'s buildup of forces for the October 7, 2023 onslaught.[10] Doha insisted that "no aid has ever been delivered to Hamas's political or military wing" and accused the Shin Bet of "scapegoating Qatar."[10] The agency's report had pointed, among many other factors, to Israel's own yearslong policy of facilitating Qatari payments into Hamas-ruled Gaza; Qatar, which has no diplomatic relations with Israel, has long hosted part of Hamas's political leadership.[10]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Qatar's defense — that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was fully involved in all money flowing from Doha to Gaza, none of it intended for Hamas — reflected more than a minor political dispute, describing it as the fracturing of the [[old-guard|Old Guard]] in the Middle East.[10] In that reading, Qatar had long been under the influence of the [[cia|CIA]], MI6 and the [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]], but Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]] — an old friend of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], likely introduced by their mutual friend [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] — appeared to be working with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[10] The commentary held it reasonable to assume the House of Thani, which traces its lineage back to Abraham through his son Ishmael, would seek to regain its sovereignty, having first settled in Qatar in 1720 and ruled the maritime nation since the 1800s — and that the price of that sovereignty is the death of the Old Guard.[10]

## The 2017 embargo and the Salwa Canal

Badlands Media has described Qatar as "quite the enigma": home to the leadership of a number of Deep State proxy groups — including the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] (attributed to [[mi6|MI6]]) and [[hamas|Hamas]] — and simultaneously a hub for the [[cia|CIA]] and MI6, their NGOs (many run by retired [[state-department|State Department]] and CIA officials), and the U.S. military.[11] With the closure of Bagram in Afghanistan in 2021, Qatar hosts the largest U.S. military base in the Middle East at [[al-udeid-air-base|Al Udeid]].[11]

The rest of the Middle East developed bad blood with the Qataris after the [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]] in 2011, when it became apparent that Qatar was connected to the orchestrated "revolutions" and [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]] became the main cheerleader for the color revolutions.[11] In June 2017 — one month after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Riyadh visit and sword dance — [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] was elevated to Crown Prince over [[mohammed-bin-nayef|Mohammed bin Nayef]], and one of his first acts was to launch an embargo against Qatar backed by Trump and Emirati President [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]], along with Jordan, Egypt, Yemen, Bahrain and several African nations.[11] Ports in Saudi Arabia and the UAE refused Qatari ships and the land border was closed, effectively making Qatar an island; MBS then proposed the [[salwa-canal|Salwa Canal]] to make it a literal one.[11] The net effect was to drive Qatar deeper into a relationship with [[iran|Iran]].[11] The embargo held until January 4, 2021, when Trump negotiated a reconciliation two weeks before leaving office.[11]

## Mar-a-Lago meeting, September 2024

On September 23, 2024, Trump met Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim Al Thani]] and Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani]] at Mar-a-Lago, hailing Qatar's leadership in a Truth Social post at a time when the country was under attack from Republican lawmakers for hosting Hamas and bankrolling Al Jazeera.[11] Qatar has argued that the request to open a Hamas bureau in Doha came from the United States, which under both Democratic and Republican administrations supported its continued operation.[11] Badlands noted the meeting's timing alongside [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s declaration of war against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], and recalled Politico's 2019 report that Qatar had hired [[corey-lewandowski|Corey Lewandowski]] to build ties with the Trump administration.[11]

## Israeli strikes on Doha (2025)

On September 9, 2025, Israeli missile and bomb attacks struck Doha, prompting condemnation from Russia and a sharp increase in military-political tensions across the Middle East.[12] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]], speaking at a Russia–[[gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] Strategic Dialogue in Sochi on September 11, called the [[2025-israeli-strike-on-doha|strike]] a gross violation of international law and of the UN Charter and an encroachment on the sovereignty and territorial integrity of an independent state.[12] He stressed Qatar's role as a key mediator in indirect negotiations between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] over Gaza ceasefire terms, describing the cynicism of attacking a principal mediator and saying Israel's action showed intent to undermine international peace efforts.[12] Badlands commentary framed the moment as Russia stepping in to confront [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], and asked how [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and Lavrov would assist Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Sheikh Tamim]] alongside [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]].[12]

## Role in the September 2025 Gaza plan

Qatar featured in President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 20-point Gaza plan of September 2025, which included a pledge by Israel not to launch further strikes on the emirate.[13] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said he would discuss that clause with the Trump administration but declined to say whether Israel would commit to it.[13] Qatari mediators, working alongside Egyptian counterparts, presented the US proposal to [[hamas|Hamas]] negotiators late on the night of 29 September 2025.[13] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] noted the irony of the arrangement against earlier claims from what he called the Uniparty Right Zionists that Qatar supports terrorism.[13]

## Mediation of Gaza hostage talks (December 2023)

Qatar continued to serve as principal mediator in Gaza hostage negotiations in December 2023, with Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Mohammad bin Abdulrahman al Thani]] meeting [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] and [[mossad|Mossad]] chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]] in Poland on December 18.[14] The same group had convened twice the previous month in Doha alongside Egyptian intelligence officials, and al Thani had held bilateral talks with Barnea days earlier in the first attempt to restart negotiations after the collapse of the Israel–[[hamas|Hamas]] ceasefire on December 1.[14]


## Mediation role after the Doha strike (September 2025)

Following [[2025-israeli-strike-on-doha|Israel's attempt to kill Hamas political leaders in Doha]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] planned to meet Qatari Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani]] in New York, a White House official said.[15] The strike, widely condemned across the Middle East and beyond, risked derailing US-backed efforts to broker a truce in Gaza; Trump described it as a unilateral action that did not advance US or Israeli interests.[15] Washington counts Qatar as a strong Gulf ally, and the country has been a main mediator in ceasefire negotiations between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas]], in efforts to free Israeli hostages held in Gaza, and in post-conflict planning for the territory.[15] Al-Thani blamed Israel for trying to sabotage the chances for peace but said Qatar would not be deterred from its role as mediator.[15]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Trump had already ordered [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] to draft a defense agreement with Qatar that would provide military protection in the event of another Israeli strike.[15]

## Witkoff ties and Hamas funding allegations (2025)

Reporting cited in Badlands coverage of the Huckabee-Witkoff divide described Qatar as the home of the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]], host for years to the late Brotherhood leader Yusuf al-Qaradawi, and a chief sponsor of [[hamas|Hamas]].[16] The account said [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], President Trump's chief negotiator, has business ties worth $623 million to the emirate and had praised Qatar's role to Tucker Carlson, calling claims that Qatar is "not well motivated" preposterous.[16] Hamas — the Gaza arm of the Brotherhood, which massacred over 1,200 people on October 7, 2023, including 32 Americans — has been funded with $1.8 billion by Qatar, which also hosted Hamas leaders in luxury hotels as they accrued personal fortunes exceeding $11 billion and funds the Al-Jazeera network, banned in Israel as a Hamas front.[16] Confirmation hearings for [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] as Ambassador to Israel were framed as a counterweight to that pro-Qatar strategy.[16]

## Boeing 747-8 gift to the Trump administration (2025)

In May 2025 the Trump administration was reported to be preparing to accept a super-luxury Boeing 747-8 jumbo jet from the royal family of Qatar — described as possibly the most valuable gift ever extended to the United States by a foreign government.[17] Sources familiar with the proposed arrangement told ABC News the aircraft, dubbed a "palace in the sky," would be available for use by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the new [[air-force-one|Air Force One]] until shortly before he leaves office, at which point ownership would transfer to the Trump presidential library foundation.[17]

The gift had been expected to be announced during Trump's visit to Qatar on the first foreign trip of his second term, but a senior White House official said it would not be presented or gifted while the president was in the country that week.[17] In a social media post on Sunday night, Trump confirmed his administration was preparing to accept the aircraft, calling it a "very public and transparent transaction" with the Defense Department.[17]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the episode to argue more broadly that the government of [[israel|Israel]] holds significant influence over the United States and particularly over Congress, and called for Americans to reclaim their sovereignty from foreign influence.[17]


## Unplanned Air Force One Refueling Stop (October 2025)

En route to the ASEAN summit in Malaysia in late October 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] made an unplanned refueling stop in Qatar at Al Udeid Air Base, where Emir [[tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]] and Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani boarded Air Force One to greet him.[18] The brief encounter drew regional and international attention, with discussion focused on the Israel-Hamas hostage deal and ongoing efforts to maintain stability in Gaza, where Qatar has acted as a mediator in coordination with international partners.[18] Tamim said that as soon as he learned Trump was coming to refuel, "I said I wouldn't allow him to take off without saying hello," and confirmed it was the first time he had joined a visiting world leader aboard a plane during a refueling stop: "Never, first time."[18]

Trump called the Emir "one of the great rulers of the world, not just the Middle East," and credited Qatari leaders as "a very big factor" in Middle East peace.[18] Badlands commentary framed the stop as deliberate overt support for Qatar at a moment when figures including Laura Loomer, Mark Levin and Benjamin Netanyahu were said to be running a coordinated campaign branding Qatar as an American enemy, and recalled the 2017 White House visit at which Trump described a long friendship with Tamim — likely dating to 2008, when Qatari money reportedly came to the aid of Jared Kushner's family real estate business at the request of Tom Barrack.[18]

Qatar also appeared in the brief as a venue for Colombian conflict diplomacy: delegates of [[gustavo-petro|President Gustavo Petro]] traveled to Doha to open surrender-and-disbandment negotiations with the [[gulf-clan|Gulf Clan]] cartel.[18]

## Role in the January 2025 Gaza ceasefire

[[qatar|Qatar]] served as the operational base for the shuttle diplomacy that produced the January 2025 [[gaza-ceasefire|Gaza ceasefire]]: [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] telephoned [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s aides from Doha to demand an immediate meeting, then returned to Qatar to seal the agreement.[22] The Qatari channel Al-Araby Al-Jadeed carried a senior [[hamas|Hamas]] source's confirmation that a meeting on the draft hostage deal was planned.[22]

Qatar's prime minister publicly acknowledged that momentum toward a deal picked up once Trump secured the White House.[22] [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] also flew to Qatar around the announcement, after which Biden attempted to claim credit for the outcome.[22]

## Role in the Libya arms pipeline

"What Difference Does Benghazi Make?" describes Qatar as the transshipment route used to circumvent the 2011 arms embargo on Libya.[23] After the [[state-department|State Department]] denied [[turi-defense-group|Turi Defense Group]]'s application to ship $195 million in Eastern European weapons to Libya on March 22, 2011, [[marc-turi|Marc Turi]] submitted an identical weapons list naming Qatar as the recipient, and that application was approved.[23] The article notes the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] already had close ties to Qatar, which would in turn forward the arms to Libya.[23] Federal prosecutors later charged Turi on the theory that "Turi's deal with Qatar ultimately was intended to get around the Libyan embargo."[23]

In December 2012 the *New York Times* reported that the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration "secretly gave its blessing to arms shipments to Libyan rebels from Qatar," but that American officials grew alarmed as evidence mounted that Qatar was passing some of the weapons to Islamic militants.[23] Turi told the paper that his own proposed pipeline was shut down "because he was getting in the way of the Obama administration's dealings with Qatar."[23]
