# John D. Rockefeller Jr.

**John D. Rockefeller Jr.** was the only son and heir of [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]], and the transitional figure between the founding of the family's oil fortune and its twentieth-century political and philanthropic reach.[1]

## Inheritance

According to biographer Alvin Moscow, quoted in Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 5," beginning in 1917 and continuing over the next five years the elder Rockefeller handed his fortune over to his only son and heir "with no strings attached."[1]

## Business practices and the Ludlow Massacre

Although Rockefeller Jr. dealt primarily with philanthropic activities, he nevertheless followed his father's business practices, particularly in his opposition to labor unions.[1] That stance softened, at least publicly, after the [[ludlow-massacre|Ludlow Massacre]] of 1914, in which Colorado militia members fired on strikers at the Rockefeller-owned Colorado Fuel and Iron Company, killing forty people.[1]

## Institution building

With his father he helped establish institutions including the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research (1901), the General Education Board (1903), the University of Chicago (1889), the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] (1913), the Lincoln School (1917) — where the Rockefeller children began their educations — and [[rockefeller-university|Rockefeller University]] in New York City.[1] During the Second World War he helped create the United Service Organization (USO) for soldiers, and he supervised the construction of Rockefeller Center in Manhattan.[1] After the war he donated the Manhattan land on which the United Nations headquarters was built.[1]

## Family

Rockefeller Jr. married a daughter of [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson Aldrich]], of Federal Reserve and Jekyll Island fame, making his children the product of a union between the Rockefeller oil fortune and the Aldrich banking-political line.[1] He fathered one daughter, Abby, who died of cancer in 1976 at age 72, and five sons: [[john-d-rockefeller-iii|John III]], [[nelson-rockefeller|Nelson]], [[laurance-rockefeller|Laurance]], [[winthrop-rockefeller|Winthrop]], and [[david-rockefeller|David]].[1] The five brothers incorporated the [[rockefeller-brothers-fund|Rockefeller Fund]] in 1940.[1]
