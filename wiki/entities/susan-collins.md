# Susan Collins

Susan Collins is a Republican United States Senator from Maine.[1] In the 2026 Senate cycle she was the incumbent targeted by Democratic challenger [[graham-platner|Graham Platner]], whose campaign framed defeating Collins as its central objective before collapsing amid sexual assault allegations against Platner.[1]

Platner cited the goal of defeating Collins explicitly when announcing that he would step back from his campaign to consider its future.[1]

## Appropriations chairmanship

Collins chairs the [[senate-appropriations-committee|Senate Appropriations Committee]], and in that capacity became one of the first senior Republicans to challenge President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s handling of federal spending.[2] In late March 2025 she accused the administration of illegally refusing to spend $2.9 billion approved by [[us-congress|Congress]], initiating a letter to the [[white-house|White House]] co-signed by the panel's senior Democrat, [[patty-murray|Patty Murray]] of Washington.[2] The letter asserted that the administration had violated the six-month spending law enacted earlier that month.[2]

The senators objected to a memo Trump had sent to Congress declaring that only part of the $12.4 billion in emergency funding would be spent "because I do not concur that the added spending is truly for emergency needs," arguing that the law requires the administration to spend all emergency money or none of it.[2] The dispute was described as an early salvo in the struggle between Congress and the White House over ultimate power over federal spending.[2] See [[2025-emergency-spending-impoundment-dispute|2025 emergency spending impoundment dispute]].[2]

## Push for ATACMS transfers to Ukraine (2023)

In September 2023 Collins joined Senators [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]], [[roger-wicker|Roger Wicker]] and [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] in a letter pressing President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to send [[atacms|MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile Systems]] to Ukraine, asserting that Ukraine "only requires a small portion of our stockpiled ATACMS."[3] Critics described the proposal as misguided and perilous for the United States and for global stability.[3]

## Vote against Kash Patel (February 2025)

Collins was one of two Republicans whose opposition held the February 20, 2025 confirmation of [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] as [[fbi|FBI]] director to a 51-49 margin.[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentary by Brad Cologero Getz noted that Collins's primary falls in early 2026 and that of fellow holdout [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] in early 2028, and called for strong America-First challengers against both.[4]
