# Lord Palmerston

Lord Palmerston was British Foreign Secretary and the cousin of [[anthony-ashley-cooper|Lord Anthony Ashley Cooper]], with whom he shaped Britain's earliest official support for Jewish restoration in Palestine.[1]

## The 1840 instruction

In 1840, Palmerston echoed Cooper's proto-Zionist outlook in a letter to the British ambassador to Constantinople: "There exists at the present time among the Jews dispersed over Europe, a strong notion that the time is approaching when their nation is to return to Palestine… It would be of manifest importance to the Sultan to encourage the Jews to return and to settle in Palestine… I have to instruct your Excellency to recommend to hold out every just encouragement to the Jews of Europe to return to Palestine."[1]

## Other operations

Palmerston worked with Cooper and the Prince of Wales (later King Edward VII) to create the [[palestine-exploration-fund]] in 1865.[1] The article also states that [[zeev-jabotinsky|Vladimir Jabotinsky]] was groomed in the Freemasonic Young Turk operation set into motion by Palmerston and Giuseppe Mazzini in the 1840s.[1] [[george-hawkins-pember]] is characterized as "a devout Christian Zionist cut from the cloth of Palmerston, Churchill, or Eichmann."[1]

## Cited in Sun Yat-sen's analysis of British policy

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] invokes Palmerston's famous assertion that the "empire has no permanent friends, but rather only permanent interests" to summarize the pattern [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]] described in his 1917 [[vital-problems-of-china|Vital Problems of China]].[2] Sun argued that when a nation is on the ascent, Britain allies with weaker declining powers to undermine it, then reverses the policy once those allies grow strong, so that "the friend who has become strong" is turned into an enemy while England "always remains in a commanding position."[2]
