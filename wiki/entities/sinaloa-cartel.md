# Sinaloa Cartel

The Sinaloa Cartel is a Mexican transnational criminal organization involved in cocaine trafficking through Central America and across the US–Mexico border.[1]

## Alleged control of the ELN

Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] disclosed that the [[national-liberation-army|National Liberation Army (ELN)]] — active since 1963 and historically cooperative with [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]] and the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín Cartel]] — was in fact a proxy of the Sinaloa Cartel, dispatched to secure smuggling routes through Central America for cocaine.[1] Badlands treated this claim as a counterweight to allegations that [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] sat atop the ELN structure.[1]

## US posture

In January 2026, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] said the United States may begin conducting land strikes against drug cartels operating in Mexico, describing cartels running cross-border drug operations as a direct national security threat and claiming that the cartels were effectively running the Mexican government.[1] Badlands, generally skeptical of US claims regarding Venezuela and Colombia, judged Trump to be "speaking the truth" on the Mexican case.[1]

## Indictment of Mexican officials (2026)

In 2026, federal prosecutors in New York charged multiple Mexican officials — including the sitting governor of Sinaloa — with conspiring alongside the Sinaloa Cartel to traffic narcotics into the United States.[2] The Department of Justice alleged a network of current and former officials who used their positions to facilitate drug shipments, provide protection, and enable cartel operations across state institutions, in what was described as one of the most sweeping corruption cases tied to Mexican governance in recent years.[2] Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] defended the accused governors and demanded proof from the United States.[2] Badlands read the choice of Sinaloa as the target as evidence that Mexico, not Venezuela, was the true US objective in Latin America, and as support for the theory that [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] was cooperating with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against the transnational cartel.[2] See [[indictment-of-mexican-officials-in-the-sinaloa-cartel-case|the indictment case page]] for detail.[2]

## Petro's allegations and the 2026 indictments

In February 2025 Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] held a press conference with his generals at which he announced that the Colombian militias operating in the jungles against the Colombian government since the early 1960s were in fact proxy forces of the Sinaloa cartel, which operates out of [[mexico|Mexico]]; according to Petro, the Sinaloa cartel is "the head of the snake" in the cocaine trade.[3] A year later the Trump administration indicted ten Mexican government officials accused of connections to the cartel, amid heightened tension between [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]].[3] Media coverage during the same period increasingly portrayed the intensified campaign against Mexican cartels — expanded intelligence operations, increased surveillance, and the designation of several cartels as foreign terrorist organizations — as laying groundwork for possible US military action inside Mexico.[3]

## Arizona bribery and election-influence allegations (2023)

In February 2023, attorney and forensic investigator [[john-thaler|John Thaler]] appeared on Lindell TV with [[brannon-howse|Brannon Howse]] and former Arizona State Representative [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] to allege that the Sinaloa Cartel used a phony mortgage and real-estate transaction scheme to bribe elected officials and exert control over the state of Arizona, including [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] elections.[4] The Arizona Senate and House Elections Committees held a joint special meeting the same week featuring a presentation alleging collusion between American politicians, appointed officials and the cartel.[4]

Coverage of the allegations emphasized the cartel's role in the fentanyl trade: Tempe police were reported to have seized over 30 million lethal doses of fentanyl, and in 2022 the [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] seized more than 50.6 million fentanyl-laced counterfeit pills and over 10,000 pounds of fentanyl powder nationwide, about half of it from Arizona.[4] Phoenix DEA Special Agent in Charge [[cheri-oz|Cheri Oz]] said the drugs flooding Arizona were "sourced primarily by one evil entity, the Sinaloa Drug Cartel."[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary treated the alleged scheme as a window into cartel–[[deep-state|Deep State]] collusion and criticized the absence of [[justice-department|DOJ]] and [[fbi|FBI]] involvement.[4]


## Record fentanyl seizure and factional violence (December 2024)

On December 4, 2024, Mexican security forces announced the largest fentanyl seizure in the country's history, impounding 1,100 kilograms — about 1.2 tons — of the synthetic opioid in the state of Sinaloa, the cartel's home territory.[5] Mexico's top security official, [[omar-garcia-harfuch|Omar Garcia Harfuch]], said several guns were also seized and two men arrested, with the narcotics found at two properties in the municipality of Ahome after intelligence work and public tip-offs.[5] President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] described the operation as the fruit of a long-running investigation.[5]

The seizure came amid worsening violence in Sinaloa, where factions of the cartel had been engaged in bitter fighting that flared after the capture of kingpin Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada in July 2024.[5] Harfuch pledged that "these actions will continue until the violence in the state of Sinaloa decreases."[5] The cartel, which takes its name from the state, was formerly headed by longtime drug lord Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman, who is incarcerated at the ADX Florence federal prison in Colorado.[5] The announcement followed days after a tariff warning from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5]

## Hired hacker and the killing of FBI informants

A Justice Department Inspector General audit published in June 2025 revealed that the cartel had employed a mercenary hacker to identify and kill [[fbi|FBI]] informants.[6] According to the report, a cartel insider associated with Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman contacted the FBI in 2018 and described the ways in which a "cybercrook" hired by the organisation had helped it track down individuals who might disclose key details about its operations.[6]

The insider said the hacker was surveilling a federal official and exploiting deep-rooted access to Mexico's critical infrastructure to locate informants.[6] The mercenary cybercriminal was described as offering "a menu of services" that included "exploiting mobile phones and other devices."[6] The disclosure formed part of a broader audit of the FBI's efforts to protect its investigations from technological surveillance.[6]

## Named in the 2025 Maduro reward announcement

In the August 7, 2025 announcement of a $50 million reward for the arrest of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] listed the Sinaloa Cartel alongside [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] and the [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel of the Suns]] among the foreign terrorist organizations she said the Venezuelan leader used to move drugs and violence into the United States.[7] Bondi described the cocaine trade as the primary funding source for cartel operations in Venezuela and Mexico.[7]
