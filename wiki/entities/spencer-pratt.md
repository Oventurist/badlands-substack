# Spencer Pratt

Spencer Pratt is a former reality television personality who ran for mayor of [[los-angeles|Los Angeles]] in the city's 2026 election.

As described in [[badlands-brief-25c|Badlands Brief: Elections are Fake; as is Trump's Friendship with Bibi]], Pratt's candidacy drew national attention because of his celebrity profile and his criticism of city leadership in the aftermath of the [[palisades-fire|Palisades Fire]]. His campaign emphasized homelessness, crime, and broad public dissatisfaction with city government, and pre-primary polling showed him competing closely with incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and City Council member [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]]. Vote counting nevertheless left him outside the top two positions required to advance to the November runoff.

Badlands commentators treat Pratt's elimination as suspicious rather than incidental. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], citing [[canncon|CannCon]], notes that Los Angeles is the only remaining U.S. jurisdiction using [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] equipment and concludes that "Spencer Pratt and Smartmatic in LA in 2026 might be about a lot more than who is going to run the City of Angels." [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] describes the primary as "the latest, most cartoonish exhibition" in an accelerating pattern of election subversion.

## Sidewalk power-washing campaign (May 2026)

Earlier in the 2026 campaign, as recorded in [[badlands-brief-3d4|Badlands Brief: Canadian Separatists & Guerilla Campaigns]] of May 26, 2026, Pratt drew national attention for a low-budget guerrilla advertising tactic: using power washers and stencils to blast clean slogans into filthy [[los-angeles|Los Angeles]] sidewalks, including "IMAGINE IF THE STREETS WERE THIS CLEAN" and "SPENCER PRATT FOR MAYOR." [[modernity-news|Modernity News]] framed the stunt as an advertisement against incumbent mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] built on contrast — the dirtier the street, the stronger the message.

At that stage his campaign was running on homelessness, street disorder, anger over the city's fire response, and anti-bureaucracy messaging, with polling and fundraising momentum placing him in serious contention for a runoff. Comedian [[drew-carey|Drew Carey]] publicly called him a "serial scammer," and other critics attacked his residency, his hotel spending and his MAGA-adjacent support. Ashe in America described the approach as "just a smart use of resources," contrasting Bass's traditional and expensive campaign media with Pratt's viral AI videos and low-cost stunts, and noted reporting that he was dominating in fundraising and polling within three points of Bass — while cautioning that the poll in question came from a private research firm with no sponsor disclosed.
