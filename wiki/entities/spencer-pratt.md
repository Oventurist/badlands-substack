# Spencer Pratt

Spencer Pratt is a former reality television personality who ran for mayor of Los Angeles in the city's 2026 primary election and failed to advance to the November runoff.[1]

Pratt's campaign emphasized homelessness, crime, and public dissatisfaction with city government, and drew national attention because of his celebrity profile and his criticism of city leadership following the Palisades Fire.[1] Public polling before the primary showed him competitive with incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and council member [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]].[1] Vote counting ultimately left him outside the top two positions needed to advance, in a field of 14 candidates.[1]

Badlands contributors treated that result as a marker of election manipulation rather than of public preference, noting Los Angeles's continued reliance on [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] systems and concluding that "Spencer Pratt and Smartmatic in LA in 2026 might be about a lot more than who is going to run the City of Angels."[1]

## Sidewalk power-washing campaign (May 2026)

Earlier in the cycle, Pratt drew coverage for a guerrilla advertising tactic: using power washers and stencils to blast clean slogans into filthy city sidewalks, including "IMAGINE IF THE STREETS WERE THIS CLEAN" and "SPENCER PRATT FOR MAYOR."[2] Modernity News framed the stunt as a guerrilla campaign ad against [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]], built on an inverted logic in which the dirtier the street, the stronger the contrast.[2] The tactic complemented a campaign already running on homelessness, street disorder, anger over fire response, and anti-bureaucracy messaging, and coverage at the time showed him with polling and fundraising momentum placing him in serious contention for a runoff.[2]

Los Angeles establishment figures reacted sharply — Drew Carey called Pratt a "serial scammer," while other critics attacked his residency, hotel spending, and MAGA-adjacent support — but the article argued the sneering only reinforced the core visual argument, since a clean city would render the ad meaningless.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the tactic as a smart use of resources against Bass's traditional, expensive campaign media, noting Pratt "keeps hijacking the energy and going viral with AI videos and low budget stunts," and that he is "an entertainer running to be mayor of a city of entertainers."[2] She cited Newsweek reporting that he was dominating in fundraising, and noted that the most recent polling cited by the *New York Times* placed him within three points of Bass — while cautioning that the poll came from a private research firm with no sponsor cited, a category she dismissed as "inbred polling."[2]

## Aftermath and fraud investigation

On June 7, 2026, five days after the primary, it was announced that Pratt had "lost" the jungle primary, with the second runoff spot called for [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]]; Badlands noted that even mainstream observers alleged fraud at that point.[3] Videos from Skid Row residents indicated that Pratt had been excluded from the candidates allegedly promoted to voters who were paid to register, while [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and Raman were promoted.[3] The allegations became part of the federal [[skid-row-voter-fraud-investigation|Skid Row voter fraud investigation]], and [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]] re-shared his March undercover footage on June 8 to underline the point.[3]

## June 2026 election-night lead erased

Pratt led City Councilmember [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]] by roughly eight points in election-night returns in the [[2026-los-angeles-mayoral-election|2026 Los Angeles mayoral race]], but the margin reversed as late mail ballots were tallied over subsequent days, leaving Raman positioned to potentially advance to a runoff against Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]].[4] The reversal was invoked by Republicans as an instance of the [[red-mirage|Red Mirage]] and cited in renewed calls for the [[save-act|SAVE Act]].[4]
