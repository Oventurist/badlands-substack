# Mark Warner

Mark Warner is a Democratic United States Senator from Virginia. In coverage of the debate over artificial intelligence and the American labor market, he appears as a co-sponsor of bipartisan legislation on AI-driven job displacement.[1]

## AI labor legislation

In November 2025, Warner joined Republican Senator [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] in introducing bipartisan legislation intended to collect data on layoffs and hiring slowdowns attributable to artificial intelligence, with the aim of informing future policy.[1] The bill was one of several AI-and-employment measures introduced in [[congress|Congress]], none of which had been enacted as of March 2026 despite hearings on the subject.[1]

The legislative effort emerged against a backdrop of rising public anxiety: a February 2026 YouGov/Economist survey found roughly 63 percent of Americans, including 62 percent of Republicans, expected AI to reduce the number of jobs, while the [[us-department-of-labor|Labor Department]] reported that employers shed 92,000 jobs in February 2026.[1]

## Disclosure on renewed federal-platform contacts (May 2024)

As chairman of the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]], Warner told reporters at a briefing during the RSA Conference in May 2024 that key federal agencies had restarted discussions with social media companies over removing disinformation ahead of the November presidential election.[2] The disclosure marked a stark reversal after the Biden administration had frozen communications with platforms for months while [[murthy-v-missouri|Murthy v. Missouri]] was pending before the Supreme Court.[2] Warner said the talks resumed roughly around the time multiple justices appeared to favor the executive branch's stance during oral argument.[2] The case had been brought by Missouri's then-Attorney General [[eric-schmitt|Eric Schmitt]], who alleged the administration violated First Amendment rights in a bid to suppress politically conservative voices online.[2]
