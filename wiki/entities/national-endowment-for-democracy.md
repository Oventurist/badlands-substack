# National Endowment for Democracy

The National Endowment for Democracy (NED) is a U.S. government-funded grantmaking body created in 1983 to promote democracy abroad. In the Badlands Media article [[a-more-perfect-labor-union|A More Perfect (Labor) Union]], NED is described as "the CIA's chief grantmaking body," funding various "CIA-adjacent cut-outs" including the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute (IRI)]], [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute (NDI)]], [[center-for-international-private-enterprise|Center for International Private Enterprise (CIPE)]], and the AFL-CIO's [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]].

## Mandate

The article characterizes NED's mandate of "democracy promotion" as "code for regime destabilization," a term employed to justify NATO activity in the post-Cold War era. NED is a recurring actor across the Badlands corpus as a central node of the "deep government" and the "plausible deniability network."

## Role in labor operations

NED's funding of the Solidarity Center forms the financial backbone of the AFL-CIO's international operations. The article states that the Solidarity Center receives $30–50 million annually from NED and USAID, and that NED grants exceeding $500,000 supported pro-Western labor groups in Ukraine, a $2 million NED package supported Hong Kong organizations, and grants exceeding $1 million per country funded labor programs in African resource states. Union leaders are described as trained in NED-funded workshops to focus on "worker rights" issues that align with U.S. corporate agendas.
