# National Endowment for Democracy

The National Endowment for Democracy (NED) entered the labor equation in 1983 and is described as the [[cia|CIA]]'s chief grantmaking body, funding a set of CIA-adjacent cut-outs.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) These include the International Republican Institute, the National Democratic Institute, the Center for International Private Enterprise, and the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]]'s [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

NED's stated mandate of "[[democracy-promotion|democracy promotion]]" is characterized as code for regime destabilization, a term used to justify NATO activity around the planet in the post-Cold War era.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article notes that NED and the NED network appear in nearly every piece the author has written in the preceding half-year.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Funding of labor operations

Together with [[usaid|USAID]], NED supplies the Solidarity Center with an estimated $30–50 million annually, money sourced from U.S. taxpayers and directed into programs that train labor activists, fund strikes and shape union leadership to align with Washington's objectives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Specific grants cited include over $500,000 to pro-Western labor groups in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] around the 2014 Maidan uprising, a share of a $2 million package for Hong Kong unions in 2019–2020, and grants often exceeding $1 million per country in mineral-rich African states such as the Democratic Republic of Congo and Zambia.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

Funding carries conditions: grant agreements explicitly or implicitly require recipient unions to align with U.S. goals, and non-compliance risks cuts that leave local unions financially stranded.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Union leaders trained in NED-funded workshops are incentivized toward "worker rights" issues compatible with U.S. corporate agendas rather than nationalization or wage demands.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article concludes that unmasking the Solidarity Center and "its NED puppet masters" is the first step toward reform.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Funding the Atlantic Council

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] lists the National Endowment for Democracy among the funders of the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]], alongside the [[state-department|State Department]], the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], [[usaid|USAID]] and other U.S. and UK government agencies.[2] [[william-burns|William Burns]], lead negotiator of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]], is identified as a member of the NED's board.[2]

## Invoked in 2025 Venezuela commentary

Badlands commentary in November 2025 invoked the National Endowment for Democracy sardonically in criticizing Nobel laureate [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]]'s call for US military action against [[venezuela|Venezuela]], framing the promotion of her candidacy as part of an externally sponsored regime-change effort alongside the [[cia|CIA]].[3]

## Cited in color revolution commentary (2025)

After the [[2025-washington-dc-national-guard-shooting|November 2025 shooting of two National Guard members in Washington, D.C.]], [[canncon|CannCon]] named the National Endowment for Democracy alongside [[usaid|USAID]] as part of the "private sector funding apparatuses" that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had cut off.[4] In his account, the loss of that funding stream left the networks behind NGO-driven protests and riots desperate enough to attempt a domestic [[color-revolution|color revolution]] by politicizing the military and intervening in the chain of command.[4]

## Founding as a plausible deniability layer

Badlands Media places NED's creation at the center of the [[plausible-deniability-network|plausible deniability network]] built in the 1980s, when [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]]'s CIA director [[william-casey|William Casey]] appealed successfully to both sides of the aisle to create new institutions carrying on the CIA's Cold War work at the NGO layer rather than the government-agency level.[5] The governing logic was that more deniability layers made it harder to trace an operation back to the CIA, the Department of Defense, or the [[state-department|State Department]].[5] NED's long-time president Carl Gershman stated plainly that it "would be terrible for democratic groups around the world to be seen as subsidized by the C.I.A. We saw that in the 60's, and that's why it has been discontinued... that's why the endowment was created."[5]

The article describes NED as "the head of the octopus," a grantmaking organization funding cut-outs specializing in regime-change operations, with the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]], the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]], and the [[center-for-international-private-enterprise|Center for International Private Enterprise]] beneath it, each covering a distinct sector of society.[5] Organizations outside the NED umbrella but inside the same network include the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]], the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]], and [[freedom-house|Freedom House]], with [[usaid|USAID]] acting as the funding mechanism.[5] NED was founded less than a year after the CIA published the manual [[psychological-operations-in-guerrilla-warfare|Psychological Operations in Guerrilla Warfare]], a sequence the article treats as the beginning of the "great restructuring" of covert influence work.[5]

## Funding of color revolutions

The 2025 article "How to Rent a Riot - The History of 'Nonviolence'" describes NED as the CIA grantmaking body and notes that it was created in 1983 to channel grants for promoting democracy in developing nations, receiving about $100 million annually from Congress.[6] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] observes that [[gene-sharp|Gene Sharp]] launched his [[albert-einstein-institution|Albert Einstein Institution]] in 1984, the same year NED was established to carry out operations formerly associated with the CIA.[6]

In Serbia, NED poured millions of dollars into the youth movement [[otpor|Otpor!]] and, according to a Guardian figure cited in the article, the U.S. government officially spent $41 million on the year-long operation that removed [[slobodan-milosevic|Slobodan Milosevic]] beginning in October 1999 — the [[bulldozer-revolution|Bulldozer Revolution]].[6] NED funds also underwrote [[freedom-house|Freedom House]]'s translation and distribution of Sharp's *[[from-dictatorship-to-democracy|From Dictatorship to Democracy]]* to 70,000 Serbian activists and operatives.[6]

During Ukraine's [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]], NED joined [[usaid|USAID]] and other foundations in sponsoring [[freedom-house|Freedom House]], the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]], the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]], the [[eurasia-foundation|Eurasia Foundation]] and [[internews|Internews]], pouring millions into Ukrainian civil society for training in protest tactics, media manipulation and election monitoring.[6] DeLarme's closing argument calls on the public to demand transparency from NED and the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] and to scrutinize the funding behind mass movements.[6]

## Continued congressional funding and calls for exposure (2025)

In August 2025 Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described NED as "one of the most subversive and toxic institutions in the game," recalling the outlet's earlier criticism of House Republicans for voting to renew its funding.[7] He argued that while NED remained intact, another major node of the same NGO web — the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] — had been successfully taken over, and framed USIP as a further plausible-deniability layer stacked atop NED in a Matryoshka-doll arrangement descending from the [[state-department|State Department]] through the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and [[usaid|USAID]].[7] DeLarme called for a "seize the institutions" template to be applied to NED, the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]] and the [[center-for-international-private-enterprise|Center for International Private Enterprise]], and for a declassification dump of the NED files comparable to the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]].[7]
