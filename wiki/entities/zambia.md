# Zambia

Zambia is a southern African country that features in the July 2024 Badlands News Brief as a case study in what the Brief characterizes as an African turn away from United States financial and military influence.[1] The Brief pairs two Zambian stories — a central bank move to criminalize domestic use of the US dollar and a parliamentary call for the withdrawal of American forces — and reads them together as evidence of an accelerating de-dollarization and sovereignty trend on the continent.[1]

## Currency policy

The [[bank-of-zambia|Bank of Zambia]] drafted new rules aimed at curbing the use of foreign currencies in local transactions, particularly the US dollar, the country's news agency reported.[1] The regulations were to be issued as a statutory instrument by the minister of finance and national planning, and would require the [[zambian-kwacha|Zambian kwacha]] and its subunit, the ngwee, to be used for all domestic public and private transactions.[1] Individuals caught using foreign currency for local transactions could face up to ten years in prison or substantial fines.[1]

The Brief notes that in February it had been reported that the kwacha was the best-performing African currency against the US dollar, and argues from this that the alleged flooding of Zambia's economy with depreciating dollars was intended to damage a growing economy rather than help it.[1] The commentary frames Zambia's criminalization of the dollar as something "that would have been inconceivable even just a year or two ago."[1]

## US military presence

Opposition Patriotic Front member of the National Assembly [[stephen-kampyongo|Stephen Kampyongo]] told [[rt|RT]] that Zambia has no need for a US military presence and questioned the relevance of security cooperation between Washington and Lusaka.[1] His statement responded to regional concerns about continued [[africom|US Africa Command]] presence in Zambia and to demands from lawmakers for withdrawal.[1] In April 2022, Washington had announced the launch of AFRICOM's Office of Security Cooperation at the US Embassy in Lusaka, described by the US government as improving "military-to-military relations" and expanding cooperation in force management, modernization and professional military education for Zambian security forces.[1]

## Regional context

The Brief connects Zambia's posture to neighboring [[zimbabwe|Zimbabwe]], recalling a fertilizer deal between [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Zimbabwe that it credits with a historic crop yield, and reporting Zimbabwean concern that the United States was consolidating military strength in Zambia and exacerbating a rift between the two historically close allies.[1] Zimbabwean President [[emmerson-mnangagwa|Emmerson Mnangagwa]] is quoted saying "The United States is making us feel lonely," which the Brief reads as a perception of encirclement by Western powers.[1] The Badlands commentary concludes the segment with a one-word verdict: "Accelerate."[1]
