# Dave Calhoun

Dave Calhoun was the chief executive officer of [[boeing|Boeing]] who announced on Monday, March 25, 2024 that he would depart the company at the end of that year, as part of an abrupt leadership overhaul at the embattled plane maker.[1]

## Departure amid the safety crisis

Boeing said it was overhauling its leadership amid its most significant safety crisis in years, and the sweeping changes announced that day included Calhoun's exit.[1] The aircraft maker had been under mounting pressure from regulators, airlines and passengers as it struggled to respond to the fallout from an incident in early January 2024 in which a panel blew off a Boeing 737 Max 9 aircraft midair during an Alaska Airlines flight.[1] The incident roiled a company considered by many to be a prized American institution and renewed concerns about its commitment to safety and quality, five years after two crashes of 737 Max 8 planes killed a total of nearly 350 people.[1]

Alongside Calhoun's announced departure, [[stan-deal|Stan Deal]], head of the division that makes planes for airlines and other commercial customers, retired immediately and was replaced by chief operating officer [[stephanie-pope|Stephanie Pope]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] read the reshuffle through the frame of a "DEI-destroying-the-aviation-industry" narrative, arguing that failures of scale at well-known corporations make the real-life danger of the "woke mind virus" obvious.[1] He asked whether the sacrifice of high-level employees and a change in management would produce a return to reason, safety and profitability, and whether Boeing might become symbolic of an American corporate giant recovering from that ideological capture.[1]
