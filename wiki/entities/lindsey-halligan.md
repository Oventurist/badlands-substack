# Lindsey Halligan

Lindsey Halligan served as a US Attorney in Virginia under the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] until her dismissal by court order, which carried significant downstream legal consequences.[1]

## Consequences of her removal

Halligan's dismissal led to the invalidation of indictments against both New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] and former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]].[1] Subsequent attempts to re-indict James in Virginia failed.[1]

Her removal was part of a broader series of rulings in which federal judges rejected the administration's efforts to install US attorneys without Senate confirmation, alongside removals in New Jersey, Delaware, Nevada, Los Angeles, and — in the case of [[john-sarcone|John Sarcone III]] — the Northern District of New York.[1]

## Halligan precedent and the "blast radius"

In March 2026 coverage, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] explained that the judge in the [[james-comey|James Comey]] case found Halligan's appointment unlawful under the interim-US attorney statute, so that all actions flowing from the defective appointment had to be set aside and the case dismissed; the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] appealed.[2] She argued the Halligan ruling could affect entire portfolios of government legal work from the previous administration — invoking the [[autopen-scandal|autopen]] question — and speculated that the precedent might "effectively erase the Biden Administration," a scandal whose blast radius would be "a mandate-creating experience for the American people."[2]

## Disqualification ruling (November 2025)

On Monday, November 24, 2025, US District Judge [[cameron-currie|Cameron Currie]] concluded that Halligan's appointment by Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to lead the federal prosecutors' office in the Eastern District of Virginia was illegal, and dismissed the criminal cases against Comey and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] because Halligan was the only prosecutor to sign off on both prosecutions.[3] Comey had been indicted in September on charges of lying to Congress in 2020 about media leaks tied to the FBI's Trump–Russia investigation, and James — who sued [[donald-trump|Trump]] in a civil fraud case in 2022 — was indicted two weeks later on mortgage fraud charges.[3] Both argued that Halligan, Trump's former personal lawyer, had been installed to carry out the president's personal vendetta, and judges in each case were weighing those "vindictive prosecution" claims when Currie ended the cases on the appointment question instead.[3]

Currie nonetheless left a window open for prosecutors to try again.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she did not know whether Halligan was "actually illegitimate or just narratively so," and pointed to 18 U.S. Code § 3288 — which permits a new indictment within six months of dismissal even after the limitations period lapses — as evidence that a path to re-prosecution remains among the roughly 93 US Attorneys in the country and its territories.[3]

## November 2025 dismissals and appeal

In November 2025, U.S. District Judge [[cameron-mcgowan-currie|Cameron McGowan Currie]] threw out the cases against [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] on the ground that Halligan, the federal prosecutor who brought them, had been illegally appointed by the [[justice-department|Justice Department]].[4] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly backed Halligan — described in press coverage as a "beauty queen lawyer" — while arguing that the defendants had escaped only "on a technicality," and the administration announced that the Justice Department would appeal the dismissals.[4]
