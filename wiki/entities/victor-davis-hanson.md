# Victor Davis Hanson

Victor Davis Hanson is an American historian and political commentator whose analysis of contemporary American politics has circulated widely in conservative and independent media. In early October 2023 he appeared in a widely discussed interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], in which he argued that the United States was "in the middle of a revolution" and warned that the following twelve months would be "the most explosive in history."[1]

## Argument on American liberalism

In the Carlson interview, Hanson asserted that American liberalism in its current form is characterized by dishonesty, telling his audience that "it's hard for most Americans to comprehend the total dishonesty of American liberalism."[1] He warned of what he described as liberal efforts to usher in a highly intolerant age, and framed the movement's methods as a form of "critical legal theory" in which traditional moral values are abandoned in favor of whatever secures power.[1]

Hanson argued that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] represents a significant threat to the specific vision held by American liberals, and that this perception has escalated into a belief that any means are justified in opposing him. In his formulation, "I think they've come to the conclusion that Trump is an existential threat and by association, half the country is" to the transformation liberals seek, "and so they feel that whatever means necessary are justified."[1] He also offered the observation that "liberals are now telling us they plan to protect American democracy and that's the clearest possible sign that they intend to end it."[1]

## Critique of the Republican response

Hanson coupled his critique of the left with a criticism of the political right. While noting that some conservatives were speaking up, he argued that they were simultaneously fighting a culture inside the Republican Party that preferred to "lose nobly" rather than win elections in an "ugly" manner — a disposition he treated as a structural obstacle to effective opposition.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary on the interview, authored by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], welcomed Hanson's framing as an overdue naming of what independent media had described for years. The commentary invoked George Orwell's image of a "never-ending present in which the party is always right" to describe the transformation of the United States over the preceding decades, and concluded that Hanson and Carlson calling the revolution by its name on one of the largest platforms in new and old media was "better late than never."[1] The episode was treated within the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] as evidence that mainstream-adjacent voices were converging on longstanding independent-media analysis of the [[information-war|Information War]].[1]
