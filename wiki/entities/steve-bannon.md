# Steve Bannon

Steve Bannon is an American political strategist and broadcaster who served as chief strategist in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first administration before departing in 2017 and building an independent media operation.[1]

## 2018 Epstein text messages

In February 2026, reporting based on material connected to the Epstein disclosures described a text-message exchange between Bannon and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] beginning on New Year's Eve 2018, shortly after Republicans lost control of the House of Representatives in the midterm elections. In the exchange, Bannon wrote: "Going to blow him up right out of the box–WH has zero plan to punch back–Fort Apache with no cavalry in sight…" Epstein replied, "He is really borderline. Not sure what he may do," to which Bannon answered, "I think it's beyond borderline — 25 amendment."[1]

The exchange was widely presented as evidence that Bannon had discussed invoking the [[twenty-fifth-amendment|25th Amendment]] against Trump.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] rejected that reading, arguing the more likely meaning is that Bannon was referring to a Democrat strategy to remove Trump via the 25th Amendment — a strategy publicized when *The New York Times* reported in September 2018 that Deputy Attorney General Rod Rosenstein had contemplated it. Q posted about the 25th Amendment for the first time on the day of that report, September 21, 2018, linking the Times article and, hours later, a second article about Omarosa Manigault Newman conspiring against Trump.[1]

The same writer argued that the August 2017 Trump–Bannon "break-up," including Trump's "Sloppy Steve" insults, may have been an early instance of [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] — narrative shielding that took media attention off Bannon while he built a media empire, and possibly a cover that allowed him to ingratiate himself with Epstein.[1] The piece speculated that Epstein, recruited out of Bear Stearns by Adnan Khashoggi as a personal accountant and money launderer, may have possessed detailed knowledge of the financial architecture of the transnational cartel, which could explain the 16-plus hours of recorded interviews he gave Bannon.[1]

Fellow Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Bannon and [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] were "firmly in the crosshairs," with parts of the MAGA base closing ranks with the same media establishment they had previously opposed.[1]

## Epstein files messages (February 2026)

In February 2026 Bannon became the latest focus of the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein files]] controversy after released materials showed what appeared to be a trusted advisor relationship between Bannon and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], with the messages between the two described as damning.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Bannon appeared in the messages as "an Epstein fan boy," ingratiating himself into the financier's inner circle before Epstein's alleged death, and noted that the relationship reportedly produced some 50 hours of footage of Epstein telling Bannon "his side" of many stories.[2] Two of those hours had been released publicly; according to her account they contain little discussion of sex trafficking or crimes against children, instead revealing Epstein's comfortable position within a tight-knit cabal of globalist bankers, with much of the video covering the group's actions around the 2008 financial collapse.[2] She suggested the remaining 48 hours, and even the first two, might furnish prosecutors probable cause to seize globalist assets.[2]

Ashe said she was withholding judgment on Bannon, arguing that the online backlash — led largely by "formerly" DeSantis-supporting [[con-inc|Con Inc]] influencers — represented an operation designed to make the public abandon the strategy aimed at defeating the globalists.[2] Writing separately, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] listed Bannon alongside [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] as targets of a coordinated campaign by the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] and the influencer class, describing Bannon as the author of some of the most culture-shifting speeches in [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s arsenal and the one MAGA voice who had consistently called out election fraud machinery for a full decade.[2]

## Supreme Court clears way for dismissal of contempt case (April 2026)

On a Monday in April 2026 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] vacated the lower-court ruling that had left Bannon's [[contempt-of-congress|contempt-of-Congress]] conviction in place, clearing the way for the case to be dismissed after the Trump [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] asked that the indictment be dropped.[3] Bannon had been convicted in 2022 for defying a subpoena from the House [[january-6-committee|Jan. 6 committee]] and later served a four-month federal prison sentence.[3] The high court did not explain its order but returned the matter for further proceedings in light of the government's new request to end the case.[3]

The ruling does not itself declare Bannon innocent on the underlying facts; instead it removes the appellate decision that had upheld the conviction and opens the door for the lower court to dismiss, giving Bannon a major legal win in a prosecution tied to the congressional investigation of the Capitol attack.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Bannon had already served the time and used the case to argue that the US justice system rarely offers restitution or even acknowledgment when government action harms Americans.[3] She said a similar conversation with her own lead attorney — about a client whose conviction was overturned but who received nothing beyond release — contributed to her decision to fire her attorneys and go pro se in the federal case brought against her by the [[naacp|NAACP]].[3] Invoking the spirit of [[john-adams|John Adams]] on the rule of law, she called Bannon's prosecution a political witch hunt that was also a precedent, observing that throwing out the conviction benefits Bannon but also benefits the regime, and said she hoped he would make them admit what they did.[3]

## "Mortal blow" to the McCain wing (July 2026)

Following the sudden death of Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] in July 2026, Bannon called the loss a "mortal blow" to the "McCain wing of the 'America Last' party," arguing that remaining hawks such as [[tom-cotton|Sen. Tom Cotton]] lacked Graham's gravitas and political skill.[4] The remark framed Graham's death — combined with [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]]'s health-related absence — as an opening for [[america-first-movement|America First]] figures to reduce interventionist pressure on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4]

The Brief separately invoked Bannon's own contempt-of-[[us-congress|Congress]] conviction and imprisonment, alongside that of [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]], as a benchmark against which contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] measured the treatment of former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]].[4]

## Epstein confrontation video (2026)

Alongside the February 2026 release of the [[epstein-library|Epstein Library]], a video circulated online showing a heated exchange between Bannon and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], in which Bannon disparaged Epstein's comments and used profanity during the interaction.[5] The confrontation reportedly began with a discussion about when human life begins, to which Epstein answered "can't be measured."[5]

## Opposition to H-1B expansion (December 2024)

In the last week of December 2024, Bannon and a host of prominent America Firsters all but called for an end to the [[h-1b-visa-program|H-1B]] skilled foreign worker program, opposing [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]], who appeared to favor its expansion.[6] Columnist David Marcus, who described the episode as a MAGA "civil war," argued that an outright end to the program would cause considerable chaos and disruption, but that the debate itself was exactly how politics is supposed to work and left both camps with clearer positions.[6] President-elect [[donald-trump|Trump]] declined to endorse expansion while saying he uses the program himself, and tensions eased by that Sunday.[6]

## "Congress as the Duma" (October 2025)

In remarks to The Wall Street Journal reported in October 2025, Bannon likened the Republican-controlled [[congress|Congress]] to Russia's largely ceremonial Duma, explaining what he saw as legislators' failure to check President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in his second term.[7] Bannon, Trump's former White House chief strategist, argued that the president's four years "in the wilderness" between 2021 and 2025 under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had hardened his resolve: "He's doing things now he wouldn't have ever considered the first time. He's jackhammering away on levels you haven't seen before."[7] The same reporting said senior administration advisers had joked about ruling Capitol Hill with an "iron fist," and offered House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s appearance at the 40th birthday party of Deputy White House Chief of Staff [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] at Ned's Club in Washington on August 23 as an illustration of congressional Republicans bending to the president's will.[7]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] went further than Bannon, writing that Congress and the Senate "are not even ceremonial" but "totally illegitimate," and comparing the arrangement to regimes elsewhere that prop up puppet legislatures to oppose anti-regime leaders.[7]

## Prediction on Trump's first 100 days (January 2024)

In an exclusive interview with [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] published in January 2024, Bannon described what the beginning of a second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] term would look like, predicting that "the first 100 days of President Trump's second term will be the equivalent of the first 100 days of FDR."[8] Where Franklin Delano Roosevelt had, in Bannon's telling, "started the foundations of building the administrative state, the deep state," Trump's opening period would mark "the beginning of the deconstruction of that and the destruction of the deep state, along with dozens and dozens and dozens of other policy proposals."[8]

Bannon, host of the "War Room" podcast, served on Trump's 2016 transition team and then as the White House's chief strategist.[8] He recalled that former New Jersey Governor [[chris-christie|Chris Christie]] had assembled the first transition team and dismissed the resulting work product as "a joke," saying "we reviewed it, but we threw that away"; Christie was quickly replaced as transition lead by [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]].[8] [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], also a member of the transition team, recalled in his memoir "Breaking History" that "during the first few days on the job, every hour felt like a race."[8] Bannon and Kushner were among the figures who set the incoming White House's initial agenda, and contemporaneous reporting often emphasised the ways the two men did not see eye to eye.[8]

## War Room and the 2023 Speaker fight

Bannon's "War Room" podcast served as the venue in which Representative [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] declared, on the Wednesday of [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s election as Speaker of the House in October 2023, that "the swamp is on the run" and "MAGA is ascendant."[9] Gaetz used the appearance to argue that the move from [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] to "MAGA Mike Johnson" demonstrated where power in the Republican Party truly lay, and to detail the internal party deliberations that followed the withdrawal of [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]].[9]

## 2025 warning of electoral catastrophe over Epstein

In July 2025, Bannon warned that Republicans could lose 40 U.S. House seats and the presidency if the backlash over the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] case continued to divide the MAGA base.[10] The controversy followed a Justice Department memo released that month claiming that no Epstein client list exists and that no evidence had been found to justify investigating uncharged third parties — a reversal from Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]]'s February statement that a client list was "sitting on my desk," and from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s repeated campaign-trail promises to declassify the files.[10] Speaking on a podcast, Bannon said the fallout could erode 10% of MAGA's support: "If we lose 10% of the MAGA movement right now, we're going to lose 40 seats in '26, we're going to lose the presidency."[10]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode inversely, arguing that Trump is "tricking the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] Right into destroying itself," that the 2025 Republican majority Congress had accomplished nothing on voters' behalf, and that the Epstein narrative "represents the story of [[state-capture|State Capture]]" — the story that must be told if America is to be saved.[10]

## "MacGuffin" remark on Iran's nuclear program

In late June 2025 Bannon called the Iranian nuclear weapons program a "MacGuffin" — a plot device used to drive a narrative forward but with no basis in reality.[11] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the remark as the closest thing to a [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] rant ever delivered by a Trump insider, and noted that Bannon's accompanying discussion of a Mossad angle matched analysis Badlands had aired during the [[devolution-power-hour|Devolution Power Hour]] and other shows since the start of the 12 Days War.[11]

## Interview with Mike Lindell (June 2025)

On Thursday, June 12, 2025, Bannon interviewed [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]] about what was expected to be the final two days of the [[coomer-v-lindell|Coomer v. Lindell]] defamation trial, confronting him over reports that he was "getting blown out."[12] "Mike Lindell, I can say one thing from the mainstream media," Bannon said. "The coverage they have of you is universally horrible. They say you're getting blown out in this case and that the jury will not be out long to rule against you."[12] Lindell replied that a loss would mean "anyone could get sued at any time with talking about anything that they decide that we don't want to hear from you," and said he hoped God and prayers would yield a favorable verdict.[12]

## Third-term advocacy (2025)

Talk of a [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] 2028 bid gained renewed momentum in October 2025 after Bannon, a Trump ally and former top adviser, said in an interview that there was a "plan" to keep Trump in office, with some influential supporters signaling readiness to test the constitutional limits restricting presidents to two elected terms.[13] Trump himself played coy when asked aboard a Monday flight to Japan, saying he would "love" to run again and citing "the best numbers ever," while ruling out a suggested workaround of running as a vice presidential candidate and then taking over after the election, and pointing instead to Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio as potential successors.[13]


## "Paper of record for the party of Davos"

Bannon's characterization of the [[financial-times|Financial Times]] of London as "the paper of record for the party of Davos" — that is, of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] — was invoked by Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] in April 2025 as an interpretive key to the paper's hostile coverage of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]].[14] On that reading, the Financial Times' displeasure with the tariff regime was itself evidence of whose interests the tariffs threatened.[14]

## War Room platform for the 2023 UN report claim

In April 2023, Bannon's [[war-room|War Room]] program hosted attorney [[elizabeth-yore|Elizabeth Yore]], who used the broadcast to publicize her contention that the [[united-nations|United Nations]] was advancing a report calling for the normalization and decriminalization of pedophilia.[15] Her on-air remarks were subsequently aggregated by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] and carried with commentary in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief, illustrating War Room's function as an originating platform for stories that circulate through the wider independent media ecosystem.[15]

## Fox News FARA accusation

In June 2025 Bannon accused Fox News of being controlled by Mossad and called for a FARA investigation into the network.[16] The remark was cited by Badlands commentators arguing that state media organizations and outlets posing as independent corporate media should both be shut down alongside domestic psychological warfare operations run by the intelligence community.[16]

## Release from prison (October 2024)

Bannon was released from federal prison on October 29, 2024, after serving four months for contempt of Congress, having been held at the Federal Correctional Institution in Danbury, Connecticut.[17] Speaking on his "WarRoom" podcast the same day, the 70-year-old declared that "the four months in federal prison not only didn't break me, it empowered me," adding that he was "more energized and more focused than I've ever been in my entire life."[17] His release followed a federal judge's October 22 denial of his request for early release; he had entered prison in July after the Supreme Court refused to take up his bid to avoid the four-month sentence.[17]

When he began serving the sentence, Bannon called himself a "political prisoner," said he was "proud of going to prison," and framed his imprisonment as standing up to Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] and what he termed a "corrupt" Justice Department.[17] A jury had convicted him in 2022 on two counts of [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] after he refused to comply with subpoenas from the House select committee investigating the January 6, 2021 Capitol breach; Bannon maintained that former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had extended executive privilege over his testimony, and his attorney [[david-schoen|David Schoen]] told the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit that the district court had wrongly excluded evidence that another attorney had advised Bannon he need not comply because of that privilege.[17]

### Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] welcomed the release, recounting the widely circulated story that when the Billy Bush tape surfaced in October 2016, Trump's advisers urged him to step aside in favor of Mike Pence, and that Bannon — who had joined the campaign only weeks earlier — was the lone voice telling Trump the American people were with him and that he should stand in the breach.[17] On that basis the commentator argued that Bannon's 2017 "firing" from the White House was kayfabe, staged so that Bannon could leave a media spotlight that had become obsessive and instead build a new media apparatus, which he duly did.[17] He further suggested the four-month absence functioned as a stress test for the wider movement, revealing who could hold together under pressure, and highlighted Bannon's prison anecdotes about befriending minority and Muslim inmates, his remarks on the "politics of money" and the Great Awakening, and his line that "politics is nothing more than professional wrestling."[17]
