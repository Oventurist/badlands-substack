# Udall Research

Udall Research (also reported as Udall Resources) was a Panama-based company, since dissolved, that built a secret 7,000-foot airstrip in Costa Rica used by the covert private supply network serving the Nicaraguan rebels during the 1980s.[1] The article characterizes it as a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] front company directly tied to the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra scandal]] and to money laundering.[1]

Costa Rican records and Contra pilots identified Udall as the builder of the runway; American crew members on rebel supply runs confirmed it had been used in the operation.[1] The company was reported to be closely tied to other firms that administered secret Swiss bank accounts funneling profits from arms sales to Iran and that set up the covert supply network itself — a network exposed when a rebel plane was shot down over Nicaragua in October 1986.[1] Those companies appear to have been overseen in part by retired Major General [[richard-secord|Richard V. Secord]], a key figure in the Iran-Contra arms affair.[1]

A prominent Panamanian lawyer, identified in reporting as Mr. Quijano, played a key role in setting up three such companies in Panama; Quijano and two clerks in his office were listed as the principal officials of Udall Resources.[1] Panama's appeal for such vehicles lay in its status as one of the world's leading financial centers with Swiss-style secrecy: more than 150,000 companies had been formed there by foreigners, representing interests ranging from major American corporations and the CIA to the governments of Cuba and Nicaragua, at least half a dozen organizations supporting the Nicaraguan rebels, narcotics traffickers, and wealthy Americans avoiding U.S. taxes.[1]

The U.S. Ambassador to Costa Rica, Lewis A. Tambs, helped secure initial Costa Rican permission for the airstrip and was deeply involved in overseeing its use, maintaining close contact with the supply network established by Lieutenant Colonel [[oliver-north|Oliver North]] of the White House National Security Council.[1]
