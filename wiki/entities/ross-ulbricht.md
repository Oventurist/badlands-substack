# Ross Ulbricht

Ross William Ulbricht is the American founder of the defunct darknet marketplace [[silk-road|Silk Road]], who served twelve years in federal prison before receiving a full and unconditional presidential pardon from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on January 21, 2025.[1]

## Prosecution and imprisonment

Ulbricht was arrested in 2013 and sentenced in 2015 to life in prison without the possibility of parole for his role in creating and operating the Silk Road marketplace.[1] Reporting on the pardon described his sentence as forty years plus two life sentences, a punishment Trump publicly characterized as "ridiculous."[1] Critics of the case, including [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators, argued that Ulbricht was never actually tried for the most inflammatory allegations against him: the murder-for-hire accusations that circulated widely in the press were never brought to trial, and the drug-trafficking framing conflated operating a website with dealing narcotics.[1]

## Pardon

Trump announced the pardon in a January 21, 2025 post on Truth Social, saying he had telephoned Ulbricht's mother to inform her that, "in honor of her and the Libertarian Movement, which supported me so strongly," he had signed a full and unconditional pardon of her son.[1] Freeing Ulbricht had been one of Trump's explicit campaign promises to the cryptocurrency and libertarian communities, with the candidate pledging to act on his first day in office if reelected.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for Badlands, [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] argued that the pardon was "long overdue" but did not erase the injustice Ulbricht endured, comparing the prosecution's logic to putting Mark Zuckerberg on trial for every pyramid scheme on Facebook.[1] Gunnels described the Silk Road as "eBay with Tor and Bitcoin" and contended that Ulbricht's real offense in the government's eyes was demonstrating that people could trade freely outside state permission.[1] He further raised the possibility that the marketplace itself was an intelligence operation designed to discredit decentralized markets, with Ulbricht serving as a patsy — concluding that "the government isn't afraid of crime. The government is afraid of justice."[1]
