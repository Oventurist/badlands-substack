# Kurt Lewin

**Kurt Lewin** was a German-American psychologist and another of the key figures who moved from the [[tavistock-clinic|Tavistock Clinic]] into the [[tavistock-institute|Tavistock Institute]] think tank.[1] He is often recognised as the "founder of social psychology" and was among the first to study group dynamics and organizational development.[1]

In the 1940s Lewin developed a model regarded as a cornerstone for understanding organizational change, framing it as a three-stage process he likened to melting a block of ice and refreezing it in a different shape.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] reads this unfreeze–change–refreeze model as a formal description of social engineering: changing a population's belief system through societal and peer pressure, forcing individuals to abandon their own beliefs in order to fit in with society's perceived beliefs — melting the ice and reshaping it into the desired mold.[1] With children, Carlson argues, the ice does not even have to be melted first, which is why the age at which a person is first exposed to a narrative largely determines how deeply it takes hold.[1]
