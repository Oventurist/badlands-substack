# Homeland Intelligence Experts Group

The **Homeland Intelligence Experts Group** was an advisory body established within the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] (DHS) under the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, announced by Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas in September 2023 to "provide advice and perspectives on intelligence and national security efforts" to the Department.[1] It was disbanded in 2024 following litigation, and internal records released afterward became the basis for reporting that the group had treated ordinary political and religious affiliations as markers of domestic terrorism risk.[1]

## Composition and criticism

[[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] (AFL) characterized the panel as "a completely partisan group designed to provide top cover for the Department's radical agenda," noting that its membership included figures associated with the Russia-collusion narrative such as [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] and [[james-clapper|James Clapper]].[1] Critics argued the body functioned less as a source of technical expertise than as political validation for pre-existing departmental priorities.[1]

## Litigation and disbandment

AFL, joined by former Ambassador [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]], filed suit against the group shortly after its formation was announced, and the litigation spurred Republican members of Congress to press the Department over the panel's legality.[1] The complaint alleged violations of the [[federal-advisory-committee-act|Federal Advisory Committee Act]], including the group's lack of ideological balance, inappropriate influence exercised over it by the Biden administration, and a failure to provide public notice and opportunity for public participation.[1] To avoid further litigation and scrutiny, the administration agreed in May 2024 to disband the group and turn over its records to AFL.[1] AFL subsequently reported that "by the time the Homeland Intelligence Experts Group was announced in September 2023, the group had already been meeting for as long as four months," meaning it may have operated for roughly a year in total before being shut down.[1]

## Disclosed materials

Newly released internal files reported in June 2024 indicated that the group had classified supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], members of the military, and people holding religious views as persons likely to commit "domestic violent extremist" attacks.[1] Badlands commentary placed the group within a lineage of federal information-control efforts including the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]], the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]], and the letter signed by 51 former intelligence officials, arguing that each closure is followed by the same personnel and functions reappearing in a new institutional form.[1]
