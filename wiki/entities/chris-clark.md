# Chris Clark

Chris Clark is an American defense attorney who represented [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] during the federal tax and firearms investigation conducted by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], and who led the negotiations that nearly produced a broad plea agreement in 2023.[1]

## Halloween 2022 letter to prosecutors

On Halloween 2022 — three weeks after news leaked that federal agents believed they had enough evidence to charge Hunter Biden with illegally buying a gun as a drug user — Clark wrote to the U.S. attorney overseeing the probe. He argued the leak was "illegal," that a prosecution would be seen as purely political, and that charging his client might even violate the Second Amendment.[1]

Clark then issued an explicit warning: if the Justice Department charged the president's son, the defense would put President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on the witness stand. "President Biden now unquestionably would be a fact witness *for the defense* in any criminal trial," Clark wrote in a 32-page letter later reviewed by [[politico|Politico]].[1]

## Collapse of the plea deal

That letter, together with more than 300 pages of previously unreported emails and documents exchanged between Hunter Biden's legal team and prosecutors, illuminated negotiations that nearly resolved his most pressing legal exposure — the gun purchase and several years of unpaid taxes — and that could have insulated him from future prosecution by a Republican-led Justice Department.[1] The documents showed the deal collapsing abruptly after Republicans attacked it publicly and a judge raised questions about its scope, renewing the prospect of a trial as Joe Biden ramped up his 2024 reelection bid.[1]
