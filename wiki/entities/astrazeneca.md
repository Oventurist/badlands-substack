# AstraZeneca

AstraZeneca is a Cambridge-based pharmaceutical company that produced one of the principal COVID-19 vaccines deployed during the pandemic.[1] In a legal document submitted to the High Court in February 2024, the company acknowledged for the first time that its COVID-19 vaccine "can, in very rare cases, cause TTS" — [[thrombosis-with-thrombocytopenia-syndrome|thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome]], a condition in which a patient suffers blood clots together with a low platelet count.[1]

## High Court class action

The admission emerged in the course of a multi-million-pound class action brought by dozens of families who allege that they or their loved ones were maimed or killed by what they describe as the company's "defective" vaccine.[1] Lawyers representing the claimants have said some of the individual cases could be worth up to £20 million in compensation.[1] AstraZeneca is contesting the claims.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] framed the admission through the lens of [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], arguing that the "chaos and ambiguity" element of that doctrine is precisely what makes the damage of the COVID injection difficult to trace: a biological weapon designed to produce a wide variety of illnesses spread over a long period would render tracking the origins back to the injection nearly impossible.[1] Under that reading, publicly conceding a "1 in 50,000 chance of severe injury or death" functions as cover for companies such as AstraZeneca, since the attention paid to the narrow admission — and to the appearance of corporate responsibility — draws focus away from rising mortality rates in every country that mandated the injections.[1]

Esler compared the admission with polling cited by [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] on [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]]'s program, according to which 25% of Americans believe they personally know someone who died from the COVID jab and 50% believe it causes severe injury and death.[1] He argued that even a limited admission of fatalities "fractures the dam" holding back public rage over the forceful mandates, and that the flip side of the injury toll is [[big-pharma|Big Pharma]] losing its authority over public minds and bodies as a health-freedom movement spreads and companies such as AstraZeneca are put on trial for corruption.[1] The deeper problem, in his account, did not begin with corrupt pharmaceutical companies but with the public giving away its own authority over health and wellness.[1]

## Worldwide withdrawal of the vaccine (May 2024)

In May 2024, months after the company's court admission of a rare side effect, the Oxford-AstraZeneca COVID vaccine was withdrawn worldwide.[3] The shot, marketed as [[vaxzevria|Vaxzevria]], could no longer be used in the European Union after AstraZeneca voluntarily withdrew its "marketing authorisation"; the application was filed on March 5, 2024 and took effect on Tuesday, May 7.[3] The company said similar applications would be made in the coming months in the United Kingdom and in other countries that had approved the vaccine.[3]

AstraZeneca attributed the withdrawal to commercial reasons, saying the vaccine was no longer being manufactured or supplied, having been superseded by updated vaccines targeting newer variants.[3] The decision ended the use of a jab that Boris Johnson had heralded as a "triumph for British science" and that had been credited with saving more than six million lives.[3]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], called the withdrawal "a good first step" and argued that the world was beginning to grasp the scale on which pharmaceutical companies and vaccine manufacturers had defrauded the public, including biotech researchers and medical professionals.[3] He described the weaponization of the medical system as one of the most effective population control schemes on earth and expressed sympathy for clinicians confronting the possibility that their profession had been used against patients.[3]

## Dressen lawsuit

On May 13, 2024, [[brianne-dressen|Brianne Dressen]], a Utah preschool teacher injured after participating in AstraZeneca's COVID-19 vaccine clinical trial, sued the company in federal court, alleging it breached its contract by refusing to pay for the medical care her injury requires.[2] The consent form Dressen signed in 2020 stated that AstraZeneca would "cover the costs of research injuries" and "pay the costs of medical treatment," yet she has spent tens of thousands of dollars on drugs to treat a nervous system disorder and other conditions.[2] Her symptoms included blurred vision, tinnitus, vomiting, extreme light sensitivity, and heart rate spikes.[2] "They left us no choice," Dressen said of the decision to sue.[2]
