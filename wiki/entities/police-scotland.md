# Police Scotland

Police Scotland is the national police service of Scotland, and the body charged with enforcing the [[hate-crime-and-public-order-scotland-act-2021|Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021]] after it took effect on April 1, 2024.[1]

## Role in hate crime enforcement

Senior police officers were reported to be expecting a deluge of complaints over online posts once the new "stirring up hatred" offence commenced.[1] During the force's training program on enforcing the law, officers were taught that even the content of plays and comedy gigs should be considered as potential hate crimes.[1]

Scottish community safety minister [[siobhian-brown|Siobhian Brown]] placed the determination of criminality squarely with the service, stating that a misgendering complaint "could be reported and it could be investigated. Whether or not the police would think it was criminal is up to Police Scotland for that."[1] Among those identified as potentially exposed to prosecution was author [[jk-rowling|J.K. Rowling]].[1]

## First days of enforcement (April 2024)

Police Scotland was deluged with nearly 4,000 complaints in the first day alone after the passage of the new hate crime law, which critics said proved the legislation was being weaponized by activists.[2] Under the legislation, anyone deemed to have been verbally "abusive" — in person or online — toward a transgender person, including "insulting" them, could face a prison sentence of up to seven years.[2] Transgender activists had prepared lists of people they were waiting on to make such comments, including author [[jk-rowling|J.K. Rowling]]; police announced she would not be investigated, though the report itself could generate a permanent "hate incident" file.[2]

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that a rush of mass reporting must be distinguished from actual mass arrests, expressing confidence that few reports would stick and noting that the first 24 hours had already created chaos and deep uncertainty about the law's practicality.[2] In his reading this made the law essentially symbolic — another [[deep-state|Deep State]] operation creating "the illusion that you're losing rights that they have no power over."[2]
