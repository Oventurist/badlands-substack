# John Allison

John Allison is an American banker and libertarian policy figure who, in Badlands Media's telling, formed part of a cluster of sound-money advocates that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] consulted during his 2016 presidential transition.[1] Some observers at the time thought Allison was in the running to be Secretary of the Treasury.[1]

## Career

Allison led the North Carolina-based BB&T bank for 19 years before becoming head of the libertarian think tank [[cato-institute|Cato Institute]], which he ran from 2012 to 2015.[1]

## Views on the Federal Reserve and sound money

Writing in the *Cato Journal* in 2014, Allison argued that he would "get rid of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] because the volatility in the economy is primarily caused by the Fed," contending that when the Fed radically changes the money supply, distorts interest rates and overregulates the financial sector, rational economic calculation becomes difficult; markets form bubbles on their own, he wrote, but the Fed makes them worse.[1] He called instead for "a private, free-banking system based on a market standard such as gold," arguing that had the United States continued with the classical [[gold-standard|gold standard]] rather than instituting what he called a government money monopoly in 1913, the country would have a radically better financial system and higher economic growth today.[1]

## Significance in the Badlands narrative

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents Allison alongside [[judy-shelton|Judy Shelton]] as evidence that Trump's transition-era personnel choices shared two common commitments: a return to the gold standard and the elimination of the Federal Reserve.[1] In this reading, the meetings were not incidental but part of a deliberate, long-running plan to restore hard money and decentralize control of the financial system.[1]
