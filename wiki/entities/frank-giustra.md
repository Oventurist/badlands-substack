# Frank Giustra

Frank Giustra is the Canadian mining financier whom the article identifies as the originator of the chain of transactions that became the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]], and as a major donor to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Quoting the New York Times, the article states that the path to a Russian acquisition of American uranium deposits began in 2005 in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]], where Giustra orchestrated his first big uranium deal with [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] at his side.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The two men flew aboard Giustra's private jet to Almaty and dined with the authoritarian president Nursultan A. Nazarbayev, where Clinton handed the Kazakh leader a propaganda coup by expressing support for his bid to head an international elections monitoring group — undercutting American foreign policy and criticism of Kazakhstan's poor human rights record, including from his wife, then a senator.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Within days of the visit, Giustra's fledgling company [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy Ltd.]] signed a preliminary deal giving it stakes in three uranium mines controlled by the state-run agency [[kazatomprom|Kazatomprom]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The article summarizes the arrangement as a sweetheart deal obtained for a kickback that transformed Giustra's small company into a major player in the uranium industry.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The story remained largely unknown in the United States until early 2008, during [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s failed presidential campaign, when the Times reported the 2005 trip's link to the Kazakhstan mining deal and revealed that several months afterward Giustra had donated $31.3 million to Clinton's foundation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article labels this quid pro quo.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

In 2007 UrAsia merged with the South African company [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] in a transaction described as worth $3.5 billion; the new company kept the Uranium One name and was controlled by UrAsia investors including [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Giustra's personal stake was estimated at about $45 million and, through a spokeswoman, he said he sold it in 2007.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article also notes that Russian investor [[sergei-kurzin|Sergei Kurzin]], formerly of [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]]'s organization, was a partner in both the Kazakh and Uranium One transactions alongside Giustra and Clinton.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
