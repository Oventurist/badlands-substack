# Slovakia

Slovakia is a Central European member state of the [[european-union|European Union]] whose government under Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] emerged in January 2026 as an unusually candid critic of the bloc from within and as a civil nuclear partner of the United States.[1]

## US–Slovakia civil nuclear agreement (January 2026)

A US–Slovakia civil nuclear cooperation agreement was signed on Wednesday, January 21, 2026, covering a wide range of civil nuclear work including reactor technology, supply chains, and safety and security best practices, according to a press release from the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]].[1] In public remarks about the deal, Fico described a shared strategic view of energy focused on "safety, sustainability, and technological maturity."[1] The agreement was signed the same day President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] that the United States was "going heavy into nuclear," and Badlands treated the pairing as evidence of an accelerating [[nuclear-renaissance|nuclear renaissance]].[1]

## Fico's critique of the EU

After meeting Trump, Fico said the EU is "not taken completely seriously" by world leaders, attributing that assessment in part to the bloc's own policy choices — what he described as suicidal migration policies and nonsense climate goals.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Fico's statement, rather than anything Trump said, was the real signal cutting through the noise at Davos, since it stated openly what other European leaders only whisper.[1]

## End of arms transfers to Ukraine (October 2023)

Following the election victory of [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]]'s left-wing Smer-SD party earlier that month on a platform highlighting an end to arms transfers, Slovakia's new government followed through on its pledge to terminate weapons shipments to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and urged the rest of Europe to adopt Bratislava's policy.[2] Fico called on the [[european-union|European Union]] to take on a peacekeeper role in the Ukraine war and said Bratislava would not support further sanctions on Moscow.[2]

Addressing legislators on a Thursday, Fico said Slovakia was abandoning the West's proxy war against [[russia|Russia]]: "I will support zero military aid to Ukraine… An immediate halt to military operations is the best solution we have for Ukraine. The EU should change from an arms supplier to a peacemaker."[2] He added that Slovakia would "no longer supply weapons to Ukraine," would not back Washington's economic war on Moscow without impact analyses, and that "the people in Slovakia have bigger problems than (dealing with) Ukraine."[2]

## Two-sexes constitutional amendment, September 2025

On Friday 26 September 2025 Slovakia's parliament passed a [[slovak-two-sexes-constitutional-amendment|constitutional amendment]] recognising only two sexes, male and female, and restricting adoption rights to married couples, while also guaranteeing equal pay for men and women and requiring parental consent for sexual education.[3] Introduced by the government of Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]], the measure passed with 90 votes in the 150-seat National Council, clearing the required three-fifths majority with the support of twelve opposition lawmakers from right-leaning parties.[3] Justice Minister Boris Susko said the amendment aims to strengthen traditional values.[3]

[[amnesty-international|Amnesty International]] condemned the vote, saying Slovakia had chosen to follow countries such as Hungary "whose policies have led to an erosion of human rights," and the [[council-of-europe|Council of Europe]]'s Commissioner for Human Rights, Michael O'Flaherty, argued that the amendment denies "the realities of trans and intersex people" and could violate Slovakia's international obligations, including access to legal gender recognition.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], contrasting the amendment with Britain's [[nhs|NHS]] guidance on [[cousin-marriage|cousin marriage]], wrote that "Slovakia is based."[3]

## May 2024: assassination attempt on the prime minister

On 15 May 2024, Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] was shot five times at close range in Handlová, northeast of Bratislava, sustaining life-threatening injuries in the first such attack on a European leader in more than 20 years.[4] The shooting sent shockwaves through what reporting described as one of Europe's most polarized countries, where inflammatory politics had become prevalent since the pandemic and had been further turbocharged by Russia's war on Ukraine.[4] Police later widened the investigation beyond the initial lone-wolf theory, and Interior Minister [[matus-sutaj-estok|Matuš Šutaj Eštok]] pointed to the deletion of the assailant's social media communications by a third party.[4]
