# Beryl Howell

Beryl Howell is a federal judge who served as Chief Judge of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, in which capacity she supervised grand jury matters arising from the investigation into efforts to derail the transfer of power after the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## Grand jury secrecy ruling (February 2023)

In a ruling issued in late February 2023, Howell rejected a bid by two news outlets — [[politico|POLITICO]] and [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] — to obtain access to details of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to block testimony by aides before the grand jury.[1] The outlets had petitioned in October of the previous year to unseal portions of the proceedings, citing the historic nature of the secret rulings she had issued; the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] opposed the unsealing.[1]

Howell held that the federal rule mandating grand jury secrecy precluded release of the court opinions and other filings concerning disputes she had resolved behind closed doors, writing in a 32-page opinion that the rule "does not permit such disclosure, at least for now and perhaps forever, and so petitioners' applications are denied."[1] She further reasoned that redaction would be inadequate because "matters occurring before the grand jury are so deeply intertwined with non-secret information" that redacted materials "would prove useless, or worse, misleading."[1]

## Motion to compel Pence testimony (February 2023)

Also in February 2023, federal prosecutors asked Howell, as chief judge of the Washington, D.C. federal court, to compel former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] to comply with a grand jury subpoena and testify as a witness in special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s investigation into the events surrounding the January 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol.[2] The motion to compel was filed in secret to Howell in the days before February 23, 2023, according to three people familiar with the investigation who spoke to CBS News.[2] It followed an assertion of executive privilege by lawyers for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in response to Pence's subpoena — consistent with how Trump's legal team had answered related subpoenas over the preceding year, typically arguing that private conversations or interactions with a president should remain confidential.[2]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued that the dispute concerned not only Trump but also Mike Pence and other members of the first Trump administration, and read Howell's reasoning as implying that readers of even redacted filings could deduce — or be misled about — the identity of the grand jury's targets.[1] Just Human took the ruling "as further indication that the targets probably ARE NOT who the public and press popularly believes them to be."[1]


## Trump Twitter subpoena fine

Howell drew renewed scrutiny in August 2023 for her handling of Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s secret subpoena of Twitter, now [[x-corp|X]], for records connected to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s account.[3] She imposed a $350,000 fine on the company over a three-day delay while it sought to address the demand, and justified the accompanying nondisclosure order in part on a finding that Trump was a flight risk.[3] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] wrote that both the secrecy and the flight-risk rationale were surprising and concerning, arguing that Trump already knew he was under investigation and that the court's reasoning that he might change his course of conduct seemed unlikely given his consistently undeterred social media practices.[3]

## Perkins Coie injunction (2025)

In 2025 Howell issued an injunction prohibiting implementation of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s March 6 executive order restricting the law firm [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]].[4] Chronicles Magazine commentary excerpted by [[badlands-media|Badlands]] described the ruling as one that "spoils a righteous core with judicial activism," conceding Howell was right that the First Amendment and principles of American justice mandate that lawyers be able to deliver candid advice and zealous advocacy to their clients, but faulting her for ignoring the case that Perkins Coie conspired with [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]] to improperly influence the 2016 election through the fraudulent [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]].[4]

The same commentary noted that Howell, an Obama appointee, previously served as chief judge for the District of Columbia, in which capacity she was a strong supporter of [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s Trump prosecution.[4]
