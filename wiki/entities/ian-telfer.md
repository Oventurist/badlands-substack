# Ian Telfer

Ian Telfer is a Canadian mining executive who became chairman of [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] after its 2007 merger with [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]]'s [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy]], and who is central to the donation trail examined in the Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 3.

Public disclosures by the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], which was required to name all contributors to avoid the perception of conflicts of interest during [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s tenure at the [[state-department|State Department]], listed Telfer as the only Uranium One official to give, at "no more than $250,000, and that was in 2007, before talk of a Rosatom deal began percolating," according to the New York Times.

A review of Canadian tax records, however, showed that through his family charity the Fernwood Foundation, Telfer "donated millions of dollars more, during and after the critical time when the foreign investment committee was reviewing his deal with the Russians." The Times itemised $1 million reported in 2009 — the year his company appealed to the American Embassy for help keeping its mines in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] — $250,000 in 2010, the year the Russians sought majority control, $600,000 in 2011 and $500,000 in 2012. These undisclosed gifts came in addition to between $1.3 million and $5.6 million in reported contributions from other people tied to Uranium One or UrAsia.

The Badlands article treats these unreported donations as a breach of Hillary Clinton's disclosure agreement with the Obama White House and notes the irony that concealing income was the same offence — tax evasion — for which [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]] was indicted and later pardoned by [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]].
