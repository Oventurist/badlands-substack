# National Association of Black Journalists

The National Association of Black Journalists (NABJ) is a US professional organization of Black journalists that hosts an annual conference featuring candidate interviews and panels.[1]

## 2024 Trump panel

On Wednesday, July 31, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] made a combative appearance at the NABJ conference during a heated question-and-answer session that at times focused squarely on the race of Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[1] Trump claimed he had not known until a few years earlier that Harris, who is Indian American and Black, was Black, and suggested without basis that she had decided to "turn Black" recently for political gain.[1]

"I've known her a long time, indirectly," Trump said. "And she was always of Indian heritage, and she was only promoting Indian heritage. I did not know she was Black until a number of years ago when she happened to turn Black, and now she wants to be known as Black."[1] He added, "I respect either one, but she obviously doesn't, because she was Indian all the way, and then all of a sudden, she made a turn and … she became Black. ... Somebody should look into that, too," before asking, "Is she Indian or is she Black?"[1]

ABC News' Rachel Scott, one of three moderators on the NABJ panel, noted that Harris — by then the de facto Democratic nominee for president — had attended Howard University, a historically Black college; Trump did not directly respond.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump had deliberately baited the press and that the resulting national argument over whether Harris campaigned as "actual" or "political" Black demonstrated that neither the media nor Harris had substantive arguments to offer.[1] The commentary predicted a swift backlash from Black voters and asserted that racial "communities" exist chiefly within a constructed central narrative rather than in the American zeitgeist.[1]
