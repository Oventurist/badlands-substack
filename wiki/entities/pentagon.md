# Pentagon

The Pentagon is the headquarters of the United States Department of Defense and, by metonymy, the department itself — the executive body responsible for the armed forces, the combatant commands and the deployment of military assets abroad.

## Role in the Badlands corpus

In the Badlands Media article [[a-dereliction-of-duty|A Dereliction of Duty]], the Pentagon occupies an unusual position: it is portrayed not as a culpable actor but as an institution that was ready to act and was prevented from doing so. Citing the report of the [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee on Benghazi]] by way of The Hill, the article states that while the Diplomatic Command Center at the [[state-department|State Department]] in Washington received word of the September 11, 2012 attack "immediately," that notice took about one hour to reach the Pentagon. The article treats this delay as deliberate, arguing that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] "delayed telling the Pentagon because she didn't want them to respond."

The article further notes, on the basis of a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit, that Department of Defense Chief of Staff Jeremy Bash immediately offered assistance to the State Department on the night of the attack, reporting that forces "are spinning up as we speak." Secretary of Defense [[leon-panetta|Leon Panetta]] testified that by 6 p.m. he had identified "three distinct capabilities" to deploy to Benghazi and that by 7 p.m. he had issued the order to deploy the identified assets. An anonymous member of a squadron at Aviano Air Base in northeastern Italy told Fox News that his team was armed, briefed and assembled on the flight line — "we were all ready to go" — but that "only they were waiting for the order. It never came." He added: "At our level, we were doing everything we were supposed to be doing. At everybody else's level above us, it was political."

The article's larger thesis extends this to the conduct of the wars generally. It argues that [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] "attempted to destroy our military (weaken)" by placing the [[white-house|White House]] at the top of the battlefield chain of command, requiring direct presidential approval for each action and thereby functioning as a standing stand down order that allowed [[isis|ISIS]] to expand. By contrast, the article credits [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with removing the White House from the chain of command, after which, it says, the military "was unleashed and destroyed ISIS in less than a year."

## Role in covert labor operations

The Badlands Media article [[a-more-perfect-labor-union|A More Perfect (Labor) Union]] (October 2025) by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes the Department of Defense as playing an integral part in the U.S. labor-influence network. The article states that the DoD "provided cover via NATO-linked training programs that inculcated pro-Western ideologies in foreign labor leaders," and that it "used unions in Asia and Africa for 'hearts and minds' work in Vietnam-era operations." It notes the [[asian-american-free-labor-institute|Asian-American Free Labor Institute (AAFLI)]] "received DoD contracts" to counter communist unions in the Philippines and Indonesia. In the modern period, the article claims the DoD's [[africom|AFRICOM]], "under the guise of 'stability operations,'" coordinates with the Solidarity Center's labor programs in Africa's resource wars to pressure governments to favor U.S. mining contracts. It further states that data gathered by union organizers "feeds directly into CIA and DoD analyses."
