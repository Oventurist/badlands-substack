# Friedrich Schiller

Friedrich Schiller is described in Badlands Media reporting as the great German "poet of freedom", cited as a standard by which to judge whether forms of government constitute better or worse "technologies".[1]

In his 1791 essay *The Legislation of Lycurgus' Sparta vs Solon's Athens*, Schiller wrote that a rule can be established for judging political institutions: "they are only good and laudable, to the extent that they bring all forces inherent in persons to flourish, to the extent that they promote the progress of culture, or at least not hinder it."[1] He added that this rule applies to religious laws as well as political ones, and that "both are contemptible if they constrain a power of the human mind, if they impose upon the mind any sort of stagnation."[1] A law imposing such stagnation, Schiller argued, would be "an assault against mankind" that no laudable intent could justify, being directed against the highest Good and the highest purpose of society.[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] invokes Schiller's criterion against the [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] doctrine promoted by [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]], which he argues imposes precisely the mental stagnation Schiller condemned, and pairs it with [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]]'s conception of government as an applied technology advancing a "science of happiness".[1]
