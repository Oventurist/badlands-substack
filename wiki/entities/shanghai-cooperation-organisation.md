# Shanghai Cooperation Organisation

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) is a Eurasian political, security and economic bloc whose 2025 summit was held in Tianjin, China from August 31 to September 1, 2025.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] described the gathering as having taken place "to remarkable effect," presenting it as evidence that an alternative security and financial architecture is coming online outside the trans-Atlantic system.[1]

## Tianjin summit (2025)

At the summit the presidents of India, China and Russia — Narendra Modi, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, representing what the article calls the three founding nations behind the SCO — stood together and were joined by other world leaders.[1] Collectively the assembled states were said to represent nearly half the world's population and roughly 20 percent of world GDP.[1] The participants advanced a new financial and security architecture premised on principles set out in the [[tianjin-declaration|Tianjin Declaration]].[1]

## Institutional development

Among the summit's commitments was the creation of a new SCO Development Bank intended to provide productive credit to all member states.[1] The article situates this alongside the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]] as components of an emerging financial architecture founded on real-world values rather than speculation.[1]

## Regional instability among members

The article notes that the summit coincided with bloody confrontations launched by Azerbaijan against SCO member Armenia, and fighting between Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan — both SCO members that host Russian bases and Chinese Belt and Road-connected infrastructure.[1] Ehret writes that while the identity of "the arsonists lighting these fires" is not yet known, the timing and character of the conflicts "smells of Anglo-Intelligence operations."[1]

## Stated orientation

The common theme of the SCO discussions and of the broader [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]] is described as the common good, common security, and coordination of action to improve the conditions of life of peoples and sovereign nations, as outlined by the [[united-nations|UN Charter]] rather than by the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]].[1] The article argues that the multipolar powers have made clear they would prefer cooperation with the trans-Atlantic community as allies over going to war, even as events have forced them to prepare for the latter.[1]
