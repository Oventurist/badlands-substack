# Discord

Discord is an instant-messaging and community-chat platform that became a target of state blocking actions during the 2024 wave of government moves against communications platforms described by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]].[1]

## Turkish access ban (October 2024)

[[turkey|Turkey]]'s Information Technologies and Communication Authority published an access-ban decision against Discord on its website in October 2024.[1] Transport and infrastructure minister Abdulkadir Uraloglu said the nature of the Discord platform made it difficult for authorities to monitor and intervene when illegal or criminal content is shared, telling reporters in parliament that "Security personnel cannot go through the content. We can only intervene when users complain to us about content shared there."[1] He added that "Since Discord refuses to share its own information, including IP addresses and content, with our security units, we were forced to block access."[1] The account was reported by Reuters on October 9, 2024.[1]

## Russian ban

[[russia|Russia]] also moved to ban Discord in October 2024, a step reported by the Washington Post and cited by DeLarme as evidence that the platform-blocking trend was not confined to a single government.[1] DeLarme grouped these bans with [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s move to limit the use of [[telegram|Telegram]] within its territory, arguing that governments had grown tired of merely pressuring social media platforms to censor and were now relying on force.[1]

## Significance

Within DeLarme's analysis, the Discord bans illustrate a pattern in which encrypted or hard-to-surveil communication tools are blocked when their operators refuse to hand over IP addresses and content to security services — the same logic underlying the French charge against [[pavel-durov|Pavel Durov]] for providing cryptology services without prior declaration.[1]
