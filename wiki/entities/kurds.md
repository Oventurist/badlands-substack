# Kurds

The Kurds are an ethnic group across Iran, Iraq, Syria and Turkey whose armed groups served as intermediaries in a U.S. attempt to arm anti-government protesters in [[iran|Iran]] in early 2026.[1]

[[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] said the United States attempted to send weapons to Iranian protesters, in what appeared to be the first public acknowledgment of such involvement.[1] He said the weapons were routed through Kurdish intermediaries but largely failed to reach the intended recipients, adding that he believes Kurdish groups may have kept them instead.[1] The effort ultimately did not deliver arms to the protesters, whose weeks-long, economically driven demonstrations had prompted unrest across the country.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the episode as the "Zionist/NeoCon [[deep-state|Deep State]]" sending guns to operatives intended to overthrow the Iranian government, only for the Kurds — described as loyal to Trump and his Muslim allies in the region — to intercept and withhold them.[1] He read this as evidence that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] is succeeding in subverting Deep State plans, and as further sign that operations are being run against [[israel|Israel]] and the anti-Iran neoconservative faction.[1]

## Role in the 2026 Iran conflict

During the March 2026 US–Israeli campaign against [[iran|Iran]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was reported to have asked the Kurds to assist with the war effort.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied this to his theory of [[davids-corridor|David's Corridor]], arguing that once the Euphrates River is crossed one enters Kurdistan, and that the Kurds could help move soldiers and equipment into Iran.[2]

## Trump rules out Kurdish involvement (March 2026)

The war involving [[iran|Iran]], [[israel|Israel]] and the United States produced debate over Kurdish participation.[3] While earlier reporting indicated U.S. officials had explored supporting Kurdish groups operating inside Iran, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] later said he did not want Kurdish forces entering the country as part of the conflict.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the reversal as a defeat for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and the NeoCons, who he said had been pushing the Kurds as the best option for a ground force invasion of Iran.[3] He noted that reports of Trump courting the Kurds had been published by the [[washington-post|Washington Post]], which he characterized as "the [[cia|CIA]]'s mouthpiece," and that Trump "poured water on these reports," expressly stating he had personally ruled out Kurdish involvement.[3]
