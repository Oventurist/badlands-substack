# USS Gerald R. Ford

The USS Gerald R. Ford is the flagship of the United States Navy, the largest aircraft carrier in the world and, by displacement, the largest warship ever built.[1] Construction of the ship began in 2005; she was christened in 2013 and formally commissioned by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in July 2017.[1] Despite being commissioned in 2017, the carrier did not undertake her first operational deployment until 2023.[1]

## Deployment history

The Ford's inaugural 2023 deployment coincided with the October 7th attack by [[hamas|Hamas]] against [[israel|Israel]], after which the carrier was rerouted to the eastern Mediterranean to bolster deterrence efforts.[1] She returned to Norfolk, Virginia in January 2024 and remained in port for seventeen months.[1]

The carrier's second deployment began on June 24, 2025, during the [[twelve-day-war|12 Day War]], when she was again dispatched toward Israel in a support role.[1] She did not arrive until July, after the war had ostensibly concluded, and spent roughly a month in theater before proceeding to Norway for a scheduled port visit and joint naval exercises with European partners.[1]

In October 2025 the Ford was redeployed to the waters off Venezuela to support operations directed against [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], then redeployed once more to the Middle East in January 2026 to "provide support" during the unrest in [[iran|Iran]] — support that Badlands commentary attributed to CIA and Mossad-linked activity rather than organic protest.[1] By that point the ship had already exceeded its planned eight-month deployment, and was projected to reach roughly eleven months at sea by the time it returned to Norfolk, approaching the record 332-day deployment set by the USS Midway during the Vietnam War.[1]

## Return transit and analytical significance

On May 6, 2026, USNI News reported that the Ford had transited the Strait of Gibraltar and entered the Atlantic, homeward bound for Norfolk after 315 days deployed; an accompanying imagery post on X was timestamped 7:08 AM EST.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that this report appeared just nine minutes before Trump posted a fresh threat to resume the bombing campaign against Iran.[1]

The Ford leads Carrier Strike Group Twelve, comprising at least three destroyers, which escorted the carrier home.[1] Working from a transit speed of 20 to 25 knots, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry estimated a four-to-five-day passage from the eastern Mediterranean to Gibraltar, placing the carrier's departure from the area of operations on the Friday or Saturday before [[project-freedom|Project Freedom]] was announced on Sunday and implemented on Monday.[1] He argued that withdrawing the Navy's most valuable asset immediately before an initiative that could plausibly produce a naval engagement made no sense as leverage, and concluded that the underlying deal with Iran had already been struck and that Project Freedom functioned as a narrative vehicle sustaining the optics of an ongoing conflict.[1]

## Repositioning from Venezuela (February 2026)

In February 2026, news broke that the United States was repositioning the Gerald R. Ford carrier strike group from waters off [[venezuela|Venezuela]] to the Middle East.[2] The move was reported the same week Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] left Washington without an announced breakthrough on [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program, and as the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] publicly normalized energy relations with Caracas.[2]

## Return from the Israel crisis deployment

In a development Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described as running "somewhat under the radar," the USS Ford carrier was announced at the start of January 2024 to be returning to the United States after an extended deployment monitoring the Israel crisis.[3] The carrier was reportedly being replaced by another with 2,000 marines aboard, and Burning Bright cautioned that "any mass change in operations in this theater should be watched closely."[3]
