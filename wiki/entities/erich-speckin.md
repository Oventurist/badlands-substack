# Erich Speckin

Erich Speckin is a forensic document examiner who served as the plaintiffs' handwriting and signature verification expert during the second phase of [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]'s election challenge trial in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona.[1]

## Testimony in Lake v. Hobbs

On Thursday, May 18, 2023 — day two of the trial in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]] — Speckin testified regarding the speed at which Maricopa County reviewers processed mail-in ballot signature comparisons.[1] In his opinion, the 45,670 signatures approved by the reviewers identified in county data as User 26 and User 9 should be removed from the pool of 321,495 signatures compared in under three seconds, because those "comparisons" were completed so quickly that they could not conceivably have been performed by a human being.[1]

Speckin's testimony was central to the plaintiffs' theory of the case: that if machines rather than human reviewers were effectively conducting [[signature-verification|signature verification]], then verification as required by Arizona statute did not take place at all.[1]

## Testimony in the Count III trial (May 2023)

Erich Speckin served as the plaintiffs' expert witness in handwriting and signature verification in the remanded Count III trial of [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], having testified as an expert in 413 cases.[2] Analyzing [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]]'s own data, he testified that purported human review had been conducted at rates impossible for a person following the lawful process, identifying 731,835 comparisons completed in five seconds or less, 615,425 in four seconds or less, 465,259 in three seconds or less, 274,319 in two seconds or less, and 70,839 in one second or less.[2]

Speckin also testified to a correlation between faster review speed and higher approval rates, citing User 79, who reviewed 54,298 signatures at an overall 98.9 percent approval rate — including 45,217 in under six seconds at 99.91 percent approval, 37,524 in under four seconds at 99.97 percent, and 27,196 in under three seconds at 100 percent.[2] He further testified that more than 45,000 signatures were approved through a batch upload, testimony that forced county elections director [[ray-valenzuela|Rey Valenzuela]] to correct his earlier claim that batch files were not used.[2] On cross-examination the defense attacked Speckin's credentials but presented no evidence or witnesses disputing his findings.[2]
