# David Ben-Gurion

David Ben-Gurion was the founding Prime Minister of [[israel|Israel]], serving 16 years — the longest tenure of any Israeli prime minister until [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

## The War of the Letters

In 1963 Ben-Gurion engaged with President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] in an exchange known as the War of the Letters, in which Kennedy pressed him about a suspected covert Israeli nuclear program.[1] Kennedy had been warned of the program by President Eisenhower during the customary transition meeting between the outgoing and incoming administrations.[1] Ben-Gurion blocked the American inspectors Kennedy dispatched to Israel, restricting their access and barring them from certain buildings, conduct Badlands commentary reads as clear concealment; Kennedy assured him he would learn the truth.[1]

After Kennedy's assassination, the optics of the situation were so damaging for Ben-Gurion that he resigned from office.[1] The episode forms part of the background of the [[israeli-nuclear-program|Israeli nuclear program]].[1]

## Pursuit of the bomb

Badlands commentary holds that Ben-Gurion was obsessed with obtaining nuclear weapons from the moment he assumed office in 1948, justifying the pursuit as a means of preventing another Holocaust.[2] He found an ally in France, which helped him develop the [[negev-nuclear-research-center|Negev Nuclear Research Center]]; construction began in 1958 and the facility became active between 1962 and 1964, with Israel said to have produced its first nuclear weapon by 1967.[2] French President [[charles-de-gaulle|Charles de Gaulle]] discovered the partnership in 1958 and sought to stop it.[2]

In 1963 Kennedy spent most of the year demanding answers from Ben-Gurion, requests that were evaded or ignored, most famously in a seven-page letter explaining why Israel could not develop the technology — particularly in secret — because doing so would inspire others to follow suit.[2] Kennedy secured agreement to inspections of alleged Israeli nuclear sites, but they were not carried out until after his assassination at the end of 1963 and were ultimately subverted.[2]
