# Imane Khelif

Imane Khelif is an Algerian boxer who competed in the 2024 Paris Olympic women's boxing tournament and became the center of an international controversy over sex and gender eligibility in women's sport.[1]

## 2024 Olympic bout

On Thursday, August 1, 2024, Khelif faced Italy's [[angela-carini|Angela Carini]] in an Olympic matchup that ended after just 46 seconds, when Carini — following two heavy blows to the head — threw her helmet to the mat and abandoned the bout, shouting "This is unjust!"[1] Carini refused to shake Khelif's hand, pulled away from the referee as he raised Khelif's arm, and left the ring in tears, saying she had never taken a punch like that.[1]

## Eligibility dispute

Khelif had previously been banned from the 2023 world championships by the [[international-boxing-association|International Boxing Association]] after failing tests intended to establish gender qualification.[1] Despite that disqualification, the [[international-olympic-committee|International Olympic Committee]] permitted Khelif to compete at the Paris Games over the protests of female athletes.[1]

Badlands commentary characterized the bout as a boxer with XY chromosomes competing against a boxer with XX chromosomes and argued the episode showed the IOC's stated commitment to equity overriding athlete safety, noting that the same committee had allowed a convicted child rapist to compete in men's volleyball at the 2024 Games.[1]

## IBA press conference on chromosome testing (August 2024)

At a press conference on Monday, August 5, 2024, the [[international-boxing-association|International Boxing Association]] categorically stated that Khelif and Taiwan's [[lin-yu-ting|Lin Yu-ting]], both then in the semi-finals of the women's boxing tournament at the [[2024-paris-summer-olympics|Paris Olympics]], are "male" because they had failed a previous gender chromosome test.[2] IBA chief executive Chris Roberts said 2022 results from an independent laboratory in Istanbul "demonstrated the chromosomes we refer to in competition rules that make both boxers ineligible," and that both athletes were told they had tested positive for XY chromosomes and could appeal to the [[court-of-arbitration-for-sport|Court of Arbitration for Sport]] at the IBA's expense.[2] Khelif filed an appeal and then withdrew it, while Lin did not appeal; both were re-tested with the same results in India during the 2023 Women's World Boxing Championships.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Khelif was scheduled to fight in the welterweight semi-final that day and that both fighters were guaranteed to leave the games with medals, criticizing the [[international-olympic-committee|International Olympic Committee]] for a stance she contrasted with its deference to the governing body of men's volleyball on the eligibility of a convicted child rapist.[2]
