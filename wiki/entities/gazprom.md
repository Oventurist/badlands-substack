# Gazprom

Gazprom is Russia's state-controlled energy company and the operator of the country's principal natural gas export infrastructure.[1] In July 2026 the company reported that Ukrainian drones had targeted the Krasnodarskaya compressor station, a facility serving the [[blue-stream-pipeline|Blue Stream]] natural gas pipeline that carries Russian gas to [[turkey|Türkiye]] beneath the Black Sea.[1]

## Krasnodarskaya compressor station strike (July 2026)

According to Gazprom, the drone attack caused only minor damage to a building at the Krasnodarskaya facility and did not interrupt gas deliveries, because repairs were carried out promptly and backup measures kept supplies flowing.[1] [[ukraine|Ukraine]] had not publicly commented on the strike at the time of reporting.[1] Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] condemned the attack as a threat to critical international energy infrastructure and said [[russia|Russia]] expected countries affected by the pipeline, including Türkiye, to respond.[1]

## Export role

Gazprom's Blue Stream pipeline is described as one of Russia's key gas export routes to Türkiye, while the separate [[turkstream-pipeline|TurkStream]] pipeline carries Russian gas through Türkiye onward to several European countries.[1] The reported strike came as Ukraine expanded long-range attacks on Russian energy infrastructure and Russia continued missile and drone attacks on Ukrainian cities and energy facilities.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the attack on Gazprom's Türkiye-bound export infrastructure was intended to disrupt gas flows to Turkey and should be read as an indirect strike carried out on behalf of [[israel|Israel]], which he described as having "pounded the war drums" toward Turkey for months.[1]

## Role in the Putin de-privatization narrative

In October 2025 Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited Gazprom as a central instrument of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s campaign to subordinate the post-Soviet oligarchy to sovereign will, writing that Gazprom reclaimed majority control over natural gas behemoths while [[yukos|Yukos Oil]] was dismantled and absorbed by [[rosneft|Rosneft]].[2] He argued that these partial nationalizations, together with state takeovers in aerospace and nuclear energy, redistributed spoils to loyalists and were framed inside Russia not as communism's return but as reclaiming the country's destiny from Western-backed predators.[2]
