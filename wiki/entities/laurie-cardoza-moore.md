# Laurie Cardoza-Moore

Laurie Cardoza-Moore is an evangelical [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionist]] leader who became the center of a public dispute with commentator [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] in June 2025 after she publicly labeled him an antisemite.[1]

## Dispute with Tucker Carlson

In February 2025, Cardoza-Moore penned a statement commenting on a photograph of Carlson with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] at the [[white-house|White House]], writing that "Tucker Carlson's antisemitism does not reflect the values of the Trump administration."[1] She went further, asking Trump to ban Carlson from the White House over his alleged "blatant antisemitism."[1]

Cardoza-Moore told *The Jerusalem Post* that after publication of a Post story about the statement, Carlson — a man she said she had never spoken to in person — telephoned her to "beg" for a public apology for labeling him an antisemite.[1] "I had clearly touched a nerve for the former Trump confidante," she said, "but I would not back down."[1] She also accused Carlson of breaking the Ninth Commandment by "bearing false witness against God's Chosen People."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] became aware of the story after [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] posted it with a smug remark, and used it to argue that an "unbridled hubris" had become rampant among defenders of Israel — both Jewish and evangelical — and was metastasizing into totalitarianism.[1] He contended that Carlson was not an antisemite and that Levin and Cardoza-Moore were seeking to make a severe example of him merely for questioning the actions of the government of Israel, adding the Badlands refrain that "the NeoCons are Leftists, and always have been."[1]

He further disputed Cardoza-Moore's theology, arguing that the crux of the New Testament is that Christ is the New Covenant — meaning Christian doctrine holds that believers in Christ constitute God's Chosen People, the House of Israel — and charging that a self-described Christian leader who taught otherwise was speaking heresy and misleading Christians in a way that was helping drive the country toward a wider war.[1]
