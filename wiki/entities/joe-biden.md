# Joe Biden

Joe Biden appears in [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s April 2025 essay in his 1990s capacity as a United States Senator and co-author of federal crime legislation.[1]

## Role in the 1994 Crime Bill

Carlson names Senator Biden alongside [[bill-clinton|President Bill Clinton]] as the politicians who came up with the [[crime-bill-1994|Crime Bill of 1994]], which increased sentences for non-violent criminals — in Carlson's gloss, drug dealers.[1] He places this legislation within a scheme to fill newly privatized prisons at a time when crime rates were falling, and states that kickbacks came to the politicians who helped pass the new crime bills.[1]

## Ghostwriter tapes (2026)

Recordings of Joe Biden speaking with his memoir ghostwriter [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]] were released in July 2026 after a lengthy legal fight, capturing Biden warning at one point that "some of this may be classified" and later stating, "this is classified."[2] The more than seventy hours of 2016 and 2017 conversations had been obtained during special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified documents probe and were published by the [[oversight-project|Oversight Project]]; Biden's attorneys spent months trying to block the release before abandoning their lawsuit, and a judge accepted the dismissal on the Monday preceding the report.[2]

In one exchange Biden tells Zwonitzer: "Some of this may be classified … So, be careful. I'm not sure … It's not marked classified but —." At another point he says, "The next thing I have here is, um, this is classified …"[2] The tapes also show Biden frequently pausing, speaking haltingly and struggling to recall events, details that align with Hur's earlier description of Biden's memory.[2] Oversight Project Vice President of Litigation [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] said the recordings should force a full reexamination of the decision to give Biden a pass for keeping and disclosing classified information.[2]
