# Joe Biden

Joe Biden, later the 46th President of the United States, appears in the Badlands Media essay A Journey of Ever changing Truths in his earlier capacity as a United States Senator and co-author of federal crime legislation.

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that "politicians like President [[bill-clinton|Clinton]] and Senator Joe Biden came up with the Crime Bill of 1994, which increased sentences for non-violent criminals … aka drug dealers." The article situates that legislation within a claimed scheme to fill newly privatized prisons at a time when crime was falling, and states that "kickbacks came to the politicians who helped pass the new crime bills." Biden is thus presented in the source not as an incidental legislator but as one of the named political enablers of prison privatization.

## The UAW picket line, 2023

The essay A Time to Strike (October 11, 2023) records that Biden made U.S. labor history in late September 2023 when he became the first sitting president to join a picket line, appearing with striking [[united-auto-workers|United Auto Workers]] members in Wayne County, Michigan on the same day the [[writers-guild-of-america|Writers Guild of America]] reached its deal with the studios. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treats the appearance as "astonishing" and "very telling," arguing that Biden's economic policy and unprecedented spending had themselves destroyed U.S. manufacturing, and referring derisively to the resulting conditions as "#Bidenomics" — an environment in which UAW demands for a 46-percent pay raise and a four-day week were, in her view, as realistic as asking for "unicorns and world peace."

## The 2024 withdrawal

The Badlands Media essay A Week to Remember disputes the conventional account that Biden's June 2024 debate performance caused his removal from the presidential ticket. [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that the press had known of and covered for his cognitive decline for years, and that the uniformity and speed of the media turn indicate the decision had already been made: "They didn't wait for their 4am talking points, as their orders were already given to them."

The article speculates that the drug regimen said to make Biden "temporarily semi-coherent" — visible, in the author's view, at his most recent State of the Union address — was deliberately withheld before the debate to expose his condition, making the debate "the event that would be used to justify the decision." It further claims Biden "fought the decision, kicking and screaming to the very end," and that he submitted only after having "his assets frozen" and fearing for his life. The essay contends he had to be replaced "before people started asking the question, 'Biden obviously isn't running the country, so who is?'"

The article also attributes to Biden's term the visible onset of the [[inflation|inflation]] it says originated in [[donald-trump|Trump]]-era money printing, and notes that [[boeing|Boeing]] stock fell from $323 to $95 within eight weeks of Biden taking office. In the essay's closing timeline, Biden withdrew from the race two days after the death of Representative [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson Lee]] and one day before [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]] testified before the [[house-committee-on-oversight-and-accountability|Committee on Oversight and Accountability]].

## Depiction in All Assets Deployed

The November 2022 essay All Assets Deployed refers to "the occupation of the White House by the Biden Administration" as the cause of ongoing "political and administrative bleeding," and describes Biden dismissively as "the sitting 'President' currently occupying the oval office, or whichever green screen they're using in its place for his latest pre-recorded, gaffe-filled mumble rant." Within the essay's [[devolution|Devolution]] framework, Biden's continued occupancy of public administrative power is treated as functionally necessary: the failures of the existing "System of Systems" must be shouldered by "the puppet and his puppeteers" rather than by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the [[america-first|America First]] movement.

## Exposure through the Trump prosecutions

"Are Jack Smith and Fani Willis in on the Plan?" (September 2023) treats the Biden administration as the party with the most to lose from the criminal cases against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. The essay argues that because Trump's defense depends on demonstrating that his claims about the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 election]] were made in good faith, the prosecutions create the first genuine forum for evidence of election fraud. Should such evidence reach the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]], the article states, it "could open a massive can of worms for the Biden administration."
