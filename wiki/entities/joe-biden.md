# Joe Biden

Joe Biden appears in Erik Carlson's *A Journey of Ever-changing Truths* in his capacity as a United States Senator during the early 1990s.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Role in the article

Carlson names Biden alongside President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] as the politicians who devised the [[crime-bill-1994|Crime Bill of 1994]], legislation that increased sentences for non-violent criminals — chiefly drug dealers, in the essay's characterization.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) The bill is presented as the legislative half of a scheme to fill the newly privatized prison system at a time when crime rates were falling, with the [[cia|CIA]]'s alleged drug importation supplying the other half.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) Kickbacks, Carlson asserts, went to the politicians who helped pass the new crime bills.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Picket line appearance (2023)

In September 2023, Biden became the first sitting US President in history to join a picket line, appearing with striking [[united-auto-workers|United Auto Workers]] members in Wayne County, Michigan, on the same day the [[writers-guild-of-america|Writers Guild of America]] reached its agreement with the Hollywood studios.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Badlands Media called the appearance astonishing and "very telling," arguing that Biden's own economic policy and unprecedented spending had destroyed US manufacturing, and mocking the UAW's demands as unattainable under "#Bidenomics."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The article notes that automakers continued announcing layoffs throughout the strike, with as much as 62% of some manufacturing workforces eliminated even as the union pressed for a four-day work week.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## The 2024 debate and withdrawal

Badlands commentary argues that Biden's disastrous 2024 presidential debate performance was not the cause of the Deep State's decision to replace him but the pretext for it.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] reasons that everyone in the media had known of Biden's cognitive issues for years and had protected him, so their immediate and universal turn against him after the debate indicates orders had already been given — "they didn't wait for their 4am talking points."[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He speculates that the drug regimen that rendered Biden temporarily semi-coherent, evident during his most recent State of the Union Address, was deliberately withheld before the debate in order to expose his mental state.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

Carlson holds that Biden did not relinquish the nomination for the good of the country but fought the decision "kicking and screaming to the very end," submitting only after having his assets frozen and fearing for his life.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Biden had to be replaced, in this account, before the public began asking who was actually running the country in his place.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Place in the July 2024 sequence

Biden withdrew from the race two days after the death of Representative [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson Lee]] and roughly eight days after the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] at Butler, Pennsylvania; Secret Service Director [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]] testified before Congress the following day and resigned the day after that.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Carlson asks whether Biden stepped down fearing he would be next.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He also notes that [[boeing|Boeing]] stock stood at $323 the day Biden took office and fell to $95 within eight weeks, and that the [[inflation|inflation]] attributed to Biden's tenure largely originated in the Covid-era money printing that preceded it.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Iran, China and the foreign policy reversal

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] presents Biden's Iran policy as the mirror image of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[maximum-pressure-campaign|maximum pressure campaign]]: within two months of taking office he allowed China to sign a $400 billion oil and gas agreement with [[iran|Iran]] that would also create a comprehensive economic and military partnership and fold Iran into the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Some might call it incompetence, DeLarme writes, but given the Biden family's now very public history of making money off China — including the millions the Bidens made from CEFC China Energy, then one of China's largest energy firms — "it starts to seem intentional."[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

DeLarme further attributes to the Biden administration a series of regime-change moves that altered the geopolitical equation, including the overthrow of the democratically elected government of [[imran-khan|Imran Khan]] in [[pakistan|Pakistan]] and a subsequent attempt to unseat [[narendra-modi|Modi]] in India, which he reads through the lens of Biden being partnered with [[communist-china|China]].[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) During the first part of his presidency, efforts also continued to have Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] ousted.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Biden's top cabinet official was [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], an architect of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]]; his [[cia|CIA]] director was its lead negotiator [[william-burns|William Burns]], the post having first been offered to [[tom-donilon|Tom Donilon]].[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Withdrawal as distraction

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] placed Biden's 2024 exit within a sequence he read as engineered diversion: the debate, the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempt on Trump's life]], Biden contracting Covid, Biden stepping down, and [[kamala-harris|Harris]] taking over were all, he argued, designed to distract and divide.[5] He maintained that the decision to remove Biden had been made before the debate exposed his diminished mental state, and that the upheaval had nothing to do with Biden and the Democrats and everything to do with [[donald-trump|Trump]] signalling the end of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s control of the American financial system.[5] Within a single week of the shooting, Sheila Jackson Lee died, Biden stepped down, and [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]] testified before [[congress|Congress]].[5] Carlson urged readers to ignore reporting by what he called legendary journalists on who forced Biden out, how he was stabbed in the back, and how he retaliated by endorsing Harris, and to listen instead to what Trump was saying about the economy.[5]

## Response to the fall of Assad

After [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] fled to Moscow in December 2024, Badlands Media wrote that Biden "shambled out from his crypt to read a prepared message from a teleprompter," delivering a narrative nearly identical to those following the defeat of the Taliban in Afghanistan, the Vietcong in South Vietnam, Saddam Hussein in Iraq, and Muammar Gaddafi in Libya.[6] The article characterized that script as: "We vanquished the tyrant, the people are finally free, we stand with them."[6]

It also named Biden, with Boris Johnson, among the Western leaders whose interference it says prolonged the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], and expressed hope that as the Biden administration prepared to transfer power to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the United States would avoid another aid package filling rebel pockets in [[syria|Syria]].[6]

## Hur recordings litigation (2026)

In July 2026 the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-dc-circuit|U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit]] denied Biden's emergency request for an injunction blocking release of audio recordings and transcripts of his 2016–2017 private conversations with ghostwriter [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]].[7] The recordings, made at Biden's home while he worked on a memoir, had been obtained by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] during Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s investigation into his handling of classified documents.[7]

The Justice Department initially withheld most of the records under FOIA exemptions but later agreed to release redacted versions to the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], [[mike-howell|Mike Howell]], and the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]]; Biden intervened to block release and, after a lower court denied a preliminary injunction, filed the emergency appeal.[7] The appellate panel held he had not shown a likelihood of success on the merits, citing the public interest in assessing whether the government had gone easy on a former vice president, and extended an administrative stay until August 3.[7]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted Biden's non-prosecution with the charges brought against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], recalled Hur's description of Biden as a "well-meaning elderly man with a poor memory," and questioned whether Biden personally signed the executive orders and family pardons issued in his name — framing the decision as movement toward accountability in the [[autopen-scandal|autopen scandal]], for which she holds "the people running him" primarily responsible.[7]

## Release of the ghostwriter tapes (July 2026)

In July 2026 more than 70 hours of [[biden-ghostwriter-tapes|recordings of Biden speaking with memoir ghostwriter]] [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]] were published by the [[oversight-project|Oversight Project]] after a lengthy legal fight.[8] The 2016 and 2017 conversations had been obtained during special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified documents probe, and capture Biden warning at one point that "some of this may be classified" and later stating, "this is classified."[8] In one exchange he tells Zwonitzer: "Some of this may be classified … So, be careful. I'm not sure … It's not marked classified but —"[8] The tapes also show Biden frequently pausing, speaking haltingly and struggling to recall events, details that align with Hur's earlier description of his memory.[8] Biden's attorneys spent months trying to block the release before abandoning their lawsuit, and a judge accepted the dismissal on a Monday.[8]

## Post-presidency criticism from Democrats (2026)

In June 2026 former Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] publicly criticized Biden, in what was described as one of the most prominent rebukes from within the Democrat establishment since he left office and a contrast with her endorsements of him in the 2020 and 2024 cycles.[9] Clinton argued that Democrats needed to move beyond the party's recent leadership and adapt to a changing political landscape, amid broader debate over the party's direction following losses and internal disagreements over messaging, candidate recruitment and generational change.[9] The remarks added Clinton to a growing list of prominent Democrats and strategists publicly reassessing Biden's leadership and electoral legacy; while Biden retained support from many allies, divisions over his presidency and its impact on the party's future had become increasingly visible.[9] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who refers to Biden as "Our Boy Blue," suggested Clinton was mourning the collapse of her own political vision and blaming Biden for it.[9]

## Ukraine long-range strike debate and the Kirby email (September 2024)

In September 2024 Biden had allowed Ukraine to fire U.S.-provided missiles across the border into Russia in self-defense but had largely limited the distance at which they could be fired.[10] Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] wrapped up a Ukraine-focused European tour amid signs that Washington and London were growing more receptive to deeper strikes, and Biden was due to meet British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] in the United States, while Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] pushed back on the request.[10] President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] warned that approving such strikes would mean [[nato|NATO]] countries were "at war" with Russia.[10]

On the anniversary of 9/11 that same week, [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] communications adviser [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] — travelling with Biden — dismissed veterans critical of the administration's 2021 [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] withdrawal in an errant "reply all" email that reached [[fox-news|Fox News]] Digital, writing that there was "obviously no use in responding" to "a 'handful' of vets indeed and all of one stripe."[10]

## Age and fitness polling (August 2023)

An [[associated-press|Associated Press]]/NORC poll released in August 2023 found that 77 percent of Americans believed Biden was too old to carry out a second term as president.[11] While 89 percent of Republicans held that view, 69 percent of Democrats agreed — an overwhelming majority within his own party.[11] By contrast, fewer than half of Americans said former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was too old to hold office.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the problem was not Biden's years but his conduct — falling up stairs, falling off a bike, missing his mark, and failing to string together coherent sentences — and that no party genuinely seeking to win a fair election would run on his record, scandals, and polling.[11]

## Economic data manipulation claims

Coverage of the July 2023 [[jolts-report|JOLTS report]] alleged that essentially every economic data point released under the Biden administration was being revised sharply lower in subsequent months, which [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized as a coordinated propaganda effort to make the economy appear stronger before quietly walking the figures back.[11]

The [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] disclosed in 2023 that it held roughly 5,400 records referencing three pseudonymous email accounts Biden used as vice president — robinware456@gmail.com, JRBWare@gmail.com, and Robert.L.Peters@pci.gov — in response to a FOIA request from the [[southeastern-legal-foundation|Southeastern Legal Foundation]].[11] The matter is covered at [[biden-pseudonym-emails|Biden pseudonym emails]].[11]

## Age and acuity polling (May 2023)

An ABC News/Washington Post poll released in May 2023 put Biden's job approval rating at a career low, with a broad 68% of Americans saying he was too old to serve another term as president.[12] By contrast, just 44% viewed his potential opponent [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as too old; Trump was 76 at the time and Biden 80.[12] Beyond chronological age, the survey found Trump far surpassing Biden on perceived mental sharpness and physical health required to serve effectively, leaving Biden trailing top Republicans in early 2024 preferences.[12]

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that, given the media sourcing of the poll, "it's clear that the Establishment doesn't want Biden anywhere near the 2024 ticket," while noting the irony that Biden's nomination would itself serve as proof of [[devolution|Devolution]] and "Patriots in Control" theories.[12]

## "Dictator" remark and China policy (June 2023)

At a June 20, 2023 campaign reception at a private residence in Kentfield, California, Biden referred to Chinese leader [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] as a dictator, saying Xi had been embarrassed by the February 2023 shootdown of a Chinese surveillance balloon because "he didn't know it was there."[14] Biden said the balloon had been "blown off course up through Alaska and then down through the United States" and carried "two boxcars full of spy equipment."[14] The remarks came one day after Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] concluded a visit to Beijing at which he met Xi.[14] China's Foreign Ministry, through spokesperson [[mao-ning|Mao Ning]], called the comments "extremely absurd and irresponsible" and "a blatant political provocation."[14]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the episode less as a deliberate insult than as "a dementia moment," describing the wider speech as "a rambling, muddled mess" in which Biden romanticized having spent 82 recorded hours with Xi over twelve years, repeated the Charlottesville "very fine people" claim, and cited Madeleine Albright's description of America as "the essential nation."[14] She argued that apparent tension between Biden and Xi was "a fabricated cover for the fact that Biden is a CCP puppet."[14] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] catalogued what he called the administration's escalatory posture — refusal to recognize [[taiwan|Taiwan]] as independent, Chinese military infrastructure activity in [[cuba|Cuba]], and outreach to [[iran|Iran]] despite its [[state-department|State Department]] designation as a state sponsor of terrorism — describing these as an inverted mirror of Trump-era policy.[14]

## Ireland visits and the CEFC question

In April 2023 Biden traveled to Northern Ireland for events in Belfast marking a quarter-century of the [[good-friday-agreement|Good Friday Agreement]], a trip marred by a security breach after a document outlining police deployments was found in the street, prompting an investigation by the [[police-service-of-northern-ireland|Police Service of Northern Ireland]].[15]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] used the occasion to revisit an earlier Biden visit to Ireland in September 2017, noting it was not the first time [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] had accompanied his father there.[15] Flight itineraries arranged by aide [[kathy-chung|Kathy Chung]] placed Joe Biden in Ireland from 11 to 17 September 2017, with Hunter and [[jim-biden|Jim Biden]] traveling on identical dates.[15] The younger Bidens met representatives of [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC China Energy]] and trade-group figures off site on 13 September because of the "sensitivity of the discussion and participants," and a follow-up email two days later referenced "the Rosneft transaction," implicating [[rosneft|Rosneft]].[15] Four days after the party returned, Hunter Biden's "office mates" email described Joe Biden sharing office arrangements with CEFC — a sequence Herold summarized with the unresolved question of whether Joe Biden attended the Ireland meeting.[15]

## Classified documents and the Afghanistan withdrawal (2023 coverage)

In March 2023 the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] disclosed to Senators [[ron-johnson]] and [[chuck-grassley]] that nine boxes of documents had been retrieved from the Boston office of Biden's attorney [[patrick-moore]], material moved from the [[penn-biden-center]] before the initial classified trove was discovered there.[16] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] described the [[biden-classified-documents-scandal|classified documents scandal]] as steadily expanding from a few misplaced pages to boxes of records and handwritten notes spanning Biden's Senate and vice-presidential years, with Joe, James and [[hunter-biden]] under scrutiny by U.S. Attorney David Weiss and Special Counsel [[robert-hur]].[16]

The same brief covered testimony to the [[house-foreign-affairs-committee]] by Marine sergeant [[tyler-vargas-andrews]], who said snipers could have stopped the [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate]] bomber during the administration's Kabul evacuation but were denied authority, and that the [[state-department]] turned Afghans away from the airport.[16] Badlands contributors treated the episode as an illustration of Biden's incompetence, one whose price was the lives of thirteen American service members.[16]

## Easter 2024 Transgender Day of Visibility proclamation

On March 29, 2024, Biden issued a proclamation recognizing March 31 as [[transgender-day-of-visibility|Transgender Day of Visibility]], as he had every year of his presidency; because Easter Sunday fell on March 31 that year, conservatives accused the Catholic president of stifling Christian values.[17] House leadership and the Trump campaign demanded an apology from the [[white-house|White House]], which responded that "as a Christian who celebrates Easter with family, President Biden stands for bringing people together and upholding the dignity and freedoms of every American."[17] [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued the proclamation was intended to inflame and fuel the [[culture-war|culture war]], noting the two dates would not coincide again for eleven years.[17]

## Lizzo fundraiser juxtaposition

Hours after former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] attended the wake of murdered [[nypd|NYPD]] officer [[jonathan-diller|Jonathan Diller]] in late March 2024, Biden appeared at a $500,000-per-ticket fundraiser with [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]], [[lizzo|Lizzo]] and other celebrities, an event that raised $25 million for his campaign.[17] Earlier that day his social media team had posted an attack advertisement portraying Biden as hard at work while Trump was idle on a golf course, and the resulting contrast drew widespread criticism.[17] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the juxtapositions between Trump and Biden were becoming "more and more cartoonish" as the [[information-war|Info War]] accelerated toward November, and argued that parading Biden publicly damaged the establishment's thin veneer of a mandate to lead.[17]

## Antitrust and censorship posture

The [[brownstone-institute|Brownstone Institute]] traced the Biden [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s [[united-states-v-apple-antitrust-case|antitrust action against Apple]] to press secretary [[jen-psaki|Jen Psaki]]'s May 5, 2021 statement pressing platforms to suppress COVID-19 and election "misinformation" while noting the president "supports better privacy protections and a robust antitrust program."[17] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the Apple case as an attempt to extend the government's earlier informal control over social platforms to device hardware and networking infrastructure.[17]

## Loss of intelligence access (March 2025)

In March 2025, Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] announced that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had officially revoked Biden's access to the [[presidents-daily-brief|President's Daily Brief]], reversing the arrangement in which Biden had barred Trump from classified briefings after the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack.[18] The directive, which Trump had first ordered begun in February, was described by ABC News as part of his retribution campaign against what he calls the weaponization of the justice and intelligence agencies under Biden and Democrats.[18] The same action revoked security clearances for former officials including [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]].[18]

## Red Sea policy criticism (December 2023)

In December 2023 Biden was accused of downplaying the threat posed by [[houthis|Houthi]] attacks on commercial shipping in the [[red-sea-shipping-crisis|Red Sea]], with Politico reporting that current and former military officials were frustrated by the administration's initial response to the Houthis' Sunday attacks on ships.[19] Defense officials said the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was being held back by the Biden White House from striking Houthi positions in Yemen, even as container ships came under daily drone and missile attack and major liners abandoned Red Sea transit.[19] Commentators attributed the restraint to Biden's desire to avoid antagonizing [[iran|Iran]] and keep global energy prices down ahead of the 2024 election, a posture Politico characterized as "looking the other way."[19]

*The Wall Street Journal* declared in an article headlined "U.S. Naval Deterrence Is Going, Going, Maybe Even Gone" that American deterrence in the region might already be beyond recovery.[19] Coverage noted that the commander-in-chief had not ordered a military response even when American warships off Yemen came under direct attack.[19] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] concluded that Biden either had no desire to interfere with the chaos in the region or, in the view he attributed to much of the Badlands community, no ability to do so — an inference he tied to the [[devolution|Devolution]] framework and to the absence of a US military response under National Essential Functions.[19]

## Pardon of Hunter Biden (December 2024)

On the evening of December 1, 2024, Biden granted his son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] a "full and unconditional pardon," reversing repeated prior vows to let his son's convictions on federal gun and tax charges stand.[20] In announcing the clemency, Biden claimed that "raw politics … infected" his son's case.[20] The pardon covered roughly a decade of conduct, reaching back to just before Hunter Biden joined the board of the Ukrainian energy company [[burisma|Burisma]], and reporting indicated it had been in preparation for months while the White House publicly denied any such plan.[20] Legal specialists compared its breadth only to Gerald Ford's 1974 pardon of [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]].[20] See [[hunter-biden-pardon|Hunter Biden pardon]].[20]

Commentary reproduced in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] tied the pardon to Biden's own role as vice president in the 2014 Ukraine portfolio, citing a timeline in which he met [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]] at the White House on April 16, 2014, visited Ukraine on April 21 as the face of U.S. policy, and saw both Archer and his son join Burisma's board within weeks.[20] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the pardon, issued in full public view, was a net benefit to the anti-corruption cause because it collapsed the establishment's "rule of law" narrative.[20]

## Hostage messaging

The Brief also criticized a statement issued by Biden's White House regarding the death of American-Israeli soldier [[omer-neutra|Omer Maxim Neutra]], arguing that the administration had allowed his family to believe for thirteen months that he was alive and had presented them publicly as part of what the author called a psyop.[20]

## Preemptive Pardon Deliberations and 2024 Reflections (January 2025)

On January 10, 2025, President Joe Biden told reporters at the [[white-house|White House]] that he was still considering [[preemptive-pardons|pre-emptive pardons]] for people whom President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had targeted, saying he was taking into account Trump's stated intentions for retribution: "The idea that he would punish people ... is outrageous... There's still consideration ... but no decision."[21] Asked whether he regretted his initial decision to seek re-election, Biden rejected the premise that it had paved the way for Trump's 2024 victory, saying, "I think I would have beaten Trump, could have beaten Trump, and I think that Kamala could have beaten Trump," referring to [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[21] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Biden had not been in control and that any such pardons amounted to the outgoing administration pardoning itself.[21]

## Record-low approval rating (October 2023)

A [[gallup|Gallup]] poll conducted October 2–23, 2023 found Biden's job approval among Democrats had tumbled 11 percentage points in a month to 75%, the worst reading of his presidency from his own party.[22] That drop pushed his overall approval down four points to 37%, matching his personal low.[22] Approval among independents fell four points to 35%, while Republicans' rating remained unchanged at just 5%.[22]

After ranging from 49% to 57% during the first eight months of his presidency, Biden's approval had been mired in the low 40s for much of the preceding two years; including the 37% reading and an identical figure in April, his approval had fallen below 40% four times in the 33 readings Gallup had taken since he took office.[22] Gallup tied the downturn to the aftermath of the October 7 surprise attack on [[israel|Israel]] by [[hamas|Hamas]] militants, which resulted in at least 1,400 deaths and more than 200 kidnappings and precipitated an Israeli counteroffensive that had produced an estimated 5,000 deaths in [[gaza|Gaza]] and a humanitarian crisis.[22]

## November 2023 approval collapse

An NBC News poll released on November 19, 2023 put Biden's approval rating at 40 percent, the lowest level of his presidency, with strong majorities of all voters disapproving of his handling of foreign policy and of the Israel-Hamas war.[24] For the first time in that survey series he trailed former President Donald Trump in a hypothetical general-election matchup, albeit within the margin of error.[24] Erosion was sharpest among Democrats, a majority of whom said Israel had gone too far in its military action in Gaza, and among voters aged 18 to 34, of whom 70 percent disapproved of his handling of the war.[24] Democratic pollster Jeff Horwitt of Hart Research Associates said Biden was "at a uniquely low point in his presidency," attributing much of it to how Americans viewed his foreign policy actions, while Republican pollster Bill McInturff of Public Opinion Strategies said he could not recall another time when foreign affairs not involving US troops had so transformed the American political landscape.[24]

## October 2023: Israel–Hamas war politics and the security supplemental

Public opinion polling in October 2023 found Americans giving mixed reactions to Biden's approach to the Israel–Hamas conflict: a plurality thought he had shown the right amount of support for Israel, but his overall handling drew a net negative response amid broad concern about a wider regional war and terrorism at home.[25] Republicans mostly disapproved and tended to feel his statements had not shown enough support for Israel, though they graded him better on the conflict (28% approval) than on his job overall (8%).[25] Most Democrats thought his level of support for Israel was right, but nearly 3 in 10 thought he had shown too much and wanted him to press harder for a diplomatic solution.[25] There was broad support for U.S. diplomacy to resolve the conflict and a split over sending weapons to Israel.[25]

The Biden administration requested more than $105 billion from [[congress|Congress]] for a national security package covering Ukraine and Israel — $61.4 billion for Ukraine, $14.3 billion for Israel, $9.15 billion in humanitarian aid, $7.4 billion for Taiwan and the Indo-Pacific, and $13.6 billion for the US–Mexico border — transmitted by OMB Director [[shalanda-young|Shalanda Young]] to Speaker Pro Tempore [[patrick-mchenry|Patrick McHenry]].[25]

## February 2024 Gallup approval decline

A Gallup poll released in late February 2024 found Biden's job approval had edged down three percentage points to 38%, one point shy of his all-time low and well below the 50% threshold that has typically preceded reelection for incumbents.[87] He registered subpar ratings on five key issues, including a new low of 28% on immigration and readings between 30% and 40% on the Israel–Hamas situation in the Middle East, foreign affairs, the economy and Ukraine.[87] Biden's approval had not risen above 44% since August 2021, and his 39.8% average for his third year in office was the second worst among post-World War II presidents elected to a first term.[87]

Badlands contributor Simon Esler argued that, given how easily polls and votes had been manipulated, such numbers should not be read as representative of reality but as an attempt to place Biden's visible struggle into a particular model of the hero's journey.[87] In his reading, the shadow government behind Biden had to concede *some* low approval numbers — low enough to be credible, high enough to support a bare-minimum underdog comeback storyline — because plausible deniability requires that figures not be too extreme in either direction.[87] He allowed that the Deep State might yet fake another Biden victory rather than replace him, or might simply be keeping pro-Biden narratives on life support out of panic while it decided, and urged readers to "hold the cornered animal's propaganda lightly."[87] At the same time, coverage of the South Carolina primary framed a 2020 Trump–Biden rematch as increasingly inevitable.[87]

## April 2024 border executive order plans
