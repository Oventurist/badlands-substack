# Bank of England

The Bank of England is the United Kingdom's central bank, headquartered in the [[city-of-london|City of London]].[1]

In January 2026 the Bank suddenly announced a warning that financial markets could collapse if the U.S. government decided to announce the existence of aliens, an assessment attributed to senior analyst Helen McCaw.[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] flagged the timing as suspicious rather than random, noting that the article itself acknowledged the disclosure trend had been years in the making, and asked why it seemed prudent to panic over it at that precise moment — concluding that it cannot be a coincidence given the parallel moves against the global banking system following the lifting of Venezuela sanctions.[1]

## Carney governorship and the Brexit referendum

[[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] served as governor of the Bank during the United Kingdom's 2016 [[brexit|Brexit]] referendum, when he was one of the most prominent establishment voices warning against leaving the European Union and was accused by pro-Brexit conservatives of running "Project Fear."[2] Carney later invoked that tenure directly when, as Canadian prime minister in May 2026, he warned [[alberta-separatism|Alberta separatists]] against repeating what he called the "dangerous bluff" of Brexit.[2]


## Alternative bail-in mechanism (April 2026)

In April 2026 the Bank of England introduced an alternative bank-resolution ("bail-in") mechanism, a move reported alongside broader efforts by global financial authorities to strengthen oversight of large institutions and manage systemic risk.[3] The step came as Swiss regulators weighed tougher capital requirements for [[ubs|UBS]] following its emergency takeover of [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]].[3]

## December 2025 private markets stress test

The Bank of England announced on December 4, 2025 that it was launching a stress test of how the private equity and private credit industries would cope with a financial shock.[4] The system-wide exploratory scenario, due to report in early 2027, focuses on the impact for the British economy as a whole rather than publishing vulnerabilities of individual firms, and Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden said a range of alternative asset managers active in private markets, including major global players, had agreed to participate.[4] Days earlier the bank's half-yearly Financial Stability Report had noted that private markets, though resilient so far, had grown substantially and remained untested against a large economic shock.[4]
