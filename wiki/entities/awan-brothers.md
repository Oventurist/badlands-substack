# Awan Brothers

The **Awan Brothers** are Pakistani-origin information-technology administrators who worked as computer staff for members of the United States Congress, and who feature in the Q drops discussed in Chapter 9 of the Badlands Media series [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] as a case study in what the author calls "infiltration, not invasion."[1]

## Congressional IT work

According to the chapter, the Awan brothers are from Pakistan and served as IT administrators for well over two dozen different members of Congress, a client list that included Democratic National Committee chair [[debbie-wasserman-schultz|Debbie Wasserman Schultz]].[1] The author emphasises that neither brother held a security clearance and that neither was properly vetted before being given access to congressional systems, a lapse he treats as evidence of systemic negligence rather than mere oversight.[1]

## Alleged Muslim Brotherhood links

The chapter states that it was later revealed that Pakistani IT administrators had been caught sending top-secret information to the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]], and it presents this claim as the substantive reason the Awan matter belongs alongside the Q drops on [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]] and Saudi influence.[1] The author summarises the episode with the phrase "[[infiltration|infiltration, not invasion]]," arguing that hostile penetration of American institutions has proceeded through staffing and access rather than through open confrontation.[1]

## Related business interests

The chapter separately identifies the [[awan-group|Awan Group]], an information-technology company with offices in Bahrain, [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Pakistan, the United Arab Emirates, Malaysia, China and the United States, as part of the surrounding picture of transnational IT networks touching American political infrastructure.[1]

## Interpretive context

Within the Chapter 9 reading of drops #68–71, the Awan story is positioned as one strand of a larger argument that foreign money and foreign personnel had acquired improper leverage over American government, running in parallel with the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]]'s Saudi donations and the alleged Saudi purchase of influence over senior U.S. officials.[1] These claims reflect the interpretive framework of the Badlands Media series rather than established findings; the federal case actually brought against Imran Awan resulted only in a bank-fraud plea, a point the chapter does not address.
