# Peter Norbeck

Peter Norbeck was a Republican United States Senator from South Dakota who, together with Senator George Norris of Nebraska, spearheaded the establishment of the U.S. Committee on Banking and Currency in 1932.[1] At the time the American economy was on life support, with unemployment at 25 percent and the population desperate enough that a fascist dictatorship "would have been welcomed with open arms, if only bread could be put on the table."[1]

The committee was a relatively impotent body when it began, but in April 1932 Norbeck called in the New York lawyer [[ferdinand-pecora|Ferdinand Pecora]] to lead its investigation, and "everything began to change."[1] Norbeck's decision gave Pecora broad subpoena powers to audit banks and compel the most powerful financiers in America to testify, producing the hearings known as the [[pecora-commission|Pecora Commission]].[1]
