# William James Flynn

William James Flynn (1867–1928) was an American investigator who served as chief of New York's Secret Service from 1912 to 1917 and as director of the young [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] from 1917 to 1919. [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents him as a representative of a patriotic counter-tradition within United States intelligence, opposed to the Anglo-American private networks centered on the [[sleepy-hollow-club|Sleepy Hollow Club]].[1]

## Career and investigations

Flynn led the anti-corruption purge of the NYPD detective bureau from 1910 to 1911.[1] As FBI director he found himself at odds with Attorney General A. Mitchell Palmer's crackdown on civil liberties, known as the Palmer Raids.[1]

In 1915 Flynn was responsible for locating the German agents operating in the United States around the figures of [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]] and [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]], and it was Flynn who followed the German diplomat Dr. Heinrich Albert and Viereck and acquired the evidence of Germany's $25 million spent buying up spy networks across the country during the war.[1] The historian Helibert von Felitzer notes that Albert's captured papers revealed German ownership of the Bridgeport Projectile Company, investments in American munitions and newspapers, market-cornering efforts, bribes to American politicians, the role of Deutsche Bank representative Hugo Schmidt, and German government payments to Viereck and The Fatherland.[1]

Building on that success, Flynn tracked down the wireless radio station on Long Island that had been transmitting signals to German intelligence — including, in Ehret's telling, information leading to the sinking of the Lusitania in 1915.[1] Flynn's interception of messages to the German high command provided the legal justification for the United States government's seizure and destruction of [[nikola-tesla|Nikola Tesla]]'s Long Island installations, both the [[wardenclyffe-tower|Wardenclyffe Tower]] and the Telefunken Wireless Station at Sayville, New York.[1]
