# Satoshi Nakamoto

**Satoshi Nakamoto** is the pseudonym used by the anonymous person or group who released the [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] White Paper and launched the network.[1]

## The "Immaculate Conception"

In a phenomenon the Badlands Media essay "Are We Entering the Millennial Reign?" calls the "Immaculate Conception," the Bitcoin White Paper was released on October 31, 2008.[1] On January 3, 2009, Satoshi began the timechain by mining the first block, known as the Genesis block, and continued mining for six days — creating the foundation — before releasing the software to the rest of the world.[1] The author observes that, whether intended or not, this can be viewed as a metaphor for the six days of creation in the Bible.[1]

Satoshi helped the network for a few years, then disappeared and has not been heard from since.[1]

## Anonymity as prophetic parallel

The essay's discussion of the [[millennial-reign|Millennial Reign]] notes that the returning Christ is described as having a name written that no one knows except Himself, leading the author to ask whether Christ returns as an anon — "Anon like Satoshi? Anon like Q? Anon like Pepe?"[1]

## The Genesis block and anonymity

The October 2024 essay "Revolution Founded at the Crossroads" describes Satoshi Nakamoto as having started the [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] network by performing the [[proof-of-work|Proof of Work]] required to mint the Genesis block, a deliberate parallel the author draws to the "Genesis" of Christ's birth.[2] The author is careful to state that Satoshi was "not God, but potentially divinely inspired."[2]

Satoshi is credited with knowing that his mission — revealing the discovery, separating money and state, decentralizing control of the network, and giving individuals peaceful, non-kinetic "digital power projection" — was revolutionary.[2] He is also described as knowing that central banks, financial institutions, governments, and others profiting from the centralized control and debasement of global currencies would seek to kill him and destroy the network; for that reason he remained anonymous and eventually went dark.[2] The essay places this pattern alongside the persecution of [[jesus]] as evidence that decentralizing forces are always met with maximal opposition from incumbent hierarchies.[2]
