# George Alan Kelly

George Alan Kelly is an Arizona rancher, aged 75 as of April 2024, who was charged with second-degree murder in the January 30, 2023 shooting death of Gabriel Cuen-Buitimea on his property near the U.S.–Mexico border.[1]

## Mistrial

On Monday, April 22, 2024, an Arizona judge declared a mistrial after jurors failed to reach a unanimous decision following more than two full days of deliberation. Superior Court Judge Thomas Fink stated: "Based upon the jury's inability to reach a verdict on any count, this case is in mistrial."[1]

The Santa Cruz County Attorney's Office retained the option to retry Kelly on any charge or to drop the case entirely, and a status hearing was scheduled for the following Monday afternoon at which prosecutors could indicate whether they intended to refile. Prosecutors did not immediately respond to requests for comment.[1]

Cuen-Buitimea, the man killed, was 48 and lived just south of the border in Nogales, Mexico.[1]

## Border Patrol call recordings

Recordings obtained by Fox News Digital in March 2023 captured calls between the [[united-states-border-patrol|Border Patrol]], Kelly and the Santa Cruz County Sheriff's Office on January 30, 2023, in which a Border Patrol staffer questioned whether the rancher was "crazy" before agents responded to his report of armed drug traffickers on his property.[2] In one call, someone from the Nogales Border Patrol Station told dispatch that agents had made contact with the caller's spouse, "who stated that there were five on the property, and the caller is currently trying to pursue them and is chasing them south," adding that the five had a large backpack and possibly a rifle.[2]

The Santa Cruz County Attorney's Office, releasing calls ranging from about thirty seconds to eight minutes, stressed that Kelly never dialed 911 and that the recordings were relays and contacts initiated by dispatch.[2] Kelly said he found the body of Gabriel Cuen Buitimea later that evening while out with his dogs checking livestock on his ranch outside Nogales, Arizona.[2] He was initially arrested for first-degree murder, a charge later downgraded to second-degree murder, and his attorney suggested in court that cartels could be buying testimony against him.[2]

Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] argued that agents could not responsibly speculate about the rancher's mental state without the facts, and that charging a man defending his own property inverted the justice system by protecting perpetrators while leaving law-abiding citizens unable to defend themselves.[2]
