# Julian Assange

**Julian Assange**, the WikiLeaks publisher, is cited by Badlands Media as the archetypal case of the tactic later applied to [[russell-brand|Russell Brand]]: the deployment of sexual-abuse allegations against a figure who had won broad public sympathy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## The allegations

Once it became evident that most people empathised with Assange, allegations of sexual abuse suddenly appeared, along with an attempt to have him extradited to Sweden.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) Assange immediately denounced the allegations, describing them as a smear campaign assembled by the CIA.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## Place in the playbook

The article uses Assange, alongside Matt Gaetz and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], to argue that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] playbook is small and repetitive, rehashing the same handful of strategies against successive targets.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship) The underlying mechanism is psychological: accusations of rape, even without proof or supporting evidence, reliably provoke anger, aggression and disgust in audiences regardless of whether the claims are true.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/5gw-counter-offensive-the-censorship)

## February 2024 appeal hearing

In December 2023 it was announced that Assange would face two High Court judges over two days on February 20–21, 2024 at the Royal Courts of Justice in London, in what was expected to be his last appeal against extradition to the United States on [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]] charges.[4] His wife [[stella-assange|Stella Assange]] confirmed the dates publicly.[4] The appeal followed High Court Judge Jonathan Swift's June 6, 2023 rejection — in a three-page ruling — of Assange's 150-page appeal application.[4] Assange sought to challenge both the home secretary's extradition decision and, by cross-appeal, the legal findings of district judge Vanessa Baraitser, who in January 2021 had blocked extradition on mental-health and prison-conditions grounds while siding with the United States on every point of law.[4] The United States successfully appealed that decision by issuing "diplomatic assurances" that Assange would not be mistreated, which the High Court accepted after a two-day hearing in March 2022; the U.K. Supreme Court subsequently declined to hear the case.[4] The proceeding is treated in detail at [[assange-extradition-case|the Assange extradition case]].[4]

## London High Court extradition ruling (March 2024)

London's High Court was scheduled to deliver its verdict on Tuesday, March 26, 2024 on Assange's potential appeal against extradition from Britain to the United States, in what could be his final legal challenge in British courts.[2] US prosecutors sought to try Assange, then 52, on criminal charges stemming from [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]]' publication of extensive caches of confidential US military records and diplomatic cables, arguing the leaks endangered the lives of their operatives and that his conduct was criminal and unjustified.[2] His supporters regard him as a journalistic hero prosecuted for exposing US misconduct.[2] Britain approved his extradition in 2022; his initial appeal attempt was rebuffed, leading to a two-day hearing the previous month at which his legal team sought to overturn that judgment, with two senior judges due to announce their decision at 10:30 GMT.[2]

## Possible plea deal (August 2023)

In August 2023 the *Sydney Morning Herald* and The Intercept reported that the United States was weighing a plea deal that would allow Assange to return to Australia.[3] U.S. Ambassador [[caroline-kennedy|Caroline Kennedy]] said there could be a "resolution" to his then-four-year detention in Britain, adding "There is a way to resolve it" but that any deal would be "up to the Justice Department."[3] Assange, held in a London prison since 2019 while contesting extradition, faced 18 U.S. charges, 17 of them alleging violations of the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]], stemming from the 2010 publication of classified material including footage of a Baghdad airstrike that killed 18 civilians and hundreds of thousands of Iraq War field reports.[3] Kennedy's remarks followed Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]]'s rebuff of Australian appeals after a July meeting with Foreign Minister [[penny-wong|Penny Wong]] in Brisbane, where he called the conduct "very serious."[3] Assange's brother Gabriel Shipton said the administration appeared to be "searching for an off-ramp" before the Australian prime minister's October state visit.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Assange's persecution foreshadowed the broader weaponization of government, that his "crime" is journalism, and that "a government that punishes journalists is the definition of tyranny."[3] She contrasted the media establishment's unified defense of Fox News during the Obama administration with the silence surrounding journalist Simon Ateba's discrimination suit against the current administration, and expressed suspicion at the timing of the apparent about-face, asking who was actually negotiating for the United States when both the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] and the [[state-department|State Department]] declined to comment.[3]

## Plea agreement and release (June 2024)

On the evening of Monday, June 24, 2024, court documents filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern Mariana Islands revealed that Assange had reached a plea agreement with the U.S. [[justice-department|Justice Department]] that would allow him to go free after five years in a British prison.[5] He was charged by criminal information — a filing form that typically signifies a plea deal — with conspiracy to obtain and disclose national defense information.[5] A letter from Justice Department official Matthew McKenzie to U.S. District Judge Ramona Manglona stated that Assange would appear in court at 9 a.m. local time on Wednesday to plead guilty, and that the department expected him to return to Australia, his country of citizenship, after the proceedings.[5] He was expected to be sentenced to 62 months with credit for time served, leaving him free to return to the country of his birth.[5]

The charges stemmed from one of the largest publications of classified information in American history, which occurred during President Barack Obama's first term.[5] Beginning in late 2009, the government alleged, Assange conspired with [[chelsea-manning|Chelsea Manning]], a military intelligence analyst, to use [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] to disclose tens of thousands of activity reports about the war in Afghanistan, hundreds of thousands of reports about the war in Iraq, hundreds of thousands of [[state-department|State Department]] cables, and assessment briefs of detainees at Guantánamo Bay.[5] National Security Council spokesperson Adrienne Watson said in a statement that the plea was "an independent decision made by the Department of Justice and there was no White House involvement in the plea deal decision."[5]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright headlined the item "Convergence," writing that mileage varies within the truth community over whether Assange acted on behalf of patriots in a clandestine manner or simply became entangled between an entrenched criminal network and a large military intelligence operation.[5] In that reading, Assange's intent is irrelevant; what matters is what he knows — and, more importantly, what the prevailing narrative claims he knows, regarding Seth Rich, the Clinton Foundation, and the DNC.[5] The commentary framed the release as part of a "War of Stories" in which core truths are delivered to the public through characters functioning as cyphers, and remarked that for long-time Anons the period felt "VERY 2017."[5]

## Strasbourg address (October 2024)

In early October 2024, Assange traveled to Strasbourg, France to deliver his first public remarks since gaining his freedom in June, following years in the Ecuadorian Embassy and then Belmarsh Prison in London.[6] His speech was given on Tuesday, October 1, before the legal affairs and human rights committee of the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe, which subsequently issued a formal resolution recognizing him as a "political prisoner" and highlighting the chilling effect his case had on journalism worldwide.[6] The U.S. government had spent years pursuing his extradition, and at one point there were alleged assassination plans hatched by U.S. operatives.[6]

Among the most widely noted portions of the address was a detailed retelling of how the [[cia|CIA]] waged war against him personally, especially under the Trump administration, given that "Trump appointed two wolves in MAGA hats. Mike Pompeo, a Kansas Congressman and former arms executive as CIA director and William Barr, a former CIA officer as US attorney general."[6] Assange also described how the CIA had infiltrated European governments and institutions via sophisticated software later exposed by [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]], and how that disclosure unleashed further government retribution against him and other whistleblowers.[6]

## Complaint against the Nobel Foundation

In December 2025, Assange filed a criminal complaint against the [[nobel-foundation|Nobel Foundation]], accusing it of misusing Nobel Peace Prize funds by awarding them to Venezuelan opposition figure [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], who has publicly called on the United States to attack her own country.[7] The complaint, dated Wednesday, December 17, 2025, was submitted to Sweden's Economic Crime Authority and War Crimes Unit, where the foundation is based, and argues that Alfred Nobel's will legally restricts Peace Prize funds to purposes promoting peace and bars their use to advance war or foreign military intervention.[7]

Assange contends that disbursing prize money to Machado violates that mandate and could constitute a crime under Swedish law, warning that the Nobel Peace Prize could be used as an "instrument of war."[7] The filing cites statements in which Machado endorsed potential US military intervention in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and justified US strikes on boats off the Venezuelan coast that had killed 95 people to date, strikes the United Nations has condemned as "extrajudicial killings."[7] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the document also names all 30 members of the Nobel Board of Directors with specific criminal code citations and full legal names.[7]

## February 2024 final UK extradition challenge

On February 20, 2024, Assange's lawyers began his final U.K. legal challenge to block extradition to the United States on spying charges.[8] The 52-year-old had by then been fighting extradition for more than a decade, including seven years in self-exile inside the Ecuadorian Embassy in London and five years in a high-security prison.[8] His attorneys asked two High Court judges, Victoria Sharp and Jeremy Johnson, to grant a new appeal hearing — described as his last legal roll of the dice in Britain.[8] If the judges ruled against him, his remaining option would be to ask the European Court of Human Rights to block the extradition, though supporters feared he could be flown to the United States before such an intervention.[8]

The judges could have ruled at the end of the two-day hearing on the Wednesday, but were considered more likely to take several weeks.[8] The press freedom group Reporters Without Borders said the hearing "marks the beginning of the end of the extradition case, as any grounds rejected by these judges cannot be further appealed in the U.K. — bringing Assange dangerously close to extradition."[8] The proceedings form part of the broader [[assange-extradition-case|Assange extradition case]].[8]
