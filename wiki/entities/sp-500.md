# S&P 500

The S&P 500 is a United States stock index comprising the 500 largest publicly traded companies in America.[1] Because it covers a far broader slice of listed firms than the 30-company [[dow-jones-industrial-average|Dow Jones Industrial Average]], it can be inferred to be a better overall representation of the stock market.[1]

## Performance benchmarks cited

In 2022 the S&P 500 fell 19.44% for the year, while the technology- and growth-weighted [[nasdaq|NASDAQ]] fell 33.1%.[1]

When the [[covid-19-lockdowns|COVID lockdowns]] began in March 2020, the S&P 500 lost 35% of its value in an extremely short period, but most of the stock market fully recovered within six months.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that this recovery did not indicate that the economy had recovered — only that the perceived value of the 0.1% of US businesses that are publicly traded had recovered.[1]

## Relationship to the wider economy

Carlson stressed that the index and the economy are correlated but not identical: the market usually falls when the economy is expected to worsen and rises when it is expected to improve, yet the two do not always move in lock step.[1] The market can fall a year before the economy visibly deteriorates, begin rising before the economy bottoms out, or drop on fear of a recession that never arrives.[1]
