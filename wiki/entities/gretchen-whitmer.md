# Gretchen Whitmer

Gretchen Whitmer is the Democratic governor of Michigan. On April 9, 2025 she met with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]], her second sit-down with the president since he took office, and was praised by him during the encounter.[1]

## April 2025 Oval Office meeting

According to a spokesperson for the governor, Whitmer used the meeting to raise an ongoing ice storm in northern Michigan, investments in the Selfridge Air National Guard Base outside Detroit, invasive fish in the Great Lakes, and the day's dominant issue — tariffs.[1] Trump spoke positively of her leadership and brought her with him into the Oval Office while he signed a number of unrelated executive orders, telling reporters, "We're honored to have Gretchen Whitmer from Michigan, the great State of Michigan, and she's been, she's really done an excellent job, a very good person."[1] Whitmer's spokesperson said the governor was "surprised she was brought into the Oval Office" for Trump's press conference "without any notice."[1] The meeting echoed a bipartisan message Whitmer had delivered in a speech in Washington, D.C. earlier the same day.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributors treated the timing as significant rather than incidental. [[canncon|CannCon]] argued that Whitmer — whom he accused of having overseen, or looked the other way at, fraud in Michigan during the 2020 election — was unexpectedly present in the Oval Office at the exact moment Trump signed the memorandum ordering an investigation into former [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|CISA]] director [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]], calling the juxtaposition "absolutely epic."[1] [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] highlighted Whitmer's facial expression during the announcement and framed the episode as evidence that exposure of [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud]] was "ramping up," predicting that DOGE would play a part as well.[1]

## Expansion of the Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act

As governor, Whitmer signed the 2023 legislative expansion of the [[elliott-larsen-civil-rights-act|Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act]], the 1977 Michigan statute barring discrimination on the basis of characteristics including religion.[2] The expansion, enacted with the Democrat-controlled state legislature, extended the act's protections to gender identity and enshrined a 2022 Michigan Supreme Court interpretation into statute.[2] That amended law formed the legal basis for the November 2023 discrimination charge brought by the [[michigan-department-of-civil-rights|Michigan Department of Civil Rights]] against salon owner [[christine-geiger|Christine Geiger]] and her business [[studio-8-hair-lab-discrimination-charge|Studio 8 Hair Lab]].[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the case as an example of [[lawfare|lawfare]], in which "the process is the punishment" for defendants who decline to participate in contested speech norms.[2]

## 2024 replacement speculation

In February 2024, as the [[hur-report|Hur report]] intensified doubts about President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s fitness, Whitmer was named among the leading candidates to replace him on the Democratic ticket.[3] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that this outcome would favor the MAGA base rather than harm it, describing Whitmer and [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] as "two of the most-reviled political figures in the United States, whose tyranny was highlighted more than any other during the Covid era."[3] He further contended that ousting Biden would cede "the LAST of their faux legitimacy" to the voting base Democrats had persuaded to elect him.[3]

## Comment on the 2024 vice-presidential debate

Speaking to Jimmy Kimmel after the October 1, 2024 vice-presidential debate, Whitmer broke with her party's framing to acknowledge that Republican Senator [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] had outperformed expectations: "You know, JD Vance was a better debater than Donald Trump. Donald Trump should have taken some lessons from him."[4] Her remark was cited by The Hill as evidence that even Democrats had noted Vance's performance against [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]].[4]

### Criticism from Tucker Carlson (September 2023)

In a September 2023 video clip, [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] used Whitmer as a case study of the disconnect he alleged between public priorities and those of elected leaders, calling her a "low IQ lunatic" and arguing that her focus on social issues such as "misgendering" diverged from her constituents' concerns: "The things that people care about, Gretchen Whitmer couldn't care less about."[5]

## Conclusion of the kidnapping plot prosecutions

On Friday, September 15, 2023, a Michigan jury acquitted the last three defendants charged over the alleged 2020 plot to kidnap Whitmer, ending the [[whitmer-kidnapping-plot-trials|Whitmer kidnapping plot trials]].[6] Eric Molitor and twin brothers William and Michael Null were found not guilty of providing material support to a terrorist act and of possessing a firearm in the process, closing out the prosecution of fourteen defendants in total.[6] Prosecutors had argued the men planned to kidnap the governor because they believed she was infringing on their freedoms during Michigan's coronavirus restrictions, while the [[fbi|FBI]] became involved after a former member of the group grew disgruntled with its message of violence.[6] Badlands commentary argued that the acquittals amounted to a conviction of the FBI itself.[6]

## Fight Like Hell PAC and Authentic Campaigns

Whitmer's political action committee, "Fight Like Hell," paid $412,000 to the Democratic consulting firm [[authentic-campaigns|Authentic Campaigns]], whose president and partner is [[loren-merchan|Loren Merchan]], daughter of the New York judge [[juan-merchan|Juan Merchan]] presiding over [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Manhattan prosecution.[7] Investigative reporter [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] reported that [[alex-soros|Alex Soros]] met with Whitmer and with various Arizona Democrats after those contracts were signed, and while the Trump trial was under way — a sequence critics presented as evidence of financial ties between Democratic donor networks and the presiding judge's family.[7]

## December 2024 conciliation toward Trump

In the wake of the Republican trifecta in the 2024 elections, Whitmer adopted a markedly softer tone toward [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], telling CNN in December 2024: "I know Donald Trump cares about Michigan. And I'm hoping that because of that, we'll be able to find some common ground in some important ways."[8] The remarks contrasted sharply with the accusations she directed at him during his first term, particularly at the height of the pandemic, when she wrote that he had said he would "stand with Michigan" but had to "prove it."[8] She added, "People in Michigan, like a majority of Americans, voted for Donald Trump, and my oath is to Michigan."[8] Trump carried the swing state with 49.7% of the vote to [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s 48.3% according to the AP tally, reversing Michigan's 2020 result for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[8]

The previous month Whitmer had announced she would not join the alliance of anti-Trump governors organized by Illinois governor [[jb-pritzker|JB Pritzker]] and Colorado governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]], distancing herself from colleagues who had pledged war on the incoming administration and aligning more closely with figures such as New York governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]].[8] Widely seen as a possible Biden replacement in 2024 and a favorite to lead the Democratic Party in 2028, Whitmer's repositioning was read as presidential preparation.[8]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] paraphrased the shift as "Sorry about that whole Nazi thing, but we should look to the future now, right?", calling Whitmer "one of the worst offenders of weaponized government and regime elitism" and arguing that Democratic authoritarians were tacking back in the hope that voters would forget their conduct under emergency powers.[8] She framed the response as a matter of memory and accountability, writing that "accountability is a requirement for the future of public trust" and that such figures "can't outmaneuver the justice phase, because justice is not political."[8]
