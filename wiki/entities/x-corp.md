# X

X is the American social media platform formerly known as Twitter. It figures in a [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] interim staff report as one of the companies that resisted compliance with Brazil's censorship regime and faced retaliation as a result.[1]

## Conflict with Brazilian authorities

The committee's report stated that when platforms such as X and [[rumble|Rumble]] refused to fully comply with takedown demands issued through the system led by Brazilian Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]], Brazilian authorities fined them and threatened or ordered them to cease operations inside Brazil.[1] The report described the orders in question as global in reach and as stripping platforms of the legal protections that would otherwise limit their liability for user posts.[1]

Because some of the targeted content originated with users inside the United States — including Brazilian journalists and commentators living in the US, posts praising President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and posts criticizing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] — the committee warned that compelling American companies to police US residents' speech creates a broader threat to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[1]

## Lawsuit against Media Matters (2023)

On Monday, 20 November 2023, X filed suit against the left-leaning non-profit [[media-matters|Media Matters]] and "all those who colluded" with it in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Fort Worth Division.[2] The complaint alleged that the organization's tactics in reporting on advertisement placement were manipulative and deceptive and that it had "completely misrepresent[ed]" the real user experience on the platform.[2] Owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had promised the "thermonuclear lawsuit" early the previous Saturday, and its filing coincided with an announcement by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] that his office was investigating Media Matters for potential fraudulent activity, including allegations that it had fraudulently manipulated data on X.com.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the filing as part of a "lawsuit cascade" in the ongoing [[information-war|Info War]], noting the significance of both its timing and the figures involved ahead of the 2024 election.[2]

## March 2025 outages

In March 2025 X suffered at least three major outages across a single Monday, reported by tens of thousands of users of both the website and app.[3] The pro-Palestinian hacker group [[dark-storm-team|Dark Storm Team]] claimed responsibility for a DDoS attack in a public Telegram post, while owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] suggested that a "large, coordinated group and/or a country" was behind the "massive" hack, noting the platform is attacked daily but that this incident "was done with a lot of resources."[3] Musk later told Larry Kudlow on Fox News that the attackers used "IP addresses originating in the Ukraine area."[3]

## Au10tix identity-verification controversy (June 2024)

In June 2024 X's partnership with the Israeli identity-verification firm [[au10tix|Au10tix]] became the subject of a social-media campaign, with activists demanding that the platform end the arrangement.[4] For some years Au10tix had supplied X with identity-verification services for its users.[4] Activists alleged that the firm's executives were veterans of [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]], [[mossad|Mossad]] and IDF Unit 8200 and that they handed over the details of X users to Israeli security authorities — claims Israeli business media reported as factually false.[4] X owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] escalated the story by posting "I will investigate this."[4] A survey run on the platform by activist Lucas Gage found 92 percent of some 50,000 respondents favoring removal of the service.[4] Badlands commentary tied the episode to X's earlier reported engagement with the Israeli cybersecurity firm [[cheq|CHEQ]] over bot removal.[4]

## Lawsuit against Australia's eSafety Commissioner (April 2024)

X sued the Australian government after its "world-first" [[esafety-commissioner|E-Safety Commissioner]] ordered an "offensive" post to be removed from the platform.[5] Daily Mail Australia had revealed that X faced an $800,000 fine if it did not remove a post written by Canadian man [[chris-elston|Chris Elston]], in which he misgendered and made "disparaging" remarks about Australian citizen [[teddy-cook|Teddy Cook]] — a female-to-male trans man who advocated taxpayer-funded surgeries for all transgender Australians and was controversially appointed to a [[world-health-organization|World Health Organisation]] expert panel.[5] X, formerly Twitter, complied with the government-run commissioner's request and geo-blocked the post in Australia.[5] The demand backfired when the offending post was re-shared in a different format and viewed hundreds of thousands of times, after which X revealed it would mount a legal challenge to "protect its user's right to free speech."[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the suit as part of the pushback against the [[censorship-industrial-complex|Censorship Industrial Complex]], writing that "Coordination = Control. That goes both ways."[5]

## GARM advertising boycott lawsuit (July 2024)

On July 11, 2024, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] announced that X would sue the "perpetrators and collaborators" behind what he called an advertising boycott racket, following an interim staff report released the previous day by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]].[6] "Having seen the evidence unearthed today by Congress, 𝕏 has no choice but to file suit against the perpetrators and collaborators in the advertising boycott racket," Musk wrote, adding, "Hopefully, some states will consider criminal prosecution."[6]

The report described a coordinated effort by the [[world-federation-of-advertisers|World Federation of Advertisers]] and its [[global-alliance-for-responsible-media|Global Alliance for Responsible Media]] initiative to demonetize and suppress disfavored content across the internet, an alliance representing roughly 90 percent of global advertising spend.[6]

## 2023 traffic data

In November 2023, new web traffic data from Google was reported to show X surpassing Instagram and Facebook, contradicting a year of corporate media predictions of the platform's demise.[7] Coverage argued that dying legacy outlets had waged an all-out assault on X because they perceived its emphasis on free speech as an existential threat to their ideological narrative control.[7] When [[meta-platforms|Meta]] launched its competing network Threads in early July 2023, corporate media coalesced around the "Twitter Killer" framing, and Bloomberg data showed that phrase erupting across headlines in what was characterised as a concerted effort by the status quo against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[7] The reports of X being on death row were described as disinformation pushed by legacy media on behalf of CBS, ABC, NBC and others.[7]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] commented that one need not like or use X to recognise its significance, arguing that the platform occupied a more powerful information-dissemination position than it had as Twitter despite steady media deployments to the contrary.[7] He held that Musk now controlled the most powerful narrative-projection platform in a sociopolitical landscape where such control was the key to winning what he called World War 3, a conflict largely taking place within the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]].[7] Either Musk's takeover was the most serendipitous stroke of good fortune to hit the online battlefield ahead of 2024, he wrote, or there was much more than meets the eye — he counted himself among those who believe Musk is a de facto custodian of X on behalf of the US military and a Sovereign Alliance.[7] Either way, in his framing, "they" lost their biggest weapon, "which means we gained it."[7]

## Reinstatement of Douglass Mackey's account

In July 2025, X reinstated the meme account of [[douglass-mackey|Douglass Mackey]] following the overturning of his conviction in an election case; as of press time the @Ricky_Vaughn99 account was active and no longer subject to platform restrictions.[8] The account had not posted since 2016, when the platform was still Twitter, and had been at the center of Mackey's prosecution because he used it to post a meme jokingly encouraging supporters of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] to cast their votes by text.[8] The Second Circuit reversed the conviction and remanded with instructions to enter a judgment of acquittal, finding the evidence insufficient to prove he knowingly agreed to join the charged conspiracy.[8]

## Supreme Court petition over government data demands (June 2024)

In June 2024 X Corp. petitioned the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to review whether social media platforms can be forced to disclose user data to the government while being prohibited from telling the affected users about the request.[9] The petition followed the company's 2023 compliance, as Twitter, with special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s demand for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s account data, obtained without Trump's knowledge after the company's attempt to delay disclosure failed.[9] The federal judge in that case questioned whether X was trying to "cozy up" to the former president, an allegation owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had faced before.[9]

## "Not A Bot" subscription test

In October 2023, X launched the [[not-a-bot-program|"Not A Bot" program]], charging new, unverified accounts in New Zealand and the Philippines a $1 annual subscription to post or interact with other posts, with existing users unaffected.[10] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] said the measure would make it "1000X harder to manipulate the platform," and the company described it as a way to reduce spam, manipulation and bot activity while balancing accessibility, insisting it was "not a profit driver" and noting that subscriptions had "proven to be the main solution that works at scale."[10] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the fee's introduction showed the platform was still struggling with bots despite a year of promises, though she considered a dollar a year a trivial price and noted X's continued growth as a public square.[10]

## Grimes's criticism (July 2025)

In July 2025, the musician [[grimes|Grimes]] — the former partner of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], with whom she shares three children — branded X "poison" and "a prison" in a post on the platform.[11] Grimes, born Claire Boucher, had joined Twitter in 2011; Musk bought the site in 2022 and rebranded it X.[11] Returning to the platform for the first time since June 14 to discuss working with the English dance musician Sub Focus, she followed up by writing that "returning here it is overrwhelmingly [sic] abundantly and profoundly clear that this place - and all of these places - are a poison - a prison of utterly short form deep sounding nonsense attached to no one that ur brain will discard imaging its learning."[11]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded with a series of rhetorical questions about whether users end up believing untrue things, getting upset about matters they forget within days, treating the consensus of the popular crowd as objective truth, and pretending for social credit that a censored site is a free speech utopia — concluding that such users belong at "the Intellectual Kids Table on XformerlyTwitter."[11]


## Preparation of EU penalties (2025)

In April 2025 the New York Times reported that [[european-union|European Union]] regulators were preparing major penalties against X for breaking the bloc's landmark law against illicit content and disinformation, the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[12] Four people with knowledge of the plans said the penalties would include a fine and demands for product changes and were expected to be announced that summer, which would make them the first ever issued under the law.[12] One person said the fine could surpass $1 billion as regulators sought to make an example of X and deter other platforms from violating the statute; European authorities were simultaneously weighing the risk of further antagonizing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], given owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s position as one of his closest advisers.[12] EU officials maintained that the investigation was proceeding independently of the tariff negotiations that followed Trump's [[liberation-day-tariffs|Liberation Day]] announcement.[12]

## First EU Digital Services Act probe (December 2023)

On December 18, 2023, the [[european-union|European Union]] opened infringement proceedings against X over suspected failure to combat content disinformation and manipulation, the first such probe brought under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[13] The bloc's regulator [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] said the move responded to suspected breaches of X's transparency obligations and its duties to counter illegal content and disinformation, as well as to what the EU characterized as a "deceptive" design of the platform's user interface relating to its blue check verification marks.[13]

X said in a pre-prepared statement distributed on social media that it "remains committed to complying with the Digital Services Act and is cooperating with the regulatory process," adding that "it is important that this process remains free of political influence and follows the law" and that the company was "focused on creating a safe and inclusive environment for all users on our platform, while protecting freedom of expression."[13] The [[european-commission|European Commission]] said it launched the proceedings on the basis of its preliminary investigation, including analysis of X's September risk assessment report, the platform's November 3 transparency report, and X's replies to a formal request for information concerning, among other matters, the dissemination of illegal content in the context of [[hamas|Hamas]]' terrorist attacks against Israel.[13]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the probe as part of a rapid cascade of moves by establishment administrative arms to cut off a form of power projection they had perfected before losing control of it with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s purchase of the platform, describing X as the most powerful information dissemination apparatus in the world.[13]

## Brazil shutdown warning (August 2024)

On the night of August 29, 2024, X's Global Government Affairs account warned that service in [[brazil|Brazil]] could be shut down "soon" after Supreme Court Judge [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] moved against the company for refusing to comply with what X called his "illegal orders to censor his political opponents."[14] The company said that when it attempted to defend itself in court, de Moraes threatened its Brazilian legal representative with imprisonment and froze her bank accounts even after she resigned, while its challenges were dismissed or ignored and his Supreme Court colleagues proved "either unwilling or unable to stand up to him."[14] X stated it was not demanding that other countries adopt US free speech law but that de Moraes was demanding the company break Brazil's own laws, pledged to publish all of his orders and related filings for transparency, and said that unlike other platforms it would "not comply in secret with illegal orders."[14] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] said separately that [[spacex|SpaceX]] would continue providing [[starlink|Starlink]] service to Brazilian schools and hospitals free of charge.[14]

## EU Digital Services Act fine (December 2025)

On December 5, 2025, the [[european-commission|European Commission]] fined X 120 million euros (approximately $140 million) after a two-year investigation under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]], faulting the platform's "deceptive" blue checkmark and the lack of transparency in its advertising repository.[15] Musk replied to the Commission's post on X with a one-word obscenity and, the next day, called for the [[european-union|European Union]] itself to be abolished with sovereignty returned to individual countries.[15] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] characterized the penalty as "an attack on all American tech platforms and the American people by foreign governments."[15]

## "The People's Town Hall" (2024)

In late May 2024, X and the cable news network NewsNation were reported by [[axios|Axios]] to be planning live town halls with former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and independent presidential candidate [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], with both having agreed in principle to separate live interviews as part of a new video series titled "The People's Town Hall."[16] Dates and locations had not been set, and the [[joe-biden|Biden]] campaign had not agreed to take part; a Biden spokesperson declined to comment, while the Trump and Kennedy campaigns did not respond to requests for comment.[16]

Axios framed the plan as significant because, while every other major social network in the United States was retreating from politics, X was leaning in as part of a wider push into creator video, with the live feeds to be available to all X users globally.[16] X CEO [[linda-yaccarino|Linda Yaccarino]] had said the previous week that X paid out over $50 million in ad revenue to creators since she became CEO.[16] Separately, Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that X users were complaining their feeds were overwhelmingly pro-Trump and blaming the platform's algorithm, a claim she dismissed alongside Trump's post-conviction $200 million fundraising haul and his 2 million TikTok followers against Biden's 337,000.[16]

## February 2025: Settlement of the Trump deplatforming suit

In February 2025 [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s X agreed to pay about $10 million to settle a lawsuit that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had brought against the company and its former chief executive, making X the second social-media platform to settle litigation Trump filed after being deplatformed over his role in the January 6, 2021 riot at the U.S. Capitol.[17] Trump and other plaintiffs filed the suit in 2021 against the company, then called Twitter, and its chief executive at the time, [[jack-dorsey|Jack Dorsey]].[17] Musk reinstated Trump on the platform in November 2022.[17]

Trump's team considered letting the lawsuit fizzle out, citing Musk's proximity to the president and the $250 million he spent to help elect him, but ultimately moved forward with the settlement despite the two men's close relationship; a spokesman for X did not respond to requests for comment.[17]

## Brazil blocking dispute and House inquiry

X received an inquiry from the [[us-house-of-representatives|U.S. House of Representatives]] in April 2024 "regarding actions taken in Brazil that were in violation of Brazilian law," according to owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who said the platform had been asked to suspend the accounts of sitting Brazilian legislators and many journalists.[18] The inquiry followed Brazilian Supreme Court judge [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] opening a probe into Musk over his stated intention to restore blocked accounts, with a threatened penalty of 100,000 reais per day for noncompliance.[18] Neither the House nor X immediately responded to press requests for comment.[18]

## Debate streaming and vendor change (June 2024)

In late June 2024, X owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] said the platform would not block or remove live streams and footage of the June 27 [[2024-presidential-debate|presidential debate]], rejecting [[cnn|CNN]]'s position that its debate feed could not be simulcast with commentary and stating that the DMCA does not apply.[19] CNN had told The Post Millennial that its debates were exclusive to the network and could only be embedded via its own YouTube player, and had informed podcaster [[tim-pool|Tim Pool]] that he could not legally simulcast with commentary and fact checks.[19]

Separately, X replaced its identity-verification contractor [[au10tix|AU10TIX]] — whose parent was identified in Badlands coverage as the Israeli firm [[i-sec|I-SEC]] — with the payment processor [[stripe|Stripe]], after users raised concerns that the verification process, which required a government-issued photo ID and home address, was handled by a company tied to Israeli intelligence.[19]
