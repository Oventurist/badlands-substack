# Revenue Act of 1913

The Revenue Act of 1913, also known as the Underwood Tariff Act, was passed by the United States Congress and signed into law by President Woodrow Wilson on October 3, 1913. According to the article, the Act dramatically lowered tariffs and implemented an income tax, thereby establishing the Internal Revenue Service (IRS).

The source claims that after the ratification of the Sixteenth Amendment, Democratic leaders agreed to seek passage of the Revenue Act of 1913. Representative Oscar Underwood is identified as having quickly moved the bill through the House of Representatives, with approval in the United States Senate following extensive lobbying by the Wilson administration.

The article frames the Revenue Act of 1913 as one half of the cabal's dual consolidation of power in 1913, alongside the Federal Reserve Act. It asserts that with the passage of the Act, "Wilson had successfully enslaved every American to the fiat monetary system." According to the source, the Act required every American to pay income tax and contributed to the continued devaluation of money.

The Revenue Act is linked in the source to the earlier Populist Party demand for a graduated income tax, which the party included in its 1892 platform and which was mirrored by the Democratic Party under William Jennings Bryan in 1894 and 1908. The article claims the income tax was designed to "permanently suppress the citizens" after the Long Depression "was doing more damage to the rich and powerful."
