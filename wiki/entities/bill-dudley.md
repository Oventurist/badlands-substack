# Bill Dudley

Bill Dudley is a former top economist at Goldman Sachs and a former president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York whose post-retirement commentary — largely delivered through opinion columns at Bloomberg — has made him a recurring symbol, in Badlands Media coverage, of the political entanglement of American central banking.[1]

## The 2019 op-ed

Dudley is best known for an August 2019 Bloomberg op-ed in which he argued that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] should refrain from cushioning the economic effects of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s trade policy, and suggested that Fed officials weigh the political consequences of their decisions. In the piece he wrote that Fed officials faced a choice between enabling the administration's "disastrous path of trade war escalation" or signalling that "the president, not the Fed, will bear the risks — including the risk of losing the next election."[1] He went further, writing that "there's even an argument that the election itself falls within the Fed's purview," on the theory that Trump's reelection presented a threat to the U.S. and global economy and to the Fed's independence.[1] Critics — including [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], whose write-up the Badlands brief excerpted — read the column as proof that the central bank functions as "a tool to perpetuate the established status quo" and to crush interlopers who might jeopardise the wealth imbalance built up since the Fed's founding in 1913.[1]

## The 2024 reversal

In July 2024 Dudley reversed his earlier hawkish stance and publicly demanded an immediate interest-rate cut, a shift ZeroHedge characterised as a violent flip-flop and as further confirmation that the Fed operates as a political instrument of an oligarchic elite.[1] The Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the reversal as a deliberate narrative deployment: an attempt by Dudley "and his vampiric banking ilk" to juice the economy ahead of the 2024 election after having tried to do the opposite during Trump's first term.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands' reading emphasised failure rather than power. Burning Bright argued that Dudley's 2019 campaign failed — the Federal Reserve under [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] ignored the advice of its "sycophants" and cut rates — and that his 2024 campaign was similarly being ignored, raising the question of who actually controls the central bank if legacy establishment figures no longer do.[1] Dudley's name, the commentary noted, means little to most Americans but is representative of the "inside baseball" financial politics that have shaped ostensibly democratic governance since the creation of the modern U.S. central bank in 1913.[1]
