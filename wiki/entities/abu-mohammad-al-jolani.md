# Abu Mohammad al-Jolani

Abu Mohammad al-Jolani is the leader of [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS), the militant coalition whose December 2024 offensive toppled [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[1]

## Media rebranding

Badlands Media's coverage argues that Western reporting on al-Jolani followed a predictable narrative "switcheroo," recasting him as a "blazer-wearing revolutionary" — a framing described as far more disarming to Western audiences than "radical jihadist."[1] The article treats this as an instance of a broader pattern in which the U.S. security state's media apparatus conditions public opinion in support of regime-change objectives.[1]

## Terrorism designation

The article notes that this is the same al-Jolani who spent five years in Abu Ghraib and other prisons and who remained on a [[state-department|State Department]] wanted list as a "Specially Designated Global Terrorist," carrying a reward of up to $10 million for information on the leader of the al-Nusrah Front.[1] That designation is presented as evidence of the contradiction between official U.S. counterterrorism policy and Western media's celebratory framing of HTS's victory.[1]

## Rehabilitation in western media, December 2024

Following the seizure of Damascus, Newsweek commentary described Jolani as a former Al Qaeda commander who had taken power in the Syrian capital to the jubilation of Washington regime-changers, noting how quickly the profane ideological origins of militant groups can be forgotten once the erstwhile extremists appear to align with U.S. geopolitical interests.[2] The commentary observed that Jolani appeared to indicate he would not persecute minority religious or ethnic factions, but judged that hardly a pledge to inspire confidence in notoriously sectarian Syria.[2]

Badlands commentary argued that over the course of a week western media, including Israeli outlets, had rebranded Jolani as a "tolerant terrorist" and promoted him as a kind of diversity-friendly jihadi.[2] It further noted that Jolani had served as a deputy to [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]], the [[islamic-state|ISIS]] leader eulogized by the Washington Post as an "austere scholar" and described by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as having "died like a dog" after being pursued into a tunnel, where he detonated a suicide vest.[2] The commentary framed the celebration of Jolani as incompatible with continued 9/11 memorialization and as evidence of a coming reckoning for the establishment.[2]
