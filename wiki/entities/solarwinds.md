# SolarWinds

**SolarWinds** is the American IT management software company whose compromise produced one of the most consequential cybersecurity breaches of United States government and corporate networks.[1]

In the Badlands Media article "Blockchain, Good. Blockchain Voting, Bad.", [[holly-at-altitude|Holly at Altitude]] cites the SolarWinds breach as evidence that even with the power and resources of the government and the military, devastating cybersecurity breaches occur regularly.[1] She describes it as one of the most devastating such breaches, notes that the U.S. government was unaware of it for months, and states that the damage remains unknown.[1]

The reference appears within the article's argument that nation-state attacks on critical infrastructure are a persistent, relentless threat, and that this threat environment undermines claims that [[blockchain-voting|blockchain voting]] could be secured once integrated with existing election infrastructure — since no hardware or software, including phones, can be completely secured, and systems are routinely breached with data manipulated through peripherals outside any blockchain.[1] The same passage observes that only a handful of electronic voting equipment companies exist worldwide, that the top three control roughly 90% of the U.S. market, and that nearly all equipment used in U.S. elections is manufactured in China, with countless billions of dollars, critical military data, and valuable intellectual property lost to equipment and software breaches.[1]
