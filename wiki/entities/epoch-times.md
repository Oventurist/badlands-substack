# The Epoch Times

The Epoch Times is a conservative-leaning news organization whose reporting is frequently cited in Truth Community media.[1]

## Bannon interview on a second Trump term

In January 2024, The Epoch Times published an exclusive interview with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] in which he described what the opening of a second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] term would look like. Bannon predicted the first 100 days would be "the equivalent of the first 100 days of FDR," but inverted: where Franklin Delano Roosevelt laid the foundations of the administrative state, Trump's opening period would begin "the deconstruction of that and the destruction of the deep state, along with dozens and dozens and dozens of other policy proposals."[1]

The interview also revisited the 2016 transition, in which Bannon served before becoming White House chief strategist. He dismissed the transition work product assembled under former New Jersey Governor Chris Christie as "a joke," saying "we reviewed it, but we threw that away," and noted that Christie was quickly replaced as transition lead by Vice President-elect [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]]. The piece cited [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]]'s memoir *Breaking History*, in which Kushner recalled that "during the first few days on the job, every hour felt like a race," and noted contemporaneous reporting on tensions between Bannon and Kushner over the administration's initial agenda.[1]

## Reporting on the Georgia certification ruling (October 2024)

The Epoch Times reported the October 14, 2024 decision by [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] Superior Court Judge [[robert-mcburney|Robert McBurney]] holding that Georgia county election officials must certify election results by the statutory deadline regardless of irregularities or suspected fraud, a story carried in the Badlands News Brief of October 16, 2024.[2] The report quoted the ruling's core holding that no election superintendent "may refuse to certify or abstain from certifying election results under any circumstance," and identified [[julie-adams|Julie Adams]] as the Republican board member who brought the suit.[2]
