# Institutional Revolutionary Party

The Institutional Revolutionary Party (Partido Revolucionario Institucional, PRI) is the Mexican political party that governed the country continuously for roughly seventy years before Vicente Fox's election ended its monopoly.[1] [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]] was its candidate and leader when he became Mexico's 60th president in 1988.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents the PRI's longevity as engineered rather than earned: Mexico's one-party system, he writes, was not a coincidence, because the [[cia|CIA]] had controlled the Mexican government for a long time and was not going to let voters change that.[1] Under the system, presidents served a single six-year term and the outgoing president literally hand-picked his successor — in Lange's assessment "a perfect recipe for corruption and graft" and an ideal environment for CIA-linked drug cartels.[1]

The party's role in the [[1988-mexican-presidential-election|1988 election]] is the article's central illustration. Outgoing president [[miguel-de-la-madrid|Miguel de la Madrid]]'s memoirs confirmed that with early returns running heavily against the PRI, the government claimed the vote-tabulating computer system had crashed and then declared Salinas the winner.[1] Three years later, in an alliance between the PRI and the conservative National Action Party, the Mexican Congress ordered the 1988 ballots burned — what Lange calls "the Mexican uni-party to the rescue."[1]

Global Research, cited in the article, adds that with Salinas's inauguration the Mexican state apparatus became criminalized, with cartel-linked figures occupying key positions; the PRI's number-two official, [[jose-francisco-ruiz-massieu|José Francisco Ruiz Massieu]], was named in U.S. filings as involved in the family's narcotics dealings and was assassinated in 1994.[1]
