# Carron J. Phillips

Carron J. Phillips is an American sports writer who wrote for [[deadspin|Deadspin]]. In November 2023 he became the center of a national controversy after publishing an article criticizing a young [[kansas-city-chiefs|Kansas City Chiefs]] fan who attended a game wearing a Native American–style headdress with his face painted black and red.[1]

## The 2023 Chiefs fan controversy

Phillips, described in coverage as a "Journalist of the Year," argued in his Deadspin piece that the [[nfl|NFL]] needed to "speak out" against the boy, who appeared to be roughly nine or ten years old. He accused the child of displaying "black face," using an image that concealed the other side of the child's face, which was painted red — red and black being the team colors of the Kansas City Chiefs.[1]

When challenged, Phillips doubled down, asserting that the red half of the boy's face "made it worse."[1] The narrative collapsed further when it was reported late on Tuesday, November 28, 2023, that the child in question was himself Native American and that his father was a tribal board member.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the episode as evidence that "regime journalists" were so desperate for racism narratives that they had begun attacking children at football games, calling the fabricated story "peak privilege." She contrasted it with what she described as left-wing policies in Colorado creating a double energy crisis for indigenous elders on the Navajo reservation in the Four Corners region, arguing that the same faction attacking indigenous children's cultural expression was harming indigenous communities materially. She called for Phillips to lose his job and for Deadspin to lose its audience.[1]
