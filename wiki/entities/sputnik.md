# Sputnik

Sputnik is a Russian state-funded international news outlet operating under the Rossiya Segodnya media group. Its Azerbaijani bureau became the focus of a diplomatic dispute between Moscow and Baku in mid-2025.[1]

## 2025 detentions in Baku

On June 30, 2025, two senior editors with Sputnik and one journalist with the Ruptly video news agency were detained by Azerbaijani authorities in Baku.[1] Russian officials said the reporters were Russian citizens and that Russian diplomats had not been able to contact them.[1] Dmitry Kiselev, the head of the Rossiya Segodnya media group to which Sputnik belongs, described the arrests as an "injustice," insisting that "there have been no objections to the journalistic work of Sputnik Azerbaijan."[1]

Kiselev added that Russian and Azerbaijani officials had agreed in early April, during a meeting in Moscow, to iron out the "formalities" of the outlet's operations in Azerbaijan, but that Baku had allegedly stalled the process.[1]

## Diplomatic fallout

Following the arrests, Russia's Foreign Ministry summoned Azerbaijani ambassador Rakhman Mustafaev.[1] Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] said that "in light of Baku's hostile actions and the unlawful detention of Russian journalists… the ambassador of the Azerbaijani Republic has been summoned to the Foreign Ministry."[1] The episode followed a police raid in the Russian city of Yekaterinburg targeting the local Azerbaijani diaspora, part of a broader souring of relations between the two states.[1]

Badlands commentary characterized Azerbaijan as an Israeli proxy state bordering Iran and suspected of a role in Israel's recent bombing campaign, arguing that the detentions — alongside Ukrainian-Azerbaijani consular consultations and Kiev's vocal support for Israeli strikes on Iran — showed the two major wars dominating headlines merging into a single conflict.[1]
