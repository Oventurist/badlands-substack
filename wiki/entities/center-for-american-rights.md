# Center for American Rights

The Center for American Rights (CAR) is a legal advocacy organization that in October 2024 filed a formal complaint with the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] against [[cbs-news|CBS News]] over the network's editing of its [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] interview with Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[1]

## FCC complaint against CBS

The complaint charged that CBS engaged in "significant and intentional news distortion" in its handling of the Harris interview.[1] CAR argued that the discrepancies between the edited and unedited portions of the broadcast "amount to deliberate news distortion — a violation of FCC rules governing broadcasters' public interest obligations."[1] The filing sought to compel CBS to release an unedited transcript of the interview in order to set the record straight.[1]

CAR president Daniel Suhr said in a statement that "This isn't just about one interview or one network," adding that "This is about the public's trust in the media on critical issues of national security and international relations during one of the most consequential elections of our time."[1] Suhr further argued that "When broadcasters manipulate interviews and distort reality, it undermines democracy itself. The FCC must act swiftly to restore public confidence in our news media."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media's analysis cautioned that the complaint was unlikely to produce consequences in the near term, noting that the FCC rules by committee and that the commission's balance at the time was 3–2 in favor of Democratic appointees, leaving Republican commissioners such as [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] ideologically outnumbered.[1]
