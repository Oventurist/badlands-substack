# ISIS-K

ISIS-K is the Afghanistan-based branch of the [[isis|Islamic State]] held responsible for the August 26, 2021 suicide bombing at the Abbey Gate entrance of Hamid Karzai International Airport in Kabul, an attack that killed 13 U.S. service members and more than 100 Afghan civilians during the American withdrawal from Afghanistan.[1]

## Killing of the Abbey Gate mastermind

In April 2023, senior U.S. administration officials disclosed that the ISIS-K leader who planned the [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate bombing]] had been killed by the [[taliban|Taliban]] in Afghanistan.[1] The [[white-house|White House]] declined to release the man's name but described him as the "mastermind" of the assault on one of the main evacuation entry points.[1] Officials said they had learned of the death weeks earlier but required time to confirm it, and that government experts held "high confidence" that the individual "was indeed the key individual responsible" for the attack.[1] The United States was not informed of the death by the Taliban; the determination came from its own intelligence gathering and monitoring of threats and actors in the region.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the killing as confirmation of an analysis advanced by [[just-human|Just Human]] in August 2021: that the Taliban had been tasked by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], under the framework of the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]], with helping to eliminate what Badlands describes as the globalist-funded terrorist threat of ISIS.[1] In this reading, the relative quiet of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] on Afghanistan reflected the fact that events there were proceeding favorably rather than poorly.[1]

## Suspected role in the 2025 Kashmir attack

Following the [[2025-kashmir-terrorist-attack|April 2025 attack]] in India-administered Kashmir that killed 26 people, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] speculated that ISIS-K was responsible, together with what he called bad actors in the U.S. [[state-department|State Department]] and [[cia|CIA]].[2] He recalled that the group carried out the Abbey Gate bombing at Kabul Airport during the Afghanistan withdrawal, killing 13 U.S. soldiers, and was subsequently hunted down by the Taliban in keeping with commitments made to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] under the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]].[2]

## Context for the 2024 Dagestan attacks

The June 2024 gun attacks on places of worship in [[dagestan|Dagestan]] came three months after ISIS-K claimed responsibility for the assault on Crocus City Hall in Moscow, which killed more than 140 people in one of Russia's deadliest terrorist atrocities in years.[3] No group claimed the Dagestan attacks, though Russian law enforcement told TASS the gunmen were "adherents of an international terrorist organization."[3]
