# Ofcom

The **Office of Communications (Ofcom)** is the United Kingdom's communications regulator, designated as the primary enforcement body under the **Online Safety Bill (OSB)** passed in **September 2023**. In the Badlands Media corpus, Ofcom is framed as the operational arm of the UK's institutional censorship apparatus, empowered to impose fines and platform bans on social media services that do not comply with the bill's content rules.

## Regulatory Mandate Under the OSB

The Online Safety Bill's full legislative title assigns Ofcom the role of regulating "certain internet services" and overseeing "communications offences." According to the Badlands source, the bill forces tech platforms to comply with its ruleset "or face humongous fines," and that if fines are ignored, the decision will be made as to "whether or not the platform will be banned." The source frames this as giving Ofcom the power to remove platforms from the UK market entirely for non-compliance with speech restrictions.

## Ofcom Leadership

**Dame Melanie Dawes** was the recently appointed Chief Executive of Ofcom at the time of the OSB's passage. The article quotes Dawes as saying at a press conference: *"Today is a major milestone in the mission to create a safer life online for children and adults in the UK. Everyone at Ofcom feels privileged to be entrusted with this important role, and we're ready to start implementing these new laws."* The source characterizes Dawes's tone as "genuinely gleeful" and uses the quote to illustrate what it portrays as regulatory enthusiasm for expanded censorship powers.

## Relationship to Censorship Framework

Within the Badlands corpus, Ofcom is positioned as the domestic enforcement mechanism for a broader international censorship regime that includes the **Trusted News Initiative**, the **Anti-Defamation League**, and sympathetic state actors such as **Rishi Sunak**'s government. The article argues that Ofcom's mandate under the OSB will enable the UK to issue threats to platforms demanding removal of "effective critic[s] of the state" on the grounds that they are "peddling harmful conspiracy theories." The source presents Ofcom not as a neutral regulator but as an institution eagerly implementing a framework designed to curtail political dissent.

## Broader Context

The article notes that the UK's approach to platform regulation has international parallels, citing **France's** 2022 demands against **Rumble** over Russian news content as an example of how European governments are willing to use access restriction as a censorship tool. Ofcom is thus framed within the Badlands corpus as part of a transatlantic pattern of regulatory pressure on alternative media platforms during election cycles.
