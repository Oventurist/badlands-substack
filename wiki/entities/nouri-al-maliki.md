# Nouri Al-Maliki

Nouri Al-Maliki is an Iraqi politician who served two consecutive terms as Prime Minister of [[iraq|Iraq]] beginning in 2006, and who in January 2026 was announced as the candidate of the Shia party in Iraq's upcoming elections — a development that drew unusually intense attention across the information battle space.[1]

## Background and installation

Al-Maliki took office in 2006, placing him among the political figures installed by the United States after the toppling of Saddam Hussein.[1] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that Iraq is a country rarely discussed in independent media because it has been presumed to be under [[deep-state|Deep State]] control since Saddam's fall, and therefore generates little geopolitical friction.[1]

## 2026 candidacy and reaction

Al-Maliki's re-emergence as the Shia party's candidate prompted visible alarm in the Western establishment, with the algorithms amplifying an Iraqi primary contest that would ordinarily attract minimal attention.[1] [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] issued thinly veiled threats warning against any Iraqi alliance with [[iran|Iran]].[1] The [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] reported allegations that Al-Maliki helped create [[isis|ISIS]], a claim Badlands treated as chronologically plausible given his time in office.[1]

## The Sunni–Shia contradiction

The Badlands commentary argued that the establishment's simultaneous concern over Al-Maliki's alleged ISIS role and his supposed pro-Iran alignment does not cohere: Iran is the stronghold of [[shia-islam|Shia Islam]] while ISIS is a notoriously [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] outfit, and ISIS was created to overthrow Syrian President Bashar al-Assad, whose key strategic ally has always been Iran.[1] The piece recalled that in ISIS's early days, Barack Obama and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] referred to the group as "rebels" fighting an oppressive regime, and that ISIS fighters used weapons looted from the armory of [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], whose killing Iran condemned and blamed on the West.[1]

Al-Maliki is now described as helping the Trump administration prosecute ISIS criminals even as ISIS mounts an alleged comeback, including operations in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] alongside the [[m23-rebels|M23]] group.[1] Badlands raised the possibility that Al-Maliki and current Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] — himself once a public ISIS leader — both once worked for one side and now work for the other.[1] The commentary also referenced [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s remark that everyone the United States installed in Iraq ended up being ISIS.[1]
