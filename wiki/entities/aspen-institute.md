# Aspen Institute

The Aspen Institute is an American think tank and convening organization described in Badlands Media reporting as a "liberal" institution whose programs intersected with media handling of the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] story ahead of the 2020 election.[1] Its digital policy arm, [[aspen-digital|Aspen Digital]], convened a September 2020 working group of social media executives, journalists, and academics to develop a coverage strategy for a hypothetical "hack and dump" of Hunter Biden's emails — an exercise documented in the [[aspen-institute-hack-and-dump-exercise|Aspen Institute hack-and-dump exercise]].[1]

## Relationship to Antony Blinken

Badlands Media reported that [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] has close ties to the Aspen Institute through membership in the [[aspen-strategy-group|Aspen Strategy Group]], but that the extent of the relationship is unclear because much of the supporting material appears to have been removed from the internet.[1] The principal surviving online link between Blinken and the institute is an Aspen Strategy Group publication titled "American Purpose," which lists Blinken as "Staff Director, U.S. Senate Foreign Relations Committee."[1] The institute's publicly filed Form 990s dating back to 2013 do not list Blinken as a member.[1]

## State Department grant

On September 1, 2021, the [[state-department|State Department]] awarded the Aspen Institute a $10.3 million grant for "Professional and Cultural Exchange Programs – Citizen Exchanges," the first State Department grant to the institute in more than a decade.[1] The only two prior State Department grants, dated September 25 and November 24, 2010, totaled $148,500 and $297,000 respectively, making the 2021 award more than 23 times the combined value of the previous 11 years of grants.[1] The article noted that the award came almost exactly one year after the hack-and-dump exercise and while Blinken led the department.[1]
