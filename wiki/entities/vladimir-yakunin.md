# Vladimir Yakunin

**Vladimir Yakunin** is the former president of Russian Railways and the public advocate of the [[trans-eurasian-belt-development|Trans-Eurasian Belt Development]], a multi-trillion-dollar development policy which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents as the complement to the [[frobisher-bay-domed-city|Frobisher Bay]] plan for northern development.[1]

On March 23, 2015 Yakunin described the programme as necessarily an "inter-state, inter-civilization, project," which "should be an alternative to the current (neo-liberal) model, which has caused a systemic crisis," adding that "the project should be turned into a world 'future zone', and it must be based on leading, not catching, technologies."[1] Ehret notes that since those remarks President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Far East development programme has been unveiled as a "21st century national priority" for Russia.[1]

## Restatement in the Alaska summit essay

Ehret repeated Yakunin's March 23, 2015 formulation in "Trump, Putin, Alaska and the Future of Multipolarity," presenting the [[trans-eurasian-belt-development|Trans-Eurasian Belt Development]] as the multi-trillion-dollar complement to Canada's [[frobisher-bay-domed-city|Frobisher Bay]] plan and noting that Russia's Far East development — encompassing new cities, mining, transport corridors, oil and natural gas — has since become a declared national priority with partners from China, Japan, South Korea, India and other APEC nations.[2]
