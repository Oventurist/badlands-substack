# Aurora, Colorado

Aurora is a city in Colorado that became a national story in late August and early September 2024 over claims that an apartment complex there had been taken over by members of the Venezuelan criminal organisation [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]].[1]

## The 2024 gang-takeover story

Rumours that the complex was being run by Tren de Aragua began circulating after a video went viral showing armed men entering the apartments.[1] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] confirmed to NewsNation that the men seen in the video were Tren de Aragua members.[1]

The City of Aurora, in an X post on August 30, confirmed that there was a "small Tren de Aragua (TdA) presence in Aurora" and said the police department had "been taking it seriously."[1] The city simultaneously pushed back on the scale of the coverage, stating that "there has been a lot of misleading information shared about what is happening in our city. Aurora is a safe community," and that media had "conflated and considerably exaggerated incidents that are isolated to a handful of problem properties alone."[1] The account was reported by USA Today.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the Aurora story as one of two paired information operations running that week, alongside the anti-American protests in Izmir, Turkey.[1] He argued that the viral "roving migrant gangs" in Colorado were being conveniently "traced" back to Venezuela, a narrative he said the mainstream media had seeded just over a month earlier by claiming that [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]'s "illegitimate" victory would flood the United States with Venezuelan criminals.[1] His conclusion was that a primary aim of the [[information-war|Info War]] for readers should be to spot propaganda in real time and thereby prevent public mandate forming behind the latest agenda.[1]

## Edge at Lowry case

Aurora drew national attention in 2024 over incidents at the Edge at Lowry apartments, at East 12th Avenue and Dallas Street.[2] Security-camera video recorded on August 18, 2024 showing armed men entering a resident's apartment went viral and fueled claims that the city had been "taken over" by a Venezuelan gang; President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] campaigned in Aurora that October, telling a crowd, "I will rescue Aurora."[2]

Aurora Police identified [[neifred-zerpa-acosta|Neifred Zerpa-Acosta]] as one of the men in the video and arrested him in December 2024 while responding to a kidnapping report that the police chief also described as torture, in which a wife was pistol-whipped and her husband stabbed in the thigh during a detention lasting more than two hours.[2] An Arapahoe County judge sentenced him to 12 years in prison in September 2025 after he pleaded guilty to using a knife and firearm while committing or attempting kidnapping, aggravated robbery, burglary and conspiracy.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the local NBC affiliate reporting the plea had previously accused Trump of exaggerating the scale of criminal migration into Colorado.[2]

## Trump rally announcement (October 2024)

On Monday, October 7, 2024, the [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign confirmed that the former president would visit Aurora on the following Friday, delivering remarks at a 1 p.m. rally at the Gaylord Rockies Resort & Convention Center.[3] The campaign's announcement called Aurora a "war zone," claiming the city had seen an "influx of violent Venezuelan prison gang members from [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]]."[3]

Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] responded that Trump would find "an incredible city with two straight years of rapidly declining crime that is filled with hard-working people from many diverse backgrounds and small businesses that are driving Colorado's economy forward," adding that Coloradans were united in love of their state.[3] Representative [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]], then representing Colorado's 3rd Congressional District and running in the 4th, said she was "ready and thrilled" to host the former president.[3]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that four different contacts across demographics had sent her the announcement and that she planned to arrive early, recalling that Trump's 2020 Colorado Springs visit drew such crowds that she and her oldest child could not get in.[3] She argued that the local left-leaning press was "gaslighting" residents about migrant crime while stores closed, everyday goods went behind locked glass and car thefts rose, and cited open records published by the Denver Gazette as proof that the government knew about a migrant gang takeover of Aurora apartment buildings and lied about it.[3] The visit, she wrote, came at a pivotal moment for a Colorado establishment "white knuckling its grip on the crumbling narrative."[3]

## Venezuelan gang takeover reports (August 2024)

Video published on August 28, 2024 showed armed men moving through an Aurora apartment complex, prompting city council member Danielle Jurinsky to state publicly that a gang had taken over several complexes in the city.[4] Jurinsky told Fox 31 the building had been overrun by a Venezuelan gang, and reporting linked the activity to [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]], though police declined to confirm the affiliation.[4] Badlands Media cited the episode as evidence that migration policy under the Biden-Harris administration had produced concrete public safety consequences, contrasting local conditions with California's move to extend home loans to illegal immigrants.[4]
