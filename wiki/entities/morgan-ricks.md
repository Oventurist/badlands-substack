# Morgan Ricks

Morgan Ricks is one of the researchers who proposed the [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] concept in 2018 and who previously worked at the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Treasury Department]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media identifies him by name as the author who developed the idea further, linking FedAccounts explicitly to postal banking.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## The FedAccounts proposal

The 2018 proposal, authored with two colleagues and published through the Great Democracy Initiative, would have the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] offer all individuals and businesses in the United States the option of opening an account with the Fed itself, as an alternative to private banks or credit unions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The article stresses that two of the three authors worked in the Treasury and that [[donald-trump|Trump]] was president at the time, suggesting the timing was not coincidental.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Merging FedAccounts with postal banking

In later writing summarized by JustMoney.org, Ricks and co-authors argued that FedAccounts might offer all the functionality of ordinary bank transaction accounts — debit cards, ATM access, direct deposit, online bill payments, and online and mobile phone access — without any fees or minimum-balance requirements.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) They further proposed that the Fed could partner with the [[united-states-postal-service|U.S. Postal Service]] to serve as a ubiquitous physical branch network servicing these accounts, merging FedAccounts with postal banking proposals to create a robust public system for money and payments in which the U.S. money-and-payments system would become fully public infrastructure akin to roads, sidewalks, public libraries and the judicial system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)
