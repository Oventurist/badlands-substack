# Election Infrastructure Information Sharing and Analysis Center

The Election Infrastructure Information Sharing and Analysis Center (EI-ISAC) is one of the information sharing and analysis centers that historically provided free and low-cost cybersecurity tools and threat intelligence to state and local governments and election officials.[1]

## Federal defunding

In late 2025 the federal government ended funding for the ISACs, including the EI-ISAC and the Multi-State Information Sharing and Analysis Center (MS-ISAC), which had supplied resources that smaller election jurisdictions with little cybersecurity budget depended upon.[1] The ISACs announced plans to continue operating, but some services were expected to be reduced and a new fee-based membership model was introduced.[1] Reporting framed the change as a challenge for election officials who must fend off a wide array of cyber threats, some launched by sophisticated actors.[1]

## Badlands criticism

Badlands commentary rejected the framing of the defunding as a loss, describing the reaction as a "hysterical story" and locating the ISACs within a mapped network of NGOs and public-private partners that purport to "instill trust" in elections.[1] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] charged that the EI-ISAC was the entity that decided to hide from the public evidence of massive machine vulnerabilities to spearphishing attacks across the country, even while its members publicly described the election as the safest and most secure in history.[1] The associated critique held that "anyone claiming they can replace a couple cogs in the black box" of election administration and thereby make elections real is protecting fake elections, and that real elections are impossible until the fake ones are honestly acknowledged.[1]
