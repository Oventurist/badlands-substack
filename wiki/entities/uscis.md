# USCIS

United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) is the component of the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] responsible for administering the nation's lawful immigration and naturalization system, and therefore the custodian of the federal records that establish citizenship status.[1]

## Role in citizenship verification for elections

USCIS data is central to the citizenship-verification architecture contemplated by President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s March 2026 executive order [[ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections|"Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections"]], which directs DHS to develop a "state citizenship list."[1] As the plan advanced through interagency talks in May 2026, DHS and USCIS officials said their internal deliberations on the matter were ongoing.[1]

## Voter-roll citizenship checks (2025–2026)

USCIS said that 60 million eligibility checks run through the [[save-program|SAVE program]] identified about 24,000 potential noncitizens, part of an effort in which the Trump administration ran at least 67 million voter registrations through [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] systems.[2] The Associated Press reported that at least 25 states had used SAVE for voter-roll checks since April 2025, and critics warned the process could remove valid voters from the rolls before the November elections.[2]

## Bribery prosecution of an agency employee (2025)

In June 2025 federal prosecutors charged [[amara-dukuly|Amara Dukuly]], a 42-year-old USCIS employee from Brookhaven, Pennsylvania and a naturalized citizen originally from Liberia, with soliciting bribes from immigrants and making false promises about what he could do to help their cases.[3] A criminal complaint unsealed in Philadelphia federal court alleged he accepted $6,000 in cash from a confidential informant posing as a Tajikistani immigrant during an April meeting in a Walmart parking lot in Eddystone, telling the informant he would use the money to pay government connections to make the man's record "clean," then taking no action on the case.[3]

## Revival of neighborhood checks (2025)

In August 2025 a [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] memo first obtained by CBS News, and later confirmed by the department to Newsweek, showed that USCIS would bring [[neighborhood-checks|neighborhood checks]] back into use in the naturalization process — a long-unused procedure for scrutinizing immigrants applying for United States citizenship.[4] Agency Director [[joseph-b-edlow|Joseph B. Edlow]] said that "the Immigration and Nationality Act directs USCIS to conduct personal investigations and incorporating neighborhood investigations will help enhance these statutorily required investigations to ensure that we are meeting congressional intent."[4] The change formed part of a broader tightening of the immigration system under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], which immigration advocates warned put up unnecessary barriers to legal status.[4]
