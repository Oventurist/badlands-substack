# Kaja Kallas

Kaja Kallas is the European Union's foreign policy chief. In January 2026 she stated that the bloc had discussed how it might respond if the United States advanced efforts to acquire [[greenland|Greenland]], describing US messaging on the subject as "extremely concerning."[1]

## Greenland and the transatlantic rift

Kallas's remarks came alongside broader European warnings that US actions under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were straining the postwar international system.[1] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the EU's preparation to confront Washington as an adversary over Greenland as a step toward what he called a diplomatic divorce between the two continents — an outcome he argued would mark the end of the ambitions set out in Agenda 2030.[1]

Separately, an [[european-union|EU Commission]] spokesperson reportedly said the bloc does not recognize the legitimacy of Venezuela's interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], while maintaining "targeted engagement" with Venezuelan authorities.[1]

## Reaction to the 2026 U.S. drawdown

At a summit in Yerevan, Armenia, Kallas said the timing of the U.S. announcement to withdraw thousands of troops from Germany came as a surprise despite prior discussions, while calling for a stronger European pillar in NATO.[2] Badlands commentary treated that formulation as an implicit concession that the prior transatlantic arrangement had never been truly mutual.[2]



## Israel sanctions letter (September 2025)

In September 2025 Kallas, as the [[european-union|EU]]'s foreign policy chief, was the intended recipient of a letter organised by Irish Deputy Prime Minister [[simon-harris|Simon Harris]] and co-signed by member states urging a swift decision on sanctions against [[israel|Israel]] over the Gaza conflict.[3] The letter reportedly stated that member states stood "ready to work with you immediately to overcome the current obstacles and ensure that sufficient pressure is applied to the Israeli government to change its course and meet its obligations according to international law."[3]

## Ukraine peace negotiations (November 2025)

Speaking after an extraordinary meeting of EU foreign ministers in late November 2025, Kallas warned that [[russia|Russia]] must reduce its military power and make serious concessions to achieve a "just and lasting peace" in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[4] "In any peace agreement, we have to put the focus on how to get concessions from the Russian side, that they stop aggression for good and do not try to change borders by force," she said.[4] She added: "We have one aggressor and one victim. The focus should be on what Russia, the aggressor, must do, not on what Ukraine, the victim, must sacrifice."[4]

Her comments followed the circulation by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s administration of a 28-point plan that alarmed Ukraine and its European allies for heavily favoring Moscow.[4] Badlands contributors were unsympathetic: [[michael-flynn|General Michael Flynn]] said he wished "these SOBs would fight for peace as much as they desire war," denouncing warmongers in Europe and the United States, while [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] drew a parallel to neoconservative maneuvering over Israel.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that legacy media predictions of a Thanksgiving peace deal were false and that Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had both signaled peace would arrive only at the "final stages" because the deals were already effectively concluded.[4]
