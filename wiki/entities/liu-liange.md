# Liu Liange

Liu Liange is a former Chinese banking executive who served as chairman of the [[bank-of-china|Bank of China]] for four years until his resignation in March 2023, several weeks before authorities revealed that he was facing corruption charges.[1] He was arrested in October 2023.[1]

## Conviction and sentence

In late November 2024, Chinese state outlet Xinhua News Agency reported that Liu had been sentenced to death with a two-year reprieve — a suspended death sentence — for accepting bribes worth the equivalent of nearly $17 million and for illegally issuing loans.[1] Under the ruling, all of Liu's personal property was to be confiscated, and all illegal gains recovered and turned over to the state treasury.[1]

The two-year reprieve was granted because the accused had cooperated with authorities and shown remorse; the sentence would be carried out only if Liu committed further crimes during the reprieve period.[1] If reprieved, the 63-year-old would instead serve a life sentence.[1]

## Context

Liu's case was described as the latest in a series of high-profile sentences handed down as part of the widespread anti-corruption campaign ordered by Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], targeting the country's roughly $60 trillion financial sector.[1] Former deputy central bank governor [[fan-yifei|Fan Yifei]] had been sentenced to death for bribery in October 2024, also with a two-year reprieve.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted Beijing's willingness to impose capital sentences on corrupt bankers with the perceived impunity of Western financial elites, arguing that such crackdowns help explain why "the Deep State suddenly hates China so much," and framing the pattern as evidence for the claim that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and like-minded world leaders had formed a [[sovereign-alliance|sovereign alliance]] aimed at reclaiming national sovereignty.[1] He also remarked sardonically on the modest size of the $17 million bribe total relative to the scale of the institution involved.[1]
