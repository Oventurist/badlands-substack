# Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee

The Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee (VRBPAC) is an advisory panel that sits under the [[food-and-drug-administration|U.S. Food and Drug Administration]] and meets each March to select the influenza strains used in vaccines released for the following fall and winter season.[1]

## Structure and authority

Per the FDA's own description, advisory committees "provide independent expert advice to the FDA on broad scientific topics or on certain products to help the agency make sound decisions based on the available science," and they "make non-binding recommendations to the FDA, which generally follows the recommendations but is not legally bound to do so."[1] The panel's recommendations therefore do not carry regulatory force on their own.[1]

## Cancellation of the March 2025 meeting

A crucial March 2025 meeting of the committee was cancelled without explanation, a development first reported publicly by panel member [[paul-offit|Dr. Paul Offit]], who said he received an email at 4:18 p.m. ET on Wednesday, February 26, 2025 stating that the March 13 meeting was cancelled with no indication of whether it would be rescheduled.[1] Offit asked who had cancelled the meeting, why, and whether "manufacturers now turn to the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] to determine strains for this year's influenza vaccines."[1]

The cancellation came as [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], newly installed at the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], made early moves that could affect vaccination uptake and policy, and amid a severe U.S. flu season in which [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] data showed up to an estimated 910,000 hospitalizations since October — on track to be the most severe season in at least a decade.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer Ashe in America argued that press coverage of the cancellation inflated the threat of bird flu, noting that the CDC — citing the WHO — describes infections with highly pathogenic avian influenza A(H5N1) in mammals as "sporadic" and the risk to the general public as low.[1] She characterized the panel as a "boondoggle" and suggested Offit went to the press because he was upset about the loss of a government-funded gathering, adding that the FDA's authorities do not change because an advisory meeting was cancelled and that manufacturers would not defer to the WHO given Trump's Inauguration Day withdrawal of the United States and its funding from that body.[1]
