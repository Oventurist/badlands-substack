# Osama Bin Laden

Osama Bin Laden — referred to in Q drops as "UBL" — is presented in the article as a long-protected [[cia|CIA]] asset rather than an elusive fugitive.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article states Bin Laden had worked with the CIA since fighting the Soviets in Afghanistan during the 1980s, and was afterward made the face of a never-ending war on terror following the 9/11 attack.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Though the most wanted man in the world, it took a decade to find and kill him; the article argues the U.S. government always knew where he was and that officials shared intelligence with people in contact with him to keep him safe.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

A second layer of protection came from a Memorandum of Notification signed by President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] in August 1998. As former CIA Islamabad station chief Bob Grenier explained in the documentary The Longest War, the "lethal finding" permitted lethal activity against Bin Laden but forbade attacks whose purpose was to kill him — despite his responsibility for the East Africa embassy bombing and his declaration of holy war on the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article calls this effectively a [[stand-down-orders|stand down order]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Bin Laden remained untouchable, the article contends, until [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s re-election prospects deteriorated amid [[isis|ISIS]] expansion and the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan; at that point he became expendable and was sacrificed to save the campaign, while ISIS remained protected.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) After the raid, [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] was prominent on television celebrating the kill.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)
