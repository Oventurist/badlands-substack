# Robert Kurlander

Robert Kurlander is an American man who, together with [[aimee-harris|Aimee Harris]], was criminally charged in connection with the 2020 theft and sale of a diary and other personal property belonging to [[ashley-biden|Ashley Biden]], the daughter of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Harris enlisted Kurlander to help her monetize the materials she had found while temporarily staying at a Delray Beach, Florida residence where Ashley Biden had been permitted to leave personal belongings.[1]

## Role in the diary case

Harris discovered Ashley Biden's personal items — described in later reporting as including "highly personal entries," tax records, a cellphone, family photographs, books, clothing, luggage and financial documents — roughly two days after Ashley Biden departed the property in June 2020.[1] Two months later Harris contacted Kurlander for assistance in selling the items, and he agreed.[1]

According to the account published by Badlands writer [[just-human|Just Human]], the pair first attempted to sell the diary to the campaign of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. The campaign declined the offer and advised them to take the material to the Federal Bureau of Investigation.[1] Instead, Harris and Kurlander sold the diary and associated materials to [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]], which paid each of them $20,000; the two travelled back to Florida to retrieve the remaining property.[1]

## Prosecution

Harris and Kurlander were indicted in August 2022 for conspiracy to commit interstate transportation of stolen property, and both pleaded guilty almost immediately after the indictment was unsealed.[1] Kurlander had not been sentenced as of April 2024, when Harris received one month in prison and a term of supervised release; his sentencing was scheduled for October 25.[1] Nicholas Biase, chief of public affairs for the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York, confirmed the status of the case to reporters.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary on the case treated the entire episode as a possible entrapment operation aimed at the Trump campaign, arguing that Project Veritas at the time functioned as an FBI asset and that the diary — which Project Veritas said it could not authenticate and never published — may have contained forged material intended to lure the campaign into disseminating false claims about Joe Biden.[1] The subsequent FBI raid on [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]] and other Project Veritas journalists, and the appointment of Barbara S. Jones as special master to review the seized materials, followed from the same chain of events; no Project Veritas figure was ever charged.[1]
