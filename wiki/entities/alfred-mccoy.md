# Alfred W. McCoy

**Alfred W. McCoy** is a historian whose research on the relationship between United States intelligence operations and the global narcotics trade is central to the case laid out by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]].[1]

McCoy has argued that during the Vietnam War the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s airline, [[air-america|Air America]], became a conduit for moving opium and heroin precursors out of Southeast Asia, with the profits helping to sustain anti-communist operations.[1] His book *The Politics of Heroin* documents on page 318 that [[usaid|USAID]] funded the airline of the Laotian warlord [[vang-pao|Vang Pao]], purportedly in the knowledge that it would be used to transport opium.[1] The passage quoted in the article records the claim that Vang Pao later used a DC-3 to fly heroin across southern Laos into central Vietnam, and that USAID officials "apparently realized that any commercial activity at Long Tieng would involve opium, but decided to support the project anyway."[1]

*The Politics of Heroin* is listed first among the article's further-reading sources.[1]

## On BCCI and Pakistani heroin money

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" quotes McCoy, via WhoWhatWhy, on the financial scale of the Afghan-Pakistani drug trade: the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] "played a critical role in facilitating the movement of Pakistani heroin money that reached $4 billion by 1989, more than the country's legal exports."[2]

His research also underpins the article's account of how [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] operations expanded opium output — from about 80 tons a year in Burma when the agency's airline CAT began covert flights in the 1950s, to as much as 1,200 tons a year from the [[golden-triangle|Golden Triangle]] during the Vietnam War, and from 200 metric tons in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] in 1980 to 1,980 metric tons in 1991.[2]
