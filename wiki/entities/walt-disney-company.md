# The Walt Disney Company

The Walt Disney Company is a major American media and entertainment conglomerate whose output includes a large volume of children's programming. It appeared in March 2024 coverage of abuse in children's television through the case of dialogue coach [[brian-peck|Brian Peck]], who was hired by Disney after being convicted as a child molester in connection with his work in the [[nickelodeon|Nickelodeon]] orbit.[1]

Badlands Media writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] cited Disney's hiring of Peck as evidence that convicted pedophiles gravitate toward such companies because they function as safe havens, and argued that the major children's-entertainment firms are sufficiently saturated with offenders that a predator subculture persists beneath the surface of the industry.[1]

## 2024 proxy fight and shareholder vote

On Wednesday, April 3, 2024, Disney chief executive [[bob-iger|Bob Iger]] fended off an aggressive challenge from activist investors seeking to redirect the company, averting what reporting described as a stunning embarrassment for one of Hollywood's leading executives.[2] Billionaire activist investor [[nelson-peltz|Nelson Peltz]] had loudly pushed the entertainment conglomerate to produce a concrete succession plan and derided its efforts to make more diverse movies and shows, but the campaign fell short when shareholders approved the board slate backed by current leadership, denying seats to Peltz and his ally [[jay-rasulo|Jay Rasulo]], a former Disney chief financial officer.[2] The vote came at a key juncture for a company positioned at the center of wider American culture wars while navigating the rise of streaming, the decline of traditional television and growing competition from social media.[2]

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] had been speculating on the net effects a Peltz addition to the board would produce, framing it in terms of the difference between the controlled demolition and the resuscitation of corrupted American institutions.[2] After the defeat he observed that Disney remained in the hands of Iger and a consortium of interests backed by [[vanguard-group|Vanguard Group]], [[blackrock|BlackRock Inc.]], T. Rowe Price, Norges Bank Investment Management and ValueAct Capital Management, noted that the share price was falling further on the news after recent optimism about a common-sense approach to the company's entertainment initiatives, and concluded that "the Controlled Demolition continues unabated."[2]

## DeSantis escalation (April 2023)

In April 2023, Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] promised a new round of measures against Disney in the [[disney-v-desantis|escalating dispute]], including scrutiny of taxes on the company's hotels and tolls on roads serving its theme parks.[3] His administration examined whether an agreement approved between a Central Florida board previously controlled by Disney and the company violated state growth laws.[3] DeSantis accused Disney of having "tried to pull a fast one on the way out the door" and, speaking at [[hillsdale-college|Hillsdale College]], declared that the company is "not superior to the people of Florida."[3] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the confrontation as a front in the [[information-war|Information War]], noting Hollywood's long-standing influence over American myth-making.[3]

## Streaming content write-off (June 2023)

In June 2023 Disney disclosed that it would take a $1.5 billion impairment charge in its June quarter after removing dozens of shows and films from Disney+ and Hulu, in a move detailed in [[2023-disney-streaming-content-write-off|an SEC filing]] describing the removal of "certain produced content" from its direct-to-consumer services on May 26, 2023.[4] The company said it anticipated removing additional produced content during the remainder of its third fiscal quarter, with possible further impairment charges of up to roughly $400 million.[4] Chief financial officer [[christine-mccarthy|Christine McCarthy]] had previously guided to a write-down of $1.5 billion to $1.8 billion; writing down the assets allowed Disney to remove them from its balance sheet and reduce its tax bill.[4] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the purge as targeting the company's most ideologically charged titles amid heavy streaming and theatrical losses and pending Florida litigation, grouping Disney with [[bud-light|Bud Light]] and [[target|Target]] in a widening consumer backlash.[4]
