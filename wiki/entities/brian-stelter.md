# Brian Stelter

Brian Stelter is an American media commentator who hosted a program on [[cnn|CNN]] before his departure from the network, and who is treated in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as an emblem of establishment "fake news."[1]

## WEF disinformation panel (January 2023)

Stelter led a panel discussion on "Disinformation" at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s January 2023 Davos meeting, an assignment The Gateway Pundit described as absurd given his record.[1] The outlet wrote that Stelter had been "the king of 'Fake News'" at CNN and a laughing stock among those on the right who knew the facts he was constantly trying to label disinformation, whether on the Mueller investigation or COVID.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that Stelter's selection to moderate a disinformation panel was further proof "we are living in the age of the clown," calling it impressive for someone who had spent the majority of his career spreading misinformation and obscuring the truth on COVID, the Mueller investigation and [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s emails.[1] DeLarme concluded that among fired CNN personnel, "nobody manages to be a more contemptible potato head than Brian Stelter."[1]

## 2024 Return to CNN as Chief Media Analyst

Two years after being let go amid [[cnn|CNN]]'s reconstruction phase, Brian Stelter announced on Tuesday, September 3, 2024 that he would return to his home network in a new role as "Chief Media Analyst," appearing on television, developing digital content, and once again helming the *Reliable Sources* newsletter.[2] In a thread announcing the move, Stelter described a change in his media perspective and vowed to be "different," saying that he had always scoffed at people who said getting fired was the best thing that ever happened to them until it happened to him.[2] He recounted tuning out the news for a time, spending time as a stay-at-home dad, and trying on "a variety of other hats: Harvard fellow, BBC talking head, magazine writer," writing for more than twenty outlets and learning about freelancing, the attention economy and the information ecosystem.[2]

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that in going out into the world to find himself, Stelter discovered that he was in fact CNN all along — appropriate, she argued, given that CNN sits consistently at the bottom of the prime-time cable news ratings.[2] She questioned the strategic aims of a network that had laid off talent and undergone a corporate restructuring only to rehire some of the worst performers and double down on a failed strategy.[2]
