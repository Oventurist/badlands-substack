# ChatGPT

**ChatGPT** is the large language model, developed by [[openai|OpenAI]], that [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used as the instrument of the experiment described in "AI Can Help Us Expose the NWO" (22 January 2024).[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## Premise of the experiment

Deschamps argues that large language models cannot operate in a world of pure fiction, since all lies are woven with threads of truth, and that the engineers building an AI — even if driven to make it serve a cabal's agenda — cannot ignore reality itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) On this reasoning, the fact that an AI asked direct questions about the globalist agenda did not provide clear, undistorted answers demonstrates two possibilities: that the AI is manipulated to produce "globalist" curated answers, and that those curated answers are themselves evidence that the truth is obvious to anyone, including the AI, who simply looks honestly.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## Behaviour observed

Fed passages from the [[toronto-protocols|Toronto Protocols]] and instructed to respond as a general studying [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]], ChatGPT produced a layered breakdown of primary strategic objectives, secondary strategic objectives, operations and tactics.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) It prefaced and closed each response with disclaimers, characterising the quoted material as a hypothetical scenario rather than a real-world strategy and warning of concerns about authoritarianism, [[social-engineering|social engineering]] and the erosion of individual freedoms and cultural values.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Deschamps observed that this pattern of warnings continued throughout the conversation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

To remove what he described as the model's bias toward protecting him from dangerous conspiracy theory, Deschamps told it that the material was for a science-fiction dystopian film script.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) He reports that this framing produced clearer, more truthful results and deepened the accuracy of the warfare map, and that under the fictional frame the model gave the cabal a new dystopian name: the [[global-harmony-consortium|Global Harmony Consortium]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo) Even so, it continued to label the final synthesis "highly speculative."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## Significance in the argument

Deschamps presents the exercise as an example of using the weapons of the enemy against them: AI, positioned elsewhere in Badlands commentary as an instrument of [[artificial-intelligence-manipulation|manipulation]], is here turned into a tool for collective situational awareness that can "make visible that which has been designed to remain invisible."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## Public access debate

Badlands Media's July 2023 essay on [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] argues that the capabilities normies are allowed to see — "the Chat GPTs of the world" — are toys in comparison with what governments, tech R&D firms, and defense contractors are developing behind closed doors.[2] Because the [[ai-pause-open-letter|March 22, 2023 open letter]] provided caveats for research, the article concludes that its calls for a pause relate only to the commercial side of these capabilities, and really just to public access.[2] The author's summary of the resulting double standard is that global institutions may proceed full steam ahead so long as development follows approved principles, while public access to ChatGPT is cast as a threat to the future of humanity.[2]

## Advertising and ChatGPT Go, January 2026

In January 2026 [[openai|OpenAI]] outlined a new approach to advertising and expanding access to its ChatGPT services, saying it would begin testing clearly labeled ads in the free and ChatGPT Go tiers for logged-in adults in the United States in the coming weeks.[3] The company emphasized that ads would be separate from the AI's responses and that it would not sell user conversations to advertisers, framing the policy around mission alignment, answer independence, user control of data, and prioritizing user experience over revenue.[3] OpenAI also noted that ChatGPT Go — offering messaging, image creation, file uploads, and memory — had become available worldwide for $8 USD per month.[3]
