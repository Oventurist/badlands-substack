# NewsGuard

NewsGuard is a company that tracks and rates online sources for what it characterizes as misinformation.[1]

## Role in the AI-content narrative

In January 2024, [[washington-post|Washington Post]] coverage of AI-generated content quoted NewsGuard analyst McKenzie Sadeghi, who observed that "because a lot of these sites are operating with little to no human oversight, these messages are directly published on the site before they're caught by a human." The remark accompanied a report on the proliferation of AI error messages — such as refusals citing [[openai|OpenAI]]'s use-case policy — appearing verbatim in product listings, social posts and blog articles.[1]

Badlands commentary treated NewsGuard's participation as part of a problem–reaction–solution sequence in which the identification of AI-driven spam is used to justify expanded content moderation, human review regimes and platform restrictions administered by the same institutional actors who defined the problem. In that reading, NewsGuard functions as a node of the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]] rather than as a neutral arbiter.[1]

## Consortium News lawsuit (2023)

NewsGuard describes itself as rating media outlets' level of "trustworthiness," a function that gives it meaningful influence over the advertising revenue available to rated sites.[2] In October 2023 the company was sued together with the Biden administration by [[consortium-news|Consortium News]], whose complaint also named the Pentagon's [[us-cyber-command|Cyber Command]] for contracting with NewsGuard to identify, report and abridge the speech of American media organizations dissenting from official U.S. foreign policy positions.[2] The complaint alleged that NewsGuard was "acting jointly or in concert with the United States to coerce news organizations to alter viewpoints" on Ukraine, Russia and Syria, imposing a form of "censorship and repression of views" at odds with U.S. and allied policy.[2]

Consortium News noted that NewsGuard had branded the entirety of its more than 20,000 articles as unreliable on the basis of just six disputed examples, a methodology the outlet said had been applied identically to [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and other independent media.[2] Separately, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] called NewsGuard a "scam" after it issued commentator [[tim-pool|Tim Pool]] a strike over five stories out of nearly five thousand that quoted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2]

## Badlands analysis of the ratings-advertising mechanism

Badlands contributor Justin Deschamps argued that the NewsGuard case exposes a collusive link between an NGO and the advertising servers — of which Google is the largest — that determine which outlets can monetize traffic.[2] Because advertisers primarily value eyeballs and attention, he reasoned, their willingness to avoid popular sites flagged by NewsGuard runs against their own commercial interest and therefore suggests that the ratings system's real purpose is to demonetize outlets that depart from the mainstream script.[2] He characterized the arrangement as conduct that would once have been treated as antitrust violation but is now normalized under ESG-style policy frameworks, amounting to a provable conspiracy to suppress free speech rather than the suspicions and anecdotes alternative media previously relied upon.[2]

Contributor Burning Bright added that NewsGuard had been candid about its own nature from the start — guardians of the media rather than of truth, veracity or journalistic integrity — and situated the firm within a modern Media Industrial Complex in which new forms of information control replaced the older newspaper-and-television gatekeepers.[2]

## Alleged link to the Global Engagement Center

In December 2024, following the shutdown of the State Department's [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]], Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reported that the GEC is connected to NewsGuard, which he described as the censorship organization that had sent a threatening letter to [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] over Badlands Media content.[3]

## FCC Scrutiny Under Brendan Carr (2024)

In letters dated November 13, 2024, incoming [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] chairman [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] asked Google, Microsoft, Meta and Apple to disclose which of their products or services partnered with NewsGuard and whether customers were required to rely on it while using those services, with responses due by December 10.[4] Carr described NewsGuard as "Orwellian named" and said it, along with other fact-checking groups and ad agencies, had helped enforce one-sided narratives as part of a "censorship cartel."[4] The firm had already drawn criticism from congressional Republicans for allegedly labeling conservative outlets as more "risky" than liberal ones.[4] Badlands Media, which said it had itself been threatened by the organization, welcomed the scrutiny and credited [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] with sustained public attention to the firm.[4]

## House Oversight Committee investigation (June 2024)

On Thursday, June 13, 2024, [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] Chair [[james-comer|James Comer]] launched an investigation into NewsGuard, citing concerns about "protected First Amendment speech" and "censorship campaigns."[5] [[axios|Axios]] described NewsGuard as a web extension that uses trained journalists to monitor and rate news and information sites' trustworthiness, assessing sites for basic practices of credibility and transparency and rating publishers from 0 to 100 across nine criteria, and noted that it had flagged issues with both conservative- and progressive-leaning websites.[5] Announcing the probe, Comer said "questions now surround the influence of NewsGuard's business relationships and other influences on its ratings process."[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode underscores why there cannot be a government-run "disinformation governance board," asking who decides what counts as misinformation and what is true, and concluding that after years of debunked government narratives the answer cannot be the government.[5] She added that in the age of public-private partnerships, private and nongovernmental entities routinely act as arms of the government, especially on misinformation, and questioned the value of pursuing every entity in what she called the RICO Regime one at a time — noting that the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] had just refused to act on the congressional contempt referral against Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]].[5]
