# Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia is the oil-producing kingdom at the center of the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] arrangement established in the early 1970s, under which oil could be sold internationally only in US dollars.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## End of the petrodollar and media coverage

Badlands Media commentary observes that very shortly after the fifty-year petrodollar agreement ended, mainstream media outlets began attempting to tie Saudi Arabia to the 9/11 attacks.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] presents this as a recurring pattern of pressure: when the Saudi royals grew close to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] a few years earlier, media reporting focused on the Saudis' murder and dismemberment of a journalist.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) Carlson contends the coverage omitted that the journalist was the nephew of possibly the largest arms dealer the world has known, and that he was a [[cia|CIA]] asset — remarking that "it's funny the details they tend to leave out."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

In Badlands Media's Benghazi reporting, Saudi Arabia is named as one of the states paying for the covert supply of weapons to the Syrian opposition.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Citing the World Socialist Web Site's summary of New York Times reporting from June 2012, the article states that CIA operatives in [[turkey|Turkey]] directed a smuggling operation moving automatic rifles, rocket-propelled grenades, ammunition and antitank weapons across the Turkish border through a shadowy network of intermediaries including the [[muslim-brotherhood|Syrian Muslim Brotherhood]], "paid for by Qatar, Saudi Arabia and Turkey."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article groups Saudi Arabia with Libya, [[qatar|Qatar]] and Turkey as the states helping the Obama administration smuggle weapons to what it characterises as America's enemies.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## The Iran question

In [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s account of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]], Saudi Arabia is the benchmark against which a sanctions-freed [[iran|Iran]] is measured: the fear was that a thriving Iran on the level of Saudi Arabia would dramatically alter Middle Eastern dynamics and empower Iranian proxies against [[israel|Israel]].[3] The Saudi–Israeli coalition born of the Obama [[state-department|State Department]]'s Iranian power play is described as one of the major catalysts that enabled [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to rise atop the Republican Party against the Clinton machine, with the kingdom among the parties financially and clandestinely supporting the populist coalition.[3] DeLarme also notes that under the $400 billion agreement China would invest in Iranian infrastructure "in much the same way that the U.S. did in Saudi Arabia," and lists Saudi Arabia among the powers with a vested interest in how the Iran question resolves.[3]

## Arabia, tribe, and dynasty

The kingdom occupies the region the article identifies as Arabia — the natural geographic axis of the entire Middle East, defined by reference to the 1916 Arab Revolt leaders and the modern [[arab-league|Arab League]].[4] Saudi society, like Arabian society generally, is organized through a tribal system built on bloodlines, with the nuclear family as the base unit; roughly 2 million of the world's 25 million [[bedouin|Bedouin]] remain in the kingdom, whose total population exceeds 32 million.[4] High-born Saudi boys have traditionally been sent to live with allied Bedouin tribes on coming of age, and the most elite Saudi military units recruit directly from Bedouin tribes, organized by tribal affiliation.[4] Saudi Arabia is also the only Arab state still using the traditional articles such as "Al" and "al-" in its names.[4]

The ruling [[house-of-saud|House of Saud]] traces to the [[banu-bakr|Banu Bakr]] clan of the [[najd|Najd]], and the modern state descends from the [[emirate-of-diriyah|First State of Saud]] founded through the 1744 [[pact-of-diriyah|Pact of Diriyah]] between [[mohammed-bin-saud|Mohammed bin Saud]] and [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab]].[4] Because al-Wahhab objected to taxation in that pact, no tax is imposed or collected by the Saudi government to this day.[4] The kingdom is considered the leader of the Islamic world and the home of [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] Islam along with Jordan and Syria, though its rulers are neither sharifs nor Banu Hashim.[4]

## The MBS era

In 2017 the kingdom was the setting for the [[sword-dance|Sword Dance]] with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and, months later, the [[ritz-carlton-purge|Ritz-Carlton Purge]] in which newly coronated Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] detained some forty relatives on charges of corruption and treason amid reports of martial law.[4] The article argues that MBS is on a redemption arc to liberate the Middle East, and rejects the view that his moves were directed by Trump.[4]

## Alleged nuclear capability (2026)

In February 2026, former [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] commander [[hussein-kanani|Hussein Kanani]] claimed in an RT interview that Saudi Arabia currently possesses a nuclear bomb and related capabilities, asserting that the United States was fully aware and that both Israel and the U.S. had been informed.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded that there is no real evidence for the claim and that nobody in the West is actually worried about a Saudi arsenal, particularly with Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] in charge — describing him as a benevolent strongman who has moved against corrupt relatives and pulled Saudi culture away from radical Islam.[5]

The commentary stressed that Saudi Arabia has signed all of the international treaties required to possess or refine nuclear materials, whereas Israel has not despite allegedly holding hundreds of warheads.[5] It further noted that Saudi Arabia had argued against kinetic action toward [[iran|Iran]], making Iranian accusations against Riyadh strategically odd, and speculated that the claim was "narrative bait" intended to lure [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s Security Cabinet into a public attack on the kingdom that could boomerang against Israel.[5]

## Civilian nuclear cooperation agreement (2026)

In July 2026, CNN reported that the Trump administration had tentatively agreed to a draft civilian nuclear cooperation agreement permitting Saudi Arabia to enrich uranium in support of its civilian nuclear program.[6] The [[us-saudi-civilian-nuclear-cooperation-agreement|draft agreement]] remained unsigned by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] despite negotiations concluding in October 2025, and had not been submitted to [[congress|Congress]] for post-signature review.[6] The draft would not require the kingdom to adopt the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] Additional Protocol, and critics said it omitted the U.S. "gold standard" commitment against domestic enrichment and reprocessing.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the outcome had been expected for years, recalling that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] told Bret Baier he wanted nuclear weapons as well as nuclear power, and that Trump promised in Riyadh and again at a White House dinner to help Saudi Arabia become a nuclear power.[6] The same commentary identified Defense Minister [[khalid-bin-salman|Khalid bin Salman]], MBS's younger brother, in a photograph of Trump's box at the [[2026-fifa-world-cup|2026 FIFA World Cup]] final.[6]

## US–Saudi civil nuclear agreement (2026)

In July 2026 the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration prepared to submit to [[congress|Congress]] a [[123-agreement|123 Agreement]] authorizing U.S. cooperation with Saudi Arabia on civil nuclear technology, expected to be signed by Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] and Saudi Energy Minister [[abdulaziz-bin-salman|Abdulaziz bin Salman]] after a preliminary agreement reached in Riyadh the previous year.[7] The pact omits the [[gold-standard-nonproliferation|Gold Standard]] barring enrichment and reprocessing, and omits the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] Additional Protocol on snap inspections, while creating a legal pathway for fuel-cycle cooperation without obligating the United States to transfer the technology.[7]

Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] has said the kingdom would seek nuclear weapons if [[iran|Iran]] obtained them; the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] by contrast accepted the Gold Standard in its own 2009 agreement.[7] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that if Congress blocks American contractors, Riyadh will simply bring in South Korean or Japanese firms — one way or another the Saudis will get a nuclear energy program.[7]

## Iran non-aggression pact proposal (May 2026)

In May 2026 Saudi Arabia floated the idea of a Middle East non-aggression pact with [[iran|Iran]] as part of postwar planning discussions with allies to follow the end of the US-Israeli conflict with Tehran, according to Financial Times reporting.[8] Diplomats said Riyadh pointed to the 1970s [[helsinki-accords|Helsinki Accords]], which eased Cold War tensions in Europe, as a possible framework for reducing regional conflict and expanding economic cooperation.[8]

Arab diplomats said most Muslim and Arab nations would likely support the concept, though including [[israel|Israel]] remained a major obstacle as regional anger over Israeli military operations in Gaza, [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and [[syria|Syria]] continued to grow.[8] Saudi Arabia was also reported to be strengthening coordination with [[pakistan|Pakistan]], [[turkey|Turkey]] and Egypt, while Pakistan's defense minister said Islamabad wants [[qatar|Qatar]] and Turkey added to the [[saudi-pakistan-defense-pact|Saudi-Pakistan defense pact]] to build a broader regional economic and military alliance aimed at reducing dependence on outside powers.[8]

## Badlands historical framing

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the proposal to rebut what he described as months of gaslighting that Saudi Arabia was secretly supporting or conducting warfare against Iran, and years of claims that the kingdom is subservient to Israel and that [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] is a Zionist puppet.[8] In his account, Saudi Arabia began serving the [[deep-state|Deep State]] in 1975 when King Faisal was assassinated, if not sooner, and for decades many Saudi royals were in bed with the [[cia|CIA]], funding operations and collaborating on projects — a relationship said to have reached its apex under the George W. Bush administration.[8]

The dynamic is described as changing during the Obama years, with the 2011 appointment of longtime Riyadh governor [[king-salman|Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud]] as Crown Prince by his brother King Abdullah beginning the sequence that led to the present.[8] Salman became King in January 2015 and appointed his son Mohammed as Deputy Crown Prince and Defense Minister, who promptly dismantled and rebuilt the government on paper, standing up a new government within ten days.[8] Mohammed was elevated to Crown Prince in June 2017, one month after President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s first visit to Riyadh; in November, Salman named him acting sovereign, and Mohammed immediately arrested the corrupt relatives said to be working with the CIA and by extension Mossad.[8]

The commentary concedes the crown prince's animus toward Iran but argues he would not pursue a campaign that destroyed the region, citing his record as a reformer who expelled radical clerics and effectively ended Wahhabism as a state institution, and his eleven-year pursuit of [[vision-2030|Vision 2030]].[8] The Deep State is said to target Saudi Arabia precisely because of its role in uniting the Muslim world behind a vision of economic prosperity rather than a destructive caliphate, an ambition that required a security architecture assembled from countries that are not historic allies.[8]

Saudi Arabia condemned the 17 May 2026 drone attack near the [[barakah-nuclear-power-plant|Barakah nuclear power plant]] in the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] and reported intercepting three drones approaching from Iraqi airspace in a separate incident, amid elevated regional tensions during a fragile ceasefire tied to the broader [[iran-war|Iran conflict]].[10]

## February 2026 arms package and bridge-state role

In early February 2026 the US [[state-department|State Department]] approved approximately $9 billion in Foreign Military Sales to Saudi Arabia, primarily Patriot Advanced Capability-3 (PAC-3) missile systems and roughly 730 interceptors with related support equipment, as part of a $15.7 billion package that also covered [[israel|Israel]].[15] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the kingdom as "perhaps the premier bridge player in the Middle East theater," primed to fill a regional void as peacemaker and stabilizer while maintaining warm, functional ties with both [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]] and anchoring the expansion of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]].[15] In that reading the simultaneous arming of Israel and Riyadh is not a contradiction but a means of strengthening both while weakening the prospect of war.[15] Saudi officials were also reported meeting in [[turkey|Turkey]] alongside Iranian, Turkish, and Qatari counterparts during the [[2026-witkoff-abu-dhabi-and-istanbul-talks|February 2026 Witkoff diplomatic round]].[15]

## Reported rift with Washington over the Iran war (July 2026)

Reports in *[[new-york-times|The New York Times]]* and the *[[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]]* in July 2026 said disagreements over the [[iran|Iran]] war had created friction between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[16] Unnamed officials cited by the reports said Saudi Arabia opposed aspects of US military strategy because of concerns about Iranian retaliation, prompting the Trump administration to consider repositioning some US forces stationed in the kingdom; both governments nonetheless continued to publicly describe the relationship as an important strategic partnership.[16]

## Guest of honor at SPIEF 2026

Saudi Arabia served as the official guest country at the [[2026-st-petersburg-international-economic-forum|2026 St. Petersburg International Economic Forum]], sending a high-level delegation that included Energy Minister Prince [[abdulaziz-bin-salman|Abdulaziz bin Salman Al Saud]] along with officials from sovereign wealth funds and major state enterprises.[17] On the sidelines the two sides signed roughly 30 agreements covering energy, investment, education, tourism and humanitarian cooperation, with Russian firms exploring petrochemicals, hydrogen, digital technologies and nuclear energy and Saudi officials seeking Russian expertise in IT, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and agriculture.[17]

The deepening ties reflect shared priorities on sovereignty and a multipolar order, alongside the kingdom's pragmatic engagement with [[brics|BRICS]] structures while maintaining flexibility on regional diplomacy.[17] Badlands commentary noted that [[russia|Russia]] and Saudi Arabia had cooperated to stabilize energy markets in 2020 and that Russia was the first nation to recognize the newly formed kingdom in 1926, exactly a century earlier, casting [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s choice of guest as deliberate and framing Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] as a generational figure whose [[vision-2030|Vision]] could transform the Middle East into a first-world society.[17]

## Abqaiq drone strike and joint operations with CENTCOM (July 2026)

On 28 July 2026, Saudi Arabian forces joined [[us-central-command|U.S. Central Command]] in precision airstrikes against multiple logistics and weapons sites in eastern [[iraq|Iraq]] used by [[iran|Iran]]-aligned militants, an operation CENTCOM said responded to more than 30 [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]]-directed drone attacks over the preceding 72 hours targeting U.S. forces and Saudi energy infrastructure.[18]

Separately, a drone strike hit the kingdom's Abqaiq oil processing complex — the world's largest crude oil stabilization facility, handling roughly 5% of global supply.[18] Yemen's Houthis claimed responsibility, describing strikes on oil transport targets as retaliation for renewed Saudi operations against them, while Saudi officials instead blamed Iran-backed militias operating from Iraq and reported only minor damage.[18] Iraq's National Security Adviser [[qasim-al-aboudi|Qasim al-Aboudi]] subsequently stated that arrested militants had confessed to acting on behalf of [[ukraine|Ukraine]], complicating the attribution.[18]

Saudi Arabia was also credited by Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmad al-Sharaa]], alongside Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]], with mediating the previous year to persuade [[donald-trump|President Trump]] to lift most U.S. sanctions on [[syria|Syria]].[18] A Saudi nuclear deal lacking explicit Israeli normalization language was reported as a point of friction between Trump and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[18]

## Lifting of restrictions on U.S. operations (May 2026)

In May 2026, Saudi Arabia and [[kuwait|Kuwait]] lifted restrictions on the U.S. military's use of their bases and airspace that had been imposed after Washington launched operations tied to reopening the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], according to the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]].[19] The reversal removed a major obstacle to President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s effort to secure commercial shipping and cleared the way for the administration to seek a restart of [[project-freedom|Project Freedom]].[19] Officials indicated that Riyadh's earlier suspension of access reflected support for [[pakistan|Pakistan]]'s diplomatic push to broker negotiations between [[iran|Iran]] and the United States.[19] A Saudi source told [[nbc-news|NBC News]] that Trump and Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] "have been in touch regularly," with Saudi officials also coordinating with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], [[centcom|U.S. Central Command]], and Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]].[19]

Badlands analyst [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the episode established Saudi Arabia as a recognized regional heavyweight despite lacking the military mass of Pakistan, [[egypt|Egypt]], Iran, or [[turkey|Turkey]], noting the kingdom's focus on special operations forces, ballistic missiles, and aircraft and drones.[19] He anticipated Pakistan facilitating a major meeting between the crown prince and Iranian leadership to end their rivalry, framing it as part of an emerging regional security architecture built on mutual cooperation rather than a centralized caliphate.[19]

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States was prepared to move forward with a [[us-saudi-civilian-nuclear-cooperation-agreement|civilian nuclear cooperation agreement]] with the kingdom only if it eventually joined the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and normalized relations with [[israel|Israel]], linking two tracks previously discussed separately.[20] Saudi officials responded by reiterating that normalization remains contingent on meaningful progress toward a Palestinian state and an end to the conflict in Gaza, and said Riyadh's conditions had not changed.[20]

A Saudi source pushed back on the linkage, saying the nuclear framework "was signed. So nothing to renegotiate" and that "tweets don't overturn signed agreements," while the Saudi Energy Ministry issued its own statement.[20] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said the kingdom does not need American participation to build reactors, noting it has already received proposals from South Korean contractor KEPCO and a Japanese company and intends to build two plants regardless, with US firms merely its preferred partner.[20]

## Iraqi pipeline partnership (April 2026)

In April 2026 [[iraq|Iraq]] sought to revive the long-idled [[iraq-saudi-pipeline|Iraq–Saudi Pipeline (IPSA)]] running through Saudi territory to the Red Sea, a route that would allow Iraqi crude to bypass the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[21] The pipeline had been inactive for decades, largely because of political tensions following the Gulf War and strained Iraq–Saudi relations.[21] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry framed the revival as a redemption arc for the Saudis, recalling allegations of Saudi involvement in the funding and proliferation of Al Qaeda and later ISIS, and noting claims that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] backed the rise of the current Syrian president.[21]

## Syrian reconstruction investments (February 2026)

On February 7, 2026, Saudi Arabia and [[syria|Syria]] signed a series of multibillion-dollar investment agreements intended to revitalize the Syrian economy after years of civil war, including nearly $1 billion in telecommunications infrastructure under the [[silklink-project|SilkLink project]], a new Saudi-Syrian low-cost airline, and a $2 billion commitment to redevelop two airports in Aleppo through a Saudi-backed investment fund.[22] Additional accords covered energy, real estate and water desalination cooperation, deepening ties with Syria's interim leadership after the lifting of Western sanctions.[22]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the Saudis, working with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the Turks, were using their massive wealth to pull Syria out of the pit into which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had cast it, noting they had previously offered to pay off Syria's debts.[22] He argued that stabilizing Syria is a necessary step toward stabilizing the region and unlocking foreign investment and development across the Middle East, describing Syria as "a Rubix cube that President Trump, MBS, and Erdogan are solving together."[22]

## Mediation between Trump and Iran (February 2025)

In February 2025, CNN reported that Saudi Arabia was open to mediating between the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and [[iran|Iran]] in pursuit of a new deal limiting Tehran's nuclear program.[23] The kingdom was concerned that Iran might be more inclined to pursue a nuclear weapon now that its regional proxies — long viewed as a deterrent against Israeli attacks — had been significantly weakened.[23] Riyadh hoped to leverage its close ties with Trump to provide Iran a diplomatic bridge to the [[white-house|White House]]; it was unclear whether a formal offer had been made, but the move underscored the kingdom's desire to build on improved relations with its former foe and secure a seat at the negotiating table.[23] Iran's message was mixed, with Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] saying talks with the United States were "not smart."[23]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued there would be no U.S. war with Iran regardless of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s posture, citing an October 2024 disclosure by Iranian rear admiral Shahram Irani that Iran and Saudi Arabia were discussing joint naval exercises, announced by the Saudi Foreign Ministry as successfully completed the following day — in his reading, evidence of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[23] He predicted that a new Iran nuclear deal would grant Saudi Arabia, Iran and eventually others fair access to nuclear technology for commercial energy production rather than weapons.[23]

## Riyadh venue for US-Russia Ukraine talks (February 2025)

Saudi Arabia hosted high-level Russian and American delegations in Riyadh beginning Tuesday, February 18, 2025, for talks aimed at restoring diplomatic relations and paving the way toward a settlement of the [[ukraine|Ukraine]] conflict; the negotiations were proposed during a 90-minute phone call between [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Trump.[23] Russia sent Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] and presidential foreign policy aide [[yury-ushakov|Yury Ushakov]], while the United States was represented by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], national security adviser [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] and Middle East envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]]; neither Ukrainian nor EU diplomats attended, and Western outlets noted the absence of Ukraine envoy [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]].[23]

Badlands commentary highlighted images of Lavrov deplaning in Riyadh, Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Foreign Minister Faisal bin Farhan meeting Rubio and Witkoff, and statements from the Jordanian court expressing full trust in MBS to lead Arabia in the negotiation, contrasting them with a "panic-stricken" [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] convening an emergency European meeting in Paris.[23]

## Jeddah meeting with Jordan (April 2025)

On Wednesday, April 23, 2025, Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] met [[jordan|Jordan]]'s [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]] during an official visit to the Kingdom.[24] King Abdullah arrived in Jeddah accompanied by Crown Prince Hussein and was received by the deputy governor of Makkah, Prince Saud bin Mishaal bin Abdulaziz, according to the Saudi Press Agency.[24] The Jordanian monarch expressed pride in the historical relations between Riyadh and Amman and acknowledged Saudi Arabia's significant role in supporting Arab and Islamic causes, while the two leaders discussed regional developments, particularly the situation in the Gaza Strip and the occupied West Bank.[24] The meeting coincided with Jordan's formal dissolution and banning of the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]], and preceded a planned visit to Riyadh by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] within weeks.[24]

## The 2024 "plan B" with Washington

In the spring of 2024, Saudi Arabia and the United States had drafted a set of agreements on security and technology-sharing originally intended to be linked to a broader Middle East settlement involving [[israel|Israel]] and the Palestinians.[25] With no ceasefire in [[gaza]] and with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government resisting Palestinian statehood, the Saudis pushed a more modest [[us-saudi-plan-b-agreements|plan B]] excluding the Israelis, under which Washington and Riyadh would sign a bilateral defence pact, cooperate on a Saudi civil nuclear energy industry, and share high-level work in artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies.[25] An offer of diplomatic normalisation with Riyadh would still stand for Israel in exchange for acceptance of a two-state solution to the 76-year Israeli–Arab conflict, but the US–Saudi deals would not hinge on Israeli assent.[25] For the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration the arrangement would cement a strategic partnership keeping encroaching Chinese and Russian influence at bay, even absent the hoped-for regional settlement.[25]

## Cultural opening: the first Saudi opera

The same period saw the kingdom stage [[zarqa-al-yamama|Zarqa Al Yamama]], the first grand opera produced in Saudi Arabia, which opened in Riyadh as part of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s ambitious plans to shift the kingdom into a new economic and social era.[25] Badlands commentary treated the premiere — high European art form performed in Arabic in the land of the Two Holy Mosques — as "inverse Shariah," a rebuttal to fears that Islamic societies would inevitably erase Western culture.[25]

## Condemnation of Israeli West Bank sovereignty call (July 2025)

On July 2, 2025, Saudi Arabia strongly condemned a call by an Israeli government minister for [[israel|Israel]] to apply full sovereignty over the occupied West Bank.[26] The Kingdom's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said it "condemned and denunciated" the statement, describing such action as a violation of international law, rejecting any attempts to expand settlements on Palestinian land, and reiterating the importance of Israel abiding by international resolutions.[26] The statement followed remarks by Israeli Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]] that "the time has come" for Israel to apply sovereignty across the West Bank.[26]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the condemnation as a rebuttal to earlier reporting by _Israel Hayom_ on "leaked details" of an expanded version of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] teased by Steve Witkoff on CNBC, which had claimed Saudi Arabia agreed to cede parts of the West Bank to Israel, calling that claim "fake news."[26]

## October 2024: joint drills with Iran and BRICS positioning
In October 2024, Iranian officials said Saudi Arabia and Iran were planning to conduct joint military exercises in the Red Sea — a first for the two regional heavyweights, which had severed diplomatic ties in 2016 and restored them the previous year under a China-brokered deal.[27] Admiral Shahram Irani, commander of Iran's navy, told the Iranian news agency ISNA that "Saudi Arabia has asked that we organize joint exercises in the Red Sea," with coordination underway and delegations to consult on the details; Riyadh did not confirm the report.[27]

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry presented the development as vindication of his reading of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, who had spent the preceding year being slandered on Arab social media as a Zionist puppet.[27] In that account, MBS refused to take Benjamin Netanyahu's bait of a region-wide war — "the only winning move is not to play" — while embracing a humanitarian posture and keeping the Muslim world's hand stayed, leaving Netanyahu isolated as his Gaza campaign drew global revulsion.[27]

Saudi Arabia's posture toward the multipolar bloc was read the same way. MBS was notably absent from the BRICS summit in Kazan, which Badlands attributed to a strategic decision to hold back from formally accepting the kingdom's invitation to join BRICS — perhaps waiting for the right moment to help end the fiat U.S. dollar.[27] Instead the Crown Prince hosted the Hashemite King of Jordan, an embrace Badlands framed as a redemption arc reaching back to 1902, when MBS's grandfather began the campaign to conquer Arabia and eventually drove the Hashemites from their ancestral home in Mecca.[27]

## Rejection of Gaza displacement (February 2025)

On Wednesday, February 5, 2025, Saudi Arabia said its long-held position that Palestinians must have their own independent state was firm and not open to negotiation, a stance Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman had reiterated many times before.[28] The Saudi Foreign Ministry statement came shortly after President Donald Trump said he wanted the United States to own the Gaza Strip after all Palestinians were displaced and sent to other countries where settlements would be constructed for them.[28] Saudi leaders have repeatedly called for justice for Palestinians, saying they deserve a state of their own alongside Israel as the route to a lasting solution, and have made any formal relations between the Kingdom and Israel contingent on the creation of a viable Palestinian state on the 1967 borders.[28]

## Immigration crackdown (February 2025)

The Saudi Ministry of Interior announced that authorities had deported 10,000 expatriates over the preceding week as part of a nationwide crackdown on residency, labor, and border violations.[28] Joint field operations across the kingdom apprehended more than 21,000 violators, including nearly 14,000 for residency infractions, 4,600 for border security violations, and more than 3,000 for labour law breaches.[28] A further 27,000 individuals were referred to their respective diplomatic missions to obtain travel documents while 2,300 others were finalising travel arrangements for deportation; security forces detained 1,477 people attempting to enter Saudi Arabia illegally, of whom 41% were Yemeni nationals, 55% Ethiopians, and 4% other nationalities.[28] Another 90 people were arrested attempting to exit the country unlawfully, and 18 individuals were apprehended for harbouring, transporting, or employing undocumented expatriates.[28] The ministry stressed that these offenses are classified as major crimes warranting arrest and are considered violations of public trust, urged residents to report violations via regional emergency hotlines, and said 34,000 foreign nationals — 31,000 men and 3,000 women — were being processed for legal action.[28] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry observed that the Saudi government justified the campaign as protecting the Saudi worker and argued that MBS was proving a crackdown at scale is possible with the right mindset.[28]

## Renewed Normalization Push (April 2024)

In April 2024 the Biden administration mounted a fresh push for a long-shot deal in which Riyadh would extend diplomatic recognition to Israel in exchange for an Israeli commitment to Palestinian statehood, according to US and Saudi officials.[29] As inducements, the White House offered Saudi Arabia a more formal defense relationship with Washington, assistance in acquiring civil nuclear power, and a renewed push for a Palestinian state — a package US officials described as being in the final stages of negotiation.[29] American officials argued the successful multicountry effort to shoot down Iranian missiles and drones that Saturday demonstrated that Israeli security against threats from Tehran could be enhanced through closer integration with the kingdom.[29] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry portrayed Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman as calmly repeating "sign the peace deal" through every escalation of the Middle East crisis, framing the standoff as the Art of the Deal with one side playing chess and the other checkers.[29]

## F-35 sale (November 2025)

On November 17, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would approve the sale of [[f-35|F-35]] fighter jets to Saudi Arabia, which would make the kingdom the first Middle Eastern country other than [[israel|Israel]] to obtain the aircraft.[30] "They want to buy. They are a great ally. We will be doing that. We will be selling them F-35s," Trump told reporters in the Oval Office.[30] The United States sells the F-35 primarily to formal military allies such as NATO members and Japan, and supplying the jets to Riyadh would change the regional military balance and affect Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|qualitative military edge]], a commitment Congress codified into law in 2008.[30] Israel did not oppose the sale but asked that it be conditioned on Saudi normalization of relations with Israel, one of the items on the agenda for Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s White House visit the following day alongside a U.S.–Saudi security agreement.[30]

## Statement on Israeli settler attacks on aid convoys (2024)

In May 2024 the Saudi Foreign Ministry issued what Badlands commentary described as a rare public statement singling out a non-state actor — Israeli citizens — after settlers attacked humanitarian aid trucks dispatched by Jordan and destroyed food bound for camps in Rafah.[31] Videos of the attacks circulated online, and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the settlers' conduct as irregular asymmetric warfare, arguing that the resulting optics compounded the difficulties facing the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration as it weighed the release of its report on Israel's conduct in Gaza.[31]

### February 2025: Miami summit and Russia-Ukraine talks
On February 19, 2025 President Donald Trump became the first American president to address the Saudi-backed Future Investment Initiative Institute, speaking at the Faena Forum on Miami Beach — a sign, the Miami Herald wrote, that the oil giant was increasingly reaching the American mainstream with a signature event designed to promote the country as a global actor seeking to diversify its economy.[32] Trump called on the kingdom to invest $1 trillion in the United States, and his Doral resort was scheduled to host the Saudi-backed LIV Golf tournament that April.[32] The day before, the United States and Russia had held talks in Saudi Arabia at which the two countries agreed to strengthen diplomatic and economic ties and to begin peace talks to end the war in Ukraine, with no Ukrainian officials invited.[32] Badlands commentary predicted Saudi Arabia would be "one of our greatest allies of the Golden Age."[32]

## State media campaign against Netanyahu (February 2025)

In February 2025 Saudi Arabia launched what the Financial Times called a ferocious and scathing state media campaign against Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], reflecting growing frustration in the Gulf state's royal court with the Israeli leader and the war in Gaza.[33] The unusually hostile barrage, which could only have been published with the authorities' approval, followed talk by US and Israeli officials of Saudi Arabia normalising relations with Israel, despite Riyadh's insistence that normalisation depended on the establishment of a Palestinian state.[33] The onslaught was triggered by Netanyahu joking in an interview about creating a Palestinian state in Saudi Arabia.[33]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] emphasized how out of character the campaign was, noting that the Saudis are known for laconic, measured, and private public communication.[33] He observed that over the preceding 15 months Riyadh had been a lifeline for Israel, organizing land convoys to move goods from the UAE across the Arabian desert when Israeli-bound ships could not traverse the Red Sea to dock at Haifa because of Houthi threats — all while being mocked across the Muslim world as cowards and traitors for choosing peace over war with Israel.[33]

He also flagged the reported postponement by Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Prince Faisal bin Farhan]] of a scheduled trip to Washington after the US president announced his Gaza plan, calling the cancellation a signal that the Saudis had lost patience with Tel Aviv, even as he assumed the kingdom remained in [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s corner and read in on his plan ahead of an anticipated Trump–[[vladimir-putin|Putin]] peace summit to be hosted in the kingdom.[33]

## Pivot to the east (March 2023)

On 29 March 2023 the Saudi state news agency reported that the government had approved partial membership in the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]], the political and security bloc that includes [[china|China]], [[russia|Russia]] and India, ahead of a May meeting of the grouping's foreign ministers at which [[iran|Iran]] had recently gained full member status.[34] The Wall Street Journal described the step as part of a broad effort by Saudi Arabia and neighbouring Persian Gulf states to expand diplomatic partnerships beyond traditional Western allies in a marked pivot to the east, undertaken amid uncertainty about Washington's long-term commitment to security in the energy-rich region as the United States focused on a rising China and a belligerent Russia.[34]

The announcement followed the kingdom's China-brokered restoration of diplomatic relations with its rival Iran earlier that month, a deal that burnished Beijing's credentials as a new power broker in the Middle East, and came as Riyadh neared an agreement to restore ties with [[syria|Syria]] through Russian-mediated negotiations.[34] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued that the logical next step was Saudi accession to [[brics|BRICS]] and asked what would happen to the dollar when the kingdom made that move, a reading echoed by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], who listed the SCO decision first among "Sovereign Alliance Tidbits" showing solidarity against Western globalists.[34]

## Condemnation of Israeli operations in Syria (December 2024)

In a statement issued by its Foreign Ministry on Monday, December 9, 2024, Saudi Arabia accused [[israel|Israel]] of attempting to undermine the restoration of stability in [[syria|Syria]] by broadening its military occupation of Syrian territory following the fall of Damascus.[36] The ministry said Israel's actions demonstrated the "continued violation of the rules of international law and its determination to sabotage Syria's chances of restoring its security, stability and territorial integrity."[36] Iraq and Qatar issued parallel condemnations, with Baghdad urging the UN Security Council to act.[36]

Badlands commentary noted that Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], who in 2018 had called Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] a "thug" and demanded his removal from power, had shifted position amid the Syrian crisis.[36] The same commentary recounted that the September 11 attacks were a catalyzing event in the young crown prince's life — he reportedly watched the second tower struck on live television and concluded the world would blame the entire Arab world for crimes committed by a few — and that he vowed revenge against those responsible, whom the writer identified as the same interests behind the toppling of Syria.[36]

## Hosting the two-state solution alliance (October 2024)

On Wednesday, October 30, 2024, Saudi Arabia hosted in Riyadh the first meeting of the [[international-alliance-to-implement-the-two-state-solution|International Alliance to Implement the Two-State Solution]], a grouping unveiled the previous month on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly that brings together nations from the Middle East, Europe and beyond to press for the establishment of a Palestinian state.[37] Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Prince Faisal bin Farhan]] said nearly 90 "states and international organizations" were taking part in the two-day meeting.[37]

"A genocide is happening with the goal of evicting the Palestinian people from their land, which Saudi Arabia rejects," Faisal said, describing the humanitarian situation as "catastrophic" and denouncing the "complete blockade" of northern Gaza.[37] Israel rejected the accusation of genocide, saying its operation aimed to eliminate [[hamas|Hamas]] and rescue the 251 hostages kidnapped on October 7, 2023, an assault that also killed some 1,200 people.[37]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified Saudi Arabia as "the tip of the spear" of a world coming together against what he called the globalist hegemony, speculated that the grouping might be renamed the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], and argued that without the rise of [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] to the Saudi throne the world would likely be in a violent third world war.[37]

## Riyadh talks on Ukraine and Black Sea security (March 2025)

Talks between senior Russian and American officials, focused on a settlement of the Ukraine conflict and maritime security in the Black Sea, concluded in Riyadh in late March 2025 after more than 12 hours, with a joint statement expected the following day according to Russian news agencies RIA Novosti and TASS.[38] The Russian Foreign Ministry released a short video showing the negotiating team leaving the talks, saying only that "the Russian-American consultations have concluded."[38] A White House source told Reuters that a "positive announcement" was expected "in the near future" following progress made in Riyadh, while Grigory Karasin, a veteran diplomat and chairman of the parliamentary International Relations Committee on the Russian delegation, described the talks as "creative" but also "technical."[38]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified a pattern of narrative repetition around the US–Russia–Saudi triangle, tracing it back into the Biden administration when Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|MBS]] helped facilitate prisoner-swap negotiations between Washington and Moscow, and predicted the process would culminate the following month with [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and President [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] meeting face-to-face in Riyadh in a historic moment shifting geopolitical norms.[38]
