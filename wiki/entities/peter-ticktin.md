# Peter Ticktin

Peter Ticktin is an American attorney serving as counsel for former Mesa County clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]. In December 2025 he advanced a constitutional argument that a presidential pardon could reach Peters despite her conviction under Colorado state law.[1]

## The pardon argument

Ticktin's theory, as reported by Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], rests on Reconstruction-era ideology and would require the federal government to claim broader power over state criminal matters than it conventionally holds.[1] Commentary sympathetic to Peters nonetheless flagged a tension in the approach: a Supreme Court ruling on the question could move American jurisprudence toward originalism and strengthen liberty generally, yet the same reasoning could foreclose the specific route to Peters' release.[1]

Speaking with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] the same week, Ticktin addressed what would happen procedurally after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued the pardon.[1] Ticktin had also claimed publicly that Peters could be pardoned by Christmas, a prediction that outside observers considered unlikely.[1]

Colorado officials — Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]], Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]], and Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] — uniformly rejected the premise, maintaining that no president has jurisdiction over state law or the power to pardon state convictions.[1] Badlands commentary observed that litigation over the pardon could ultimately strengthen states' rights protections under the Tenth Amendment even as it tested federal pardon power.[1]

## December 2025 letters to the White House

In a letter to President Trump and his pardon counsel dated Sunday, December 7, 2025, Ticktin wrote that six months earlier inmates had threatened to stab and kill Peters, then aged 70, and that even after she transferred units she was attacked by other inmates and required guards to pull them off her.[2] He said a request to move Peters to a safer unit had been denied six times.[2] "Of course, prison is a horrible experience for anyone, but it is worse for those who are innocent and wrongfully prosecuted," he wrote, adding, "There are dangerous people who truly belong locked up, but Tina Peters is not one of them."[2] Reporting noted that the letter presented no evidence for the violent claims and that the Colorado corrections department could not immediately verify them.[2]
A separate letter Ticktin published on December 6 advanced a novel constitutional theory that the President may pardon all offenses — excepting impeachment — based on the plural pronouns the framers used for the "united States," citing Article III, Section 3's treason clause: "Treason of the United States shall consist only in levying war against them, or in adhering to their enemies, giving them aid and comfort."[2] Ticktin argued that the framers spoke of "United Countries" and that it would make no sense for individual states to be able to circumvent the pardon power.[2] The letter's legal work was attributed by Ticktin, in an interview with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]], to election attorney [[stephanie-lambert|Stephanie Lambert]].[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the maneuver as effectively imposing a deadline on Trump to act outside his authority, framed as a simple matter of doing the right thing, and predicted that a failure to free Peters by Christmas would be blamed on Trump or on officials such as [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] — a narrative she dismissed as likely fake and stupid.[2]
