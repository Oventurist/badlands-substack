# Academy of Motion Picture Arts & Sciences

The **Academy of Motion Picture Arts & Sciences** is the nonprofit organization that presents the annual Academy Awards, better known as the Oscars.[1]

In May 2024 the Academy launched a $500 million global fundraising drive intended to bolster its finances and counter a decades-long decline in the audience for the Oscars television broadcast, which generates most of its revenue.[1] According to chief executive [[bill-kramer|Bill Kramer]], the Academy had already raised about $100 million, with individual donors including billionaire [[leonard-blavatnik|Leonard Blavatnik]], and had signed sponsorship deals with global luxury brands including the Dorchester Collection.[1]

The television audience for the Oscars shrank from nearly 44 million US viewers in 2014 to 19.5 million in 2024, according to Statista.[1] The Academy's broadcast deal with ABC, the Walt Disney-owned network, runs through 2028 — the 100th anniversary of the awards ceremony — with renewal negotiations set to begin soon.[1] Kramer described the current ABC arrangement as "very healthy" and the partnership with Disney as "an amazing partnership," but said that with streaming upending the film and television business the Academy had decided to embark on a "revenue diversification campaign."[1] He added that "No healthy company or organisation should rely on one source of support to a degree that could cause concern if that support decreases."[1]
