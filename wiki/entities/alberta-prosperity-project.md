# Alberta Prosperity Project

The Alberta Prosperity Project (APP) is a Canadian independence advocacy organization campaigning for the secession of [[alberta|Alberta]] from [[canada|Canada]]. It became the subject of international attention in January 2026 after reporting that its representatives had met repeatedly with United States officials in Washington.

## Meetings with the State Department

The [[badlands-brief-1ce|January 30, 2026 Badlands Brief]], citing the *Financial Times*, reports that officials from the [[state-department|State Department]] met with APP members three times since April 2025 in Washington, with a fourth delegation planned for the following month. [[jeffrey-rath|Jeffrey Rath]], co-founder and legal counsel for the APP, reportedly attended the meetings and told the *Financial Times*: "The US is extremely enthusiastic about a free and independent Alberta."

A State Department spokesperson downplayed the contacts: "The department regularly meets with civil society types. As is typical in routine meetings such as these, no commitments were made."

## The $500 billion credit line

The APP is seeking a $500 billion line of credit from the United States government to support Alberta in the event of a successful independence referendum. No referendum has been called to date. Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] stated that he expects the US administration to respect Canadian sovereignty, noting that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has never raised Alberta separatism with him.

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] endorsed the project, writing that "the most productive part of Canada wants to leave the globalist proxy state" and that "Canadian liberation is a worthy cause–for both us and them." She framed helping Alberta gain "independence from Britain" as both a core value and a national security imperative, arguing that "having the global regime on the US northern border is ostensibly our greatest national security threat." She noted that Alberta provides 80 percent of Canada's oil and 60 percent of its natural gas, and suggested Trump "should call King Charles and make a deal."
