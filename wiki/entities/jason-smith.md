# Jason Smith

Jason Smith is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from Missouri who played a public role in releasing testimony from [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] whistleblowers concerning the federal investigation of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]].[1]

## Role in the IRS whistleblower disclosures

In June 2023, Smith stated that U.S. Attorney [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] had sought to be appointed as a special counsel in the Hunter Biden case during the previous year and had been denied.[1] That claim formed part of a broader set of whistleblower allegations that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] under Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] had buried evidence of Hunter Biden's tax crimes and had reduced recommended felony charges to two misdemeanors.[1]

## Statements on denied charging venues

As chairman of the [[house-ways-and-means-committee|House Ways and Means Committee]], Smith also asserted that Weiss "tried to bring charges in the District of Columbia around March of 2022 and was denied," and that Weiss "sought to bring charges in the Central District of California in the fall of 2022 and had that request denied in January 2023."[2] These statements were central to the argument advanced by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and House Republicans that Garland's March 1, 2023 sworn testimony — that the U.S. attorney had full authority to bring cases in other districts — could not be reconciled with the record, and they underpinned House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s June 2023 threat to open an impeachment inquiry into Garland.[2]

## Role in the Biden impeachment inquiry

In an exclusive Breitbart News interview on Wednesday, September 13, 2023, Smith said the newly announced [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] would "expedite" House Republicans' investigations of the wider Biden family.[3] Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] had announced the formal launch of the inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for "abuse of power, obstruction, and corruption" the previous day, saying Republicans had uncovered "serious and credible allegations," and had designated Smith alongside [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] and [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] to lead it.[3]

Smith explained that the announcement would "speed up" and "expedite" the process after the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] had "continued to stall" regarding their investigations, noting that the three chairmen had asked 13 federal employees to appear before the committees for interviews.[3] He further lamented that Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] had announced a special counsel, which Smith said was being used to "obstruct" a congressional investigation.[3]
