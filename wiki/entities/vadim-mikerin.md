# Vadim Mikerin

Vadim Mikerin was the Russian nuclear official identified by the FBI as the central figure in a bribery, extortion and money-laundering scheme surrounding the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]].[1] He headed Tenex, a subsidiary of the Russian state nuclear corporation [[rosatom|Rosatom]], and was described in reporting cited by Badlands Media as "the main Russian overseeing Putin's nuclear expansion inside the United States."[1]

## FBI investigation

Federal agents began building a case against Mikerin as early as 2009, using a confidential U.S. witness working inside the Russian nuclear industry to gather financial records, make secret recordings and intercept emails.[1] The evidence indicated that Moscow had compromised an American uranium trucking firm through bribes and kickbacks in violation of the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, and that senior officials at Rosatom were aware of Mikerin's scheme.[1] The article stresses that the FBI had accumulated this evidence well before the U.S. government committee approved the transfer of American uranium assets to Russian ownership.[1]

In 2010 Mikerin was dispatched to the United States on a work visa approved by the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration to open Rosatom's new American arm, Tenam.[1] Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats that visa approval as evidence that Obama and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] knew of the ongoing FBI investigation before signing off on the deal.[1]

## Kickbacks and Transport Logistics International

Mikerin awarded a contract for the sensitive job of transporting Russia's uranium around the United States to the American trucking firm [[transport-logistics-international|Transport Logistics International]] in return for more than $2 million in kickbacks from some of its executives, according to court records.[1] The article argues that Uranium One was added to Transport Logistics International's roster of companies permitted to export uranium without Uranium One itself being granted an export licence, and that the bribes served to keep the arrangement quiet.[1]

## Prosecution and plea deal

The Mikerin probe began in 2009 under FBI Director [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] and ended in late 2015 under Director [[james-comey|James Comey]].[1] Although Mikerin, a Russian financier and trucking-firm executives were arrested and charged in 2014, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and FBI took little public credit; the only public statement was a little-noticed press release issued in August 2015, days before Labor Day, noting that the defendants had reached plea deals.[1] By then the criminal case had been narrowed to a single money-laundering charge covering a scheme that officials conceded ran from 2004 to 2014, with no mention of the influence-peddling conversations the informant had witnessed concerning Russian nuclear officials seeking to ingratiate themselves with the Clintons.[1]

On December 15, 2015 the Justice Department announced that Mikerin, "a former Russian official residing in Maryland," had been sentenced to 48 months in prison and ordered to forfeit more than $2.1 million.[1] In 2014 he had pled guilty in a U.S. court to orchestrating more than $2 million in bribe payments routed through accounts in Cyprus, Latvia and Switzerland.[1] Lange argues the narrow charge and muted publicity were designed to prevent Washington from grasping that a major Russian nuclear corruption scheme with national security implications had been uncovered, and to shield [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]]'s foundation from exposure.[1]
