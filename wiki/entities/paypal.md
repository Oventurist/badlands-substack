# PayPal

PayPal is an online payments company widely described as one of the first firms to offer peer-to-peer payment services over the internet, allowing users to pay for transactions, receive payments and send money to other people.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] credits the company with helping to launch e-commerce, because it made it possible to transfer payments securely rather than mailing paper checks, and describes it as now one of the most used payment systems in the world.[1]

## Origins in X.com and Confinity

PayPal's corporate lineage runs through [[x-com|X.com]], the online bank founded by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] in 1999, and Confinity Inc., a software company that had developed a strong online payment system.[1] X.com merged with Confinity, but where Musk wanted the combined digital bank to compete directly with the legacy banks, other leaders of the company favored a different direction.[1] Musk was removed as chief executive, the company was renamed PayPal, and its focus shifted to becoming a global payment system rather than a full-service online bank.[1]

Musk has repeatedly characterized this as a missed opportunity, saying that had PayPal executed the plan he wanted, "they would probably be the most valuable company in the world," and that the company "would be called 'X.'"[1]

## Later developments

Musk quietly repurchased the X.com domain name from PayPal, a step Lange reads as evidence that Musk is again pursuing his original digital-banking vision through [[twitter|Twitter]].[1] Lange also situates PayPal within the broader [[financial-technology|financial technology]] wave, noting that companies such as Block are seen as differentiating themselves from established payment platforms like Visa and PayPal by building on [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] infrastructure.[1]

## Partnership with the ADL

In July 2021, [[reuters|Reuters]] reported that PayPal Holdings Inc. was partnering with the non-profit [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]] to investigate how extremist and hate movements in the United States take advantage of financial platforms to fund their activities.[2] The initiative was led through the ADL's Center on Extremism and focused on uncovering and disrupting financial flows supporting white supremacist and anti-government organisations, with the information collected to be shared with other firms in the financial industry, law enforcement and policymakers.[2] ADL chief executive [[jonathan-greenblatt|Jonathan Greenblatt]] described the effort as "a unique opportunity to further understand how hate spreads and develop key insights that will inform the efforts of the financial industry, law enforcement, and our communities in mitigating extremist threats."[2]

Badlands Media treated the partnership as a form of financial attack on political dissenters rather than a genuine counter-extremism measure, arguing that the framing of "combating the spread of this-and-that" creates a pretext to target opponents of establishment orthodoxy.[2] The article placed PayPal alongside fundraising platforms such as [[gofundme|GoFundMe]] and Patreon, which it said had frozen the funds of reader-funded news outlets, citing GoFundMe's August 2023 freeze on [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]].[2]
