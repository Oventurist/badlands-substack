# Michigan Supreme Court

The Michigan Supreme Court is the highest court of the state of Michigan. On December 27, 2023, it rejected an attempt to remove former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the state's 2024 presidential primary ballot under the "insurrectionist ban" of Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].[1]

The outcome was generally expected and was treated as a victory for Trump, though challengers retained the option of renewing the effort ahead of the general election.[1] The decision stood in direct contrast to the ruling days earlier by the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]], which had removed Trump from that state's primary ballot over his role in the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot — a decision stayed pending appeal.[1]

## Procedural posture

Unlike the [[colorado-ballot-disqualification-case|Colorado case]], the Michigan lawsuit never reached trial and was dismissed early in the process; an intermediate appellate court upheld the dismissal on procedural grounds.[1] The Michigan Court of Claims judge who first heard the matter held that state law gives election officials no leeway to police the eligibility of presidential primary candidates, and further concluded that the suit raised a political question inappropriate for judicial resolution.[1]

## Significance

The dueling Michigan and Colorado outcomes sharpened the need for a definitive ruling from the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]], particularly with the 2024 primary calendar imminent.[1]
