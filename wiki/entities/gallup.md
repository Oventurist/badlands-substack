# Gallup

Gallup is an American polling organization whose long-running survey of moral acceptability, tracked since 2012, provided the data behind a June 2026 report showing Americans expressing lower moral acceptance for several personal-liberty issues than in recent years.[1]

## 2026 morality poll

The share of Americans viewing birth control as morally acceptable fell to 83 percent, down from 90 percent the previous year and the lowest level recorded since tracking began.[1] Acceptance of having a baby outside marriage dropped to 58 percent from a recent peak of 70 percent in 2022–2023, while moral approval of gambling reached a new low amid the expansion of prediction markets and widespread sports betting.[1]

Majorities continued to view eight other behaviors as morally wrong, including sex between teenagers, extramarital affairs, human cloning, polygamy, suicide, animal cloning, pornography, and changing one's gender.[1] Acceptance of changing one's gender declined alongside softening support for same-sex marriage and for the morality of gay and lesbian relationships.[1]

Partisan divides remained stark: Democrats were far more likely than Republicans to consider abortion and gender transition morally acceptable, while Republicans showed greater acceptance of the death penalty, wearing animal fur, and medical testing on animals.[1] Independents accounted for much of the recent downward shift on birth control and out-of-wedlock births.[1] Overall the results pointed to a modest reversal of prior liberalization on certain social issues, coinciding with renewed religiosity among young men and broader conservative political gains.[1]

## Reception

[[axios|Axios]] framed the findings as a "more puritanical view of personal liberty" coinciding with a Republican resurgence, young men's rebounding religiosity and conservative backlash to LGBTQ+ advancements.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] countered that a drop from 90 to 83 percent still leaves most Americans "pretty sin tolerant," and that what the outlet described as puritanism is simply reduced tolerance of sin.[1]

## October 2023 Biden approval poll

Gallup's poll of October 2–23, 2023 recorded [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s job approval among Democrats falling 11 percentage points in a month to 75%, the worst reading of his presidency from his own party, and pushed his overall approval down four points to 37%, matching his personal low.[2] Approval among independents declined four points to 35%, while Republicans' rating held unchanged at 5%.[2]

Gallup noted that after ranging from 49% to 57% during Biden's first eight months in office, his approval had been mired in the low 40s for much of the following two years, and that including the 37% reading and an identical figure in April, his approval had fallen below 40% four times in the 33 readings the firm had taken since he took office.[2] The pollster linked the downturn to the aftermath of the October 7 attack on [[israel|Israel]] by [[hamas|Hamas]] militants — at least 1,400 deaths and more than 200 kidnappings — and the ensuing Israeli counteroffensive, which had produced an estimated 5,000 deaths in [[gaza|Gaza]] and a humanitarian crisis.[2]

## 2024 ideology and gender research

In February 2024 Gallup published analysis showing that US women have become markedly more liberal since the 1990s while men's ideological self-identification has remained mostly stable, a finding detailed at [[gallup-political-ideology-gender-gap|Gallup political ideology gender gap]].[3] The organization's national figures placed the country at center-right, with 36 percent identifying as conservative, 25 percent liberal, and 36 percent moderate, and noted that the liberal share sat one point below its all-time high as the moderate category shrank.[3] The findings drew on annual averages of Gallup telephone surveys of 12,000 or more US adults per year, with at least 500 adults in each gender-by-age subgroup, analyzed by age group at time of survey rather than by cohort.[3]

## February 2024 Biden approval poll

In late February 2024, Gallup reported that approval of President Joe Biden's job performance had edged down three percentage points to 38%, one point above his all-time low and well below the 50% threshold that has typically led to reelection for incumbents.[4] The organization also found subpar ratings on five key issues — a new low of 28% on immigration, and figures from 30% to 40% on the Israel–Hamas conflict, foreign affairs, the economy and Ukraine — and noted Biden's approval had not exceeded 44% since August 2021, with his 39.8% third-year average the second worst among post-World War II presidents elected to a first term.[4] Badlands commentary treated the release less as a measurement than as narrative management, arguing that published numbers were calibrated low enough to be credible but high enough to sustain a comeback storyline.[4]

## Immigration tops the "most important problem" list (February 2024)

A Gallup survey conducted February 1–20, 2024 found that significantly more Americans named immigration as the most important problem facing the United States (28%) than a month earlier (20%), pushing it past "the government" — which had ranked first each month from January through November 2023 — for the top position.[5] In the same poll, 20% named the government, 12% the economy and 11% inflation; immigration was the only issue showing meaningful month-over-month change.[5] Gallup, which began compiling mentions of immigration in 1981, noted that the 28% reading essentially tied the record 27% from July 2019, and that immigration had previously ranked first five years earlier during a surge of attempted border crossings by Central American migrants, as well as in July and November 2018 and July 2014.[5]

## Biden's 13th-quarter approval rating (April 2024)

An April 2024 Gallup poll found that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] held the worst job approval rating of any president since Dwight Eisenhower for a recently completed 13th quarter in office, at 38.7%.[6] The previous low for that quarter was George H. W. Bush's 41.8% in 1992; [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Barack Obama averaged 46.8% and 45.9% respectively at the same point, and before Bush, Jimmy Carter was the only other president with a sub-50% average in his 13th quarter.[6] Richard Nixon, Ronald Reagan, Bill Clinton and George W. Bush averaged between 51% and 55%, while Eisenhower recorded the highest at 73.2%.[6] Gallup's historical series, which dates to 1945, placed Biden's rating 277th out of 314 presidential quarters — the bottom 12% — and it was the lowest of his own presidency, which had been running in the low 40% range since the fourth quarter of his term.[6]

## 2023 media confidence survey

Gallup's annual survey of trust in U.S. institutions, conducted September 1–23, 2023, found Americans' confidence that mass media — newspapers, television and radio — would report the news fully, accurately and fairly had plummeted to lows matching the previous record set in 2016.[7] Twenty-nine percent of U.S. adults reported "not very much" trust in the media, while a record-high 39 percent reported "none at all" — one percentage point above the previous record and 12 points higher than the 2016 reading, when the most common sentiment was "not very much" trust at 41 percent.[7] Gallup characterized the result as the grimmest view of the media in its polling history, part of a half-century of steadily declining trust.[7] The 2016 low had coincided with sharp criticism of the press from then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[7]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the collapse as a precondition for narrative change, writing that before the American public can be told a new story "buoyed by the truth," they must first disengage from the current story and its storytellers — and that the data showed they were doing so.[7] He closed by asking readers to recall what happened in 2016, the last time public trust in the Central Narrative was this low.[7]
