# Irgun

The Irgun was a Zionist paramilitary organization described in Badlands reporting as a terrorist organization that engaged in irregular warfare activities against Palestinian civilians throughout the 1930s and 1940s.[1]

Members of the Netanyahu family — [[nathan-mileikowsky|Nathan Mileikowsky]] and his sons — affiliated with the Irgun after Mileikowsky joined the [[revisionist-zionism|Revisionist Movement]] under [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]].[1] Irgun propaganda posters prominently displayed the "[[greater-israel|Greater Israel]]" map, which the Badlands account identifies as identical to the map displayed on the podium at which Israeli minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] spoke at an event in March 2023.[1]

The Irgun was formally disbanded in 1949 — the same year [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was born — after the Zionists succeeded in establishing their government.[1] Its membership, however, morphed into the [[herut-party|Herut Party]], which in turn became the [[likud-party|Likud Party]] in 1977, the same period in which Netanyahu was cycling out of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] and partnering with his father to plot his political career.[1]

## Origins, Italian sponsorship and the Lehi split

Badlands commentary describes the Irgun as a Jewish paramilitary group that operated in Mandate Palestine in the 1930s and 1940s and was characterized as a terrorist organization by Albert Einstein, the United Nations, British and American authorities, and John F. Kennedy, among others.[2] It was founded by [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]], leader of the [[revisionist-zionism|Revisionist Zionist]] movement, which explicitly held that violence was a legitimate form of political expression; Jabotinsky's personal secretary was [[benzion-netanyahu|Benzion Netanyahu]], father of the current Israeli prime minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[2]

According to the same account, the Irgun was armed by [[benito-mussolini|Mussolini]]'s regime and received combat training in Italy, particularly in the use of explosives, with Italian instructors remarking on the aptitude of Polish-Russian Jews formed by more than 125 years of hardship in the Pale of Settlement.[2] The Bolsheviks emerged from the same region, and it was the Bolshevik seizure of power in Moscow in 1917 that ended the Pale — which the commentary offers as the reason nearly every Israeli political activist of the 1930s identified as a socialist and many as Bolsheviks.[2]

In the late 1930s the organization split over strategy. Jabotinsky sought to make peace with the British colonial authorities in order to negotiate the creation of a Jewish government, while a faction led by Avraham Stern argued that National Socialism was Israel's natural political ideology and proposed an alliance with the Axis powers; Stern's faction broke away to form the [[lehi|Lehi]] militia, also known as the Stern Gang.[2] The Irgun and Lehi reunited in 1948, together with other paramilitary groups, to form the [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli Defense Force]].[2] Lehi militant Yitzhak Shamir later became prime minister in the 1980s and appointed Benjamin Netanyahu his foreign minister in 1990.[2]

## Jabotinsky's networks in the Nazi-era Middle East

Matthew Ehret places the fascist-admiring networks of Zeev Jabotinsky's Irgun — described as Greater Israel fanatics obsessed with destroying the Arabs living in the vast region of "Greater Israel" — alongside Grand Mufti Haj Amin al-Husseini's demands to kill all the Jews as two halves of a single formula of geopolitical control of chaos in a strategically important area where all major civilizations intersect.[3] He links this dynamic to Nazi Germany's own pro-[[zionism|Zionist]] transfer policy under the 1933 [[haavara-agreement|Haavara Agreement]] crafted by [[adolf-eichmann|Adolf Eichmann]].[3]
