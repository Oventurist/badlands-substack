# Brooke Rollins

Brooke Rollins is the Secretary of Agriculture in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1]

## Meatpacking and national security

During the May 2026 [[beef-packer-antitrust-investigation-2026|beef packer antitrust investigation]], Rollins called foreign-owned packers such as [[jbs|JBS]] and [[national-beef|National Beef]] a national security threat amid reports of slave labor ties.[1] She said that "half of these meatpacking giants, including the largest meat packer in the world, are either foreign-owned or have significant foreign ownership and control, making them a threat not just to our cattle producers, but a threat to America itself."[1] Her [[department-of-agriculture|Department of Agriculture]] joined the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] in announcing the intensified probe.[1]

## USDA grant standardization and farmer payments

At the end of December 2025, Rollins directed all USDA agencies and staff offices to immediately adopt and implement the first-ever set of USDA General Terms and Conditions for all future awards, a step framed as strengthening national security and protecting taxpayers.[2] "Since Day One, the Trump Administration has been working to promote government efficiency, streamline unnecessary regulations, and eliminate waste, fraud, and abuse in all USDA programs," she said, describing the updating of over 100 sets of terms and conditions — some without termination clauses — as a "herculean task" arising each time a new policy or priority was announced.[2] She added that the action reduces bureaucracy and makes programs easier to access while strengthening USDA's ability to act swiftly when recipients, cooperators, and even subaward recipients and subcontractors fail to comply with federal law and applicable executive orders.[2]

The directive accompanied the release of per-acre commodity payment rates under the [[farmer-bridge-assistance-program|Farmer Bridge Assistance Program]], and on December 30, 2025, Rollins announced that regenerative farming would be among USDA's top five research and development priorities for 2026.[2]
