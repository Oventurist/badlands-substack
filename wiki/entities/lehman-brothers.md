# Lehman Brothers

Lehman Brothers was the investment bank whose collapse triggered the broader financial meltdown of 2008 and whose bankruptcy was the largest in U.S. history.[1] [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] earned more than $14 million over roughly seven years as an adviser to the firm and, after the 2008 bankruptcy, to Barclays, which took over Lehman — a chapter of his career the article says he prefers not to discuss.[1]

As the firm fell into insolvency in 2008 because of its bad bet on real estate, Bush took part in the rescue effort. The firm-wide process of raising new capital and disposing of bad balance-sheet assets was known internally as "Project Green"; Bush's portion, "Project Verde" (Spanish for green), sought to convince the Mexican billionaire [[carlos-slim|Carlos Slim]], then the world's richest man and already a client who had lost heavily as Lehman's stock collapsed, to make a potentially life-saving investment.[1] The effort failed: examiner Anton Valukas records that on 5 July 2008 Bush reported the meeting was unsuccessful because Slim "did not express interest in jv (joint venture) or stock purchase."[1]
