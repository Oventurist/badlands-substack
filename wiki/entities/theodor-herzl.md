# Theodor Herzl

Theodor Herzl is described as the founder of modern political [[zionism]], the figure who took up "the cause of convincing the world's Jews to live in the desert" within a project whose deeper architecture, in the account given by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], had already been laid down by British imperial planners decades earlier.[1]

## Israel as an "outpost of civilization"

In his book *Der Judenstaat*, Herzl wrote that a Jewish state would "form a portion of the rampart of Europe against Asia, an outpost of civilization as opposed to barbarism," remaining "as a neutral state ... in contact with all Europe, which would have to guarantee our existence."[1] The article reads this as an open admission that the projected state was conceived as a European strategic instrument rather than a purely national revival.[1]

Herzl, like what the article calls his "British Imperial (and typically antisemitic pro-fascist) sponsors," envisioned Israel's borders extending "from the Brook of Egypt to the Euphrates" — the [[greater-israel]] conception.[1] The article compares maps of Herzl's envisioned empire with maps of Babylon in 539 BCE and calls them "eerily similar."[1]

## Site selection and Blackstone

In the 1890s Herzl had not yet settled on the specific location of the Jewish national homeland.[1] William Eugene Blackstone, a devotee of [[john-nelson-darby]] and the [[plymouth-brethren]], sent Herzl a voluminous report arguing that Jerusalem was the only location ordained by God.[1]

## Question of patronage

In a footnote, the article argues Herzl "was many things, but his own man was likely not one of them," noting that his rise from low-level journalist in 1893 to leader of global Zionism within three years is unprecedented and "doesn't happen without vast institutional patronage."[1] It cites his connection from 1894 to 1904 with Colonel Goldsmid, head of London's Maccabee movement, who played a role in the Boer War alongside the new Round Table movement and oversaw the British Empire's Jewish colonial project in Argentina.[1] Such schemes — Argentina, and the Uganda plan proposed by Chamberlain and submitted by Herzl to the World Zionist Congress in 1903 — are characterized as indirect means of corralling Jews from Russia and Europe into controlled zones of British imperial domain as stepping stones toward a final Palestinian Zionist infusion.[1] The article also asserts that Herzl was himself an antisemite who "saw great practical use in antisemitism to make Europe and Russia unliveable for the Jews."[1]

The article groups Herzl with [[zeev-jabotinsky|Vladimir Jabotinsky]] and Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook as figures who may have believed they were creating a Greater Israel capital for a New World Order, in contrast to British imperialists like [[winston-churchill|Churchill]], Lord Shaftesbury and Lord Balfour who saw Zionism as an instrument of the Great Game.[1]
