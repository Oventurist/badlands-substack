# Bank for International Settlements

The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) is the international institution that serves as a bank for central banks and a coordinating body for global monetary policy. Badlands Media covered the BIS in February 2023 after its head, [[agustin-carstens|Agustin Carstens]], declared that fiat money had defeated cryptocurrency.[1]

## Carstens' declaration

In an interview with Bloomberg TV, Carstens said of the contest between crypto and state-issued money: "That battle has been won … A technology doesn't make for trusted money," adding that "only the legal, historical infrastructure behind central banks can give great credibility" to money.[1] He anticipated a "strong statement" from the G20 nations calling for stronger regulation of digital assets, and said crypto as a financial activity could exist only "under certain conditions."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that monetary policy is one of the few domains in which "a mere declaration is enough to have an effect on an entire industry," and characterized the BIS as part of a "self-declared and government endorsed international banking cartel" repeating the strategy it used before the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in 1913.[1] He contended that a long free-banking and tighter-controls period functioned as a decades-long psychological operation to strip public confidence in citizen-based currency systems, contrasting it with the debt-free and interest-free [[colonial-scrip|Colonial Scrip]] used in the American colonies.[1] In his view cryptocurrency represented a return to citizen-produced money, and the central bankers waging war on it were "winning—at least for now."[1]

## Origins as the "Central Bank of Central Banks"

The BIS was established in 1930 under the [[young-plan|Young Plan]] and was nominally steered by [[hjalmar-schacht|Hjalmar Schacht]] as a mechanism for German debt repayments arising from the First World War.[2] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that the Swiss-based "Central Bank of Central Banks" was in reality the key mechanism through which international financiers funded the Nazi machine, and that it stood under the total control of [[montagu-norman|Montagu Norman]], Governor of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]], throughout the entirety of the Second World War.[2] Dutch central banker Johan Beyen described Norman as "the apostle of central bank cooperation" who "made the central banker into a kind of arch-priest of monetary religion," concluding that "the BIS was, in fact, his creation."[2]

The founding members of the Board included the private central banks of Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Belgium, together with three private American banks — [[jp-morgan|JP Morgan]], First National of Chicago and First National of New York — which merged after the war into what are today [[citigroup|Citigroup]] and [[jpmorgan-chase|JP Morgan Chase]].[2] In its founding constitution the BIS, its directors and its staff were granted immunity from all sovereign national laws, and not even Swiss authorities were permitted to enter its premises.[2] This history was recounted in the 2013 book *Tower of Basel: The Shadowy History of the Secret Bank that Runs the World*.[2]

## Attempted dissolution and postwar survival

[[harry-dexter-white|Harry Dexter White]] led the fight to shut down the BIS at the July 1944 [[bretton-woods-conference|Bretton Woods conference]], but although his resolutions to dissolve the Bank and audit its books were passed, they were never put into action.[2] [[john-maynard-keynes|John Maynard Keynes]] defended the Bank and pushed instead to redefine the post-war system around a one-world currency called the Bancor, controlled by the Bank of England and the BIS.[2] Ehret writes that the institution that should have been dissolved in 1945 today controls the [[financial-stability-board|Financial Stability Board]] and thereby regulates the world derivatives trade, which he characterizes as a weapon of mass destruction capable of unleashing more chaos than Hitler could have dreamed.[2]
