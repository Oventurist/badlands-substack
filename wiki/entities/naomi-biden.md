# Naomi Biden

Naomi Biden is a granddaughter of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and a protectee of the [[united-states-secret-service|United States Secret Service]]. She became the focus of national news coverage in November 2023 when agents assigned to her detail discharged a firearm during an attempted break-in in Washington, D.C.[1]

## Georgetown shooting incident

Late on Sunday, November 12, 2023, Secret Service agents protecting Naomi Biden were with her in the Georgetown neighborhood of Washington when they observed three people breaking a window of a parked and unoccupied unmarked Secret Service SUV, according to a law enforcement official who spoke to the [[associated-press|Associated Press]] on condition of anonymity because the investigation could not be discussed publicly.[1] One of the agents opened fire; the Secret Service confirmed in a statement that no one was struck by the gunfire.[1] The three individuals were seen fleeing in a red car, and the agency issued a regional bulletin asking the Metropolitan Police Department to be on the lookout for the vehicle.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the episode as an indictment of both rising urban crime and federal competence, remarking that "if you're going to break into a car, you probably shouldn't pick a Secret Service vehicle" and that as crime expanded "in the lawmaking municipality, the criminals are getting dumber."[1] The commentary noted that the same reporting referenced at least three incidents in which congressional staffers had been robbed, carjacked, or stabbed, and questioned why the federal government could not better secure its own streets.[1]

Ashe in America emphasized that the agent "discharged his firearm — and missed," and that the three suspects escaped, concluding sardonically that "Naomi Biden must be feeling pretty safe at this point."[1] The broader argument drawn from the incident was a Second Amendment one: if that was the level of skill and expertise afforded to presidential family members, ordinary citizens had all the more reason to retain the right to defend themselves.[1]
