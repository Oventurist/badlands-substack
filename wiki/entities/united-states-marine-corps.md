# United States Marine Corps

The United States Marine Corps is the naval expeditionary service branch of the U.S. armed forces.[1] Its domestic employment is politically sensitive, and a June 2025 episode in California brought that sensitivity to the fore.[1]

## Prepared-to-deploy status in Los Angeles, June 2025

About 500 Marines from the Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center at Twentynine Palms were placed in a "prepared to deploy status" in June 2025, ready to assist authorities in Los Angeles as protests continued around immigration enforcement raids.[1] According to [[united-states-northern-command|U.S. Northern Command]], the Marines were held in reserve to support the Department of Defense's mission of protecting federal property and personnel, alongside roughly 2,000 federalized [[california-army-national-guard|California Army National Guard]] soldiers.[1]

Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] characterized President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threat to deploy Marines into Los Angeles as an escalation of the situation.[1]

## 2023 F-35B mishap in South Carolina

In September 2023 the Marine Corps asked for the public's help locating an [[f-35|F-35B Lightning II]] worth upwards of $80 million that went missing over South Carolina after its pilot ejected during a "mishap."[2] A Corps spokesperson said search-and-recovery efforts were ongoing and the mishap was under investigation, and the service began a recovery process after a debris field was located in Williamsburg County near [[joint-base-charleston|Joint Base Charleston]].[2] The episode is covered at [[2023-f-35b-disappearance-in-south-carolina|2023 F-35B disappearance in South Carolina]].[2]

## "The President's Fist"

Badlands contributor Joe Lange devotes a substantial portion of "[[irregular-warfare|Irregular Warfare]]" to the argument that the Marine Corps occupies a unique constitutional position as the president's own instrument.[3] Citing We Are The Mighty, he describes the Marines as the expeditionary force a leader deploys when diplomacy fails and sending the full Army is not viable — "The President's Fist."[3] Citing the U.S. Naval Institute, he notes that in 1798 Congress provided that the Corps serve not only aboard ships and in garrisons and forts but should be prepared "for any other duty on shore that the President may direct," language carried forward in the National Security Act of 1947 as "such other duties as the President may direct."[3]

Combined with the [[war-powers-act|War Powers Act]] and declared national emergencies, Lange concludes that [[donald-trump|Trump]] can direct the Marines domestically or abroad without congressional approval or a governor's consent, and that such duty is not limited to combat.[3] [[qanon|Q]] drop 14 (October 31, 2017) is cited asking what authority the president has over the Marines if Soros-funded operations turn to domestic terrorism and local officials refuse to enforce the law; Lange suggests the Marines have already acted in the background, attributing the collapse of Antifa and BLM as threats to military infiltration.[3] He further notes that [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] was a Marine officer in Vietnam and, as a retiree drawing benefits, remained subject to military jurisdiction under Article 2 of the Uniform Code of Military Justice — the basis for Lange's speculation that Mueller faced court martial.[3]

## Navajo Code Talker Program

The Marine Corps' [[navajo-code-talkers|Navajo Code Talker Program]] was established in September 1942, following a February recommendation from Philip Johnston to Major General Clayton B. Vogel, USMC, Commanding General of the Amphibious Corps, Pacific Fleet.[4] Johnston, the son of a missionary who had lived among the Navajos for twenty-four years and was fluent in the language, argued that using Navajo as a code language in radio and wire voice transmission could guarantee communications security, since the language was unwritten, unintelligible to non-speakers, and rich enough for military code words to be devised within it.[4] The Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers" presents the program as a demonstration that entire languages can be leveraged by militaries to win a contest of power projection.[4]
