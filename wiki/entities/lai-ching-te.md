# Lai Ching-te

Lai Ching-te is the president of [[taiwan|Taiwan]], described in June 2024 reporting as an "anti-Beijing hawk" who had just ascended to the office.[1]

## Context of the 2024 Taiwan tensions

Lai's inauguration coincided with heightened speculation over American commitments to the island. In a Time magazine interview published in early June 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said he was "not ruling out" placing US boots on the ground in Taiwan in the event of a future Chinese invasion, adding that any response "would depend on the circumstances" and that the United States was "continuing to supply capacity" while consulting allies in the region.[1] Biden said other options were on the table that he could not disclose, remarking that "you would then criticize me with good reason if I were to tell you."[1] Commentators noted that such hypothetical answers from American politicians were nothing new, but that they landed at a tense moment given Lai's ascent.[1]

## Meeting with Japanese lawmakers, December 2025

In December 2025 Lai met with [[koichi-hagiuda|Koichi Hagiuda]], a senior figure in Japan's ruling [[liberal-democratic-party|Liberal Democratic Party]], as part of a wave of visits to Taiwan by Japanese lawmakers.[2] Hagiuda described Tokyo–Taipei relations as "the best they have ever been," according to a statement from Taiwan's Presidential Office, and Lai said he hoped to further deepen cooperation and that the two sides could come together to advance a "free and open Indo-Pacific."[2] The visits came amid heightened tensions between Japan and [[china|China]] following Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]]'s statement that Japan's military could theoretically be deployed if China attacked Taiwan, which drew Chinese retaliation including limits on tourist travel to Japan.[2]

## December 2024 Pacific transit and Chinese response

In early December 2024 Lai made unofficial stops in Hawaii and the U.S. territory of Guam, sparking Beijing's ire and putting Taiwan on high alert from Monday, December 9 as it braced for expected Chinese military drills.[4] Taipei subsequently reported multiple formations of Chinese naval and coast guard vessels in regional waters and around the Taiwan Strait, which its Defense Ministry described as China's largest regional maritime deployment in decades, though Beijing neither announced drills nor acknowledged the deployment.[4]
