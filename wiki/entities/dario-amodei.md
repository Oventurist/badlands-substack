# Dario Amodei

Dario Amodei is the chief executive of [[anthropic|Anthropic]] and, alongside [[openai|OpenAI]]'s [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]], one of the two frontier-laboratory leaders most closely identified with the push for government regulation of advanced artificial intelligence.[1]

## Role in the open-source AI dispute

Anthropic under Amodei has generally supported stronger safeguards for frontier AI systems, a posture that placed the company in direct conflict with advocates of [[open-source-artificial-intelligence|open-source and open-weight models]] during the July 2026 policy fight.[1] Open-source advocates, including venture capitalist [[chamath-palihapitiya|Chamath Palihapitiya]] and members of the Trump administration's Innovation Council, argued that excessive regulation would entrench incumbent firms such as Anthropic and reduce America's ability to compete globally against China.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued on the Devolution Power Hour that the concerted effort by Amodei and Altman to obtain government regulation of a nascent and rapidly accelerating industry was not an attempt to supercharge an American Age of Abundance but to slow and control it, placing the technology in "collectivist, oligarchic hands."[1] In this framing, Amodei and Altman revealed themselves as retardants rather than accelerants to the AI agenda — a reading the commentator said was corroborated when former White House AI and Crypto Czar [[david-sacks|David Sacks]] stated that the two companies sought regulation to stave off competitors.[1]

## Leaked memo and clash with the Department of War (March 2026)

A leaked internal memo of roughly 1,600 words that Amodei wrote to [[anthropic|Anthropic]] staff escalated the company's public dispute with the US [[department-of-war|Department of War]] in March 2026, laying out objections to certain military uses of its AI systems.[2] Amodei had established "red lines" barring applications such as autonomous weapons and certain surveillance functions, positions the memo described as points of disagreement with the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] over a defense contract worth roughly $200 million.[2]

After the dispute became public, the DoW designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk," and Pentagon officials allegedly threatened to cancel the contract if the restrictions stood.[2] Amodei subsequently said the company was attempting to "de-escalate" the dispute while continuing discussions about its participation in government AI programs.[2]
