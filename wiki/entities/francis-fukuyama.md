# Francis Fukuyama

Francis Fukuyama is described in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as a neo-conservative and trans-humanist author whose writings supplied the intellectual charter for the strategic doctrine known as "The End of History."[1] According to that account, Fukuyama asserted that the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1992 meant that the bi-polar age which had characterized the Cold War had been replaced by a new age of unipolar management of the earth by an un-elected elite masterclass.[1]

## The End of History as a governing agenda

Ehret's treatment presents "The End of History" less as an academic thesis than as an operating agenda that defined the entire globe from 1992 until 2013.[1] In this reading, the doctrine's practical content was the retirement of the sovereign nation state as the primary unit of decision-making, and its replacement by a trans-national managerial layer operating through both left and right mainstream political parties across the Trans-Atlantic world.[1] Ehret identifies these currents as, on the left, Fabian and Rhodes Scholar networks, and on the right, neo-conservative circles he characterizes as Trotskyist or Straussian in derivation, both of them adept at using "noble lies" and psychological warfare to keep populations fearful, ignorant and complacent.[1]

## Relationship to Brzezinski

Fukuyama is identified as a close friend of [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]], whom Ehret calls the "neo-con whisperer" and who had defined the same technocratic elite two decades earlier in his 1970 manifesto *Between Two Ages: America's Role in the Technetronic Age*.[1] In Ehret's chronology the Fukuyama thesis is therefore a restatement, in post-Cold War language, of a program already articulated by Brzezinski.[1]

## Claimed end of the doctrine

Ehret dates the practical breakdown of the End of History framework to 2013, when China and Russia are said to have gained sufficient strength to refuse the depopulation agenda and to begin building alternative institutions such as the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]] and the [[brics|BRICS]] grouping.[1]
