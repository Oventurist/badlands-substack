# Allied Democratic Forces

The **Allied Democratic Forces (ADF)** is an Islamist armed group operating in the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] and identified as an affiliate of the [[isis|Islamic State]].[1] The group has been responsible for repeated mass-casualty attacks on civilian populations in the Congolese provinces bordering Uganda, and its violence forms one strand of the broader security collapse in the eastern DRC.[1]

## Lubero massacre (February 2025)

On 13 February 2025, ADF militants beheaded seventy Christians with machetes in the Lubero district of the eastern DRC, according to Open Doors, an organization that monitors terrorism and religious persecution.[1] The victims were rounded up from their homes and executed inside a Protestant church in Kasanga.[1] Witnesses said the rebels shouted "Get out, get out" as they forced residents from their houses, and many Christians fled the region afterward in search of safety.[1]

Muhindo Musunzi, director of the Kombo Primary School, stated that churches, schools, and health centers in the area had already closed because of the deteriorating security situation, and that "we had to move all activities towards Vunying."[1] Families were unable to recover or bury their dead for several days because of ongoing insecurity.[1] A church elder from the CECA20 church described the community's despair, saying "we don't know what to do or how to pray; we've had enough of massacres. May God's will alone be done."[1]

## Coverage and commentary

Badlands Media noted that despite the scale of the atrocity, the massacre received very little international media attention, with Newsweek among the few outlets to report it.[1] Commentator Ashe in America argued that the absence of coverage was itself suggestive — that the event likely happened precisely because it "doesn't fit anyone's narrative," in contrast to the several competing narratives circulating about the DRC at the time.[1] She framed the killings within the broader theme of Christian persecution, citing Matthew 24:9, and warned against the assumption that such persecution would remain confined to distant regions.[1]
