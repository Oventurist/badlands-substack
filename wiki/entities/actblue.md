# ActBlue

ActBlue is an online political fundraising platform launched in 2004 to channel small-dollar grassroots donations to Democratic candidates and progressive causes. It has become the dominant conduit for Democratic online fundraising and, since 2024, the subject of sustained congressional investigation into its donor-verification practices.

## Congressional scrutiny

[[badlands-brief-029|The June 12, 2026 Badlands Brief]] reports that scrutiny of ActBlue and its Republican counterpart [[winred|WinRed]] intensified that week as House Republicans held a hearing examining allegations of fraud, foreign contributions, and weaknesses in donor-verification systems. ActBlue chief executive [[regina-wallace-jones|Regina Wallace-Jones]] repeatedly invoked her Fifth Amendment rights when questioned. Democrats on the panel argued that Republicans were using the hearing to target Democratic fundraising operations and noted that similar concerns had been raised about WinRed; Republicans said questions remained about suspicious donation patterns, identity verification, and the possible use of straw donors or foreign funds. Reporting cited by the Brief noted that the hearing produced no new evidence of criminal wrongdoing by either platform, but that the investigations would continue into the 2026 midterms.

The Brief recalls an April 2025 House Republican staff report which stated that ActBlue changed its fraud policy twice during the 2024 campaign, adopting "more lenient" standards that allowed foreign and other suspicious donor money to flow into US campaigns, and which announced that investigators would seek transcribed interviews with ActBlue employees responsible for fraud prevention and review suspicious activity reports.

## Badlands interpretation

The Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argues that Democrats "harnessed the power of 'grassroots donors' through a meeting of the minds to engage in a highly coordinated portfolio of crime," and, crediting the fraud investigator Peter Bernegger, lists statutes she believes may have been violated: money laundering (18 U.S.C. § 1956), monetary transactions in criminally derived property (§ 1957), false statements (§ 1001), contributions in the name of another (52 U.S.C. § 30122), wire fraud (§ 1343), mail fraud (§ 1341), conspiracy to defraud the United States (§ 371), identity and document fraud (§ 1028), the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (§ 1030), the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809), and the Bank Secrecy Act (31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq.), together with state analogues. She asserts that ActBlue "is now being prosecuted — which is why Wallace Jones pled the fifth," and rejects the defences that such practices are simply how campaign finance now works or that both parties do the same, concluding: "Prosecute every one of them."
