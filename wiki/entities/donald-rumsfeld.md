# Donald Rumsfeld

Donald Rumsfeld is a former U.S. Secretary of Defense listed by Badlands Media among the members of the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]].[1]

Rumsfeld appears in the article's roster of PNAC figures alongside [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], [[paul-wolfowitz|Paul Wolfowitz]], and [[john-bolton|John Bolton]], drawn from the neoconservative milieu of the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]] and organized by [[william-kristol|William Kristol]] and [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]].[1] The article connects that circle to the post-9/11 [[pentagon|Pentagon]] planning described by General Wesley Clark, which reportedly envisioned taking out seven countries in five years beginning with Iraq and Syria.[1]

## Missing Pentagon trillions

On September 10, 2001, the day before the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]], Rumsfeld famously announced that the [[pentagon]] was missing $2.3 trillion from its books.2 Badlands Media notes that the Pentagon comptroller who would have had control over those funds at the time was [[dov-zakheim|Dov Zakheim]], a rabbi who had worked in the Pentagon during [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] and who later ran a firm manufacturing equipment to remotely control aircraft.2

Rumsfeld is also listed among the neoconservative figures associated with the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for a New American Century]], whose document "Rebuilding America's Defences" anticipated a "catastrophic and catalyzing event" akin to a "new Pearl Harbor."2

## Trump Doctrine: Peace Through Space

"[[trump-doctrine-peace-through-space|Trump Doctrine: Peace Through Space]]" cites Rumsfeld's statement of September 10, 2001 that "We cannot track $2.3 Trillion," pairing it with [[government-accountability-office|GAO]] findings on unauditable [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] financial statements to argue that defense spending is effectively opaque.[4]

## G.D. Searle and aspartame approval

Badlands Media's survey of food additives describes Rumsfeld as an establishment Republican and American businessman who served as president of [[gd-searle|G.D. Searle]], the company that originally held the patent on [[aspartame]].[3] After a 1980 [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] Board of Inquiry of three independent scientists confirmed the sweetener "might induce brain tumors" and the agency banned it, Rumsfeld is said to have vowed that he would "call in his markers" to get aspartame approved.[3] The approval followed, and [[monsanto|Monsanto]] later bought out Searle.[3]
