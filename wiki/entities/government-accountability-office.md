# Government Accountability Office

The Government Accountability Office (GAO) is the audit and investigative arm of the United States Congress, responsible for reviewing how federal agencies spend appropriated funds.[1]

## Oversight of Ukraine aid

In March 2024, the GAO reported on the lack of oversight over funds and materiel flowing to Ukraine during the war, finding that the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] lacked the ability to properly account for the resources being sent.[1] The GAO wrote that "DOD does not have quality data to track delivery of defense articles to Ukraine" and that "DOD guidance on PDA does not clearly define at what point in the delivery process defense articles should be recorded as delivered or provide clear instructions for how DOD service branches are to confirm delivery."[1] The office added that full documentation of funding sent to the military effort had been lacking.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] paired the GAO's findings with Ukrainian reporting on diverted fortification funds in Kharkiv, asking whether the Pentagon genuinely lacked delivery data or was simply declining to share it — a question he tied to speculation about [[continuity-of-government|continuity of government]] operations.[1]

## 2025 IT modernization report and DOGE

In January 2025, as [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] began carrying out its mandate to modernize federal information technology and identify inefficiencies, the GAO published a report that observers described as a playbook for where the newly revamped White House office could start.[2] The report found that 463 of the office's 1,881 IT-related recommendations to the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] and to agencies since 2010 had not been addressed, creating challenges for the federal government in "effectively acquiring IT and managing IT projects."[2] It added that 32 of its 69 priority recommendations for government IT over the same period had likewise not been enacted, and estimated that full implementation of the outstanding recommendations could "potentially achieve hundreds of millions in savings."[2]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] quoted the office's self-description as "an independent, non-partisan agency within the legislative branch that works for Congress" whose core mission is "to help Congress and federal agencies ensure that taxpayer dollars are spent effectively and efficiently," then asked how its staff still had jobs.[2] She argued that the GAO, established in 1921, had for 103 years done the exact opposite of that mission and, given its failure to achieve the objectives for which it was created, should sit the modernization effort out — "Actually, they should have been fired first."[2]
