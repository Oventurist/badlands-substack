# John Brennan

John Brennan served as President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s top counter-terrorism adviser, or "anti-terror czar," during the 2012 Benghazi attack, and later became Director of the [[cia|CIA]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Nomination and White House role

Obama initially nominated Brennan to head the CIA on taking office, but Brennan withdrew after congressional criticism over his involvement in the CIA rendition and torture programs; his White House counterterrorism post nonetheless gave him substantial power.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Brennan ran the drone strike program from the White House and, in 2010, announced the administration would use a "scalpel" instead of a "hammer" against terrorists — a "Drone Doctrine" that Investors Business Daily judged to have failed to eradicate al-Qaida.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article emphasizes that Brennan sat at the top of the chain of command that required White House approval for battlefield action, which it argues hampered the military and allowed [[isis|ISIS]] to expand.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Benghazi and the cover-up

Investors Business Daily noted that Brennan was visible on television after the bin Laden raid but silent twenty days after Benghazi, even as the secretaries of state and defense conceded the attack was terrorism.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article contends Brennan, as anti-terror czar, would necessarily have known Al Qaeda was responsible for killing Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Brennan became CIA director only months after the attack, which the article frames as a reward for his role in the cover-up.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Testifying before Congress, and pressed by Rep. Devin Nunes of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, Brennan said security operators involved in Benghazi rescue efforts were required to re-sign [[non-disclosure-agreements|non-disclosure agreements]] because the documents were being updated.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The Weekly Standard reported the NDAs were presented at a May memorial service at CIA headquarters in Langley honoring [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]] and [[glen-doherty|Glen Doherty]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article summarizes Brennan's posture with the CIA maxim "admit nothing and deny everything," and notes he is one of three figures — with Obama and Valerie Jarrett — described in a Q drop as having a Muslim background.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## FBI criminal probe (April 2026)

In April 2026 the [[fbi|FBI]] was reported to be preparing to question several witnesses in the criminal investigation into Brennan, the former [[cia|CIA]] director, focusing on the 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] and how it was developed.[2] Investigators were expected to interview a small group of former intelligence officials involved in drafting the assessment and to examine the origins of the inquiry.[2] The probe, led by the US Attorney's Office in Miami, had been underway for months and had already included initial witness interviews.[2]

The investigation centered in part on Brennan's 2023 congressional testimony about the creation of the assessment, and prosecutors had previously informed him that he was a target of the inquiry, according to a letter from his attorney.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that after Brennan is prosecuted over the 2017 ICA, the record should also be revisited on unauthorized drone strikes and the many occasions on which he allegedly lied to [[congress|Congress]], arguing that the weaponized agencies running illegal psychological operations against Americans were most likely operating under Brennan's authority and direction.[2] She cautioned that preparing to interview witnesses is not itself accountability, but is a necessary step of the kind expected if accountability were coming.[2]

## DOJ request for House Intelligence Committee records (March 2026)

In March 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] requested records from the [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]] tied to Brennan as part of a reported criminal inquiry.[3] Punchbowl noted that Brennan, CIA director from 2013 to 2017, set up the counterintelligence investigation into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2016, and that Trump stripped him of his security clearance in 2018 and again in 2025.[3] The request centered on documents generated or held by the committee during prior intelligence oversight activities, and was described as a "rare instance" of the executive branch seeking access to legislative branch materials in a prosecutorial context.[3] No charges had been announced and the scope of the inquiry remained unclear, though observers flagged possible separation-of-powers disputes over compliance.[3]

The committee's vote to cooperate split along party lines, with Democrats objecting that they had received the material only days earlier and lacked time to review it.[3] Badlands framed the development as a marker of how far accountability efforts had advanced: two years earlier Brennan had been treated as untouchable and celebrated by legacy media as an American hero, whereas by March 2026 he was under investigation and, in the outlet's telling, unable to stop it.[3]

## Criminal investigation leadership change (2026)

In April 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] selected [[joseph-digenova|Joseph DiGenova]], who previously represented President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in challenges to the 2020 election, to take over the criminal investigation into Brennan.[4] The appointment followed the removal of prosecutor Maria Medetis Long and placed DiGenova in a senior advisory role to acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]], overseeing the case out of the Southern District of Florida — the district of U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon.[4] Chris DeLorenz, a former law clerk to Judge Cannon during the classified documents probe, also joined the case.[4]

## April 2026: Senate records dispute

The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] requested transcripts and records from two Senate committees involving Brennan in his capacity as former [[cia|CIA]] Director, setting a February 23, 2026 deadline for compliance.[5] As of the following Thursday night the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] had not transmitted the materials, a delay reported to be affecting part of an investigation based in Fort Pierce, Florida.[5] Aides to Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] said he was seeking unanimous consent to release the records while discussing the matter with Democrats to avoid a floor vote, and prosecutors were reportedly allowed to review one Senate Intelligence Committee report in a secure room.[5]

## February 2026: expanded subpoenas and target status

In February 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] issued additional subpoenas in the [[florida-criminal-probe-of-2016-russia-assessment|Florida criminal probe of the 2016 Russia assessment]], seeking documents from the years following the January 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|intelligence community assessment]] that concluded Russia interfered in the presidential race to help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defeat [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[6] Brennan, who played a central role in producing that report, has been told he is a target of the investigation.[6] His attorneys, in a December letter to the chief judge of the Southern District of Florida, labeled the probe "manufactured" and said it remained unclear what potential crimes prosecutors were investigating.[6] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Brennan has held positions of influence or authority continuously since 9/11 and argued that acts furthering the alleged conspiracy after officials knew it lacked grounding in fact or law would amount to sedition or treason.[6]

## FBI interviews of CIA officers (May 2026)

By May 2026 the [[fbi|FBI]] had begun interviewing current and former [[cia|CIA]] officers as part of the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] investigation into Brennan's role in the [[2017-intelligence-assessment-on-russian-interference|2017 intelligence assessment]] on Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. election.[7] Agents from the Miami field office questioned about a dozen CIA employees who worked on the assessment at CIA headquarters in McLean, Virginia, and more interviews were expected in the following weeks.[7] The questioning focused on Brennan's involvement in producing the assessment and the extent to which its conclusions were shaped by the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]].[7]

The probe examines whether Brennan made false statements to [[congress|Congress]] in 2023 testimony about the assessment, which concluded that [[russia|Russia]] interfered to help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defeat [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[7] Brennan has said the CIA opposed including the dossier in the assessment and that it was added only as a compromise with the FBI.[7] The investigation was referred to the DOJ by Republican Representative [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] in October and is overseen by [[joe-digenova|Joe DiGenova]] after the previous lead prosecutor was removed.[7] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the interrogations encouraging and described Brennan's "alleged portfolio of treason" as extensive and spanning decades, framing Russiagate as a cover-up.[7]

## Brennan briefing memo in Durham's testimony (2023)

Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] told the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on June 21, 2023 that a referral memorandum containing intelligence from a high-level briefing with Brennan, then Director of the [[cia|CIA]], had been withheld by [[james-comey|Jim Comey]] from the agents working the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] case.[8] The intelligence concerned [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s attempts to tie [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Russia.[8]


## Ratcliffe criminal referral (July 2025)

In July 2025 CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] made a criminal referral to FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] concerning possible criminality by Brennan, following an internal CIA review released the same week that critiqued his actions relating to the Trump–Russia collusion investigation.[9] Because Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] had earlier passed on prosecuting Brennan and a host of other key [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] figures, the decision on whether to charge the former Obama spy chief fell to Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] she oversees.[9]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the suggestion that the administration was amplifying Russiagate to distract from the Epstein controversy, arguing that Russiagate "gets you all of it" because it involves the same conspirators, black-and-white treason, and leaked evidence of a meeting of the minds spanning a decade-plus conspiracy with multiple overt acts.[9] She noted that Patel had written a book focused heavily on Russiagate and that [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] had produced hundreds of hours of content on it, and that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had never retracted his claim that the conspirators were all caught.[9]

## The July 2016 collection and the 2025 tradecraft review

The July 2025 release by CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] of a declassified tradecraft review of the post-2016 Intelligence Community Assessment renewed attention on Brennan's role.[10] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] said the still-classified report referenced in the review was the one Brennan "collected in July" 2016 and kept highly classified throughout, and argued its release would be enlightening.[10] Herold placed it alongside two dates: July 26, 2016, when [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] is alleged to have approved a plan to vilify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and July 28, 2016, when Brennan first met [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] to discuss "Russian malign influence" and create the inter-agency Fusion Cell.[10]

## Role in the 2016 Obama briefing timeline

Commentary in the Badlands News Brief laid out a compressed 2016 timeline in which Brennan figures centrally: on July 26, 2016, [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] approved the plan to vilify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; on July 27, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] gave a DNC speech saying Trump "cozies up to Putin," with his return to the White House absent from his daily schedule; and on July 28, Brennan briefed Obama about the Clinton plan and the Fusion Cell was created.[11] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] raised the question of why Obama would assert in his speech that Trump cozied up to [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] before being briefed on the intelligence by Brennan.[11]

## Miami grand jury target (December 2025)

Lawyers for Brennan confirmed on Monday, December 22, 2025 that federal prosecutors had identified him as a target of a criminal grand jury investigation in Miami, and asked the chief federal judge to intervene, accusing the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] of attempting to steer the case to a favored judge.[12] In a letter to U.S. District Judge [[cecilia-altonaga|Cecilia M. Altonaga]], Brennan's attorneys said U.S. Attorney [[jason-reding-quinones|Jason Reding Quinones]] informed them the grand jury was examining Brennan's role in the [[2017-intelligence-assessment-on-russian-interference|2017 Intelligence Community Assessment]] on alleged Russian interference in the 2016 election.[12]

The probe centers on the assessment's claim that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] sought to help President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a conclusion later challenged by whistleblower evidence released by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]], which showed internal dissent and questioned the report's rushed release.[12] The disclosures also raised concerns about Brennan's push to include the discredited [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] in the assessment, prompting Ratcliffe and Gabbard to refer him for possible prosecution.[12]

Brennan's lawyers alleged the investigation was politically driven and claimed prosecutors were manipulating grand jury and case assignment procedures to ensure the matter would be overseen by U.S. District Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]], citing alleged leaks that violate grand jury secrecy rules.[12]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that based on information available in the public domain, Brennan appeared to have been not just involved in but one of the primary decision makers in the "Russia Gate Hoax" — a label she called "too cutesy for the magnitude of what occurred," describing oath-swearers actively subverting the President of the United States, "enemies inside the gates, in positions of trust, with the highest security clearance."[12]

## Crossfire Hurricane declassification letter (2023)

In June 2023, the publication of a January 19, 2021 letter signed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was reported as having declassified the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] documents, described by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] as evidence that Brennan, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], [[james-comey|James Comey]], [[james-clapper|James Clapper]], the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[state-department|State Department]] had spied on Trump and knew the collusion narrative was a scam.[13]

## Ratcliffe perjury allegation (July 2025)

Speaking to Maria Bartiromo on Fox News on July 28, 2025, [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] alleged that Brennan — [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s CIA director — along with former [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], had lied under oath about their involvement in the Russian election-interference narrative, and confirmed that he had made criminal referrals on that basis.[14] Ratcliffe characterized the underlying operation as "a Hillary Clinton campaign scheme" centered on the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[14]


## New York Times op-ed with Clapper (July 2025)

On July 30, 2025, Brennan and [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] published a New York Times op-ed defending the intelligence community's 2016 Russia findings against claims by DNI [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and CIA Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] that Obama administration officials manufactured politicized intelligence, silenced intelligence professionals and engaged in a "treasonous conspiracy" against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; the two called those claims "patently false."[15] They restated the January 2017 assessment's conclusions that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] personally ordered an influence campaign to damage [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and that Russia developed a "clear preference" for Trump, using hacked Democratic emails, state-funded media, intermediaries and paid social media users, and argued that multiple yearslong reviews validated the assessment.[15]

Badlands commentary framed the op-ed as damage control published one day before the declassification of the [[durham-annex|Durham annex]], and pointed to accompanying signs of what it described as regime panic, including Marc Elias urging the media not to cover the scandal and Chris Cuomo distancing himself with admissions that he had been misled.[15]

## Alleged protection of Hezbollah (Badlands analysis)

Badlands contributor Joe Lange writes that [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] was a major worldwide drug trafficker protected by the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration after its connections to cartels in Mexico and Colombia were discovered, and identifies John Brennan, as Obama's [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] director, as one of the group's biggest defenders during that administration.[16] Lange flags this as an important point and states that Brennan will re-emerge in a significant way later in his article series.[16]
