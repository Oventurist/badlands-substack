# Al-Nusra Front

Jabhat al-Nusra, or the Al-Nusra Front, was an Al-Qaeda affiliate operating in Syria during the Syrian civil war, described in the Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" as a brutal and bloodthirsty organization.[1]

## Relationship to the Islamic State of Iraq

In April 2013 [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]], leader of the [[islamic-state-of-iraq|Islamic State of Iraq]], released a recorded audio message announcing that Jabhat al-Nusra was an extension of Al-Qaeda in Iraq operating inside Syria.[1] Al-Baghdadi stated that al-Nusra's leader, Abu Mohammad al-Jawlani, had been dispatched to Syria along with a group of men to link up with pre-existing cells, and that the Islamic State of Iraq had supplied al-Nusra with the plans and strategy for the Syrian conflict as well as funding on a monthly basis.[1]

In May 2013 al-Baghdadi travelled from Iraq to Syria's Aleppo Governorate and began attempting to take over the leadership of al-Nusra.[1] Media reports described a resulting split, with many of al-Nusra's foreign fighters operating under the banner of the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant while many Syrian Nusra fighters left to join other Islamist brigades.[1] The same month, masked men identifying themselves as members of the Islamic State of Iraq and al-Sham publicly executed three captured Alawite officers in the eastern town of Raqqa.[1]

## Significance in the article

The timeline matters to the article's central claim: al-Baghdadi was physically in Syria consolidating control over al-Nusra in the same month that Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] made his May 28, 2013 trip to meet purported rebel leaders, which the author treats as more than coincidence.[1] The question of where al-Baghdadi obtained the money he was funnelling to al-Nusra is answered, in the author's telling, by McCain's visit.[1]
