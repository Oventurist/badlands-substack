# Johnny Q

Johnny Q is a [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] host who co-presented [[sauce-and-effect|Sauce and Effect]] with [[woke-societies|Woke Societies]] on Monday evenings as of the January 2023 schedule.[1]

A second Badlands Media schedule post, published January 3, 2023 to open the 2023 broadcast year, carried the same listing and description, and reiterated that all network shows air live on the Patel Patriot Rumble channel with replays archived on the Badlands Media library channel.[2]

## News Brief commentary

Johnny Q contributed commentary to the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of May 9, 2023, responding to a segment of [[the-view|The View]] in which co-host [[ana-navarro|Ana Navarro]] argued that Hispanic and Black individuals can also be white supremacists while discussing the [[allen-texas-mall-shooting|Allen, Texas mall shooting]].[3] He called the exchange "cackling from the hen-house" that reached "all new levels of ridiculous and absurd," and wrote that under such reasoning having "morals, principles, and values" could brand a person of any ethnicity a white supremacist.[3] Johnny Q accused the program of amplifying "woke virtue signaling" and of exploiting a mass-casualty event, argued that the left had "become the Marxist monsters they claim the right and conservatives are," and urged Americans to reject what he described as an effort to subvert constitutional freedoms.[3]

## March 2023 commentary

In the Badlands News Brief covering Monday, March 13, 2023, Johnny Q contributed two segments.[4] On the case of Arizona rancher [[george-alan-kelly|George Alan Kelly]], he questioned how [[united-states-border-patrol|Border Patrol]] agents could speculate about a caller's mental status without the proper facts, or ignore that the man faced a lethal threat on his own property.[4] He argued it was worse still that Kelly was being charged as though he were the criminal for defending himself, describing a justice system that protects perpetrators and leaves law-abiding citizens legally unable to defend themselves in life-or-death situations.[4] He added that such citizens are placed in a precarious position they did not ask for and should have every right to stand their ground, and attributed the degeneration of the legal system to design, funded by district attorneys and judges corrupted by domestic and foreign lobbyists.[4]

In a second segment, on a test question removed by [[fairfax-county-public-schools|Fairfax County Public Schools]] that equated political ideology with race and gender, he argued that such material was holding the United States back in global education rankings and that [[critical-race-theory|CRT]], equity and inclusion programming divided children rather than unifying them or teaching necessary life skills.[4] He concluded that parents, not the school system, should be the arbiters of their children's education.[4]

## Commentary on the Oak Fire arson arrest

In the June 20, 2023 Badlands News Brief, Johnny Q wrote on the arrest of [[edward-hackerman|Edward Hackerman]], a retired firefighter and donor to Democratic campaigns and the [[lincoln-project|Lincoln Project]], for allegedly starting the [[oak-fire|Oak Fire]] in California.[5] He described Hackerman as a "mostly peaceful Democrat donor" and argued that identical conduct by a Republican would have been labeled a domestic terrorism case, questioning whether Hackerman would be sentenced severely or "let off the hook" because "justice seems to swing in favor of being politically correct."[5] Johnny Q added that the destruction of the majestic land was the real tragedy and noted the irony that the fire had first been blamed on climate change.[5]

## Commentary on Twitter and the EU (2023)

In the June 6, 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], Johnny Q welcomed [[twitter|Twitter]]'s withdrawal from the [[eu-code-of-practice-on-disinformation|EU Code of Practice on Disinformation]], describing it as a step away from "Orwellian, 1984-like censorship" and arguing that free debate is necessary and healthy for society.[6] He characterized [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]]'s enforcement warning as a display of fear and said the [[european-union|EU]] was underestimating both European citizens and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], concluding: "Bravo, Twitter. This is the way."[6]

## Commentary, January 2023

With [[absolute-1776|Absolute1776]], Johnny Q wrote in the January 20, 2023 Badlands News Brief on [[ivan-provorov|Ivan Provorov]]'s refusal to wear a pride jersey for the [[philadelphia-flyers|Philadelphia Flyers]], arguing that America was tired of the woke agenda and that public support for the player showed the silent majority becoming loud.[7] He described the episode as [[cancel-culture|cancel culture]] targeting religious conviction and warned that the [[national-hockey-league|NHL]] risked the fate of the NFL and MLB by turning sport from an escape into a virtue-signalling tool.[7]

## Commentary on the Rushingbrook Children's Choir incident (2023)

In the June 8, 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] brief, Johnny Q wrote that "patriotism was likened to terrorism for young kids who just wanted to show respect for our Country" after [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] stopped the [[rushingbrook-childrens-choir|Rushingbrook Children's Choir]] from singing the national anthem in the Capitol.[8] He praised [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for denouncing the episode, called the Capitol Police account absurd and loaded with contradictions, and argued the refusal was contrived over political viewpoints and predetermined.[8] He contended that songs about gender equality or transgender rights would have drawn no objection, and that questioning such ideologies would have brought accusations of bigotry.[8]

## Commentary on consumer boycotts (June 2023)

In June 2023, Johnny Q argued that the surefire way for people to protest is with their purchasing power, or the choice not to exercise it, and offered reporting on the anti-[[esg|ESG]] revolt as proof that the movement was having an impact.[9] He cited [[bud-light|Bud Light]], [[target|Target]] and Kohl's as brands made to bear the example of backlash, drawing ire from parents over what he described as the hypersexualization pushed on children.[9]

He characterized woke indoctrination, political polarization and virtue signaling as beginning to fail, describing the phenomenon as "cancel culture cancels itself" and as the most effective tool against itself — in his framing the best way to defeat Marxism and a sign of a collective awakening.[9] He urged readers not to forget the power of boycotting and to relearn its use in what he called the information war of Good versus Evil.[9]
