# Melania Trump

Melania Trump is the First Lady of the United States and wife of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

In January 2026 the Office of the First Lady posted on social media that she encouraged people to "use AI to unlock new parts of your imagination" while warning against allowing [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] to replace personal thinking, urging users to remain creative and thoughtful and not to "surrender your thinking to AI."[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] endorsed the message, framing AI as a tool to unleash rather than outsource critical thinking: "Use the tool. Don't be used by the tool."[1]

## Security concerns and the *Melania* film (February 2026)

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote in February 2026 that the documentary film *Melania* makes "authentically, abundantly clear" that the First Lady has serious safety concerns about her husband and family, dedicating significant time to her anxiety about 2025 Inauguration Day and her statement that Barron would not get out of the car if the parade were held outside — the inaugural parade was ultimately held indoors.[2] The commentary used this to push back on "full control" theories, asking why the First Family would have produced a fabricated intimate portrait, and argued that keeping the public in a state of suspended trust would resemble the destabilization operations of the three-letter agencies rather than the promised Golden Age of accountability.[2]

Following the February 22, 2026 armed breach at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]], [[donald-trump|President Trump]] publicly addressed the threats against him with a remark directed at Melania: "I don't know how long I'll be around, got a lot of people gunning for me, don't I, hon?"[2] Ashe in America described the line as heartbreaking and called on readers to pray for the President's ongoing protection.[2]

## Open channel with Vladimir Putin on displaced Ukrainian children (October 2025)

In October 2025 the First Lady announced from the Blue Room of the [[white-house|White House]] that she and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had maintained an "open channel of communication" concerning children impacted by the Russia-Ukraine war.[4] She said that her personal representative had been working directly with the Russian president, and emphasized that eight children had already been returned to their families in the preceding 24 hours.[4] "We must foster a future for our children which is rich with potential, security, and complete with free will. A world where dreams will be realized rather than faded by war," she said.[4] The initiative followed a letter she shared in August 2025 addressed to Putin calling for peace and urging him to think of the children affected by the war.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described her as "the angel on the battlefield" and argued that she was "all but disclosing the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] in this moment."[4] He tied the story to long-circulating claims about child-trafficking rings in Ukraine and to a 2022 video in which a visibly distressed Russian soldier described what was allegedly found in the city of Izyum, framing the conflict as a war against the devil in which the Sovereign Alliance fights for the spiritual as well as political freedom of humanity.[4]

## Sanctions motion against Michael Wolff (July 2026)

On Tuesday, July 21, 2026, the First Lady filed a motion for sanctions against journalist [[michael-wolff|Michael Wolff]] after he unsuccessfully sued her in an attempt to block her $1 billion defamation lawsuit.[3] Wolff had claimed that Melania Trump was heavily involved in the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] scandal; her team sent a pre-action notice demanding a retraction, which Wolff ignored in favor of filing his own suit.[3] A federal judge dismissed Wolff's case earlier in the year, calling it a "contorted" effort and "not how the federal courts work."[3]

Her legal team argued that sanctions were warranted because Wolff manufactured a jurisdictional dispute to keep the case in New York state court, used litigation for improper purposes, and made frivolous claims lacking evidentiary support, and sought costs and reasonable attorneys' fees on the grounds that the suit was a fundraising drive and political spectacle.[3] The filing stated that "Plaintiff and his counsel asserted a legally frivolous claim, propped it up with factual contentions that lacked any evidentiary support, and used them to run a political and commercial campaign."[3] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] treated the case on The Daily Herold as a preview of the future of frivolous lawfare, and suggested the First Lady may be offering a playbook for punishing those who weaponize the courts.[3]

## Advocacy for the Take It Down Act (March 2025)

In early March 2025, First Lady Melania Trump spoke in support of the [[take-it-down-act|Take It Down Act]], legislation to protect Americans against the nonconsensual sharing of intimate images, including deepfakes.[5]
