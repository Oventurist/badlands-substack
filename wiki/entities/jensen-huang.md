# Jensen Huang

Jensen Huang is the chief executive officer of [[nvidia|Nvidia]], the semiconductor company whose accelerators underpin most large-scale artificial intelligence training, and a prominent voice against restrictive AI regulation in the United States.[1]

## Position in the open-source AI debate

In July 2026 Huang warned policymakers against allowing fear-driven regulation to slow American innovation, arguing that the United States must remain globally competitive rather than handicap its own AI ecosystem.[1] His intervention came amid a widening Washington debate over whether the US should embrace [[open-source-artificial-intelligence|open-source artificial intelligence]] to compete with China or restrict it on national-security grounds.[1]

The debate had sharpened as Chinese firms released increasingly capable open-weight models, while major American developers including [[openai|OpenAI]] and [[anthropic|Anthropic]] urged policymakers to scrutinize or restrict Chinese open-weight systems.[1] Critics of that approach countered that excessive regulation would entrench the largest AI companies and make competition harder for smaller American developers — a concern aligned with Huang's own warning.[1]

Badlands commentary situated Huang's remarks within a larger argument that the perceived US–China AI arms race is in part a narrative device, and that centralization pressure from figures such as [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] and [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] poses a greater risk to a free-market AI industry than foreign competition does.[1]


## Remarks on the China AI market (2025)

Speaking on a Tuesday in May 2025, Huang said China's artificial intelligence market would likely reach about $50 billion in the next two to three years and that being locked out of it would be a "tremendous loss."[2] He argued that selling into China would bring back revenue and taxes and "create lots of jobs here in the United States."[2] "We just have to stay agile," Huang told CNBC's Jon Fortt in an interview alongside ServiceNow CEO Bill McDermott, both executives appearing in Las Vegas for ServiceNow's Knowledge 2025 conference. "Whatever the policies are of the government, whatever is in the best interest of our country, we'll support," he said.[2]
