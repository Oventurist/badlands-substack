# Janssen Pharmaceuticals

**Janssen Pharmaceuticals** is the pharmaceutical subsidiary of [[johnson-and-johnson|Johnson & Johnson]] and the entity that received the federal vaccine contracts central to Badlands Media's account of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s COVID-19 vaccine policy.[1]

## Federal contracts

Records obtained by The US Sun and cited in Badlands' December 29, 2022 essay "Why Trump Pushed the Vax" show a contract of $689,525,867 awarded to Janssen on December 19, 2019 by the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response.[1] At the time the award was described as funding development of an influenza vaccine, following remarks Trump had made at the White House while signing an executive order aimed at "modernizing influenza vaccines in the United States to promote national security and public health."[1] Trump had warned in September 2019 that in the event of an influenza pandemic "the virus could kill more than half a million Americans and inflict trillions of dollars of damage on our economy," and criticised reliance on slow, egg-based vaccine production carried out overseas.[1]

A second contract worth $216,169,222 followed in February 2020, bringing the total to $905,645,089.[1] Badlands contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] emphasises that the first award predates widespread public awareness of the coronavirus, and argues the contract pivoted into [[operation-warp-speed|Operation Warp Speed]] in May 2020.[1]

## COVID-19 vaccine

The Johnson & Johnson single-dose COVID-19 vaccine developed through Janssen was approved on February 28, 2021.[1] The [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] and the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] paused its use on April 13, 2021 after reports of rare blood clots, lifting the pause ten days later on April 23.[1] Gillespie speculates that the pause was an attempt by the "cabal" to tarnish a vaccine produced outside its control — possibly containing saline or the Army's "supervax" — and that the effort backfired by drawing attention to clotting reports associated with the [[moderna|Moderna]], [[pfizer|Pfizer]] and [[astrazeneca|AstraZeneca]] products, prompting a rapid walk-back.[1] These are the author's speculations rather than established findings.[1]

Using data from Our World in Data covering 44 countries and the European Union, Gillespie counted 66,885,169 administered doses of the Janssen/J&J vaccine, with the United States (18,950,000), the European Union (18,680,000) and South Africa (9,370,000) the largest recorded users.[1]
