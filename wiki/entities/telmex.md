# Telmex

Teléfonos de México (Telmex) was Mexico's national telephone monopoly and the centerpiece asset of the [[privatization-in-mexico|Salinas privatization program]].[1] In 1990 president [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]] put Telmex on the auction block as part of his drive to privatize inefficient state companies; Mexican telecom was then so retrograde, Wired observed, that it "had nowhere to go but up."[1]

[[carlos-slim|Carlos Slim Helú]] took control with a $1.76 billion bid for 20.4 percent of the company's stock.[1] Conveniently, just after the bid was accepted the government authorized a 40 percent rate increase for local service — a windfall so dramatic that when the new revenue figures were made public in early 1991 the stock began "a long, steady climb into the stratosphere."[1] Salinas's policies further guaranteed Slim a private monopoly on long-distance service until 1997.[1] In the early 1990s Telmex was one of the most profitable companies in the world, and Slim's personal fortune reached US$4.4 billion by 1994.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that the sale never ended the monopoly at the local level; it simply transferred it from the state to a private owner who was a friend of the president and a member of his election committee, while ordinary Mexicans paid higher rates.[1]
