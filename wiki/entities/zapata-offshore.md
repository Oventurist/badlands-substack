# Zapata Offshore

Zapata Off-Shore Company was an offshore oil drilling firm, an 80 percent-owned subsidiary of Zapata Petroleum Corporation, which [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and J. Hugh Liedtke — later head of Pennzoil — founded in Houston in 1953.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The Badlands Media account describes it plainly as a CIA front company and Bush's base of operations from the 1950s onward.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

Its early development was financed by [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]], created the same year, through loans for drilling rigs and the vessels that tended them; Zapata became one of that bank's most significant oil-related accounts of the late 1950s.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) TNB later became [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

Zapata was linked to the [[cia|CIA]]'s abortive [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]] invasion of Cuba in 1961: three company ships financed by TNB — the *Houston*, the *Barbara J* and the *Zapata* — were loaned by Bush's firm to the agency for the operation, which the CIA code-named OPERATION ZAPATA.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article presents the naming as evidence that Bush may have been running the operation, and offers Zapata as the model for how oil companies serve as vehicles for laundering covert and narcotics revenue — the same technique [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] later applied in Venezuela.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Founding partners and CIA cover

Citing Wikipedia's article on HRG Group, Badlands Media recorded that the company traces its origins to Zapata Petroleum Corporation, founded in 1953 by Bush with John Overbey, Hugh Liedtke, Bill Liedtke and [[thomas-j-devine|Thomas J. Devine]].[2] A CIA internal memo dated November 29, 1975 stated that Zapata Petroleum began through Bush's joint efforts with Devine, a CIA staffer who resigned his agency position that year to enter private business but continued working for the agency under commercial cover.[2] Zapata Off-Shore Company was formed in 1954 as a subsidiary of Zapata Oil with Bush as president, and in 1959 Bush bought control of it with $800,000, splitting Zapata Corporation into two independent companies while the Liedtkes retained Zapata Petroleum.[2]

## Global operating locations

Although Zapata Offshore had only a few drilling rigs, it established operations in the Gulf of Mexico, the Persian Gulf, Trinidad, Borneo and Medellín, Colombia, and counted the Kuwait Shell Petroleum Development Company among its clients.[2] Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argued that every one of these "very specific locations" would become a centre for global drug trafficking — the Mexican cartels, the Mujahideen and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], Venezuela, Indochina, and the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín cartel]] of [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]] — and that the company's global footprint, established in the 1960s, was the infrastructure of a CIA drug-trafficking expansion under perfect commercial cover.[2] Devine went on to accompany Bush to Vietnam in late 1967 as a cleared agency asset and served as his informal foreign affairs advisor while Bush was a member of Congress.[2]
