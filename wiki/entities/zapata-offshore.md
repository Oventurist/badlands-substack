# Zapata Offshore

Zapata Off-Shore Company was an oil-drilling firm headquartered in Houston, an 80-percent-owned subsidiary of Zapata Petroleum Corporation, which [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and J. Hugh Liedtke formed in 1953.[1] The Badlands article characterizes it as a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] front company that served as Bush's base of operations from the 1950s onward and as a template for how oil-industry revenues could be used to launder narcotics profits.[1]

Its early development was financed by [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]], created the same year as Zapata Petroleum, through loans for drilling rigs and tending vessels; the account was among the bank's most significant oil-related relationships of the late 1950s, and the relationship carried over to successor [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]], whose 1986 centenary book described the connection as cozy.[1]

Three Zapata-owned, TNB-financed ships — the *Houston*, the *Barbara J* and the *Zapata* — were loaned to the CIA for the 1961 [[bay-of-pigs-invasion|Bay of Pigs invasion]] by exiled Cuban mercenaries. The operation's CIA code name was OPERATION ZAPATA, which the article argues is not coincidental and raises the question of whether Bush was running the operation.[1]
