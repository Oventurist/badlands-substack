# Tafari Campbell

Tafari Campbell was an American chef who served as a sous chef in the White House kitchen during the presidency of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and who was subsequently hired as the Obama family's personal chef after the end of Obama's term in office.[1] He died at the age of 45 in a drowning incident near the Obamas' estate on Martha's Vineyard in July 2023, a death that drew immediate attention in alternative-media circles because of its proximity to the former first family's property.[1]

## Death

Campbell drowned while paddleboarding on Edgartown Great Pond, the body of water adjacent to the Obamas' 30-acre Martha's Vineyard estate at 79 Turkeyland Cove Road.[1] Emergency responders were dispatched to the estate on the evening of Sunday, July 23, 2023, after reports of a "possible drowning" in the water nearby.[1] An overnight search effort was mounted from the property itself and involved divers, United States Coast Guard helicopters and resources drawn from multiple agencies.[1] Campbell's body was recovered at approximately 10 a.m. on Monday, July 24, 2023, located in roughly eight feet of water about 100 feet from the banks of the Obama property, after searchers deployed sonar scanners from a boat.[1] Police confirmed that the Obamas were not at the residence at the time of the incident.[1]

## Reaction in alternative media

The death became a subject of commentary in the Truth Community, where the circumstances were compared to the long-running body-count folklore surrounding Bill and Hillary Clinton.[1] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] acknowledged that there was "nothing overtly suspicious" that he could identify in the case — "no smoking guns," "no black books," and no burner phones with Ukrainian numbers — but argued that the Obamas' one degree of separation from the Clintons was sufficient to raise questions.[1] Deschamps connected the episode to the [[global-intelligence-files|Global Intelligence Files]] and the [[pizzagate|Pizzagate]] controversy, and speculated that a household cook might have "seen something he shouldn't have."[1] No evidence of foul play was presented, and the commentary was explicitly framed as speculation rather than established fact.[1]

## Judicial Watch records release (August 2023)

In August 2023 [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] announced that it had obtained 18 pages of records from the Edgartown, Massachusetts, Police Department under the Massachusetts Public Records Law showing that the [[united-states-secret-service|United States Secret Service]] had reported Campbell missing and that his body was ultimately located using sonar.[2] The records further reported that clothing was found separate from the body and that Campbell was not wearing a life vest, and several other details concerning the July 23, 2023 drowning on Martha's Vineyard were redacted.[2] The release included a July 23 computer-aided dispatch (CAD) record.[2]

A detailed timeline emerged from the incident report: a Secret Service agent placed the 911 call at 7:56 p.m. on July 23; Campbell was wearing all black when he went missing; his clothing and paddle board were recovered by the Secret Service at 8:02 p.m. after agents deployed a zodiac boat with rescue swimmers; and the U.S. Coast Guard was engaged by 8:36 p.m., ten minutes after the 911 call.[2] The search was suspended at 1:21 a.m. on July 24 and resumed at 7:22 a.m. that morning, with the body reported recovered at 10:01 a.m.[2] The report also stated that the investigation would be handled going forward by the Massachusetts State Police and the Cape and Islands District Attorney's Office.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that each new disclosure in the case produced more questions than answers.[2] She highlighted the first line of the incident chronology, which recorded 42 past police incidents at the Obama estate on Martha's Vineyard — a figure she called striking given that personal protection at the residence is provided by the Secret Service.[2] She questioned what Campbell was wearing and whether it was a wetsuit, noting that New England water is brisk even in July and that his clothes were recovered hours before his body was.[2] Ashe argued that the most important question was why Judicial Watch had been forced to fight for the records at all, observing that the first section of the 18 pages consists of law-enforcement communications attempting noncompliance, and dismissing anticipated "ongoing investigation" and "family wants privacy" justifications as inadequate for a mysterious death on the property of a former president patrolled by the Secret Service.[2] She characterized the handoff to state authorities as "the rich people police" finalizing a cover-up, contrasted the privacy standard applied to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] with that applied to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and said Campbell's family deserved answers.[2]
