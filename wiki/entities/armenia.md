# Armenia

Armenia is a South Caucasus state that in February 2026 received the first visit by a sitting United States vice president or president.[1]

## Vance visit (February 2026)

Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] arrived in Armenia, becoming the first sitting US vice president or president to visit the country, according to AP News.[1] The trip followed a US-facilitated agreement and peace deal between Armenia and Azerbaijan, which included an August 2025 White House summit and a transit-corridor accord aimed at advancing regional peace.[1]

## Trump endorsement and transit corridor (May 2026)

In May 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] praised Armenian Prime Minister [[nikol-pashinyan|Nikol Pashinyan]] as "a great friend and Leader" who shares his vision of peace and prosperity for Armenia and the South Caucasus, and announced that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had travelled to Armenia to advance several bilateral deals.[2] Trump said the two countries would soon break ground on the [[trump-route-for-international-peace-and-prosperity|Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity]], which would transform the South Caucasus and help American energy companies gain access "from Central Asia all the way to the United States," and gave Pashinyan his complete endorsement for re-election on June 7, 2026.[2]

Badlands analysis noted that Armenia sits at the center of the geographic triangle formed by Georgia, [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]], and [[iran|Iran]], and that it lies in West Asia rather than Central Asia — the region between the Caspian Sea and [[china|China]] — suggesting Trump's reference pointed to [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] and to US participation in the [[north-south-transport-corridor|North-South Transport Corridor]].[2]

## Withdrawal from the CSTO and Western realignment (June 2024)

In June 2024, Armenia announced that it would withdraw from the Russian-led [[collective-security-treaty-organization|Collective Security Treaty Organization]], while simultaneously announcing that its top military brass would take part in [[nato|NATO]] Military Committee meetings.[3] Badlands read the pair of announcements as a signal that one of Russia's oldest allies was abandoning it to join what the brief termed the Western Hegemony, a shift underscored by a United States–Armenia Strategic Partnership Commission announced at the same time.[3] The analysis traced the realignment to a 2018 [[cia|CIA]]-led color revolution in Armenia which it assessed to have succeeded.[3]

Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Mikhail Galuzin accused the European Union of using a "monitoring mission" in Armenia to spy on the neighboring government of Azerbaijan.[3] The two countries, at war since 2020, saw their ceasefire broken in September 2023 by a 24-hour kinetic engagement that ended with Russian troops deploying to restore peace, in a warzone situated less than 50 miles from the site where Iran's president was killed in a helicopter crash the following year.[3] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that these moves, together with parallel pressure on Georgia, amounted to a desperate attempt by the hegemony to control the Caucasus and sever the International North–South Transportation Corridor developed under the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]].[3]

## Digitization of ancient manuscripts (2025)

In July 2025 Radio Free Europe reported that experts at the [[matenadaran|Matenadaran]], Armenia's purpose-built manuscript repository, had begun a groundbreaking project to place all accessible Armenian manuscripts — from ancient bibles to medieval cake recipes — into a free, searchable online database.[4] Many of the manuscripts held in Armenia were carried out of Ottoman territory by survivors of the mass killings that began in 1915, some hidden inside their clothing; many others remain scattered in museums and private collections worldwide.[4]


## Alleged rogue nuclear state and CIA proxy

Badlands contributor Joe Lange argues that Armenia is an unacknowledged "rogue nuclear state" and one of the most important, least discussed proxies of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and shadow government.[5] He notes that [[qanon|Q]] drop 1245, dated April 23, 2018, placed Armenia in a specific sequence -- "SA -> NK. NK -> ARMENIA. ARMENIA -> Iran" -- and insisted that "the order is important," implying a deliberate ordering to the dismantling of cabal-controlled states beginning with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the arrest of [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]], continuing through [[north-korea|North Korea]], then Armenia, and next [[iran|Iran]].[5] The same drop asked readers to "define hostage" and "define protection," which Lange interprets as meaning that rogue nuclear states shield the CIA's proxy terrorist networks and drug trafficking operations.[5]

Citing the Jerusalem Post, the article observes that while Iran and North Korea absorbed the world's non-proliferation attention, Armenia -- Iran's neighbor and long-time regional partner -- went unexamined.[5] Former prime minister [[hrant-bagratyan|Hrant Bagratyan]] stated publicly at a press conference that Armenia already possesses nuclear weapons, remarks that drew almost no international coverage.[5] Days earlier, Georgian authorities arrested three Armenian nationals and three Georgian citizens attempting to sell roughly $200 million of weapons-grade uranium 238; since 2014, eight Armenians had been arrested for attempting to smuggle or sell nuclear materials in Georgia, including three caught with cesium 137 in January 2016 and two in August 2014.[5] These arrests occurred amid intelligence reports that Islamic State and other groups were seeking materials for a "dirty bomb."[5]

## Alleged drug transit role

Lange contends that Armenia served for roughly thirty years as a heroin transit corridor from [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] through Iran into Europe, using the occupied territories of [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] and the 81-mile border strip adjacent to [[nagorno-karabakh|Nagorno-Karabakh]].[5] Azerbaijani president [[ilham-aliyev|Ilham Aliyev]] said that after Azerbaijan regained control of that border section following the [[second-karabakh-war|Second Karabakh War]], heroin seizures elsewhere on the Azerbaijan-Iran frontier doubled, which he presented as proof of Armenian collusion with Iran in trafficking to Europe.[5] Azerbaijani officials also claimed evidence that Lebanese, Syrian and other Middle Eastern fighters -- which Lange reads as [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] -- fought on Armenia's behalf.[5] The [[armenian-mafia|Armenian mafia]], described by Spanish law enforcement as the largest such network in Europe, was targeted in coordinated raids in Spain, France and Germany near the end of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term.[5]

## 2018 velvet revolution

Mass anti-government demonstrations beginning April 13, 2018 forced the resignation of Prime Minister [[serzh-sargsyan|Serzh Sargsyan]], who had circumvented presidential term limits through a 2015 constitutional referendum that shifted power to an unconstrained prime ministership.[5] Opposition leader [[nikol-pashinyan|Nikol Pashinyan]] demanded a snap parliamentary election, and Armenia subsequently established a new anti-corruption agency and indicted Sargsyan and other officials.[5] Lange links this "velvet revolution" to the claim that four CIA [[snow-white-supercomputers|Snow White supercomputers]] located in Armenia were taken offline on the same day, August 30, 2018, and to the anomaly that Armenia ranked first worldwide in Google searches for "qanon."[5] He also highlights U.S. House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s "rather spontaneous" September 2022 visit to Armenia, made with two Armenian-American members of Congress after border clashes killed more than 200 people, as a panicked response to the loss of a proxy state.[5]
