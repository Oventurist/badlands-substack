# Litani River

The Litani River in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] became, in March 2026, the declared northern limit of Israeli territorial ambitions in the country.[1]

## March 2026 escalation

On March 23, 2026, Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] said Israel's border "must be the Litani," signaling support for extending control northward.[1] A day later, Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] stated that Israeli forces would occupy territory up to the Litani River and control bridges and a broader "security zone," formalizing an intent to hold a significant portion of southern Lebanon.[1]

[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] described any Israeli occupation south of the Litani as an "existential threat" and vowed resistance, while the United Nations warned that the prospect of a sustained Israeli presence in the area was "very much concerning."[1] Lebanese authorities reported more than 1,070 deaths and over one million displaced in the fighting that intensified after March 2, when Hezbollah launched missiles into Israel and Israel responded with evacuation orders, expanded strikes, and ground activity.[1]

## June 2026 ceasefire framework

The river's line reappeared as the central geographic term of the US-backed [[israel-lebanon-ceasefire|Israel-Lebanon ceasefire framework]] announced in June 2026, which requires [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] to withdraw its operatives from areas south of the Litani while security zones in southern Lebanon pass to the control of the [[lebanese-armed-forces|Lebanese Armed Forces]].[2] The demand reflects a long-standing international position that Hezbollah's armed presence south of the Litani violates earlier ceasefire arrangements and should be replaced by Lebanese state authority, mirroring previous agreements that envisioned Hezbollah forces withdrawing north of the river.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] predicted that once Hezbollah pulls back, [[israel|Israel]] would move quickly to annex the land south of the Litani.[2]

## Security zone north of the river (June 2026)

During the June 2026 Israeli offensive around Nabatieh in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]], the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] published a map of the security zone in which its soldiers were operating.[3] Badlands noted that while Israel had announced such an objective before, this appeared to be the first published map in which the security zone included areas north of the Litani River — and that because the offensive itself was being waged against Nabatieh and its surrounding region, the map likely did not show the full extent of the territory Israel intended to claim.[3]

## Israeli airstrikes (September 2025)

On the evening of Thursday, 18 September 2025, the Israeli military launched a series of airstrikes on areas south and north of the Litani River, after issuing urgent warnings for residents to stay away from six designated locations.[4] The tactic mirrored the approach used during the Israeli war on [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] that began in October 2023 and ended with a ceasefire effective 27 November 2024.[4] Strikes hit sites around Dibbin and three locations in Mays Al-Jabal, injuring a Syrian national according to initial reports, and a house in Kfar Tibnit was targeted; a second wave struck Burj Qalaya and Al-Shahabiya in the Tyre district.[4] A Lebanese security source linked the timing to Hezbollah's commemoration of the first anniversary of the assassinations of [[hassan-nasrallah|Hassan Nasrallah]] and Hashem Safieddine, and expected escalation until 27 September.[4]
