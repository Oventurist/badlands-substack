# Marie-Thérèse Kaiser

Marie-Thérèse Kaiser is a German politician and the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD) leader in Rotenburg, Lower Saxony, who became internationally known in 2024 after being convicted of incitement to hatred over a social media post about crime statistics.[1]

## Conviction

The Verden regional court in Lower Saxony upheld a verdict against Kaiser for incitement to hatred, and in an appeal hearing the 27-year-old was found guilty of inciting hatred against Afghan local workers.[1] She was sentenced to 100 day fines — a German penalty calibrated to daily income — plus a fine of €60, totalling €6,000. Because a sentence exceeding 90 day fines confers a criminal record under German law, the penalty left her formally convicted.[1]

The charge rested on a post Kaiser had circulated on her social media accounts in August 2021 reading: "Afghanistan refugees; Hamburg SPD mayor for 'unbureaucratic' acceptance; Welcoming culture for gang rape?"[1] Among other material, she had linked to an article indicating that Afghans in Germany were disproportionately represented in gang rape cases.[1]

## Reaction

The verdict drew the attention of X chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who reposted a summary of the punishment and asked: "Are you saying the penalty was imposed for repeating accurate government statistics? Was there anything wrong with what she said?"[1] The case was carried by Badlands Media as an example of European speech prosecution in its May 2024 news brief.[1]
