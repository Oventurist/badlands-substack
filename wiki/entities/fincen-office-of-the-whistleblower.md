# FinCEN Office of the Whistleblower

The **Office of the Whistleblower** is the unit of the [[financial-crimes-enforcement-network|Financial Crimes Enforcement Network]] (FinCEN), a bureau of the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]], that administers the federal whistleblower award program for financial-crime reporting.[1]

## 2026 tips portal

On February 13, 2026, the Treasury announced a new website operated through the Office of the Whistleblower to accept confidential whistleblower tips on fraud, money laundering and sanctions violations, expanding federal efforts to combat financial crime.[1] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said the portal would allow individuals to report suspected misconduct involving violations of the [[bank-secrecy-act|Bank Secrecy Act]], U.S. sanctions programs and other financial laws, and that people whose tips lead to successful enforcement actions may be eligible for financial awards.[1] The Treasury encouraged submitters to provide detailed documentation supporting their claims.[1]

The initiative was framed as strengthening enforcement tools and encouraging reporting by offering whistleblowers both a confidential channel and potential monetary incentives, and came amid broader federal scrutiny of financial abuses and fraud schemes.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] praised the program as a superior version of earlier proposals for privately funded bounties on corrupt politicians, arguing that those schemes suffered from subjective metrics and corruptible incentive structures, whereas the FinCEN program focuses on objective, traceable money flows and is funded from fines collected from guilty parties.[1] He described the approach as "weaponizing the financial system against itself" by targeting financial institutions, the core intermediaries in organized crime networks.[1]
