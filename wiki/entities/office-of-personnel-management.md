# Office of Personnel Management

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) is the federal agency responsible for managing the civil service workforce. In July 2026 it occupied a central role in the debate over whether the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] had been wound down or merely decentralized.[1]

## Role in the DOGE transition

OPM Director [[scott-kupor|Scott Kupor]] told [[reuters|Reuters]] that DOGE "doesn't exist," then clarified on X that the initiative "may not have centralized leadership" but that "the principles of DOGE remain alive and well."[1] Administration officials described a transition in which DOGE personnel moved into permanent roles across the federal government to oversee implementation of the initiative's reforms — contract review, spending reduction, technology modernization, streamlining of operations, and workforce efficiency measures — placing personnel policy and staffing questions squarely within OPM's remit.[1]

The [[white-house|White House]] framed the July 4, 2026 transition as the beginning of a long-term, government-wide management effort rather than an end point, with agencies expected to continue tracking and publicizing savings.[1]

## "What did you do last week?" email (February 2025)

On Saturday 22 February 2025, OPM sent federal workers an email with the subject line "What did you do last week?", instructing recipients to reply with five examples of what they had accomplished in the past week, excluding any classified information, and to include their supervisor in the response.[2] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], overseeing the White House's [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]], posted on X that "failure to respond will be taken as a resignation" and said the directive was "consistent with President [Trump's] instructions," though the email itself contained no such threat.[2]

The deadline to reply was Monday at midnight.[2] At least three agencies told their workers to ignore the request, raising doubts about the enforceability of Musk's threat: federal workers outside OPM do not report to the agency, and OPM cannot dismiss employees directly.[2]

## Education Department buyout (2025)

In late February 2025, ahead of [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]]'s confirmation as Secretary of Education, the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] offered most of its employees a buyout of up to $25,000, described in a department-wide email as "a one time offer in advance of a very significant Reduction in Force."[3] Badlands contributor Ashe in America attributed the offer to OPM, calling it a generous arrangement for soon-to-be-terminated federal employees and describing it as OPM preparing the department for a major workforce reduction in anticipation of McMahon's first day on the job.[3]

## Government-wide buyout offer (January 2025)

On Tuesday, January 28, 2025, the White House issued a memo offering to pay federal workers who did not want to return to the office through September 30, provided they resigned by February 6, as part of the [[2025-federal-workforce-buyout-offer|2025 federal workforce buyout offer]].[4] OPM described the arrangement as administrative leave with pay and benefits, and a spokeswoman confirmed that agencies would receive implementation details later that day and would be able to carve out exemptions.[4] A senior administration official said the government-wide email was intended to ensure all federal workers were on board with the administration's return-to-office plan, noting that just 6 percent of federal employees worked full-time in office five years after COVID.[4]
