# Virginia Giuffre

**Virginia Roberts Giuffre** is a victim of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] whose allegations became central to civil litigation surrounding Epstein's network.[1]

## Allegations against Prince Andrew

Giuffre has accused [[prince-andrew|Prince Andrew]] of having sex with her when she was 17 years old.[1] Both Prince Andrew and Harvard law professor [[alan-dershowitz|Alan Dershowitz]] were understood to be among the individuals named in the final batch of [[epstein-court-document-unsealing|Epstein-related court documents]] slated for unsealing in 2023.[1]

## Recruitment and litigation

Giuffre took legal action against [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] in 2015, alleging she was sex trafficked at the age of 16 after Epstein's ex-lover and convicted madam [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] recruited her from her job as a locker room or spa attendant at [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s Mar-a-Lago resort in Palm Beach, where she was approached in 2000.[2] She came forward publicly after the initial investigation ended in an 18-month Florida jail term for Epstein, who struck a secret deal and was released in 2009.[2]

She alleged she was forced to have sex with [[prince-andrew|Prince Andrew]] three times when she was 17 — including at Epstein's Little St. James island, in New Mexico, and at Maxwell's London home, where the notorious photograph of her posing with King Charles's brother was taken.[2] American-born, she lived in Australia for years and became an advocate for sex trafficking survivors, emerging as a central figure in Epstein's prolonged downfall.[2]

## Death (April 2025)

Giuffre died at her home in Western Australia on Friday, April 25, 2025, at the age of 41; her publicist confirmed the death, which was reported as suicide.[2]

Attention immediately turned to a post she had made on X in 2019, replying to a user who claimed the "F.B.I. will kill her to protect the ultra rich and well connected."[2] She wrote: "I am making it publicly known that in no way, shape or form am I suicidal. I have made this known to my therapist and GP – If something happens to me – in the sake of my family do not let this go away and help me to protect them. Too many evil people want to see me [quieted]."[2]

Speaking to reporters on the tarmac at a New Jersey airport, President Trump called the death "a horrible thing," adding that it was "a very sad situation, the whole thing" and that "that whole situation is very sad – her and others."[2]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that media outlets were rushing "right on cue" to connect the latest Epstein narrative to Trump, against a backdrop of Trump reordering global trade and laying the foundation for a global monetary and energy reset.[2] Whether or not Giuffre killed herself, he wrote, was secondary to the larger monetary, trade and energy realignment being announced openly by Trump and members of the Sovereign Alliance — urging readers to "consider whose op you're boosting" and to "focus on signal."[2]
