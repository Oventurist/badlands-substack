# Mark Meadows

Mark Meadows served as White House Chief of Staff during the final period of the first administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] He figures prominently in disputes over what the White House did or did not do to secure Washington, D.C. ahead of January 6, 2021.[1]

## January 6 National Guard question

In a transcribed interview conducted on January 28, 2022, Deputy Chief of Staff [[anthony-ornato|Anthony Ornato]] told the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] that he overheard Meadows push Washington, D.C. Mayor [[muriel-bowser|Muriel Bowser]] to request as many National Guard troops as she needed to protect the city.[1] That testimony corroborated Trump officials' longstanding claim that the White House had communicated a desire for a substantial National Guard deployment — a claim the committee, under Vice Chair [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]], publicly said it had "no evidence" to support.[1] The transcript containing Meadows's alleged advocacy was suppressed from public release until obtained by The Federalist in 2024.[1]

## Georgia RICO indictment and removal bid (2023)

In August 2023 Meadows was charged by a Fulton County grand jury with violating Georgia's Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations (RICO) Act, alongside former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and seventeen other individuals.[2] Within days he moved to transfer the prosecution from state to federal court, and legal analysts widely predicted the effort would succeed.[2]

His attorney [[timothy-parlatore|Timothy Parlatore]] argued in a filing that "Mr. Meadows is entitled to remove this action to federal court because the charges against him plausibly give rise to a federal defense based on his role at all relevant times as the White House Chief of Staff to the President of the United States."[2] His lawyers further wrote that "the conduct giving rise to the charges in the indictment all occurred during his tenure and as part of his service as Chief of Staff."[2] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the removal request as a meaningful indicator of where the Georgia RICO case might ultimately be headed.[2]

## Contemporary commentary on the removal strategy

Meadows's attempt to move his Georgia indictment to federal court was cited days later by Trump counsel [[alina-habba|Alina Habba]] and by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] as part of a broader defense strategy in the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Georgia case]], in which the choice of state venue by [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] was characterized as deliberate and designed to foreclose a presidential pardon.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the parallel removal motions as pushing prosecutors into "a series of Chinese finger traps," with all roads leading to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[3]

## En banc petition (January 2024)

Meadows's bid to move his Georgia election interference case to federal court failed at the appellate level: in December 2023 a three-judge panel of the 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals unanimously ruled that he could not remove the case because the conduct at issue in the indictment was unrelated to his official duties as White House chief of staff.[4] In a filing on Tuesday, January 2, 2024, Meadows asked the appeals court to rehear the case en banc — before the full court — arguing that "The panel's decision is profoundly wrong – it defies text, precedent, and common sense – and profoundly consequential."[4] Should the court decline to rehear the matter, Meadows could appeal the December decision to the U.S. Supreme Court.[4]

## Emergency motion to block arrest (August 2023)

On Tuesday, August 22, 2023, Meadows asked a federal judge to block his arrest over the Georgia election charges while he pursued removal of his case to federal court.[5] In a filing in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia, his lawyers submitted an emergency motion seeking either an immediate ruling on removal or an order prohibiting Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] from seeking his arrest if he did not surrender to the Fulton County jail by the noon Friday deadline she had set.[5] The filing stated that Willis "has made clear that she intends to arrest Mr. Meadows before this Court's Monday hearing and has rejected out of hand a reasonable request to defer one business day until after this Court's hearing."[5] Later that day a judge ordered Willis to respond to Meadows's requests in writing by 3 p.m. Wednesday, and a federal judge scheduled a hearing for the following Monday on the removal request.[5]
