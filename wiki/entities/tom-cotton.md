# Tom Cotton

Tom Cotton is a Republican United States Senator known for hawkish positions on defense and nuclear policy.[1]

## New START and nuclear modernization

In a February 2026 *Wall Street Journal* opinion piece, Cotton argued that the United States should not fall behind in nuclear capabilities and framed modernization as essential to deterrence.[1] He opened the essay by writing: "The New Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty expired this month. The end of New Start is a watershed moment in American nuclear strategy. Far from a failure of diplomacy, this expiration is an overdue correction of a strategic mistake that left America vulnerable to two nuclear rivals: Russia and China."[1]

The piece appeared alongside the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] and [[pentagon|Department of War]] demonstration of a transportable [[nuclear-microreactor|nuclear microreactor]], amid rising "arms race" rhetoric; no new US nuclear weapons policy had been announced.[1]

## Remaining hawk after Graham's death (July 2026)

Following the death of Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] in July 2026, [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] singled out Cotton as one of the hawks left behind, arguing that he and other remaining interventionists lacked Graham's gravitas and political skill and could not fill the resulting "hawkish void" in Republican foreign policy.[2]

## Latin America rhetoric (January 2026)

In January 2026, after the US-led removal of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] from power in Venezuela, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] circulated a clip of Cotton discussing US policy toward Latin America and called him "a sociopath," arguing that his rhetoric represented "the essence of tyranny."[3] The commentary grouped Cotton with Senator [[rick-scott|Rick Scott]], who had said the United States would next "fix" [[cuba|Cuba]] and Nicaragua and seek political change in [[colombia|Colombia]], as neoconservatives who had "taken the bait" after President [[donald-trump|Trump]] "chummed the waters."[3]

## Push for ATACMS transfers to Ukraine (2023)

In September 2023 Cotton joined Senators [[roger-wicker|Roger Wicker]], [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] and [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] in a letter urging President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to send [[atacms|MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile Systems]] to Ukraine, arguing that failing to do so would "only prolong the war."[4] The senators claimed the transfer could be made "without any appreciable risk" to U.S. combat capability and that stockpiles could easily be replenished, a claim critics countered by citing Biden's own acknowledgement that U.S. ammunition supplies were running low.[4] Badlands commentary grouped the signatories with the [[military-industrial-complex|Military-Industrial Congressional Complex]] and described the GOP as still infested with security-state assets.[4]

## Immigration enforcement (2025)

On Sunday, June 15, 2025, Cotton said he did not believe [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] should "pull back on any kind of enforcement," responding to the Trump administration's direction that the agency pause raids against workers in the agriculture, hotel and restaurant industries.[5] Asked by CBS News's [[margaret-brennan|Margaret Brennan]] on *Face the Nation* whether he agreed with the move given agricultural business in Arkansas, Cotton said "we need to have robust worksite enforcement," adding: "I think worksite enforcement in all industries needs to be able to move forward. And I think ICE agents on the front lines need the support of political leadership."[5] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] had confirmed a shift in deportation policies in a statement to NewsNation the previous day, days after President [[donald-trump|Trump]] signaled that "changes are coming" in a post on Truth Social.[5]

## Warnings on Chinese farmland purchases

Breitbart's September 2024 coverage of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s farmland policy noted that senators had been warning for years about Chinese Communist Party purchases of American land, citing Cotton, who in August 2022 warned that China buying farmland in the United States constitutes a national security threat.[6] The concern gained prominence in connection with the Chinese spy balloon first spotted over Montana, and with CCP-affiliated purchases near US military bases.[6]

## New York Times op-ed affair and the Hochul deployment (2024)

In March 2024 Cotton drew attention to what critics called hypocrisy at [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] over its coverage of troop deployments.[7] Noting the paper's matter-of-fact treatment of Democratic New York Governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]]'s plan to send troops into New York City to crack down on crime, Cotton posted a note on Twitter reading simply, "Sending in the troops to help restore law and order…"[7] His point was that roughly four years earlier the newspaper had publicly denounced him after running his opinion piece calling for the use of [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops to quell violent riots in Washington.[7]

The Cotton column led to editors being forced out amid public confessions and recriminations, with writers including Taylor Lorenz, Caity Weaver, Sheera Frankel and Jacey Fortin arguing that such columns put black reporters in danger.[7] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]], who had previously written on the affair, noted that critics never explained what was historically false in the column, and described the paper's response as "a breathtaking surrender" in which it apologized, promised an investigation into how an opposing view reached its pages, and pledged to reduce the number of editorials.[7] After Democratic politicians actually ordered such a deployment — Hochul sending 750 National Guard members into the New York City subway system to assist the [[nypd|New York Police Department]], including bag searches at busy stations — the Times offered, in Turley's words, "little more than a journalistic shrug."[7]
