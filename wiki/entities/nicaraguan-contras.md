# Nicaraguan Contras

The **Contras** were a Nicaraguan rebel movement that fought the leftist Sandinista government during the 1980s and formed a key element of the [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] administration's anti-communist strategy in Central America.[1] Their financing became, in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], perhaps the most infamous example of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s entanglement with narcotics trafficking.[1]

## Funding and the drug connection

When [[congress|Congress]] cut off official funding through the [[boland-amendment|Boland Amendment]], the CIA and its allies allegedly turned to an alternative source of money: the drug trade.[1] The 1989 [[kerry-committee-report|Kerry Committee report]], based on a two-year Senate investigation, found that Contra supporters, suppliers, and pilots were deeply entangled in cocaine trafficking, often with the knowledge of U.S. officials who declined to act "for fear of jeopardizing the war efforts against Nicaragua."[1]

The report documented payments from the [[state-department|State Department]] to companies owned by known traffickers, ostensibly for "humanitarian aid," in some cases even after those individuals had been indicted.[1] Roughly $806,000 was funnelled to narcotics-linked firms for Contra supplies, raising the question of whether drug money was regarded as a "perfect solution" to the movement's funding gaps.[1]

## Dark Alliance

Journalist [[gary-webb|Gary Webb]]'s 1996 "Dark Alliance" series pushed the allegations further, contending that Contra-linked traffickers such as Danilo Blandón and Norwin Meneses funnelled cocaine into Los Angeles, fuelling the crack epidemic, with the profits flowing back into the CIA-backed war.[1]

## Iran-Contra and Mena

The Contra resupply network run by [[oliver-north|Oliver North]] connected the movement to the airstrip at [[mena-arkansas|Mena, Arkansas]], where the pilot [[barry-seal|Barry Seal]] operated; the October 1986 crash of Seal's former aircraft in Nicaragua with a cargo of weapons pulled Mena into the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] scandal.[1]

## Framing in the Gladio primer

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] uses the Contras as her central example of how the vocabulary of anti-communism was deployed as cover.[2] She writes that the United States spent millions upon millions of dollars funding the Contras in Central America and that the Reagan and Bush administrations repeatedly told the public they were the good guys — a characterization she rejects, just as she rejects the parallel claim that the [[sandinista-national-liberation-front|Sandinistas]] were communists.[2] In her account the Sandinistas were fighting a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-installed dictator put in place after a previous government objected to [[united-fruit-company|United Fruit]] owning 42% of the country's farmable land, and once that dictator fell the United States dispatched Special Forces, [[operation-gladio|Gladio]]-trained Cuban exiles and mercenaries to overthrow the new government on behalf of the [[international-syndicate|International Syndicate]].[2]
