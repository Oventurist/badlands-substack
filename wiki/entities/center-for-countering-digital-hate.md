# Center for Countering Digital Hate

The Center for Countering Digital Hate (CCDH) is an advocacy organization that campaigns against what it designates online hate and misinformation, and which independent media treat as a node in the wider [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]].

In December 2023 commentary, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the brains and, more importantly, the money behind CCDH and [[media-matters|Media Matters for America]] had also been attacking [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] in the mainstream, attempting to get him deplatformed and marked with the same "pick-your-controversy scarlet letter" applied to other targets.[1] He placed these campaigns in the same category as the lawfare and narrative disruption tools deployed against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## 2024 "kill Musk's Twitter" leak

In October 2024, an explosive leak of CCDH internal documents became a major story in the closing weeks of the U.S. presidential election.[2] The documents, reported by journalists Paul D. Thacker and [[matt-taibbi|Matt Taibbi]] via The Disinformation Chronicle and Racket News, showed the group planning in writing to "kill Musk's Twitter" while strengthening its ties with the Biden/Harris administration and with Democrats such as Senator [[amy-klobuchar|Amy Klobuchar]], who has introduced multiple bills to regulate online "misinformation."[2] Klobuchar's office did not respond to a request for comment.[2]

The leak drew particular attention because CCDH's founder, British political operative [[morgan-mcsweeney|Morgan McSweeney]], was by then advising the [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] campaign — prompting the reporters' framing that "The British are coming, to meddle in our elections!"[2] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] responded to the report with the statement "This is war," which [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read as an acknowledgment that the Info War is an accurate description of a Fifth-Generation War against the machine rather than a rhetorical flourish.[2]

## "The New Climate Denial" study (2024)

On January 16, 2024, CCDH released [[the-new-climate-denial-report|"The New Climate Denial"]], a study finding significant skepticism toward climate alarmism among young audiences.[3] The report documented a significant increase between 2018 and 2023 in YouTube content expressing the views that "climate solutions won't work," that "climate science and the climate movement are unreliable," or that "the impacts of global warming are beneficial or harmless."[3] It also found that roughly one-third of teenagers — the platform's predominant audience — hold views such as that climate policies cause more harm than good, or that climate change is "a hoax to control and oppress people."[3]

CCDH described the findings as alarming and "startling" and urged big-tech platforms such as YouTube to censor content that "contradicts the authoritative scientific consensus" on climate change.[3] Coverage of the report characterized CCDH as a left-leaning organization that was sued by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] for allegedly running a "scare campaign" to drive away advertisers.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the censorship push would backfire, noting that the voices targeted included "Nobel laureates, theoretical physicists, meteorologists, professors, and environmental scientists from across the world."[3]

## Threatened litigation by X Corp (2023)

In late July 2023 [[x|X Corp]], the parent company of [[twitter|Twitter]], was reported to be exploring a lawsuit against CCDH, described as a UK-based dark money nonprofit run by a far-left British Labour Party operative named Imran Ahmed.[4] Earlier that month journalist Paul Thacker had published a Twitter Files expose via The Disinformation Chronicle showing that pre-[[elon-musk|Musk]] Twitter employees acted against several conservative accounts after CCDH released a report alleging that just twelve accounts produced the majority of anti-vaccine disinformation on social media; [[facebook|Facebook]] rejected the same report, stating "There isn't any evidence to support this claim."[4] A July 20 letter from X's attorneys to CCDH and its chief executive Ahmed accused the group of targeting the company with multiple unfounded accusations intended to harm it financially, citing a June CCDH report titled "Twitter Fails To Act On 99% Of Twitter Blue Accounts Tweeting Hate" that X called "false, misleading or both."[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that contesting the internet's largest public square on constitutional grounds was significant and encouraging, even though the rebranded platform remained imperfect and inconsistent in its rule enforcement.[4]
