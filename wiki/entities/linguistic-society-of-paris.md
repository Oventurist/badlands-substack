# Linguistic Society of Paris

The **Linguistic Society of Paris** (Société de Linguistique de Paris) is a French scholarly body that, in 1866, adopted a statute barring the presentation of papers on the origin of language. In the [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series this rule is characterized as an official policy that all research into the possibility of a common origin for all languages "shall be forbidden from publication."[1]

## Interpretation in the series

The article treats the 1866 ban as inexplicable for a research body except as deliberate suppression: an absurd rule to make "unless there were a specific reason why such information must be kept hidden."[1] The author situates the decision in a wider mid-19th-century context in which atheists had, in his account, become the dominant leaders of standardized, industrial-scale education, claiming to fight the "tyranny of the church" while enacting a tyranny of their own.[1]

## Link to religious monogenesis

Because the doctrine that all languages share a common origin is embedded in religious teaching worldwide — most familiarly the Tower of Babel narrative — the article argues that suppressing linguistic monogenesis was tantamount to suppressing traditional religious viewpoints.[1] The author poses the question of what follows if the religious position is in fact the factually correct one, and contends that biblical history explains the observed pattern of a single original tongue followed by isolation and gradual differentiation far better than mainstream history does.[1]

The same section notes the professional consensus that Hungarian is an isolated language, which the article dismisses as possibly "lazy scholarship" or the result of a deliberate cover-up.[1]
