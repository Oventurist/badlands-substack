# Ecuador

Ecuador is a South American nation that became, in early 2026, a theater of joint counter-narcotics military operations with the United States.

Badlands Brief: Conflicts, Cartels, & Clemency reports that Ecuadorian and US forces carried out coordinated operations against Designated Terrorist Organizations in Ecuador on March 3, 2026, according to [[united-states-southern-command|US Southern Command]]. SOUTHCOM Commander General [[francis-l-donovan|Francis L. Donovan]] praised "the men and women of the Ecuadorian armed forces for their unwavering commitment to this fight." Ecuador's Defense Ministry described the effort as an "offensive" operation conducted with US support and said operational details remain classified.

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked the operation to Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s longstanding identification of Mexico and Ecuador as the principal cartel problem areas, arguing that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s focus on those two countries indicated genuine coordination with other regional leaders.


## March 2026: first FBI office

The March 13, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the United States established its first [[fbi|FBI]] office in Ecuador, announced by the US embassy on a Wednesday, to strengthen cooperation on drug and weapons trafficking, money laundering, and terrorism financing. Interior minister [[john-reimberg|John Reimberg]] said the decisive change was the permanent presence of FBI agents working directly with a newly formed unit inside Ecuador's national police. The step followed a recent joint US–Ecuadorian operation against trafficking networks and came amid President [[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]]'s military-backed campaign against organized crime. Acting Chargé d'Affaires [[lawrence-petroni|Lawrence Petroni]] said the arrangement would expand both governments' ability to "identify, dismantle, and bring to justice those who traffic drugs, launder money, smuggle weapons, and finance terrorism."

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] situated the office in a lineage running from the Special Intelligence Service of 1940 through the 1980s Legal Attaché program to the bureau's global liaison expansion in the 2010s, noting that the FBI works through host-nation authorities rather than exercising law-enforcement powers abroad — an arrangement she called a "reverse-sicario" and cited as evidence against those who dismiss claims of overlap between the FBI and the [[cia|CIA]].
