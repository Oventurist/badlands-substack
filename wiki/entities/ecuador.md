# Ecuador

Ecuador is a South American nation that in March 2026 became a theater of joint counter-narcotics military operations conducted with the United States.[1]

## Joint operations against narco-terrorists

On Tuesday, March 3, 2026, Ecuadorian and US forces carried out coordinated operations targeting Designated Terrorist Organizations inside the country, according to [[united-states-southern-command|US Southern Command]].[1] Ecuador's Defense Ministry described the effort as an "offensive" operation conducted with US support and said operational details remained classified.[1]

SOUTHCOM Commander Gen. Francis L. Donovan praised the Ecuadorian armed forces for "unwavering commitment to this fight," citing their courage and resolve in continued actions against narco-terrorists.[1] The command framed the operations as part of a shared regional commitment across Latin America and the Caribbean to confront organizations responsible for terror, violence, and corruption.[1]

Badlands analysis situated Ecuador alongside Mexico as one of the two theaters Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] had repeatedly identified as the core of the cartel problem, treating US action there as evidence of genuine multilateral coordination against transnational criminal networks.[1]

## First US FBI office (March 2026)

In March 2026, the United States opened its first [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] office in Ecuador, announced by the US embassy on X as "another strategic and operational milestone in security."[2] The office is intended to strengthen cooperation with Ecuadorian authorities on investigations involving drug and weapons trafficking, money laundering, and the financing of terrorism.[2] Interior minister [[john-reimberg|John Reimberg]] said the key change is the permanent presence of FBI agents working directly with a newly formed unit within Ecuador's national police, while Acting Chargé d'Affaires [[lawrence-petroni|Lawrence Petroni]] said the agreement would expand both governments' ability to dismantle trafficking and terror-financing networks.[2]

The opening came as President [[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]] continued a military-backed campaign against organized crime that has been the central focus of his administration, and followed a joint Ecuadorian–US operation targeting drug trafficking networks inside the country.[2] Badlands commentary characterized the arrangement as a "reverse-sicario," noting that the FBI can operate abroad only alongside a local agency — the mirror image, in its telling, of the CIA's domestic constraints.[2]

## Rupture with Mexico (2024)

In April 2024 Ecuadorian police stormed the Mexican embassy in Quito to arrest
former Vice-President [[jorge-glas|Jorge Glas]], who had taken refuge there in
December 2023 after Ecuador issued an arrest warrant against him for alleged
corruption.[3] [[mexico|Mexico]] had granted Glas political asylum "after a
thorough analysis" of the situation, an action Ecuador viewed as illegal, and
Ecuador's Presidency declared that "Ecuador is a sovereign nation and we are
not going to allow any criminal to stay free."[3] Mexico responded by cutting
diplomatic ties.[3] Glas was flown under police guard to Guayaquil to await
trial in a maximum security prison.[3]

The rupture came amid deepening US security engagement with the government of
[[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]]: the [[white-house|White House]] announced in
January 2024 the delivery of over 20,000 bulletproof vests and more than $1
million in security equipment, increased FBI presence supporting the Ecuadorian
National Police and Attorney General's Office, and
[[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] personnel training Ecuadorian police
and prosecutors.[3]

## US presidential delegation (May 2025)

On May 21, 2025, [[donald-trump|President Donald J. Trump]] announced a Presidential Delegation to Quito to attend the presidential inauguration of [[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]] on May 24, 2025, led by Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]][4]


## Assassination of Fernando Villavicencio

On August 9, 2023, presidential candidate [[fernando-villavicencio|Fernando Villavicencio]] was gunned down at a campaign rally in the capital, Quito, prompting the government to declare a countrywide state of emergency for 60 days the following morning.[5] A legislator in the National Assembly and a former investigative journalist, Villavicencio had campaigned tirelessly against organized crime, drug trafficking and government corruption, describing Ecuador as a "narco state" led by a "political mafia."[5] His killing was widely blamed on those same criminal elements; the suspected gunman was wounded in a shootout with security personnel and died in police custody, while nine other people and two police officers were injured.[5]
