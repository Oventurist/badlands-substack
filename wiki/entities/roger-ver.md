# Roger Ver

Roger Ver is an early [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] investor widely known by the nickname "Bitcoin Jesus."[1] On April 30, 2024, the United States [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] announced criminal charges against him, including mail fraud, tax evasion and filing false tax returns, and said that authorities in Spain had arrested him on the basis of those charges.[1]

## Charges

The U.S. government alleged that Ver defrauded the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] of roughly $48 million by failing to report capital gains on his sale of Bitcoin and other assets.[1] According to an indictment filed on February 15, 2024 and unsealed on April 29, Ver allegedly took control of roughly 70,000 BTC in June 2017 — before the bull run that followed — and sold many of them for $240 million.[1] U.S. officials said they planned to extradite Ver from Spain to the United States to stand trial.[1]

## Reaction

Reactions to Ver's arrest on social media were mixed.[1] Bitcoiner Dan Held, the former growth lead at the exchange Kraken, said Ver "deserves everything that he's about to get" after he "nearly destroyed Bitcoin," a reference to Ver's role in the block-size disputes and his promotion of a competing chain.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Bitcoin and cryptocurrency remain controversial within the [[truth-community|Truth Community]], but described himself as a believer that Bitcoin represents an opportunity to project peaceful sovereign power in a way free-thinking, free-trading citizens have not had in some time — up to and including under a gold and silver standard — because of its decentralized and open-source code, its codification as a commodity, and the absence of a central issuer or custodian.[1] He added that there are many scammers in the cryptocurrency space, many of whom have attempted to spin up their own versions of the original protocol, thereby painting themselves as anathema to the core tenets of decentralization.[1] While expressing no affection for the IRS and general discomfort with imprisonment for tax "crimes," he observed that such charges often represent convenient means of rolling up "the meek and the wicked at once," leaving open which category Ver belongs to: "Sometimes, you reap what you sow."[1]
