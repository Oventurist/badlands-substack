# Department of Justice Office of Inspector General

The Department of Justice Office of Inspector General (DOJ OIG) is the internal watchdog of the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], with jurisdiction over investigations of misconduct within the department and its components, including the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]].[1]

## Role in the 2026 FBI records disclosure

In March 2026 the DOJ OIG emerged as a gatekeeper in the dispute over a cache of up to 1.9 million pages of FBI records stored in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility at bureau headquarters. Officials stated that many of the legacy records in the cache fall under the authority of the Office of Inspector General and must be reviewed in consultation with that office before any release can occur.[1]

The OIG consultation requirement was one of several procedural conditions — alongside digitization, classification review, and coordination with other federal agencies — that the FBI cited in projecting a 10-to-12-month timeline before it could determine which materials were responsive to a Freedom of Information Act request.[1]

## Report on Jeffrey Epstein's death (June 2023)

On Tuesday, June 27, 2023, the OIG released a report detailing the failures that occurred beginning one month before [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] died in the custody of the [[federal-bureau-of-prisons|Bureau of Prisons]] at New York City's [[metropolitan-correctional-center|Metropolitan Correctional Center]].[2] The report concluded that Epstein died by suicide by hanging on August 10, 2019, while awaiting trial on federal sex trafficking charges, and stated that foul play "was not possible."[2] It recorded that Epstein hanged himself with an orange noose fashioned from "a sheet or a shirt," and that a search of his cell afterward recovered extra bedsheets, another mattress and a noose; Officer Michael Thomas, charged criminally in the case, was quoted saying "Breathe, Epstein, Breathe" and "We're going to be in a lot of trouble."[2] See [[2023-doj-inspector-general-report-on-epsteins-death|2023 DOJ Inspector General report on Epstein's death]].[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] criticised the report's conclusion as insulting to public intelligence, pointing to broken cameras known to be broken, the removal of Epstein's cellmate, skipped guard checks, the moving of the body, the abundance of linens available to an inmate on suicide watch, and the investigators' inability after months of work to say whether the ligature was a shirt or a sheet.[2]
