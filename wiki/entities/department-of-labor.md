# Department of Labor

The US Department of Labor is the federal agency responsible for administering labor programs, including the guest worker visa certification processes at the center of a July 2026 fraud investigation.[1]

## H-1B and PERM investigation (July 2026)

The Department's Office of Inspector General launched a major investigation into fraud and human trafficking tied to the [[h-1b-visa-program|H-1B visa]] and Program Electronic Review Management (PERM) systems.[1] The OIG press release stated that "these abuses undermine the integrity of Department of Labor programs designed to address genuine labor shortages — not to line the pockets of bad actors at the expense of American jobs," and described the investigation as reflecting an "unwavering commitment to dismantle human trafficking and forced-labor networks that exploit the foreign guest worker visa system."[1]

Inspector General [[anthony-desposito|Anthony P. D'Esposito]] said the matter "isn't just paperwork fraud—it's the exploitation of vulnerable workers, forced labor, the displacement of American workers, and abusive human trafficking," naming exposure of fraud, protection of American workers, and "putting criminals in cuffs" as his top priorities.[1]

The OIG also launched a nationwide reporting campaign urging US and foreign workers to report suspected program abuses, with officials saying rewards may be available for information leading to successful prosecutions.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read D'Esposito as front-running a "this is just paperwork" narrative from the administration's critics, and noted the repeated invocation of human trafficking in the press release.[1] She framed the investigation as encouraging and as a callback to the 2024 H-1B controversy that divided the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]] and "ultimately cost us [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek]]."[1]
