# Moody's

Moody's is one of the major American credit rating agencies. On Friday, May 16, 2025, it stripped the United States of its last remaining top-tier sovereign rating, downgrading the country one notch from Aaa to Aa1.[1]

## The May 2025 downgrade

The Financial Times, which had examined the implications of a prospective downgrade some months earlier when Moody's "still seemed determined to ignore the writing on the wall," questioned whether the move mattered in practical terms.[1] From a stock market perspective the paper professed uncertainty about the reaction when trading opened on the following Monday, noting that [[standard-and-poors|S&P]]'s U.S. sovereign downgrade in August 2011 had prompted the worst single-day fall in U.S. stock prices since the then-recent global financial crisis, though the market recovered quickly — a reaction it attributed to fears about the effect on the financial plumbing.[1] Mechanically, the FT concluded, the answer to whether the downgrade mattered to "financial plumbers" was "almost certainly 'not at all'."[1]

## Political response

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] told Meet the Press that "Moody's is a lagging indicator. I think that's what everyone thinks of credit agencies," citing [[larry-summers|Larry Summers]]'s similar assessment of the 2011 downgrade and comparing the situation to [[sean-duffy|Sean Duffy]]'s remarks on the air traffic control system: "we didn't get here in the past 100 days."[1] Bessent attributed the deterioration to "the Biden administration and the spending that we have seen over the past four years," said the administration had inherited a 6.7% deficit-to-GDP ratio — the highest outside a recession or a war — and pledged to reduce spending while growing the economy.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Chris Paul argued that after more than a decade of warnings that the U.S. credit rating must remain AAA, the downgrade's apparent irrelevance was itself the point: "This credit downgrade is an info op that removes the fear of a credit downgrade," which he grouped with what he called other "existential risk hoaxes."[1] He tied the outcome to a broader claim that "the financial system is being reset" and that systems are being swapped.[1] He also flagged the aftermath of the 2011 S&P downgrade as more consequential, noting the Justice Department investigation of S&P, the departure of CEO Deven Sharma within weeks, and the eventual $5 billion federal lawsuit over pre-2008 ratings, which S&P called direct retaliation for its downgrade.[1]

## Israel credit warnings (2025)

In March 2025, Moody's Investors Service warned of [[israel|Israel]]'s "very high political risks that have weakened economic and fiscal strength."[2] In a regular update on the country's credit rating, the agency said that "Uncertainty over Israel's longer-term security and economic growth prospects are much higher than is typical, with risks to the high-tech sector particularly relevant, given its important role as a driver of economic growth and significant contributor to the government's tax take," adding that such developments "would have potentially severe implications for the government's finances and may mark a further erosion in institutional quality."[2] The agency had cut Israel's rating by two notches to Baa1 the previous year and maintained a negative outlook, which it said reflected persistent downside risks.[2] Among the challenges cited were "very high exposure to geopolitical risks, polarized political system, which weighs on governance and policy effectiveness, [and] labor-market participation of religious minorities, resulting in high-income inequality and elevated social tensions."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who discussed the Israeli economy on his show [[geopolitics-with-ghost|Geopolitics with Ghost]], argued that Israel is not built for wars of attrition given its limited natural resources and manufacturing base and its reliance on food imports, and that emigration combined with conscription had opened a labor-force gap contributing to the erosion of the Israeli middle class.[2] He tied the ratings pressure to the political position of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], whose coalition would likely collapse if the war stopped and who faces felony indictments pending since 2019.[2]
