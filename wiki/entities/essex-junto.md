# Essex Junto

The Essex Junto was a New England Federalist cabal which, in the account given by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] for Badlands Media, served as the organizing core of the early nineteenth-century plot to dissolve the American union into two British-aligned confederacies.[1] Its guiding hand was [[timothy-pickering|Timothy Pickering]], the former Secretary of State under President John Adams, and its leadership included the opium-trading merchant [[george-cabot|George Cabot]].[1]

## Aims

The Junto's premise was that slave states and free states could not co-exist.[1] Rather than continue the struggle to abolish slavery by imposing the authority of the Constitution, its members argued that it was best to dissolve the nation and the constitution completely.[1] Under the design, British Canada would merge with the northern "free states" in a new Anglo-Saxon confederation while the slave power created its own southern confederation; both would be defined by a special relationship with England and dominated by the [[city-of-london|City of London]]'s economic web of finance.[1]

## The New York hinge and Burr

After [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]]'s defeat by [[thomas-jefferson|Thomas Jefferson]] in the 1800 presidential election left him a distrusted lame-duck vice president, direct federal support for dissolution was no longer attainable, and a new plot was hatched in 1803 requiring Burr's control of New York State as the economic powerhouse capable of fusing the free states into British Canada.[1] Junto senator [[william-plumer|William Plumer]] recorded a conspiratorial meeting at his house in the winter of 1803–04 involving Senator James Hillhouse, Burr, and Senator Uriah Tracy, at which Hillhouse "unequivocally declared that it was his opinion that the United States would soon form two distinct governments," and at which Burr's own view appeared to be that a separation was not only coming but necessary.[1]

The scheme failed when [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] devoted all of his energy to defeating Burr's 1804 run for Governor of New York, which ended with 28,000 votes for Burr against 35,000 for Morgan Lewis.[1]
