# Lewis Kaplan

Lewis Kaplan is the federal judge who presided over the civil defamation litigation brought by writer [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] against [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], proceedings that produced an $83.3 million judgment later stayed on appeal.[1]

## Criticism from Badlands commentators

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Kaplan as "a political hack whose story is still being written," and argued that the appellate review of the case was overdue given what she called one of the most egregious abuses of process and power in recent memory.[1] She contended that the appeal "must go all the way," warning that the legal process will be weaponized again unless there are consequences for everyone who participated, and stated that accountability should "start with Kaplan."[1]

## Bankman-Fried bail rulings (February 2023)

Kaplan presided over the bail conditions of FTX founder [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] ahead of his criminal fraud trial.[2] After Assistant U.S. Attorney [[danielle-sassoon|Danielle R. Sassoon]] flagged in a February 13, 2023 letter that the defendant had been using a virtual private network — raising concerns about crypto trading in violation of bail or access to the dark web — Kaplan ordered him to stop using a VPN, notwithstanding defense claims that it had been used only to stream NFL playoff games and the Super Bowl.[2] He also ruled that encrypted messaging applications would not be permitted under the conditions of bail, a restriction Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] read as a sign of concern over whom Bankman-Fried might be communicating with.[2]


## Bankman-Fried bail proceedings (2023)

As Senior U.S. District Judge for the Southern District of New York, Kaplan presided over the pretrial release proceedings of FTX founder [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]].[3] At a February 9, 2023 hearing convened after prosecutors discovered the defendant's encrypted communications with FTX's general counsel, Kaplan held both the government and the defense to account over how to keep Bankman-Fried from contacting key witnesses.[3] He told defense counsel, "I am far less interested in the defendant's convenience, given the record here," and urged the parties to tighten monitoring across all avenues of communication and submit a more restrictive proposal on the $250 million bond package for his approval.[3]


## Sentencing of Sam Bankman-Fried

On March 28, 2024, Kaplan sentenced [[ftx|FTX]] founder [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] to 25 years in prison and ordered $11 billion in forfeiture to the U.S. government for the fraud and conspiracy that destroyed the cryptocurrency exchange and the related hedge fund [[alameda-research|Alameda Research]].[4] "There is a risk that this man will be in position to do something very bad in the future," Kaplan said before imposing the sentence on the 32-year-old, adding, "And it's not a trivial risk at all."[4] He observed that he had never heard "a word of remorse for the commission of terrible crimes" from the defendant, and that in 30 years on the federal bench he had "never seen a performance" like Bankman-Fried's trial testimony, describing him as either "outright lying" or evasive under cross-examination.[4] The sentence fell well below the 40 to 50 years prosecutors sought but far above the five to six-and-a-half years proposed by the defense.[4]
