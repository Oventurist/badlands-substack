# Ebrahim Raisi

Ebrahim Raisi was the President of the Islamic Republic of Iran who was killed, along with the country's foreign minister, in a helicopter crash on Sunday, May 19, 2024, in Iran's remote northwestern region, as confirmed by Iranian state news media.[1] He was 63 years old at the time of his death.[1]

## Political profile

Raisi was described as a conservative hardliner and a protégé of Iran's supreme leader, [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]].[1] His loss was expected to sow further uncertainty in a country already buckling under significant economic and political strain, with tensions with nearby Israel at a dangerous high.[1]

Commentary published by Badlands Media emphasized the succession stakes: Raisi had been widely expected to become the next Supreme Leader, with the sitting Ayatollah described as effectively in hospice care.[1] Raisi had not always been the odds-on favorite for the position. His predecessor, former President [[hassan-rouhani|Hassan Rouhani]], was once considered the leading candidate to be selected Supreme Leader by the [[assembly-of-experts|Assembly of Experts]], until Rouhani was officially disqualified from consideration by Iranian courts in January 2024.[1]

## The crash

The crash occurred as Raisi and Foreign Minister [[hossein-amir-abdollahian|Hossein Amir-Abdollahian]] were returning from a ceremony marking the opening of a dam on Iran's border with Azerbaijan, according to state media.[1] Also aboard the aircraft were three crew members, the governor of Eastern Azerbaijan province, an imam, Raisi's head of security, and a bodyguard, according to the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]]-run media outlet Sepah.[1]

Drone footage of the wreckage taken by the Red Crescent and carried on the state-affiliated FARS News Agency showed the crash site on a steep, wooded hillside, with little remaining of the helicopter beyond a blue and white tail.[1]

## Information environment

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] documented the confusion surrounding the initial reporting, noting that conflicting reports circulated throughout the day: first that Raisi had been rescued and was fine, then that he was hospitalized in critical condition, then that his condition was unknown, then that the crash site had not been located at all.[1] A number of videos — from both UAV drones and searchers trekking through the wilderness on foot — purported to show crash sites that turned out to be bad intelligence.[1] Only later did imagery emerge showing a debris pile with a helicopter tail protruding, consistent with reports that there were no survivors.[1]

## Badlands interpretation of his death

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued in June 2024 that Raisi, whatever his personal flaws, had declined to assume the escalatory role [[iran|Iran]] was expected to play after [[israel|Israel]] bombed an Iranian embassy in Syria.[2] In this reading, Raisi had been courted by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Xi Jinping to defect from what Badlands calls the Unipolar Hegemony, with BRICS and a multipolar sovereign alliance offered as an alternative path — a possible motive for his death alongside Foreign Minister [[hossein-amir-abdollahian|Hossein Amir-Abdollahian]], who had built unusually positive relations with the Saudi foreign ministry and telephoned Riyadh regularly, including during the restrained Iran–Israel missile exchange.[2] Those calls were said to be continuing after the two men's deaths.[2] Iran held a presidential election at the end of June 2024 to replace him, which advanced to a run-off between the top two contenders.[2]

## Death and its electoral consequences

Raisi's death in May 2024 triggered a constitutional requirement that [[iran|Iran]] hold a presidential election within 50 days, forcing the political establishment into a decision it had sought to avoid amid years of plummeting voter turnout.[3] Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] was left choosing between an open, competitive contest and a further round of candidate disqualifications that risked record-low participation.[3]

## Helicopter crash and death (May 2024)

Iranian television reported that a helicopter carrying President Raisi and his accompanying delegation was forced into a "hard landing" in East Azerbaijan Province; state media attributed the emergency to bad weather, which also hindered relief teams.[4] Raisi had been returning from Azerbaijan, where he inaugurated a joint border dam and met his Azerbaijani counterpart Ilham Aliyev.[4] Iranian media reported that the helicopter — also carrying Foreign Minister [[hossein-amir-abdollahian|Hossein Amir Abdollahian]], East Azerbaijan Governor Malek Rahmati, and the Imam of Tabriz Mosque, Ayatollah Al Hashemi, among other officials — crashed, and that all passengers were pronounced dead.[4] [[israel]]'s Channel 13, citing Israeli officials, reported that Israel had nothing to do with the accident, quoting the message that "Tel Aviv has nothing to do with the incident."[4] Israeli Ambassador to the UN [[gilad-erdan|Gilad Erdan]] complained that the UN Security Council had held a moment's silence "to remember a mass murderer, Iranian President Raisi."[4]
