# Stanley Fischer

Stanley Fischer is a central banker who served as Vice Chairman of the Board of Governors of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] until his resignation in 2017.[1] He was widely regarded within financial media as an influential centrist voice on the Board.[1]

## Resignation from the Federal Reserve

Fischer announced his departure in a letter sent to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in September 2017, and retired from the Board of Governors by October 13 of that year, citing "personal reasons."[1] His exit left his leadership post one year early and cut short his general term on the Board by two years.[1]

## Role in the "Trump controls the Fed" thesis

In a January 2023 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presented Fischer's early departure as one of three resignations that — combined with two vacancies left unfilled at the end of the Obama administration — allowed Trump to appoint five of the seven Federal Reserve governors during his term.[1] Lange suggested some of those resignations "may have been forced by Trump himself," naming Fischer specifically.[1] In this reading, Fischer's exit was a step in a broader transfer of control over the central bank away from the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]] financial interests Lange holds responsible for the [[global-debt-system|global central bank debt system]].[1] These claims are the author's interpretation rather than documented fact; the contemporaneous reporting cited only Fischer's stated personal reasons.[1]
