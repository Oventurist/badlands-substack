# Boris Yeltsin

Boris Yeltsin was the first president of post-Soviet Russia, whose 1990s tenure is remembered in Badlands commentary as the era of "shock therapy" privatizations that produced a rapacious Russian oligarchy.[1]

## Shock therapy and the oligarchs

In an October 2025 Badlands News Brief analysis, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] recalled the post-Soviet chaos of the 1990s, when Yeltsin's shock-therapy privatizations birthed a class of tycoons — among them [[mikhail-khodorkovsky|Mikhail Khodorkovsky]] and [[boris-berezovsky|Boris Berezovsky]] — who snapped up state assets at fire-sale prices and amassed fortunes in energy, media and metals while ordinary Russians plunged into poverty.[1] The commentary framed this period not as genuine market reform but as a "disguised piratization" of Russian national wealth by Western-backed predators.[1]

## Legacy under Putin

The Yeltsin settlement was subsequently inverted by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], who after ascending in 2000 waged a calculated campaign against the new elite — not to eradicate wealth, but to subordinate it to sovereign will through state stakes in strategic companies.[1] [[yukos|Yukos Oil]], once Khodorkovsky's empire, was dismantled through tax-evasion charges and its assets absorbed by state-controlled [[rosneft|Rosneft]]; [[gazprom|Gazprom]] reclaimed majority control over natural-gas assets; and aerospace and nuclear energy saw outright state takeovers.[1] Badlands commentary treats this reversal of the Yeltsin era as the template later echoed, in its reading, by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[1]
