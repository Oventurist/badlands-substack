# Goldman Sachs

Goldman Sachs is a New York-based global investment bank whose philanthropic arm was identified as a conduit in the alleged [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]] funding network.[1]

## GS Donor Advised Philanthropy Fund

According to figures reported in the grand jury coverage, $110,376,701 of roughly $278 million moved from Singham's Shanghai base passed through the GS Donor Advised Philanthropy Fund For Wealth Management Inc., described as Goldman Sachs' philanthropy arm in New York City.[1] Funds from that and other channels were then directed into US nonprofits including [[peoples-forum|People's Forum Inc.]], [[tricontinental|Tricontinental Ltd.]], and [[codepink|CodePink Women For Peace]].[1]

Sources indicated that Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] met with Goldman Sachs Chairman and CEO [[david-solomon|David Solomon]] in New York earlier in 2026 and delivered a direct message urging cooperation with investigators, while warning of potential scrutiny over the philanthropy fund's role in moving Singham's money.[1]

## Ruemmler resignation (2026)
[[kathryn-ruemmler|Kathryn Ruemmler]], the bank's top lawyer and a former Obama White House counsel, resigned in 2026 amid scrutiny of her years-long relationship with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], shortly before she was questioned by the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]].[2]

## April 2025 recession call reversal

On the afternoon of April 9, 2025, Goldman Sachs economists walked back a recession forecast after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced a 90-day pause on tariffs that had threatened to destabilize the economy.[3] The bank sent out a note calling for a 45% chance of a recession within a year, retracting a call it had made just over an hour earlier that had raised the probability to 65%.[3] The about-face highlighted the difficulty of predicting the economy's trajectory amid frequent changes to trade policy, which economists had flagged as a major risk because businesses may delay investment and hiring decisions until they know what rules will apply long term.[3] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] noted the timing — a US recession called at 12:57 p.m. and rescinded 73 minutes later — and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the episode showed Trump exposing the global stock market as narrative, writing "He who wins the story wins the war."[3]

## Rishi Sunak appointment

In July 2025 former British prime minister [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]] was reported to be returning to Goldman Sachs as a senior adviser, his first major role since resigning as Conservative leader.[4] Sunak had begun his career at the bank as a summer intern in 2001 and left as a junior analyst in 2004 to work at a hedge fund.[4] The UK's independent Advisory Committee on Business Appointments recommended that he not take up the role until at least twelve months after leaving government and that he avoid lobbying-related projects for a further year; Sunak said he would donate his entire salary from the bank to the Richmond Project, a maths-skills charity he founded with his wife.[4]

## ESG posture and fossil fuel financing

In September 2023, Goldman Sachs CEO [[david-solomon|David Solomon]] publicly rejected calls to end the bank's relationships with fossil fuel companies, saying traditional energy firms were "hugely important" both to the global economy and to Goldman Sachs and that the firm would "continue to finance traditional companies for a long time."[5] He acknowledged that an energy transition was needed but insisted it would take time.[5] The comments came amid mounting scrutiny of [[esg|ESG]] investing, including a $25 million [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] penalty against a Deutsche Bank subsidiary over ESG misstatements and anti-money-laundering violations.[5]

Goldman Sachs had itself been fined millions of dollars by the SEC in 2022 for misreporting and mismanaging its ESG investment vehicles.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the bank's apparent retreat was rhetorical rather than real, citing a market-sizing study that listed Goldman Sachs alongside [[blackrock|BlackRock]] among the leading contributors to projected ESG market growth through 2030, and contending that Goldman needed fossil fuel revenue to subsidize its green agenda.[5] She wrote that "the consent on ESG has been largely manufactured by entities like Goldman Sachs," and drew a parallel with [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]]'s earlier apparent rebuke of ESG's effect on business performance.[5]
