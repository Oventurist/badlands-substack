# Jeffrey Goldberg

Jeffrey Goldberg is the editor-in-chief of The Atlantic.[1]

In March 2025 Goldberg was mistakenly included in a [[signal|Signal]] group chat in which Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and other senior Trump administration officials discussed military plans to strike Houthi targets in Yemen.[1] His inadvertent inclusion in the chat on an unclassified commercial application became the factual basis for a lawsuit filed by the watchdog group [[american-oversight|American Oversight]], which alleged that the officials' conduct violated the [[federal-records-act|Federal Records Act]].[1]

## 2024 Vanessa Guillén report

In October 2024, Goldberg published a report in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] alleging, on the basis of an anonymous source, that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had reacted with outrage upon learning that the funeral of slain soldier [[vanessa-guillen|Vanessa Guillén]] cost $60,000, and had ordered then–White House Chief of Staff [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]] to deny payment.[2] The account was described by critics as an attempt to rewrite the history of Trump's well-documented outreach to the Guillén family, which included extending death benefits and offering to pay funeral costs personally.[2]

Guillén's sister Mayra publicly rejected the story on X, writing that the magazine was exploiting her sister's death for politics and stating that Trump had shown nothing but respect to her family; her post drew more than three million views.[2] Badlands commentary called for Trump to file a slander and libel suit against Goldberg, characterizing the piece as an "absurd work of fiction."[2]

Commentators in the brief also scrutinized Goldberg's background, noting his Israeli-American dual citizenship and his service in the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] as a prison guard during the First Intifada, and questioning why prominent Israeli-American figures opposed Trump despite his record toward [[israel|Israel]] — a question Trump himself had raised when urging such voters to "get smart" about supporting him.[2]
