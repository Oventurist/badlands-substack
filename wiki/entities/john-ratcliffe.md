# John Ratcliffe

John Ratcliffe is the Director of the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] under the second Trump administration.[1]

In January 2026 Ratcliffe traveled to Caracas to meet acting Venezuelan President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], the highest-ranking U.S. official visit since the ouster of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1] The two-hour meeting focused on strengthening bilateral cooperation, including security matters and efforts to curb Venezuela's role as a haven for criminal networks.[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the visit as evidence that several important players from [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s [[devolution|Devolution]] theory are involved in the diplomacy with the Maduro/Rodríguez government, which he described as operating under a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocol.[1]

## Cuba backchannel (May 2026)

As CIA Director, Ratcliffe reportedly met in May 2026 with Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] and senior regime officials for talks.[2] The meeting signaled that backchannel communication between Washington and Havana remained active even as the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration tightened sanctions and pursued regional isolation amid worsening blackouts and fuel shortages in [[cuba|Cuba]].[2] Analysts read the combination of economic pressure and quiet diplomatic engagement as an attempt to force concessions from Havana while preventing broader regional instability.[2]

## Caracas visit (January 2026)

Ratcliffe became the first senior [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration official to visit Caracas following the capture of former Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], meeting interim President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] and military leaders.[3] He warned that [[venezuela|Venezuela]] could no longer serve as a safe haven for US adversaries such as [[china|China]], Russia and [[iran|Iran]].[3] The United States had backed Rodríguez over opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] on the basis of classified CIA assessments, and the agency was leading initial re-entry efforts, prioritizing an intelligence annex for informal liaison channels ahead of full diplomatic operations.[3] See [[cia-re-entry-into-venezuela-2026|CIA re-entry into Venezuela (2026)]].[3]

## March 2026 Senate Intelligence hearing

As [[cia|CIA]] Director, Ratcliffe joined Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and other intelligence chiefs at the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]]'s annual hearing on Wednesday, March 18, 2026, a session dominated by the departure of [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]] Director [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]] the previous day.[4] The hearing covered US policy toward [[iran|Iran]], regional tensions, military posture in the Middle East and escalation risks, and concluded with a classified session.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted an exchange in which Senator [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]] (D-CO) "got into a slap fight" with Ratcliffe and asked the chairman to make the Director stop talking, describing the panelists overall as calm and patient under hostile questioning.[4]

## 2025 tradecraft review of the 2016 election IC assessment

On July 2, 2025, as CIA Director, Ratcliffe released a declassified "lessons-learned" review of the procedures and tradecraft employed in preparing the Intelligence Community Assessment conducted shortly after the 2016 election.[5] The release was publicized by the agency and framed as an internal tradecraft review.[5] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] said he would like to see Ratcliffe also release the still-classified report referenced in the review — the material [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] "collected in July" of 2016 and kept highly classified.[5] Herold linked that timeline to July 26, 2016, when [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] is alleged to have approved a plan to vilify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and to July 28, when Brennan first met [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] to discuss "Russian malign influence" and create the inter-agency Fusion Cell.[5]

## Statements on the Durham annex (July 2025)

In a July 2025 interview with Maria Bartiromo on Fox News' "Sunday Morning Futures," Ratcliffe said newly released material showed that [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] and [[james-comey|James Comey]] had pushed the known-fake [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] into intelligence community assessments, forming the basis for [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]].[6] He said he had spent the preceding months making declassification recommendations on the underlying intelligence and had sent them to the [[justice-department|Department of Justice]] for release in the [[durham-annex|Durham report classified annex]].[6]

Ratcliffe stated that the intelligence shows part of the operation "was a [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] plan," while part was an FBI plan to act as an accelerant to the fake dossier and Russia collusion claims "by pouring oil on the fire, by amplifying the lie and burying the truth of what Hillary Clinton was up to."[6] He added that Clinton had testified before [[john-durham|John Durham]] under oath in 2022 and that much of that testimony is "frankly completely inconsistent" with the intelligence set for declassification in the annex.[6]

## Statute of limitations comments (July 2025)

On Sunday, July 27, 2025, Ratcliffe said that the statute of limitations likely would not rule out potential charges against former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and key Obama intelligence officials alleged to have conspired to undermine [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] through intelligence reporting on Russian interference in the 2016 election.[7] "I don't think statutes of limitations are going to impact because in the conspiracy, the statute of limitation doesn't start to run until the last act and furtherance of that conspiracy," he said during a Fox News appearance.[7]

He added that the matter was important because "the people behind this are still furthering the conspiracy," refusing to admit or acknowledge that what they did in 2016 and 2020 was wrong.[7] The comments followed Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s accusation that Obama, former DNI [[james-clapper|James Clapper]], former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] and former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] held a meeting after Trump's 2016 victory at which they allegedly planned the "Russia Hoax."[7] See [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate hoax]].[7]

## Confirmation as CIA director (January 2025)

The [[united-states-senate|Senate]] voted on Thursday, January 23, 2025, to confirm Ratcliffe as [[cia|CIA]] director under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], approving the former Texas congressman 74–25 in what was described as the second major appointment of the new administration.[8] During his confirmation hearing Ratcliffe vowed to keep politics out of intelligence-related decisions and assured senators that loyalty tests would not influence the hiring or firing of CIA personnel.[8] He had previously served as Trump's Director of National Intelligence during the final eight months of the first term, having been confirmed to that post in May 2020 by a narrow Senate vote of 49–44.[8]

## Statement on the DC National Guard shooting suspect

Following the [[2025-washington-dc-national-guard-shooting|November 26, 2025 shooting of two National Guard members]] near the White House, Ratcliffe as [[cia|CIA]] Director announced on the Thursday after the attack that the suspect, [[rahmanullah-lakanwal|Rahmanullah Lakanwal]], had been admitted into the United States in September 2021 "due to his prior work with the US Government, including the CIA, as a member of a partner force in Kandahar, which ended shortly following the chaotic evacuation."[9] The acknowledgment confirmed that the alleged shooter had been a paid partner-force asset in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] and made the agency's Afghan partner programs a central subject of the ensuing controversy.[9]

## Brennan criminal referral and the Miami grand jury (December 2025)

Whistleblower evidence released by Ratcliffe as [[cia|CIA]] Director and by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] challenged the [[2017-intelligence-assessment-on-russian-interference|2017 Intelligence Community Assessment]]'s conclusion that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] sought to help President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], showing internal dissent and questioning the report's rushed release.[10] The disclosures raised concerns about former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]'s push to include the discredited [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] in the assessment, prompting Ratcliffe and Gabbard to refer Brennan for possible prosecution; by December 2025 federal prosecutors had identified Brennan as the target of a criminal grand jury investigation in Miami.[10]

## Suggestion of prosecutions for Hillary Clinton (July 2025)

In a July 28, 2025 interview with Maria Bartiromo on Fox News, Ratcliffe expanded on the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s allegations that former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and some of his officials fabricated "Russiagate" to undermine [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2016, and suggested that several figures — including Trump's 2016 rival [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], who served as Obama's secretary of state — could face indictments or prosecutions.[11] "This was a Hillary Clinton campaign scheme," Ratcliffe said, alleging that Clinton conspired to "falsely accuse" Trump of colluding with Russia in what became known as the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[11] He further claimed that Clinton, former [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] and former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] lied under oath about their involvement in the Russian election-interference narrative.[11] Asked by Bartiromo whether there remained "an opportunity for indictments, potential prosecutions, accountability," Ratcliffe replied: "Well, that's why I've made the referrals that I have."[11]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that "Grand Conspiracy" would become the legally codified label for Disclosure across the American political, legal and clandestine battlespace, noting that Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] had codified the framing days earlier and that the words "treason" and "conspiracy" were being restored to their original usage after decades of obfuscation by US intelligence since 1963.[11] He contended that the parallel runs of disclosure on the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein narrative]] and [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] gave Trump a bipartisan public mandate and durable "narrative shielding."[11]
