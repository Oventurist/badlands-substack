# Mike Johnston

Mike Johnston is the mayor of Denver, Colorado, and a central figure in the confrontation between municipal officials and federal immigration authorities during the second Trump administration.[1]

## Executive Order 152

On Thursday, February 26, 2026, Johnston signed Denver Executive Order 152, an action framed as a response to concerns over federal immigration enforcement in the city, including mass deportations and operations by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]].[1] The order bars ICE and other federal immigration agents from using city-owned or city-controlled property for immigration enforcement operations absent a judicial warrant, subpoena, or court order, and bars city agencies from sharing databases or technology with the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] or its immigration partners without legal compulsion.[1]

The order further directs the Denver Police and other local law enforcement to employ de-escalation protocols to protect peaceful protesters during civil immigration enforcement operations; to identify ICE agents using body cameras when tactically safe; to render life-saving aid to anyone harmed; and to intervene in cases of excessive force, including the potential detention of federal agents.[1]

Johnston stated that the city would "continue" to cooperate with ICE on violent criminals where a warrant exists.[1] Explaining his rationale, he said: "My first job is to run the city. But in today's America, that means answering questions from our residents about what happens if ICE troops descend on our city."[1]

## Reception and criticism

The Department of Homeland Security responded on Friday by calling the order "legally illiterate."[1] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] endorsed that characterization, arguing that local governments cannot lawfully prevent federal agents from enforcing immigration law in public spaces or on non-city property because federal supremacy generally prevails in immigration enforcement, and suggested that knowingly proceeding anyway amounts to "insurrection."[1] She also noted that violent crime in Denver increased 15.6% under Johnston and is now declining, arguing the decline is attributable to federal pressure.[1] Badlands commentary framed the dispute as a long-anticipated "Rocky Mountain Showdown" between Johnston and border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], predicted since 2024, and paired it with the more than 50 lawsuits filed by [[phil-weiser|Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser]] against federal policy.[1]

## March 2025 House Oversight testimony

Johnston had earlier testified, on Wednesday, March 5, 2025, before the Republican-led [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] alongside fellow Democratic mayors [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] of New York, [[brandon-johnson|Brandon Johnson]] of Chicago and [[michelle-wu|Michelle Wu]] of Boston, defending their jurisdictions' [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary]] immigration policies.[2] Republicans accused the mayors of defying President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s immigration agenda and releasing dangerous criminals onto the streets, while the mayors argued the policies were crucial so undocumented immigrants would report crimes and access city services without fear of deportation.[2] Representative [[anna-paulina-luna|Anna Paulina Luna]] told Johnston — who Badlands reported had spent $2 million of taxpayer dollars preparing for the hearing — that she was referring him to the Department of Justice for harboring violent criminal illegal aliens.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she was skeptical anything would come of the threat but called it "the right direction under the circumstances," adding that "Denver taxpayers are chumps."[2]

## Backpedaling on sanctuary posture (January 2025)

Badlands Media coverage of the opening week of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term reported that the posture of United States law enforcement toward illegal immigration had changed radically "with the stroke of a pen," with [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] going to work and local jurisdictions defying their states by refusing sanctuary status and cooperating with federal authorities.[3] Contributor Ashe in America singled out Denver Mayor Mike Johnston as "backpedaling" amid the shift, framing the trend as strong leadership and stating that "criminal invaders are finally learning about consequences."[3]

## Pledge to resist ICE (November 2024)

In November 2024, ahead of the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s planned mass deportation campaign, Johnston said he would direct Denver's police force to resist [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] officials entering the city, telling a local news outlet that he would go so far as to be arrested over the matter, and suggesting the country might see a new Tiananmen Square moment.[4] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the remarks as evidence of domestic officials preparing to obstruct federal enforcement, contrasting them with border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]]'s warning that "in sanctuary cities, expect a lot of collateral arrests."[4]
