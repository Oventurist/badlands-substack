# Eugene Hasenfus

Eugene Hasenfus was the American crewman captured by Nicaraguan troops on October 5, 1986 after the cargo plane he was flying in was shot down over [[nicaragua]], an event that began the unravelling of the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] cover-up.[1] Hasenfus had been shipping military supplies into Nicaragua for use by the Contras, an anti-Sandinista force created and supported by the United States in violation of congressional action stopping the funding, and run by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[1]

The aircraft was a [[southern-air-transport|Southern Air Transport]] C-123K loaded with 10,000 pounds of ammunition, dispatched from the [[ilopango-air-base|Ilopango]] air base in El Salvador.[1] Sandinista government artillerymen knocked the plane out of the sky, killing the pilot and copilot; Hasenfus, the third crewman, survived and was captured.[1] One of the men killed was former CIA proprietary pilot William J. Cooper, who carried a Southern Air identification card; the business card of company president Langton was also found among the papers aboard.[1]

The incident caused an immediate firestorm in Washington that led to years of media headlines, congressional investigations and prosecutions by a Special Counsel, and became the largest stain on the presidency of [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]].[1] Joe Lange characterizes it as one of the rare moments when the entrenched [[shadow-government]] appeared genuinely vulnerable.[1]
