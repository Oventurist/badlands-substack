# Colorado

Colorado is a US state whose overseas voter registration guidance became the subject of litigation in June 2026.[1]

## Overseas voting litigation

The [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], joined by Douglas County Clerk [[sheri-davis|Sheri Davis]] and US Representative [[jeff-crank|Jeff Crank]], filed suit in Denver District Court against Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]].[1] The complaint targets state guidance interpreting the federal [[uocava|Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act]], under which some citizens abroad may register in Colorado without ever having lived there if a parent, guardian, spouse, or domestic partner previously resided in the state.[1]

The plaintiffs contend that residency cannot be established through family ties and that persons who have never personally lived in Colorado do not qualify as residents under the state constitution; they ask the court to void the policy and strike affected registrations.[1] The filing came during the active election period, immediately before Colorado's 2026 primary election day — timing that [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued nearly guarantees the suit's failure and raises the question of whether the litigation amounts to political bolstering and abuse of process.[1]

## Badlands commentary

In June 2026 [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing about proposals to tax [[anti-weaponization-fund|anti-weaponization fund]] disbursements at 100%, described Colorado as "the communist country of Colorado" and apologized to the country for its senator, [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]].[2]

## 2024 ballot litigation and lawfare

Colorado remained a central battlefield in the election-lawfare domain at the start of 2024, with the Anderson v. Griswold appeals heading to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]], the trial of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] scheduled for early February, and a separate trial challenging the lawful First Amendment activities of the Colorado grassroots set for that same week.[3] Although [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] remained on the Colorado primary ballot, the trial court's finding of fact that January 6 was an insurrection — upheld by the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] — was described by Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] as the decisive product of the case, supplying the predicate for Maine Secretary of State [[shenna-bellows|Shenna Bellows]] to bar Trump from that state's ballot.[3] She warned the same finding could bar military-veteran January 6 attendees, such as [[ron-hanks|Ron Hanks]], from running for office in Colorado.[3] In September 2021 Colorado Newsline had published a list of "Colorado's Top 10 Most Dangerous Election Deniers" naming both Hanks and Ashe in America.[3]

## Loss of Space Command (2025)

On September 2, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that [[united-states-space-command|U.S. Space Command]] would relocate from Colorado to [[huntsville-alabama|Huntsville, Alabama]], reversing a Biden-era decision to keep the command in Colorado Springs.[4] The command employs roughly 1,700 people in Colorado Springs and, per the Denver Metro Chamber of Commerce, supports about 1,400 additional direct regional jobs, contributing an estimated $1 billion a year to the local economy through direct spending, salaries and indirect activity.[4] Badlands commentary added that the loss carries multiplier effects: depressed economic growth, sunk costs on Space Command infrastructure, and discouraged future investment in the state's space sector.[4]

Trump publicly attributed the decision in part to Colorado's elections, saying "the problem I have with Colorado… they do mail-in voting, they went to all mail-in voting, so they have automatically crooked elections."[4] Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the relocation as one of the "harsh measures" Trump had promised over the prosecution of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] in [[mesa-county|Mesa County]] — a state-level case he cannot pardon — leaving funding and investigations as his available levers.[4] Colorado legislators had recently held a special session attempting to address the impact of an earlier round of federal cuts to the state, without success.[4]
