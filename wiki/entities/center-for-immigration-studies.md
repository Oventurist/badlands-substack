# Center for Immigration Studies

The **Center for Immigration Studies** (CIS) is a Washington-based research organization focused on U.S. immigration policy. In March 2024 the group's ongoing Freedom of Information Act litigation produced disclosures about a [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration program flying inadmissible aliens directly into American airports.[1]

Through the FOIA lawsuit, the public learned that [[customs-and-border-protection|U.S. Customs and Border Protection]] had approved secretive flights that in a single year ferried hundreds of thousands of inadmissible aliens from foreign airports into some 43 American ones, all pre-approved through a cell phone application.[1] CBP withheld from the Center — and has apparently declined to disclose — the names of the 43 receiving U.S. airports that took in 320,000 inadmissible aliens between January and December 2023, as well as the foreign airports of departure, with agency lawyers initially citing a general "law enforcement exception" and "the sensitivity of the information."[1]

CIS reporting, authored by Todd Bensman, highlighted a subsequent government answer as novel and newsworthy: the public cannot know the receiving airports because the CBP-authorized arrivals have created such "operational vulnerabilities" that "bad actors" could undermine efforts to "secure the United States border" if they knew the volume of CBP One traffic processed at each port of entry.[1] The organization framed this as a government admission that a legally dubious program to fly inadmissible aliens over the border had created law enforcement vulnerabilities too grave to release publicly.[1] The reporting also noted that large immigrant-receiving cities and much of the media had instead blamed the influx on Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]]'s migrant busing program.[1]
