# District of Columbia Bar

The District of Columbia Bar and its associated disciplinary authorities regulate the licensing and professional conduct of attorneys practicing in Washington, DC — a body of lawyers that includes much of the federal government's legal workforce. That overlap placed the DC bar at the center of a constitutional dispute with the executive branch in 2026.

## Clark disciplinary matter and DOJ suit

As reported in the [[badlands-brief-323|Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026]], DC disciplinary authorities pursued a case against former Assistant Attorney General [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] centered on internal deliberations related to potential fraud in the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]], ultimately recommending disbarment over conduct characterized as dishonest and tied to an unsent draft memorandum. The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] responded by filing a complaint against the disciplinary authorities, alleging they are improperly using the attorney discipline process to regulate the official actions of federal government lawyers and asking a court to void the proceedings as violations of the [[supremacy-clause|Supremacy Clause]] and Article II.

Department officials argued that the disciplinary process improperly targets executive branch deliberations and risks deterring federal attorneys from providing candid legal advice. Commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the dispute as a test of whether "local bar regulators have no oversight of presidential legal decision-making through 'ethics' reviews," suggesting the disciplinary campaign "may be boomeranging" and expressing hope that a favorable ruling would establish precedent applicable at other levels of government.
