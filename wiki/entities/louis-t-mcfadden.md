# Louis T. McFadden

Louis T. McFadden was a United States congressman known for his denunciations of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] system from the floor of the House. Badlands Media cites his remarks in the Congressional Record as primary evidence for its account of how the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]] was enacted.[1]

## Congressional Record remarks

The passage quoted by Badlands appears in the Congressional Record at pages 12596–12693, dated June 10, 1932.[1] In it McFadden reproduces a letter written by [[edward-mandell-house|Colonel Edward Mandell House]] on the night of December 23, 1913, when the Federal Reserve bill became law, addressed to what McFadden calls House's "hidden master in Wall Street."[1] The letter praises the recipient's silent but effective work on currency legislation and concedes that no perfect bill was possible, describing the measure as good enough to start with and open to later perfection.[1]

McFadden interpreted the letter as striking evidence of the manner in which predatory interests sought to control the United States government by surrounding the executive with the influence of a "financial Judas."[1] He argued that, left to its own legislative functions and free of executive pressure, Congress would not have passed the Federal Reserve Act, and that the Act became law the day before Christmas Eve in 1913 because President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] stood firm under the control of New York financiers acting through House.[1] McFadden added that shortly afterwards the German international bankers [[kuhn-loeb-and-co|Kuhn, Loeb & Co.]] sent one of their partners to run the new central bank.[1]
