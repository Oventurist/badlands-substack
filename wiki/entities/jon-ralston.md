# Jon Ralston

Jon Ralston is a veteran Nevada political reporter who has covered Silver State politics for three decades and writes for the *Nevada Independent*.[1] His early-voting blog is treated by national outlets as a benchmark for reading turnout trends in the state.[1]

## 2024 early-voting commentary

In an October 2024 blog post, Ralston reported that Republican voters were outnumbering Democrats in Nevada's in-person early voting, an outcome he characterized as unusual for a presidential year: "The numbers look pretty GOP so far, and that never happens in a presidential year."[1] He noted that Democrats led statewide mail-in voting by 17,298 ballots while Republicans led in-person voting by 25,173, including in [[clark-county|Clark County]] and Washoe County, home to Las Vegas and Reno respectively.[1] Statewide, Republicans accounted for 52 percent of in-person early voting, Democrats 28 percent, and unaffiliated or "other" voters 20 percent.[1]

Ralston called the in-person figures "startling" and warned that "a few more days like this...and the Democratic bedwetting will reach epic proportions."[1] He also observed that the so-called "Clark firewall" — the Democratic vote cushion generated by the Las Vegas area — stood at only about 6,500, roughly one-seventh of its 2020 level, and that the Republican turnout lead in rural [[nevada|Nevada]] was more than double the Democratic lead in urban Nevada.[1] From this he coined a new formulation for the cycle: "The [GOP] rural firewall. It's a thing."[1]

## Reception in alternative media

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Ralston's numbers as a "very damning data point" while cautioning readers that turnout by party registration is not the same as vote totals.[1] The same commentary linked the trend to the work of [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]], who treats early-vote turnout as a leading indicator, and speculated that similar patterns in Georgia, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin could point to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] winning the national popular vote.[1]
