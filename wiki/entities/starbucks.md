# Starbucks

Starbucks is an American coffeehouse chain. In the Badlands corpus it serves as the principal anecdotal illustration of the claim that the built environment of American commerce has been deliberately reshaped to reduce face-to-face human contact.

## The drive-through observation

Erik Carlson's [[america-has-a-cia-run-economy|America Has a CIA-Run Economy]] recounts that in the years immediately preceding the Covid lockdowns, the author noticed how many Starbucks stores without drive-throughs were being replaced by stores with them, and that almost all newly built locations included a drive-through. He pairs this with the company's simultaneous push toward ordering ahead, a feature that "allows one to minimize the interactions they have with anyone in the store." Carlson asks pointedly, "How did they foresee what was coming?"

## Removal of seating

In the five years after the lockdowns, the essay reports, much of the seating in many Starbucks locations has been permanently removed, "as if they don't want to promote people being in community with each other." Carlson emphasizes the significance of this in smaller towns, where a Starbucks often functions as the town center — the place people go to meet, have a cup of coffee, and share a conversation. That role, he argues, is being replaced by customers who never leave their cars or who order ahead and walk in and out without any human interaction.

## Wider thesis

The Starbucks case supports the essay's larger claim that "part of the technology push we have experienced the past 20 years is to minimize the human interactions we have with each other on a daily basis" — reducing connection, empathy, and ultimately procreation, with an endpoint in which people rarely leave their apartments in fifteen-minute cities. The article anticipates the objection that these changes are simply about convenience and dismisses it: "Sure it is."
