# Communist China

Communist China is described by Erik Carlson as a state run by the [[city-of-london|City of London]] rather than an independent adversary of the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Claimed origins of China's rise

Carlson observes that when the Soviet Union fell in the early 1990s, Japan was regarded as the largest economic competitor to the United States; instead Japan entered a thirty-year economic funk while China became an economic juggernaut.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) He reads this as the City of London pivoting from controlling the Soviet Union — which it had held since the [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik Revolution]] — to controlling Communist China.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Role in Trump's campaign

In his second term, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is onshoring manufacturing, moving it out of Communist China and back to America.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) Carlson argues that while observers focus on Israel, Iran or China, the enemy actually in charge occupies just one square mile of London, and that China should be understood as the recognizable face rather than the decision-maker — his *Office Space* manager analogy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## The $400 billion Iran agreement

In [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s account of the [[iran|Iran]] question, China is a central actor: within two months of taking office [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] allowed China to sign a $400 billion deal with Iran, an oil and gas agreement that would also create a comprehensive economic and military partnership between the two nations.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Iran would be folded into the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], the centerpiece of Chinese foreign policy, so that Beijing would not only pay large sums for oil and gas but would also initiate sweeping infrastructure investments in Iran in much the same way the United States did in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Alignments and consequences

DeLarme argues that the wizards behind [[nato|NATO]] have been engaged in an undeclared second cold war with Russia "all while being in bed with China," and that China was aligned with the Atlanticists and the Democrats/neoconservatives while Trump world, populated by notorious China hawks, was pushed toward the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Relations between China and [[israel|Israel]] had been relatively good and mutually profitable, but strained once Beijing committed nearly half a trillion dollars to the state funding the proxies on Israel's borders; Iran was in any case far more essential to the BRI than Israel could ever be.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Chinese money also flowed to the [[carnegie-endowment-for-international-peace|Carnegie Endowment]] under [[william-burns|William Burns]], and the Biden family had a public history of profiting from CEFC China Energy.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) The extent to which China is willing to back Iran is presented as one of the great unknowns: if it protects its investment by arming Iran and its proxies beyond what the Iron Dome and David's Sling can intercept, the question becomes whether the United States would risk hot war with China.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Response to the 2026 strikes on Iran

In early March 2026, after the United States joined [[israel|Israel]] in military strikes against [[iran|Iran]], China condemned the strikes and called for an immediate ceasefire and a return to diplomatic negotiations.[3] Badlands commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], like [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], was incentivized to remain on the sidelines while the initial geopolitical shock settled, allowing Beijing to save face domestically if the post-conflict Iranian government closely resembles the one it replaced.[3]
