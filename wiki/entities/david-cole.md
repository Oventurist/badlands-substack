# David Cole

David Cole is a Republican Alabama state representative for House District 10, based in Huntsville, who was arrested in August 2023 on charges of voter fraud.[1]

## Election and residency dispute

Cole took office in 2022 following the ten-year tenure of Mike Ball.[1] He won the House District 10 seat with 51.6% of the general-election vote; Democrat Marilyn Lands received 45% and Libertarian [[elijah-boyd|Elijah Boyd]] 3.4%.[1]

Earlier in 2023, Cole's residency was questioned by Boyd, who alleged that Cole had claimed a residence in District 10 when declaring for the race while tax documents showed his residence to be in District 4.[1] Boyd filed a complaint contesting the election in Madison County Circuit Court on November 22, arguing that Cole was ineligible under state law because he was not a resident of the district, nor had he been a resident for the year preceding the November 8 general election.[1] Alabama's constitution requires state representatives to be citizens and residents of the state for three years and residents of their county or district for one year before their election.[1]

## Arrest

Cole was arrested on Tuesday, August 29, 2023 on charges stemming from long-standing complaints about the 2022 election, charged with fraud by voting at multiple or unauthorized locations.[1] He was booked into the Madison County Jail and held on a $2,500 bond.[1] The full extent of the charges remained unclear, though the office of Alabama Attorney General [[steve-marshall|Steve Marshall]] was involved in the case.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for Badlands Media, argued that "everyone involved in election fraud must be prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law," describing election fraud as non-partisan and asserting that "both wings of the uniparty engage in election fraud, from manipulation of the candidate selection processes to the primaries to the generals."[1] She acknowledged that the specifics of the charges were still unclear and that Cole had a right to due process, but said that if convicted "he should have the book thrown at him," adding a hope that the case would help people on the right recognise that "it's not left vs. right. It's us vs. them."[1]
