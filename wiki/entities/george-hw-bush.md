# George H.W. Bush

George H.W. Bush, the 41st president of the United States, is portrayed in the Badlands series as the central figure of a multigenerational family enterprise fused with the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]]. The article argues that while the Biden family's corruption appears rooted in greed, Bush family corruption "is far more evil," involving far more money and a disregard for human life, and placing the family above the law for decades.[1]

## Houston and Zapata

Bush made Houston his home and his base of operations from the 1950s onward, running the CIA front company [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]] from the city.[1] He and J. Hugh Liedtke, later head of Pennzoil, formed Zapata Petroleum Corporation with Houston headquarters in 1953 — the same year [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]] was created through the merger of South Texas National and Union National Bank. The article treats the simultaneity as deliberate: a CIA bank established to service a CIA front company.[1]

## Bay of Pigs

A vanity book marking [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]]'s 1986 centenary, commissioned by bank president Ben Love, describes the institution's cozy relationship with Bush and Zapata Off-Shore, which was linked to the CIA's abortive [[bay-of-pigs-invasion|Bay of Pigs invasion]] of Cuba in 1961.[1] Three Zapata-owned ships financed by Texas National Bank — the *Houston*, the *Barbara J* and the *Zapata* — were loaned by Bush's firm to the CIA for the invasion, which the agency code-named OPERATION ZAPATA.[1]

Bush consistently denied any CIA membership before becoming director in 1976, a posture the article summarizes as the family motto: "Admit nothing and deny everything."[1] It contends he had led clandestine operations since the early 1950s and commanded deep ties to the Cuban exile population the CIA recruited for the Bay of Pigs and later operations — a force trained in paramilitary work, terrorism, assassination and money laundering — and notes that the CIA named its new headquarters after him.[1]

## Networks and succession

Bush's closest institutional ally was [[james-baker|James Baker]], whose family founded Texas Commerce Bank and who served in his administration; a single call to Baker secured a CIA non-official-cover post for his son [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] in the bank's international division.[1] Through the earlier article in the series, Bush is tied to the [[salinas-family|Salinas family]] of Mexico — "Intimo Amigos" — and to [[nafta|NAFTA]], which the author characterizes as a mechanism for expanding drug trafficking across the southern border to increase CIA profits.[1] Cuban exile businessmen in Miami, among them [[armando-codina|Armando Codina]], were early financial supporters of his presidential campaign and later enriched his son.[1]
