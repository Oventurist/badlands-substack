# George H.W. Bush

George Herbert Walker Bush was a Texas oilman, Director of Central Intelligence, Vice President and President of the United States. In the Badlands Media account, he is the central figure of a multi-generational family enterprise that the author describes as far more corrupt than the Biden family whose dealings the mainstream media was, at the time of writing, finally being forced to acknowledge — a corruption said to involve far more money and a complete disregard for American lives.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Houston and Zapata

Bush made Houston, Texas his home, and the city served as his base of operations going back to the 1950s for what the article characterizes as his CIA front company, [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) In 1953 Bush and J. Hugh Liedtke — later head of Pennzoil — formed Zapata Petroleum Corporation with headquarters in Houston, the same year that [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]] was created through the merger of South Texas National and Union National Bank.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article treats the simultaneity as deliberate rather than coincidental: a bank established to service a CIA front company.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Bay of Pigs

Three ships owned by Zapata and financed by Texas National Bank — the *Houston*, the *Barbara J* and the *Zapata* — were loaned by Bush's firm to the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] for the 1961 invasion of Cuba by exiled Cuban mercenaries, an operation the agency code-named OPERATION ZAPATA.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) Bush always denied being a member of the CIA before becoming its director in 1976, a posture the author summarizes as the family motto: "Admit nothing and deny everything."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article argues that the mainstream media has never reported the [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]] connection because Bush was "an untouchable of the highest order."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Director of Central Intelligence (1976)

In 1976 President [[gerald-ford|Gerald Ford]] nominated Bush to be Director of the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]] in an attempt to save an agency at the lowest point in its history, under Senate investigation with its "dirty laundry" exposed and its very existence in question.[2] Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] described the appointment as a turning point that would shape the future of the CIA and of the world, and not for the better.[2]

Quoting the Miami Herald, Badlands noted that Bush "loved the CIA" and called it "part of my heartbeat"; he was the only president who ever ran the agency and the last who truly believed in its Cold War code, "Admit nothing, deny everything."[2] During his eleven months as director he spent much of his energy battling senators concluding their year-long investigation, which uncovered assassination plots, secret wars, antidemocratic coups and mind-control experiments using LSD on unwitting subjects.[2] Bush decried the revelations rather than the facts revealed, saying they "devastated the morale of perhaps the finest group of public servants this country has."[2] Lange argued Bush cared more about morale than about the agency's lawbreaking, and that he was installed to make the investigations go away — steering the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] era toward reforms rather than criminal charges.[2]

## Capture of the President's Daily Brief

A CIA.gov biography quoted by Badlands records that on becoming DCI, Bush insisted upon direct access to President Ford, usually editing the [[presidents-daily-brief|President's Daily Brief]] or briefing at National Security Council meetings in order to convey the agency's analysis directly to the president.[2] Ford frequently included Bush in high-level meetings, and Bush brought Agency officers along to brief the president, a tactic that impressed Ford and boosted morale at Langley.[2] As [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s vice president from 1981 he read the PDB avidly, and as the 41st president from 1989 he made a CIA briefing his first order of daily business.[2] Lange treats this institutional capture as Bush's most consequential achievement: it elevated the CIA within the executive branch, gave it influence over every administration since, and accelerated the formation of a [[shadow-government|shadow government]].[2]

## Denials of earlier CIA service

The central act was the [[deregulation|deregulation]] of banking, which allowed savings and loans to make commercial and unsecured business loans and to invest heavily in commercial real estate with 100 percent financing, under far more lenient standards; combined with the 1980 increase in [[deposit-insurance|deposit insurance]] this removed all risk from thrift operators.[7] Lange argues the real purpose was to create a nationwide [[real-estate-money-laundering|real estate money laundering]] system to fund the [[shadow-government|shadow government]] outside congressional and presidential oversight, and that many writers allege the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] used the thrift deregulation process to pay for covert operations Congress would not support.[7]

## Arming Saddam and the Gulf War distraction

In "Shifting the Narrative," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that Bush deliberately armed and financed [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] in the months before the Iraqi invasion of [[kuwait|Kuwait]], then reversed course and painted him as a new Hitler in order to prepare the American public for war.[14] Speaking in Mashpee, Massachusetts on the eve of the 1990 midterm campaign swing, Bush suggested the Iraqi president had surpassed Adolf Hitler in brutality and pledged to "see his aggression turned back," while the White House said he was preparing the American people for "any eventuality."[14] Lange reads the Hitler comparison as a purposeful emotional trigger — a cabal strategy in use since the end of the Second World War — and Bush's simultaneous professions that he "desperately" wanted a peaceful resolution as window dressing designed to cast him as a reluctant warrior.[14]

The record of material support is extensive in Lange's telling. After international bankers cut off virtually all loans to Saddam by the fall of 1989, [[james-baker|James Baker]] called Secretary of Agriculture Clayton Yeutter on October 31 and pressed for a billion dollars in new agricultural-loan guarantees for Iraq over the objections of the Agriculture Department and other agencies.[14] In January 1990 Bush waived congressional restrictions on Iraq's use of the Export-Import Bank while overlooking new evidence that Iraq was testing ballistic missiles and stealing nuclear technology; that April, after Saddam boasted he would "burn half of Israel" with chemical weapons, the Commerce Department's attempt to stop the flow of U.S. technology to Iraq was stymied by the White House, with one senior official explaining that "the President does not want to single out Iraq."[14] As late as May 1990 the administration was still sharing intelligence with Iraq, in July it pushed for additional agricultural loans and rebuffed Defense and Commerce Department efforts to restrict dual-use exports, and at the end of July Bush opposed congressional efforts to impose sanctions.[14] All told, the Reagan and Bush administrations provided Saddam with more than five billion dollars in loan guarantees, enabling him to become a major military power in the Persian Gulf and to invade Kuwait.[14]

Lange also cites Roger Morris's account that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] midwifed the Baathist regime through coups in 1963 and 1968 and that Saddam was on the agency payroll, concluding that Bush was the figure behind those operations and that he installed Saddam as dictator just as he had installed [[manuel-noriega|Manuel Noriega]] in Panama — both men customers of [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] alongside the CIA and the National Security Council.[14] He characterizes Bush as living by a CIA motto of "admit nothing and deny everything," and as possibly the most corrupt person ever to lead the country.[14]

The motive Lange assigns is distraction. As Congress issued subpoenas and foreign authorities raided BCCI branches in the biggest money-laundering scandal ever exposed, Bush — whose associate [[kamal-adham|Kamal Adham]] was a central BCCI front man and whose CIA depended on the bank for funding outside congressional control — needed the narrative changed, and Kuwait became "the sacrificial lamb."[14] Lange notes that Bush had deep ties to Kuwait going back to the 1950s, with one [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]] drilling site located there, and that BCCI had created an entity called the Kuwait International Finance Company (KIFCO), raising the question of what documents and wealth the war destroyed or plundered.[14]

## NAFTA and the Salinas family

In "The Legacy Of George H.W.
