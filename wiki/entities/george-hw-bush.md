# George H.W. Bush

George H.W. Bush (1924–2018) was the 41st President of the United States, serving from 1989 to 1993, and previously the director of central intelligence from 1976 to 1977. In the Badlands Media article [[a-family-affair]], he is portrayed as a lifelong CIA operative whose pre-political career was deeply embedded in covert operations, drug trafficking infrastructure, and money-laundering networks.

## Early career and Zapata Offshore

According to the source, Bush formed [[zapata-offshore]] (originally Zapata Petroleum Corporation) in Houston in 1953 with J. Hugh Liedtke. The article claims the company was a CIA front from its inception, financed in its early years by [[texas-national-bank]]. Three Zapata-owned ships—the *Houston*, *Barbara J*, and *Zapata*—were reportedly loaned to the CIA for the 1961 [[bay-of-pigs-invasion]], which the agency code-named [[operation-zapata]]. The source emphasizes that Bush consistently denied CIA membership before his 1976 appointment as director.

## Texas Commerce Bank

The article describes [[texas-commerce-bank]]—which evolved from Texas National Bank—as the financial hub of Bush's CIA network. Its board included figures such as [[james-baker]]'s family, Lady Bird Johnson, and Gerald Ford. The bank's "international division" is identified in the source as a typical placement point for CIA non-official cover officers, through which Bush allegedly placed his son [[jeb-bush]] as a CIA operative.

## Leadership of the CIA

Bush served as director of central intelligence from January 1976 to January 1977. According to the article, shortly after leaving the CIA directorship, he arranged for Jeb Bush to be hired by Texas Commerce Bank's international division as a CIA NOC. The source argues this placed Jeb at the center of the Bush family's financial and covert operations in Latin America.

## Connections to drug trafficking and corruption

The article ties George H.W. Bush to drug trafficking through his family's banking relationships, the Gulf Cartel deposits at Texas Commerce Bank, and the Bush network's political relationships with [[carlos-salinas]] and [[carlos-andres-perez]]. The CIA headquarters in Langley, Virginia, is noted in the source as having been named after Bush, which the article interprets as recognition of his leadership in clandestine operations dating to the early 1950s.
