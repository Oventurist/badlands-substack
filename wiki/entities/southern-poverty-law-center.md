# Southern Poverty Law Center

The **Southern Poverty Law Center** (SPLC) is an American nonprofit legal advocacy organization, based in Montgomery, Alabama, best known publicly for its litigation against white-supremacist groups and for its "hate map" and hate-group designations, which are widely cited by journalists, technology platforms, and government agencies.

## Treatment in Badlands coverage

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treats the SPLC as an example of a nominally independent civil-society body operating as an enforcement arm of a broader narrative and [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship apparatus]], with its designations serving to place disfavored organizations and individuals outside the bounds of acceptable discourse.

[[badlands-blitz-episode-14|Badlands Blitz Episode 14]], published April 25, 2026, opens with what the announcement calls "the massive indictment against the SPLC," presenting it as the lead item in a week of what hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] describe as uniformly encouraging news and as the opening chapter of a "dawning age of American accountability." The episode announcement gives no detail on the charges, the jurisdiction, or the defendants, and treats the indictment principally as a signal that institutional consequences are beginning to reach organizations previously regarded as untouchable. In the episode the SPLC discussion is said to dovetail directly into the collapse of the [[russiagate|Russiagate]] narrative, reflecting the program's view that the two involve the same set of institutional actors and methods.

## The 2026 superseding indictment

The June 4, 2026 [[badlands-brief-2c0|Badlands Brief]] supplies detail absent from earlier coverage. It reports that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] announced it had secured a superseding indictment against the SPLC, adding new allegations about how the organization's tax-exempt donations were used to fund informants inside extremist groups. A grand jury in the Middle District of Alabama alleges that roughly $4.1 million was used to pay individuals embedded in hate organizations, who prosecutors say then took part in activities including recruiting members and obtaining materials linked to cross burnings and [[ku-klux-klan|Ku Klux Klan]] regalia.

The updated indictment reportedly removes language referring to "false or misleading" statements to banks, following the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruling in [[thompson-v-united-states|*Thompson v. United States*]] clarifying that the relevant statute applies only to false statements. The organization faces eleven counts of wire fraud, bank fraud, and conspiracy to commit money laundering, with prosecutors alleging that it defrauded donors and banks by routing funds through shell accounts to individuals inside the groups it publicly opposed. The SPLC has pleaded not guilty and has moved to dismiss the case, calling it a vindictive prosecution.

## Alleged connections to manufactured incidents

In commentary accompanying that report, Ashe in America speculates about whether the SPLC was involved in the staged cross burning that preceded the 2023 election of [[yemi-mobolade|Yemi Mobolade]] as mayor of [[colorado-springs|Colorado Springs]], arguing that "when supply for racism outweighed demand, racism had to be manufactured." She further states that she and her colleagues are tracking the SPLC connections of the people and entities that filed what she calls fabricated claims under the [[ku-klux-klan-act-of-1871|Ku Klux Klan Act]] against them in 2022, and proposes reviving the common-law offense of [[barratry|barratry]] "beginning with the SPLC and their collaborators."
