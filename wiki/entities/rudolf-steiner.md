# Rudolf Steiner

Rudolf Steiner was an Austrian occultist, former Theosophy leader and founder of Anthroposophy who appears in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account of wartime occult intelligence as a possible coordinator of occult networks operating through "neutral Switzerland."[1]

## Occult affiliations

According to his own autobiography, Steiner was initiated into the Ancient and Primitive Rite of Memphis and Misraim by John Yarker together with his wife Marie von Sievers, although by 1912 he and von Sievers had publicly distanced themselves from Yarker and [[theodor-reuss|Theodor Reuss]].[1] Steiner had worked alongside Reuss, Yarker, Carl Kellner and [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]] in the orbit of the Memphis-Misraim rite, the same milieu in which Crowley was made outer head of the [[ordo-templi-orientis|Ordo Templi Orientis]] in 1912 and head of the Memphis and Misraim rite in 1913.[1]

Steiner was a devoted advocate of Rosicrucianism and lectured extensively on what he called the secret wisdom of the Rosicrucians through the ages, placing his thinking, in Ehret's reading, in direct alignment with the Hermetic orders of Crowley, Reuss and Yarker.[1] In his 1912 lecture Esoteric Christianity and the Mission of Christian Rosenkreutz, Steiner spoke of a Martian home of the spirit beings Buddha, Christ and the founder of Rosicrucianism, whom he proposed were avatars of a single "Christ consciousness" spirit.[1]

## Intelligence allegations

A United States Office of Naval Intelligence report dated 10 July 1917 focused on Theodor Reuss and stated that Crowley "maintained some mysterious means of communication with their chief in Berlin, Rudolf Steiner."[1] Ehret notes that Steiner also served as personal spiritual guru to the German Chief-of-Staff Helmut von Moltke, and remarks that while it is impossible to know what advice Steiner gave the general, Moltke walked into every trap set for Germany by British strategists in the run-up to World War I.[1]
