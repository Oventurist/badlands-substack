# Black's Law Dictionary

Black's Law Dictionary is the standard American reference work of legal definitions, and functions in Badlands Media legal commentary as a primary evidentiary source for recovering the technical meanings of words that have drifted in popular usage.[1]

## Definitions cited

The article "Is the United States a Nation?" cites two editions. From page 373 of the 4th Edition (1951), the entry for **Congress** reads: "CONGRESS. In International Law. An assembly of envoys, commissioners, deputies, etc., from different sovereignties who meet to concert measures for their common good, or to adjust their mutual concerns."[1] The author treats the phrase "from different sovereignties" as confirmation that the several states retain national character, since an assembly of envoys presupposes distinct sovereigns rather than subdivisions of one.[1]

From page 1176 of the Revised 4th Edition (1968), the article quotes the distinction between two forms of government: "A national government is a government of the people of a single state or nation, united as a community by what is termed the 'social compact,' and possessing complete and perfect supremacy over persons and things... A federal government is distinguished from a national government, by its being the government of a community of independent and sovereign states, united by compact."[1]

## Use in the Badlands argument

Because [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] treats legalese as a quasi-formal language in which words must carry singular meanings, dictionary entries of this kind are given weight comparable to case law such as [[cherokee-nation-v-georgia|Cherokee Nation v. Georgia]] and [[people-ex-rel-attorney-general-v-naglee|People ex rel. Attorney General v. Naglee]].[1] The recency of the 1951 and 1968 editions is emphasized to rebut the idea that these definitions are archaic relics.[1] The article's broader claim is that social engineers benefit when the public never recovers "the true meaning of words," and that the schoolbook description of the United States as a democratic nation is therefore false.[1]
