# Gabor Maté

**Dr. Gabor Maté** is a physician and writer on trauma and addiction, cited in the April 19, 2025 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] feature "The Remembering: Fractured Selves and the Path Beyond Ego" by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]].[1]

The essay quotes him that "safety is not the absence of threat, but the presence of connection."[1] The line appears in the article's section on moving from survival to thriving, where the author applies it inwardly: inner coherence offers "a felt sense of connection to self that becomes unshakeable," so that a person "become[s] your own safe haven."[1] Thriving, in the surrounding argument, is presence rather than performance — "sitting in your wholeness and knowing that nothing within you is too wild, too wounded, or too weird to belong."[1] See [[psychological-integration|psychological integration]] and [[parts-work|parts work]].

## "Transcending Ego" (April 2025)

Maté is quoted again in the same author's earlier April 12, 2025 Badlands Media feature "Transcending Ego: Meeting the Mirror at the Threshold," for the formulation "The ego is not who we are. The ego is who we think we are."[2] The essay uses the line to establish its central premise that the ego is a construct, a defense, and "a scaffolding to survive in a world that may not have offered safety" — intelligent and protective in childhood, but hardening over time into a structure that confines.[2] The citation sits alongside a quotation from Alan Watts comparing the ego to a ship's radar mistaken for the whole ship, and supports the essay's argument for [[ego-transcendence|ego transcendence]] by evolution and reassignment rather than destruction.[2]

## "Walking the Path" (March 2025)

Maté appears again in the earlier March 29, 2025 feature "Walking the Path: From Awakening to Integration," where he is described as a trauma expert and the author of *When the Body Says No* and quoted that "What we don't process owns us."[3] The line anchors the essay's treatment of the [[dark-night-of-the-soul|Dark Night of the Soul]], the disorienting stretch in which a person mourns the familiar while embracing the unknown.[3] The author extends the thought to argue that unprocessed emotions stored in the body from years of suppression rise during this stage and, if left unchecked, keep a person vigilant and resistant to expansion — though the episode is a transition and a shedding rather than an ending.[3]
