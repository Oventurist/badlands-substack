# Eduard de Stoeckl

Eduard de Stoeckl was the Russian Ambassador to the United States during the 1860s and, in Matthew Ehret's telling, one of the central figures of the [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] era foreign policy doctrine that treated America as Russia's natural partner against British imperial strategy.[1]

Before the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]], [[grand-duke-constantine|Grand Duke Constantine]] wrote to Stoeckl arguing that the United States "should in the course of events be eager to conquer all of North America," would inevitably meet Russia, and would seize the Russian colonies without great effort, leaving Russia in no position to retain them.[1]

During the war Stoeckl wrote to Foreign Minister [[alexander-gorchakov|Gorchakov]] that "the disintegration of the United States as a nation would from our point of view be something to be deplored," since the American confederation acted as a counterpoise to British power and its continued existence was an important element in the balance of power; he desired "the preservation of the American Union as an indivisible nation."[1]

Recalled to Russia in 1866, Stoeckl met at length with the Czar and received approval to initiate the [[alaska-purchase|sale of Alaska]].[1] On the evening of 29 March 1867 he delivered the news to Secretary of State [[william-seward|William Seward]] at Seward's private residence; the treaty was drafted overnight and finalized at $7.2 million by sunrise on 30 March.[1]
