# Alliance of Sahel States

The Alliance of Sahel States is a bloc of West African military-led governments identified in Badlands coverage as "Conflict A" among three major African theaters under discussion in January 2026.[1] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized [[russia|Russia]]'s Vladimir Putin as the external power involved in this conflict, contrasting it with the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Congo]] theater ("Conflict B") in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is engaged, and a third in which "the Arabs are running point."[1]

[[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] under Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] was presented as the alliance's most active member during this period, following its dissolution of all political parties and its simultaneous nuclear and thermal power agreements with Russia and the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]].[1] Badlands framed these developments as evidence that "the Sovereign Alliance lives, and that doesn't bode well for globalist wannabes."[1]

## Alleged French destabilization campaign (February 2026)

In February 2026 Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] told the Russian parliament that [[france|France]] was pursuing "revanchist" policies in Africa and "attempting to overthrow undesirable nationalist governments in the Sahara-Sahel region," working through opposition figures, "outright terrorist groups and Ukrainian militants."[2] Badlands commentary read the statement as confirmation that the alliance is the primary target of a coordinated subversion campaign, identifying [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] as its leading figure and noting that the alliance's forces were trained in Syria by the Russian military and the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]].[2] The commentary raised the prospect of Russia re-mobilizing and redeploying to the Sahel in response.[2]

## Conflict with ECOWAS and the May 2026 strike

Badlands commentary places the Alliance of Sahel States within the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and dates its rebellion against the UN-affiliated [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] bloc to 2022, when Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] led a military coup in [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] against his own commander.[3] The AES states are former ECOWAS members, and the commentary argues that terrorist groups funded through the [[state-department|State Department]], the [[cia|CIA]], the [[united-nations|United Nations]], [[nato|NATO]] and affiliated NGOs are used to destabilize them so that ECOWAS and the UN can justify intervention.[3]

[[russia|Russia]] has assisted the AES against [[islamic-state|ISIS]]-linked groups, and both Russia and the AES have accused [[ukraine|Ukraine]] of funding, arming and training those groups with money appropriated by [[congress|Congress]] — resources the commentary links to the gold of Burkina Faso and the uranium of [[niger|Niger]].[3] The killing of [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]] in May 2026 was characterized as a service to the AES rather than to ECOWAS.[3]

## Talks with Russia at the UN General Assembly (September 2025)

On September 24, 2025, the foreign ministers of [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], Mali and [[niger|Niger]] met Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] on the sidelines of the 80th UN General Assembly in New York, in what Moscow called the second joint discussion between the AES and Russia.[4] The talks focused on stronger cooperation and preparations for the third Russia-Africa Summit, scheduled for 2026, with Lavrov saying the two sides share interests extending beyond trade, defense and cultural affairs into cooperation at the UN and other international bodies.[4] The alliance, formed in September 2023, groups three military-led governments that have cut defense ties with France, accusing their former colonial power of failing to contain the deadly jihadist insurgency devastating the Sahel.[4] Badlands commentary described the AES, led by Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], as regarding Russia as the one earnest and trustworthy actor on the world stage.[4]

## ECOWAS proxy conflict (2025 commentary)

Badlands analysis published in November 2025 described the Alliance of Sahel States — formed by Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger after their departure from [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] — as the target of an ongoing proxy war waged by the bloc, with [[nigeria|Nigeria]] and Ivory Coast accused of subversive action against Sahelian leadership.[5] Russia and others have openly accused France and Ukraine of funding the [[isis|ISIS]] terror cells attacking the Sahel states.[5] The same commentary noted that Burkina Faso's President Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] declined President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s invitation to his January inauguration.[5]
