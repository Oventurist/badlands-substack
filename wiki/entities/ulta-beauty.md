# Ulta Beauty

Ulta Beauty is the cosmetics retailer cited in Badlands Media commentary as a company that has publicly attributed falling profits to merchandise theft while declining to act against it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] reports reading that Ulta Beauty expects profits to be down throughout the year because of expected, continued, massive merchandise theft.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) His objection is not to the forecast but to the passivity behind it: the company knows exactly what is causing profits to fall, yet is not willing to do anything about it, and specifically is not willing to return to the store policies that were in place before the original BLM looting three years earlier.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

Ulta is paired with [[lululemon|Lululemon]] in the article as evidence of the wider [[retail-theft-wave|retail theft wave]], which Carlson argues cannot be explained by corporate corruption alone because tolerating theft cuts directly into profits — leading him to conclude the companies have "puppet masters" pursuing a [[communist-takeover-of-america|Communist takeover]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## April 2024 demand warning and share collapse

Shares of Ulta Beauty plunged 15 percent on Wednesday, April 3, 2024 — the largest daily decline since March 2020 — after the company warned investors at a JPMorgan Chase & Co. conference that first-quarter demand for beauty products had cooled.[2] Chief executive [[dave-kimbell|Dave Kimbell]] told investors, "We have seen a slowdown in the total category," explaining that the company had expected moderation after several years of strong growth but not at the observed pace.[2] He guided first-quarter comparable sales to the lower end of the low-single-digit growth range and acknowledged lost market share in prestige makeup and hair.[2] Kimbell called the slowdown "a bit earlier and a bit bigger" than anticipated and pointed to high credit card debt and student loan payments as pressures on consumers; Oppenheimer analyst Rupesh Parikh wrote that his team was "surprised by the moderation."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] read the numbers as a verdict on [[bidenomics]], arguing that discretionary spending in luxury beauty had become almost nonexistent and that women were downgrading brands, maintaining their own eyebrows and coloring their own hair.[2] She framed the retrenchment as an opportunity, promoting [[the-kate-awakening|The Kate Awakening]]'s Clean Living Project and natural alternatives to "corporate slop," and argued that every purchasing decision in 2024 is an activist choice.[2]
