# Scott Bessent

Scott Bessent is the U.S. Treasury Secretary in the second Trump administration.[1]

In January 2026 Bessent lifted U.S. sanctions on Venezuela, a step Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read as part of a coordinated move against the global banking system in tandem with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order protecting Venezuelan oil revenue from bankers and creditors.[1] With sanctions lifted, the civil action lawsuit brought by [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] against bank, commodity-trading and oil-company executives — dismissed without prejudice in 2019 on national security grounds tied to those sanctions — could be resumed.[1]


## Goldman Sachs meeting over the Singham probe
Sources cited in 2026 reporting on the [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]] grand jury investigation said Bessent, as Treasury Secretary, met earlier that year in New York with [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] Chairman and CEO [[david-solomon|David Solomon]].[2] He reportedly delivered a direct message urging the bank's cooperation with investigators and warning of potential scrutiny over the role of its donor-advised philanthropy fund in moving roughly $110 million of Singham's money.[2]

## Davos 2026

At the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in January 2026, Bessent played a central diplomatic role, working to steady global markets and allied partners amid the political storm triggered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s aggressive foreign and trade policy initiatives.[3] He repeatedly urged European leaders and markets not to retaliate against Trump's threatened tariff measures linked to the administration's pressure campaign over [[greenland|Greenland]], arguing that escalation would damage long-term relations and citing the importance of established trade frameworks.[3] He also sought to defuse speculation that European governments might sell U.S. Treasury holdings as leverage, dismissing such scenarios as exaggerated and not widely supported.[3]

On the forum sidelines Bessent described U.S.–Europe relations as stronger than perceived and encouraged counterparts to "take a deep breath" about Washington's hard line on Greenland.[3] He engaged in sharply worded exchanges with domestic political figures, publicly mocking California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s criticism of Trump's diplomacy as lacking economic credibility.[3] In remarks that reverberated beyond Europe, he suggested that the western Canadian province of Alberta could serve as a "natural partner" for the United States, referencing discussions about the province's political identity and autonomy.[3] The broader controversy eased mid-week when Trump announced what he called a "framework of a future deal" with NATO Secretary General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]], temporarily pausing the tariff actions.[3]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump did not go into Davos alone, bringing Bessent and Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] into "enemy territory" and letting them take the lead for much of the week.[3] He noted that Bessent had spoken in early 2024 of envisioning a "Global Economic Re-Ordering" he wanted to be part of, and argued that Bessent is not merely an architect of Trump's Golden Age agenda but "the prime harbinger of the old System's demolition" — feared by central bankers precisely because he came from inside the machine.[3]

## Operation Economic Fury

In April 2026, under Bessent's leadership, the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|US Department of the Treasury]] sanctioned 35 individuals and entities tied to [[iran|Iran]]'s shadow banking network under the banner of [[operation-economic-fury|Operation Economic Fury]], targeting shell companies, exchange houses, and front operations funneling tens of billions of dollars to the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] and its proxies.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], who had dubbed Bessent "The Wolf of Main Street" in 2025, argued that the action's real target was the broader [[shadow-banking-empire|shadow banking]] architecture underpinning the unipolar order rather than Iran alone.[4]

## Targeting funding networks of political terrorism (July 2026)
At the July 2026 Washington summit of more than 65 countries convened by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], Bessent said the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] would intensify efforts to investigate and disrupt funding networks supporting violent far-left political organizations, as part of the [[global-crackdown-on-far-left-political-terrorism|global crackdown on far-left political terrorism]].[5] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the joint appearance by Bessent, Rubio and [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] as a signal that the administration was moving against the ideological bedrock of what he described as an insurgency.[5]

## Framing alongside Lutnick

In February 2026, amid a wave of attacks on Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] over alleged tariff-refund profiteering at [[cantor-fitzgerald|Cantor Fitzgerald]], Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] grouped Bessent with Lutnick as figures who understand the blueprint of the old financial order and may have been positioned as a scalpel against it.[6] He argued that readers need not declare loyalty to either man, only note when the opposition broadcasts its anxieties about them in perfect harmony.[6]

## Dark-money NGO probes and the fraud estimate (January 2026)

In January 2026 Bessent signaled active Treasury probes into "dark-money" NGOs accused of funding and organizing protests that obstruct federal operations, describing the approach as tracking illicit financing networks and linking the activity to potential "color revolution" tactics backed by left-wing billionaires and foreign entities.[7] In related comments he stated that 5–10% of the federal budget is lost annually to fraud and improper payments, and emphasized ongoing efforts to eliminate waste through deliberate investigations and a new whistleblower program — work he framed as continuing the mission of [[doge|DOGE]].[7]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], who had named him the "Wolf of Main Street" in 2025, wrote that Bessent was now invoking the anti-mafia playbook directly: follow the money, flip the rats, dismantle the hierarchies.[7] Asked about the full set of allegations, Bessent's reported quote was "Yes, yes, and yes."[7] Burning Bright placed the Treasury crackdown at the center of what he called [[cartel-convergence|Cartel Convergence]], unfolding in parallel with the kinetic roll-up of cartels across the Western Hemisphere and the [[powell-renovation-criminal-investigation|criminal investigation]] of Fed Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]].[7]

## Escalate-to-de-escalate framework (2026)

In the April 7, 2026 Badlands Brief, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] credited Bessent with having laid out, two weeks earlier, the interpretive framework for the whiplash pattern of the [[iran-war|US-Iran conflict]]: sometimes you must [[escalate-to-de-escalate|escalate to de-escalate]].[8] Burning Bright judged the maxim to be "AS Narrative as it is Actual," treating it as both a description of real bargaining behavior and a template for reading [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s narrative deployments.[8]

## Iran disentanglement signals (March 2026)

In late March 2026, as Trump announced negotiations with what he called a "new, more reasonable regime" in [[iran|Iran]], Bessent publicly described a strategy combining pressure with potential de-escalation, stating that US policy may involve "escalate to de-escalate" dynamics in the conflict.[9] He also characterized US actions as weakening the Iranian regime and indicated that sustained economic and military pressure was intended to alter Iran's behavior and negotiating position.[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called Bessent his "personal favorite cognitive cypher" on the Trump team and said his comments that week carried the heaviest payload within the Info War static, arguing that Bessent "doesn't speak in riddles" but "in frameworks."[9] Burning Bright read the escalate-to-de-escalate line as "a masterclass in reprogramming the enemy's own pressure valves into off-ramps they never anticipated," followed by a pivot signaling that America would retake the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] over time while markets remained supplied through temporary deals and redirected flows breaking calcified routes in favor of pragmatic multipolarity.[9] In his estimation Bessent's remarks are the clearest articulation yet of the [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] template, whose actual target is the Global Energy Cartel and by extension the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] architecture underwriting decades of engineered scarcity and foreign entanglement.[9]

## Fraud estimate and NGO probes (January 2026)

In a January 2026 interview with [[christopher-rufo|Chris Rufo]] centered on the status of [[doge|DOGE]], Bessent said that roughly 10% of the US federal budget is lost to fraud.[10] He framed the figure as an annual loss amounting to trillions of dollars cumulatively.[10]

That same week Bessent signaled the launch of comprehensive probes into dark-money NGOs, describing the effort explicitly as a mafia-style "follow the money" operation amid rising [[color-revolution|color revolution]] concerns — language mirrored by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the same period.[10] He pledged accelerated whistleblower pipelines and the nationwide expansion of the [[minnesota|Minnesota]]-seeded fraud investigations, a posture paralleled by Trump's moves to cut off funding to sanctuary cities and states.[10] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read these steps not as reactive policy but as "harvested mandate in motion," with public fury over food-stamp and benefits fraud transmuted into administrative will.[10]

## "Mr. Bean on crack" allegation

A book by New York Times reporters [[maggie-haberman|Maggie Haberman]] and [[jonathan-swan|Jonathan Swan]] alleged that Bessent privately used derogatory language to describe Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] before a contentious Oval Office meeting involving President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], reportedly saying Zelensky "acted like Mr. Bean on crack."[11] According to excerpts cited by RT and attributed to material published by The Guardian, Bessent described concerns about Zelensky's behavior ahead of the meeting.[11]

The meeting was intended to finalize the [[us-ukraine-minerals-agreement|U.S.–Ukraine minerals agreement]], which Bessent had helped negotiate and during whose discussion he was present.[11] The meeting deteriorated into a confrontation over U.S. policy toward Ukraine and Russia, Zelensky was asked to leave the White House, and the agreement was ultimately signed about two months later.[11] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded that Bessent "continues to be the pipe-hitting gangster in the Trump Administration."[11]


## Venezuela sanctions relief (January 2026)

Bessent told Reuters that additional U.S. sanctions on [[venezuela|Venezuela]] could be lifted "as soon as next week" to facilitate oil sales and economic recovery, and said he planned to meet with leaders of the [[international-monetary-fund|International Monetary Fund]] and the [[world-bank|World Bank]] to discuss their re-engagement with the country.[12]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued this announcement was the hidden purpose of the whole Venezuelan sequence: because [[executive-order-13850|Executive Order 13850]] merely paused rather than ended the [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] fraud litigation, lifting the sanctions would revive discovery and restart monitored bank wires, generating new Suspicious Activity Reports and investigations that could link banks and oil companies to the cartels.[12]

## FinCEN whistleblower portal (February 2026)

On February 13, 2026, the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] under Bessent announced a new website to accept confidential whistleblower tips on fraud, money laundering and sanctions violations.[13] Bessent said the portal, operated through the [[financial-crimes-enforcement-network|Financial Crimes Enforcement Network]]'s Office of the Whistleblower, would let individuals report suspected misconduct involving violations of the [[bank-secrecy-act|Bank Secrecy Act]], U.S. sanctions programs and other financial laws, with tipsters whose information leads to successful enforcement actions eligible for financial awards.[13]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called Bessent's approach brilliant because it creates a practical incentive structure with a reliable source of reward money — fines collected from guilty parties — and inherently targets financial institutions, which he identifies as the core culprit in organized crime syndicates.[13] He wrote that the program would pay criminals to inform on their friends and pay bureaucrats and accountants to report misconduct they observe, describing Bessent as "weaponizing the financial system against itself by tapping into base human instincts."[13]

## Davos 2026 de-escalation effort

At the January 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos, Bessent worked to calm allies alarmed by the [[greenland-tariff-dispute-2026|tariff threats issued over Greenland]], reassuring them that U.S.-[[nato|NATO]] ties remain strong despite the disagreement and advising partners to "take a deep breath" rather than retaliate.[14] He accused mainstream media of manufacturing "hysteria," compared the reaction to past trade disputes, expressed confidence in a non-escalatory resolution, and firmly dismissed speculation that Europe might dump U.S. Treasuries as a "completely false narrative."[14] Business leaders and chief executives at the forum echoed his call for pragmatism over emotional responses, warning that outrage at [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s style could hinder strategic negotiations.[14] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] highlighted Bessent's framing of the European narrative blitz as recycled theater identical to the 2025 "Tariff Tantrum," which dissipated once concrete negotiations began.[14]

Under [[executive-order-on-institutional-homebuyers|Trump's executive order on institutional homebuyers]], Bessent as Treasury Secretary is also tasked with reviewing rules on institutional ownership of single-family homes.[14]

## Russian oil sanctions relief (March 2026)

Amid the March 2026 oil price surge caused by the [[iran-war|Iran war]] and disruption around the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], a social media post circulating on X cited Bessent as saying that additional relief from sanctions on Russian oil could be considered because of a temporary global oil shortage, a claim that appeared in a social post and was not independently verified within it.[15] The remark followed the U.S. decision to grant a 30-day waiver allowing Indian refiners to purchase [[russia|Russian]] crude already at sea.[15]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Bessent's signal as reinforcing the direction of American policy: that the United States might unsanction still more Russian oil to stabilize the temporary shortfall while Europe was "left to twist in geopolitical winds they can no longer control."[15] In his framing this made the Treasury Secretary an instrument of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]'s energy pincer against the European project.[15]

## Iran oil framework (June 2026)

In June 2026, Bessent said the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration had reached a framework agreement with [[iran|Iran]] following ongoing talks in Switzerland, including commitments on free transit through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and on permitting [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspections.[16] He announced that Treasury had issued a temporary 60-day general license authorizing the production, delivery, and sale of Iranian oil as part of the arrangement.[16] A Treasury press release confirmed that [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|OFAC]] had issued [[iran-general-license-x|Iran General License X]], covering Iranian crude oil, petrochemical and petroleum products through August 21, 2026, and permitting certain dollar-denominated payments as well as limited imports into the United States tied to permitted transactions.[16]

## April 2025 tariff pause and the non-retaliation doctrine

On April 9, 2025 Bessent announced that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 90-day tariff pause had benefited more than 75 countries that chose to negotiate with the United States rather than retaliate, saying "it took great courage for [President Trump] to stay the course until this moment."[17] He restated the administration's standing message — do not retaliate and you will be rewarded — telling reporters, "Every country in the world that wants to come and negotiate: we are willing to hear you," while singling out [[china|China]], then facing a new 125% tariff, for "their insistence on escalation."[17] He went on to raise the prospect of special agreements with particular trading partners in the aftermath of the tariff shift.[17]

Speaking outside the [[white-house|White House]] alongside press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]], Bessent rejected the term "trade war" as a description of the shifting global trade arrangements, and dismissed a reporter's suggestion that 125% tariffs on China amounted to a de facto embargo.[17] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Bessent was "a calming influence," exuding competence and, like Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] in a different register, appearing "little more than humored by the drama of it all," and framed the [[us-china-trade-war-2025|trade escalation]] as the administration delivering on its America First campaign promises.[17] In a remark quoted by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] the same week, Bessent said, "What gives me a lot of confidence is the relationship between President Trump and Chairman [[xi-jinping|Xi]]."[17]

## US-China trade war remarks, April 2025

On 22 April 2025 Bessent warned investors at a private conference hosted by JPMorgan in Washington that the US-China trade war was "not sustainable" and that the two countries would have to de-escalate, comments that buoyed financial markets hoping for a trade deal.[18] He said he expected Washington and Beijing to reach a deal in the "very near future," though several people familiar with his remarks said markets had reacted too optimistically, noting that he had made clear no trade talks were then under way and had conceded that any negotiations with China would "be a slog."[18] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode through the "War of Stories" frame, arguing that from peace deals to trade deals the right answers were being allowed to emerge because the wrong ones were being eliminated.[18]

## Strong-dollar policy (March 2025)

In March 2025 [[the-economist|The Economist]] reported that Bessent, as the new treasury secretary, had stressed that the strong-dollar policy first articulated by Robert Rubin in 1994 remained in place, even as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]] argued that the greenback's strength was a problem for American industry.[19] The magazine noted that the administration was "not speaking with one voice" as protectionist trade policy and a suddenly weakening dollar — a five-month low against the yen and a 4.5% weekly euro gain, the fastest since 2009 — called three decades of Treasury orthodoxy into question.[19]

You're a total fraud!", after which Musk slammed his shoulder into Bessent's ribs "like a rugby player" and Bessent retaliated before bystanders separated them.[20] See [[musk-bessent-white-house-altercation|the Musk–Bessent White House altercation]].[20]

## "Global economic reordering" (August 2025)

Amid the pressure on [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] and the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in August 2025, Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Bessent had openly anticipated a "global economic reordering" and had been positioning himself to drive it, defending President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s trade activism as part of reshaping the world order away from centralized control.[21] The remark was cited alongside the fast-tracking of [[stephen-miran|Stephen Miran]] to a Fed board vacancy as one of the "moves on the edges" signalling a coming monetary reset.[21]

## Tariff revenue and cash balance guidance (July 2025)

On July 8, 2025, Bessent said the U.S. government had already collected around $100 billion in tariff revenue during 2025 and was on track to triple that figure by year's end, projecting tariff income could reach $300 billion by December — a fiscal boost he attributed to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s aggressive trade policy.[22] The statement accompanied the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]]'s announcement that it would rebuild its cash balance to $500 billion by the end of July following passage of Trump's $5 trillion tax and spending bill, which included a significant debt ceiling hike.[22] See [[treasury-general-account|Treasury General Account]].[22]


## Debt ceiling and China talks (May 2025)

Testifying before a House Appropriations subcommittee in May 2025, Bessent told lawmakers that a fresh estimate of the [[debt-ceiling-x-date|X-date]] was "forthcoming" and that the debt ceiling was already "on the warning track."[23] He explained that the Treasury was still counting tax receipts from the April 15 filing deadline and declined to give a hard figure, while assuring lawmakers the forecast would land soon.[23] "Just as an outfielder running for a fly ball, we are on the warning track," he said. "And when you're on the warning track, it means the wall's not far away."[23]

Bessent also said the United States would never default on its debt and that the debt ceiling would be raised, and stated that a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]] is a sign of weakness that he would not support the Federal Reserve issuing.[23]

Ahead of the first major meeting between U.S. and Chinese officials since Trump imposed large tariffs on China — scheduled for that weekend in Switzerland — Bessent said he expected "de-escalation" to be the primary topic.[23] "We've agreed to talk – then on Saturday and Sunday, we will agree on what we're going to talk about," he told Fox News host Laura Ingraham, adding: "My sense is that this will be about de-escalation, not about the big trade deal. We've got to de-escalate before we can move forward."[23]

## March 2025 meeting with Smotrich

Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] said on March 4, 2025 that he had flown overnight to the United States to "strengthen economic cooperation" and "deepen the strategic alliance between our two countries," and that he would meet US officials including Treasury Secretary Bessent.[25] The visit came ahead of an expected announcement by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the future of the West Bank, with Smotrich saying he would emphasize Israel's stance in the fight against terror and the need for clear American support.[25]

## SNAP contingency funding (November 2025)

As Treasury Secretary, Bessent said in early November 2025 that contingency funds released to maintain [[snap|SNAP]] benefits at 50 percent during the 34-day federal government shutdown could begin flowing by Wednesday, while cautioning that some states might face delays in distributing the benefits.[26]

## The $20 billion Argentina gamble (October 2025)

In October 2025 Bessent announced a $20 billion lifeline for [[argentina|Argentina]], designed to pull the country's financial markets out of deepening turmoil and a close political ally out of a hole.[27] The United States offered swap arrangements to shore up the peso and had already stepped in directly to buy the currency, a move with few precedents in recent decades.[27] "It's not a bailout at all," Bessent told Fox News, though to many observers still awaiting details it looked like one, delivered by an administration that had promised to put America first to a country with a long record of squandering other people's money and defaulting on its debts.[27] Argentine President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]] — described as perhaps the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s strongest backer in Latin America, where superpower rivalry with China is heating up — vowed to leave that history behind by putting public finances in order and taking a chainsaw to the budget.[27] Bessent posted that Milei "is trying to break 100 years of bad cycles," adding, "We do not want another failed or China-led state in Latin America."[27]

Analysts noted that although President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Bessent described the arrangement as a "swap," what Treasury was likely offering was not a swap but a loan from the Exchange Stabilization Fund, since central banks extend swap lines to one another while finance ministries do not — a distinction bound up with wider concerns about [[politicised-dollar-liquidity|politicised dollar liquidity]].[27] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed Bessent as "Donald Trump's Financial General," writing that he "absolutely exploited" the rules of free currency exchange on behalf of one of the most powerful financial forces in the world, which is likely what spared him from the System's wrath.[27]

## Price floors and a strategic mineral reserve (October 2025)

In an exclusive CNBC interview on October 15, 2025, Bessent announced that the administration would set [[price-floors-industrial-policy-2025|price floors]] across a range of industries to combat market manipulation by [[china|China]], saying Beijing had used its dominance in rare earth refining and processing to slash prices and drive foreign competitors out of business over two decades.[28] "When you are facing a nonmarket economy like China, then you have to exercise industrial policy," he told Sara Eisen at CNBC's Invest in America Forum in Washington, D.C., adding that the administration would pair price floors with forward buying and apply the approach across multiple sectors without naming them beyond rare earths.[28] Bessent also said the United States needs a strategic mineral reserve and that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] was interested in helping to create one.[28] Badlands contributor Burning Bright described Bessent as holding "the most interesting role in the 2025 Shadow War," arguing that Chinese conduct now supplies narrative cover for policies Bessent had telegraphed in 2024.[28]

## "Vaudevillian" characterization of Zelenskyy (April 2025)

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Bessent in April 2025 as "one of the prime signal setters at work in the War of Stories," noting that in a recent communication the Treasury Secretary referred to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy as a "vaudevillian" — a performer in a variety-act theatrical form.[29] The remark was read by Badlands as a deliberate narrative signal about the theatrical character of the Ukraine conflict's public presentation.[29]

## Granting DOGE access to federal payments, 2025

As Treasury Secretary, Bessent gave representatives of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] access to the federal payment system late on Friday, January 31, 2025, according to five people familiar with the change.[30] The decision handed [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and his team a powerful tool to monitor and potentially limit government spending, and followed a standoff in which career [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] official [[david-lebryk|David Lebryk]] resisted allowing DOGE into the system before being placed on leave and retiring.[30] Reporting noted the system could give the administration another mechanism to unilaterally restrict disbursement of funds appropriated by Congress.[30]

## Tariff revenue and the June 2025 surplus

In July 2025, Bessent said the United States had brought in nearly $100 billion in tariff revenue so far and was on track for $300 billion for the year — almost 1% of GDP — and that June had delivered a budget surplus with higher revenue and lower spending.[31] "This is how we clean up the fiscal mess we inherited," he wrote.[31]

## Enforcement of the Liberation Day tariff regime

During the April 2025 rollout of the [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]], Bessent publicly threatened to increase tariffs on any country that retaliated against the new US duties, a warning that functioned as the enforcement mechanism behind the reciprocal-tariff schedule.[33] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] applied that threat to Israel, arguing that after Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] dropped Israel's long-standing 33 percent tariffs on US goods the administration nonetheless imposed a 17 percent tariff on Israel — reportedly justified by longstanding Israeli theft of American intellectual property — leaving Israel with a 0 percent tariff against the United States and no retaliatory option under Bessent's rule.[33]

## Soros connection and the IRS appointment, October 2025

[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] commentary in October 2025 highlighted that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent made his name in global financial markets by shorting the British pound in 1992 while working for Soros Fund Management — the trade that made [[george-soros|George Soros]] famous but which the commentary attributes to Bessent — noting alongside it that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had threatened Soros with RICO charges that summer.[34]

In the same period, Bessent appointed [[frank-bisignano|Frank Bisignano]], head of the [[social-security-administration|Social Security Administration]], to serve additionally as the first-ever chief executive officer of the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]], a move that bypassed Senate confirmation; Bessent remained the IRS's acting commissioner.[34] "Frank is a businessman with an exceptional track record of driving growth and efficiency in the private and now public sector," Bessent said, citing shared "customer service and technological goals" between the IRS and SSA.[34]

### February 2025 reopening of the Treasury building
Badlands commentary reported that on February 19, 2025 Treasury Secretary Bessent reopened the Department of the Treasury, apparently for the first time since March 2020, an action framed as part of a wave of rapid and radical change under the new administration.[35]

## Minnesota tax fraud investigation (December 2025)

On December 1, 2025, Bessent announced on X that the U.S. Treasury Department was investigating allegations that tax dollars from Minnesota may have been diverted to the Somali militant group [[al-shabaab|Al-Shabaab]].[36] "At my direction, the Treasury Department is investigating allegations that under the feckless mismanagement of the Biden Administration and Governor Tim Walz, hardworking Minnesotans' tax dollars may have been diverted to the terrorist organization Al-Shabaab," Bessent said.[36] The announcement came days after President Trump called Minnesota a "hub of fraudulent money laundering activity" under Democratic Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]], responding to unverified media reports circulated by several Republican lawmakers.[36]

## April 2025 South Korea trade invitation

South Korea's finance ministry said on Wednesday, April 16, 2025 that Finance Minister [[choi-sang-mok|Choi Sang-mok]] would meet Bessent the following week to discuss trade issues during a visit to the United States made at Bessent's suggestion.[37] Choi was also expected to attend a G20 finance ministers meeting on the sidelines of the spring International Monetary Fund and [[world-bank|World Bank]] meetings in Washington.[37]

Bessent had said the United States was in talks with a handful of countries including [[south-korea|South Korea]] and Japan over tariffs announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and that there was a "first mover advantage" especially for allies that could reach "an agreement in principle" soon.[37] South Korea, under acting President [[han-duck-soo|Han Duck-soo]], engaged actively with senior Trump administration officials with the goal of lowering the 25% reciprocal tariff announced for the country and since paused.[37]

Separately, Badlands analysis of Trump's April 2025 escalation against [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] argued that Bessent, alongside [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]], appeared far less concerned about short-term economic strife than about an end game of completely reordering and resetting global trade.[37]

## Response to the Moody's downgrade (May 2025)

Appearing on Meet the Press after [[moodys|Moody's]] stripped the United States of its last top rating, Bessent dismissed the move: "Moody's is a lagging indicator. I think that's what everyone thinks of credit agencies."[38] He noted that [[larry-summers|Larry Summers]], with whom he said he does not agree on everything, had said the same of the 2011 downgrade, and drew an analogy to [[sean-duffy|Sean Duffy]]'s comments on the air traffic control system: "we didn't get here in the past 100 days."[38] Bessent blamed "the Biden administration and the spending that we have seen over the past four years," said the administration had inherited a 6.7% deficit-to-GDP ratio — "the highest when we weren't in a recession, not in a war" — and pledged to bring spending down and grow the economy.[38]

## February 2025: Kyiv mission

In February 2025 Bessent was expected to become the first senior [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration official to visit Ukraine, traveling to Kyiv a day after Russia released an American prisoner in a development Trump called a positive signal for cease-fire talks.[39] The trip came amid anxiety in Kyiv and among American allies over whether U.S. support for the Ukrainian war effort would be maintained.[39]

The administration had already sought an arrangement for the United States to secure rights to Ukrainian natural resources, including rare earth minerals, as a guarantee for further assistance.[39] Trump wrote on Truth Social that he was sending Bessent because "this war must and will end soon — too much death and destruction," and that the visit would address American assistance, which he said had cost billions "with too little to show" for it.[39] Trump summarized the mission bluntly: "Treasury's Bessent is going to Ukraine to make sure we get money back."[39] Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] welcomed the visit, saying, "We are already prepared for meetings with representatives of the United States — negotiations will begin soon."[39]

## US-Ukraine minerals deal (February 2025)

As Treasury Secretary, Bessent announced in late February 2025 that the United States and Ukraine had finalized their agreement on rare earths and other natural resources, and that [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] was expected to sign the final document without further negotiation during a meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[40] "The deal is done. President Zelensky is coming here to sign the deal tomorrow, so there is no more negotiation, there is nothing else," Bessent told Fox Business.[40] Trump had repeatedly said the United States wanted to "get back" the money it had spent on military and financial aid to Kiev through the minerals deal, while Zelensky had previously refused to sign without concrete U.S. security guarantees.[40]

Bessent had traveled to Kiev earlier that month but returned without an agreement after Zelensky reportedly lashed out at him over a U.S. demand that Ukraine hand over 50% of its critical minerals as compensation for past aid; according to Financial Times sources, Zelensky's frustration boiled over in the closed-door meeting as Bessent pressed for an immediate signature.[40] Badlands commentary praised the hard-line posture as "power moves," noting that the package appeared to extend beyond rare earth minerals to oil and gas assets.[40]

## Shadow Fed chair proposal

Bessent first floated the concept of a [[shadow-fed-chair|"shadow" Fed chair]] the year before he became Treasury secretary — the idea that a successor to Federal Reserve Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] could be named far in advance of the end of Powell's term.[41] The proposal resurfaced in June 2025 when President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would announce his pick "very soon," an unprecedented step in the central bank's 111-year history according to CNN.[41]

## Three-Legged Stool Framework (2025)

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Bessent in May 2025 as the clearest exponent of the argument that tariffs are only one component of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s economic agenda, with the Treasury Secretary repeatedly explaining that the different levers of American profitability work in concert with one another.[42] She summarized his framing of Trump's economic reform program as a "three-legged stool": cut spending intentionally while avoiding a recession, deregulate to jump-start the private sector, and reorder international trade through tariffs and related measures to bring back manufacturing.[42] Bessent's underlying diagnosis was quoted directly: "The U.S. doesn't have a revenue problem—we have a spending problem."[42] The argument was invoked in the context of the [[2025-april-consumer-price-index-report|April 2025 Consumer Price Index report]], which showed inflation slowing to 2.3 percent despite the year's tariff increases.[42]


## Position on tariffs and income tax (December 2025)

As President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] predicted at a December 2, 2025 cabinet meeting that federal income tax would be abolished and replaced entirely by tariff revenue, Treasury Secretary Bessent did not go as far as the president in forecasting the total eradication of income taxes, but claimed that Trump's tariffs could deliver Americans "income tax relief."[43]

## Geneva tariff talks with China (May 2025)

Bessent led the US delegation at weekend talks in Geneva that produced the [[2025-us-china-geneva-tariff-agreement|May 2025 US–China tariff agreement]], a 90-day mutual reduction under which US tariffs on Chinese goods fell from 145% to 30% and Chinese duties on US imports fell from 125% to 10%.[44] At a news briefing after two days of talks, Bessent said the prior tariff levels would have amounted to a complete blockage of each side's goods, an outcome neither side wanted.[44] "The consensus from both delegations this weekend is neither side wants a decoupling," he said, describing the earlier posture as "the equivalent of an embargo" and adding, "We do want trade. We want more balanced trade. And I think that both sides are committed to achieving that."[44]

## Tren de Aragua sanctions statement (June 2025)

In a Treasury statement released on Tuesday, June 24, 2025 announcing sanctions against [[giovanni-vicente-mosquera-serrano|Giovanni Vicente Mosquera Serrano]], the alleged leader of the Venezuelan gang [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]], Bessent accused the organization under Mosquera Serrano's leadership of "terrorizing our communities and facilitating the flow of illicit narcotics into our country."[45] The designation was issued through the [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] and accompanied a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] indictment on drug trafficking and terrorism charges.[45]

## Federal Reserve chair search (August 2025)

On Tuesday, August 19, 2025, Bessent said he would begin interviewing candidates for chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] as soon as the [[white-house|White House]] narrowed what had become a crowded field.[46] In a CNBC "Squawk Box" interview, he confirmed that the race to replace sitting Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] involved 11 candidates — an array including past and present central bank officials, economists, a White House advisor, and several Wall Street market experts.[46]

"In terms of the interview process, we've announced 11 very strong candidates. I'm going to be meeting with them probably right after Labor Day, and to start bringing down the list to present to President Trump," Bessent said, calling the field "an incredible group."[46] Although Powell's term as chair did not end until May 2026, the White House was keen to advance the process as it pressed what it described as an urgent need for interest rate cuts.[46] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the succession fight as part of a broader "war for the future itself," arguing that "the Golden Age isn't coming. It's here."[46]


## October 2025: China Framework and Colombia Sanctions

Bessent announced in late October 2025 that the United States and China had agreed a framework for a trade deal on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit in Malaysia, days before a planned [[donald-trump|Trump]]-[[xi-jinping|Xi]] meeting.[48] He said the framework would remove the threat of 100% tariffs on Chinese imports scheduled for 1 November and would include "a final deal" on the sale of TikTok in the United States.[48]

Days earlier, Bessent announced sweeping U.S. sanctions on Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]], his family, and Interior Minister Armando Benedetti, stating that since Petro took office in 2022 "cocaine production in Colombia has exploded to the highest rate in decades, flooding the United States and poisoning Americans," and that Trump was taking "strong action to protect our nation."[48] Badlands commentary has repeatedly characterized Bessent as an "American cypher" whose moves bridge apparent adversarial camps, comparing him in this respect to [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]].[48]

## Union lawsuit over DOGE data access (February 2025)

In early February 2025 three union groups — the [[alliance-for-retired-americans|Alliance for Retired Americans]], the [[american-federation-of-government-employees|American Federation of Government Employees]] and the [[service-employees-international-union|Service Employees International Union]] — sued Treasury Secretary Bessent, accusing him of illegally sharing their members' information with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[department-of-government-efficiency|Department of Government Efficiency]] team.[49] The suit led U.S. District Judge [[colleen-kollar-kotelly|Colleen Kollar-Kotelly]] to issue an order on February 5 temporarily restricting DOGE access to the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]]'s payment records system to two named employees and Treasury officials.[49]

## Nomination as Treasury Secretary (November 2024)

President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced on Friday, November 22, 2024 that he was selecting Bessent, then a hedge fund chief executive, to serve as Treasury Secretary.[50] In his statement Trump said he was "most pleased to nominate" Bessent, describing him as "widely respected as one of the world's foremost international investors and geopolitical and economic Strategists," and said Bessent would support policies that "drive U.S. competitiveness, and stop unfair trade imbalances."[50]

Bessent, then 62, was the founder of the Connecticut-based hedge fund Key Square Group, and according to a source deeply involved in transition planning had been making a full-court press for the post.[50] Trump had previously called him a "nice-looking guy" and "one of the most brilliant men on Wall Street"; Bessent joked on Fox News that he was in "violent agreement" with Trump on the assessment.[50]

## Comments on the Federal Reserve chair search (August 2025)

On August 12, 2025, Treasury Secretary Bessent told Fox Business host Larry Kudlow that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had a "very open mind" about who should replace [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chairman [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], whose term as chairman expires in May 2026.[51] Bessent said the administration was already interviewing potential replacements and casting a "very wide net," adding that Trump "even considered re-appointing [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]], so we want to see what everyone's thinking."[51] Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued Powell would remain in place until Trump was finished with him, on the theory that Powell was serving to expose "the rot of the fiat central banking class" while building a public mandate for change.[51]

## Bond market remarks (April 2025)

In an interview with Bloomberg Television on Monday, April 14, 2025, while visiting Buenos Aires, Argentina, Bessent played down the recent selloff in the bond market and rejected speculation that foreign nations were dumping their holdings of US Treasuries.[47] "I don't think there's a dumping" by foreign investors, he said, pointing to increased foreign demand at auctions for 10-year and 30-year Treasury securities the previous week and reiterating his view that the decline was mainly a product of deleveraging.[47] "I have no evidence that it's sovereigns" behind the drop, he said.[47]

Bessent said the United States was "a long way" from needing to take action, but that "we have a big toolkit that we can roll out" if required, including the department's [[treasury-buyback-program|buyback program]] for older securities: "We could up the buybacks if we wanted."[47] The Treasury had begun the first regular buyback program since 2000–02 the previous year, designed to improve liquidity in older securities that trade less frequently than on-the-run Treasuries.[47] Badlands commentary noted Bessent's disclosure that he has breakfast with [[jerome-powell|Powell]] every week, and read his remarks as signalling that if the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] does nothing, the Treasury could prop up the bond market through expanded buybacks in lieu of quantitative easing.[47]
