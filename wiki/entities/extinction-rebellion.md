# Extinction Rebellion

**Extinction Rebellion** is the climate protest movement which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues was not a genuine grassroots rebellion but a creation of establishment social engineers.[1]

Ehret notes that a leading figure behind the movement, Alex Evans, was a former consultant to Prince Charles's International Sustainability Unit and co-author of the US National Intelligence Council's *Global Trends 2025: A Transformed World*, which became an environmental and foreign-policy blueprint for the Obama administration in 2008.[1] Evans also runs the Collective Psychology Project, described on its own terms as the place "where psychology meets politics."[1]

Other British intelligence-linked figures Ehret names as managing the movement include Farhana Yamin and Sam Gaell of [[chatham-house|Chatham House]], which he describes as the controlling institution behind the New York-based [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] overseen by [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]].[1] Ehret concludes with the question: "How much of a rebellion can it really be... if the rebellion is set up by the establishment?"[1]

## Rhetoric of its co-founders

Ehret returned to the movement in "The Rising Scourge of Eco-Terrorism," describing it as an outfit grown out of disenfranchised youth energy and billionaire patronage in England, developing in tandem with [[deep-green-resistance|Deep Green Resistance]].[2] Although it advocates non-violence on the surface, co-founder [[roger-hallam|Roger Hallam]] told an [[amnesty-international|Amnesty International]] meeting on 4 February 2019: "We are going to force the governments to act. And if they don't, we will bring them down and create a democracy fit for purpose. And yes, some may die in the process."[2] Asked how billionaire funding squared with the group's anti-capitalism, Hallam answered that the donors "cry at night, just as we do," adding "we want them to ring us up and give us that million quid."[2] Another co-founder, [[gail-bradbrook|Gail Bradbrook]], previously worked for [[king-charles-iii|King Charles]]' Business in the Community / Responsible Business Network, now integrated into the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[2]

## Funding

A 2019 Executive Intelligence Review study by Dean Andromedas reported that [[george-soros|George Soros]] topped the group's list of donors in documents obtained by [[breitbart-news|Breitbart News]], with the amount redacted, and that the [[childrens-investment-fund-foundation|Children's Investment Fund Foundation]] gave £121,140 (about $155,000).[2] A further patron is the hereditary peer [[anthony-st-john|Lord Anthony St John]], a merchant banker and mining director whose environmental philanthropy ties back to the [[united-nations-environment-programme|UN Environment Programme]] and the [[world-wildlife-fund|World Wildlife Fund]].[2]

## Tactics and demands

Ehret notes that the group's professed non-violence did not prevent calls to fill the airfields of Heathrow Airport with drones, disrupting air traffic control and placing thousands of passengers at risk — an act he says would have made [[ted-kaczynski|Ted Kaczynski]] proud, given that Kaczynski's first terrorism attempt was a bomb that failed to detonate on a passenger jet in 1979.[2] The organization also demands non-negotiable Net Zero from all governments by 2030, an 85% elimination of carbon dioxide emissions that Ehret argues would cause massive death worldwide.[2] It was at an Extinction Rebellion event in November 2021 that [[david-suzuki|David Suzuki]] warned pipelines would be blown up if leaders failed to act.[2]
