# Alex Saab

Alex Saab is a Colombian-born businessman and close associate of Venezuelan president [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], long described by U.S. officials as Maduro's "bag man."[1] In May 2026 the Venezuelan government deported Saab to the United States, less than three years after [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] pardoned him in a prisoner swap; Venezuelan authorities said the move was tied to multiple ongoing U.S. criminal investigations.[1]

## Background and first arrest

Saab was first arrested in 2020 after his plane made a stop in Cape Verde while en route to [[iran|Iran]].[1] He was subsequently convicted in the United States in connection with money-laundering schemes involving the Venezuelan government and served roughly two years in prison before being pardoned by Biden in 2023 and returned to Caracas in a prisoner swap that also secured the release of imprisoned Americans and the return of fugitive defense contractor "Fat Leonard."[1]

Federal prosecutors have investigated Saab's alleged role in a bribery scheme connected to Venezuela's CLAP food import program, identifying him as "Co-Conspirator 1" in a Miami-based case alleging that shell companies were used to secure inflated food contracts through bribery.[1]

## Venezuelan government roles and removal

Maduro appointed Saab Minister of Industry in October 2024.[1] After acting interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] took power on January 3, Saab was removed from key government positions, including his Cabinet role and his portfolio overseeing foreign investment deals tied to Venezuela.[1] Rodríguez announced the departure on Telegram, thanking Saab "for his service to the Homeland" and stating that "he will be taking on new responsibilities."[1]

## The February 2026 arrest confusion

On February 4, 2026, the Venezuelan-facing outlet Caracol published an article claiming Saab had been arrested in a joint U.S.–Venezuela law enforcement operation carried out by Venezuelan intelligence (Sebin), and speculated he would be extradited to the United States within days.[1] Saab's attorney Luigi Giuliano denied the account, telling Reuters, "It is simply not true that he has been arrested," and adding that there were no charges against him and that Saab wanted to meet with the president for clarification.[1] Reuters reported that journalists aligned with Venezuela's government also denied on social media that Saab had been taken into custody, and Parliament President [[jorge-rodriguez|Jorge Rodríguez]] said he had no information on the matter.[1] No charges were formally filed or announced, and Venezuelan law explicitly prohibits the state from extraditing its own citizens to foreign countries — complications the Brief treated as evidence that the episode was not a conventional extradition.[1]

The New York Times reported that Saab and other businessmen were being sought for their knowledge of money-laundering schemes involving the Venezuelan government, and that the Venezuelan government — likely through Sebin — was working closely with the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] to obtain that knowledge.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the relevant question is not whether Saab is a "white hat or a black hat" but whether he possesses knowledge of specific activities and would be a reliable witness; on that reading, Saab's evident loyalty to Maduro makes his prospective testimony more, not less, trustworthy.[1] The commentary connected the case to note 32 ("Investigation Case") on pages 78–79 of the 2015 Consolidated Financial Statement of [[pdvsa|PDVSA]], which described a U.S. criminal indictment of multiple PDVSA representatives who pleaded guilty, with PDVSA casting itself as a victim of the fraud and promising an internal investigation.[1] That investigation, the author contended, led to Maduro's corruption purges, to lawsuits Maduro filed against 42 corrupt oil executives in U.S. court, and to Saab cooperating with U.S. authorities during the first Trump administration "with the full knowledge and support of Maduro," according to Saab's own lawyers.[1]

The Brief's conclusion was that Saab, Maduro, Delcy Rodríguez and the Venezuelan government are working with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against a cabal that has controlled both countries and the oil and energy sectors, and that the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] and legacy media have framed the transfer as an arrest and extradition while anticipating a show trial of Maduro.[1]
