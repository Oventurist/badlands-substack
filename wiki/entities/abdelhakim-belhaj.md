# Abdelhakim Belhaj

Abdelhakim Belhaj (sometimes spelled Belhadj), born 1 May 1966, is a Libyan politician and military leader described by Wikipedia as leader of the Islamist al-Watan Party, former head of the Tripoli Military Council, and emir of the defunct [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] (LIFG), an anti-Gaddafi guerrilla group.[1] The Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest" treats him as "a major player" whose career traces the arc of the alleged CIA/shadow-government project in Libya.[1]

## Afghanistan and Al-Qaeda

Belhaj fought in Afghanistan in the 1980s alongside the Mujahideen against Soviet forces — a force the article characterizes as a private army created and funded by the [[cia|CIA]] to protect its heroin trade.[1] There he first met Osama bin Laden.[1] The article states he followed bin Laden to Sudan and back to Afghanistan, ran [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] training camps there, and was not merely a member but a close associate of bin Laden and one of the organization's most important leaders — which is why he went into hiding immediately after 9/11 and was arrested in Malaysia.[1]

## CIA custody and Libyan imprisonment

Africa Intelligence records that Belhaj was interrogated in CIA prisons in the early 2000s and then held in Libya's Abu Salim Prison.[1] The article explains this by noting that after 9/11 [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] took President George W. Bush's war on terror seriously, voluntarily surrendered his weapons of mass destruction programs, made peace with Western nations, and agreed to take custody of Belhaj and other captured LIFG members.[1]

## Release and the 2011 uprising

In 2009 Belhaj negotiated his liberation with Gaddafi's son Saïf al-Islam with the help of [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] preacher [[ali-sallabi|Ali Sallabi]].[1] The article speculates that the newly elected Obama administration asked Gaddafi to release him and that Gaddafi complied to preserve goodwill.[1] In 2011 Belhaj went to Qatar to take command of the elite [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]] as the insurrection began — the same brigade that later helped topple and kill Gaddafi and that was assigned to security at the U.S. consulate in [[benghazi|Benghazi]], where, the article notes, it did nothing to stop the attack.[1] [[john-mccain|John McCain]] was photographed meeting Belhaj in Benghazi in 2011 while asserting the rebels "are not Al-Qaeda."[1]

## Businessman

After Gaddafi's fall Belhaj built an extensive business empire. Africa Intelligence reported that he remained influential with the Tripoli government and the Fajr Libya militia coalition while extending business activities from his fiefdom in Mitiga — where he controls the airport and surrounding area — and a rear base in Turkey, active in health care, property, air transport and media across Sudan, Tunisia, Turkey and Libya, supported by a network of businessmen and influential figures.[1] Global Watch Analysis reported that by autumn 2012 his name appeared on the list of the richest Libyans of the post-Gaddafi era with a fortune estimated at more than $2 billion, and that he founded the airline al-Ajniha ("the wings"), establishing a monopoly on flights into Maatiga airport, the only operational airport after the destruction of Tripoli International.[1] The article alleges Al-Qaeda fighters trained in Benghazi were flown on his airline to Syria through Turkey to join [[isis|ISIS]].[1]

## United Nations negotiations and downfall

United Nations special representative for Libya Martin Kobler negotiated with Belhaj and other militia leaders from March 2016 to install Fayez El Sarraj's national unity government in Tripoli, meaning a designated al-Qaeda-affiliated leader was helping to shape Libya's government near the end of the Obama administration.[1] Following the 2017 Qatar diplomatic crisis, Belhaj was placed on a terrorist watchlist in June 2017 on suspicion of terrorism-related activities with Qatari support, with Egypt, the UAE, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia and Libya's Tobruk government supporting the claim.[1] In January 2019 a British parliamentary report pointed to his involvement in migrant smuggling and Libyan public prosecutor Siddiq Al Sour issued a warrant against him for "abuses and war crimes," prompting him to move to Istanbul.[1]

The article concludes that Belhaj was a CIA asset from the beginning and a major component of the plan to expand ISIS and remove Bashar al-Assad.[1]
