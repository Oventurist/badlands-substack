# Abdelhakim Belhaj

Abdelhakim Belhaj (also spelled Belhadj, born 1 May 1966) is a Libyan politician and military leader, founder and emir of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] (LIFG), later head of the Tripoli Military Council and leader of the Islamist al-Watan Party.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The Badlands account treats him as the pivotal and largely unknown figure connecting [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]], the [[cia|CIA]], the overthrow of [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]], and the pipeline of fighters that fed [[isis|ISIS]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Afghanistan and Al-Qaeda

Belhaj fought in Afghanistan in the 1980s among the CIA-backed Mujahideen against Soviet forces, and it was there that he first met Osama Bin Laden.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He followed Bin Laden to Sudan and then back to Afghanistan after Bin Laden was expelled, running Al-Qaeda training camps.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article argues he was not merely a member but a close associate of Bin Laden and one of Al-Qaeda's most important leaders — which is why he went into hiding immediately after 9/11 and was arrested in Malaysia.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## CIA custody and imprisonment

Belhaj was interrogated in CIA prisons in the early 2000s and then rendered to Libya, where he was held in Abu Salim Prison.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article explains this through the post-9/11 accommodation between Gaddafi and the [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] administration: Gaddafi surrendered his weapons of mass destruction programmes, made peace with Western nations, and agreed to take custody of Belhaj and other captured LIFG members.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Release and the 2011 uprising

In 2009 Belhaj's release was negotiated with Gaddafi's son Saïf al-Islam through the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] preacher Ali Sallabi.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article contends that the newly elected [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration requested the release and that Gaddafi complied to preserve goodwill.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) On release Belhaj went to [[qatar|Qatar]] to take command of the elite [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]], the force he later led against Gaddafi — and the same Libyan brigade later assigned to security at the U.S. consulate in [[benghazi|Benghazi]], which the article says did nothing to stop the 2012 attack.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Politician and businessman

After Gaddafi's fall Belhaj became a politician, founding his own party, and negotiated with United Nations special representative Martin Kobler from March 2016 over installing Fayez El Sarraj's national unity government in Tripoli.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He remained influential with the Tripoli government and the Fajr Libya militia coalition while building business interests in health care, property, air transport and media across Libya, Sudan, Tunisia and Turkey, operating from a fiefdom in Mitiga where he controls the airport, with a rear base in Turkey.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) By autumn 2012 his name appeared on lists of the richest Libyans of the post-Gaddafi era, with a fortune estimated at more than $2 billion.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He founded the airline al-Ajniha ("the wings"), establishing a monopoly on flights into the Libyan capital through Maatiga (Mitiga) airport, the only operational airport after the destruction of Tripoli International.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article argues this airline carried Al-Qaeda fighters trained in Benghazi onward to Syria via Turkey to join ISIS.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Watchlisting and warrant

In June 2017, following the [[qatar-diplomatic-crisis-2017|Qatar diplomatic crisis]], Belhaj was placed on a terrorist watchlist over suspected terrorism-related activity with Qatari support, backed by Egypt, the UAE, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia and Libya's Tobruk government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) In January 2019 a British parliamentary report pointed to his involvement in migrant smuggling and Libyan public prosecutor Siddiq Al Sour issued a warrant against him for "abuses and war crimes," after which Belhaj relocated to Istanbul.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article emphasises that both events occurred under President [[donald-trump|Trump]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article's conclusion is that Belhaj was a CIA asset from the beginning and a major part of the plan to expand ISIS and remove [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in Syria.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)
