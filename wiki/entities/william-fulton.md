# William Fulton

William Fulton was a journalist for the Chicago Tribune who in 1951 published a series of exposés documenting the penetration of American foreign policy by Oxford-trained [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholars]].[1]

## The 1951 Chicago Tribune series

Badlands Media describes the series as an incredible body of reporting on the "cancerous penetration" of hundreds of Rhodes Scholars who had taken over American foreign policy and were, in Fulton's assessment, directing the country toward a third world war.[1]

On July 14, 1951, Fulton wrote that "key positions in the United States department of state are held by a network of American Rhodes scholars," men who "obtained supplemental education and indoctrination at Oxford University in England with the bills paid by the estate of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil John Rhodes]], British empire builder."[1] He quoted Rhodes on his ambition to cause "the ultimate recovery of the United States of America as an integral part of the British empire," adding that the late diamond and gold mining tycoon "aimed at a world federation dominated by Anglo-Saxons."[1]

## Legacy of the reporting

Ehret treats Fulton's series as contemporaneous confirmation of a network whose influence he traces through the Cold War: Escott Reid's designs for [[nato|NATO]], George McGhee's advance of the [[truman-doctrine|Truman Doctrine]], [[dean-rusk|Dean Rusk]]'s guiding hand behind the Korean War and American involvement in Vietnam, and Senator J. William Fulbright's promotion of globalism and a European Union.[1] Rhodes Scholars, he writes, occupied critical positions in the State Departments of both the USA and Canada, in the UN bureaucracy, and in the foundations, operating with a discipline and coherence unseen in any modern imperial civil service.[1]
