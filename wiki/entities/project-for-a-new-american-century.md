# Project for a New American Century

The Project for a New American Century (PNAC) is a neoconservative think tank founded in 1997, described in the Badlands corpus as the intellectual engine behind decades of American regime-change policy, including the eventual targeting of [[syria]].

## Founding and membership

According to [[assad-day-in-damascus]], PNAC was created in 1997 by [[william-kristol]] and [[robert-kagan]] — the latter identified as the husband of [[victoria-nuland]] — and maintained strong ties to the "storied neocon hotbed" of the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]]. Its membership included [[dick-cheney]], [[paul-wolfowitz]], [[john-bolton]] and [[donald-rumsfeld]], among others. Of the 25 original signatories, the article states, 14 were given positions in the administration of George W. Bush.

## Doctrine

PNAC's vision of a "New American Century," the source argues, involved the takeover of countries including but not limited to Libya, Iraq, Iran and Syria in order to achieve [[pax-americana|"Pax Americana"]] — described as the pretext used by the United States to effect regime change across the globe in the name of democracy. The group's report [[rebuilding-americas-defenses|Rebuilding America's Defenses]] declared that "the American peace has proven itself peaceful, stable, and durable" while warning that "even a global Pax Americana will not preserve itself," and concluded that the global order "must have a secure foundation on unquestioned U.S. military preeminence."

The article draws particular attention to the report's observation that "the process of transformation is likely to be a long one, absent some catastrophic and catalyzing event — like a new Pearl Harbor," noting that the document predates the attacks of September 11, 2001 and inviting readers to draw their own conclusions.

## Place in the broader analysis

PNAC is presented as one component of what commentator [[mike-benz]] calls "the Blob," the interlocking Western foreign policy and national security apparatus. Its agenda is read alongside General [[wesley-clark]]'s account of a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] memo listing seven countries to be taken out in five years, and alongside the [[cia|CIA]]'s earlier cultivation of the [[mujahideen]], as evidence of continuity in American proxy warfare.
