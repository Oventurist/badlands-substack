# Writers Guild of America

The Writers Guild of America (WGA) is the labor union representing screenwriters in American film and television. It appears in the Badlands Media corpus principally in [[a-time-to-strike|A Time to Strike]] (October 11, 2023), which treats the guild's 2023 walkout against the studios as the opening act of that year's strike wave and as a test case for how organized labor confronts [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].

## The 2023 strike and settlement

The article reports that after striking for almost 150 days, the writers reached a deal with the studios and voted to end the strike on Wednesday, September 27, 2023. Summarizing the terms, the source states that the deal runs until May 2026 and includes a 5% minimum pay increase upon ratification of the contract, a further 4% increase on May 2, 2024, and another 3.5% increase on May 5, 2025. Writers obtained significant protections against the use of artificial intelligence: AI cannot write or rewrite literary material, and AI-generated material will not be considered source material. On streaming, the guild negotiated a new residual based on viewership. By Monday, October 9, 2023, 99% of members had ratified the agreement.

The author notes that reporting left unclear whether the guild secured protections for the "writer's room" and staffing minimums, demands that featured prominently in member testimonials published on the WGA Contract 2023 website.

## Interpretation in the source

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reads the contract's relatively short 18-month horizon as strategically significant, predicting a far more consequential negotiation over AI when the deal expires: as generative AI grows more sophisticated, the studios will hold significantly greater leverage over content creation. She recounts having wanted to stand with the writers at the outset of the strike while remaining unpersuaded by their demands, and initially framed the disruption in [[hollywood|Hollywood]] as the same optimization wave that corporate back offices absorbed a decade earlier — a comparison she later revises on the grounds that back-office workers neither unionize nor strike, and that their eliminations were celebrated rather than resisted. The guild's gains, in her assessment, are real but short-lived against the trajectory described in [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]]'s [[future-of-work|future of work]] scenarios.
