# Writers Guild of America (WGA)

The Writers Guild of America is the union representing screenwriters in American film and television, and the party to the 2023 Hollywood writers' strike that dominated coverage of the [[summer-of-strikes-2023|Summer of Strikes]] over the summer of 2023.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## The 2023 strike and settlement

After striking for almost 150 days, the writers reached a deal with the studios and voted to end the strike on Wednesday, September 27, 2023; by the following Monday, 99% of members had ratified the contract.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

The reported terms of the deal run until May 2026 and include a 5% minimum pay increase upon ratification, a further 4% bump on May 2, 2024, and another 3.5% increase on May 5, 2025.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Writers also obtained significant protections against the use of [[artificial-intelligence-manipulation|artificial intelligence]]: AI cannot write or rewrite literary material, and AI-generated material will not be considered source material.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) For streaming, the guild negotiated a new residual based on viewership.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The article notes it is unclear from the reporting whether the guild secured protections for the "writer's room" and staffing minimums, demands featured in member testimonials on the WGA Contract 2023 website.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Assessment

The author expects a far more in-depth conversation about AI when the deal expires in 18 months, and argues the short contract term is itself significant: as generative AI becomes more sophisticated, the studios will hold significantly greater leverage over content creation at the next negotiation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) The piece also reflects that while the author initially wanted to stand with the writers, their demands proved a sticking point, and that the transformation the writers faced echoed the optimization wave that swept corporate back offices a decade earlier — with the difference that back offices do not unionize and do not strike, and their job losses were celebrated rather than contested.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Joined by SAG-AFTRA (July 2023)

By July 2023 the 11,000 WGA screenwriters who had been on strike since May were joined on the picket lines by tens of thousands of [[sag-aftra|SAG-AFTRA]] members, halting the film and television industry for the second time in three years.[2] Reports that studios aimed to prolong the strike long enough for writers to lose their homes drew a widely circulated angry response from actor [[ron-perlman|Ron Perlman]].[2] See [[2023-hollywood-labor-strikes|2023 Hollywood labor strikes]].[2]

## Tentative agreement ending the 2023 strike

On September 24, 2023 the Writers Guild reached a tentative agreement with the [[alliance-of-motion-picture-and-television-producers|Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers]] to end its strike after nearly five months, and picketing was suspended.[3] The parties finalized the framework of the deal after untangling a stalemate over artificial intelligence and writing-room staffing levels.[3] The guild told members it had "reached a tentative agreement on a new 2023 MBA, which is to say an agreement in principle on all deal points, subject to drafting final contract language," an announcement that came just after sunset and the start of Yom Kippur following five days of long negotiations.[3] Details were withheld pending release ahead of membership ratification votes.[3]

## June 2023: sixth week of the strike

By the June 9, 2023 Badlands News Brief, the WGA strike had entered its sixth week with no deal in sight.[4] Production and writers rooms had shut down across all of late-night television and on major series including ABC's *Abbott Elementary* and Netflix's *Stranger Things*, while some productions such as the U.K.-based set of Amazon Prime's *The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power* continued shooting with nonwriting producers.[4] That same week [[sag-aftra|SAG-AFTRA]] authorized a strike by a roughly 98 percent margin while the [[directors-guild-of-america|Directors Guild of America]] reached a separate tentative deal with the studios, a split Badlands commentary characterized as a collapse of guild solidarity.[4]
