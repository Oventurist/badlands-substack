# John Lennon

John Lennon is presented in Badlands Media's essay "Heroes of the Great Awakening" as one of a group of cultural figures whose deaths the outlet's contributors read as attempts to arrest a rising popular awareness in the United States.[1] The article groups Lennon with [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]], Bob Marley and the Kennedys as figures killed, in the author's telling, in "a seeming attempt to stop the spread of awareness in America" during and after the 1960s.[1]

The essay's specific charge concerning Lennon is that he "heavily questioned the Military Industrial Complex."[1] The author draws a distinction between an ordinary citizen voicing such criticism and a mass-culture figure doing so: when "a major culture creator with the type of staying power that lasts generations" attacks the war economy, the article argues, "whatever mafia sits at the top of their control pyramid takes care of the problem before it spreads."[1] Lennon therefore functions in the piece less as a biographical subject than as evidence for its central thesis that threats to the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] and allied structures are treated by the [[deep-state|Establishment]] as fatal.[1]

Lennon also appears in the article's closing argument about technology and democratised media. The author writes that culture creators were once "groomed by Hollywood and Record companies before being presented to us," whereas now "anyone with a smart phone can... sing a peace ballad like John Lennon and share it with the world."[1] In this framing Lennon is a template for a decentralised protest culture that free-speech platforms such as [[rumble|Rumble]] and [[substack|Substack]] are said to make possible, and censorship the weapon deployed against it.[1]

## 1980 murder and the Reagan era

A December 21, 2024 Badlands essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] placed Lennon's murder on the streets of Manhattan one month after the 1980 election, framing it as part of a [[deep-state|Deep State]] effort to dampen the spirit igniting in Americans after [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s victory, and drawing a parallel between its timing and details and events of 2024.[2] Carlson wrote that the killing affected [[baby-boomers|Baby Boomers]] most acutely because Lennon had been a childhood idol to many of that generation.[2] He speculated that Lennon and Yoko Ono may have gone into hiding in the years before the murder, asking whether Lennon had decided he no longer wanted to participate in the Cabal system that made him famous and had thereby become a threat to it.[2]
