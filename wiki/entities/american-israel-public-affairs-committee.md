# American Israel Public Affairs Committee

The American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) is a pro-[[israel|Israel]] lobbying organization in the United States that advocates on behalf of the U.S.–Israel relationship before [[congress|Congress]] and the executive branch.[1]

## Appearance in the UFC Freedom 250 plot allegations

AIPAC appears in federal allegations surrounding the disrupted plot against [[ufc-freedom-250|UFC Freedom 250]] on the [[white-house|White House]] South Lawn in June 2026.[1] According to the [[fbi|FBI]], participants in encrypted Signal conversations involving up to 23 individuals discussed targeting "capitalist elites," billionaires, or politicians tied to AIPAC, alongside planning for explosive-laden drones, sniper positions, staging areas, escape routes and communications protocols.[1] At least five American citizens were arrested across multiple states by June 16, 2026, on charges including conspiracy to commit murder.[1]

## 2024 primary campaigns against progressives

In the summer of 2024, pro-Israel super PACs led chiefly by AIPAC's own super PAC spent a combined $25 million on advertising to defeat Representatives [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] of New York and [[cori-bush|Cori Bush]] of Missouri, producing the two most expensive House primaries ever held.[2] The spending was prompted by both members' criticism of Israel during its war with [[hamas]], and resulted in more mainstream Democrats — George Latimer in New York and Wesley Bell in Missouri — advancing in safe blue districts.[2]

Politico reported that progressive Democrats concluded they had no organized way to counter such money and feared AIPAC and allied groups would target even bigger names in 2026 and beyond because the strategy had been shown to work.[2] Senator [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] said of AIPAC, "I think they smell success," adding that the deeper problem was "the intimidating presence they have over every member of [[congress|Congress]]" and that "it bothers me that there hasn't been more outrage."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode through a controlled-opposition lens, arguing that the Right/Left dynamic around Israel was engineered to culminate in exactly this confrontation, and that hate-speech measures advancing in Republican-controlled state legislatures function less to protect a religion than to shield a foreign government from criticism.[2] He tied the discussion to [[horseshoe-theory|Horseshoe Theory]] as the basis for renewed dialogue between the earnest Left and earnest Right.[2]
