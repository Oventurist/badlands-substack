# Morris Brown College

Morris Brown College (MBC) is a private historically black liberal arts school in Atlanta, Georgia, which drew national attention in August 2023 when it reinstated a campus mask mandate three years after the height of the COVID-19 pandemic.[1]

## August 2023 mask mandate

The college announced on Instagram that a mask mandate would apply to all students and employees on campus for a two-week period as a precautionary measure, even though its administration had not reported any positive cases among its own students.[1] Several students in the Atlanta University Center — a consortium that includes several HBCUs, among them MBC — had tested positive for the virus after returning to the city from summer break the previous week.[1]

College president Dr. Kevin James wrote in the announcement, "We prioritize your safety and seek your cooperation in preventing another pandemic," and thanked students for "being proactive in safeguarding our community."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the announcement as an opening move in a renewed pandemic-restriction cycle, writing that authorities were "going to try to mask you up, vax you up and lock you down," and noting the timing alongside the Georgia prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a case predicated on his claims that the 2020 election was stolen through tactics implemented during the first pandemic wave.[1] While allowing that the development could be read pessimistically, the commentary argued it could instead become "the biggest redpill germination campaign of all time" — "our second chance at a last impression."[1]

## 2023 mask mandate reinstatement

On August 20, 2023 Morris Brown College announced in an Instagram post that it had reinstated its COVID-19 mask mandate "effective immediately" following reports of infections among students in the Atlanta University Center.[2] All students and employees were required to wear face masks for a fourteen-day period, with students also directed to observe physical distancing.[2] The announcement was among the first of several institutional mandates that summer and figured prominently in coverage of the resulting backlash; see [[election-variant|Election Variant]].[2]
