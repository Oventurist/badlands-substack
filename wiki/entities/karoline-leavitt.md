# Karoline Leavitt

Karoline Leavitt is the [[white-house|White House]] Press Secretary in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## 2020 election materials briefing

Leavitt addressed media coverage of the [[fbi|FBI]]'s seizure of 2020 election materials from [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], noting contrasts between that coverage and prior reporting on claims of Russian election interference.[1]

## Midterm cancellation remarks

In January 2026, after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said in a Reuters interview that the United States "shouldn't even have an election" that year because of what he described as the success of his administration, Leavitt responded to criticism by saying that the president had been speaking facetiously and was referring to his view that the country was "on the right track."[2] Her characterization of the remarks as a joke became the White House's official position on the episode ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[2]

## March 2026: demand for ABC News retraction

As White House press secretary, Leavitt called on [[abc-news|ABC News]] to retract prior reporting that the [[fbi|FBI]] had warned of an Iranian drone attack on California in retaliation for US actions.[3] ABC had reported, citing law-enforcement sources, that the FBI assessed [[iran|Iran]] had considered or aspired to conduct drone attacks in California and that investigators were examining intelligence indicating Iran had explored launching drones from ships or other platforms near the US West Coast.[3] Leavitt said the report was inaccurate and demanded a correction or retraction, arguing it misrepresented intelligence about potential Iranian retaliation.[3] No Iranian attack on California occurred, and officials said authorities continued to monitor potential threats.[3]

## April 2026: Iran ceasefire deadline dispute

In April 2026 Leavitt said that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had not set a firm deadline for receiving a proposal from the Iranians to extend the ceasefire.[4] Israeli officials had said the current arrangement was understood to run only through Sunday, suggesting a near-term expiration of the pause in hostilities, a characterization that created confusion about the agreement's status and duration.[4] The White House disputed that interpretation, with Leavitt emphasizing that no specific timetable had been imposed by the administration.[4] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described her remarks as a clarification that the "anonymous sourced reporting" carried by the Israeli outlet YNetNews was false and that Trump was satisfied with [[iran|Iran]]'s cooperation.[4]

## Amplification of the "super soldiers" video (January 2026)

Over the weekend of January 10–11, 2026, Leavitt amplified a viral video describing "energy weapons and super soldiers" in connection with the reported American operation against [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in [[venezuela|Venezuela]]; no official confirmation of the claims was issued.[5] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] emphasized that the eyewitness accounts were "amplified, not denied by the White House itself," treating the press secretary's amplification as a deliberate element of narrative power projection rather than an accidental endorsement.[5]


## Amplification of the Venezuela "super soldier" story

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that a viral account attributed to a [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] security guard, recounting in Spanish a terrifying story of super soldiers and energy weapons, dropped on a Friday night and was shared early the following Saturday by White House Press Secretary Leavitt, amplifying its credibility.[6] Ashe argued the episode functioned as power projection and propaganda, noting that directed-energy weapons had been publicly disclosed more than a decade earlier and were now, on the story's terms, confirmed operational.[6]

## Response to the 2025 BLS jobs revision

After the [[bureau-of-labor-statistics|Bureau of Labor Statistics]] announced on September 9, 2025 that employers had added 911,000 fewer jobs over the prior 12 months than first estimated — apparently the largest downward revision on record — Leavitt said the adjustment both confirmed the weakness of the Biden-era economy and underscored the agency's lack of credibility.[7] "Today, the BLS released the largest downward revision on record proving that President Trump was right: Biden's economy was a disaster and the BLS is broken," she said, adding that new leadership was needed to restore confidence in the agency's data.[7] Her statement followed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s dismissal of BLS Commissioner [[erika-mcentarfer|Erika McEntarfer]] weeks earlier.[7]

## April 2025 tariff messaging

Responding to China's decision to raise duties on American goods to 84% and Beijing's vow to "fight to the end," Leavitt said from the [[white-house|White House]] podium on April 9, 2025: "When you punch at the United States of America, President Trump is going to punch back further."[8] Later that day she appeared outside the White House alongside Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]], who rejected the framing of the [[us-china-trade-war-2025|tariff escalation]] as a "trade war" or a de facto embargo.[8]

## Announcement of the May 2025 Gulf tour

At a [[white-house|White House]] press briefing on 22 April 2025 Leavitt announced that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would travel to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], [[qatar|Qatar]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] from 13 to 16 May, a three-country Middle East tour that would be his second foreign trip since returning to office, following his planned attendance at the Vatican for the funeral of Pope Francis.[9] She gave no further detail on the itinerary.[9]

## White House speaker system

In November 2025 Leavitt publicized the installation of a new speaker system at the [[white-house|White House]], telling reporters on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s behalf: "The president wanted me to let all of you know that he is testing the speakers for what will be the best event in the history of the White House."[10] She said Trump could control the music from an iPad in the Oval Office.[10] The system drew attention on Friday, November 14, when music from "The Phantom of the Opera" played loudly enough to be heard outside the White House, as reported by Politico correspondent Jake Taylor, with AFP's Danny Kemp later hearing James Brown's "It's a Man's Man's Man's World."[10]

## Response to Mamdani's polling-threat remarks (November 2025)

After New York City mayoral candidate [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] linked the Trump administration to bomb threats that forced voters out of multiple New Jersey polling places on November 4, 2025, Leavitt said his remarks were "completely irresponsible" and not based in fact.[11] Mamdani had described the threats, which were found to be non-credible, as "an illustration of the attacks we're seeing on our democracy" and as part of an approach of intimidating voters with baseless allegations of voter fraud.[11]

## Boycott of the Correspondents' Dinner (March 2025)

In March 2025 Leavitt announced during an episode of "The Sean Spicer Show" — hosted by Sean Spicer, who served as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first White House press secretary in 2017 — that she would not attend the [[white-house-correspondents-association|White House Correspondents' Association]] dinner scheduled for April 26.[12] "I will not be in attendance at the White House Correspondents' Dinner, and that's breaking news for 'The Sean Spicer Show,'" she said, adding that the WHCA "has truly become a monetized monopoly over the White House and the coverage of the president of the United States in America."[12]

Leavitt argued that the association's founding rationale — that presidents of the era were not holding enough press conferences — no longer applied under Trump, and that "the priorities of the media have shifted, especially with this new digital age."[12] She described the WHCA as an exclusive group that had "not really welcomed other people, new media, independent journalists, with open arms," which she offered as justification for the White House itself determining "who gets to be part of that 13-person press pool, who gets to ask the president of the United States questions in the Oval Office, aboard Air Force One."[12] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the announcement as a signal that the administration's press-access overhaul was genuine rather than a "rugpull," reasoning that the WHCA's power rests on legitimacy that collapses when principals decline to attend its events.[12]

## 2024 CNN interview cut short

Before joining the White House, Leavitt served as national press secretary for the Trump 2024 campaign.[13] In June 2024, days before the [[cnn|CNN]]-hosted presidential debate, she appeared on the network and began discussing its historical bias against her candidate, focusing on moderator [[jake-tapper|Jake Tapper]]'s record of anti-Trump claims.[13] Anchor [[kasie-hunt|Kasie Hunt]] cut her microphone within seconds and ended the interview, prompting the campaign's response that "this proved our point" and widespread derision of Hunt on social media.[13]

## October 2025 clash with S.V. Date

In October 2025 Leavitt publicly accused [[huffpost|HuffPost]] correspondent [[sv-date|S.V. Date]] of being a "left-wing hack" who "constantly bombards" her phone, after she shared a question the reporter had texted her the previous week.[14] Date had asked whether President Donald Trump was "aware of the significance of Budapest" when he agreed to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin in the Hungarian capital for peace talks, adding, "Does he not see why Ukraine might object to that site? Who suggested Budapest?" — a reference to the 1994 [[budapest-memorandum|Budapest Memorandum]] under which Ukraine surrendered its inherited Soviet nuclear weapons in exchange for Russian assurances against force or economic coercion.[14] Leavitt answered, "Your mom did," and when Date replied "Is this funny to you?", the exchange circulated widely.[14]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] praised the response, writing that Leavitt "talks like a winner" and casting the episode as a model for reclaiming public shaming from what he described as bad-faith operators in the press.[14]

## Response to threats against Trump nominees (November 2024)

Ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday in November 2024, while designated to serve as [[white-house|White House]] press secretary after the inauguration, Leavitt disclosed that "several" of President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Cabinet picks had been targeted with bomb threats and [[swatting|swatting]] — false emergency calls directed at their residences.[15] At least four incoming officials reported bomb threats at their homes.[15] "In response, law enforcement and other authorities acted quickly to ensure the safety of those who were targeted," Leavitt said in a statement posted online, adding that "President Trump and the entire Transition team are grateful for their swift action."[15] She continued that the president-elect and the transition were "focused on doing the work of uniting our nation by ensuring a safe and prosperous future," and that "with President Trump as our example, dangerous acts of intimidation and violence will not deter us."[15] The [[fbi|FBI]] subsequently confirmed it was aware of numerous such incidents targeting incoming nominees and appointees and was working with its law enforcement partners.[15]

## Clarifying Bondi's Epstein remarks (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, after the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] issued a memo stating that [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] kept no "client list," Leavitt as White House Press Secretary and Justice Department spokesperson Chad Gilmartin told reporters that Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] had meant to say that all of the files regarding Epstein were on her desk — not a specific list of co-conspirators — when she had told Fox News in February that such a document was "sitting on my desk."[16]

## Transition spokesperson statements (November 2024)

As spokesperson for the 2024 [[donald-trump|Trump]] transition, Leavitt responded to reporting that [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] was likely to be tapped for a senior [[fbi|FBI]] or Justice Department post by telling Axios that "President-elect Trump has made brilliant decisions on who will serve in his second administration at lightning pace," and that "remaining decisions will continue to be announced by him when they are made."[17] She also framed the transition's forthcoming energy package, which would approve liquefied natural gas export permits and expand offshore and federal-lands drilling, by stating that "the American people can bank on President Trump using his executive power on day one to deliver on the promises he made to them on the campaign trail."[17]

## October 2025 defense of the Portland National Guard deployment

On Monday, October 6, 2025, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt sharply criticized a federal judge for halting the administration's plan to send [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops to Portland, Oregon, calling the ruling "untethered in reality and in the law."[18] "The president has the right to call up the National Guard in cases where he deems it appropriate," Leavitt told reporters at a press briefing, accusing protesters outside a Portland [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] facility of "inciting violence."[18] Her remarks followed US District Judge [[karin-immergut|Karin Immergut]]'s temporary injunction blocking President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s order to deploy Guard troops from other states to Portland; the administration filed an appeal, and Leavitt expressed confidence that the president's powers would be upheld.[18]

## Statements on the Gaza war (May 2025)

Amid reporting that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had privately warned [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government that the United States would withdraw its backing if [[israel|Israel]] did not conclude the war in [[gaza|Gaza]], Leavitt said publicly that "the president wants the war in Gaza to end."[19] Her remark followed the surprise release of Israeli-American soldier Edan Alexander, secured through direct US–[[hamas|Hamas]] negotiations conducted without Israel's involvement.[19]

## Disclosure of Trump's MRI results

On Monday, December 1, 2025, Leavitt disclosed details of President Trump's recent MRI at the White House press briefing, describing the results as "perfectly normal" and part of a routine physical.[20] She read a statement from Physician to the President Sean P. Barbabella, who said the scan was ordered as a precautionary measure and that "men in his age group benefit from a thorough evaluation of cardiovascular and abdominal health."[20] The report noted no evidence of arterial narrowing, inflammation, clotting, or abnormalities in the heart, major blood vessels, or abdominal organs, and concluded that Trump "remains in excellent overall health."[20]

## Response to the "Seditious Six" video (November 2025)

After President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the six Democratic lawmakers behind the [[seditious-six-video|"Seditious Six" video]] "traitors" and accused them of "SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR," Leavitt said the president wanted to see the six face consequences but did not want to execute members of Congress.[21] The lawmakers involved reported receiving a variety of threats following Trump's post.[21]

## October 2025 economic data (November 2025)

On Wednesday, November 12, 2025, Leavitt said the October jobs report and inflation data would likely never be released even after the government reopened, because the [[bureau-of-labor-statistics|Bureau of Labor Statistics]] had been largely idle throughout the 40-plus-day [[october-2025-government-shutdown|shutdown]].[22] "Democrats may have permanently damaged the federal statistical system with October CPI and jobs reports likely never being released," she told reporters, warning that the impairment would leave policymakers at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] "flying blind at a critical period."[22]

## Response to the leaked DIA Iran assessment

After media outlets published a leaked low-confidence Defense Intelligence Agency assessment downplaying the effect of U.S. strikes on Iran's nuclear program, Leavitt condemned the disclosure on June 25, 2025.[23] "The leaking of this alleged assessment is a clear attempt to demean President Trump, and discredit the brave fighter pilots who conducted a perfectly executed mission to obliterate Iran's nuclear program," she said.[23] She added: "Everyone knows what happens when you drop fourteen 30,000 pound bombs perfectly on their targets: total obliteration."[23]

## Statement on inauguration invitations, December 2024

As spokesperson for the Trump presidential transition in December 2024, Leavitt commented on reports that President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had invited Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and other foreign dignitaries to his January 20 inauguration, saying that "world leaders are lining up to meet with President Trump because they know he will soon return to power and restore peace through American strength around the globe."[24]

## June 2025 briefing on the Israel–Iran decision

At a [[white-house|White House]] press briefing in June 2025, Leavitt delivered a message directly from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on whether the United States would join the Israel–Iran conflict: "Based on the fact that there's a substantial chance of negotiations that may or may not take place with Iran in the near future, I will make my decision whether or not to go within the next two weeks."[25] Asked what the message was for "everyday Trump supporters" concerned about US involvement, Leavitt said to "trust in President Trump," adding that the president's top priority was ensuring that [[iran|Iran]] does not successfully construct a nuclear weapon.[25] The announcement came 24 hours after Trump said he had not made up his mind, and fit a broader administration posture of strategic ambiguity that revealed little publicly about its thinking or potential courses of action.[25] Iran's deputy foreign minister meanwhile told the BBC that US involvement would cause "hell" in the region, insisting "this is not America's war."[25]

## DC federal law enforcement surge (August 2025)

Announcing the seven-day federal patrol of Washington, D.C. that began at midnight on August 7, 2025, Leavitt said the capital "is an amazing city, but it has been plagued by violent crime for far too long," adding that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had directed an increased federal law enforcement presence to protect innocent citizens.[26] "Starting tonight, there will be no safe harbor for violent criminals in D.C.," she said.[26] The operation was led by the [[united-states-park-police|U.S. Park Police]] with participation from the [[fbi|FBI]], [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]], [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|ATF]] and [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] divisions, all in marked units.[26]

## Defense of the March 2025 Alien Enemies Act deportations

On Sunday, March 16, 2025, Leavitt defended the removal of alleged [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] members carried out under the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]] despite Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]]'s order blocking the law's use, appearing to point to the fact that the judge's written order did not explicitly mention planes already in the air.[27] She stated that the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] had arrested almost 300 members of the Venezuelan gang over that weekend.[27]

## United Nations escalator dispute (September 2025)

After the [[united-nations|United Nations]] attributed a stalled escalator at the General Assembly to a videographer from the U.S. delegation, Leavitt posted an article alleging that jokes had circulated beforehand about stopping the escalator to inconvenience President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] by forcing him to walk.[28] "If someone at the UN intentionally stopped the escalator as the President and First Lady were stepping on, they need to be fired and investigated immediately," she wrote on social media.[28]

## October 2025 briefings on Xi and Putin

On Thursday, October 23, 2025, Leavitt announced that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would meet Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] the following Thursday on the sidelines of the [[asia-pacific-economic-cooperation|APEC]] summit, as part of a trip to Malaysia and South Korea, without providing further details.[30] The announcement came amid an escalating trade war in which Trump had threatened an additional 100 percent tariff on Chinese goods from November.[30]

In the same period Leavitt told a press briefing that a summit between Trump and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] "is not completely off the table," a day after Trump called off a planned Budapest meeting.[30] Asked about the cancellation and recent US sanctions on major Russian oil companies, she said the president and the entire administration hoped such a meeting could happen again one day, and that Trump "has not seen enough interest in enough action" from Russia toward a peace settlement.[30]
