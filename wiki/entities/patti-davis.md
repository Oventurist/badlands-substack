# Patti Davis

Patti Davis is an American author and the daughter of former President [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]], who in February 2024 entered the debate over the fitness and advanced age of the leading 2024 presidential candidates.[1]

## Comments on cognitive testing

In a February 19, 2024 interview on NBC News' "Meet the Press," Davis said that requiring cognitive tests for presidential candidates would "probably be a good idea," citing "what we know about what age can do," while allowing that age does not always produce decline.[1] She added a personal frame of reference: "My father was 77 when he left office after two terms. It seems so young now, doesn't it?"[1] Her remarks were reported by [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] against a backdrop of polling showing broad voter unease: an Ipsos survey found 59 percent of respondents considered both [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "too old" to serve again, and an earlier November poll found 77 percent believed Biden was too old to remain in office.[1]

## Commentary on political fear and division

Davis also argued that American political divisions "really started because we're all so scared," and that "fear morphs into anger," a dynamic she described as unsustainable.[1] Badlands Media commentator [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] treated this as unintentionally deep insight, reading it through the lens of a [[deep-state|Deep State]] narrative in which the presidency has been controlled since Reagan's departure, with the media functioning as an arm of that apparatus and the September 11 attacks marking the inversion of externally projected fear into a domestic security-state phenomenon.[1] Esler linked the Reagan connection to the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] framing, invoking Reagan's line — later quoted by Trump and amplified in Q posts — that "when you can't make them see the light, make them feel the heat."[1]
