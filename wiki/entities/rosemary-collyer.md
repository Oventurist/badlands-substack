# Rosemary Collyer

Rosemary M. Collyer is a judge of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia who also served as a member of the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-court|Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court]].[1] She is cited in Badlands Media commentary as the author of the judicial rebuke that first documented systematic misuse of intelligence databases by the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration.[1]

## The April 2017 memorandum opinion

The July 2025 article "Shall We Play a Game?" by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], quoting the Daily Signal, reports that Collyer signed a 99-page reprimand issued on April 26, 2017 — three months after [[donald-trump|Trump]] was sworn in.[1] The opinion recorded that the court had learned in October 2016 that analysts at the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] were conducting prohibited database searches "with much greater frequency than had previously been disclosed to the court."[1] It criticised the NSA's Office of Inspector General and its Office of Compliance for Operations for "an institutional lack of candor" and described the matter as raising "very serious Fourth Amendment issues."[1]

## Significance in the Badlands account

Lange treats the Collyer opinion as the documentary anchor of [[fisagate|FISAgate]], arguing that it establishes that FISA abuse predated and was separate from the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page warrant]] and that the court's scrutiny began before the public had heard of Fusion GPS, Christopher Steele, or the Nunes memo.[1] He credits Admiral [[mike-rogers|Mike Rogers]] with bringing the violations to the court's attention before the 2016 election, and the article quotes Joe DiGenova's claim that Rogers briefed the chief judge personally and worked with her for months to identify who had lied to the court, with the resulting information later passed to Attorney General [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]].[1] The phrase "institutional lack of candor," drawn from Collyer's opinion, becomes one of the article's organising legal motifs, applied in turn to [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] and [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]].[1]
