# Public Investment Fund

The Public Investment Fund (PIF) is Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund and the sponsor of the [[future-investment-initiative|Future Investment Initiative]], the international forum through which the kingdom courts global capital and promotes economic diversification.[1]

## 2026 Miami summit

In February 2026 the PIF-affiliated Future Investment Initiative Priority Summit convened in Miami, drawing global investors, policymakers and business leaders.[1] [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] delivered the keynote — the second consecutive year he had attended and spoken — discussing economic policy and promoting the United States as a destination for international investment.[1] The event drew political attention in Florida, with reporting noting scrutiny of connections between U.S. political figures and foreign-backed investment initiatives.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the speech resembled Trump's Riyadh address of the prior year, touting Gulf Arab states' successes and their intent to diversify into technology, infrastructure, tourism and the U.S. economy.[1] He interpreted Trump's viral jab that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] "didn't think he would be kissing my ass" and "better be nice to me" as a sign of close friendship rather than insult, given MBS's pledge of trillions in U.S. investment.[1]

## Hollywood expansion (2026)

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund committed approximately $12 billion as part of a $24 billion Gulf equity package financing Paramount's $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, structured as passive stakes to ease U.S. regulatory review.[2] The commitment is part of Riyadh's multi-billion-dollar push into entertainment under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's Vision 2030 plan, which also includes a PIF-led $55 billion acquisition of Electronic Arts in 2025, full control of MBC Group, a $1 billion studio partnership with Arena SNK, and funding for domestic film production and festivals including the Riyadh Comedy Festival, Soundstorm, and the Red Sea International Film Festival.[2]

## Investment in Affinity Partners

By the end of 2021 the PIF had committed $2 billion to [[affinity-partners|Affinity Partners]], the private equity firm founded by [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] after he left the White House, anchoring a $3 billion debut fund of which nearly 99 percent came from non-U.S. sources.[3] PIF staff reportedly raised red flags about the commitment, which Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] is said to have overridden.[3] The arrangement became the subject of a June 2024 investigation by the [[senate-finance-committee|Senate Finance Committee]] under chairman [[ron-wyden|Ron Wyden]].[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary treated the PIF stake as strategically significant rather than merely transactional, arguing that Affinity's stated focus on the Israeli technology sector — combined with the sector's wartime strain — indicated a deliberate use of Saudi sovereign capital to stabilize the Israeli economy even as MBS publicly denounced the Netanyahu cabinet.[3]

## Electronic Arts buyout, September 2025

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund joined [[silver-lake-partners|Silver Lake Partners]] and [[affinity-partners|Affinity Partners]], the firm led by [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], in a $55 billion agreement announced on 29 September 2025 to acquire [[electronic-arts|Electronic Arts]].[4] Stockholders were to receive $210 per share in cash, a 25 percent premium, and the deal remained subject to regulatory approval specifically because of Saudi involvement, though the Saudi-Trump family relationship had been notably close in recent years.[4]
