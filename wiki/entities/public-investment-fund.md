# Public Investment Fund

The Public Investment Fund (PIF) is Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund and the principal financial vehicle behind the Kingdom's [[saudi-vision-2030|Vision 2030]] economic diversification agenda.[1]

At the [[saudi-us-investment-forum|Saudi-US Investment Forum]] in Riyadh on May 13, 2025, Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] said that the United States is "a major destination for the Public Investment Fund, accounting for approximately 40 percent of the fund's global investments."[1] He tied the fund's American exposure to a broader package of deals worth more than $300 billion signed during the visit, and to a pipeline of investment opportunities he valued at $600 billion with ambitions of reaching $1 trillion.[1]

The crown prince presented joint investment through the fund as one of the most important pillars of the Saudi–American economic relationship, while framing cooperation with Washington as extending beyond commerce to peacemaking in the region and the wider world.[1]

## Entry into the space sector (2024)

On Monday, May 27, 2024, the PIF launched [[neo-space-group|Neo Space Group]] (NSG) to develop the Kingdom's satellite and space industry, describing it as the fund's first investment focused on the space sector.[2] NSG is intended to develop and enhance commercial space operations in Saudi Arabia, providing satellite and space solutions locally and globally, and to invest in localization, technology, start-ups and knowledge within the sector as part of the Kingdom's economic diversification drive.[2] Omar al-Madhi, the PIF's co-head of MENA Direct Investments, called the establishment of NSG "an important milestone in the development of the growing satellite and space sector in Saudi Arabia."[2]

Badlands commentary linked the announcement to broader speculation about Saudi capital and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], noting that the same line of inquiry surfaced Saudi participation — via [[kingdom-holding|Kingdom Holding]] — in Musk's [[xai|xAI]] funding round.[2]

## Badlands account of the fund's near-insolvency and rescue

Badlands Media, writing in November 2024, described the Public Investment Fund as holding roughly $930 billion in total assets, making it one of the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world, and its governor [[yasir-al-rumayyan|Yasir Al-Rumayyan]] as one of the most influential figures in the court of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[3]

According to that account, when King Abdullah died in January 2015 and MBS was appointed Defense Minister by his father, the new king Salman, MBS concluded that the PIF had been mismanaged — perhaps intentionally — and was losing roughly $100 billion a year, about a quarter of the entire annual Saudi government budget, putting it on course for insolvency by the end of 2017 and threatening to bankrupt the state.[3] MBS is said to have locked himself in a room with his four closest advisers until they produced a plan to restructure the Saudi government from scratch; the plan took twelve hours to draft and ten days to execute on paper, and Badlands links it to the Saudi royal purge of the weekend of November 4, 2017.[3] Al-Rumayyan, one of those four advisers, was then tasked with fixing and managing the fund.[3]

In the decade that followed, the fund oversaw a divestment of Saudi wealth away from oil, the first public sale of [[saudi-aramco|Aramco]] shares in history, the creation of sports leagues and world-class resorts, and heavy investment across sectors, particularly high technology and video games.[3]
