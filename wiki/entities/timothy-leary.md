# Timothy Leary

Timothy Leary was a Harvard psychologist who returned to [[harvard-university|Harvard]] in 1960 and, with the blessing of departmental chairman [[henry-a-murray|Henry A. Murray]], began experiments with psilocybin on students.[1] In Badlands Media's account, Leary is presented not as an independent counterculture pioneer but as a recruited instrument within a broader establishment program to re-engineer human personality, overlapping in time and place with the [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]] experiments that also swept up [[ted-kaczynski|Theodore Kaczynski]].[1]

## Harvard and the psilocybin work

A June 22, 2000 article in the London *Guardian* observed that by the late 1950s Murray had become interested in hallucinogens including LSD and psilocybin, and that soon after his experiments on Kaczynski and his classmates were under way, Leary returned to Harvard and began his psilocybin work with Murray's approval.[1] That work, the [[harvard-psilocybin-project|Harvard Psilocybin Project]], ran from 1960 to 1962 — the same window in which Kaczynski underwent what the article calls his drug-assisted transformation — and is described as having been overseen directly by [[sidney-gottlieb|Sidney Gottlieb]] of MK Ultra.[1]

In his autobiography Leary described Murray as "the wizard of personality assessment" who, as [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]] chief psychologist, had monitored military experiments on brainwashing and sodium amytal interrogation, adding that Murray "expressed great interest in our drug-research project and offered his support."[1]

## Recruitment and stated aims

In a 1991 presentation titled *From Psychedelics to Cybernetics*, Leary described his recruitment: "In the year 1960, I was invited to come to Harvard University, and I was given a license by them. They asked me to come to Harvard to develop new ways of behavior change."[1] The article treats this admission as evidence that behavior modification, not scientific curiosity, was the institutional purpose of the program.[1]

Leary was recruited to the project of retooling humanity under what the article calls a new drug-based pagan religion by [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]], with whom he collaborated for years.[1] Writing in 1983, Leary said the two had "run up against the Judeo-Christian commitment to one God, one religion, one reality," argued that drugs opening the mind to multiple realities "inevitably lead to a polytheistic view of the universe," and concluded that "the time for a new humanist religion based on intelligence, good natured pluralism and scientific paganism had arrived."[1]

## Relationship to cybernetics

Leary, like [[gregory-bateson|Gregory Bateson]], is described as a devotee of [[cybernetics|cybernetics]], the science of control developed by acolytes of Bertrand Russell and led by his disciple [[norbert-wiener|Norbert Wiener]].[1] In this reading the psychedelic movement and the cybernetic management of populations were two faces of the same social-engineering project, supplying the cultural conditioning that a [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] order would require.[1]
