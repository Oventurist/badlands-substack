# Xi Jinping

Xi Jinping is the president of China and one of the three leaders of the founding nations of the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organisation]] who appeared together at the 2025 summit in Tianjin.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Tianjin summit

Xi hosted the summit held August 31 – September 1, 2025, standing with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] alongside other world leaders representing nearly half the world's population and 20% of world GDP.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) Together they advanced a new financial and security architecture premised on the principles of the [[tianjin-declaration|Tianjin Declaration]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Development architecture

The productive financial architecture of China and its growing array of allies is credited with having unleashed over $3 trillion in large-scale infrastructure investment through the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], tied increasingly to the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Putin's Beijing visit and the "Sovereign Alliance" (May 2026)

In May 2026, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] traveled to Beijing to meet Xi directly on the heels of a [[donald-trump|Trump]]–Xi summit in the same city.[3] The [[center-for-european-policy-analysis|Center for European Policy Analysis]] argued that the Putin–Xi relationship had matured beyond a tactical "marriage of convenience" into an alliance rooted in a "shared consciousness," noting that the two leaders had met nearly 50 times since 2012 — more than any other world leader has met Xi — culminating in the February 2022 "no limits" friendship declaration issued days before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.[3] CEPA held that Beijing has sustained Moscow's war effort through dual-use exports, coordinated information operations, and economic lifelines, that both powers back [[iran|Iran]], and that they seek to reorder the international system against the West, urging Washington to abandon hopes of splitting the pair.[3]

## Back-to-back summits with Trump and Putin (May 2026)

Xi hosted U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for a state visit in May 2026 that included meetings on trade, Taiwan and the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]], and was scheduled to receive Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Beijing on 19–20 May, less than a week later.[4] Chinese state media described the sequence of visits by the U.S. and Russian presidents as unusual and indicative of Beijing's growing diplomatic influence.[4]

Ahead of the Russian visit, Xi and Putin exchanged congratulatory messages, with state media quoting Xi as saying China–Russia cooperation had "continuously deepened and solidified."[4] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the Trump–Xi meeting, dismissed by financial media as a dud for lack of deliverables, in fact signalled the arrival of the [[multipolar-world|Multipolar World]] and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[4]

## Alleged military coup and purge (January 2026)

In late January 2026, social-media reports alleged a military coup attempt in China, with claims that generals [[zhang-youxia|Zhang Youxia]], [[liu-zhenli|Liu Zhenli]] and [[zhong-shaojun|Zhong Shaojun]] had been arrested simultaneously along with Zhang's family, reportedly by [[wang-xiaohong|Wang Xiaohong]]'s special service bureau, in a sweep said to total seventeen officials.[5] Commentary circulating with the reports held that if Zhang Youxia had genuinely been arrested, it would mean Xi and the CCP's "red families" had completely broken off and reached the point of no return.[5]

The Wall Street Journal reported that Zhang Youxia had been accused of leaking classified intelligence on China's nuclear weapons technology to the United States.[5] Reporting cited alongside the story noted that since the summer of 2023 the party had unseated top officers across the army, air force, navy, strategic-missile force, paramilitary police and major theater commands, with more than fifty senior officers and defense-industry executives investigated or removed, leaving the [[central-military-commission|Central Military Commission]] with only one active uniformed officer.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed Xi as the "Good Twin" in China — the counterpart to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the United States — with the arrested military leaders and the Uniparty representing the opposing faction.[5] He distinguished the purged red-family aristocracy and military command from the Chinese technocrats, the group that brought China into the western banking system and was effectively overthrown when Xi became Secretary General in 2012, and speculated about links between that technocratic faction and Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]]'s China dealings.[5] Citing Financial Times reporting that the move consolidates Xi's power, he argued Xi was simply ahead of Trump in cracking down on internal subversion.[5]


## May 2026 Beijing summit with Trump

In May 2026 Xi was scheduled to host President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Beijing for a summit framed by US media as potentially "legacy-defining," with talks expected to focus on the [[iran|Iran]] conflict, sanctions, coordination on artificial intelligence, trade, and escalating US–China tensions over [[taiwan|Taiwan]] and military posture in the Pacific.[6] Analysts cited by Western media argued Beijing entered the meeting with increased leverage after months of instability tied to the Iran war and global energy disruptions, and Trump brought a delegation of American industrial and financial executives aiming to secure business deals and purchase agreements; see [[2026-trump-xi-beijing-summit|the 2026 Trump–Xi Beijing summit]].[6] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cast Xi's Pacific pressure campaign as one of three pillars of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], with Xi supplying the "economic rails" alongside Trump's and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s military-political leverage.[6]

## Beijing summit with Putin (May 2026)

In May 2026 Xi and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] signed a joint declaration in Beijing calling for a "[[multipolar-world|multipolar world]]" and criticizing what they described as destabilizing US foreign and nuclear policies.[7] The summit produced more than 40 agreements spanning trade, technology, energy, media cooperation, and strategic coordination, and framed Russia and China as defenders of a new international order less dominated by the United States and Western alliances.[7]

Both leaders condemned "hegemonism," warned against a return to "the law of the jungle" in global affairs, criticized US missile-defense initiatives including President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s proposed [[golden-dome|Golden Dome]] system, and objected to the expiration of key nuclear arms limitations between Washington and Moscow.[7] The meeting came days after Trump's own visit to Beijing, underscoring Xi's effort to position China as a central balancing power between major blocs even as it pursues stable relations with Washington.[7] The summit also exposed limits in the partnership: Putin reportedly failed to secure final Chinese approval for the [[power-of-siberia-2|Power of Siberia 2]] gas pipeline, with pricing and long-term dependency concerns unresolved.[7]

## February 2026 call with Trump

On February 4, 2026, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on Truth Social that he had completed "an excellent telephone conversation" with Xi, describing it as "long and thorough."[8] Per Trump's readout the call covered trade, military matters, an April presidential trip to China, Taiwan, the Russia–Ukraine war, the situation with [[iran|Iran]], Chinese purchases of U.S. oil and gas, additional agricultural purchases including a soybean count lifted to 20 million tons for the current season and a commitment of 25 million tons for the next, and airplane engine deliveries.[8] Trump said the bilateral and personal relationship was "extremely good" and predicted "many positive results achieved over the next three years of my Presidency"; no additional agreements or timelines were announced.[8]

## Biden "dictator" remark (June 2023)

In June 2023, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] publicly called Xi a dictator while recounting the February 2023 Chinese surveillance balloon shootdown, saying Xi had been embarrassed because he had not known the balloon was over the United States.[11] Beijing's Foreign Ministry responded that the remarks were "extremely absurd and irresponsible" and constituted "a blatant political provocation."[11] The exchange followed Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]]'s Beijing visit, during which Xi reportedly warned that Washington must choose between "cooperation and conflict."[11]

## November 2025 phone call with Trump

On Monday, November 24, 2025, Xi held a phone call with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that neither country had previously flagged, after which Trump described U.S.–China relations as "extremely strong."[12] The call came weeks after the two leaders met in South Korea, where they agreed on a framework for a trade deal that had yet to be finalized.[12] According to the official Xinhua news agency, Xi told Trump that "China and the United States once fought side by side against fascism and militarism, and should now work together to safeguard the outcomes of World War Two," and that "[[taiwan|Taiwan]]'s return to China is an integral part of the post-war international order."[12]

The exchange occurred while China was locked in its biggest diplomatic crisis in years with U.S. ally Japan, after Japanese Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]] said that any Chinese attack on democratically governed Taiwan could trigger a Japanese military response.[12] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] commented that while Trump "may be the rugmaster," he was not rugging anyone on this particular issue.[12]

## "Firm" ties with Russia after the 2023 San Francisco summit

On November 20, 2023, days after travelling to San Francisco to meet President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and executives of major U.S. technology companies, Xi hailed "firm" ties between China and Russia, saying positive relations with Moscow would "inject more stability into the world," according to China Central Television.[13] He affirmed that Beijing stood ready to work with Moscow "resolutely" on bilateral relations and on building a permanent friendship.[13] The San Francisco meeting had ended on a negative note after Biden, responding to a reporter's question at a wrap-up press conference, referred to Xi as a "dictator."[13] The same day, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] sent Xi a congratulatory letter marking the tenth meeting of the dialogue mechanism between the two countries' ruling parties, describing the comprehensive strategic partnership of coordination as being at its highest level in history and citing joint work through the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organisation]] and [[brics|BRICS]].[13]

## Fortress economy strategy

Xi has advanced an economic model, described as a [[fortress-economy|"fortress economy"]], designed to bolster national self-sufficiency and resilience against external shocks including geopolitical conflict and global pandemics.[14] A July 30, 2024 study by Jimmy Goodrich of the University of California Institute on Global Conflict and Cooperation analyzed official CCP speeches and policy documents and concluded that the strategy is meant to allow China to withstand "extreme situations" including protracted armed conflict.[14]

The pivot was driven by rising U.S.-China tensions, the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and the supply-chain disruptions of the COVID-19 pandemic, which together exposed the fragility of interconnected global trade.[14] Central to it is [[dual-circulation|"dual-economic circulation"]], reorienting the economy from export dependence toward a balanced model strengthening domestic industry while still engaging international trade, alongside food and energy security, supply-chain robustness, civil defense mobilization, and strategic reserve infrastructure.[14]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] situated the strategy within a broader thesis that the COVID lockdown era accelerated the emergence of a multipolar, sovereign world envisioned by nationalist leaders including [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Xi, and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], and argued that the U.S.-China "trade war" was in substance a mutual arrangement papering over an underlying transition away from globalism.[14]

At the close of his March 2023 state visit to Moscow, Xi told [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], "Change is coming that hasn't happened in 100 years and we are driving this change together," to which Putin replied, "I agree" — remarks that circulated widely as a summary of the two leaders' pledge to shape a new world order.[16] The visit was followed by Chinese advice to the United States on the war in Ukraine and by Beijing's endorsement of Russia's draft [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] resolution on the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream sabotage]] probe.[16] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the exchange within a broader picture of a strengthening ruble, a Western banking crisis cascade and European labor unrest, arguing that Presidents Trump, Putin and Xi were calling for "peace, reconstruction ... and restitution."[16]

## Accusation that the US sought to provoke a Taiwan invasion

In June 2024 the Financial Times reported that Xi had accused the United States of trying to trick China into invading [[taiwan|Taiwan]], while stating that his country would not take the bait.[18] The accusation was reportedly made in April 2023 during a meeting with European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] and represented the first known instance of Xi making the claim to a foreign leader, though he had issued the same warning to officials inside China.[18] The Chinese press statement issued at the time recorded Xi saying Taiwan was at the "core" of China's interests and that "if anyone expects China to compromise and concede on the Taiwan question, they are having a pipe dream and would shoot themselves in the foot," but omitted the accusation against the United States.[18]

Badlands commentary treated the story's release as deliberately timed for the day before [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s scheduled arrival in [[north-korea|North Korea]], and questioned why von der Leyen surfaced the year-old remark at that particular moment, reading it as an episode in the narrative contest between the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and the [[old-guard|Old Guard]].[18]

## APEC meeting with Biden (November 2023)

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was scheduled to meet Xi on Wednesday, November 15, 2023, in the San Francisco Bay Area on the sidelines of the Indo-Pacific forum, the [[asia-pacific-economic-cooperation|Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation]].[22] Senior administration officials said the agenda would cover the U.S. relationship with the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]], the importance of open lines of communication, managing the competition responsibly, and "a range of regional global and transnational issues."[22]

Officials cited new rules on outbound investment and updated export controls on semiconductors and semiconductor manufacturing equipment, along with actions taken against PRC entities involved in human rights abuses, forced labor, nonproliferation and supporting Russia's war in Ukraine, and continued freedom-of-navigation operations in the region.[22] One official said that "intense competition requires and demands intense diplomacy to manage tensions and to prevent competition from verging into conflict or confrontation," adding: "We know efforts to shape or reform China over several decades have failed, but we expect China to be around and to be a major player on the world stage for the rest of our lifetimes."[22]

## October 2023 Beijing meeting with Putin

On Tuesday, October 17, 2023, Xi welcomed Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to Beijing for a visit framed around the two leaders' shared vision of an international order no longer dominated by the United States and its democratic allies.[24] The meeting came as geopolitical fault lines hardened — first over Russia's invasion of Ukraine, launched three weeks after their previous Beijing meeting in 2022, and then over Israel's war against [[hamas|Hamas]], which threatened to spiral into a broader Middle East conflict.[24] Both Beijing and Moscow criticized Israel's actions and called for a ceasefire, positioning themselves as an alternative leadership to Washington, which affirmed Israel's right to retaliate.[24]

## 2025 SCO summit in Tianjin

Opening the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]] leaders' meeting in Tianjin on Monday, September 1, 2025, Xi called on members to continue resisting "hegemonism and power politics" in order to build a fairer international system.[25] In a keynote speech to leaders and representatives of member and partner nations, he invoked the "Shanghai spirit" of "mutual trust, mutual benefit, equality, consultation, respect for diverse civilizations, and pursuit of shared development."[25] Xi praised the bloc's progress since its founding in 2001 and outlined new priorities, saying members must "oppose the Cold War mentality, bloc confrontation, and bullying practices" — terms Beijing often uses to criticize US and Western policies.[25] He advocated "an equal and orderly multipolar world and a universally beneficial and inclusive economic globalization," and urged members to pursue win-win cooperation while respecting national differences, strengthening people-to-people exchanges and pushing for greater efficiency and evidence-based policymaking within the SCO.[25] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry said he agreed with Xi's remarks while stressing that China remains an economic rival, and criticized the [[state-department|State Department]] and the US political class for gratuitously antagonizing [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and the Sovereign Alliance.[25]

## BRICS emergency meeting on Gaza (November 2023)

Addressing an extraordinary virtual meeting of the [[brics|BRICS]] grouping on the Gaza crisis on November 21, 2023, Xi declared that all parties involved in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict should immediately ceasefire and suspend hostilities.[31] He called for safe passage for humanitarian relief, pressed for an end to the forced relocation of the Gaza Strip's civilian population, and emphasised that supplies of water, energy and electricity must be restored in the Palestinian enclave.[31] Xi argued that the only way out of the longstanding crisis was to implement the [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]], "restore the legitimate rights of the Palestinian nation," and establish a sovereign and independent State of Palestine, cautioning that without a fair settlement of the Palestinian question there could be no lasting peace and stability in West Asia.[31] Indian External Affairs Minister [[s-jaishankar|S. Jaishankar]] condemned the killing of civilians at the same meeting but stopped short of echoing Xi's ceasefire demand.[31]
