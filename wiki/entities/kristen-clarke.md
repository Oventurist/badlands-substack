# Kristen Clarke

Kristen Clarke is the head of the Civil Rights Division of the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] under the Biden administration, described by *The Federalist* as the "hyper-partisan" leader of the agency's civil rights arm.[1]

## Virginia voter roll lawsuit

In October 2024, Clarke issued the public statement accompanying the Justice Department's lawsuit seeking to block the Commonwealth of Virginia from removing noncitizens and other ineligible registrants from its voter rolls.[1] "The right to vote is the cornerstone of our democracy and the Justice Department will continue to ensure that the rights of qualified voters are protected," Clarke said.[1] The suit targeted election-integrity efforts undertaken by Republican Governor [[glenn-youngkin|Glenn Youngkin]] and the Virginia Department of Elections, and alleged violations of the "Quiet Period Provision" of the 1993 [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]].[1]

Badlands commentary focused on Clarke's use of the word "qualified" rather than "eligible," arguing that under the Department's theory a voter who reached the rolls before the 90-day quiet period began is treated as qualified regardless of actual eligibility.[1] The Badlands writer characterized this as the federal government suing a state "to keep ineligible voters on the rolls."[1]

*The Federalist* also referenced prior reporting alleging that Clarke had knifed her ex-partner and lied about it, presenting this as part of a broader critique of her fitness for the post.[1]
