# Archivist of the United States

The **Archivist of the United States** is the head and chief administrator of the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] (NARA), responsible for the supervision and direction of the National Archives.[1] The Archivist is appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate and is charged with safeguarding and making available for study all the permanently valuable records of the federal government, including the original Declaration of Independence, Constitution and Bill of Rights displayed in the Archives' main building in Washington, D.C.[1] The National Archives also carries extensive duties regarding the preservation of presidential papers and materials.[1]

In the Badlands Media treatment by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], the office is presented as far more consequential than its low public profile suggests: the Archivist's job is described as having two halves — safeguarding all federal and presidential documents, and "making available" those documents for public viewing in presidential libraries and museums or in response to court orders under the [[freedom-of-information-act|Freedom of Information Act]].[1] Because the Archivist is a presidential appointee who controls the release valve on presidential records, Lange argues the position has historically been vulnerable to capture by the very presidents whose records it governs.[1]

## Statutory duties and executive control

Under an executive order on presidential records cited in the article, the Archivist "shall abide by any instructions given him by the incumbent President or his designee unless otherwise directed by a final court order."[1] Lange highlights this clause as the mechanism by which a sitting president can override a former president's assertion of [[executive-privilege|executive privilege]], and by which a final court order can in turn override the incumbent.[1]

The 2014 amendment to the [[presidential-records-act|Presidential Records Act]] further requires the Archivist, upon determining to make publicly available any presidential record not previously released, to promptly provide written notice to the former President during whose term the record was created, to the incumbent President, and to the public, and to make the record available within 60 days unless a former or incumbent President notifies the Archivist of a claim of constitutionally-based privilege.[1] The same legislation transferred responsibility for federal records management from the Administrator of the General Services Administration to the Archivist, which Lange characterizes as a centralization of control over all government records in a single office — a decision he calls a strategic blunder by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1]

## Occupants discussed

Two Archivists feature centrally in the article: [[don-w-wilson|Don W. Wilson]], appointed December 4, 1987 near the end of the Reagan administration, who signed an agreement granting [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] "exclusive legal control of all Presidential information" and then left to run the Bush presidential library;[1] and [[david-ferriero|David Ferriero]], nominated by Obama in 2009, who also headed the new [[national-declassification-center|National Declassification Center]] and remained in office through the entire [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration until his retirement in April 2022.[1]
