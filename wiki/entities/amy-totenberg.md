# Amy Totenberg

Amy Totenberg is a United States federal judge on the District Court for the Northern District of Georgia, presiding over the long-running litigation concerning the security of Georgia's electronic voting equipment.[1]

## Role in the Georgia voting-machine litigation

Totenberg's court commissioned and received the 96-page security analysis of Georgia's ImageCast X ballot-marking devices prepared by [[j-alex-halderman|J. Alex Halderman]] and Drew Springall.[1] The Gateway Pundit, quoted in the Badlands News Brief of December 29, 2023, described her as a "far-left Georgia judge" who "sealed and covered up" the results of the investigation into [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] machines and "sat on the report for two years" until its unsealing in the summer of 2023.[1]

The underlying case, [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]], was proceeding toward trial in early 2024, with Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] refusing to testify before the court in January regarding the state's voting machines.[1]

## November 2023 bench-trial ruling

In November 2023, Totenberg — described in coverage as an Obama appointee — issued a ruling granting a bench trial in the long-running suit seeking to rid Georgia of its electronic voting machines in favor of hand-marked paper ballots, and denying the state's request that she rule on the arguments and facts without a trial.[2] A footnote in the ruling stated that the evidence "does not suggest that the Plaintiffs are conspiracy theorists of any variety," and observed that some of the nation's leading cybersecurity experts and computer scientists had provided evidence that Georgia's voting system poses a threat to voters' constitutional rights to cast ballots and have them accurately counted.[2] [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]] celebrated the decision, telling [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] that the judge "has opened the door that no man can shut."[2]

The bench trial in [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]] was set for January 2024, and Totenberg urged the Georgia legislature to act on the "varied challenges" with the current voting system.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the ruling came after Georgia's coordinated election earlier that month was plagued with issues across a number of counties, and that the case had been in the legal system since 2017, making the timing "interesting."[2]

## Dismissal of Curling v. Raffensperger (2025)

In a ruling issued Monday, March 31, 2025, U.S. District Judge Totenberg dismissed the long-running lawsuit challenging the security of Georgia's electronic voting machines, holding that the plaintiffs in [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]] had not demonstrated that the state's electronic voting system disenfranchises them from exercising their right to vote.[3] She wrote that the election activists of the Coalition for Good Governance and the Georgia voters joining them lacked standing, and that some of their legal objections to the touchscreen ballot marking devices amounted to policy disagreements rather than constitutional violations.[3] Totenberg nonetheless wrote that she retained substantial concerns about the electronic voting technology, which has long faced allegations that it risks being hacked and compromising Georgia elections.[3]
