# Cory Booker

Cory Booker is a Democratic United States Senator from New Jersey. In March 2026 he led a group of Democratic senators demanding federal scrutiny of the proposed merger between Paramount Skydance and [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]].[1]

## FCC letter on the Paramount–Warner merger

On March 23, 2026, Booker and colleagues including [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] and Elizabeth Warren sent a letter to the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] demanding a full and independent investigation into the $111 billion deal.[1] The senators raised national-security and foreign-influence concerns over the transaction's financing, which includes roughly $24 billion from Middle Eastern sovereign wealth funds — [[saudi-public-investment-fund|Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund]], the Qatar Investment Authority, and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority — plus $1 billion in equity from China's [[tencent|Tencent]].[1]

The letter argued that the investments, structured as non-voting equity and often routed through firms such as Apollo Global Management, appeared designed to sidestep mandatory review by the Committee on Foreign Investments in the United States (CFIUS).[1] Booker and his co-signers warned that such stakes could give foreign governments leverage over editorial decisions, content licensing, and strategic priorities at major US news outlets owned by the merged entity, including CBS News and CNN.[1] They specifically flagged risks tied to Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], linked to the 2018 Khashoggi murder, and Chinese national-security laws compelling technology firms to cooperate with Beijing's intelligence services.[1]

The senators urged the FCC to reject Paramount Skydance's claim that the foreign-investment portion requires only minimal scrutiny, calling instead for rigorous review under the Communications Act in coordination with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], CFIUS, and intelligence agencies.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright interpreted the Democratic senators' intervention as evidence of establishment panic over Middle Eastern capital entering "the final major theater of narrative control," arguing that the opposition signals a desire for permanent cultural and geopolitical fracture rather than normalization.[1]
