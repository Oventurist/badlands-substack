# Mike Mansfield

Mike Mansfield was the Senate Majority Leader who oversaw the creation and staffing of the 1975 [[church-committee|Church Committee]], the Senate select committee that investigated abuses by the United States intelligence community.[1]

## Role in shaping the committee

Whether out of a fear of public awareness or a general concern over how the investigation's findings would be received, Mansfield cautioned the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] "against letting the affair become a television extravaganza."[1] Working with Republican Leader [[hugh-scott|Hugh Scott]], he carefully selected the committee's members, balancing experienced lawmakers with junior members and ensuring that the membership represented a variety of political viewpoints.[1]

When Senator Philip Hart declined to lead the committee for health-related reasons, Mansfield selected Democrat [[frank-church|Frank Church]] of Idaho as chairman — a choice justified by Church's 16 years on the Committee on Foreign Relations and his prior co-chairmanship of a special committee that critically examined the executive branch's consolidation of power during the Cold War.[1]
