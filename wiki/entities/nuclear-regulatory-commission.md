# Nuclear Regulatory Commission

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) is the independent United States agency responsible for licensing and regulating civilian nuclear materials, including the export of uranium. In the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]] the NRC is presented as an agency that gave [[congress|Congress]] and the public false assurances about the [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] transaction.

Following approval of the sale of Uranium One's American assets to ARMZ, a subsidiary of Russia's [[rosatom|Rosatom]], the NRC declared in a November 2010 press release that "No uranium produced at either facility may be exported." A year later, then-Chairman Gregory Jaczko repeated the assurance in a letter to Senator John Barrasso, the Wyoming Republican in whose state Uranium One operated mines, writing that "Neither Uranium One Inc. nor AMRZ holds a specific NRC export license. In order to export uranium from the United States, Uranium One Inc. or ARMZ would need to apply for and obtain a specific NRC license authorizing the exports of uranium for use in reactor fuel."

Reporting by The Hill, quoted at length in the article, found that NRC memos nevertheless showed the commission had approved shipments of yellowcake uranium from the Russian-owned mines to Canada in 2012 "through a third party," with the Obama administration later approving onward movement of some material to Europe. NRC officials told The Hill that exports flowed from Wyoming to Canada and on to Europe between 2012 and 2014 and that "the approval involved a process with multiple agencies."

The mechanism, according to the same reporting, was designed to avoid scrutiny: rather than granting Rosatom a direct export license, "which would have raised red flags inside a Congress already suspicious of the deal," the NRC in 2012 authorised an amendment to an existing export license held by the Paducah, Kentucky trucking company [[rsb-logistics-services|RSB Logistics Services Inc.]], simply adding Uranium One to the list of clients whose uranium it could move to Canada. The amended license, dated March 16, 2012, raised the volume of uranium ore concentrate RSB could ship to the Cameco Corp. plant in Ontario from 7,500,000 to 12,000,000 kilograms. The Hill concluded that "the move escaped notice in Congress," a point the Badlands author treats as deliberate concealment by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].
