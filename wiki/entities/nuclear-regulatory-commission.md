# Nuclear Regulatory Commission

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) is the United States agency that, in the article's account, publicly assured Congress that American uranium could not leave the country while quietly authorizing the paperwork that let it happen.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

After the Obama administration approved the sale of a Canadian mining company with significant U.S. uranium reserves to a firm owned by Russia's government, the NRC assured Congress and the public that the new owners could not export any raw nuclear fuel from America's shores.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) "No uranium produced at either facility may be exported," the commission declared in a November 2010 press release announcing that ARMZ, a subsidiary of the Russian state-owned [[rosatom|Rosatom]], had been approved to take ownership of [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] and its American assets.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) A year later, then-Chairman Gregory Jaczko repeated the assurance in a letter to Senator John Barrasso, a Wyoming Republican in whose state Uranium One operated mines, writing that neither Uranium One nor ARMZ held a specific NRC export license and that one would be required to export uranium for use in reactor fuel.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

NRC memos reviewed by The Hill showed otherwise.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The commission did approve the shipment of yellowcake uranium — the raw material used to make nuclear fuel and weapons — from the Russian-owned U.S. mines to Canada in 2012 through a third party, and the Obama administration later approved some of that uranium going all the way to Europe.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) NRC officials told The Hill that exports flowed from Wyoming to Canada and on to Europe between 2012 and 2014, and that the approval involved a process with multiple agencies.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The mechanism, according to The Hill, avoided visibility: rather than give Rosatom a direct export license, which would have raised red flags inside a Congress already suspicious of the deal, the NRC in 2012 authorized an amendment to an existing export license held by a Paducah, Kentucky trucking firm called RSB Logistics Services Inc., simply adding Uranium One to the list of clients whose uranium it could move to Canada.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The license, dated March 16, 2012, increased the volume RSB Logistics could ship to the Cameco Corp. plant in Ontario from 7,500,000 to 12,000,000 kilograms and added Uranium One to the "other parties to Export."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The move escaped notice in [[congress|Congress]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The article treats this as deliberate concealment by the Obama administration and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], noting that [[barack-obama|Obama]] had assured Americans none of the uranium would ever leave the country.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
