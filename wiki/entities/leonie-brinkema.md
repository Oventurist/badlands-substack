# Leonie Brinkema

Leonie Brinkema is a United States District Judge whose April 2025 ruling in the government's advertising-technology antitrust case against [[google|Google]] marked a significant legal setback for the company.[1]

## 2025 advertising technology ruling

On Thursday, April 17, 2025, Brinkema found that the Alphabet Inc. unit had illegally monopolized certain online advertising technology markets, violating antitrust law in the markets for advertising exchanges and for the ad server tools that websites use to sell ad space.[1] She declined, however, to find that the company met the definition of a monopolist in a third market — the tools advertisers use to buy display ads.[1] Alphabet shares fell as much as 3.2% on the ruling before paring losses, trading down 1.1% at $153.78 in New York early that afternoon.[1]

The decision was the second time within a year that Google had been found by a court to be an illegal monopolist, following Judge Amit Mehta's August 2024 ruling that the company had unlawfully secured default search engine status on devices.[1] A trial on remedies in the search case was set to begin the following Monday in Washington, with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] seeking to force Alphabet to divest its Chrome browser.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator Ashe in America criticized both rulings as punishing market leadership rather than protecting consumers, arguing that Brinkema had sided with the government against an ad-tech business built through innovation and strategic acquisitions such as DoubleClick, and warning that proposed structural remedies including the separation of Chrome and Android would set a dangerous precedent.[1]
