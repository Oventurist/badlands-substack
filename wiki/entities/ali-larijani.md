# Ali Larijani

Ali Larijani is a senior Iranian official and long-standing figure in the Islamic Republic's political establishment.[1]

During the March 2026 war between [[iran|Iran]] and the United States and [[israel|Israel]], Larijani said the war would not end before Iran's enemies were made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."[1] The statement came as the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] announced strikes on dozens of targets in Tehran and as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] warned that Israel could target Iran's Supreme Leader if the conflict escalated further.[1]

## Reported death

Later in March 2026, Iranian state media confirmed the death of Larijani, then Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council and one of Tehran's highest-ranking officials, who was reported killed along with his son, a deputy and bodyguards in U.S.-Israeli airstrikes at his daughter's home.[2] Israel separately announced the elimination of [[gholamreza-soleimani|Gholamreza Soleimani]], longtime commander of the Basij militia, in the same wave of strikes.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the development notable mostly because Larijani was the Iranian official responsible for implementing a form of [[devolution|Devolution]] — continuity of government — in August 2025 after the 12-Day War.[2] Because of that COG protocol, he argued, a successful "decapitation strike" would be unlikely since authority had already been devolved to lower-level commanders.[2]

Iran was not quick to affirm the IDF's claim that Larijani had been killed, and according to many reports initially denied it; after some time, somebody took control of Larijani's X account and posted about the matter.[2] Badlands treated the evidence as short of definitive, raising the possibility that Iran might have reason to misstate his status in order to protect him by moving him into hiding.[2]

## Aftermath

In retaliation for the strikes, Iran escalated into what it called a "new phase of oil war," launching drone swarms and attacks on energy infrastructure including the Shah oil and gas field in the UAE, Iraq's Majnoon field, and targets in Saudi Arabia, while continuing to assert control over the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and warning that the waterway's security situation had permanently changed.[2]

## March 2026 statement on the war

Amid Israeli strikes on Tehran in March 2026, Larijani said the war would not end before [[iran|Iran]]'s enemies were made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."[3] The remark came as the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] reported striking dozens of sites in the Iranian capital in what it described as a campaign to deepen the blow against the Iranian regime.[3]

## Continuity of Government claim

In March 2026 Badlands commentary, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that when Larijani of the National Supreme Security Council implemented a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] plan the previous August, the office of supreme leader was removed from the Iranian chain of command, rendering the ayatollah an irrelevant figurehead.[4] The claim was offered to explain why [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]]'s prolonged public absence and the [[cardboard-ayatollah|"Cardboard Ayatollah"]] meme mattered less to Iran's actual decision-making than media coverage implied.[4]

## Assassination and succession

By March 2026 Larijani had been assassinated, and Tehran appointed former IRGC commander [[mohammad-bagher-zolghadr|Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr]] as its new national security chief in his place during a wartime leadership reshuffle.[5] The change came in the fourth week of a heavy exchange of fire between Iran and Israel and amid a US-backed one-month ceasefire proposal delivered through Pakistani intermediaries.[5]

## March 2026 Hormuz safe-passage messaging

In coverage dated March 11, 2026, Larijani was again described as Secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and as the originator of the message — posted to X in multiple languages, originally in Russian — that preceded Tehran's offer of safe passage through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] to Arab and European states willing to expel American and Israeli ambassadors.[6] He warned that security and stability in the strait were unlikely to be achieved while the war ignited by the United States and Israel continued, a remark made in reply to French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s suggestion that France might send warships to the waterway.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Larijani as the official responsible for implementing Iranian "Devolution" in August 2025 following the [[twelve-day-war|12 Day War]], a decentralization of political and military power in which the entire [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] was restructured over infiltration concerns and the Ayatollah was removed from the chain of command to forestall a Hezbollah-style decapitation attempt.[6] One consequence, the commentary noted, is that no central authority now controls the Iranian military, raising doubts about how readily Tehran could halt the conflict if individual missile commanders chose to continue.[6]

Larijani also claimed publicly that US reporting of "only 5 or 6" American military casualties was false and that the true figure would rise sharply, adding that US soldiers had been captured; a subsequent [[white-house|White House]] press briefing by [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] put the American toll at roughly 150, which Badlands read as evidence that Larijani had accurate insight into the battlefield.[6]
