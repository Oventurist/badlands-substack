# Federal Security Service (FSB)

The **Federal Security Service (FSB)** is Russia's principal domestic security and counterintelligence agency, directed by [[aleksandr-bortnikov|Aleksandr Bortnikov]].[1]

The agency's handling of foreign warnings drew scrutiny after the March 2024 [[crocus-city-hall-attack|Crocus City Hall attack]] near Moscow, in which more than 140 people were killed.[1] The United States had passed a private warning to Russia in addition to a March 7 public advisory issued by the [[us-embassy-in-moscow|US Embassy in Moscow]]; Bortnikov described the private warning as general in nature.[1] Sources cited by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] said Russia tightened security after the warning but may have relaxed it once no attack occurred inside the 48-hour window flagged by the Americans.[1]

## Alleged Ukrainian child-abduction operation (October 2024)

On Thursday, October 3, 2024, the FSB reported that it had caught agents of Ukraine's [[main-directorate-of-intelligence|military intelligence]] (HUR) illegally crossing the border from Lithuania into Russia's Kaliningrad exclave, and claimed that an HUR officer had tried to use his agency's resources to kidnap his own son from Russia.[2] "A secondary goal of the operation was the kidnapping and cross-border smuggling of an underage Russian citizen, who is the son of an HUR staff member," the FSB statement said.[2] Footage released by the service showed the arrest of a car driver and a child who appeared to be in his early teens being removed from the vehicle, with those involved confirming on camera that they had been tasked with a kidnapping on Russian soil.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the episode as "the perfect embodiment of the modern Deep State" and an official act of war.[2]

The FSB is also the parent service of the "Callisto Group," the cyber unit named in an October 2024 U.S. [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] warrant over a spear-phishing campaign against American government targets.[2]
