# Department of Commerce

The United States Department of Commerce occupies the center of the Badlands account of the [[bill-clinton|Clinton]] administration's alleged sale of American secrets, because it was the department headed by [[ron-brown|Ron Brown]] from 1993 until his death in April 1996.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

Two functions of the department are at issue. The first is its trade missions: [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] litigation established that seats aboard Commerce Department trade missions were sold to raise funds for the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] and the 1996 Clinton/Gore campaign, a scheme the article says Bill and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] knew of and approved.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) At the time of his death Brown was under subpoena, in Judicial Watch's suit against the department, to produce documents on those seat sales; the documents were withheld in violation of both the subpoena and a FOIA request.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

The second and, in the article's judgment, graver function is export approval. Transfers of American technology approved and overseen by Brown's Commerce Department are alleged to have breached national security.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1) Bernard Schwartz of [[loral-corporation|Loral Corporation]], a major DNC donor, accompanied Brown on a 1994 trade mission to [[communist-china|China]], where Brown arranged his meeting with a Chinese government official; that meeting led to the missile-technology transfer later investigated by [[congress|Congress]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-1)

## USA Rare Earth letter of intent (2026)

In January 2026 the Department of Commerce issued a non-binding letter of intent to back a $1.6 billion funding package for [[usa-rare-earth|USA Rare Earth]], a rare-earth mining and processing company, comprising $277 million in proposed federal funding and a $1.3 billion senior secured loan under the [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS and Science Act]].[2] Under the arrangement the U.S. government is expected to acquire roughly a 10% equity stake through 16.1 million shares and about 17.6 million warrants.[2]

The package forms part of a broader effort to strengthen domestic rare-earth production and reduce American reliance on [[china|China]] for critical infrastructure inputs, with Badlands framing the department's role as incentivizing domestic processing capacity rather than extraction alone.[2]

## Huawei export licences

The department administers the export licences that govern American technology sales to [[huawei|Huawei]].[3] Under the [[donald-trump|Trump administration]]'s severe restrictions it nonetheless granted licences to some companies for products unrelated to high-speed 5G telecom networks.[3] In late January 2023 the Financial Times reported that the department had notified some companies it would no longer grant such licences, a step toward a total ban on sales of American technology to the Chinese telecom equipment maker, which national security officials believe assists Chinese espionage.[3]
