# Jill Lepore

**Jill Lepore** is an American historian, a chaired professor at Harvard University, and one of the nation's most widely read writers on American history.[1]

## "How Originalism Has Killed the Constitution"

Lepore drew a sustained response from constitutional scholars after publishing an essay in The Atlantic titled "How Originalism Has Killed the Constitution."[1] Writing in Law & Liberty, critics characterized her piece as a broadside distinct in kind from the more rigorous historical scholarship that has challenged the originalist project, arguing that her essay "distorts quotations out of context, misstates facts, and confuses concepts."[1]

The central thesis attributed to Lepore — that [[originalism|originalism]] thwarts constitutional change and kills the amendment process — was said by her critics to get matters "precisely backward," on the grounds that originalism preserves the Constitution and its [[article-v|Article V]] amendment process as the lawful mechanism for change in a constitutional republic.[1] Legal scholar Robert Natelson and historian Paul Moreno both published defenses of originalism against what one writer called Lepore's "constitutional despair."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media's coverage of the debate focused less on Lepore herself than on the practical fights over Article V, including state-legislature campaigns to certify a balanced budget amendment and congressional term limits.[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that term limits are "non-solutions that sound like good solutions to people who don't understand the problems," comparing the issue to debates over the [[smith-mundt-act|Smith-Mundt Act]].[1]
