# Klaus Schwab

**Klaus Schwab** is named in "AI Can Help Us Expose the NWO" as the author of the [[great-reset|Great Reset]], which [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] instructed ChatGPT to incorporate as an additional plan for the fictional [[global-harmony-consortium|Global Harmony Consortium]] to utilise.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

The Great Reset enters the article's warfare map alongside the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]], the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and the [[esg|ESG]] system, all of which Deschamps asked the model to place within the strategic, operational and tactical layers of the Consortium's campaign toward a [[new-world-order|New World Order]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/ai-can-help-us-expose-the-nwo)

## Remarks on AI, chatbots and digital identities (February 2023)

In February 2023 Schwab discussed artificial intelligence, chatbots and digital identities and declared that "who masters those technologies — in some way — will be the master of the world," a clip circulated by Disclose.tv on February 14 and reported by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]].[2] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], treated the statement as "open, naked disclosure" that the technologies of the [[fourth-industrial-revolution|Fourth Industrial Revolution]] are servants of masters seeking total control, and argued that the WEF model of expert-led governance — dumbing down the population, making life hard, and supplying media-backed experts — is the technocratic dream of 1960s and 1970s globalists now to be scaled by AI presented as an incorruptible "uber expert."[2]

## Davos 2023

Schwab opened the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s January 2023 annual meeting with a call for leaders to "master the future."[3] Remarks that week by FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] endorsing deeper collaboration between government and technology companies were described as being in line with Schwab's ideas on public-private partnership.[3]

## Named among the "Four Horsemen of the Climate Cult"

Badlands commentary in February 2025, discussing the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]]'s withdrawal from the [[network-of-central-banks-and-supervisors-for-greening-the-financial-system|NGFS]], cited commentator [[warhamster|WarHamster]] naming Schwab as one of the "Four Horsemen of the Climate Cult" alongside [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] and [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]].[4] The piece framed the climate agenda as a descendant of [[thomas-malthus|Thomas Malthus]]'s population theory and as an instrument of artificial scarcity.[4]


## Resignation as WEF Chair (April 2025)

Schwab resigned as chair of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]]'s board of trustees, the organization announced on Monday, April 21, 2025.[5] In a statement he said that "following my recent announcement, and as I enter my 88th year, I have decided to step down from the position of Chair and as a member of the Board of Trustees, with immediate effect"; the board accepted the resignation at an extraordinary meeting held on April 20, and Vice Chairman Peter Brabeck-Letmathe became interim chairman.[5] Badlands commentary noted the departure had first been announced on May 21, 2024, with CNBC reporting the leadership change would be completed before the January 2025 Davos gathering, so the exit took several months longer than expected; the commentary observed that Schwab had led the WEF for 50 years and argued the body should be dissolved rather than given a new leader.[5]

## "God complex" succession reporting (January 2023)

Breitbart reported on January 17, 2023 that current and former staffers claimed Schwab possessed a "god complex" and intended to run the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] until his death, in the manner of a pope or monarch.[6] Individuals involved with the organisation were said to be discussing its post-Schwab future as the annual conference opened in Davos that week, with Schwab due to turn 85 in March 2023 prompting calls for clarity on succession.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that Schwab, "groomed by the likes of Herman Kahn and [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]]," could be seen as the living incarnation of globalism, and that his infatuation with [[transhumanism|transhumanism]] might be rooted in a desire to live forever.[6] DeLarme judged that Schwab would eventually pass the torch "whether he wants to or not," naming European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde or a member of Schwab's own family as potential successors, while concluding that business in Davos would continue as usual regardless.[6]

## May 2024: stepping back from executive management

On Tuesday, 21 May 2024, the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] confirmed that its founder Klaus Schwab was moving away from day-to-day management of the organization after more than 50 years at the helm.[7] The Forum described the change as part of a multiyear strategy shifting governance to a president and managing board — [[borge-brende|Børge Brende]] was then president — and said Schwab would transition to chairman of the board of trustees, with the board organized around four strategic committees.[7] No successor was named to become the face of the Forum's annual Davos meeting.[7]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Schwab had run the WEF since 1971 and grown it into 'the pinnacle of the Global World Order,' and called the timing of his departure notable.[7] Her questions were 'why now' questions: why step down when the vision was said to be near completion, whether the choice was his, how the timing affected key programs, and who was actually calling the shots at the WEF.[7]

## 2025 resignation and WEF investigation

In April 2025 the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] confirmed a Wall Street Journal report that its board of trustees had unanimously supported the Audit and Risk Committee's decision to open an [[2025-wef-investigation-into-klaus-schwab|independent investigation]] following a whistleblower letter containing allegations against Schwab as former chairman.[8] Schwab, then 87, said there was "not a shadow of proof" for the accusations and told the Swiss newspaper Blick that he had initiated defamation proceedings.[8] He had resigned suddenly the preceding Monday, "with immediate effect," with the WEF board praising his "outstanding achievements" over 55 years in charge.[8] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] compared the affair to the tax-evasion prosecution of Al Capone, arguing that "sometimes the accounting is the only route to justice" and reading the alleged financial crimes as signal.[8]
