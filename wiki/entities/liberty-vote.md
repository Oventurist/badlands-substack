# Liberty Vote

**Liberty Vote** is the American voting technology company created in October 2025 when former Republican election official [[scott-leiendecker|Scott Leiendecker]] acquired [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] and rebranded it.[1] The purchase transferred control of one of the most litigated names in recent American election administration to new ownership, and with it the company's outstanding defamation litigation.[1]

## Resolution of the Lindell litigation

After acquiring Dominion, Leiendecker chose not to pursue further recovery from [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]], the MyPillow chief executive Dominion had sued for $1.3 billion in February 2021 over claims that the company rigged the 2020 presidential election.[1] Liberty Vote Holdings, [[mypillow|My Pillow, Inc.]], a Dominion subsidiary, and Lindell filed a joint stipulation in federal court in Washington, D.C., agreeing to dismiss all claims and causes of action with prejudice, meaning they cannot be refiled.[1] Each party agreed to bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, and [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] was not a party to the dismissal agreement.[1]

## Remaining litigation

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that, as far as could be determined, the only remaining Dominion — now Liberty Vote — defamation action still outstanding was the case against [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]].[1] Whether that case is dropped or litigated was treated as a potentially significant test of whether the remaining [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election-fraud litigation]] would produce disclosure or simply be settled away.[1]

## Initial reaction to the acquisition (October 2025)

In the days immediately after the purchase was announced, the picture was considerably murkier. Reporting indicated that, before completing the acquisition, Liberty Vote had required Dominion to settle many of its lawsuits against allies of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — but that [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]] and [[mypillow|MyPillow]] were not among the settlements.[2] Lindell reacted angrily in an interview with [[alex-jones|Alex Jones]], saying the company had told Dominion "they're going to drop every lawsuit that they have" and then "didn't drop MyPillow and Mike Lindell! They're lying!"[2] He vowed never to settle regardless of any offer.[2]

## Corporate structure questions

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the arrangement as Dominion "doing business as" Liberty Vote from a legal-entity standpoint, so that Dominion remains its own entity under Leiendecker ownership.[2] A chart of the deal circulated by Pat Colbeck on X prompted the question of whether [[knowink|KNOWiNK]] — a separate business under the same owner — belonged on it; the assessment was that KNOWiNK would appear on a Leiendecker family tree but not on a Dominion-specific chart, unless KNOWiNK also began doing business as Liberty Vote.[2]

Attendees of a Colorado county clerks' meeting with the Liberty Vote CEO — a long-time Dominion regional representative — reportedly heard that the biggest, and possibly only, reason for the change was branding and reputation management to combat the industry's crisis of public trust, summarized as "Same team, same service, different name."[2] Open questions raised included whether Dominion and KNOWiNK relationship managers would be merged, whether a reduction in force would follow, how client portfolios overlap, and whether Liberty Vote would ever become more than a branded holding company.[2] The provisional conclusion was that the practical level of change to elections resulting from the ownership change appeared low, pending verifiable information on the roadmap for legal structure, governance, management, and technical architecture.[2]

## Outreach to county clerks and equipment decommissioning

Following the acquisition, Liberty Vote's chief executive began engaging county election officials directly.[3] The El Paso County Clerk in Colorado told constituents in October 2025 that Liberty Vote was working with the [[election-assistance-commission|Election Assistance Commission]] to decommission all old Dominion equipment so that it could not be voted on, information he said he learned on a conference call of large-county clerks with the Liberty Vote CEO, with a further call expected for clerks from all counties.[3] The clerk added that Idaho Labs was under consideration as an independent third-party election auditor unaffiliated with any state or federal government, and that all manufacturing and all source code were returning to the United States.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that Colorado officials including [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] and [[matt-crane|Matt Crane]] were said to have been blindsided by the sale, which she read as evidence that internal changes had already occurred.[3] See [[liberty-vote-acquisition-of-dominion-voting-systems|Liberty Vote acquisition of Dominion Voting Systems]].[3]
