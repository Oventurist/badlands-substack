# Drew Springall

Drew Springall is a computer security researcher and professor at Auburn University who co-authored, with University of Michigan computer science professor [[j-alex-halderman|Dr. J. Alex Halderman]], the court-commissioned security analysis of Georgia's [[dominion-imagecast-x|Dominion ImageCast X]] ballot-marking devices.[1]

## Role in the Georgia examination

Springall and Halderman were granted access to a Georgia [[ballot-marking-device|ballot marking device]] in September 2020, prior to that year's presidential election, in connection with the [[curling-v-raffensperger|Curling v. Raffensperger]] litigation.[1] Their examination concluded that vulnerabilities existed in "nearly every part of the system that is exposed to potential attackers."[1] The most critical of these was an arbitrary-code-execution flaw that could be used to spread malware from a county's central election management system to every ballot marking device in a jurisdiction, without physical access to any of the individual machines.[1]

The resulting 96-page report was filed under seal in the case and was not made public until June 2023, nearly two years after its submission, following its sealing by Judge [[amy-totenberg|Amy Totenberg]].[1]
