# Constitutional Court of Romania

The Constitutional Court of Romania is the country's highest court of constitutional review and the body that adjudicates disputes arising from national elections. It became a focus of international controversy for its interventions in [[romania|Romania]]'s 2024–2025 presidential contest.[1]

## Annulment of the November 2024 election

The court intervened in the November 2024 presidential election after populist candidate [[calin-georgescu|Călin Georgescu]] secured a surprise victory in the first round of voting.[1] Amid allegations that Russia had interfered to help his campaign, the court annulled the election to prevent Georgescu from advancing to the second round.[1]

## March 2025 ruling on Georgescu's candidacy

In a final and binding decision issued in March 2025, the Constitutional Court unanimously rejected eleven appeals lodged by Georgescu against the Central Electoral Bureau's (BEC) ruling barring his candidacy in the May elections.[1] The BEC had grounded its ban on claims that Georgescu failed to "respect the rules of the electoral procedure" and "violated the obligation to defend democracy."[1] Georgescu's appeal argued that the BEC decision of the preceding Sunday contained "procedural irregularities" and that the body had outsourced its competency to the Constitutional Court, according to Romanian broadcaster Digi24.[1] Supporters characterised the ruling as an affront to the democratic process.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the sequence as the whole of the Romanian government deciding that the people do not get to choose their leaders, banning Georgescu's bid ostensibly for his dangerous populism and "Romania First" ideals.[1]
