# Gun Violence Archive

The Gun Violence Archive is a data-collection organization whose tallies of shooting incidents are widely cited by American news outlets. Its counting methodology became a subject of dispute in March 2023 when [[cnn|CNN]] used Archive data to report that "the U.S. has surpassed 100 mass shootings in 2023," noting that the country had reached that number by the first week of March.[1]

## Methodology dispute

Critics argued that both CNN and the Gun Violence Archive departed from the standard definition of a mass shooting. The Rand Corporation has observed that the conventional definition derives from the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 1980s definition of a "mass murderer" as an individual who "kills four or more people in a single incident (not including himself)."[1] Under the Archive's approach, by contrast, an incident with no fatalities can still be classified as a mass shooting, so that drive-by shootings, gang hits, homicides and double homicides are recategorized and counted as mass shootings — the practice that allowed the figure of more than 100 mass shootings by early March 2023 to be reported.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized the CNN report as part of a longer pattern in which groups opposed to the [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] manipulate crime statistics, singling out the practice of folding suicides and accidents in with homicides, which some critics claim has more than tripled the headline numbers.[1]
