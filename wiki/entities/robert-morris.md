# Robert Morris

Robert Morris, known as "the Financier," was the Superintendent of Finance of the United States during the Confederation period and the driving force behind the creation of the [[bank-of-north-america|Bank of North America]] in 1782.[1] In Badlands Media's account of American central banking, Morris occupies the position of the first American statesman to successfully institutionalize a privately subscribed national bank on the model of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]].[1]

## The Bank of North America

The Articles of Confederation had extended to Congress the sovereign power to generate bills of credit, and later in 1782 Congress passed an ordinance incorporating a privately subscribed national bank, following the pattern of the Bank of England.[1] The institution was nonetheless thwarted in fulfilling its intended role as a genuine national bank because of objections raised over "alarming foreign influence" and "fictitious credit."[1]

## Legacy

Morris's project did not die with the Bank of North America. In 1791 a former Morris aide, [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]], then Secretary of the Treasury, accepted a compromise with Southern legislators to ensure the continuation of Morris's bank project from a decade earlier, resulting in the chartering of the [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|First Bank of the United States]].[1] Badlands Media frames this continuity as evidence that the push for a national bank was a sustained elite project rather than an improvised response to circumstance.[1]
