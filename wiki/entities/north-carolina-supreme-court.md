# North Carolina Supreme Court

The North Carolina Supreme Court is the highest court in the state of North Carolina. Its partisan composition shifted sharply after the November 2022 elections, when voters replaced a Democratic majority with a 5–2 Republican majority.[1]

## 2023 rehearing of voting cases

On February 3, 2023, the new Republican majority took the unusual step of agreeing to rehear two recently decided cases dealing with partisan gerrymandering and voting rights, signaling that it was open to overturning them outright.[1] The two decisions — one holding that a state [[north-carolina-voter-id-law|voter ID law]] discriminated against voters of color, and one reaffirming the court's earlier decision to redraw the state's election maps — had been handed down only about a month and a half earlier by the outgoing Democratic majority.[1]

One of the cases, Harper v. Hall, held that North Carolina Republicans had drawn maps producing a partisan advantage in the state's congressional delegation and in the state House.[1] Rehearing is rare at the state supreme court level in North Carolina: a petition for rehearing had been granted only twice in the previous thirty years.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-media|Badlands]] news brief, [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] argued that the episode demonstrated that "voting matters," contending that the outgoing Democratic justices had "legislated from the bench" against the wishes of state residents by striking down voter ID and finding unlawful partisan gerrymandering, and that both findings were inaccurate.[1] The commentary framed the likely reversal of the two rulings as a direct consequence of North Carolinians turning out in November 2022 to vote out justices the author described as biased if not corrupt.[1]
