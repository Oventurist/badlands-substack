# Joost Meerloo

**Joost Meerloo** was a psychoanalyst whose book *Rape of the Mind* supplied the central framework for Badlands Media's analysis of psychological warfare against populations.[1]

## Rape of the Mind

Meerloo argued that the manipulation of populations "is simply a question of reorganizing and manipulating collective feelings in a particular way," and gave the name [[menticide]] — "a killing of the mind" — to the general method by which this is accomplished.[1] He characterized menticide as "an old crime against the human mind and spirit but systematized anew," an "organized system of psychological intervention and judicial perversion, through which a ruling class can imprint their opportunistic thoughts upon the minds they plan to use."[1]

## Techniques described

Meerloo described "waves of terror," in which periods of induced fear alternate with periods of calm so that each successive wave lands on "a public already softened up" and morale steadily declines.[1] He warned that modern technology conditions people to accept the world they are shown without reflection, overloading the senses so that "the screen provides all the answers."[1] Drawing on [[ivan-pavlov|Ivan Pavlov]]'s research, he observed that conditioned reflexes form most readily in isolation and low-stimulus environments, and that totalitarian regimes exploit this by isolating their political victims.[1] Once propaganda has reduced rational minds to "playhouses of irrational forces," rulers can offer a return to normalcy in exchange for the population's surrender of self-reliance and individual responsibility.[1]

## Prescribed remedy

Meerloo also advised confronting demagogues with ridicule, writing that "we must learn to treat the demagogue and aspirant dictators in our midst with the weapon of ridicule," because the demagogue is himself almost wholly incapable of humor and "if we treat it with humor, it will most assuredly collapse."[1]
