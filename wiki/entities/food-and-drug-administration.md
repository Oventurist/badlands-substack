# Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) is the federal regulator of drugs, medical devices and diagnostics.[1]

In Badlands coverage of the [[las-vegas-biolab|Las Vegas biolab]] affair, the FDA appears as the agency to which Chinese national [[jia-bei-zhu|Jia Bei Zhu]] made a false statement — one of the counts on which he was convicted, alongside conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud, and distributing adulterated and misbranded medical devices through [[universal-meditech|Universal Meditech Inc.]].[1] The conduct involved fraudulently selling more than a million faulty COVID-19 tests imported from China for nearly $4 million between 2020 and 2023.[1]

## 2026 suppression of vaccine safety studies

The FDA blocked the publication of several studies that supported the safety of widely used COVID-19 and shingles vaccines, withdrawing the research before publication, according to reporting first published by The New York Times.[2] The studies concerned COVID-19 vaccines from Pfizer and Moderna and GSK's Shingrix; HHS spokesman Andrew Nixon said they were withdrawn because the authors drew broad conclusions unsupported by the underlying data and that the agency acted "to protect the integrity of its scientific process."[2] The action came amid broader policy shifts under Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and is detailed in [[fda-blocked-vaccine-safety-studies|FDA Blocked Vaccine Safety Studies]].[2]

## September 2023 COVID-19 vaccine authorization

On September 11, 2023 the FDA authorized updated [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] from [[moderna|Moderna]] and [[pfizer|Pfizer]] in a bid to counter the poor effectiveness of the existing shots, approving them for people aged 12 and older and granting emergency authorization down to six months of age.[3] The shots targeted the XBB.1.5 Omicron subvariant, which the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] reported had already been largely displaced by newer strains such as EG.5; no mention was made of [[novavax|Novavax]]'s U.S.-available vaccine.[3] Agency official [[peter-marks|Peter Marks]] said vaccination "remains critical to public health" and encouraged eligible people to get vaccinated.[3] Critics at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued the clearance was issued without clinical trial data and predicted renewed mandates.[3] See [[2023-fda-authorization-of-updated-covid-19-vaccines]].[3]


## 2025 crackdown on pharmaceutical advertising

In September 2025 the FDA became the enforcement arm of a presidential memorandum, signed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Tuesday, September 9, ordering the health department to crack down on direct-to-consumer pharmaceutical advertising.[4] The push aligned with prior pledges by Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and aimed to increase scrutiny of social media companies and influencers who promote medicines without proper disclosures.[4] The agency issued warning letters to drugmakers reminding them that direct-to-consumer ads must comply with existing regulations, which bar misleading impressions of a drug and require a fair balance of risk and benefit information.[4] A senior administration official said on a press call that roughly 100 cease-and-desist enforcement letters and thousands of warning letters would be sent, notifying companies that the rules already on the books would be enforced.[4]

## Synthetic food dye phase-out, 2025

On 22 April 2025 the FDA announced that it would move to rid the American food supply of several synthetic dyes — Yellow 5 (tartrazine), Red 40, Blue 1 and Green 3 — by the end of the following year.[4] For two decades the agency had declined to ban Yellow 5, citing inconclusive evidence, even as European regulators required products containing it to carry a warning that it "may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children."[4] Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], who oversees the agency, characterized the measure not as a ban but as an "understanding" reached with food companies to phase the dyes out.[4] The announcement is treated in more detail at [[2025-fda-synthetic-food-dye-phase-out|the 2025 FDA synthetic food dye phase-out]].[4]

## Lab-grown chicken approvals

The FDA green-lit two different [[lab-grown-meat|lab-grown]] "chicken meat" products for human consumption: one produced by [[upside-foods|Upside Foods]] and the other by [[good-meat|GOOD Meat]], both based in California, with GOOD Meat also maintaining a Singapore office.[5] Upside Foods harvests cells from live animals and chicken tissue and grows meat in stainless-steel tanks known as bioreactors.[5] The agency announced in 2022 that the company's laboratory-derived chicken was "safe to eat," later stating it had "no further questions" about its safety, and Commissioner [[robert-califf|Robert M. Califf]] declared the product safe for human consumption.[5]

[[natural-news|Natural News]] described the agency as a "corrupt federal regulatory agency" for the approvals and characterized the products as meat grown from cancer cells.[5]

## Pfizer Western blot submissions (2023)

In February 2023 The Epoch Times reported that [[pfizer|Pfizer]] had submitted Western blot figures to the FDA and the [[european-medicines-agency|European Medicines Agency]] that were digitally generated rather than derived from actual experiments, as part of a broader account of [[truncated-mrna|truncated mRNA]] in the company's COVID-19 vaccine.[6] The report described an alarming lack of action by health authorities on the issue and attributed the conduct of both industry and regulators to a lack of ethics.[6]

## Approval of gene-edited pork (2023)

In May 2023 the FDA approved the sale of genetically modified pigs for supermarket shelves, clearing gene-edited pork for the American food supply.[7] The animals were developed by scientists at [[washington-state-university|Washington State University]] using [[crispr|CRISPR]], and the resulting meat was to be sold as German-style sausages, reportedly providing adequate nutrition for human consumption.[7] Lead researcher Jon Oatley said the project sought to improve both meat quality and livestock resilience in harsh environments in order to increase protein sources in underdeveloped countries.[7]

Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] criticized the decision as "madness," warning that DNA modifications to livestock could not have been tested for long-term effects and noting that the gene-editing program Recombinetics had taken only one year to secure FDA approval.[7] He urged consumers to heed farmers and ranchers, support local agriculture, and exercise caution over what they purchase and consume.[7]

## Expanded myocarditis warning for COVID-19 vaccines (2025)

In May 2025 the FDA directed [[pfizer|Pfizer]] and [[moderna|Moderna]] to expand the warning labels on their COVID-19 vaccines regarding the risk of a possible heart injury side effect linked to the mRNA shots, primarily in teenage boys and young men, citing a study published the previous year together with new agency data.[8] The orders were posted publicly on a Wednesday in letters dated April 17 to Pfizer and BioNTech concerning their Comirnaty vaccine and to Moderna concerning Spikevax.[8]

Both products already carried warnings that the risk of myocarditis — inflammation of the heart muscle — and pericarditis — inflammation of the membrane surrounding the heart — appeared elevated in young men, generally within the first week after vaccination.[8] Whereas earlier labels specified ages 18 to 24 for Moderna's vaccine and 12 to 17 for Pfizer's, the revised warning applies to males aged 16 to 25 for both.[8]

[[canncon|CannCon]] responded that everyone who lost a YouTube channel for making these claims should sue for what he estimated as billions of dollars, arguing that the platforms must be held accountable for censoring and banning thousands of Americans for saying "exactly this."[8] He noted that his own channel had been shut down instantly, ending his job, "based on a lie."[8]

## Real-time adverse event reporting (August 2025)

In August 2025 the FDA began "real-time reporting of adverse event data," a change FDA Commissioner [[marty-makary|Marty Makary]] described on his X account as "radical transparency."[9] The agency called the move a "significant step forward in modernizing" the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS).[9] "Adverse event reporting should be fast, seamless and transparent," Makary said, adding that people navigating the government's clunky adverse event reporting websites "should not have to wait months for that information to become public. We're closing that waiting period and will continue to streamline the process from start to finish."[9] The FDA, which sits under the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] led by Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], defines an adverse event as "any undesirable experience associated with the use of a medical product in a patient."[9]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued it would be a revelation for ordinary Americans to learn that FDA adverse event data had not previously been reported in real time, noting that the agency costs Americans billions of dollars a year — roughly $60 billion over the preceding decade by conservative estimates — while the public is told that vaccines are known to be safe and effective because of the FDA.[9] With accurate health data, she wrote, the [[make-america-healthy-again|MAHA]] revolution would accelerate, because it is easier to drive change with reliable data.[9]

## Ivermectin posts removed (2024)

In March 2024 the FDA agreed to take down social media posts and online materials cautioning against the use of [[ivermectin|ivermectin]] as a treatment for COVID-19, following a lawsuit filed by three doctors in 2022.[10] The case was initially dismissed but revived by the 5th Circuit Court of Appeals, which remanded it to the lower court; on review, the lower court determined that only Dr. [[mary-bowden|Mary Bowden]], one of the original plaintiffs, had standing.[10] Bowden emphasized that the agency's article "Why You Should Not Use Ivermectin to Treat or Prevent COVID-19" failed to mention that doctors retained the legal authority to prescribe ivermectin despite the FDA's discouragement.[10] Among the removed items was the post reading "You are not a horse. You are not a cow. Seriously, y'all. Stop it," linked to that article; the FDA was given a three-week deadline to remove the material across platforms.[10]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "the regime screeched 'horse paste!' at the top of their lungs" to discourage Americans from a medication that would likely have cured them if taken at the appropriate time and within an appropriate regimen, and that the thousands of doctors who said effective treatments existed were banned from the public square, with many losing medical licenses and at least one imprisoned.[10] She argued that ivermectin's availability would have precluded the emergency use authorization for the COVID-19 vaccines, which is why the FDA, the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] and the global health regime had to demonize treatments they knew to be effective, and she predicted that revelations about hydroxychloroquine would follow.[10]

## VRBPAC cancellation and the "healthy" label delay (February 2025)

In February 2025 the FDA cancelled, without explanation, the March 13 meeting of its [[vaccines-and-related-biological-products-advisory-committee|Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee]], the panel that meets each March to select influenza strains for the following season's shots.[11] Panel member [[paul-offit|Paul Offit]] disclosed the cancellation to CNBC and questioned whether manufacturers would turn to the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] for strain selection instead.[11] The agency describes its advisory committees as providing independent expert advice and making non-binding recommendations that the FDA generally follows but is not legally bound to adopt.[11]

Separately, [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] delayed until April 28 a 2024 FDA rule tightening the nutritional criteria for foods labelled "healthy," which would disqualify high-sugar cereals, protein bars, and yogurts while newly qualifying olive oil, salmon, canned vegetables, water, and coffee.[11]

## Approval of Vowst fecal-derived oral pill (2023)

In early May 2023 the FDA announced that it had authorized Vowst, the first orally administered pill made from donated human feces.[12] The approval followed the clearance of an enema-based medicine in December 2022, making Vowst the second treatment derived from human feces to receive agency approval.[12] The pills were approved for adults aged 18 and older with a history of repeated infections with Clostridium difficile, a bacterium that can cause severe nausea, cramps, and diarrhea.[12] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] acknowledged the treatment's apparent clinical benefit while using the approval as a springboard for broader criticism of elite-promoted food interventions, including insect-based foods containing chitin, and noted the existing use of beaver-derived flavorings in the food supply.[12]

## Pfizer myocarditis study date change

In January 2023 The Epoch Times reported that the FDA had quietly changed the end date for a required study of subclinical myocarditis following [[pfizer|Pfizer]]'s COVID-19 vaccine.[13] The agency had listed the completion date in 2021 as June 20, 2022, with Pfizer obliged to submit results by the end of 2022 as one of the postmarketing requirements imposed as a condition of approving the Pfizer-BioNTech vaccine.[13] After that deadline lapsed, the agency's listing was revised to say the study had "an original expected completion date of June 30, 2023."[13] Badlands Media contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] characterized the revision as the goalposts being moved again and derided the agency as the "Fraud & Death Administration."[13]

## RFK Jr. call to eliminate FDA departments (November 2024)

On Wednesday, November 6, 2024, the day after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election victory, [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] said the FDA's bureaucracy should be winnowed down, telling MSNBC that "there are entire departments, like the nutrition department at the FDA … that have to go—that are not doing their job. They're not protecting our kids."[14] Before the election Trump had floated Kennedy as having a role in his administration targeting federal agencies overseeing health care, food, and drugs.[14] Asked whether he would remove any health agencies outright, Kennedy replied that "to eliminate the agencies, as long as it requires congressional approval, I wouldn't be doing that," adding, "I can get the corruption out of the agencies."[14] In a separate Fox News interview that week he said, "we don't know what I'm going to do. I talked to the president about it yesterday, and he asked me what I wanted, and I said, we're developing a proposal now."[14]
