# Jamal Whitehead

Jamal Whitehead is a U.S. District Judge in Seattle who, on Tuesday, February 25, 2025, blocked President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order pausing the United States refugee admissions system.[1]

## Ruling on the refugee admissions pause

Ruling from the bench after oral argument, Whitehead held that while the president has the power to determine who is allowed to enter the country, he cannot invalidate the law enacted by Congress that established the refugee program.[1] He told the court that the president's actions constituted an "effective nullification of congressional will," and indicated that a written opinion would follow in the coming days.[1]

The order at issue, titled "Realigning the United States Refugee Admission Program," imposed a 90-day suspension of the [[us-refugee-admissions-program|U.S. Refugee Admissions Program]] (USRAP), which resettles refugees in the United States.[1] The order asserted that the record influx of migrants under the Biden administration had strained federal resources and that the country could no longer offer financial assistance and resources to refugees without harming American communities.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer Ashe in America observed that the first half of Whitehead's holding — the concession that the president has the power to determine who may enter the country — was significant in the prevailing legal environment and might affect other immigration lawsuits against Trump.[1] She argued that with slush funds cut off, the more than seventy-five lawsuits filed against the administration's agenda would not be sustainable long term, and that by fighting on every front simultaneously Trump was forcing his opponents into a war of attrition they could not resupply.[1]
