# Ben Bernanke

**Ben Bernanke** is an American economist who served as Chairman of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] from 2006 to 2014.[1]

## Tenure

Bernanke was appointed head of the Fed in 2006 by [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and was in position when the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial collapse]] occurred.[1] He was reappointed by [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] in 2010 and served until 2014, when Obama appointed [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] as his successor.[1]

## Assessment in Badlands coverage

Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that both Bernanke and Yellen carry "a reputation of believing printing more money is the answer to anything and everything that ails an economy, and that there are no consequences from printing too much money."[1] Under Bernanke's chairmanship the Fed responded to the 2008 collapse by increasing the [[federal-reserve-balance-sheet|Federal Reserve balance sheet]] by roughly 250 percent within a matter of months, purchasing government securities and [[mortgage-backed-securities|mortgage-backed securities]] on the stated reasoning that financial markets would otherwise collapse and banks would become illiquid.[1] Carlson argued that neither Bernanke nor Yellen made any serious attempt during the Obama years to normalize interest rates or reduce the balance sheet, sustaining what he termed a [[sugar-high-economy|"sugar high" economy]] for the full eight years.[1]

## Cited in the Ivy Dominion essay

The June 2025 Badlands Media essay "The Ivy Dominion" names Bernanke as a product of [[princeton-university|Princeton]]'s [[ivy-club|Ivy Club]], which it says "has birthed generations of central bankers."[2] His career is offered as evidence for the article's claim that Princeton functions as the "temple of central banking" within the alleged [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]], with a pathway running from the Ivy Club to the [[group-of-thirty|Group of Thirty]], the [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]] and the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]], and from [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] doctrine outward to global monetary control.[2]
