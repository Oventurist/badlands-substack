# Kristen Welker

Kristen Welker is the NBC News anchor of *Meet the Press* whose June 2026 interview with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ended when the President walked out following a heated exchange about elections being fake.[1]

## The walk-out

The moment went viral on the Sunday of the broadcast.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], used the exchange to attack self-described Trump supporters who tell election-integrity advocates to stop talking about fake elections lest they harm the President's agenda, yet then oppose the President himself when he raises the same subject.[1] "It's almost like it isn't about supporting the President at all, but rather about preserving and protecting fake elections," she wrote.[1]

Her argument was that continuing to pretend elections are real is what actually damages the America First agenda, because executing that agenda inside the system requires real elections — "so everything the President said to Welker is true and justified."[1] She added that Trump "has all the evidence," and that she and [[canncon|CannCon]] have presented substantial evidence with witnesses and expert analysis across three years of the Why We Vote program.[1]

## Trump's post-election "Meet the Press" interview (December 2024)

Welker moderated President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first sit-down interview after winning a second term, which aired on Sunday, December 8, 2024.[2] The two discussed Trump's plans for his first day in office, several of his more controversial Cabinet choices, and whether he would like the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to pursue his critics and political rivals.[2]

Welker asked Trump about [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], his choice to lead the [[fbi|FBI]], who had spoken about the need to target the "deep state."[2] Trump replied that "if they think that somebody was dishonest or crooked or a corrupt politician, I think he probably has an obligation to do it"; asked directly, "Are you going to direct him to do it?", Trump answered, "No. Not at all. Not at all."[2] He said prosecution decisions regarding figures such as special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] would rest with attorney general nominee [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and, to a different extent, Patel.[2]
