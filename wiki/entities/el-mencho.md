# Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes (El Mencho)

Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes, known by the alias "El Mencho," was the alleged leader of the [[jalisco-new-generation-cartel|Jalisco New Generation Cartel]] (CJNG) and for years one of Mexico's most wanted drug traffickers, with authorities previously offering multimillion-dollar rewards for information leading to his capture.[1]

## Death in the Jalisco raid (February 2026)

Mexican military forces killed El Mencho during an operation in Jalisco state over the weekend of February 21–22, 2026.[1] A reported 25 members of the Mexican national guard were killed in the operation.[1] Following the raid, violence erupted in parts of western [[mexico|Mexico]], including vehicle burnings and road blockades attributed to cartel retaliation, and security forces reportedly remained deployed in Jalisco and surrounding areas amid concerns of further reprisals.[1] The Mexican government did not release a full after-action report.[1]

## US involvement and the reported prisoner transfer

Reporting by the *New York Post* said the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration had quietly secured the transfer of roughly 100 cartel leaders from Mexico to US custody prior to the operation targeting El Mencho — a group reported to include El Mencho's brother.[1] No additional details about the nature or scope of US coordination were provided.[1] In response to news of the operation, President Trump posted, "Mexico must step up their effort on Cartels and Drugs!"[1]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the episode was consistent with months of speculation that Mexico would become the real target of a US-adjacent military campaign, while cautioning that the sequence could be [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] designed to keep the cartels guessing.[1]

## Killing in Tapalpa

Mexican security forces announced on a Sunday in February 2026 that they had killed Oseguera Cervantes during a military operation in Tapalpa, Jalisco.[2] He was considered one of the most powerful and most-wanted cartel bosses in both Mexico and the United States, where a $15 million reward had been offered for information leading to his arrest.[2]

The operation killed several [[jalisco-new-generation-cartel|CJNG]] members and injured Mexican troops, and authorities seized heavy weapons and armored vehicles.[2] The raid triggered widespread cartel reprisals — blockades, vehicle burnings and attacks on infrastructure — that left at least 14 people dead and prompted the [[state-department|U.S. Department of State]] to issue shelter-in-place warnings for Americans in Jalisco and other affected states.[2]
