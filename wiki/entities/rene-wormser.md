# Rene A. Wormser

**Rene A. Wormser** was an American attorney who served as General Counsel to the [[reece-committee|Reece Committee]], the congressional body that investigated [[tax-exempt-foundations|tax-exempt foundations]] between 1953 and 1955.[1]

Wormser wrote *Foundations: Their Power and Influence*, characterized by Badlands Media as a documented exposé of his experience with the committee.[1] In it he observed that "Foundations were originally created to support existing institutions and to undertake certain 'operating' functions" — a baseline against which he measured their later political activity.[1]

Wormser is also credited with the phrase **"tax-exempt complex"**, describing the way individual foundations merge in "cartel-like" fashion to fund shared political projects, a tendency he warned would "endanger the freedom of our intellectual and public life."[1] He further summarized the earlier work of the [[commission-on-industrial-relations|Commission on Industrial Relations]], noting that it began with a study of labor exploitation and moved on to concentrations of economic power, interlocking directorates, and the role of the then relatively new large charitable foundations — especially those of Carnegie and Rockefeller — as instruments of power concentration.[1]
