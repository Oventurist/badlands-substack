# Antonio Tajani

Antonio Tajani is Italy's Foreign Minister, serving in the government of Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]].[1]

## Criticism of Zelensky's Davos speech

In January 2026, after Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] delivered a speech at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos faulting the European Union's handling of security and its support for Ukraine, Tajani called the address "not generous" given the scale of European backing for Kiev.[1] European aid since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022 was put at nearly €193 billion in military and financial assistance.[1]

Tajani's rebuke was milder than that of Lega Nord lawmaker [[rossano-sasso|Rossano Sasso]], who demanded an end to Italian and EU weapons transfers, and contrasted with European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]]'s reaffirmation of support.[1]

## Suspension of the Israel defence memorandum (2026)

As Foreign Minister of Italy, Tajani was among the senior ministers consulted before Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's April 13, 2026 decision to suspend the automatic renewal of Italy's 2003 defence cooperation memorandum of understanding with Israel.[2] The decision followed Italian criticism of Israeli operations in Lebanon and an incident in which Israeli forces fired warning shots at Italian UNIFIL troops, after which Italy summoned Israel's ambassador.[2]

## Statements on Russia and drone claims, September 2025

Speaking at a Forza Italia party event in Telese Terme on Sunday 28 September 2025, Tajani said Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was not interested in igniting a global conflict, responding to claims that Moscow could send drones against [[italy|Italy]].[3] "I don't think Putin wants to start World War III, so I want to reassure all Italians," he said, adding "I don't believe Putin wants to send drones to swarm us" and noting that he had discussed the subject with Italian Defense Minister [[guido-crosetto|Guido Crosetto]] that morning.[3] The remarks followed a claim a day earlier by Ukraine's [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]] that Russia could target Italy or other European nations with drone attacks.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the intervention as Italy once again sending a [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] signal that Russia is neither a bad actor nor an enemy of the West.[3] He noted that Tajani, appointed Foreign Minister by [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] when she took office in 2022, was also made Deputy Prime Minister alongside [[matteo-salvini|Matteo Salvini]] — the figure implicated in an alleged scheme described by the EU in 2017 in which he was accused of taking Russian money, through Rosatom and Banca Intesa, to launch his populist party.[3]
