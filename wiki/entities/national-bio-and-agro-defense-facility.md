# National Bio and Agro-Defense Facility

The National Bio and Agro-Defense Facility (NBAF) is a $1.2 billion United States biocontainment laboratory in Manhattan, Kansas, built to study animal-to-human ("zoonotic") pathogens.[1] Completion of the facility was celebrated on Wednesday, May 24, 2023.[1]

## Mission and capability

NBAF is a Level-4 biolab with maximum biocontainment, the highest classification used for research on pathogens for which there is no reliable treatment or vaccine.[1] Its research program concerns transboundary animal diseases and pathogens capable of crossing from animals into humans. NBAF researcher [[lisa-hensley|Lisa Hensley]] has described a career spent studying what she called an "alphabet soup" of pathogens including SARS, monkeypox, MERS, Marburg and Ebola.[1]

## Relationship to Plum Island

NBAF replaces the aging [[plum-island-animal-disease-center|Plum Island]] biolab off the coast of New York, a site originally chosen for its isolation so that dangerous research would be physically separated from the American mainland.[1] Both Plum Island and NBAF are owned and operated by the [[department-of-homeland-security|US Department of Homeland Security]].[1]

## Controversy over the Kansas siting

The decision, first announced in 2008, to relocate the nation's most dangerous animal-pathogen research into the middle of the American food supply has drawn sustained public criticism.[1] Kansas is a central agricultural state — ranked first nationally in grain sorghum production with almost 64% of the crop, first in winter wheat with 24% of the crop, second in sorghum for silage, and third in cattle production and beef processing.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the siting decision has "puzzled Americans" since it was announced and that federal authorities proceeded despite citizen concerns.[1]

Hensley's account of the Liberian Ebola outbreak also illustrated the public-trust dimension of outbreak response: she described populations that "didn't even believe in Ebola," thought the disease was "something the government was doing," refused to take objects from her hands, and in some regions attacked treatment centers on the belief that the virus was not naturally occurring.[1]
