# Charles Warren

Sir Charles Warren was a British army officer, archaeologist, Freemason and police commissioner who occupies a central place in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account of occult interest in ancient human sacrifice.[1]

## Excavations at the Temple Mount

Warren excavated the rock on the mythic site of Solomon's temple on behalf of Edward VII, and as a Freemasonic grand master presumed it to be the location upon which the blood of all mass sacrifice victims of the ancient pagan syncretic cult system of Israel had flowed.[1] The site is the same rock, beneath the [[dome-of-the-rock|Dome of the Rock]], on which [[abraham|Abraham]] is said to have nearly sacrificed his son.[1]

His excavations of the Templar headquarters in Jerusalem — the caverns under the [[temple-mount|Temple Mount]] — unearthed an array of caverns and tunnels he hypothesized were conduits for mass human sacrifice overseen by an ancient priesthood. Writing on December 12, 1867, Warren recorded that "the discovery of a shaft down to the water of the virgin's fount threw considerable light upon the object of the rock-cut canals about Jerusalem, as proving then as, had been conjectured by some, to have been for conducting away the refuse and blood from the temple."[1] He further believed the two small caverns under the sacrificial rock, and the tunnel network extending from the lowest cavern, were designed to infuse the ground of Jerusalem with the blood of her children in a pact with dark spirit forces.[1] Ehret links these entities to the being the Gnostic order of [[knights-templar|Templars]] were accused of worshipping under the Temple Mount in the form of Baphomet during the early 14th century.[1]

Warren was also convinced that [[solomons-temple|Solomon's Temple]] embodied Kabbalistic proportions built around the Vesica Pisces — the same geometric configuration Ehret associates with the placement of the [[whitechapel-murders|Whitechapel murder victims]] of 1888 — and that the temple was a vast complex of mass human sacrifice which he and other Rosicrucians believed to be a source of power for channeling demonic forces.[1]

## Zionism and the Metropolitan Police

Beyond archaeology, Warren led the [[palestine-exploration-fund|Palestine Exploration Fund]], which Ehret credits with shaping modern [[zionism|Zionism]], and served as leading Grand Master of the [[quatuor-coronati-lodge|Quatuor Coronati lodge]], which set the stage for reviving Solomon's Temple.[1] Ehret characterizes him as a champion of a movement designed to convince the Jews of Russia and Europe to relocate to the desert.[1]

Warren was simultaneously Chief of London's Metropolitan Police during the Whitechapel Murders of 1888, which Ehret describes as involving a serious anti-Jewish propaganda campaign intended to terrorize the Jews of England into leaving Europe for the desert — an operation he treats as closely connected to the Zionist project.[1]
