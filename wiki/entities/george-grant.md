# George Grant

George Grant was a Canadian philosopher, Rhodes Scholar, and the figure Badlands Media contributor Matthew Ehret identifies as the father of Canada's "New Nationalism," which was established in 1963 with the unveiling of Canada's modern national flag.[1] He was the grandson of George Parkin, a president of the Rhodes Trust, placing him directly within the hereditary networks of the [[round-table-movement|Round Table]] milieu created by [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]].[1]

## The universal and homogeneous state

Grant described the political trajectory of the modern world as the creation of a "universal and homogeneous state" — a system in which technocrats rule and national identity is erased in the name of global governance.[1] In his 1963 work *Lament for a Nation*, Grant wrote that "the aspirations of progress have made Canada redundant," describing the universal and homogeneous state as "the pinnacle of political striving," where "universal" implies a world-wide state that eliminates war among nations and "homogeneous" means all men would be equal, eliminating war among classes.[1] He added that this state "will be achieved by means of modern science- a science that leads to the conquest of nature."[1]

Ehret argues that this formulation showed Grant's adherence to the techniques of mind control elaborated by [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]] in the *Brave New World* blueprint for a New World Order, rather than a straightforward conservative lament.[1]

## Family and political legacy

Grant's grandson, Count Michael Grant Ignatieff, became head of Canada's Liberal Party from 2008 to 2011, during the period when [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] was still being groomed for the leadership by [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] and [[canada-2020|Canada 2020]] privy council officials.[1] Ignatieff in turn worked closely with Rhodes Scholar [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], who succeeded him as Liberal leader from 2011 to 2013.[1]
