# Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons

The Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) is the international body based in The Hague charged with implementing the [[chemical-weapons-convention|Chemical Weapons Convention]] and investigating alleged violations of the treaty.[1]

## Russian request regarding Ukraine

In July 2025, [[russia|Russia]] asked the OPCW to investigate its claim that Ukrainian troops were using toxic munitions.[1] Speaking at a session of the OPCW Executive Council in The Hague, Russian envoy [[vladimir-tarabrin|Vladimir Tarabrin]] said that "the Russian side continues to document not only instances of Ukrainian nationalists using toxic chemicals and military-grade poison agents, but also evidence of a widespread system for their mass production in Ukraine."[1] Moscow requested that the organization dispatch experts to verify its findings.[1]

Earlier that month Russia had announced the discovery of a makeshift laboratory allegedly used by Ukrainian troops to store [[chloropicrin|chloropicrin]], a choking agent banned under the Chemical Weapons Convention, and claimed that Ukrainian forces had dropped toxic substances from drones.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the referral a fascinating development and situated it against the history of U.S. chemical-weapons policy, noting [[scott-horton|Scott Horton]]'s account — given in an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] — that in 1980 the United States sold illegal chemical weapons to [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] and encouraged their use against Iran during the [[iran-iraq-war|Iran-Iraq War]].[1] He observed that the same weapons later served the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and [[cia|CIA]] as justification for the 2003 invasion of Iraq, and noted that four nations still neither signed nor accede to the legitimacy of the treaty regime.[1]
