# Burundi

Burundi is a landlocked state in Africa's Great Lakes region, bordering the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] near Lake Tanganyika.[1]

## Role in the eastern Congo conflict

Burundi is a regional rival of [[rwanda|Rwanda]] and an ally of the DRC. Badlands analysis in December 2025 framed Burundi and the DRC together as the principal opposition to [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]]'s government in Kigali, which the same commentary characterized as a globalist proxy and close ally of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

The city of [[uvira|Uvira]], seized by [[m23|M23]] rebels in December 2025, sits on Lake Tanganyika near the Burundian border and had served as an important military and administrative base for Kinshasa in South Kivu following the earlier fall of the provincial capital, Bukavu.[1] Its capture therefore brought the conflict directly to Burundi's frontier and, according to the Congolese government, displaced more than 200,000 people in a "generalized offensive" attributed to Rwanda's Defense Forces and the rebels.[1]

## Accusation of M23 cross-border bombing (December 2025)

In December 2025, Burundi accused [[m23|M23]] rebels based in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] of bombing its territory, marking an escalation in regional hostilities despite a peace agreement signed the previous week between the presidents of the DR Congo and [[rwanda|Rwanda]].[2] The alleged strike hit Cibitoke province, which borders the DR Congo, and a local news agency reported that several civilians, including children, were injured when two projectiles fell near a market in Rugombo; the precise casualty count was not confirmed.[2] Foreign Minister [[edouard-bizimana|Edouard Bizimana]] said on X that "the terrorists of the AFC/M23, supported by Rwanda, dropped a bomb on Burundian territory," and warned that appropriate actions would be taken to protect the population.[2]

The rebel [[alliance-fleuve-congo|Congo River Alliance]] countered through spokesperson [[lawrence-kanyuka|Lawrence Kanyuka]] that bombs had fallen continuously from Burundian territory for more than three days, striking villages in eastern DR Congo, and said Burundi "should focus on improving the living conditions of its people."[2]

Badlands background noted intense international controversy in 2016 over accusations that [[israel|Israel]] was shipping weapons to Burundi and possibly Rwanda that could reach regional rebels, which led the Israeli Ministry of Defense to require Burundi- and Rwanda-based customers to apply for licenses; this followed the 2015 political crisis in which a failed coup against the Burundian government was attributed to rebel groups accused of Rwandan backing.[2] Burundi is allied with the DR Congo against M23, which is widely linked to the Rwandan government.[2]
