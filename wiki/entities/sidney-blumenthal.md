# Sidney Blumenthal

Sidney Blumenthal is a longtime confidant of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] whose private emails to her about Libya became central evidence in the congressional Benghazi investigations.[1]

## Relationship to the Clintons

Blumenthal did not work for Clinton at the [[state-department|State Department]]; his closeness to her dates to the Clinton administration, and at the time of the Libya intervention he worked for the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], where the Badlands account describes his role as securing deals in exchange for finder's fees.[1] [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] obtained Benghazi-related emails directly from Blumenthal and used them to demonstrate that Clinton had not turned over all of her Benghazi correspondence to Congress — none of the Clinton–Blumenthal exchanges had been produced.[1]

## The Osprey proposal

On July 14, 2011, Blumenthal sent Clinton a note about an upcoming visit to [[turkey|Turkey]], where she could meet retired Army Major General David Grange, another arms broker.[1] Blumenthal urged the U.S. to sign a $114 million contract for Grange's company, [[osprey-global-solutions|Osprey Global Solutions]], to deliver equipment to anti-Gadhafi forces via a hospital ship, writing: "This is a private contract. It puts Americans in a central role without being direct battle combatants. The TNC (National Transitional Council) wants to demonstrate they are pro-U.S."[1] Reporting noted the proposal "sounded much like Turi's gambit," a reference to the parallel private-broker plan pursued by [[marc-turi|Marc Turi]].[1]

In an FBI interview Blumenthal acknowledged ties to Osprey and that he stood to receive a finder's fee if the deal came to fruition.[1] A single day after his email to Clinton, the Obama administration recognized the [[national-transitional-council|National Transitional Council]] as Libya's government, giving it access to $30 billion held in U.S. accounts previously controlled by [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]].[1] The Badlands article treats that one-day sequence as evidence that the Clinton Foundation expected a share of the Libyan arms business.[1]

Blumenthal's communications went to Clinton's private email account, which itself became the subject of fierce controversy and congressional investigation.[1]
