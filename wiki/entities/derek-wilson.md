# Derek Wilson

**Derek Wilson** is a British historian and biographer of the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild family]], cited repeatedly in Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" as a sympathetic mainstream source whose own admissions support the article's more critical thesis.[1]

## Cited admissions

Wilson is quoted on the advantage the family gained from its early clients: the Rothschilds "prospered because they received before their rivals news of market trends, commodity prices, and major political events," a benefit DeLarme attributes in part to the family's business with the Thurn und Taxis courier house of the Holy Roman Empire.[1]

Described in the article as a "Rothschild-friendly biographer," Wilson is quoted acknowledging the disputed origin of the family fortune: "It was the temporary diversion of the immense sums of money originating in Hesse-Kassel which enabled N. M. [as [[nathan-rothschild|Nathan]] liked to be called] to launch his banking operation, providing him with both liquidity and prestige."[1] Those sums are the funds paid by the British government to [[william-ix-of-hesse-kassel|William IX]] for Hessian troops.[1]

Wilson is further cited for the observation that in 1810 Nathan was merely one of several financiers operating in London, but by 1815 had become the principal financier to the British government and its [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] — "a remarkable coup [that] could only have been achieved by a complex series of dealings, many of which were encased in a secrecy which cannot now be penetrated."[1]
