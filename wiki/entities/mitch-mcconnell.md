# Mitch McConnell

Mitch McConnell is a long-serving United States Senator and a longtime colleague and friend of South Carolina Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]].[1] In Badlands Media commentary he is characterized, alongside Graham, as one of the two most notorious NeoCon Republican senators, and the pairing of the two men in a single news cycle became the organizing hook of the July 13, 2026 edition of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]].[1]

## July 2026 death rumors

On the same day that Graham's death was announced, reports circulated that McConnell had also died.[1] A video filmed in front of McConnell's home showed him being loaded into an ambulance; commenters observed that the ambulance crew displayed no sense of urgency in their movements, which was widely interpreted online as a sign that McConnell had already passed.[1]

McConnell subsequently released a written statement asserting that he was alive and recovering in hospital, accompanied by a photograph of himself with his wife.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reported that the image was widely speculated to be AI-generated, that most observers agreed the statement had not been written by McConnell himself, and that genuine doubt persisted as to whether he was actually alive.[1] The statement acknowledged that McConnell had been hospitalized after a serious fall that left him injured, offering this as the explanation for his absence from public view over the preceding month.[1]

## Significance in Badlands commentary

The near-simultaneous reporting of Graham's death and McConnell's rumored death was described as one of the most bizarre coincidences of recent memory — an event strange enough that ordinary, non-conspiratorial observers ("normies") began questioning the verisimilitude of official narratives.[1] The episode was treated as a data point in the broader Badlands thesis that senior [[neoconservatives|NeoCon]] figures in the [[united-states-senate|U.S. Senate]] are being removed from the board, and it fed the expectation within the movement's readership that a major Senate death announcement was imminent.[1]

## Absence and the hawkish void (July 2026)

McConnell's health-related absence from the Senate in July 2026 was cited alongside the sudden death of Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] as further eroding the traditional "McCain wing" of the Republican Party that had pushed interventionist policies since [[john-mccain|John McCain]]'s death in 2018.[2] [[america-first-movement|America First]] figures welcomed the combination as an opportunity to reduce interventionist pressure on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] calling the moment a "mortal blow" to that faction.[2]

## The 2023 January 6 footage dispute

In March 2023 McConnell sided with Democratic leader [[chuck-schumer]] in condemning [[tucker-carlson]] and [[fox-news]] for airing Capitol surveillance video that challenged the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]]'s account of that day.[3] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] characterized his stance as siding with Schumer and against the American people.[3]

## Departure from Senate leadership (2024)

McConnell announced in early 2024 that he would step down as Senate Republican leader in November, while intending to keep his Senate seat until his term ended in January 2027, when he would be within weeks of turning 86.[4] ZeroHedge described him as "periodically malfunctioning," referring to public episodes in which he froze mid-remarks.[4] The announced field to succeed him consisted of [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]] of Texas and [[john-thune|John Thune]] of South Dakota, with John Barrasso of Wyoming opting to seek the whip position instead, and [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] teasing a bid backed by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[4]

## Role in the 2024 border bill

As Senate Minority Leader in early 2024, McConnell was the principal Republican negotiator of the [[bipartisan-border-bill-2024|bipartisan Senate border bill]], though he assigned the public-facing role to Senator [[james-lankford|James Lankford]] of Oklahoma.[5] Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] made clear that McConnell called the shots for Republicans, telling reporters, "I have never worked more closely with Leader McConnell on any piece of legislation as we did on this."[5] Badlands commentary noted that the two leaders had frequently worked in lockstep, most recently on budget-busting spending bills and on American taxpayer subsidization of Ukraine's war effort.[5]

Senator [[mike-lee|Mike Lee]] responded by calling for new Senate GOP leadership immediately, describing the outcome as a "disqualifying betrayal" and accusing leadership of hiding the bill text from members for months.[5] Badlands commentary questioned whether McConnell had been "glitching the whole time" given the final state of the bill he negotiated.[5]

## August 2023 freezing episodes

On Wednesday, August 30, 2023, McConnell — then 81 and the longtime Kentucky Republican leader, who had appeared increasingly diminished and frail after a series of falls and a serious head injury that year — froze up suddenly during a news conference in Covington, Kentucky.[6] It was the second such on-camera episode in recent weeks.[6] He had been taking questions from reporters after an event hosted by the Northern Kentucky Chamber of Commerce when he was asked about running for re-election in 2026; appearing thinner and paler than in recent months, he began to answer with a slight chuckle, then abruptly stopped speaking for about 30 seconds, standing motionless while gripping his lectern with his mouth pursed and his eyes fixed.[6] When an aide approached to ask whether he had heard the question, he mumbled "yes" but seemed unable to continue speaking or to move.[6] The New York Times reported that the incident, the second in two months, intensified questions about his health, his ability to serve and his future in the [[united-states-senate|Senate]].[6]

Badlands writer [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] asked "what in the bloody hell is going on with the turtle?", recalling that weeks earlier McConnell had frozen "like an old TV set" mid-statement, and said that whatever the cause of these "momentary moments of impersonating a mannequin," the episodes made it painfully obvious that term limits are needed.[6] Citing these incidents together with Senator [[dianne-feinstein|Dianne Feinstein]] being told how to vote on the floor, he concluded that a fair portion of the elected government is either too old or too cognitively impaired to do the job required of them.[6]

## Vote against RFK Jr.'s confirmation (February 2025)

When the [[us-senate|Senate]] confirmed [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] as Secretary of Health and Human Services by a 52-48 vote on Thursday, February 13, 2025, McConnell voted against him after raising concerns about Kennedy's position on vaccination.[7] McConnell, who survived polio before Jonas Salk's vaccine breakthrough in the 1950s, had as former Republican majority leader angered some of [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s supporters with votes and views that conflicted with the president's.[7]

## Opposition to the January 6 footage release

On March 7, 2023, in his capacity as Senate Minority Leader, McConnell called it a "mistake" for [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] and [[fox-news|Fox News]] to broadcast never-before-seen [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|January 6 security footage]], a position he took alongside Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]]'s call for [[rupert-murdoch|Rupert Murdoch]] to stop Carlson from airing further material.[8] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[patel-patriot|Patel Patriot]] argued that both McConnell and Schumer had promoted transparency in government only when it benefited their own political agendas, and asked what they were afraid of.[8]
