# Woodrow Monte

Woodrow Monte is a researcher cited in Badlands Media's account of food additives as the author of the peer-reviewed paper *Aspartame: Methanol and the Public Health*.[1]

Monte is quoted for the finding that when diet sodas and soft drinks sweetened with [[aspartame]] are used to replace fluid loss during exercise and physical exertion in hot climates, "the intake of methanol can exceed 250 mg/day, or 32 times the Environmental Protection Agency's recommended limit of consumption for this cumulative poison."[1] Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] presented Monte's work as part of the body of published science that, in his view, contradicts industry assurances about the sweetener's safety and is downplayed by outlets funded by the corporations that profit from it.[1] The [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]] limit referenced in the quotation serves as the benchmark against which Monte's exposure estimate is measured.[1]
