# Capitol Times Magazine

Capitol Times Magazine, also referred to as The Capitol, is an American publication that launched its inaugural edition in June 2023 with a cover featuring entrepreneur [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]] under the label "DHS Domestic Extremist."[1]

## Inaugural edition

The launch was announced via a press release carried on the newswire service AccessWire and highlighted in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief for June 26, 2023.[1] The centerpiece of the issue was an interview-format article of more than eighty pages in which Byrne set out a series of disclosures about his dealings with United States government officials and intelligence personnel.[1]

The piece carried a foreword from General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]], who wrote that "Patrick's story is for real; he's for real, and the corruption he's exposed is for real, and it only gets worse the further you read."[1]

## Content and reception

The article recapped Byrne's earlier accounts of his affair with [[maria-butina|Maria Butina]], his participation in what he describes as a blackmail operation targeting [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], and his self-characterization as an eager intelligence asset acting in the cause of peace, while adding previously undisclosed material.[1] Among the new claims were an assertion that a bipartisan group of senators — including [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] — recruited him in 2006 through the Senate Judiciary Committee to disrupt systemic corruption, and an allegation that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] solicited an $800 million bribe from the Iranian government in the fall of 2021 in exchange for the unfreezing of $8 billion held in a South Korean bank account.[1]

Byrne briefly posted the article on Telegram before removing it, reportedly because of copy errors throughout the text that appeared to result from an issue with tracked changes; publication in The Capitol was expected the same week.[1] Writing for Badlands Media, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the piece as "consuming," said it "reads like the confessions of a spy," and urged readers to prioritize reading it.[1]
