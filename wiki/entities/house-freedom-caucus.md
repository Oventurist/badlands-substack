# House Freedom Caucus

The House Freedom Caucus is a bloc of conservative Republican members of the U.S. House of Representatives that has repeatedly used government funding fights as leverage for spending cuts and border security demands.[1]

## June 2023 rule-vote revolt

On Tuesday, June 6, 2023, House conservatives staged what the Associated Press described as a mini-revolt in retaliation for Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s handling of the previous week's vote to raise the [[debt-ceiling|debt ceiling]].[2] Led by outspoken members of the Freedom Caucus, a group of eleven Republicans broke with their party on an otherwise routine procedural rule vote, throwing the day's schedule and the rest of the week into disarray; it was the first such procedural rule vote to fail in nearly two decades.[2] Many in the group were the same conservatives who had tried to stop the [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt ceiling bill]] from advancing and who then threatened to move to oust McCarthy after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] signed the package into law.[2] Short of taking that step, they demanded a meeting with McCarthy, leaving the standoff unresolved.[2] Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] said, "We're frustrated with the way this place is operating. We're not going to live in the era of the imperial speaker anymore."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that it was hard to feel sympathy for the caucus given that it had caved and elected McCarthy Speaker, and that McCarthy had turned out to be exactly the Speaker they should have expected.[2] She argued he had reneged on the concessions extracted during the Speaker negotiation — releasing all the [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|J6 footage]], fiscal responsibility, and the various rules commitments members had touted as wins — noting the footage was still withheld, the debt ceiling bill had eliminated any prospect of fiscal restraint, and the House had repeatedly suspended the rules to pass legislation.[2] She added that the holdout tactic only works while Democrats keep voting against the rule, since McCarthy had shown on the debt ceiling bill that he commands a coalition sufficient to pass what he wants, and she noted dryly that McCarthy had been Speaker for exactly five months — hardly an "era."[2]

## Opposition to the January 2024 spending deal

On Sunday, January 7, 2024, Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] and Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] announced topline funding numbers for fiscal year 2024 totaling $1.59 trillion — $886 billion for defense and roughly $704 billion in non-defense spending — plus a $69 billion side deal in adjustments directed to non-defense domestic programs, bringing overall spending to close to $1.66 trillion.[1] The Freedom Caucus called the agreement a "total failure," a rebuke that presented an immediate problem for Johnson, who was leading an extremely narrow majority.[1]

Conservatives in the caucus had pressed for the funding bills to carry more stringent immigration and border security provisions, and that demand remained one of the principal hurdles ahead of the January 19 and February 2 funding deadlines.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the bipartisan character of the deal as the alarming feature, arguing that with Schumer, Johnson and the [[white-house|White House]] aligned, the "uniparty" had again united against the America First majority, with the Freedom Caucus cast as the designated loser.[1] She questioned whether any of its members had "any fight left," and linked the outcome to Johnson's unanimous election as Speaker by his caucus, which she said she had predicted on the morning of the vote and which was becoming easier to explain in hindsight.[1]

## SCORE Act floor delay (December 2025)

On Tuesday, December 2, 2025, a handful of Freedom Caucus members delayed action on the House floor in protest of the Student Compensation and Opportunity Through Rights and Endorsements (SCORE) Act, a bill regulating compensation college athletes receive for their name, image and likeness.[3] Representatives [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]] of Florida, [[scott-perry|Scott Perry]] of Pennsylvania and [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]] of Texas voted with all Democrats against the procedural rule that would have teed up votes on the SCORE Act and other measures, stalling the tally at 207-209.[3] House Majority Leader [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] huddled with Roy, Freedom Caucus Chair [[andy-harris|Andy Harris]], Representative [[andrew-clyde|Andrew Clyde]] and other members and staff; Harris and Clyde eventually voted in favour, and leaders waited for an absent Republican to arrive so the rule could pass.[3] The episode coincided with Representative [[elise-stefanik|Elise Stefanik]]'s public accusation that Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] was lying and protecting the "deep state," underscoring a Republican conference on a razor's edge.[3]

## January 2025 Speaker vote

Ahead of the House vote to elect a Speaker at the start of the 119th Congress, Rep. [[mike-lawler|Mike Lawler]] said on ABC's "This Week" on December 29, 2024 that [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] would be reelected because no member of the Freedom Caucus could assemble the 218 votes needed.[4] Lawler argued that members pushing for Johnson's removal were "playing with fire" and were "kidding themselves" if they believed a more conservative Speaker was available, recalling that the [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy|removal of Kevin McCarthy]] by [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] and colleagues alongside 208 Democrats was "the single stupidest thing I've ever seen in politics."[4] President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] subsequently gave Johnson his "complete and total endorsement" on Truth Social, a move seen as key given Johnson could afford only a handful of GOP defections.[4]
