# Fair Fight

Fair Fight is a liberal voting-rights organization active in Georgia that brought the federal lawsuit against [[true-the-vote|True the Vote]] over the group's mass challenges to voter eligibility in the state's early-2021 U.S. Senate runoff elections.[1]

## Litigation against True the Vote

Fair Fight alleged that True the Vote's effort to challenge the eligibility of hundreds of thousands of Georgia voters violated the clause of the [[voting-rights-act-of-1965|Voting Rights Act]] that outlaws voter suppression and intimidation.[1] On January 2, 2024, Judge Steve C. Jones of the United States District Court for the Northern District of Georgia ruled against Fair Fight in a 145-page opinion, holding that while the court "maintains its prior concerns" about how the challenges were pursued, Fair Fight had failed to show that the efforts were illegal.[1] The court found no violation of Section 11(b) of the Voting Rights Act by any named defendant and directed the clerk to enter judgment for the defendants.[1]

## Significance

The decision was narrow in scope, binding only within Jones's northern Georgia district, and *The New York Times* assessed that it would do little to change a status quo in which right-wing election groups had already generated thousands of voter-registration challenges across multiple states.[1] Election officials and voting-rights groups warned that an expanded mass-challenge campaign could intimidate voters away from the ballot box during the 2024 presidential election.[1] Badlands Media commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the same outcome in the opposite direction, describing it as a First Amendment win and a vindication of the lawful activities of [[catherine-engelbrecht|Catherine Engelbrecht]] and [[gregg-phillips|Gregg Phillips]].[1]
