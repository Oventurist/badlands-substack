# Steven Sund

Steven A. Sund is the former Chief of the United States Capitol Police who commanded the force on January 6, 2021, and who subsequently became one of the most prominent institutional critics of the congressional leadership's handling of Capitol security that day.[1]

## Role on January 6, 2021

As the [[us-capitol-police|United States Capitol Police]] chief on the day of the Capitol riot, Sund was the senior uniformed official responsible for securing the complex.[1] He has since alleged that [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] and Democratic leadership bore culpability for the security failures of that day, and that he was used as a scapegoat and pressured to resign as Police Chief in the aftermath.[1] Sympathetic outlets have argued that Pelosi's treatment of Sund was intended to obscure her own responsibility for the inadequate defense of the Capitol.[1]

## September 2023 testimony

On Tuesday, September 18, 2023, Sund testified before a House subcommittee hearing titled "Oversight of United States Capitol Security: Assessing Security Failures on January 6, 2021."[1] The hearing began roughly an hour late because the House was voting on defense spending, and was comparatively short; Sund delivered a condensed version of the longer account he had previously given in an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].[1]

[[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] complained that no mainstream outlet covered the hearing, contrasting the silence with the saturation primetime coverage given to the Democrat-run January 6th select committee, and noting that apart from C-SPAN's routine airing of congressional proceedings it was the only outlet reporting on the session.[1]

Commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] held that the hearing "didn't really move any needles" but did spotlight arguments for ending qualified immunity and for placing legislators under oath when they speak in public hearings, since as matters stand they enjoy effective immunity to make false statements to the public.[1] The same commentary singled out California Representative Norma Torres as the hearing's chief practitioner of gaslighting for asserting that the January 6 crowd was armed and marched on the Capitol intending to "hang Mike Pence."[1]

Within the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] framing, Sund's testimony was treated as part of the ongoing exposure of what Badlands writers call the [[fedsurrection|FedSurrection]], and the near-simultaneous charging of [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]] was read as an attempt to distract from it.[1]

## Tucker Carlson interview, August 2023

In episode 15 of [[tucker-on-twitter|Tucker on Twitter]], released in August 2023, [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] sat down with Sund to hear what the former chief said really happened on January 6.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the episode was drowned out amid an unusually heavy news week, and described Sund's account as shocking evidence that January 6 was not an insurrection by Trump supporters but "the final step of traitors in government to solidify their November 3 coup."[2]

According to that account, Sund was set up to be a patsy: multiple agencies withheld intelligence from him and denied him support for 71 minutes while the U.S. Capitol was under siege.[2] His attempts to tell the story had been repeatedly suppressed — Carlson had interviewed him while still at [[fox-news|Fox News]] and the network refused to air the discussion, [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] used less than ten minutes of his side, and although he was interviewed by the Department of Defense and the Senate, his account was withheld from the public until the X release.[2] Ashe in America called for truth and reconciliation and accountability for the agencies that designed and carried out the operation, arguing that "we were gassed and then gaslit" and that ultimately "the buck stops with [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]."[2]
