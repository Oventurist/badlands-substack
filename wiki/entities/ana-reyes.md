# Ana Reyes

Ana Reyes is a United States district judge on the US District Court for the District of Columbia.[1]

## Haitian TPS ruling

Reyes blocked the Trump administration from terminating [[temporary-protected-status|Temporary Protected Status]] for Haitian nationals, preventing the policy change from taking effect while litigation continues.[1] The ruling extends protections that had previously been set to expire following earlier extensions granted under the Biden administration.[1] Badlands cited the decision as an example of district-court restraints on executive immigration policy during the second Trump term.[1]

## Littlejohn sentencing

In January 2024 Reyes sentenced [[charles-littlejohn|Charles Littlejohn]] to five years in prison for the unauthorized disclosure of income tax returns belonging to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and thousands of the nation's wealthiest people.[2] Reyes emphasized the gravity of the crime, saying multiple times that it amounted to an attack against the United States and its legal foundation: "What you did in attacking the sitting president of the United States was an attack on our constitutional democracy."[2] She described Littlejohn as someone who "pulled off the biggest heist in IRS history," and compared his actions to the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack, noting that "your actions were also a threat to our democracy."[2]

## Transgender military service ruling (March 2025)

On Tuesday, March 18, 2025, Reyes, sitting as a U.S. District Judge in Washington, D.C., blocked enforcement of Trump's executive order banning transgender people from military service, ruling that the order likely violated their constitutional rights.[3] It was the latest in a string of legal setbacks for the administration's agenda, and Reyes was the second judge that day to rule against it.[3] Both rulings came within hours of Trump's call to impeach a third judge, [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]], who had temporarily blocked deportation flights — a demand that drew a rare rebuke from Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]].[3]

Reyes, who was nominated to the bench by President Joe Biden, delayed her order until Friday morning to give the administration time to appeal.[3] In her opinion she wrote: "The court knows that this opinion will lead to heated public debate and appeals. In a healthy democracy, both are positive outcomes... We should all agree, however, that every person who has answered the call to serve deserves our gratitude and respect."[3] Badlands commentary rejected the ruling's legitimacy outright, with Chris Paul writing that judges "have no say in any of these matters."[3]
