# Haaretz

Haaretz is the oldest newspaper in [[israel|Israel]] and is editorially opposed to Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

In March 2026, Badlands Media cited Haaretz reporting that nearly a dozen Iranian smart missiles — each carrying dozens of smaller submunitions with independent guidance systems — penetrated the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] and struck central Israel overnight, in one of many salvos that hit the country during the night.[1] Badlands commentators read the reporting as evidence that Israel's air defenses had failed and that the country was militarily vulnerable in its ongoing war with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## April 2026 reporting

Described by Badlands as the oldest operating newspaper in [[israel|Israel]], Haaretz published an April 2026 interview in which [[pinchas-wallerstein|Pinchas Wallerstein]], a founder of the modern [[israeli-settler-movement|Israeli Settler Movement]], said he was horrified by the rise of [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]] and labelled it treason against the State of Israel.[2]

The paper also published a piece by Tom Levinson documenting an explosion in suicide, depression, and PTSD among [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] reservists conscripted after October 7th, many psychologically broken by what they witnessed inflicted on Palestinian civilians in Gaza and the West Bank.[2] Haaretz further reported that reservists already diagnosed with PTSD were pressured into repeated tours and that those who refused were arrested.[2]

## Nova music festival friendly-fire report (2023)

On November 18, 2023, Haaretz reported that an Israeli police investigation into the October 7 Hamas attack on the Nova music festival near the Gaza border had found that an Israeli attack helicopter fired on some of the festival attendees.[3] According to a police source cited in the report, the combat helicopter arrived from the Ramat David base and fired at Hamas fighters and other Palestinians who had crossed the border fence, but also hit Israelis at the festival; police put the total killed at the site at 364.[3] The report marked the first official acknowledgement that Israeli forces killed some of their own at the festival, after the military and rescue services had said 260 Israelis were killed there in a deliberate massacre by Hamas and Palestinians.[3]

## "Israel Is on the Verge of Fascism" op-ed (June 2024)

On June 24, 2024, Haaretz published an opinion piece asking whether Israel was on the verge of crossing the threshold into fascism.[4] The op-ed compared the sound of thugs' shoes in the alleys of the Old City on Jerusalem Day to the SA marches of 1920s and 1930s Germany, describing yellow-shirted militants — followers of a leader with a criminal past who controls the police — beating, kicking and swearing at Arabs and journalists, and observing that it was hard to distinguish between the thugs and the Border Police in what it called an annual fascistic ritual.[4] It argued that teenagers from the religious Zionist sector who believe in the supremacy of the Jewish race recalled European fascists who once hunted socialists, communists and Jews, and that proto-fascist groups were on the rise in Israel in parallel with Europe's populist wave.[4]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry said he showcased the piece because it echoed his own recent arguments, but faulted it for presenting fascism as a right-wing doctrine, noting that the term was coined in 1922 by [[giovanni-gentile|Giovanni Gentile]], [[benito-mussolini|Mussolini]]'s chief political philosopher, who defined it as the merger of corporate and government power.[4] He also listed glaring omissions from the op-ed — [[irgun|Irgun]], [[lehi|Lehi]], Revisionism, the Stern Gang, [[zeev-jabotinsky|Jabotinsky]], and the Jerusalem Agreement — and used that history to argue that Israel has always been a fascist government, a claim he presented as verifiable rather than polemical.[4]

## Israeli government boycott (November 2024)

On Sunday, November 24, 2024, the Israeli Cabinet unanimously agreed to sanction Haaretz, the country's oldest newspaper, citing its critical coverage of the Israel–Hamas war.[5] The Cabinet approved a proposal by Communications Minister [[shlomo-karhi|Shlomo Karhi]] calling for all government staffers and staffers of state-owned companies to end communications with Haaretz and to stop advertising and subscription purchases with the paper.[5]

Press-freedom advocates condemned the move as part of a broader effort to silence voices critical of Israel's war in Gaza.[5] Jodie Ginsberg, chief executive of the [[committee-to-protect-journalists|Committee to Protect Journalists]], said the organization deplored the attempt to silence a respected Israeli outlet by hurting its advertising and subscription revenue, and called Israel's increasing restrictions on critical media disturbing evidence of efforts to prevent coverage of its actions in Gaza.[5] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry framed the boycott as unsurprising given Israel's long-standing media regulations on reporting about government and military activity, and warned that similar censorship impulses were being promoted in the United States.[5]


## Zehava Galon's August 2024 column

On Sunday, August 4, 2024, Haaretz published a column by [[zehava-galon|Zehava Galon]] headlined "If Israelis Don't Rise Up Now, We'll Have Nothing Left to Fight For," in which she argued that Israel was steadily becoming a pariah state as international sanctions on settlers and organizations turned from a trickle into a daily occurrence.[6] Galon wrote that such sanctions were a privilege and a warning signal — the equivalent of an intervention by friends no longer willing to pay Israel's bills — and that the Israeli government responded instead by crying "antisemitism!"[6] The piece named [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]], who comes and goes freely at the [[knesset|Knesset]] that bars relatives of the hostages, and [[tzav-9|Tzav 9]], whose members she said are protected by the [[israeli-police|police]] and by the minister responsible for them.[6] Badlands Media quoted the column at length as evidence of internal Israeli alarm at the direction of the country's politics.[6]
