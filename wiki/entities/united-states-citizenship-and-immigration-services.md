# United States Citizenship and Immigration Services

United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) is the component of the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] responsible for administering lawful immigration, naturalization and citizenship records. Because it holds the federal government's authoritative data on who has become a citizen, it is central to any program that seeks to verify citizenship status for purposes other than immigration.

The Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026 reports that USCIS officials, alongside DHS, said internal deliberations were ongoing regarding implementation of [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s March 2026 executive order [[ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections|"Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections"]], which directs DHS to develop a "state citizenship list." That list would be assembled from the kind of records USCIS maintains and shared with states for use in voter-roll verification, work coordinated in [[white-house|White House]] interagency talks involving the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]].

## SAVE program check results (May 2026)

The May 18, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that the Trump administration has run at least 67 million voter registrations through the [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] [[save-program|SAVE program]], with USCIS saying 60 million checks identified about 24,000 potential noncitizens. The [[associated-press|Associated Press]] reported at least 25 states have used SAVE for voter-roll checks since April 2025. The checks are the administrative counterpart to the [[save-america-act|SAVE Act]] (H.R. 22), which would require documentary proof of U.S. citizenship to register to vote in federal elections.
