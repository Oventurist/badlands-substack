# Benin

Benin is a West African nation situated between [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] — the leading state of the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] (AES) — and [[nigeria|Nigeria]], the seat of power of [[ecowas|ECOWAS]], the regional governing authority from which the AES countries have defected in pursuit of sovereignty.[1] This geography places Benin at the fault line of the wider contest between France, the European Union and the United Nations system on one side, and the AES and its Russian-aligned partners on the other.[1]

## December 2025 coup attempt

In December 2025 a group of soldiers announced their seizure of power over the national television channel SRTB, declaring the suspension of state institutions and of the Constitution of Benin and closing the country's air, land and maritime borders.[1] The mutineers appointed Lieutenant Colonel [[pascal-tigri|Pascal Tigri]] chairman of a Military Committee for Refoundation (CMR) "effective today," after which the channel's signal was cut.[1]

Beninese Foreign Minister Olushegun Adjadi Bakari told Reuters that "a small group" of soldiers had mounted the attempted coup but that forces loyal to President [[patrice-talon|Patrice Talon]] were restoring order, affirming that "a large part of the army is still loyalist."[1] According to a government report issued after a cabinet meeting, the mutineers first targeted senior military officials, including the president's military chief of staff, General Bertin Bada, whose wife was killed when they stormed his home; the soldiers also abducted General Abou Issa, the army chief of staff, and Colonel Faizou Gomina, releasing them only the following morning.[1] Mutineers clashed with the Republican Guard around 5am local time while attempting to storm Talon's residence, producing casualties on "both sides" before loyal troops forced a retreat; no death toll was provided.[1]

The Benin government credited Nigerian airstrikes with decisively crushing the attempt, stating that "Nigeria came to our assistance by carrying out airstrikes later in the day, which immobilized some of the armored vehicles used by the mutineers."[1]

## Regional implications

Commentators noted allegations that the coup was backed by the AES, pointing to Burkinabè President Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]]'s prior accusations that Benin had permitted French military bases near its border — claims Talon has publicly denied over several years.[1] [[kemi-seba|Kemi Seba]], a French-born Beninese politician and advisor to Niger's junta leader Abdourahamane Tchiani, claimed that Nigerian and French special forces, and also Rwandan troops, had been deployed to Benin to thwart the coup.[1] The day after the Nigerian operation, Burkina Faso grounded a Nigerian Air Force C-130 for violating its airspace, an incident treated by Badlands as directly connected to events in Benin.[1]
