# Department of Agriculture

The US Department of Agriculture (USDA) is the federal department responsible for agriculture, food safety, and rural policy.[1]

## Role in the 2026 beef packer probe

Under Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke Rollins]], the USDA joined the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] in announcing an intensified [[beef-packer-antitrust-investigation-2026|antitrust investigation]] into [[jbs|JBS]], [[cargill|Cargill]], [[tyson-foods|Tyson Foods]], and [[national-beef|National Beef]], citing price manipulation, foreign ownership risks, and supply chain issues.[1] The department framed foreign ownership of major packers as a national security threat to American cattle producers and to the country itself, against a backdrop of US beef herds falling to 1950s lows amid drought and declining ranch numbers.[1]

## Farmer Bridge Assistance Program and grant reform

On December 31, 2025, the USDA announced commodity payment rates for the [[farmer-bridge-assistance-program|Farmer Bridge Assistance Program]], releasing per-acre rates by commodity under a program of one-time payments meant to help farmers manage market disruptions and ongoing economic pressures.[2] The program is part of a $12 billion farmer support package, of which $11 billion is designated for direct commodity payments intended to provide short-term relief and support operational stability for U.S. agricultural producers.[2]

The department simultaneously moved to eliminate fraud in grant and other farmer support programs, with Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke L. Rollins]] directing all USDA agencies and staff offices to immediately adopt the first-ever set of USDA General Terms and Conditions for all future awards.[2] Rollins said the change reduces bureaucracy, eases access to USDA programs, and strengthens the department's ability to act swiftly against non-compliant recipients, cooperators, subaward recipients, and subcontractors, replacing more than 100 separate sets of terms and conditions — some of which lacked termination clauses.[2] On December 30, 2025, she also announced that regenerative farming would be a top-five research priority for USDA.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized farmer bailouts generally, arguing that global corporations set input and output prices, erase margins, and drive small and medium farms into debt that the bailouts then service, transferring wealth from Americans to corporations while leaving producers where they started.[2]

## SNAP contingency funding during the 2025 shutdown

On November 3, 2025, with the federal shutdown in its 34th day, the USDA announced it would use contingency funds to maintain [[snap|SNAP]] benefits, but only at 50 percent of usual funding levels.[3] The move followed an order by Judge John McConnell of the U.S. District Court in Rhode Island, who rejected as "arbitrary" the administration's argument that the contingency fund could be tapped only in scenarios such as natural disasters and directed that funds begin being released by midday Monday.[3]
