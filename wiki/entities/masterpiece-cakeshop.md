# Masterpiece Cakeshop

**Masterpiece Cakeshop** is a bakery in Lakewood, Colorado, owned by Christian baker [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]], and the subject of more than a decade of litigation over whether the state may compel a business owner to create custom products celebrating events that conflict with his religious beliefs.[1]

## Scardina litigation

The most recent case concerned a request by transgender attorney [[autumn-scardina|Autumn Scardina]] for a pink-and-blue cake celebrating her gender transition, which Phillips declined to make on the grounds that doing so would violate his Christian faith.[1] On October 8, 2024, the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] declined in a 4–3 decision to rule on the merits, dismissing the case on procedural grounds.[1] Justice Melissa Hart wrote for the majority that the underlying constitutional question — how governments should balance the rights of transgender individuals to be free from discrimination in public accommodations against the rights of religious business owners operating in the public market — "has become the focus of intense public debate," but that "we cannot answer that question."[1]

Scardina's attorney, John McHugh, said he was disappointed and evaluating remaining legal options, arguing that the court "decided to avoid the merits of this issue by inventing an argument no party raised."[1] Phillips's attorney, Jake Werner of the [[alliance-defending-freedom|Alliance Defending Freedom]], said his client had been pursued and mocked for years: "Enough is enough. Jack has been dragged through courts for over a decade. It's time to leave him alone."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the "technicality" invoked by Colorado's institutional press was in substance the U.S. Constitution's protection of religious liberty and freedom of expression, and that the state's district and appeals courts had ruled against Phillips where the high court declined to rule.[1] She argued that activists in Colorado deliberately target Christian business owners to generate discrimination claims, that the courts have repeatedly found the government cannot compel participation in a ceremony violating deeply held religious beliefs, and that nevertheless "the process is the punishment."[1]

## Cited alongside the 2023 303 Creative decision

Following the Supreme Court's June 2023 decision for web designer [[lorie-smith|Lorie Smith]] in [[303-creative-llc-v-elenis|303 Creative LLC v. Elenis]], Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] pointed to Masterpiece Cakeshop owner [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]] as evidence that a favorable Supreme Court ruling does not end the legal exposure of religious objectors in Colorado, since Phillips had continued to face action from the [[colorado-civil-rights-commission|Colorado Civil Rights Commission]] after his own victory.[2]

## Continued litigation (2023)

By October 2023 the shop's owner, [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]], had spent more than eleven years in court over his refusal to create certain custom cakes, and the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] agreed to hear the second case against him, brought by a transgender lawyer who had requested a cake celebrating a gender transition.[3] Phillips maintained throughout that he serves everybody but will not create custom works communicating messages that conflict with his religious convictions — a position the US Supreme Court partially vindicated in 2018.[3] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] closed her item on the case by pledging that her next cake purchase would be from Masterpiece.[3] See [[masterpiece-cakeshop-litigation]].[3]
