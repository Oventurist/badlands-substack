# Tanya Chutkan

Tanya Chutkan is a U.S. District Judge in Washington, D.C., appointed by former President Barack Obama, who in February 2025 presided over litigation brought by Democrat-led states seeking to bar [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and his allies from accessing federal data or causing firings across the federal government.[1]

## Trump federal election case scheduling

Chutkan, who sits on the federal district court in Washington, presided over the case brought by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] against former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over alleged efforts to thwart the transfer of presidential power after the 2020 election.[2] At a hearing on Monday, August 28, 2023, she rejected the January 2024 trial date proposed by federal prosecutors, saying it did not give Trump enough time to prepare.[2] She also rejected the April 2026 date sought by the former president, well past the next presidential election, as "far beyond what is necessary" and as risking witnesses becoming unavailable or their memories fading.[2]

Instead Chutkan set the trial to begin on March 4, 2024 — one day before Super Tuesday, when more than a dozen states hold presidential primaries and while Trump was undertaking his third bid for the White House.[2] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the scheduling was deliberate, asking whether the establishment, fresh from a weekend in which Trump's mugshot became the most memetic image in American history, had chosen the date for maximum psychological impact on the electorate, and concluding with the single word "Control."[2]

## The DOGE restraining order hearing

At an hourlong hearing held by videoconference on the Presidents Day federal holiday, Monday, February 17, 2025, Chutkan appeared poised to reject the states' request for an immediate restriction on Musk's operations, saying the plaintiffs lacked sufficient concrete evidence to justify so extraordinary a remedy.[1] She nonetheless agreed that Musk's operations through the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] were taking place in troubling secrecy, and acknowledged that DOGE was moving so swiftly that it was difficult to reach quick conclusions about the legality of its actions.[1]

"DOGE appears to be moving in no sort of predictable and orderly fashion and plaintiffs are obviously scrambling to find out what's next," Chutkan said at the hearing. "I don't know if that's deliberate or not."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] cautioned that coverage framing the hearing as a win for the administration was not really accurate, noting that Chutkan had taken the motion under advisement with a ruling still forthcoming, and stated he had "zero faith" that she would rule in favor of the Trump administration.[1]

## Protective order cited in the SCIF dispute

The protective order Chutkan proposed in August 2023 barred [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his legal team from disclosing sensitive materials — recordings, transcripts and interview reports — to anyone outside his defense.[3] Prosecutors for [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] argued that Trump had misrepresented that order in seeking a dedicated [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|SCIF]], noting it required only that classified discussions occur in a SCIF, not in the specific facility housing classified discovery.[3]

## Gag order in the 2020 election case (October 2023)

Chutkan granted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] a temporary hold in October 2023 on her previous gag-order ruling in the 2020 election conspiracy case while his legal team appealed the order as unconstitutional.[4] Earlier that month she had ordered Trump not to make posts or statements regarding possible witnesses, special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], Smith's family, or court staff, while permitting general statements about the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and Washington itself.[4] Prosecutors argued that Trump's social media posts could incite violence or contaminate the jury pool, while his attorneys countered that the order violated his free speech rights, particularly as a leading candidate in the 2024 presidential election.[4]

Days after the temporary lift, Trump publicly criticized Smith on Truth Social as "deranged" and said he would seek a change of venue, arguing there was "NO WAY" he could get a fair trial in the District of Columbia.[4]

## Imposition of the limited gag order (October 16, 2023)

On Monday, October 16, 2023, Chutkan imposed the [[2023-trump-gag-order|limited gag order]] itself, restricting Trump from making public statements attacking witnesses and specific prosecutors or court staff members in the federal case concerning his efforts to overturn the 2020 election.[8] She held that Trump's free speech rights did not permit him "to launch a pretrial smear campaign" against those individuals, adding, "No other defendant would be allowed to do so, and I'm not going to allow it in this case."[8] The narrowly tailored order nonetheless left Trump free to keep disparaging the Justice Department and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as he campaigned, allowed him to assert that his prosecution was politically motivated, and apparently left him leeway to attack Chutkan herself.[8] She further ruled that Trump could criticize former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] — simultaneously a witness and a 2024 rival — so long as the attacks did not touch on Pence's role in the criminal prosecution.[8]

## Stay of the 2020 election case pending appeal

On December 13, 2023, Judge Tanya Chutkan issued a brief order largely granting Donald Trump's request to pause proceedings in the federal 2020 election case while he appealed the rejection of his presidential immunity claim.[5] She wrote that the appeal gave the D.C. Circuit jurisdiction over the case and required her to automatically stay further proceedings moving it toward trial, adding that if the case returned to her she would consider "whether to retain or continue the dates of any still-future deadlines and proceedings, including the trial scheduled for March 4."[5] The D.C. Circuit announced later that evening that it would fast-track its review of her underlying immunity decision.[5]

## Dismissal of the federal election case (November 2024)

On November 25, 2024, special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] filed a six-page motion with Chutkan seeking dismissal of the federal election subversion case against President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], writing that the Justice Department's position was that the Constitution required dismissal before the defendant's inauguration and that the outcome was "not based on the merits or strength of the case against the defendant."[6] Chutkan formally dismissed the case without prejudice that same afternoon.[6]

## February 2024 vacatur of the March trial date

In a court order released on Friday, February 2, 2024, Chutkan wrote that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federal election interference trial in Washington, D.C. would no longer begin on March 4.[7] It was unclear when the trial would start; the case had been on pause for nearly two months after Trump's team requested a stay on December 7 and it was granted on December 13, meaning the soonest a trial could begin was likely late April or early May.[7] A start in early May could easily push the trial's conclusion past the [[2024-republican-national-convention|Republican National Convention]], scheduled for July 15–18 in Milwaukee.[7] In a previous order Chutkan had reiterated that seven months was "sufficient time" for Trump to prepare for trial, not counting the period the case spent on pause.[7]

The ruling came while the D.C. Circuit Court had yet to decide whether the former president was immune from prosecution; a panel of federal appeals judges heard oral arguments on January 9 under an expedited schedule.[7] "The court will set a new schedule if and when the mandate is returned," Chutkan's order said.[7] A lawyer for Trump and a spokesman for the special counsel both declined to comment.[7]
