# Herman K. Beebe

Herman K. Beebe was a Louisiana banker and, in the account cited by Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], the most consequential and least known figure of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan scandal]].[1]

Stephen Pizzo wrote in *Inside Job: The Looting of America's Savings and Loans* that by the end of 1981 Beebe was a man to be reckoned with, prepared to use everything he had learned about banking and newly deregulated thrifts to build potentially one of the most powerful and corrupt banking networks ever seen in the United States.[1] When Congress announced it intended to deregulate thrifts, Beebe immediately saw the possibilities: real estate in Texas was white hot and deregulated thrifts could make more commercial real estate loans with fewer restrictions than ever before.[1] He began to diversify, investing in more S&Ls and helping his friends do the same, until word got around that anyone who needed money to gain control of a thrift should see Beebe.[1]

Rather than exert ownership outright, Beebe often held control behind the scenes.[1] The Comptroller of the Currency listed 55 state banks and 29 thrifts controlled at various times by Beebe and his associates, and as much as $10 billion in reckless loans was eventually handed out.[1] Pizzo alleged many Beebe connections to high-placed senior Mafia figures and often to "the Godfather himself," while journalist Pete Brewton described Beebe as a "Louisiana organised crime associate."[1] Lange remarks that a man with mafia ties controlling 84 institutions raised no red flags, and that despite responsibility for around $10 billion in losses nobody has ever heard of him.[1]

Beebe also supplied the $2.8 million in seed money for the Washington-based [[palmer-national-bank|Palmer National Bank]], founded in 1983 by [[stefan-halper|Stefan Halper]] and [[harvey-mclean-jr|Harvey McLean Jr.]], both of whom had worked on [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]]'s 1980 presidential campaign — a link Lange takes as evidence Bush already knew Beebe.[1] Texas, the epicenter of Beebe's network, had the most S&L failures of any state.[1]
