# Poncho Nevarez

Poncho Nevarez is a former Democratic Texas state lawmaker and rancher whose 500-acre property sits on the Rio Grande at the edge of Eagle Pass, Texas, one of the most heavily trafficked crossing points on the United States–Mexico border.[1] His ranch became an emblematic setting for reporting on the 2024 immigration debate because state and federal enforcement infrastructure was layered directly onto and around his land.[1]

## Border enforcement on his land

Coils of barbed wire installed at the edge of Nevarez's ranch were intended as a deterrent to migrants crossing the river, but in his account they mainly tore at the bodies and clothing of migrants who clambered through them into Eagle Pass.[1] The fencing was installed by the [[national-guard|National Guard]] as part of the Texas border-enforcement push, which also brought state police, helicopters, drones and river buoys to the area surrounding his property.[1] Nevarez described the fence as both "horribly barbaric and largely ineffective," and characterized the broader enforcement build-out in the same terms.[1]

Standing beside a mound of discarded clothes, diapers and other detritus left behind by migrants on his land, Nevarez told Bloomberg that "none of this stuff solves the problem."[1] He also argued that the handling of the border would be decisive in the 2024 presidential race, saying "this is gonna decide who becomes the president, how it's handled."[1]

## Significance in the 2024 immigration debate

Nevarez's remarks were used to frame reporting on immigration's emergence as a defining issue of the 2024 ballot, with a Bloomberg News/Morning Consult poll finding it second only to the economy among voters' top concerns.[1] The same reporting described courts bogged down by unprecedented case levels, with federal agents encountering roughly 10,000 people per day crossing the southern border in December, and depicted the United States immigration system as underfunded, opaque and bursting at the seams.[1]

Badlands Media commentary on the story, written by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], treated the border situation as a signal that the country was "not living in normal times with normal rules," arguing that under normal political conditions [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s reelection prospects would have been jeopardized and Democrats would be working to replace him.[1] The commentary attributed the surge to the reversal of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s immigration executive orders on the first day of the Biden administration, and cited a representative of the Denver mayor's office filmed asking migrants to leave and offering them passage elsewhere as evidence that sanctuary-city resources were finite.[1]
