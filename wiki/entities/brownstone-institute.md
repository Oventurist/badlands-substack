# Brownstone Institute

The **Brownstone Institute** is a research and publishing organization known for its criticism of COVID-19 policy, censorship, and administrative overreach.[1]

In March 2024 the institute published an analysis titled "The White House Makes Good on Its Antitrust Threats," examining why the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] moved against [[apple|Apple]].[1] The piece traced the action back to press secretary [[jen-psaki|Jen Psaki]]'s May 5, 2021 warning to social-media companies, in which she coupled demands to suppress COVID and election "misinformation" with a statement that the president supported "a robust antitrust program."[1] The institute argued that on its face the Apple case concerned the company's secure communications network, with the Justice Department seeking to force Apple to share its services with other networks, but that in substance it represented the government taking sides in a competitive dispute between companies — in this instance on behalf of Samsung and other smartphone providers, who resent the way Apple products all work together and want that changed.[1]

## Coverage of the 2025 public health freeze

In January 2025 the institute published an analysis of the communications freeze imposed across the federal health bureaucracy in the opening days of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, describing an explosion hitting "the whole of public health bureaucracies" and noting that Trump blamed those agencies in part for the meltdown of his previous term.[2] The piece rejected [[paul-krugman|Paul Krugman]]'s claim that Trump wanted people to die, observing that Krugman had supported lockdowns and vaccine mandates and had welcomed the great reset, and concluded that what actually seemed to be "dying the death" was the public health bureaucracy itself.[2] See [[2025-public-health-communications-freeze|the 2025 public health communications freeze]].[2]

## Warning on the WHO IHR amendments (May 2023)

A May 2023 Badlands Media article, "Behold, a Trojan Horse," cited the Brownstone Institute as warning that the proposed amendments to the [[international-health-regulations|International Health Regulations (IHR)]] would "expand the definitions of pandemics [and] health emergencies, including the introduction of 'potential' for harm rather than actual harm."[3] The article also reported that Brownstone noted the amendments would change the IHR's recommendations for states "from 'non-binding' to mandatory," a shift presented as evidence the [[world-health-organization|WHO]] was being empowered to override national sovereignty through the [[pandemic-treaty|pandemic treaty]] process.[3]

## Critique of WHO pandemic proposals

Badlands Media's "The WHO's Last Stand" cited [[david-bell|Dr. David Bell]], a Senior Scholar at the Brownstone Institute, public health physician and biotech consultant in global health, as a leading technical critic of the [[world-health-organization|WHO]]'s pandemic preparedness and response proposals.[4] Bell was quoted arguing that the COVID lockdown strategy did not originate with the WHO itself: "The idea of lockdowns didn't come from the WHO, it came from these external influences."[4]
