# John Ferguson

John Ferguson is the founder of [[saxon-aerospace|Saxon Aerospace]], an aerospace company specializing in military-grade unmanned aircraft, who became a prominent voice in the December 2024 debate over the [[2024-northeast-drone-sightings|mystery drone sightings over New Jersey]].[1]

## TikTok video on the New Jersey drones

Ferguson published a video on the Chinese-owned social media platform TikTok offering his professional assessment of the sightings, an assessment alarming enough that observers joked they would prefer Paul Krugman's alien-invasion theory be true instead.[1] In the video Ferguson recounted speaking "a few months ago" with a man who claimed to have physically handled a nuclear warhead left over from Ukraine and who was attempting to raise the alarm at the highest levels of the United States government because he believed the device was headed toward the United States.[1] Ferguson added his view that "everyone knows that this administration is pushing to get into war with Russia."[1]

## The "nuclear sniffer" theory

Ferguson drew a parallel to an earlier episode in which unexplained drones were observed flying across Interstate 70 from Colorado into Nebraska, Kansas and Missouri, which he said was believed at the time to be a search for radioactive material that had gone missing in the United States.[1] Discussing the payloads drones can carry — laser sensors, optics and thermal imagers — he argued that "drones have no reason to fly at night" because mapping work must be done in daylight unless thermal optics are fitted.[1]

Applying that logic to New Jersey, Ferguson concluded that the aircraft were "not nefarious in intent," but that the only reason for craft to fly that low at night would be that "they're trying to smell something on the ground," whether gas leaks, radioactive material "or whatever."[1] The theory circulated alongside reports of elevated radiation readings in the New York City area, giving rise to the "nuclear sniffer" framing.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the episode as an illustration of the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] thesis that the very absurdity of the central narrative is a feature rather than a bug, arguing that the Fear Narrative must grow ever more preposterous to remain jarring, and pointing to the contrast between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] memeing about drone warfare while the media and military treated the threat with total seriousness.[1]
