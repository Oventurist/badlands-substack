# Raúl Salinas de Gortari

Raúl Salinas de Gortari is the elder brother of Mexican president [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]] and, in Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s account, "the key to exposing the 'real purpose' behind NAFTA."[1] While his brother was president, Raúl held a low-profile civil-service post in the administration's food-distribution agency and was one of Mexico's best-paid civil servants, never officially earning more than $190,000 a year — yet he accumulated holdings Mexican authorities said could exceed $200 million.[1]

## The Citibank pipeline

Every few weeks throughout 1993 a courier delivered a cashier's check in pesos worth $3 million to $5 million to the 16th floor of [[citibank|Citibank]]'s Mexico City headquarters, where clerks operating under instructions from New York to "ask no questions" converted the proceeds to dollars and wired them to New York and on to Switzerland.[1] More than $80 million left the country this way for a client the Mexico City office did not know was Raúl Salinas.[1] He told Swiss investigators in December 1995 that he had opened overseas accounts under false names in 1989, shortly after his brother took office, "to avoid political scandal," but that once he entered the world of private banking in 1993 Citibank officers in New York "came up with a whole strategy" under which aliases were unnecessary: money was transferred to European banks and deposited under the names of untraceable Cayman Islands shell corporations set up for him with Citibank's help.[1] His private banker was [[amy-g-elliott|Amy G. Elliott]], a Cuban-American who joined Citibank's human resources department in 1967 and rose to head the New York unit advising wealthy Mexicans.[1]

Lange uses the episode to argue that [[money-laundering|money laundering]] laws are "a big joke": large international banks cater to criminals with dedicated relationship managers and offshore shell companies, and when caught pay fines that are a fraction of the profits earned.[1]

## The Swiss report

After a nearly three-year inquiry into drug corruption in Mexico, Swiss police investigators concluded in a secret 369-page report that Raúl Salinas played a central role in Mexico's cocaine trade, taking huge bribes to protect the flow of drugs into the United States.[1] The report states that when Carlos became president in 1988, "Raul Salinas de Gortari assumed control over practically all drug shipments through Mexico," with army and police officials supporting and protecting the business through bribes paid with drug money.[1] From the food-distribution agency he commandeered government trucks and railroad cars to haul cocaine north, skimming payoffs the Swiss estimated at upwards of $500 million, and arranged "green light days" on which loads could transit Mexico without being checked by the army, coast guard or federal police.[1] He also channelled drug money into his brother's presidential campaign.[1] Swiss federal prosecutor Carla del Ponte called him a "drug kingpin" and one of the main figures of the Mexican drug cartel.[1]

Raúl was imprisoned in a maximum-security facility in Mexico, charged with financial wrongdoing and with masterminding a rival's murder.[1] His brother-in-law [[jose-francisco-ruiz-massieu|José Francisco Ruiz Massieu]], the number-two official in the PRI, was assassinated in 1994.[1]

## The Bush connection

Raúl Salinas was a close personal friend of [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]]; the two families vacationed together for many years, the Salinases at Jeb Bush's home in Miami and the Bushes at Raúl's ranch, Las Mendocinas, in Puebla.[1] The friendship, Lange writes, originated with their fathers: [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and the family patriarch [[raul-salinas-lozano|Raúl Salinas Lozano]] were "íntimo amigos."[1] For Lange, Raúl's role connects the trade agreement to its concealed function — government transport moving cocaine to the border, then the [[nafta|NAFTA]] "National Goods" designation carrying it past state and local inspection into the United States.[1]
