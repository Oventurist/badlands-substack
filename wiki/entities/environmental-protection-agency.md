# Environmental Protection Agency

The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is the U.S. federal regulator whose 2009 endangerment finding provided the legal foundation for federal greenhouse gas regulation and which, under Administrator [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]], moved in February 2026 to rescind that finding.[1]

## The 2009 endangerment finding

The [[endangerment-finding|2009 endangerment finding]] is a landmark scientific and legal determination that greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide endanger public health and welfare, providing the foundational basis under the [[clean-air-act|Clean Air Act]] for regulating emissions from vehicles, power plants, oil-and-gas facilities, and other sources.[1]

## 2026 rescission

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] prepared a final rule rescinding the finding, to be issued on a Thursday following a [[white-house|White House]] ceremony with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Zeldin.[1] The rule followed a proposed rescission announced the previous July and an executive order issued on Inauguration Day directing a review of the finding's legality.[1] Administration officials described the move as the largest act of deregulation in U.S. history, projecting over $1 trillion in regulatory cost savings — primarily through reduced vehicle compliance expenses averaging more than $2,400 per light-duty car, SUV, or truck — and emphasizing benefits for energy affordability, fossil fuel production, economic growth, and national security.[1]

The rescission initially targets motor vehicle emissions standards, eliminating requirements to measure, report, certify, and comply with greenhouse gas limits, while paving the way for potential future rollbacks of power plant and stationary-source rules.[1]

## Opposition and litigation

Environmental groups including [[earthjustice|Earthjustice]] and the [[environmental-defense-fund|Environmental Defense Fund]] strongly condemned the action as a major assault on federal climate policy, warning of increased pollution, thousands of premature deaths, higher health and fuel costs, and accelerated climate impacts, and pledged immediate court challenges based on settled science and Supreme Court precedent upholding the finding.[1] Democratic-led states signaled opposition and potential independent emissions standards, while the fossil fuel industry welcomed relief from regulatory burdens.[1] Legal experts anticipated prolonged litigation during which some rules may remain unenforced, as the administration framed the rollback as essential for lowering energy prices amid broader efforts to support coal plants and re-industrialization.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the rescission as "the LARGEST environmental deregulation in history" and part of a "Golden Age," arguing that environmentalism functions as a cult and has nothing to do with the environment.[1] Quoting her own November 2024 brief, she wrote that windmills beach whales, plastic bags replaced paper bags to save trees but became the nation's leading pollutant, and hydroelectric power destroys river ecosystems, concluding that environmentalist solutions consistently produce worse outcomes after large taxpayer expenditures.[1] She argued the finding, enabled by [[massachusetts-v-epa|Massachusetts v. EPA]], unlocked sweeping carbon mandates across energy, manufacturing, transportation, and logistics, and that ending the mandates should remove compliance costs baked into power, fuel, freight, and production prices — a "structural reset on climate communism."[1]

## Criminal referrals over green energy grants (2026)

In June 2026 the agency's administrator, [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]], made criminal referrals to the EPA inspector general and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] alleging self-dealing in the distribution of Biden-era green energy grants funded by the [[inflation-reduction-act|Inflation Reduction Act]].[2] Zeldin canceled or paused roughly $29 billion in such grants, including $20 billion from the [[greenhouse-gas-reduction-fund|Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund]] routed through eight nonprofit "cutouts" and a $2 billion residential decarbonization grant to [[power-forward-communities|Power Forward Communities]].[2]

Cases cited in the referrals included former fund director [[jahi-wise|Jahi Wise]] overseeing a $5 billion grant to his prior employer, the Power Forward Communities award to a nonprofit tied to [[stacey-abrams|Stacey Abrams]] that had reported $100 in prior-year revenue, and a $20 million grant to [[young-gifted-and-green|Young, Gifted & Green]] whose chief executive applied while sitting on the White House Environmental Justice Advisory Council.[2] Zeldin said the money went to "pass-through entities" tied to former Obama–Biden officials and Democratic donors, calling the rushed 2024 distribution "blatant waste and abuse."[2]

## 2024 heavy-duty vehicle emissions litigation

In May 2024 a coalition of 24 states filed a petition for review in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit challenging the EPA's new regulation setting stronger greenhouse gas emissions standards for heavy-duty vehicles, naming the agency and its administrator [[michael-regan|Michael Regan]] as defendants.[3] Nebraska Attorney General Hilgers announced the filings on May 13, 2024, part of two legal challenges by Republican-led states against the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration and the State of California over electric vehicle mandates on truck owners and operators nationwide.[3] Plaintiffs argued that the EPA's stringent tailpipe standards effectively force manufacturers to build more electric trucks and fewer internal combustion trucks.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the suits, arguing that the federal government is operating far outside its constitutional limitations and that "freedom is only possible by constantly struggling for it."[3]

## Electric vehicle mandate and the California waiver

The EPA's tailpipe emissions standards adopted under the Obama and Biden administrations underpin what critics call an [[epa-electric-vehicle-mandate|electric-vehicle mandate]], which would effectively require automakers to shift at least 54 percent of production to EVs and 16 percent to hybrids to meet 2032 requirements.[4] In December 2024 the Biden-era EPA granted California a waiver allowing the state to set stricter environmental regulations than the federal government, greenlighting California's aggressive zero-emission vehicle mandate; commentators urged the incoming [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] to revoke the waiver, anticipating litigation.[4] Because other states may adopt California's climate rules — New York, Maryland and Virginia among them — the waiver's effects extend nationwide.[4]

### Inflation Reduction Act climate funds and the Power Forward Communities award
In February 2025 the EPA disclosed that the Biden administration had awarded $2 billion to Power Forward Communities, a nonprofit partnered with multiple left-wing groups founded by Stacey Abrams.[5] The money had been parked at Citibank, an outside financial institution, before Biden left office, and formed part of a larger $20 billion pot the agency received through the Inflation Reduction Act to dole out to climate groups.[5] Administrator Lee Zeldin said it was "extremely concerning that an organization that reported just $100 in revenue in 2023 was chosen to receive $2 billion," roughly 20 million times the group's reported revenue.[5] Badlands commentary noted that the funds were discovered by DOGE.[5]
