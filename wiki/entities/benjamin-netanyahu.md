# Benjamin Netanyahu

## Media campaign over Israel's reputation (July 2026)

A growing number of major media outlets argued in July 2026 that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has inflicted lasting damage on [[israel|Israel]]'s standing with the American public, contending that years of partisan political alignment, the ongoing Gaza war, and repeated clashes with US administrations eroded what was once broad bipartisan support.[1] Commentators argued Netanyahu had transformed Israel from a largely bipartisan issue into an increasingly polarizing one in American politics.[1] [[pew-research-center|Pew Research Center]] polling found unfavorable views of Israel and declining confidence in Netanyahu had risen since 2022, worsening across demographic groups and among younger voters in both major parties.[1]

The criticism coincided with renewed strains between Washington and Jerusalem: while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] praised [[turkey|Turkey]] as a "tremendous ally," he also publicly criticized Israeli military operations during a meeting with Netanyahu, underscoring increasingly visible policy differences even as the alliance remained intact.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified a repeating pattern: the last time Netanyahu "tried to crash the White House," in September 2025 during the UN General Assembly, Trump responded by inviting [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]] to the White House the day before Netanyahu was scheduled to address the assembly; with Netanyahu again inviting himself, Trump was again using Erdogan to signal his real intent.[1] In that commentator's view, Trump's plan is not to wage kinetic war against Israel but to destroy its public reputation "by feeding into its greatest indulgences and allowing it the space to show the world what it truly is," with Israel's war against Iran costing it the goodwill accumulated over fifteen years.[1]
